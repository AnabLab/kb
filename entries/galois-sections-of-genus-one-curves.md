**Setting.** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$ with absolute Galois group $G_k$, and let $W$ be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve of genus $1$ over $k$, with Jacobian $E = \mathrm{Pic}^0_W$. Then $W$ is a torsor under $E$, through the Abel–Jacobi isomorphism $W \cong \mathrm{Pic}^1_W$, so it has a class $[W]$ in the Weil–Châtelet group $H^1(k, E)$, and $W(k) \ne \emptyset$ exactly when $[W] = 0$. For an abelian group $M$, write $\mathrm{Div}(M)$ for its maximal divisible subgroup. These facts on torsors are standard and are not checked in a source here.

**Criterion.** The projection $\pi_1(W) \to G_k$ has a continuous section if and only if $[W] \in \mathrm{Div}\, H^1(k, E)$.

This is [Harari–Szamuely's theorem](abelianized-galois-sections.md) [@harariszamuely2009, Theorem 1.2, pp. 2–3] for $X = W$, after three identifications, checked here.

- The geometric fundamental group $\pi_1(W_{\bar k})$ is that of an elliptic curve, which is abelian. So the abelianized fundamental sequence of $W$ is the fundamental sequence itself, as Wittenberg notes for genus one [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, Remarque 3.5, p. 8].
- For curves, the Néron–Severi group is torsion-free, so the extension $\Pi$ of Harari–Szamuely's theorem is the abelianized sequence [@harariszamuely2009, p. 2].
- For a curve, $\mathrm{Alb}^1$ is $\mathrm{Pic}^1$ under its Jacobian. For genus one, the Abel–Jacobi map is an isomorphism $W \cong \mathrm{Pic}^1_W$ by Riemann–Roch, which Wittenberg uses in the form $\mathrm{Pic}^1(W_K) = W(K)$ for every extension $K$ of the base [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, p. 6]. So the class of $\mathrm{Alb}^1_W$ is $[W]$.

Çiperiani and Stix state the "only if" direction for torsors under any abelian variety, citing Harari–Szamuely and Stix [@ciperianistix2013, §1.1, p. 2].

**Elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$.** Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$, and consider the genus-one curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with Jacobian $E$.

1. *Positive rank.* If $E(\mathbb{Q})$ is infinite, then $\mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E) = \mathrm{Div}\, Ш(E/\mathbb{Q})$ [@ciperianistix2013, Proposition 21, p. 18]. If moreover $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is finite, both are $0$, so by the criterion such a curve has a section only if it has a rational point. This applies when $E$ has analytic rank $1$, where $\mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E) = 0$ [@ciperianistix2013, Corollary 22, p. 18]. Harari and Szamuely had shown the triviality of the maximal divisible subgroup under a similar hypothesis, for products of elliptic curves (see [their entry](abelianized-galois-sections.md)).
2. *Rank zero.* If $E(\mathbb{Q})$ is finite, then $\mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E)$ contains a copy of $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ [@ciperianistix2013, Proposition 17 (1), p. 15], and it is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ if $E$ has analytic rank $0$ [@ciperianistix2013, Theorem 18 (2), p. 15]. So, as checked here, there are infinitely many genus-one curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with Jacobian $E$ that have a section but no rational point: each nonzero class in $\mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E)$ gives one, by the criterion. Two classes give isomorphic curves only if they differ by an automorphism of $E$, because an isomorphism of curves $W \to W'$ is equivariant for the automorphism of $E$ that it induces on $\mathrm{Pic}^0$, and $\mathrm{Aut}(E)$ is finite. When $E$ has analytic rank $0$, the $p$-primary part of $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cap \mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E)$ is trivial for every odd prime $p$ of good reduction such that $E[p]$ is an irreducible Galois module [@ciperianistix2013, Theorem 18 (1), p. 15]. So a class in $\mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E)$ whose order is divisible by such a $p$ is not in $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q})$, and its curve has no points over some completion of $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Selmer's curve.** Let $S$ be the plane cubic $3X^3 + 4Y^3 + 5Z^3 = 0$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Its Jacobian $E$ is $X^3 + Y^3 + 60Z^3 = 0$, with $E(\mathbb{Q}) = 0$ and analytic rank $0$ [@ciperianistix2013, §8, p. 18]. Its class is a nonzero element of $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{Z}/3$, a computation that Çiperiani and Stix attribute to Mazur and Rubin, and the other nonzero elements are represented by the curves
$$S' : X^3 + 5Y^3 + 12Z^3 = 0, \qquad S'' : X^3 + 4Y^3 + 15Z^3 = 0, \qquad S''' : X^3 + 3Y^3 + 20Z^3 = 0,$$
which together with $S$ form the list of isomorphism classes of these genus-one curves [@ciperianistix2013, p. 19]. A torsor $W$ and its inverse $-W$ are isomorphic as curves [@ciperianistix2013, p. 19, footnote 4].

- *Theorem (Çiperiani–Stix).* $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cap \mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E)$ is generated by $[S]$ [@ciperianistix2013, Proposition 23, p. 20, and Theorem B, p. 3]. Hence $\pi_1(S) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ splits, although $S$ has points everywhere locally and no rational point [@ciperianistix2013, Remark 25, p. 21].
- *The other three curves have no section.* This is checked here. The classes of $S'$, $S''$ and $S'''$ are nonzero elements of $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q})$. None of them is $\pm[S]$, since a curve whose class is $\pm[S]$ is isomorphic to $S$, and the four curves represent distinct isomorphism classes. So none lies in $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q}) \cap \mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E) = \{0, \pm[S]\}$, and by the criterion none of these curves has a section. All three have points everywhere locally and no rational point. So among torsors under one elliptic curve, all in $Ш$, some have sections and some do not.

Stix had constructed a Brauer class that explains the absence of rational points on $S$, and found an adelic family of sections that passes his Brauer–Manin test for sections, and he left open whether $\pi_1(S) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ splits [@stix2011bm, Question 45, p. 29] (see [descent obstructions](descent-obstructions-for-galois-sections.md)). The theorem answers this question, which Çiperiani and Stix cite as Question 49 of the journal version [@ciperianistix2013, Remark 25, p. 21]. The proof of the theorem computes the Selmer group with no condition at $3$, which gives $\mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E)$ meeting $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q})$ in a group of order $3$, and then identifies $[S]$ as a generator. Through a Bockstein map over $\mathbb{Z}[1/30]$, this reduces to the vanishing of a Weil–Bockstein pairing of the class of $S$ with itself, which follows from the antisymmetry of the Weil pairing [@ciperianistix2013, pp. 19–20]. The prime $3$ is outside the hypotheses of item 2, since $E$ has additive reduction there and $E[3]$ is reducible [@ciperianistix2013, p. 19].

**The Reichardt–Lind curve.** Let $X \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be given by $2T^2 = A^2 - 17B^2$ and $AB = C^2$. It is a genus-one curve whose Jacobian $E : y^2 = x(x^2 + 17)$ has $E(\mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ and analytic rank $0$ [@stix2011bm, §5.1, p. 15], and its class is a nonzero element of $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q})$ [@stix2011bm, §5.1, p. 16]. Wittenberg proved that $\pi_1(X) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ does not split [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, Théorème 1.1, p. 1], answering a question of Stix [@stix2011bm, Question 34, p. 23]; see [Wittenberg's criterion](wittenberg-nonsplitting-criterion.md). By the criterion, as checked here, $[X]$ lies in $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q})$ but not in $\mathrm{Div}\, H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E)$, which is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ by item 2.

**What this shows.**

- *Harari and Szamuely's question.* They asked whether the class of $\mathrm{Alb}^1_X$ can be a nonzero element of the maximal divisible subgroup of $H^1(k, \mathrm{Alb}_X)$ [@harariszamuely2009, Question 3.1, p. 7], and wrote that they did not know whether a nonzero element of a Tate–Shafarevich group can be divisible in $H^1(k, A)$ [@harariszamuely2009, §4, p. 10]. Selmer's curve gives a positive answer to both over $\mathbb{Q}$, with a curve that has points everywhere locally.
- *Hyperbolicity is needed.* The [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) is stated for hyperbolic curves. Selmer's curve and the curves of item 2 show that its analogue for proper curves of genus one fails over $\mathbb{Q}$, even for curves with points everywhere locally, while the Reichardt–Lind curve and $S'$, $S''$, $S'''$ show that sections can still be absent in genus one for reasons invisible locally.
