**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite, $C = \widehat{\bar K}$, $I_K \subseteq G_K$ the inertia group and $\chi = \chi_K$ the $p$-adic cyclotomic character. Representations are continuous, on finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_p$-vector spaces. As in [Sen's inertia criterion](sen-inertia-criterion.md), $\mathbb{Q}_p(1)$ has Hodge–Tate weight $1$. Brinon and Conrad use the opposite sign [@brinonconrad2009, Example 2.3.5, p. 17], and the citations below are translated. For a representation $V$ and $h \in \mathbb{Z}$ put
$$D^h(V) = \big(C \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V(-h)\big)^{G_K},\qquad V(-h) = V \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \mathbb{Q}_p(-h),$$
a $K$-vector space; it is Brinon and Conrad's $W\{-h\}$ for $W = C \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V$. Then $\sum_h \dim_K D^h(V) \le \dim V$, with equality exactly when $V$ is Hodge–Tate [@brinonconrad2009, Lemma 2.3.1, p. 16, and Definition 2.3.4, p. 17]. In that case $C \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V \cong \bigoplus_h C(h)^{\dim D^h(V)}$, and $h$ is a weight of multiplicity $\dim_K D^h(V)$ [@brinonconrad2009, Example 2.3.5, p. 17].

**Definition.** Call $V$ *potentially ordinary* if there are a finite extension $K'/K$ in $\bar K$, a filtration $0 = F_0 \subsetneq F_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq F_m = V$ by $G_{K'}$-stable subspaces, pairwise distinct integers $h_1, \dots, h_m$, and an open subgroup $J \subseteq I_{K'}$ that acts on each $F_i/F_{i-1}$ by $\chi^{h_i}$. Call $V$ *ordinary* if this holds with $K' = K$ and $J = I_K$. No ordering of the $h_i$ is imposed. The definition uses only $I_K$, $\chi$ and open subgroups of $G_K$, all of which an isomorphism of absolute Galois groups transports.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Extensions with disjoint weights.* Let $0 \to V' \to V \to V'' \to 0$ be an exact sequence of representations of $G_K$, with $V'$ and $V''$ Hodge–Tate and no Hodge–Tate weight in common. Then $V$ is Hodge–Tate, and the multiplicity of each weight in $V$ is the sum of its multiplicities in $V'$ and $V''$.
2. *Potentially ordinary representations are Hodge–Tate.* With the notation of the definition, $V$ is Hodge–Tate, with weight $h_i$ of multiplicity $\dim F_i/F_{i-1}$ and no other weights.
3. *Transport.* Let $L/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite and $\alpha : G_L \to G_K$ a continuous isomorphism. If $V$ is potentially ordinary, so is $V \circ \alpha$, with the same filtration, the same integers $h_i$ and $J$ replaced by an open subgroup of inertia over a finite extension of $L$. So $V \circ \alpha$ is Hodge–Tate with the same weights and multiplicities as $V$. In particular every potentially ordinary representation is [Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md).
4. *Ordinary $p$-divisible groups.* Let $G$ be a $p$-divisible group over $\mathcal{O}_K$, with connected–étale sequence $0 \to G^0 \to G \to G^{\mathrm{et}} \to 0$, and suppose that the height of $G^0$ equals the dimension of $G$. Then $V_pG = T(G) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathbb{Q}_p$ is potentially ordinary with $K' = K$: $V_p(G^0)$ is a $G_K$-stable subspace on which an open subgroup of $I_K$ acts by $\chi$, and an open subgroup of $I_K$ acts trivially on the quotient $V_p(G^{\mathrm{et}})$. So part 3 applies to $V_pG$.
5. *Abelian varieties.* Let $A/K$ be an abelian variety of dimension $n$, and suppose that for some finite $K'/K$ there is an abelian scheme $X$ over $\mathcal{O}_{K'}$ with generic fibre $A_{K'}$ whose formal group $X(p)^0$ has height $n$. Then the $p$-adic Tate module $V_pA$ is potentially ordinary, with weights $1$ and $0$, each of multiplicity $n$. So $V_pA \circ \alpha$ is Hodge–Tate for every $\alpha$ as in part 3. For an elliptic curve, the height condition says that the reduction has nonzero Hasse invariant [@tate1967pdivisible, §2.3, Examples, b), p. 166].

The converse of part 2 also holds in the following form. If $V$ is Hodge–Tate and has a $G_K$-stable filtration whose graded pieces each have a single weight, and these weights are pairwise distinct, then $V$ is potentially ordinary with $K' = K$. Indeed, by [Sen's inertia criterion](sen-inertia-criterion.md) each piece has an open subgroup of $I_K$ acting by the corresponding power of $\chi$, and $J$ can be taken to be the intersection of these finitely many subgroups. Consecutive pieces of the same weight can be merged first, because a subquotient of a Hodge–Tate representation is Hodge–Tate [@brinonconrad2009, Proposition 2.4.4, p. 19].

**Relation to known results.**

- *Dimension at most $2$.* A Hodge–Tate character has a single weight, so it is potentially ordinary with $m = 1$, and part 3 gives the implication from Hodge–Tate to Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate in Hoshi's Theorem 2.7 [@hoshi2024, Theorem 2.7, p. 8]. Let $V$ be reducible of dimension $2$ and Hodge–Tate. Its line and quotient are Hodge–Tate characters [@brinonconrad2009, Proposition 2.4.4, p. 19]. If their weights are equal, $V$ has a single weight, and if not, $V$ is potentially ordinary with $m = 2$. So part 3 also gives that implication in Hoshi's Theorem 2.10 [@hoshi2024, Theorem 2.10, p. 9], whose proof goes through a criterion special to dimension $2$ [@hoshi2024, Lemma 2.8, p. 8]. The case $m = 1$ in every dimension is [Sen's inertia criterion](sen-inertia-criterion.md).
- *Beyond dimension $2$.* For $n \ge 2$, the Tate modules of part 5 are reducible of dimension $2n \ge 4$, so they are covered by neither of Hoshi's theorems. Part 4 likewise covers Tate modules of $p$-divisible groups of every height whose connected component has height equal to its dimension.
- *Contrast.* Irreducible representations need not behave this way. For $p \ge 5$ there is an irreducible Hodge–Tate representation of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ of dimension $2$ that is [not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate](two-dimensional-representation-of-g-qp-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md). Its two weights are distinct, as they must be by [Sen's inertia criterion](sen-inertia-criterion.md), but no stable filtration separates them. The Lubin–Tate representations of part 2 of [the overview](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md) are further examples.

**Proof of part 1.** Fix $h \in \mathbb{Z}$. Tensoring with $C$ and twisting gives an exact sequence $0 \to C \otimes V'(-h) \to C \otimes V(-h) \to C \otimes V''(-h) \to 0$ of $C$-vector spaces with semilinear $G_K$-action, so $0 \to D^h(V') \to D^h(V) \to D^h(V'')$ is exact. There are two cases.

- *$h$ is not a weight of $V'$.* Let $x \in D^h(V'')$, choose a lift $y \in C \otimes V(-h)$, and put $c(g) = g(y) - y$. Then $c$ is a continuous $1$-cocycle of $G_K$ with values in $C \otimes V'(-h)$. As $V'$ is Hodge–Tate, $C \otimes V'(-h) \cong \bigoplus_j C(h'_j - h)$, where the $h'_j$ are the weights of $V'$ with multiplicity. Each $h'_j - h$ is nonzero, and $H^1_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_K, C(r)) = 0$ for $r \ne 0$ [@brinonconrad2009, Theorem 2.2.7, p. 15]. So $c(g) = g(z) - z$ for some $z \in C \otimes V'(-h)$, and $y - z$ is a $G_K$-invariant lift of $x$. Hence $D^h(V) \to D^h(V'')$ is surjective, and $\dim D^h(V) = \dim D^h(V') + \dim D^h(V'')$.
- *$h$ is a weight of $V'$.* Then $h$ is not a weight of $V''$, so $D^h(V'') = 0$ and again $\dim D^h(V) = \dim D^h(V') + \dim D^h(V'')$.

Summing over $h$, and using that $V'$ and $V''$ are Hodge–Tate,
$$\sum_h \dim_K D^h(V) = \dim V' + \dim V'' = \dim V.$$
So $V$ is Hodge–Tate, and the multiplicities add. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** View $V$ as a representation of $G_{K'}$, and let $\chi_{K'} = \chi|_{G_{K'}}$, the cyclotomic character of $K'$. By [Sen's inertia criterion](sen-inertia-criterion.md), applied over $K'$, each $F_i/F_{i-1}$ is Hodge–Tate with the single weight $h_i$, since $J$ acts on it by $\chi_{K'}^{h_i}$. By induction on $i$, $F_i$ is Hodge–Tate with weights $h_1, \dots, h_i$, of multiplicities $\dim F_j/F_{j-1}$: for $i \ge 2$, apply part 1 over $K'$ to $0 \to F_{i-1} \to F_i \to F_i/F_{i-1} \to 0$, where $h_i$ differs from $h_1, \dots, h_{i-1}$. So $V$ is Hodge–Tate as a representation of $G_{K'}$. The natural map $K' \otimes_K D_K(W) \to D_{K'}(W)$ of graded vector spaces is an isomorphism for every $W$, so $V$ is Hodge–Tate as a representation of $G_K$, with the same weights and multiplicities [@brinonconrad2009, Theorem 2.4.6, p. 20]. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** The subgroup $\alpha^{-1}(G_{K'})$ is open in $G_L$, so it is $G_{L'}$ for a finite extension $L'/L$ in $\bar L$, and $\alpha$ restricts to an isomorphism $\alpha' : G_{L'} \to G_{K'}$. By the [functorial invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md) applied to $\alpha'$, $\alpha'(I_{L'}) = I_{K'}$ and $\chi_{K'} \circ \alpha' = \chi_{L'}$; these are the constructions Hoshi recalls [@hoshi2024, Remark 2.4.1 (i)–(ii), p. 7]. The subspaces $F_i$ are stable under $(V \circ \alpha)(G_{L'}) = V(G_{K'})$, the subgroup $J' = \alpha'^{-1}(J)$ is open in $I_{L'}$, and for $g \in J'$ the element $V(\alpha(g))$ acts on $F_i/F_{i-1}$ by $\chi_{K'}(\alpha'(g))^{h_i} = \chi_{L'}(g)^{h_i}$. So $V \circ \alpha$ is potentially ordinary with data $(L', F_\bullet, h_\bullet, J')$, and part 2 applies to it. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** All facts about $p$-divisible groups below are from Tate, over a complete discrete valuation ring of characteristic $0$ with perfect residue field of characteristic $p$ [@tate1967pdivisible, §3, opening, p. 170], which includes $\mathcal{O}_K$.

- *The filtration.* For each $v$, the levels form an exact sequence $0 \to G^0_v \to G_v \to G^{\mathrm{et}}_v \to 0$ of finite group schemes, and these give the connected–étale sequence [@tate1967pdivisible, §1.4, p. 160; §2.2, sequence (4), p. 164]. On $\bar K$-points the left map is injective with image the kernel of the right map. The generic fibres are étale, as $K$ has characteristic $0$ [@tate1967pdivisible, §2.4, p. 169], so the groups of $\bar K$-points have the orders of the group schemes, and these orders multiply in an exact sequence [@tate1967pdivisible, §1.3, p. 160]. So $0 \to G^0_v(\bar K) \to G_v(\bar K) \to G^{\mathrm{et}}_v(\bar K) \to 0$ is exact. Inverse limits of short exact sequences of finite groups are exact, so $0 \to T(G^0) \to T(G) \to T(G^{\mathrm{et}}) \to 0$ is an exact sequence of $\mathbb{Z}_p[G_K]$-modules, with $T(H) = \varprojlim_v H_v(\bar K)$ [@tate1967pdivisible, §2.4, p. 169].
- *Weights.* For every $p$-divisible group $H$ over $\mathcal{O}_K$, with dual $H'$, Tate gives a $G_K$-isomorphism $\mathrm{Hom}(T(H), C) \cong t_{H'}(C) \oplus t^*_H(C) \otimes_C \mathrm{Hom}(T(\mathbf{G}_m(p)), C)$, where $t_{H'}(C)$ and $t^*_H(C)$ have dimensions $\dim H'$ and $\dim H$ over $C$ [@tate1967pdivisible, §4.1, Theorem 3, Corollary 2, p. 180; §2.4, p. 168]. As $T(\mathbf{G}_m(p)) = \mathbb{Z}_p(1)$, taking $C$-duals gives $C \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V_pH \cong C^{\dim H'} \oplus C(1)^{\dim H}$. So $V_pH$ is Hodge–Tate, with weight $1$ of multiplicity $\dim H$ and weight $0$ of multiplicity $\dim H'$.
- *The two pieces.* The dimension of a $p$-divisible group is that of the formal Lie group of its connected component [@tate1967pdivisible, §2.2, p. 164]. So $\dim G^0 = \dim G$, and $\dim G^{\mathrm{et}} = 0$, as the connected component of an étale group is trivial. Dimensions of a $p$-divisible group and its dual add up to the height [@tate1967pdivisible, §2.3, Proposition 3, p. 166]. By hypothesis $\dim (G^0)' = \mathrm{ht}(G^0) - \dim G^0 = 0$, so $V_p(G^0)$ has the single weight $1$, and $V_p(G^{\mathrm{et}})$ has the single weight $0$. By [Sen's inertia criterion](sen-inertia-criterion.md), an open subgroup of $I_K$ acts on $V_p(G^0)$ by $\chi$, and an open subgroup acts trivially on $V_p(G^{\mathrm{et}})$; their intersection serves as $J$.

So $0 \subseteq V_p(G^0) \subseteq V_pG$, with a zero piece omitted when $G^0$ or $G^{\mathrm{et}}$ is trivial, exhibits $V_pG$ as potentially ordinary with $K' = K$ and weights $1$ and $0$. $\square$

**Proof of part 5.** The $p$-divisible group $X(p)$ of an abelian scheme of relative dimension $n$ has height $2n$ and dimension $n$, and $X(p)^0$ is the formal completion of $X$ along its zero section [@tate1967pdivisible, §2.3, Examples, b), p. 166]. Its Tate module is the $p$-adic Tate module of the generic fibre [@tate1967pdivisible, §2.4, Examples, 2), p. 170], so $T(X(p)) = T_pA$ as a $\mathbb{Z}_p[G_{K'}]$-module. By hypothesis $\mathrm{ht}(X(p)^0) = n = \dim X(p)$, so part 4 over $K'$ gives a $G_{K'}$-stable filtration of $V_pA$ and an open subgroup of $I_{K'}$ acting on its pieces by $\chi$ and trivially. The heights of $X(p)^0$ and $X(p)^{\mathrm{et}}$ add up to $2n$, as the orders in the connected–étale sequence multiply [@tate1967pdivisible, §1.3, p. 160], so both have height $n$, and the two graded pieces of $V_pA$ have dimension $n$ [@tate1967pdivisible, §2.4, p. 169]. The filtration and $J$ for $K'$ show that $V_pA$ is potentially ordinary as a representation of $G_K$. $\square$

**Not covered.**

- *A weight that recurs.* Part 1 fails without the disjointness hypothesis: the extension of the trivial character by itself through $\log \chi$ is not Hodge–Tate ([Sen's operator](sen-operator-hodge-tate-criterion.md)). In the proof, the case $h'_j = h$ would need $H^1_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_K, C) = 0$, but this group is $1$-dimensional over $K$ [@brinonconrad2009, Theorem 2.2.7, p. 15]. So the method says nothing about a Hodge–Tate representation whose only stable filtrations with single-weight pieces repeat a weight in non-adjacent pieces. The smallest shape is dimension $3$, with pieces of weights $0, 1, 0$. Whether such representations are Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate is not decided here.
- *No single-weight filtration.* Representations with an irreducible piece of several weights, such as the example of dimension $2$ cited above, are outside the theorem.
