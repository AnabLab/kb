**Setting.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with algebraic closure $\bar k$, and let $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ be the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\bar k$. For a smooth, geometrically connected curve $Z$ over a finite extension $k_Z \subseteq \bar k$ of $k$, let $\Delta_Z$ be the kernel of $\Pi_Z = \pi_1(Z) \to G_{k_Z}$. A-qLT-type is defined in [Lubin–Tate characters and semi-absolute anabelian geometry](lubin-tate-criterion-for-semi-absolute-anabelian-geometry.md) [@mochizuki2012, Definition 3.1 (v), p. 36]. For a character $\rho$ of a profinite group with values in $F^\times$, where $F/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is finite, $V_\rho$ denotes $F$ with the group acting through $\rho$.

Curves over $\bar k$ are smooth and connected. A curve $Y$ *lies over* a curve $C$, written $Y \Rightarrow C$, if some connected finite étale cover of $Y$ admits a dominant morphism to $C$. For smooth projective curves this is the relation of Bogomolov and Tschinkel, which Poonen notes is reflexive and transitive [@poonen2005unramified, Definition 1.1 and the paragraph after it, p. 1]. A smooth projective curve over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ is *universal* if it lies over every smooth projective curve over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@bogomolovtschinkel2002unramified, Introduction, p. 1]. Let $C_6$ be the smooth projective curve of genus $2$ given by $y^2 = x^6 - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here by combining the cited results.

1. *Base change.* If a hyperbolic curve $X$ over $k$ is of A-qLT-type, then so is $X_{k'}$ for every finite extension $k'$ of $k$.
2. *Transfer.* Let $X$ and $C$ be hyperbolic curves over $k$ with $X_{\bar k} \Rightarrow C_{\bar k}$. If $C$ is of A-qLT-type, then so is $X$.
3. *One curve for all hyperelliptic curves.* Fix $p$, and suppose that $C_6$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is of A-qLT-type. Let $U$ be a hyperbolic curve over a finite extension $k$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ whose smooth compactification $X$ satisfies $X_{\bar k} \Rightarrow H$ for some hyperelliptic curve $H$ of genus at least $2$ over $\bar k$. Then $U$ is of A-qLT-type.
   - This applies when $X_{\bar k}$ is hyperelliptic, whatever its moduli. By Poonen, it also applies when $X$ has genus at least $2$ and a group $G$ of automorphisms of $X_{\bar k}$ with $X_{\bar k}/G$ of genus at most $2$ satisfies one of his conditions (1)–(4), for instance $G$ solvable, or $X_{\bar k}/G$ of genus $1$ or $2$ [@poonen2005unramified, Theorem 1.7 and Corollary 1.8, p. 2].
   - As $C_6$ is hyperelliptic, $C_6$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is of A-qLT-type exactly when every hyperelliptic curve over every finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is.
4. *Universality.* Let $X$ be a hyperbolic curve over $k$ such that $X_{\bar k} \Rightarrow C_{\bar k}$ for every smooth projective curve $C$ over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. Then $X$ is of A-qLT-type. In particular, if $C_6$ is universal, then for every $p$ every curve $U$ as in part 3 is of A-qLT-type.

**Remarks.**

- *Scope.* As far as the sources opened here show, no proper curve is known to be of A-qLT-type (see [the problem](a-qlt-problem-for-p-adic-hyperbolic-curves.md)), so parts 2 and 3 are reductions. Part 3 reaches proper curves with transcendental moduli, because Bogomolov and Tschinkel's result holds over every algebraically closed field of characteristic other than $2$ and $3$ [@bogomolovtschinkel2002unramified, Proposition 1.8, p. 3].
- *Tamagawa's criterion is a case of part 4.* By Belyi's theorem, every smooth projective curve over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ has an open subscheme that is a finite étale cover of the projective line minus three points [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1, p. 43]. So if a finite étale cover of $X$ maps dominantly to such a tripod over $k$, components of fibre products show that $X$ satisfies the hypothesis of part 4. The proof of part 4 is Tamagawa's derivation of his criterion from $(\ast^{\mathrm{CM}})$, with universality in place of Belyi maps [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1, p. 43].
- *Why $C_6$ is not settled.* The map $(x, y) \mapsto x^6$ from $C_6$ to $\mathbb{P}^1$ is the double cover $C_6 \to \mathbb{P}^1_x$, branched where $x^6 = 1$, followed by $x \mapsto x^6$, branched over $0$ and $\infty$, so it is unramified outside $\{0, 1, \infty\}$. Hence an open subscheme of $C_6$ satisfies Tamagawa's criterion. But $C_6$ is proper, so no finite étale cover of it maps dominantly to a tripod (see [the problem](a-qlt-problem-for-p-adic-hyperbolic-curves.md)).
- *Universality is open.* Bogomolov and Tschinkel ask whether some curve over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ is universal, and whether every curve of genus at least $2$ over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ is [@bogomolovtschinkel2002unramified, Questions 1.3 and 1.5, p. 2]. They prove that over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ with $p \ge 5$ every hyperelliptic curve of genus at least $2$ is universal [@bogomolovtschinkel2002unramified, Theorem 1.7, p. 3]. Their curve $C_0$, given by $\sqrt[6]{z(1-z)}$, is isomorphic to $C_6$ over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$: with $u = 2z - 1$ the equation $w^6 = z(1-z)$ becomes $4w^6 = 1 - u^2$, and $x = 4^{1/6}w$, $y = \sqrt{-1}\,u$ give $y^2 = x^6 - 1$. Poonen states their Proposition 1.8 for $C_6$ [@poonen2005unramified, Theorem 1.5, p. 2].

**Proof.**

*Step 1: a reformulation.* A hyperbolic curve $Z$ over $k$ is of A-qLT-type if and only if the following holds for every open subgroup $H \subseteq G_k$ and every character $\rho : H \to F^\times$ of qLT-type as in the definition. There are a finite extension $k'' \subseteq \bar k$ of $k$, a connected finite étale cover $Z'' \to Z_{k''}$ with $Z''$ geometrically connected over $k''$, and an open subgroup $J_0$ of $I_k \cap H \cap G_{k''}$, such that $V_\rho$ restricted to $J_0$ is isomorphic to a subquotient of the $G_{k''}$-module $\Delta_{Z''}^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ restricted to $J_0$.

- *Such data give the definition.* The subgroup $J = \Pi_{Z''} \times_{G_{k''}} J_0$ is open in the inverse image of $I_k \cap H$ in $\Pi_Z$. It satisfies $J \cap \Delta_Z = \Delta_{Z''}$, it maps onto $J_0$, and $J_0$ acts on $\Delta_{Z''}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ through the Galois action.
- *The definition gives such data.* Let $J$ be as in the definition, with image $J_G$ and $J_\Delta = J \cap \Delta_Z$.
  - $\Delta_Z$ is topologically finitely generated, so it has an open characteristic subgroup $V \subseteq J_\Delta$, which is normal in $\Pi_Z$. The centralizer of the finite normal subgroup $\Delta_Z/V$ of $\Pi_Z/V$ is open, and its inverse image normalizes $J_\Delta$. So the normalizer $N$ of $J_\Delta$ in $\Pi_Z$ is open, and it contains $J$.
  - The image of $N \cap \Delta_Z$ in $N/J_\Delta$ is finite, so some open normal subgroup of $N/J_\Delta$ meets it trivially. Its inverse image $W$ is open in $\Pi_Z$ with $W \cap \Delta_Z = J_\Delta$. So $W = \Pi_{Z''}$ for a connected finite étale cover $Z'' \to Z_{k''}$, where $k''$ is the fixed field of the image of $W$, and $\Delta_{Z''} = J_\Delta$.
  - Let $J_0$ be the image of $J \cap W$, which is open in $J_G$. Elements of $J \cap W$ act on $J_\Delta^{\mathrm{ab}}$ by conjugation inside $\Pi_{Z''}$, which is the Galois action on $\Delta_{Z''}^{\mathrm{ab}}$. So the subquotient for $J_G$ restricts to one for $J_0$.

*Step 2: part 1.* Let $H \subseteq G_{k'}$ be open and $\rho$ of qLT-type. Then $H$ is open in $G_k$ with the same fixed field, so Step 1 for $X$ gives $k''$, $Z''$ and $J_0$. Replace $k''$ by $k'k''$ and $Z''$ by its base change, which is still geometrically connected with the same geometric fundamental group. Since $J_0 \subseteq H \subseteq G_{k'}$ and $I_{k'} = I_k \cap G_{k'}$, these data satisfy Step 1 for $X_{k'}$.

*Step 3: dominant morphisms.* Let $f : Y \to C$ be a dominant morphism of smooth curves over a finite extension $k_1$ of $k$, both geometrically connected. Then $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p \to \Delta_C^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ is a surjection of $G_{k_1}$-modules.

- *Equivariance.* This holds because $f$ induces a map $\Pi_Y \to \Pi_C$ over $G_{k_1}$.
- *The image is open.* Let $n$ be the degree of $\bar k(Y)$ over $\bar k(C)$. Let $V \subseteq \Delta_C$ be open and normal, with Galois cover $C_V \to C_{\bar k}$. The connected components of $Y_{\bar k} \times_{C_{\bar k}} C_V$, which is finite étale over $Y_{\bar k}$, correspond to the orbits of $f(\Delta_Y)$ on $\Delta_C/V$. They also correspond to the factors of $\bar k(Y) \otimes_{\bar k(C)} \bar k(C_V)$. Each factor contains a copy of $\bar k(C_V)$, so it has degree at least $[\Delta_C : V]/n$ over $\bar k(Y)$, and as these degrees add up to $[\Delta_C : V]$ there are at most $n$ factors. So $f(\Delta_Y)V$ has index at most $n$ for every $V$. The image $f(\Delta_Y)$ is closed, so it has index at most $n$, and it is open.
- *Surjectivity.* For an open subgroup $U \subseteq \Delta_C$, restriction $H^1(\Delta_C, \mathbb{Q}_p) \to H^1(U, \mathbb{Q}_p)$ is injective, because corestriction after it is multiplication by $[\Delta_C : U]$. Dually, $U^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p \to \Delta_C^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ is onto. Apply this to $U = f(\Delta_Y)$.

*Step 4: part 2.* Let $H$ and $\rho$ be given. Step 1 for $C$ gives $k''$, a cover $C'' \to C_{k''}$ and $J_0$.

- Let $Y' \to X_{\bar k}$ be a connected finite étale cover with a dominant morphism $Y' \to C_{\bar k}$. Let $Y''$ be a connected component of $Y' \times_{C_{\bar k}} C''_{\bar k}$. It is a connected finite étale cover of $Y'$, hence of $X_{\bar k}$.
- A dominant morphism of smooth curves is flat, so the projection to $C''_{\bar k}$ is flat, hence open, and $Y'' \to C''_{\bar k}$ is dominant.
- Choose a finite extension $k''' \supseteq k''$ over which $Y''$ and its maps to $X$ and $C''$ are defined, with $Y''$ geometrically connected. By Step 3, $V_\rho$ restricted to $J_0 \cap G_{k'''}$ is a subquotient of $\Delta_{Y''}^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$. Step 1 for $X$ applies.

*Step 5: part 3.* Poonen's statement of Bogomolov and Tschinkel's result gives $H \Rightarrow (C_6)_{\bar k}$ for every hyperelliptic curve $H$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic other than $2$ and $3$ [@poonen2005unramified, Theorem 1.5, p. 2] [@bogomolovtschinkel2002unramified, Proposition 1.8, p. 3]. By transitivity $X_{\bar k} \Rightarrow (C_6)_{\bar k}$. Let $Z \to X_{\bar k}$ be a connected finite étale cover with a dominant morphism to $(C_6)_{\bar k}$. Its restriction over $U_{\bar k}$ is a dense open subscheme of $Z$, so it is a connected finite étale cover of $U_{\bar k}$ with a dominant morphism to $(C_6)_{\bar k}$. Thus $U_{\bar k} \Rightarrow (C_6)_{\bar k}$. By part 1, $C_6$ over $k$ is of A-qLT-type, and part 2 applies.

*Step 6: part 4.* Let $H$ and $\rho$ be given, and let $k_H$ be the fixed field of $H$.

- Tamagawa's statement $(\ast^{\mathrm{CM}})$, applied over $k_H$, gives an abelian variety $A$ with complex multiplication over a finite extension $k_A$ of $k_H$ and a character $\rho'$ of $G_{k_A}$, inertially equivalent to $\rho$ restricted to $G_{k_A}$, such that $V_{\rho'}$ is a subquotient of the $p$-adic Tate module $V_p(A)$ as a $G_{k_A}$-module [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1, p. 43]. Mochizuki verifies $(\ast^{\mathrm{CM}})$ in an appendix [@mochizuki2015, Appendix, pp. 1149–1153].
- $A$ is defined over a number field, as Mochizuki notes in deriving Tamagawa's criterion [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1, p. 43]. So there are a number field $M \subseteq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ and an abelian variety $A_0$ over $M$ with $A_0 \otimes \bar k \cong A \otimes \bar k$. There is a smooth projective, geometrically connected curve $C$ over $M$ whose Jacobian $J_C$ maps onto $A_0$ [@milne1986jacobian, Theorem 10.1, p. 33].
- By hypothesis there are a connected finite étale cover $Y' \to X_{\bar k}$ and a dominant morphism $Y' \to C_{\bar k}$. Choose a finite extension $k_2 \supseteq k_A M$ inside $\bar k$ over which these, an isomorphism $A_0 \otimes k_2 \cong A \otimes k_2$ and a point $P \in C(k_2)$ are defined, with $Y'$ geometrically connected.
- There are surjections of $G_{k_2}$-modules
  $$\Delta_{Y'}^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p \to \Delta_C^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p \cong V_p(J_C) \to V_p(A_0) \cong V_p(A).$$
  The first is Step 3. The isomorphism after it comes from the Abel–Jacobi map based at $P$ [@milne1986jacobian, Proposition 9.1, p. 30], together with the identification of the étale fundamental group of an abelian variety with its Tate module. The next map is onto because a surjective homomorphism of abelian varieties is onto on rational Tate modules. These last two are standard facts, not checked in a source here.
- So $V_{\rho'}$ restricted to $G_{k_2}$ is a subquotient of $\Delta_{Y'}^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$. On an open subgroup $J_0$ of $I_k \cap G_{k_2}$, the characters $\rho$ and $\rho'$ agree, so $V_\rho$ restricted to $J_0$ is a subquotient there too. Step 1 applies.

For the last sentence, base change from $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ to $\bar k$ preserves finite étale covers, dominant morphisms and connectedness. So if $C_6$ is universal, then $C_6$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ satisfies the hypothesis of part 4, and part 3 applies. $\square$
