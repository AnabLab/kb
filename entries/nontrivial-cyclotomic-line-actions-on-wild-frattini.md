**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd and $K/\mathbb Q_p$ finite of degree $d\ge3$. Write $G=G_K$, let $P$ be wild inertia, and put
$$\Gamma=G/P,\qquad p^s=|\mu_{p^\infty}(K^{\mathrm{tr}})|,\qquad
R=\mathbb Z/p^s,\qquad \Lambda_s=R[[\Gamma]].$$
Use the presentation and notation of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). Thus $G$ is generated by a copy of $\Gamma$ and $x_0,\ldots,x_d\in P$, with relator
$$A(x_0)x_1^{p^s}C=1,\qquad A(X)=(X^\sigma)^{-1}(X,\tau)^\kappa.$$
Here $C$ is the product of the ordinary commutator pairs when $d$ is even; for odd $d$ its first pair is $[x_1,y_1]$. Let $\omega_s:\Lambda_s\to R$ be cyclotomic evaluation and $\rho^*=\omega_s(\rho)\rho^{-1}$. Write $\omega$ for its reduction modulo $p$.

For $H$ pro-$p$ let $H_1=H$ and $H_{j+1}=H_j^{p^s}[H_j,H]$, with closed generated subgroups. Put
$$N=P/P_2,\qquad M=P_2/P_3,\qquad Q=P/P_3.$$
In particular $M$ is central in $Q$ and has exponent dividing $p^s$. The wild Frattini module is
$$V=P/\Phi(P)=L\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^d\Lambda\bar x_i,
\qquad L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0,\qquad\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]].$$

**Theorem (proved here).** Suppose $p\ge5$ and $\mu_p\not\subset K$. For every $c\in(\mathbb F_p^\times)^2$ with $c\ne1$, there is an automorphism $\alpha$ of $G$, of finite order dividing $p-1$, inducing the identity on $\Gamma$, such that
$$\alpha(v)=zv\quad(v\in V),\qquad
z\in Z(\Lambda)^\times,\qquad z^*z=c=\omega(z).$$
Consequently $\alpha|_L=c$, and $z$ is nonconstant. Finite outer conjugacy of $\alpha$ is not established.

**Multiplier lifting lemma (proved here).** Suppose $a\in Z(\Lambda_s)^\times$ and $b\in R^\times$ satisfy
$$a^*a=b,\qquad \omega_s(a)=b.\tag{1}$$
Then there is a tame-trivial automorphism of $G$ whose action modulo $P_2$ fixes the tame generators and sends $\tilde x_0$ to $b\tilde x_0$ and $\tilde x_i$ to $a\tilde x_i$ for $i\ge1$.

*Proof of the lemma.* First, the proof of the Frattini decomposition in the tame-group-algebra theorem works over $R$ as well. The free operator group has abelianization modulo $p^s$ freely generated over $\Lambda_s$ by the $x_i$; this follows by mapping to $R[\Gamma/U]^{d+1}\rtimes\Gamma$ for all open normal $U$, exactly as in that proof. Modulo the second term its relator is $u_s\tilde x_0$, where
$$\ker\omega_s=u_s\Lambda_s=\Lambda_su_s.$$
Taking the quotient therefore gives
$$N=R\tilde x_0\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^d\Lambda_s\tilde x_i,
\qquad\rho\tilde x_0=\omega_s(\rho)\tilde x_0.\tag{2}$$

We work in $Q$, rather than in the free operator group. The commutator map $\beta:N\times N\to M$ is alternating and $R$-bilinear. By (2), any two tame conjugates of $x_0$ commute in $Q$: their commutator is
$$\beta(\omega_s(\rho)\tilde x_0,\omega_s(\rho')\tilde x_0)=0.$$
Hence the closed $\Gamma$-stable subgroup $\mathcal B$ of $Q$ generated by $x_0$ is abelian. Let $B$ be an integer representing $b$. The map $h\rho\mapsto h^B\rho$ on $\mathcal B\rtimes\Gamma$ is an endomorphism fixing $\Gamma$. The word $A(x_0)$ lies in $\mathcal B$: the tame component of $(x_0,\tau)$ is $\tau^\pi=1$ in the notation of the presentation. Applying that endomorphism to this word gives
$$A(x_0^B)=A(x_0)^B\quad\text{in }Q.\tag{3}$$
The actual relator implies $A(x_0)\in M$, so the right side is $bA(x_0)$ in additive notation.

Choose $v_i\in P$ with $\tilde v_i=a\tilde x_i$. Send the tame generators to themselves, $x_0$ to $x_0^B$, and $x_i$ to $v_i$ for $i\ge1$, with values in $G/P_3$. The universal property of the free operator group gives a homomorphism before imposing the relator. We check its relator image in $M$.

The $p^s$-power map $\wp:N\to M$ is $\Lambda_s$-linear, because $p$ is odd. Thus
$$\wp(a\tilde x_1)-b\wp(\tilde x_1)
=(a-b)\wp(\tilde x_1)\in u_sM.$$
For each ordinary commutator pair, Lemma (c) of the tame-group-algebra theorem gives
$$\beta(a\tilde x_i,a\tilde x_j)
\equiv\beta(\tilde x_i,a^*a\tilde x_j)
=b\beta(\tilde x_i,\tilde x_j)\pmod{u_sM}.$$
For odd $d$, the word $y_1$ has class $\delta_s\tilde x_1$ in the free operator module, as in [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md). Its new class is $\delta_sa\tilde x_1$. Centrality of $a$ gives
$$\beta(a\tilde x_1,\delta_sa\tilde x_1)
\equiv\beta(\tilde x_1,a^*\delta_sa\tilde x_1)
=b\beta(\tilde x_1,\delta_s\tilde x_1)\pmod{u_sM}.$$
Together with (3), these identities show that the relator image differs from $b$ times its original, zero image by some $D\in u_sM$.

Choose $w\in M$ with $u_sw=-D$, and a representative $t\in P_2$. Change the image of $x_0$ to $x_0^Bt$. The Computation in the tame-group-algebra theorem changes $A$ by $u_sw$, so the relator now maps to one. This gives a homomorphism $G\to G/P_3$ preserving wild inertia, and hence an endomorphism of $G/P_3$.

It is surjective: it fixes the tame subgroup and its wild image is a closed $\Gamma$-stable subgroup surjecting onto wild Frattini, since $b$ and $a$ are units. The Frattini property makes that image all of $Q$. The group $G/P_3$ is topologically finitely generated, so its surjective endomorphism is an automorphism. [Wingberg's lifting theorem](wingberg-lifting-for-the-p-s-central-series-of-wild-inertia.md) lifts it to an automorphism of $G$, with agreement modulo $P_2$, proving the lemma. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** Let
$$T=\ker\bigl(\Gamma\longrightarrow\Gamma/I\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_p\bigr),$$
where $I$ is tame inertia. The group $T$ is normal and pro-prime-to-$p$: it is an extension of $I$ by $\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$. Since $\omega\ne1$ and $\Gamma/T\cong\mathbb Z_p$, the restriction $\omega|_T$ is nontrivial.

In $\Lambda_s$ define normalized averages
$$e_0=\int_T t\,dt,\qquad
e_\omega=\int_T\omega_s(t)^{-1}t\,dt.$$
Concretely these are the compatible averages over finite images of $T$, after taking open normal subgroups on which $\omega_s$ is trivial. Their orders are prime to $p$, so the denominators are units in $R$. Character orthogonality gives
$$e_0^2=e_0,\quad e_\omega^2=e_\omega,\quad e_0e_\omega=0.$$
For the last equality the character sum is zero over $R$: some character value differs from one by a unit, since $\omega|_T\ne1$. Both idempotents are central, because $T$ is normal and its character extends to $\Gamma$. Changing $t$ to $t^{-1}$ in the averages gives
$$e_0^*=e_\omega,\qquad e_\omega^*=e_0,\qquad
\omega_s(e_0)=0,\qquad\omega_s(e_\omega)=1.\tag{4}$$

Choose $h\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ with $h^2=c$, and let $\hat h\in R^\times$ be its lift with $\hat h^{p-1}=1$. This lift exists by successively solving $X^{p-1}=1$ modulo powers of $p$, whose derivative is a unit at $h$. Put
$$b=\hat h^2,\qquad
a=e_0+b e_\omega+\hat h(1-e_0-e_\omega).\tag{5}$$
Orthogonality shows that $a$ is a central unit and $a^{p-1}=1$. Equation (4) gives $a^*a=b$ and $\omega_s(a)=b$. Apply the multiplier lifting lemma.

Let $f$ be the resulting action on $G/P_2$. It fixes the tame subgroup and acts on its abelian wild kernel $N$ by multiplication by $a$, including the first summand of (2), on which $a$ evaluates to $b$. Thus $f^{p-1}=1$. It is nonidentity since its action on $L$ is $c\ne1$.

Take the inverse image of $\langle f\rangle$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. Its kernel is pro-$p$ by Nishio's Theorem 1.2 [@nishio2026finite, Theorem 1.2, p. 6]. The inverse-image splitting argument of his Corollary 1.4 supplies a subgroup mapping isomorphically onto $\langle f\rangle$ [@nishio2026finite, proof of Corollary 1.4, p. 7]. An element mapping to $f$ is the required $\alpha$. Its action on $V$ is multiplication by the reduction $z$ of $a$.

Finally $\operatorname{aug}(e_0)=1$ and $\operatorname{aug}(e_\omega)=0$, so $\operatorname{aug}(z)=1$ whereas $\omega(z)=c\ne1$. Therefore $z$ is nonconstant. $\square$

**Scope and checks.** This answers the existence question for nonidentity square multipliers in the final paragraph of [the unitarity theorem's sharpness discussion](unitarity-of-central-scalars-on-wild-frattini.md), for every $s$, including wildly ramified $K$. It does not classify nonsquare multipliers. At $p=3$ there is no nonidentity square in $\mathbb F_p^\times$; when $\mu_p\subset K$, the two idempotents coincide and this construction does not apply, consistently with that theorem's $c=1$ conclusion.

For a small check, take $p=5$, $K/\mathbb Q_5$ unramified of degree three, and $h=2$. In the tame inertia quotient $C_4$, order the characters as $1,\omega,\omega^2,\omega^3$. The eigenvalues of (5) are $1,4,2,2$; the twisted involution swaps the first two characters and the last two. Their products are $4$ in both pairs, and the cyclotomic value is $4$. This is a nonconstant central action with multiplier $-1$.

The essential change from the multiplier-one construction is (3). It uses the cyclotomic relation for $x_0$ in the actual third wild quotient; that relation is absent in the free operator group. The $p^s$-power term is checked over $R$ throughout. The lifting and finite-order inputs are used through the linked results; Nishio's pro-$p$ kernel and splitting proof were reopened at the cited locations. The multiplier lemma and the construction (5) are proved here. A targeted literature search found no statement of this construction, but was not exhaustive.
