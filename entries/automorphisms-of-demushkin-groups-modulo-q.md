**Setting.** Let $p$ be an odd prime, $q = p^m$ with $m \ge 1$, and $n = 2g \ge 2$. Let $F$ be the free pro-$p$ group on $x_1, \dots, x_n$. Following Serre, write $(x, y) = xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$ [@serre1963, Remarque a after Théorème 3.1, p. 147], and for $z = (z_1, \dots, z_n) \in F^n$ put
$$r_0(z) = z_1^q(z_1, z_2)(z_3, z_4) \cdots (z_{n-1}, z_n).$$
Let $r = r_0(x)$ and $D = F/(r)$, where $(r)$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by $r$. Then $D$ is a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $n$ with invariant $q$ [@serre1963, Lemme 6.1, p. 150], and every Demushkin group with $p$ odd and $q \ne 0$ is isomorphic to such a $D$, since then $q \ne 2$ [@serre1963, Théorème 3.1 and Remarque b, p. 147]; see the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md).

*Serre's filtration.* Put $F_1 = F$ and let $F_{h+1}$ be the closed subgroup generated by $F_h^q$ and the commutators $(a, b)$ with $a \in F$, $b \in F_h$. Let $\mathrm{gr}_h = F_h/F_{h+1}$. For $p$ odd and $q \ne 0$, Serre records the following [@serre1963, §6, p. 149].

- $F_h = \varepsilon^{-1}(1 + \mathfrak{m}^h)$, where $\varepsilon$ is the Magnus embedding of $F$ into the units of the ring $A$ of noncommutative power series in $t_1, \dots, t_n$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, $x_i \mapsto 1 + t_i$, and $\mathfrak{m}$ is the ideal generated by $q$ and the $t_i$. A series lies in $\mathfrak{m}^h$ exactly when its coefficient on each monomial of degree $k < h$ lies in $q^{h-k}\mathbb{Z}_p$, and only finitely many monomials have degree less than $h$; so the $1 + \mathfrak{m}^h$ are open and form a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$ in $1 + \mathfrak{m}$. As $\varepsilon$ is a continuous injection of a compact group, the $F_h$ are open normal subgroups of $F$ forming a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$.
- $\mathrm{gr}(F) = \bigoplus_h \mathrm{gr}_h$ is the free Lie algebra over $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}/q[\pi]$ on the classes $y_1, \dots, y_n$ of the $x_i$, graded by giving $\pi$ and the $y_i$ degree $1$. Multiplication by $\pi$ is induced by $z \mapsto z^q$, and the bracket by $(a, b)$. In particular $\mathrm{gr}_2$ has the $\mathbb{Z}/q$-basis $\pi y_i$ ($1 \le i \le n$) and $[y_k, y_l]$ ($k < l$), the classes of $x_i^q$ and $(x_k, x_l)$.

So $E = \mathrm{gr}_1 = F/F_2$ is free over $\mathbb{Z}/q$ with basis $y_1, \dots, y_n$. For $z, z' \in F$ with classes $\bar z, \bar z' \in E$, the class of $z^q \in F_2$ in $\mathrm{gr}_2$ is $\pi\bar z$, that of $(z, z')$ is $[\bar z, \bar z']$, and classes of products of elements of $F_2$ add. Hence for $z \in F^n$,
$$r_0(z) \in F_2 \quad\text{has class}\quad \pi\bar z_1 + \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^g [\bar z_{2i-1}, \bar z_{2i}] \in \mathrm{gr}_2. \tag{1}$$
Since $r \in F_2$, the preimage in $F$ of $D^q(D, D)$, the closed subgroup generated by $q$-th powers and commutators, is $F_2$; so $D/D^q(D, D) = E$. Likewise $D/\Phi(D) = E/pE$, where $\Phi$ is the Frattini subgroup.

*The group $H$.* Let $\Omega$ be the alternating $n \times n$ matrix with $\Omega_{2i-1, 2i} = 1$, $\Omega_{2i, 2i-1} = -1$ and all other entries $0$. For $R = \mathbb{Z}/p^k$ ($k \ge 1$), identify $R^n$ with $E \otimes R$ via the basis $e_i = y_i$, put $\langle v, w\rangle = v^{\mathsf T}\Omega w$, and let
$$H(R) = \{B \in \mathrm{GL}_n(R) : B\Omega B^{\mathsf T} = u\Omega \text{ and } Be_1 = ue_1 \text{ for some } u \in R^\times\}.$$
Inverting $B\Omega B^{\mathsf T} = u\Omega$ and using $\Omega^{-1} = -\Omega$ shows that it is equivalent to $\langle Bv, Bw\rangle = u\langle v, w\rangle$ for all $v, w$. So $H(R)$ consists of the symplectic similitudes whose multiplier $u = \mu(B)$ is also their eigenvalue on $e_1$. Let $W$ be the span of $e_3, \dots, e_n$; then $\langle e_1, e_2\rangle = 1$, and $e_1, e_2 \perp W$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p$ be odd and $D$ as above.

1. For every continuous automorphism $\alpha$ of $D$, the induced automorphism $\bar\alpha$ of $D/D^q(D, D) = E$ has matrix in $H(\mathbb{Z}/q)$.
2. Every $B \in H(\mathbb{Z}/q)$ is $\bar\alpha$ for some automorphism $\alpha$ of $D$. So $\alpha \mapsto \bar\alpha$ maps $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$, and also $\mathrm{Out}(D)$, onto $H(\mathbb{Z}/q)$.
3. The image $\bar G$ of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ in $\mathrm{GL}(D/\Phi(D)) = \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is $H(\mathbb{F}_p)$.
4. *Consequences.* (a) $\bar G$ preserves the line $\ell = \mathbb{F}_p\bar e_1$ and the hyperplane $\ell^\perp = \mathbb{F}_p\bar e_1 \oplus \bar W$, so $D/\Phi(D)$ is not an irreducible $\bar G$-module. The elements of $\bar G$ acting trivially on $\ell$ and on $\ell^\perp/\ell$ form a normal subgroup $U$ of order $p^{n-1}$. And $-I \notin \bar G$. (b) If an automorphism of $D$ acts on $D^{\mathrm{ab}}/T$, where $T$ is the torsion subgroup, as multiplication by $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, then $\lambda = 1$.

For example, $x_2 \mapsto x_2x_1$, fixing the other $x_i$, fixes $r$, since $(x_1, x_2x_1) = x_1x_2x_1x_1^{-1}x_1^{-1}x_2^{-1} = (x_1, x_2)$. It induces an automorphism of $D$, with inverse induced by $x_2 \mapsto x_2x_1^{-1}$, whose image in $\bar G$ is the nontrivial element $\bar e_2 \mapsto \bar e_2 + \bar e_1$ of $U$.

For $n = 2$, $H(\mathbb{Z}/q)$ consists of the matrices $\begin{pmatrix} u & \gamma \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ and has order $q\varphi(q)$. This agrees with [rank-two Demushkin groups](rank-two-demushkin-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md), where $\mathrm{Aut}(D) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ with $D$ corresponding to $p^m\mathbb{Z}_p \rtimes (1 + q\mathbb{Z}_p)$, so that $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ also has order $q \cdot (p-1)p^{m-1} = q\varphi(q)$.

*Corollary ($p$-adic fields).* Let $p$ be odd and $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of degree $d$ with $\zeta_p \in K$, and let $q$ be the largest power of $p$ with $\mu_q \subset K$. Then $G_K(p)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $d + 2$ with invariant $q$ ([maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md)), so parts 1–4 apply to $G_K(p)$, through an isomorphism with the group $D$ above for $n = d + 2$. For instance, $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K(p))$ acts on $H^1(G_K(p), \mathbb{Z}/p) = \mathrm{Hom}(G_K(p)/\Phi(G_K(p)), \mathbb{Z}/p)$ through the transposed inverses of the elements of $\bar G$, so this action is reducible and its image has a nontrivial normal $p$-subgroup.

**Why this matters for Tsujimura's question.** The positive answer for $q = 0$ in [pro-$p$ surface groups inside their automorphism groups](pro-p-surface-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md) uses three facts about $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$: its image modulo $p$ acts irreducibly and has no nontrivial normal $p$-subgroup (Lemma B, used in Steps 2, 4 and 5); for odd $p$ it contains $-I$ (Steps 3 and 4); and some automorphism acts on $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ as a scalar that is not a root of unity (Steps 5 and 6). By part 4, none of the three holds when $p$ is odd and $q \ne 0$, including for $G_K(p)$. So that proof does not extend as it stands; see [the problem](demushkin-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md).

**Lemma 1 (Serre's Lemme 6.3).** For $c = (c_1, \dots, c_n) \in \mathrm{gr}_{h-1}^n$ put
$$\bar d(c) = \pi c_1 + \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^g \big([c_{2i-1}, y_{2i}] + [y_{2i-1}, c_{2i}]\big) \in \mathrm{gr}_h.$$
For $h \ge 2$, $\bar d$ is surjective [@serre1963, Lemme 6.3, p. 150].

*Proof.* Serre states this and sketches why. Here is a proof. Let $L$ be the free Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Z}/q$ on $y_1, \dots, y_n$, graded by degree. Then $\Lambda \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}/q} L$ with the $\Lambda$-bilinear bracket has the universal property of the free Lie algebra over $\Lambda$, so it is $\mathrm{gr}(F)$, and $\mathrm{gr}_h = \bigoplus_{a=0}^{h-1}\pi^aL_{h-a}$. Also $L_k = \sum_j [y_j, L_{k-1}]$ for $k \ge 2$: the span of the brackets $[y_{j_1}, [y_{j_2}, [\dots, y_{j_k}]]]$ contains the $y_j$ and is closed under brackets, by induction on the length of the first factor using the Jacobi identity $[[y_j, s], t] = [y_j, [s, t]] - [s, [y_j, t]]$. Put
$$\delta(c) = \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^g \big([c_{2i-1}, y_{2i}] + [y_{2i-1}, c_{2i}]\big), \qquad c \in L_{k-1}^n.$$
For each $j$ and $s \in L_{k-1}$, $[y_j, s]$ is a value of $\delta$: take $c_{j+1} = s$ if $j$ is odd, and $c_{j-1} = -s$ if $j$ is even, all other $c$'s $0$. So $\delta$ maps onto $L_k$ for $k \ge 2$. If $k = 2$, this still holds when $c_1 \in L_1$ is prescribed: the remaining entries give every $[y_j, y_l]$ with $j$ odd, by $c_{j+1} = y_l$, and every $[y_l, y_j]$ with $j \ge 4$ even, by $c_{j-1} = y_l$; a bracket $[y_k, y_l]$ with $k \ne l$ is missed only if neither index is odd and neither is an even number at least $4$, which would force $k = l = 2$.

Now let $t = \sum_a \pi^a t_a \in \mathrm{gr}_h$ with $t_a \in L_{h-a}$, and seek $c_j = \sum_{a=0}^{h-2}\pi^a c_{j,a}$ with $c_{j,a} \in L_{h-1-a}$. The $\pi^a$-component of $\bar d(c)$ is $c_{1,a-1} + \delta(c_{\cdot,a})$ for $a \le h-2$, with $c_{1,-1} = 0$, and $c_{1,h-2}$ for $a = h-1$. For $a = 0, 1, \dots, h-3$ in turn, choose $c_{\cdot,a}$ with $\delta(c_{\cdot,a}) = t_a - c_{1,a-1}$, which is possible as $h - a \ge 3$. Finally choose $c_{\cdot,h-2} \in L_1^n$ with $c_{1,h-2} = t_{h-1}$ and $\delta(c_{\cdot,h-2}) = t_{h-2} - c_{1,h-3}$, which is possible by the case $k = 2$. Then $\bar d(c) = t$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (approximation).** Let $s \in F$, and let $z_1, \dots, z_n$ generate $F$ topologically with $s \equiv r_0(z) \pmod{F_3}$. Then there are $z'_1, \dots, z'_n$ generating $F$ topologically with $z'_i \equiv z_i \pmod{F_2}$ and $s = r_0(z')$.

*Proof.* This is Serre's successive approximation in the proof of his Théorème 5.1 [@serre1963, §6, pp. 150–151]; it starts from a congruence modulo $F_3$ and changes the generators only by elements of $F_2$. In detail: a family generates $F$ topologically if and only if every homomorphism $F \to \mathbb{Z}/p$ vanishing on it is zero [@serre1963, 2.1, p. 145], and such homomorphisms kill $F_2$, as $q$ is a power of $p$. So changing generators modulo $F_2$ gives generators again. So the classes of $n$ generators of $F$ generate $E$, hence form a basis of it, which freely generates the Lie algebra $\mathrm{gr}(F)$ over $\Lambda$, and Lemma 1 applies to it in place of the $y_i$.

Suppose $z^{(h)}$, with $h \ge 3$, generates $F$ and $s = r_0(z^{(h)})v$ with $v \in F_h$. For $c \in F_{h-1}^n$ let $x'_i = z^{(h)}_ic_i^{-1}$. Serre shows that $r_0(z^{(h)}) = r_0(x')\,d(c)$ with $d(c) \in F_h$, whose class in $\mathrm{gr}_h$ depends only on the classes of the $c_i$ in $\mathrm{gr}_{h-1}$ and is given by the formula for $\bar d$ in Lemma 1, with $y_j$ the class of $z^{(h)}_j$ [@serre1963, §6, p. 150]. By Lemma 1 choose $c$ with $\bar d$ equal to minus the class of $v$. Then $d(c)v \in F_{h+1}$, so $z^{(h+1)} = x'$ satisfies $s \equiv r_0(z^{(h+1)}) \pmod{F_{h+1}}$, generates $F$, and has $z^{(h+1)}_i \in z^{(h)}_iF_{h-1}$.

Starting from $z^{(3)} = z$, this gives sequences with $z^{(h')}_i \in z^{(h)}_iF_{h-1}$ for $h' \ge h$. As the $F_h$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$, each sequence converges, to some $z'_i$ lying in every coset $z^{(h)}_iF_{h-1}$, since these are closed. In particular $z'_i \equiv z_i \pmod{F_2}$, so the $z'_i$ generate $F$. By continuity $r_0(z') = \lim_h r_0(z^{(h)})$, and $r_0(z^{(h)})^{-1}s \in F_h$ tends to $1$, so $s = r_0(z')$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the group $H$).** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}/p^k$.

1. $B \mapsto (\mu(B), C, w_0, \gamma)$ is a bijection from $H(R)$ onto the set of tuples with $u \in R^\times$, $C \in \mathrm{GL}(W)$ satisfying $\langle Cw, Cw'\rangle = u\langle w, w'\rangle$ on $W$, $w_0 \in W$ and $\gamma \in R$. Here $Be_1 = ue_1$, $Be_2 = e_2 + w_0 + \gamma e_1$, and $Bw = Cw + \langle w_0, Cw\rangle e_1$ for $w \in W$.
2. Reduction $H(\mathbb{Z}/q) \to H(\mathbb{F}_p)$ is surjective.

*Proof.* 1. The coefficient of $e_2$ in a vector $v$ is $\langle e_1, v\rangle$. Let $B \in H(R)$ with $u = \mu(B)$. Then $\langle e_1, Bv\rangle = u^{-1}\langle Be_1, Bv\rangle = \langle e_1, v\rangle$. So $Be_2$ has $e_2$-coefficient $1$, and for $w \in W$, $Bw$ has $e_2$-coefficient $0$; write $Be_2 = e_2 + w_0 + \gamma e_1$ and $Bw = Cw + \lambda(w)e_1$ with $w_0, Cw \in W$. Then $\langle Cw, Cw'\rangle = \langle Bw, Bw'\rangle = u\langle w, w'\rangle$, since $e_1$ is orthogonal to $e_1$ and to $W$; this also shows that $C$ is injective on $W/pW$, hence invertible. And $0 = u\langle e_2, w\rangle = \langle Be_2, Bw\rangle = \lambda(w)\langle e_2, e_1\rangle + \langle w_0, Cw\rangle = -\lambda(w) + \langle w_0, Cw\rangle$. Conversely, for a tuple as in 1, the $B$ so defined is invertible, as it is triangular with respect to $Re_1 \subset Re_1 \oplus W \subset R^n$ with invertible diagonal parts, and the same computations show $\langle Bv, Bv'\rangle = u\langle v, v'\rangle$ on all pairs of basis vectors: the pairs $(e_1, e_2)$, $(e_1, w)$, $(e_2, w)$ and $(w, w')$ give $u$, $0$, $0$ and $u\langle w, w'\rangle$.

2. Here $W$ is taken over $\mathbb{Z}/q$. By 1, which is compatible with reduction, it suffices to lift $C \in \mathrm{GL}(W/pW)$ with multiplier $\bar u \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ to some $\tilde C \in \mathrm{GL}(W)$ with multiplier a lift $u$ of $\bar u$; the entries $w_0$ and $\gamma$ lift freely. Let $S_u$ act on $W$ by $e_{2i-1} \mapsto ue_{2i-1}$ and $e_{2i} \mapsto e_{2i}$ for $i \ge 2$; its multiplier is $u$. Then $A = CS_{\bar u}^{-1}$ has multiplier $1$, and if $\tilde A$ lifts $A$ with multiplier $1$, then $\tilde C = \tilde AS_u$ works. So it remains to lift $A$ to an element of multiplier $1$. The columns $\bar a_i = Ae_{2i+1}$, $\bar b_i = Ae_{2i+2}$ ($1 \le i \le g - 1$) satisfy $\langle \bar a_i, \bar b_j\rangle = \delta_{ij}$ and $\langle \bar a_i, \bar a_j\rangle = \langle \bar b_i, \bar b_j\rangle = 0$. Lift them arbitrarily to $a_i, b_i \in W$. For $i = 1, \dots, g-1$ in turn: replace $b_i$ by $\langle a_i, b_i\rangle^{-1}b_i$, which is allowed as $\langle a_i, b_i\rangle \equiv 1 \pmod p$; then replace each $v \in \{a_j, b_j : j > i\}$ by $v - \langle v, b_i\rangle a_i + \langle v, a_i\rangle b_i$, which is orthogonal to $a_i$ and $b_i$ because $\langle a_i, b_i\rangle = 1$. These changes do not alter reductions modulo $p$, since the pairings involved vanish there, and they preserve orthogonality to the pairs already treated. At the end the $a_i, b_i$ satisfy the same relations as their reductions, so the matrix with these columns lifts $A$ and has multiplier $1$. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.**

*Part 1.* Let $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, let $B \in \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z}/q)$ be the matrix of $\bar\alpha$, and choose $z_i \in F$ mapping to $\alpha$ of the image of $x_i$ in $D$, so that $\bar z_i = By_i$ in $E$. In $D$, $r_0(z)$ maps to $\alpha$ of the image of $r_0(x) = r$, which is $1$, so $r_0(z) \in (r)$. Since $(F_2, F) \subseteq F_3$, a conjugate of $r$ is congruent to $r$ modulo $F_3$, and $\mathrm{gr}_2$ is finite, so the image of $(r)$ in $\mathrm{gr}_2$ is the subgroup generated by the class $\bar r = \pi y_1 + \sum_i [y_{2i-1}, y_{2i}]$, namely $(\mathbb{Z}/q)\bar r$. By (1),
$$\pi By_1 + \textstyle\sum_i [By_{2i-1}, By_{2i}] = c\,\bar r \quad\text{for some } c \in \mathbb{Z}/q.$$
The bracket identifies the span $L_2$ of the $[y_k, y_l]$ with $\Lambda^2E$, and for any $B$,
$$\textstyle\sum_i [By_{2i-1}, By_{2i}] = \sum_{j < l}\Omega_{jl}\sum_{a, b}B_{aj}B_{bl}[y_a, y_b] = \sum_{a < b}\big(\sum_{j \ne l}B_{aj}\Omega_{jl}B_{bl}\big)[y_a, y_b] = \sum_{a < b}(B\Omega B^{\mathsf T})_{ab}[y_a, y_b], \tag{2}$$
using $\Omega_{lj} = -\Omega_{jl}$ and $\Omega_{jj} = 0$. As $\mathrm{gr}_2 = \pi E \oplus L_2$ with the $\pi y_i$ linearly independent, comparing components gives $By_1 = cy_1$ and $(B\Omega B^{\mathsf T})_{ab} = c\,\Omega_{ab}$ for $a < b$, hence $B\Omega B^{\mathsf T} = c\Omega$ since both sides are alternating. Finally $c$ is a unit, because $cy_1 = By_1$ is part of a basis of $E$ and so does not lie in $pE$. So $B \in H(\mathbb{Z}/q)$.

*Part 2.* Let $B \in H(\mathbb{Z}/q)$ with $\mu(B) = u$, choose $\tilde u \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ lifting $u$, and put $s = r^{\tilde u}$. Choose $z_i \in F$ with $\bar z_i = By_i$; they generate $F$ by the criterion recalled in the proof of Lemma 2, as $B$ is invertible modulo $p$. By (1) and (2), $r_0(z)$ has class $\pi uy_1 + u\sum_i [y_{2i-1}, y_{2i}] = u\bar r$, which is also the class of $s$. So $s \equiv r_0(z) \pmod{F_3}$, and Lemma 2 gives generators $z'_i \equiv z_i \pmod{F_2}$ with $r_0(z') = s$. Let $\phi : F \to F$ be the continuous homomorphism with $\phi(x_i) = z'_i$. It is surjective and $\phi(r) = s \in (r)$, so it maps $(r)$ into $(r)$ and induces a surjective continuous endomorphism $\alpha$ of $D$, with $\bar\alpha(y_i) = \bar z'_i = By_i$.

$\alpha$ is injective. For each $k$, $D$ has only finitely many open normal subgroups of index $k$, as each is the kernel of a surjection onto a group of order $k$ determined by the images of $n$ generators. Since $\alpha$ is surjective, $V \mapsto \alpha^{-1}(V)$ maps this finite set injectively into itself, hence onto itself, so every open normal subgroup contains $\ker\alpha$, and $\ker\alpha = 1$. A continuous bijection of profinite groups is an isomorphism, so $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$. Inner automorphisms act trivially on the abelian group $E$, so $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ also maps onto $H(\mathbb{Z}/q)$.

*Part 3.* Since $D/\Phi(D) = E/pE$, $\bar G$ is the reduction modulo $p$ of the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ in $\mathrm{GL}(E)$, which is $H(\mathbb{Z}/q)$ by parts 1 and 2. Its reduction is $H(\mathbb{F}_p)$ by Lemma 3 (2).

*Part 4.* (a) By Lemma 3 (1) over $\mathbb{F}_p$, every $B \in H(\mathbb{F}_p)$ maps $\bar e_1$ into $\ell$ and $\bar W$ into $\bar W \oplus \ell$. As $n \ge 2$, $\ell$ is a proper nonzero subspace. The action on $\ell$ and on $\ell^\perp/\ell \cong \bar W$ gives a homomorphism $B \mapsto (\mu(B), C)$, whose kernel $U$ corresponds to the tuples $(1, \mathrm{id}, w_0, \gamma)$; it has $p^{n-2} \cdot p$ elements. The matrix $-I$ has multiplier $1$ but eigenvalue $-1 \ne 1$ on $\bar e_1$, as $p$ is odd, so $-I \notin H(\mathbb{F}_p)$.

(b) Let $\chi : D \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ be the canonical character. For $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, pulling back cocycles along $\alpha$ identifies $H^1(D, I_j(\chi))$ with $H^1(D, I_j(\chi \circ \alpha))$ compatibly with reduction, so $\chi \circ \alpha$ has the property that characterizes $\chi$, and $\chi \circ \alpha = \chi$ by uniqueness [@labute1967, p. 106]. Since $q$ is the largest power of $p$ with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) \subseteq 1 + q\mathbb{Z}_p$ [@labute1967, p. 106] and $q \ne 0$, $\chi$ is nontrivial, and it takes values in $1 + q\mathbb{Z}_p$, a torsion-free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of rank $1$. So $\chi$ factors through a nonzero $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear map $\bar\chi : D^{\mathrm{ab}}/T \to 1 + q\mathbb{Z}_p$. If $\alpha$ acts on $D^{\mathrm{ab}}/T$ as $\lambda$, then $\bar\chi(v)^\lambda = \bar\chi(v)$ for all $v$; taking $v$ with $\bar\chi(v) \ne 1$ gives $\lambda = 1$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Scope.* The proof uses Serre's description of $\mathrm{gr}(F)$, which he gives for $p \ne 2$ and $q \ne 0$. Demushkin groups with $p = 2$ and $q \ne 0$ are not treated.
- *Sources.* The statement and proof are not taken from a source. No novelty is claimed; a short literature search did not find the statement.
