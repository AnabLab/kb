**Setting.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with valuation ring $\mathcal{O}_k$, valuation $v$ and residue field $F$. Let $X/k$ be a smooth, geometrically connected [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) with smooth compactification $\bar X$ and function field $K$. A *normal model* is an integral, normal scheme $\mathcal{X}$, proper and flat over $\mathcal{O}_k$, with generic fibre $\bar X$. Write $\mathcal{X}_F$ for its special fibre.

**Theorem.** Suppose that $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$ has a section, and let $\mathcal{X}$ be a normal model. Then $\mathcal{X}_F(F) \neq \emptyset$, or $\mathcal{X}_F$ has an irreducible component $Z$ such that $F$ is algebraically closed in the function field $\kappa(Z)$ of $Z$ with its reduced structure.

For rational points the first alternative always holds: by properness a $k$-rational point of $\bar X$ extends to an $\mathcal{O}_k$-point of $\mathcal{X}$, whose closed point lies in $\mathcal{X}_F(F)$. For proper $X$, the $p$-adic section conjecture therefore predicts $\mathcal{X}_F(F) \neq \emptyset$ whenever a section exists. The theorem is proved here from the [Pop–Stix valuative section theorem](valuative-section-theorem-for-p-adic-curves.md). Its two ingredients are stated by Pop and Stix, the residue field statement in [@popstix2017, §6.2, p. 21] and a version of the conclusion for stable models in [@popstix2017, Theorem 40, p. 24], but the theorem is not stated in this form in the sources opened here.

**Proof.** Let $s$ be a section. Notation is as in the valuative section theorem: $\tilde K$ is the function field of the universal pro-étale cover, so that $\pi_1(X) = \mathrm{Gal}(\tilde K/K)$, and the projection to $G_k$ is restriction to $\bar k \subseteq \tilde K$. The algebraic closure $\bar k$ lies in $\tilde K$ because $X \otimes_k k' \to X$ is a connected finite étale cover for every finite $k'/k$. By that theorem there is a valuation $\tilde w$ of $\tilde K$ prolonging $v$ with $s(G_k) \subseteq D_{\tilde w}$, the stabilizer of $\tilde w$ in $\pi_1(X)$ [@popstix2017, Theorem 26, p. 18]. Put $w = \tilde w|_K$, with valuation ring $R_w$ and residue field $\kappa(w) \subseteq \kappa(\tilde w)$.

*Step 1: $F$ is algebraically closed in $\kappa(w)$.* Since $k$ is complete, $v$ has a unique prolongation to $\bar k$, which is therefore $\tilde w|_{\bar k}$, and its residue field is an algebraic closure $\bar F$ of $F$. So $\bar F \subseteq \kappa(\tilde w)$, and every element of $\kappa(\tilde w)$ algebraic over $F$ lies in $\bar F$. Each $\sigma \in D_{\tilde w}$ preserves $R_{\tilde w}$ and fixes $K$, so it induces an automorphism $\bar\sigma$ of $\kappa(\tilde w)$ fixing $\kappa(w)$, and $\bar\sigma|_{\bar F}$ is the reduction of $\sigma|_{\bar k}$. For $\tau \in G_k$ we have $s(\tau)|_{\bar k} = \tau$, so $\overline{s(\tau)}$ acts on $\bar F$ through the image of $\tau$ under the reduction map $G_k \to G_F$, which is surjective. Now let $a \in \kappa(w)$ be algebraic over $F$. Then $a \in \bar F$, and $a$ is fixed by every $\overline{s(\tau)}$, hence by all of $G_F$. So $a \in F$.

*Step 2: the centre.* By the valuative criterion of properness, $\mathrm{Spec}\, K \to \mathcal{X}$ extends to $\mathrm{Spec}\, R_w \to \mathcal{X}$. Let $x$ be the image of the closed point. Since $w$ prolongs $v$, the maximal ideal of $R_w$ contains $p$, so $x \in \mathcal{X}_F$. The map $\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},x} \to R_w$ is a local inclusion inside $K$, so $\kappa(x) \subseteq \kappa(w)$.

*Step 3: the two cases.* $\mathcal{X}$ is integral of dimension $2$ and $\mathcal{X}_F$ is a proper closed subset, so $\dim \mathcal{X}_F \le 1$.

- If $x$ is closed in $\mathcal{X}_F$, then $\kappa(x)$ is finite over $F$, since $\mathcal{X}_F$ is of finite type over $F$. It lies in $\kappa(w)$, so $\kappa(x) = F$ by Step 1, and $x \in \mathcal{X}_F(F)$.
- Otherwise $x$ is the generic point of an irreducible component $Z$ of $\mathcal{X}_F$ of dimension $1$. Since $\dim \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},x} + \dim Z \le \dim \mathcal{X}$ and $x$ is not the generic point of $\mathcal{X}$, the ring $\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}$ is a normal noetherian local domain of dimension $1$, hence a discrete valuation ring of $K$. A valuation ring is maximal for domination among the local subrings of its fraction field: if $r \in R_w \setminus \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}$, then $r^{-1}$ lies in the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}$, hence in that of $R_w$, which is absurd. So $R_w = \mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},x}$ and $\kappa(w) = \kappa(Z)$, in which $F$ is algebraically closed by Step 1. $\square$

**Example over $\mathbb{Q}_2$.** Let $\mathbb{F}_4 = \mathbb{F}_2(\omega)$ with $\omega^2 = \omega + 1$. Put
$$A = z^2 + xy, \qquad B = x^2 + xy + y^2, \qquad N = A^2 + AB + B^2,$$
as forms with integer coefficients. For a quartic form $G \in \mathbb{Z}[x, y, z]$, let $X_G \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ be the plane quartic $N + 2G = 0$. All of the following is checked here.

1. *Some $X_G$ is smooth.* The singular plane quartics over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ are those in the image of the incidence variety $\{(H, P) : H \text{ and its partial derivatives vanish at } P\} \subset \mathbb{P}^{14} \times \mathbb{P}^2$. For fixed $P$ these are $3$ independent linear conditions on $H$, since $H(P) = 0$ follows from Euler's formula. For $P = (0:0:1)$ they say that the coefficients of $xz^3$, $yz^3$ and $z^4$ vanish, and $\mathrm{PGL}_3$ acts transitively on $\mathbb{P}^2$. So the incidence variety has dimension $11 + 2 = 13$, and its image is a proper Zariski-closed subset of $\mathbb{P}^{14}$ defined over $\mathbb{Q}$. It lies in the zero set of some nonzero polynomial $h$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ in the $15$ coefficients. Then $G \mapsto h(N + 2G)$ is a nonzero polynomial, since $G \mapsto N + 2G$ is an invertible affine map, so it does not vanish on all of $\mathbb{Z}^{15}$. Fix $G$ with $h(N + 2G) \neq 0$. Then $X = X_G$ is a smooth plane quartic, so it is a smooth projective geometrically connected curve of genus $3$.
2. *A normal model whose special fibre has no $\mathbb{F}_2$-point.* Let $\mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{Z}_2}$ be defined by $N + 2G$. Its special fibre is $Y = V(N) \subset \mathbb{P}^2_{\mathbb{F}_2}$. Over $\mathbb{F}_4$, $N = Q \cdot Q^\sigma$ with $Q = A + \omega B = \omega x^2 + \omega y^2 + z^2 + \omega^2 xy$, where $\sigma$ is the Frobenius of $\mathbb{F}_4$, because $(a + \omega b)(a + \omega^2 b) = a^2 + ab + b^2$. If $P \in \mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_2)$, then $N(P) = a^2 + ab + b^2$ with $a = A(P)$ and $b = B(P)$ in $\mathbb{F}_2$, which vanishes only if $a = b = 0$. But $B(P) = 0$ forces $P = (0:0:1)$, where $A = 1$. So $Y(\mathbb{F}_2) = \emptyset$.
3. *$\mathcal{X}$ is a normal model.* It is proper, and flat over $\mathbb{Z}_2$ because $N + 2G$ is not divisible by $2$. The conic $Q$ is smooth: in characteristic $2$, the conic $a_1x^2 + a_2y^2 + a_3z^2 + b_1 yz + b_2 xz + b_3 xy$ is smooth exactly when $a_1 b_1^2 + a_2 b_2^2 + a_3 b_3^2 + b_1 b_2 b_3 \neq 0$, because the partial derivatives vanish together only at $(b_1 : b_2 : b_3)$. For $Q$ this expression is $\omega^4 = \omega$, so $Q$ and $Q^\sigma$ are smooth, hence geometrically irreducible, conics. Neither is a scalar multiple of the other, or of a form over $\mathbb{F}_2$, since their coefficients of $x^2$ and $z^2$ are $\omega, 1$ and $\omega^2, 1$. So $N$ is squarefree, an $\mathbb{F}_2$-factor of $N$ would be $Q$ or $Q^\sigma$ up to a scalar, and $N$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{F}_2$. Hence $Y$ is integral. Since $\mathcal{X}$ is a hypersurface in a regular scheme, it is Cohen–Macaulay. Its points of codimension $1$ are the closed points of the smooth curve $X$ and the generic point $\eta$ of $Y$. Since $Y$ is reduced, $\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\eta}/(2) = \kappa(\eta)$, so the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X},\eta}$ is principal and this ring is a discrete valuation ring. By Serre's criterion, $\mathcal{X}$ is normal, and it is integral because it is flat with integral generic fibre $X$.
4. *$\mathbb{F}_2$ is not algebraically closed in $\kappa(Y)$.* The form $B$ does not vanish identically on $Y$, since $P_0 = (1 : 0 : \omega^2)$ satisfies $Q(P_0) = \omega + \omega^4 = 0$ and $B(P_0) = 1$. So $t = A/B$ is a rational function on $Y$, and $t^2 + t + 1 = N/B^2 = 0$ on $Y$. Hence $\mathbb{F}_2(t) \cong \mathbb{F}_4$ lies in $\kappa(Y)$.
5. *Conclusion.* $Y$ is the only component of $\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{F}_2}$. By the theorem, $\pi_1(X) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ has no section. Also $X(\mathbb{Q}_2) = \emptyset$, since a $\mathbb{Q}_2$-point would reduce to a point of $Y(\mathbb{F}_2)$.
6. *The index of $X$ is $2$.* At $P_0$ we have $Q^\sigma(P_0) = \omega^2 + \omega = 1 \neq 0$, and $\nabla N(P_0) = Q^\sigma(P_0)\nabla Q(P_0) \neq 0$ because $Q$ is smooth. So $\mathcal{X}$ is smooth over $\mathbb{Z}_2$ at $P_0$, and by Hensel's lemma $P_0$ lifts to a point of $X$ over the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$, a closed point of degree $2$. Conversely, let $k'/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite with residue field $\mathbb{F}_{2^f}$ and $f$ odd. Then $\omega \notin \mathbb{F}_{2^f}$, so $a^2 + ab + b^2$ is anisotropic over $\mathbb{F}_{2^f}$, and the argument of item 2 gives $Y(\mathbb{F}_{2^f}) = \emptyset$. So $X(k') = \emptyset$. Every closed point of $X$ thus has even residue degree, hence even degree, and the index of $X$ is $2$.

The index is a power of $2$, and so is the period, which divides it. So the condition in [Stix's period and index theorem](section-conjecture-period-index.md) that both be powers of $p$ gives no obstruction here. For $p = 2$ that theorem also gives equality of period and index under an extra hypothesis on $X$. The period of $X$ is not computed here, so whether that part applies is left open.
