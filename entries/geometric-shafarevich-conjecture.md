**Statement.** Let $p$ be a prime, let $k$ be a subfield of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$, such as a finite field, and let $F$ be a function field of one variable over $k$. Let $\tilde F = F\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ be the compositum in an algebraic closure of $F$, and let $F^{\mathrm{ab}}$ be the maximal abelian extension of $F$. Then $G_{\tilde F}$ and $G_{F^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ are free profinite groups of countably infinite rank [@harbater2009, Theorem 4.1 and its proof, p. 13].

The statement for $\tilde F$ is due to Harbater and to Pop, and Harbater deduces the one for $F^{\mathrm{ab}}$ from it [@harbater2009, §1, p. 1, and proof of Theorem 4.1]. Their 1995 papers (Contemp. Math. 186 and Invent. Math. 120) have not been opened for this entry. Adjoining all roots of unity to $F$ gives $\tilde F$, since the roots of unity in characteristic $p$ are exactly the nonzero elements of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$. So the case of $\tilde F$ is the function field case of the generalization of [Shafarevich's conjecture](shafarevich-conjecture.md) to the maximal cyclotomic extension of any global field, and the case of $F^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the function field case of its generalization to the maximal abelian extension. Harbater reports that both generalizations are open for number fields [@harbater2009, §1, p. 1].

**Proof.** This is Harbater's argument, with the abelianness of $\tilde F/F$ checked here. The field $\tilde F$ is the function field of a smooth projective curve over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$, so $G_{\tilde F}$ is free of countably infinite rank, by the theorem of Harbater and Pop [@harbater2009, proof of Theorem 4.1]. Restriction identifies $\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde F/F)$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p/F \cap \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p)$, a closed subgroup of the abelian group $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p) \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. So $\tilde F/F$ is abelian, and $F \subseteq \tilde F \subseteq F^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Then $F^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is an abelian extension of $\tilde F$, because $\mathrm{Gal}(F^{\mathrm{ab}}/\tilde F)$ is a subgroup of the abelian group $\mathrm{Gal}(F^{\mathrm{ab}}/F)$. Finally, if the absolute Galois group of a field is free of countably infinite rank, then so is that of every abelian extension of it [@harbater2009, Proposition 3.2(b)]. Harbater proves this by realizing a free profinite group of countably infinite rank as the absolute Galois group of a countable Hilbertian PAC field and applying a theorem of Pop [@harbater2009, proof of Proposition 3.2, p. 11].

**What the group remembers.** This is checked here. For every infinite cardinal $m$ there is a free profinite group of rank $m$, unique up to isomorphism [@harbater2009, §2, p. 3]. So let $F_1, F_2$ be function fields of one variable over subfields of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p_1}$ and $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p_2}$, where $p_1$ and $p_2$ may differ. Then
$$G_{F_1^{\mathrm{ab}}} \cong G_{F_2^{\mathrm{ab}}} \cong G_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p_1}(t)} \cong G_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p_2}(t)}.$$
These absolute Galois groups determine neither the field nor its characteristic. By contrast, [Uchida's theorem](uchida-global-function-fields.md) says that global function fields with isomorphic absolute Galois groups are isomorphic. So all that $G_F$ records about a global function field $F$ lies in the extension
$$1 \to G_{F^{\mathrm{ab}}} \to G_F \to G_F^{\mathrm{ab}} \to 1,$$
that is, in how the free group $G_{F^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ sits inside $G_F$, and none of it in $G_{F^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ as an abstract group. The same holds for $\tilde F$ in place of $F^{\mathrm{ab}}$. The analogous statement for curves over algebraically closed fields of any characteristic is in [function fields of curves carry no reconstruction data](function-fields-of-curves-have-free-galois-groups.md).

**Real curves.** Harbater also proves that the function field $K$ of a smooth projective curve $X$ over a real closed field $R$ has a free absolute Galois group if and only if $X(R) = \emptyset$, and that its rank is then the cardinality of $R$ [@harbater2009, Theorem 4.2]. An example is the fraction field of $\mathbb{R}[x, y]/(x^2 + y^2 + 1)$ [@harbater2009, Remark 4.3(a)]. By the uniqueness above, as checked here, all such $K$ over a given $R$ have isomorphic absolute Galois groups. For curves over $\mathbb{R}$, Harbater remarks, by a deformation argument that he sketches but does not state as a theorem, that $G_K$ depends only on the topological type of the real curve: its genus, the number of connected components of $X(\mathbb{R})$, and one further invariant taking the values $0$ and $1$. He asks for the structure of $G_K$ when $X(\mathbb{R}) \ne \emptyset$ [@harbater2009, Remark 4.3(c)].
