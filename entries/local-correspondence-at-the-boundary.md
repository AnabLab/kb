**Setting.** Let $K$ be a number field and $S$ a finite set of primes of $K$ containing the set $S_\infty$ of archimedean primes. Let $K_S/K$ be the maximal extension unramified outside $S$, and $G_S = G_{K,S} = \mathrm{Gal}(K_S/K)$, the étale fundamental group of $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{K,S}$ [@ivanov2015, §1, p. 1]. Write $S_f = S \setminus S_\infty$, whose primes are the boundary points of $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{K,S}$, and $S_p$ for the set of primes of $K$ above a rational prime $p$. Write $p \in \mathbb{N}(S)$ when $S_p \subseteq S$, that is, when $p$ is a unit in $\mathcal{O}_{K,S}$ [@ivanov2014, Notation, p. 3]. For a prime $\bar{\mathfrak{p}}$ of $K_S$, $D_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}} \subseteq G_S$ is its decomposition group, and $S(L)$ is the set of primes of a field $L \subseteq K_S$ above $S$.

**Statement.** In parts 1 and 2, assume that $\mathbb{N}(S)$ contains at least two primes, and let $p \in \mathbb{N}(S)$.

1. *Full local groups.* For every $q \in \mathbb{N}(S)$ and every prime $\bar{\mathfrak{p}}$ of $K_S$ above $q$, with $\mathfrak{p} = \bar{\mathfrak{p}}|_K$, the natural surjection from the absolute Galois group of $K_{\mathfrak{p}}$ onto $D_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}$ is injective [@ivanov2014, Corollary 2.6]. Ivanov deduces this from the case $K = \mathbb{Q}$, due to Chenevier and Clozel: if $T$ is a finite set of rational primes with at least two elements, then for every $p \in T$ the natural map $\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_p/\mathbb{Q}_p) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}, T \cup \{\infty\}}$ is injective [@chenevierclozel2009, Théorème 5.1]. Ivanov notes that the hypothesis does not by itself give this for all primes of $S$, but that it does for the primes in the largest subset of $S$ defined over a totally real subfield, by [@chenevierclozel2009, Remarque 5.3(i)] [@ivanov2014, §1, p. 2].
2. *Equivalent data.* Given $G_S$ and $p$, each of the following data can be deduced from each of the others [@ivanov2014, Theorem 1.1]:
   - (i) the embeddings $D_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}} \hookrightarrow G_S$ for the primes $\bar{\mathfrak{p}}$ of $K_S$ above $S_f$;
   - (ii) the $p$-cyclotomic character $\chi_p : U \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ on some open subgroup $U \subseteq G_S$;
   - (iii) for every open $U \subseteq G_S$ whose fixed field $L$ is totally imaginary, the $S$-class group $\mathrm{Cl}_S(L)$;
   - (iii)' for every such $U$, the order of $\mathrm{Cl}_S(L)/p$;
   - (iv) for every open $U \subseteq G_S$ with fixed field $L$, the number $\# S(L)$.

   If the decomposition groups at all primes of $S_f$ are full local groups, the full cyclotomic character on some open subgroup is a further equivalent datum [@ivanov2014, Theorem 1.1]. In particular, if $G_{K_1,S_1} \cong G_{K_2,S_2}$ and one of these data coincide for the two pairs, then so do the others [@ivanov2014, Notation, pp. 3–4]. For $p = 2$, Ivanov's proof uses a description of rank-$2$ Demushkin groups that needs a repair; see *The prime $2$* under Method.
3. *Local correspondence at the boundary.* For $i = 1, 2$, let $K_i, S_i$ be as above, with at least two primes in $\mathbb{N}(S_i)$, and let $p \in \mathbb{N}(S_1) \cap \mathbb{N}(S_2)$, with $p$-cyclotomic characters $\chi_{i,p}$. Let $\sigma : G_{K_1,S_1} \to G_{K_2,S_2}$ be an isomorphism with $\chi_{2,p} \circ \sigma = \chi_{1,p}$ on some open subgroup. Then for each prime $\bar{\mathfrak{p}}_1$ of $K_{1,S_1}$ above $S_{1,f}$ there is a unique prime $\sigma^*(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}_1)$ of $K_{2,S_2}$ above $S_{2,f}$ with $\sigma(D_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}_1}) = D_{\sigma^*(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}_1)}$. This gives an equivariant bijection, which induces compatible bijections between the primes above $S_{1,f}$ and $S_{2,f}$ in corresponding finite subextensions. If the decomposition groups at the primes of $S_{1,f}$ are full local groups, these bijections preserve the residue characteristic and the absolute degree of primes. If $p$ is odd, and for $i = 1, 2$ some open $U_i \subseteq G_{K_i,S_i}$ has finite second Tate–Shafarevich group with coefficients $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p(\chi)$ for every character $\chi : U_i \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ with torsion-free image, then the hypothesis on cyclotomic characters holds automatically [@ivanov2014, Corollary 1.4].
4. *Counting boundary points.* Let $p \in \mathbb{N}(S)$ be odd with $\mu_p \subset K$. Suppose that for every character $\chi : G_S \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ that is trivial modulo $p$, the second Tate–Shafarevich group of $G_S$ with coefficients $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p(\chi)$ is finite. Then each $H^2(G_S, \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p(\chi))$ has finite corank, and $\# S_f(K) = 1 + \max_\chi \operatorname{corank} H^2(G_S, \mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p(\chi))$ [@ivanov2014, Proposition 1.3]. Ivanov records partial evidence for the finiteness hypothesis, for the cyclotomic character and some of its tensor powers, and uses this proposition on small open subgroups to recover datum (iv) from $G_S$ and $p$ under the hypothesis [@ivanov2014, §1, p. 2].
5. *Global invariants.* If $S \supseteq S_p \cup S_\infty$, then $G_S$ and $p$ determine $[K:\mathbb{Q}]$, $r_1(K)$ and $r_2(K)$. If the Leopoldt conjecture holds for $K$ and all primes, then $G_S$ alone determines them and $\mathbb{N}(S)$ [@ivanov2014, Proposition 4.1].
6. *From $G_S$ and $p$, conditionally.* Let $S \supseteq S_p \cup S_\infty$, and assume the Greenberg conjecture, in the form Ivanov cites, for all finite subextensions of $K_S/K$ and the prime $p$. Then $G_S$ and $p$ determine the embeddings of the decomposition groups at the primes above $S_f \setminus S_p$ [@ivanov2015, Theorem 1.1]. If at least two primes lie in $\mathbb{N}(S)$, the Leopoldt conjecture holds for all these fields and all primes, and the Greenberg conjecture holds for all these fields and at least two primes in $\mathbb{N}(S)$, then $G_S$ alone determines the decomposition groups at all primes above $S_f$ [@ivanov2015, Corollary 1.2].

**Method.** This follows [@ivanov2014, §§2–3], described here for an odd prime $p \in \mathbb{N}(S)$. Ivanov describes Demushkin groups in §2.1 only for invariant $q \ne 2$ [@ivanov2014, §2.1, p. 4], and for $p = 2$ the structural claims in the first item fail for one group; see the last item. Parts 2 and 3 of the statement are as Ivanov states them, for every $p \in \mathbb{N}(S)$.

- *Subgroups of $p$-decomposition type.* A pro-$p$ group is of $p$-decomposition type if it is a non-abelian [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $2$ [@ivanov2014, Definition 2.1]. For odd $p$, such a group $H$ is $\mathbb{Z}_p \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_p$ with a faithful action, and it has a unique maximal closed normal procyclic subgroup $H_n$. The action of $H/H_n$ on $H_n$ gives a character $\chi_H : H \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ [@ivanov2014, §2.1 and Lemma 2.2(iii), p. 5]. If $\mathfrak{p} \in S_f \setminus S_p$, the $p$-Sylow subgroups of $D_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}$ are of $p$-decomposition type. The proof shows that tame ramification of every $p$-power degree at $\mathfrak{p}$ occurs in $K_S$, using Kummer extensions of a field that contains $\zeta_{p^n}$ and in which $\mathfrak{p}$ becomes principal [@ivanov2014, Lemma 2.3].
- *Criterion.* A closed subgroup $H \subseteq G_S$ of $p$-decomposition type lies in $D_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}$ for some prime $\bar{\mathfrak{p}}$ above $S_f \setminus S_p$ exactly when $\chi_p$ and $\chi_{H_0}$ agree on some open subgroup $H_0 \subseteq H$, and then $\bar{\mathfrak{p}}$ is unique. If $\mu_p \subset K$, this holds exactly when $H$ lies in the kernel of the reciprocity map $U \to \mathrm{Cl}_S(U)/p$ for every open $U \supseteq H$, where $\mathrm{Cl}_S(U)$ is the $S$-class group of the fixed field of $U$ [@ivanov2014, Proposition 3.5].
- *Separating primes.* Let $U \subseteq G_S$ be open and normal, with totally imaginary fixed field $L \supseteq \mu_p$, and small enough. Among the closed subgroups of $U$ of $p$-decomposition type that satisfy the criterion, the maximal ones are the $p$-Sylow subgroups of the decomposition groups in $\mathrm{Gal}(K_S/L)$ at primes above $S_f \setminus S_p$. Two of them, $H$ and $H'$, lie over the same prime of $L$ exactly when the restriction maps from $H^2(U, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ to $H^2(H, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ and to $H^2(H', \mathbb{Z}/p)$ have the same kernel [@ivanov2014, Lemma 3.9]. This recovers the primes above $S_f \setminus S_p$ with their $G_S$-action, and their decomposition groups as stabilizers [@ivanov2014, §3.4, pp. 14–15]. The decomposition groups at primes above $p$, which are full local groups by part 1, are recovered with the same criteria for subgroups isomorphic to the absolute Galois group of a local field of characteristic $0$ [@ivanov2014, Remark 3.6 and §3.4, p. 13].
- *The obstruction.* Neukirch's characterization of decomposition groups in $G_K$ rests on an argument with Brauer groups [@ivanov2014, §1, p. 1]; compare the local–global injectivity on $H^2$ in [Neukirch's local theory](neukirch-local-theory.md). For open $U \subseteq G_S$ with fixed field $L \supseteq \mu_p$, Poitou–Tate duality instead gives an exact sequence
  $$0 \to \mathrm{Cl}_S(L)/p \to H^2(U, \mathbb{Z}/p) \to \bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p} \in S(L)} H^2(D_{\mathfrak{p}}, \mathbb{Z}/p)$$
  [@ivanov2014, proof of Proposition 3.5, p. 12], whose first term need not vanish. Ivanov names the non-vanishing second Tate–Shafarevich group as the reason that Neukirch's argument does not carry over [@ivanov2014, §1, p. 1]. In the proof of the criterion, this kernel disappears in the limit over the open subgroups containing $H$, by the $S$-version of the principal ideal theorem [@ivanov2014, proof of Proposition 3.5, p. 12].
- *The prime $2$.* For $p = 2$ the class of Definition 2.1 also contains $H = \mathbb{Z}_2 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2$, where a generator $b$ of the second factor acts on a generator $a$ of the first by $bab^{-1} = a^{-1}$. This is the pro-$2$ group with the single relation $x_1^2(x_1, x_2)$, where $(x, y) = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$: the relation says $x_2^{-1}x_1x_2 = x_1^{-1}$, so $x_1$ generates a closed normal procyclic subgroup with quotient $\mathbb{Z}_2$ generated by $x_2$, and the map onto $H$ sending $x_1, x_2$ to $a, b$ is an isomorphism because $a$ has infinite order. The relation is the case $N = 1$, $f = \infty$ of the relations in [@labute1967, Theorem 1], a Demushkin group of rank $2$ with $q = 2$; see the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md). Its action is not faithful. Since $b^2$ is central, $\langle a, b^2 \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^2$ is an abelian open subgroup, so parts (i) and (ii) of [@ivanov2014, Lemma 2.2] fail for $H$. The normal procyclic subgroups generated by $a$ and by $b^2$ are distinct maximal ones, so $H_n$ and $\chi_H$ are not well defined. A step in the proof of that lemma that fails for $p = 2$ is that a homomorphism $\mathbb{Z}_p \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ has trivial image or is injective, since for $p = 2$ the image can be $\{\pm 1\}$ [@ivanov2014, proof of Lemma 2.2, p. 5]. The subgroups the method is after are not of this kind. Let $2 \in \mathbb{N}(S)$, and let $\mathfrak{p} \in S_f \setminus S_2$ have norm $N\mathfrak{p} = q_{\mathfrak{p}}$. Then a $2$-Sylow subgroup of $D_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}$ is isomorphic to one of the tame quotient of the absolute Galois group of $K_{\mathfrak{p}}$ [@ivanov2014, §2.2 and Lemma 2.3, p. 6]. The hypothesis $\mathfrak{p} \in S$ is needed: for a prime outside $S$, $K_S/K$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$, so $D_{\bar{\mathfrak{p}}}$ and its $2$-Sylow subgroups are procyclic. That is an extension of the $2$-Sylow subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_2$ of $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ by $\mathbb{Z}_2(1)$. In the tame quotient a Frobenius lift acts on tame inertia by raising to the power $q_{\mathfrak{p}}$, by the standard description of the tame Galois group, which this entry has not checked in a source. As $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ is pro-$2$, this action of $\hat{\mathbb{Z}}$ on $\mathbb{Z}_2(1)$ factors through $\hat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{Z}_2$, so a lift of the generator of the $2$-Sylow subgroup also acts by $q_{\mathfrak{p}}$. The odd integer $q_{\mathfrak{p}} \ge 3$ is not $\pm 1$, and the torsion of $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ is $\{\pm 1\}$, so $q_{\mathfrak{p}}$ has infinite order and the action is faithful. Excluding $H$ from Definition 2.1 is therefore a natural repair for $p = 2$, but this entry has not checked the rest of Ivanov's argument for $p = 2$ with that change.

**Context.**

- The proof of the theorem of Chenevier and Clozel uses proven cases of automorphic base change and results of Harris and Taylor, and needs the full group $G_{\mathbb{Q},S}$ rather than its maximal solvable quotient [@ivanov2014, §2.3, p. 7]. Chenevier and Clozel say that the case $T = \{p\}$ seems much more delicate, and that it is tempting to conjecture that their theorem holds there too [@chenevierclozel2009, Remarque 5.3(iii)].
- Ivanov writes that, in contrast with the function field case, the position of the decomposition groups at the boundary seems harder to describe intrinsically in terms of $G_S$ [@ivanov2014, Abstract]. He compares giving $G_S$ with the cyclotomic character to giving the fundamental group of a curve over a finite field together with its outer Galois representation [@ivanov2014, §1, p. 2].
- For infinite sets of primes, Shimizu bases his local correspondence on these results and on his recovery of the cyclotomic character [@shimizu2022, §0, p. 2]; see [restricted ramification](restricted-ramification-neukirch-uchida.md).
- For what is known away from the boundary, see [the problem for arithmetic curves](isom-problem-for-arithmetic-curves.md).
