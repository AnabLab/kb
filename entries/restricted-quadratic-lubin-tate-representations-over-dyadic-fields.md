**Setting.** Let $k/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite of degree $d$ inside $\bar k$, with $i = \sqrt{-1} \notin k$ and $k(i)/k$ unramified; then $d$ is even ([the unramified dyadic entry](unramified-dyadic-outer-automorphisms.md), first paragraph of the proof). Let $E \subseteq k$ be a quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$, say $E = \mathbb{Q}_2(\delta)$ with $\delta^2 \in \mathbb{Q}_2$. Fix a uniformizer $\pi_E$ of $E$, let $\chi_E : G_E^{\mathrm{ab}} \to \mathcal{O}_E^\times$ be the inverse of reciprocity followed by the projection killing $\pi_E$, and let $\rho_E : G_E \to \mathcal{O}_E^\times \subset \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_2}(E_+)$ be the resulting Lubin–Tate representation, $\chi_{\pi_E, \mathrm{id}}$ in Hoshi's notation [@hoshi2024, Definition 1.7, p. 5]. Put $\rho = \rho_E|_{G_k}$, a representation of $G_k$ on the $2$-dimensional space $E_+$. A representation of $G_k$ is *Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate* if its composite with every continuous automorphism of $G_k$ is Hodge–Tate [@hoshi2024, Definition 1.3, p. 3]. For $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ let $\theta_+$ be the induced $\mathbb{Q}_2$-linear automorphism of $k_+$ ([the overview](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md), Method of part 2). It depends only on the class of $\theta$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$, and $\theta \mapsto \theta_+$ is a homomorphism. Let $T = \mathrm{Tr}_{k/\mathbb{Q}_2}$ and $f_\delta(x) = \mathrm{Tr}_{k/\mathbb{Q}_2}(\delta x)$, elements of $k_+^* = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}_2}(k, \mathbb{Q}_2)$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. $\rho$ is irreducible, abelian and Hodge–Tate.
2. The classes $\alpha \in \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ for which $\rho \circ \tilde\alpha$ is Hodge–Tate, for a representative $\tilde\alpha$, lie in a closed subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ of Haar measure zero. In particular $\rho$ is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate.

**Corollary (dimension two).** Let $k/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite with $k(i)/k$ unramified, the case $i \in k$ included, and suppose that $k$ contains a quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$. Then $G_k$ has an irreducible abelian Hodge–Tate representation of dimension $2$ that is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate. For $i \notin k$ this is the theorem. For $i \in k$ it is the corollary of [the restricted Lubin–Tate entry](restricted-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md). This answers Hoshi's question, whether $G_k$ has a $2$-dimensional Hodge–Tate representation that is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate [@hoshi2024, Introduction, p. 2], for these fields.

*Example.* Let $k = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt3, \sqrt[3]{2})$. It has degree $6$, is not Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, does not contain $i$, and $k(i)/k$ is unramified ([the unramified dyadic entry](unramified-dyadic-outer-automorphisms.md), Examples). With $E = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt3)$, the theorem gives a $2$-dimensional example over $k$. When $k = E$ is quadratic, $\rho = \rho_k$, and part 2 is the case $d = 2$ of part 3 of the unramified dyadic entry.

**Notation from the unramified dyadic entry.** Diekert's presentation gives generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_d$ of $G_k$ with wild relation $\sigma x_0 \sigma^{-1} = (x_0\tau)^{\varepsilon g}x_1^{2^s}[x_1, x_2] \cdots [x_{d-1}, x_d]$, recorded in [generators and relations](local-galois-group-presentations.md). Let $y_j \in k_+$ be the image of $x_j$ under wild inertia $\to G_k^{\mathrm{ab}}$, inverse reciprocity and the logarithm. The unramified dyadic entry, in its proof, shows:

- *(a)* $y_1, \dots, y_d$ is a basis of $k_+$. Write $y_1^*, \dots, y_d^*$ for the dual basis.
- *(b)* Each of the following substitutions, fixing all other generators, defines an automorphism of $G_k$: $\varphi_0 : x_2 \mapsto x_2x_1$, and for $2 \le j \le d/2$, $\varphi_j' : x_{2j-1} \mapsto x_{2j-1}x_{2j}$ and $\varphi_j'' : x_{2j} \mapsto x_{2j}x_{2j-1}$. On abelianizations they add the class of one generator to that of another, so $(\varphi)_+ = 1 + N_\varphi$ with $N_{\varphi_0}(y_2) = y_1$, $N_{\varphi_j'}(y_{2j-1}) = y_{2j}$, $N_{\varphi_j''}(y_{2j}) = y_{2j-1}$, and $N_\varphi$ zero on the other basis vectors. Each $N_\varphi$ has $N_\varphi^2 = 0$.
- *(c)* $\ker T$ is spanned by the $y_j$ with $j \ne 2$. As $T \ne 0$, this says $T \in \mathbb{Q}_2 y_2^*$.
- *(d)* $\theta \mapsto \theta_+$ is continuous on $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$.

**Proof of part 1.** $\rho$ is abelian because $\rho_E$ factors through $G_E^{\mathrm{ab}}$. $\rho_E$ is Hodge–Tate by Hoshi's lemma for the Galois field $E$, with $\phi = \chi_{\pi_E, \mathrm{id}}$, exponents $i_{\mathrm{id}} = 1$ and $i_\iota = 0$, and $J$ the whole inertia group [@hoshi2024, Lemma 1.8, p. 5]; the lemma is stated for every finite extension of every $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Restriction to the open subgroup $G_k$ keeps it Hodge–Tate, since $B_{\mathrm{HT}}^{G_k} = k$ ([Tate–Sen invariants](ax-sen-tate-fixed-field-theorem.md)). Irreducibility is proved as in part 1 of [the restricted Lubin–Tate entry](restricted-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md): $\rho(G_k)$ contains the elements $u^{[k:E]}$, $u \in \mathcal{O}_E^\times$, whose $\mathbb{Q}_2$-span is $E$, so a $G_k$-stable subspace of $E_+$ is an $E$-subspace. That argument uses no hypothesis on $p$. $\square$

**A lemma on transvections.** *Let $f \in k_+^*$ with $f \notin \mathbb{Q}_2 y_2^*$. Then one of the automorphisms $\varphi$ in (b) satisfies $f \circ (\varphi^n)_+ \notin \mathbb{Q}_2 f$ for every integer $n \ne 0$.*

*Proof.* Some $m \ne 2$ has $f(y_m) \ne 0$. Take $\varphi = \varphi_0$ if $m = 1$, $\varphi = \varphi_j''$ if $m = 2j - 1 \ge 3$, and $\varphi = \varphi_j'$ if $m = 2j \ge 4$, and write $N = N_\varphi$. Then $f \circ N$ is $f(y_1)\,y_2^*$, $f(y_{2j-1})\,y_{2j}^*$ or $f(y_{2j})\,y_{2j-1}^*$ respectively, which is nonzero. Since $N^2 = 0$, $(\varphi^n)_+ = (1 + N)^n = 1 + nN$. Suppose $f + n\,f \circ N = \lambda f$ with $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}_2$. Composing on the right with $N$ gives $f \circ N = \lambda\, f \circ N$, so $\lambda = 1$, and then $n\, f \circ N = 0$, so $n = 0$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $B \subseteq \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ be the set of $\alpha$ with $f_\delta \circ \alpha_+ \in \mathbb{Q}_2 f_\delta$. It is a subgroup, since $\alpha \mapsto \alpha_+$ is a homomorphism and $\alpha_+$ is invertible. It is closed by (d), because the $\beta \in \mathrm{GL}(k_+)$ with $f_\delta \circ \beta \in \mathbb{Q}_2 f_\delta$ form a closed subset.

- *The Hodge–Tate classes lie in $B$.* If $\rho \circ \theta$ is Hodge–Tate, then $\theta_+$ maps $H_E = \{x \in k : \mathrm{Tr}_{k/E}(x) \in \mathbb{Q}_2\}$ onto itself, by part 2 of [the restricted Lubin–Tate entry](restricted-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md). Its proof shows that $H_E = \ker f_\delta$ and gives $f_\delta \circ \theta_+ = (q_1 - q_\iota)\,f_\delta$ with rational $q_1, q_\iota$. It uses Hoshi's lemma, norm compatibility of reciprocity, and the relation between $\theta^\times$, $\theta_+$ and reciprocity from [the non-Galois entry](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), none of which depends on $p$ or on a presentation of $G_k$.
- *$f_\delta$ is not a multiple of $T$.* If $f_\delta = cT$ with $c \in \mathbb{Q}_2$, then $\mathrm{Tr}_{k/\mathbb{Q}_2}((\delta - c)x) = 0$ for all $x \in k$, so $\delta = c$, because the trace form of the separable extension $k/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is nondegenerate. But $\delta \notin \mathbb{Q}_2$. By (c), $f_\delta \notin \mathbb{Q}_2 y_2^*$.
- *$B$ has infinite index.* The lemma gives an automorphism $\varphi$ with $f_\delta \circ (\varphi^n)_+ \notin \mathbb{Q}_2 f_\delta$, that is, $\bar\varphi^n \notin B$, for every $n \ne 0$. So the cosets $\bar\varphi^n B$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, are pairwise distinct: $\bar\varphi^m B = \bar\varphi^n B$ would put $\bar\varphi^{m-n}$ in $B$.

A closed subgroup of infinite index in the profinite group $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ has Haar measure zero, since any $N$ of its cosets have total measure $N\mu(B) \le 1$. Inner automorphisms change $\rho$ only up to isomorphism, so the condition in part 2 does not depend on the representative. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What is different from odd $p$.* The restricted Lubin–Tate entry proves its part 3 for $p$ odd or $i \in k$ using Kondo's $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ block, which it cites from Kondo only in that range. For a quadratic $E$ the block is not needed: the elementary transvections on each commutator pair, which the unramified dyadic entry already has, are enough. For $E$ Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of degree at least $3$ these transvections alone do not suffice, since a subspace such as $\mathbb{Q}_2 y_2^* + \mathbb{Q}_2 y_3^* + \mathbb{Q}_2 y_4^*$ is stable under all of them. That case is not treated here.
- *Not covered.* Dimension $2$ over dyadic $k$ with $k(i)/k$ ramified, which includes $\mathbb{Q}_2$ and every $k$ of odd degree, since $k(i)/k$ unramified forces even degree; for these fields the knowledgebase records no checked presentation of $G_k$. And dyadic $k$ with $k(i)/k$ unramified but no quadratic subfield.
