**Definitions.** Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field of characteristic $0$ with residue characteristic $p$, and $X$ a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $K$ with function field $K(X)$. A valuation $v$ of $K(X)$ is a *$p$-valuation* if $\mathcal{O}_v \cap K = \mathcal{O}_K$, and *residue-transcendental* if moreover its residue field is transcendental over that of $K$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Definition 2.2 (i), p. 30]. A *compactified model* of $X$ is a proper, flat, normal $\mathcal{O}_K$-scheme whose generic fibre is the smooth compactification of $X$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Definition 2.1 (i), p. 26]. Every discrete residue-transcendental $p$-valuation is the valuation of an irreducible component of the special fibre of some compactified model [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Proposition 2.3 (ii), p. 36]. Conversely, the valuation $\alpha$ of such a component $C$ is one. It is discrete because the model is normal, $\mathcal{O}_\alpha \cap K$ is a valuation ring of $K$ that contains $\mathcal{O}_K$ but not $\pi^{-1}$ for a uniformizer $\pi$, since $\pi$ vanishes on $C$, so it is $\mathcal{O}_K$, and the residue field of $\alpha$ is the function field of the curve $C$.

For a set of primes $\Sigma$, $X$ *satisfies $\Sigma$-RNS* if for every discrete residue-transcendental $p$-valuation $v$ of $K(X)$ there is a connected, geometrically pro-$\Sigma$, finite étale Galois cover $Y \to X$ such that $Y$ has stable reduction over its own base field and $v$ is the restriction of the valuation of an irreducible component of the special fibre of the stable model of $Y$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Definition 2.2 (vii), p. 33]. If $\Sigma$ contains a prime other than $p$, that component can be chosen with normalization of genus at least $2$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Remark 2.2.2, p. 34]. If $\Sigma$ has at least two elements and contains $p$, then $\Sigma$-RNS is equivalent to resolving every closed point $x$ of the special fibre of every compactified model $\mathcal{X}$: after a finite extension of $K$, some such cover has a compactified stable model that maps to $\mathcal{X}$ and has an irreducible component of genus at least $1$ mapping to $x$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, pp. 3–4]. The name refers to this: nonsingular points are modified into singular reduction [@tamagawa2004, §0, p. 1292].

**Theorem (Mochizuki–Tsujimura, preprint).** Let $\Sigma$ be a set of at least two primes, $K$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with $p \in \Sigma$, $X$ a hyperbolic curve over $K$, and $L$ a complete discretely valued field of characteristic $0$ and residue characteristic $p$ that contains $K$ as a topological subfield. Then $X_L$ satisfies $\Sigma$-RNS if and only if the residue field of $L$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Theorem 2.17, p. 71, and Introduction, Theorem A, p. 4].

The "only if" part is a short argument. If the residue field of $L$ is not algebraic over that of $K$, then blowing up a closed point of the special fibre that is not defined over an algebraic extension of the residue field of $K$ gives a valuation that no stable model of a cover can produce [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Remark 2.2.3 (iv), p. 35]. The proof of the theorem builds Artin–Schreier coverings of genus at least $1$ in special fibres from iterates of the $p$-th power map of $\mathbb{G}_m$, and extends them to coverings of an arbitrary hyperbolic curve using $p$-divisible groups [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, pp. 9–10].

**Earlier cases.**

- *Tamagawa.* Let $S$ be the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic whose residue field is algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and $(X^*, D)$ a generically smooth stable marked curve over $S$. For every closed point $x$ of $X^*$, after a finite extension of $S$, there is a generically tame stable covering of $(X^*, D)$ whose vertical components, those lying in positive-dimensional fibres of the covering map, have image containing $x$ [@tamagawa2004, Theorem 0.2 (v) and condition (III′), pp. 1293–1294, and the definition on p. 1301]. This concerns the closed points of the stable model, not those of every model [@lepage2013, Introduction, p. 1], [@popstix2017, Remark 15 (1), p. 12]. Tamagawa remarks that for elliptic modular curves, where congruence coverings resolve only the supersingular points, his theorem makes every closed point a "non-congruence supersingular point" [@tamagawa2004, Remark 0.3, p. 1294].
- *Lepage.* Every Mumford curve over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ satisfies resolution of nonsingularities, in the form that every point of type 2 of its Berkovich space is the image of a point over the generic point of a component of the stable reduction of a finite étale cover [@lepage2013, Definition 2.1, p. 6, and Theorem 2.6, p. 9]. So do the projective line minus three points and punctured Tate curves, which have finite étale covers that are open subsets of Mumford curves [@lepage2013, p. 9].
- *Origin.* Tamagawa credits Mochizuki with bringing the technique into anabelian geometry, to reduce the Grothendieck conjecture for proper hyperbolic curves over number fields to one for singular stable curves over finite fields [@tamagawa2004, §0, p. 1292].

**Consequence for the $p$-adic section conjecture.** This is checked here. Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with residue field $F$, and $X/k$ a proper hyperbolic curve. Pop and Stix call the valuation $\alpha$ of a component $Y_\alpha$ of the special fibre of a regular model *visible* if $Y_\alpha$ is covered by a component of positive genus in the reduction of a model of some finite étale cover of $X$. Pop asked whether every such $\alpha$ is visible, and Pop and Stix could not decide this [@popstix2017, Definition 13 and Question 14, p. 12]. The theorem answers this positively:

1. By the converse in the first paragraph, $\alpha$ is a discrete residue-transcendental $p$-valuation of $k(X)$.
2. By the theorem with $L = k$ and $\Sigma$ the set of all primes, together with Remark 2.2.2, there is a connected finite étale cover $Y_1 \to X$, with base field $k_1$, and a component $D_1$ of genus at least $2$ in the stable reduction of $Y_1$ whose valuation $\beta$ restricts to $\alpha$.
3. Let $\mathcal{X}$ be the model and $\mathcal{X}_1$ the normalization of $\mathcal{X}$ in the function field of $Y_1$. It is a proper flat model of $Y_1$, finite over $\mathcal{X}$ because $\mathcal{X}$ is excellent. The centre $z$ of $\beta$ on $\mathcal{X}_1$ lies over the generic point of $Y_\alpha$, so its residue field is finite over the function field of $Y_\alpha$. Hence $z$ is the generic point of a component $D'$ of the special fibre. The local ring of $\mathcal{X}_1$ at $z$ is then a discrete valuation ring dominated by $\mathcal{O}_\beta$, so it equals $\mathcal{O}_\beta$. Thus $D'$ is birational to $D_1$, has genus at least $2$, and maps onto $Y_\alpha$.
4. Resolving $\mathcal{X}_1$ to a regular model with strict normal crossings keeps the strict transform of $D'$, so $\alpha$ is visible.

This is the implication (b) $\Rightarrow$ (a) of [@popstix2017, Lemma 16, p. 12] in a direct form. So there are no invisible components, hence no bridge elements, which are invisible by definition [@popstix2017, Definition 34, p. 22]. Pop and Stix had noted that an unproved stronger form of Tamagawa's theorem would give exactly this [@popstix2017, Remark 35 (2), p. 22]. Every connected finite étale cover of $X$ is again a proper hyperbolic curve over a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, so the hypothesis of their Corollary 41 holds for every such $X$. Hence for every section $s$ of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$, either a unique valuation $\tilde w$ of the function field of the universal cover, prolonging the valuation of $k$, has $s(G_k)$ in its decomposition group, or a unique $\tilde\alpha$ of type 1v has a refinement $\tilde w$ of type 2v with $s(G_k) \subseteq D_{\tilde w} \subseteq D_{\tilde\alpha}$ [@popstix2017, Corollary 41, p. 25]; see [the valuative section theorem](valuative-section-theorem-for-p-adic-curves.md). Lepage had pointed out that resolution of nonsingularities would give this through Corollary 41 [@lepage2013, Introduction, p. 1]. The worry that invisible projective lines might carry non-geometric sections [@popstix2017, §7.3, p. 25] is therefore moot, but the $p$-adic section conjecture remains open as far as these sources show. For the fixed points of sections on Berkovich spaces, see [Berkovich points fixed by sections](berkovich-points-fixed-by-p-adic-sections.md).

**Other applications.** With combinatorial anabelian geometry, Mochizuki and Tsujimura use the theorem to reconstruct the set of $\mathbb{C}_p$-points of a hyperbolic curve over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ from its geometric tempered fundamental group, and the decomposition groups of closed points of a hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field from its geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental group. They call the latter "a sort of a weak version of the Section Conjecture" [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, Theorem C and pp. 6–7]. See [the reconstruction of points from tempered fundamental groups](points-of-p-adic-curves-from-tempered-fundamental-groups.md). This yields the [absolute Grothendieck conjecture over $p$-adic fields](absolute-grothendieck-conjecture-for-p-adic-curves.md) [@mochizukitsujimura2024, proof of Theorem 3.12, p. 91]. Lepage's case for the projective line minus three points also enters their proof that the $p$-adic versions of the [Grothendieck–Teichmüller group](grothendieck-teichmuller-group.md) in the literature coincide with the metrized one [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, Theorem G and the paragraph before it, pp. 8–9].

**Sources.** The Mochizuki–Tsujimura paper is a preprint, cited in its version of 30 March 2024. Lepage is cited by the arXiv version, where his main theorem is Theorem 2.6; Mochizuki and Tsujimura cite it as Theorem 2.7 of the journal version.
