**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $k/\mathbb Q_p$ finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_k$, and $P$ its wild inertia. Let $\varepsilon:G\to\{\pm1\}$ cut out the unramified quadratic extension of $k$, and let $\omega:G\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ be the action on $\mu_p$. Identify $\{\pm1\}$ with its image in $\mathbb F_p^\times$, and assume $\varepsilon\ne\omega$; equivalently, $k(\mu_p)$ is not the unramified quadratic extension of $k$. Put
$$E_\varepsilon=\{\phi:P\to\mathbb Q_p\text{ continuous homomorphisms}:\phi(gvg^{-1})=\varepsilon(g)\phi(v)\text{ for }g\in G,v\in P\}.$$

**Theorem (proved here).** Precomposition defines a continuous right action of $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ on $\mathbb P(E_\varepsilon)$. Every point has infinite orbit. Consequently, for each nonzero $\phi\in E_\varepsilon$, the subgroup
$$S_\phi=\{[\theta]\in\operatorname{Out}(G):\phi\circ\theta\in\mathbb Q_p^\times\phi\}$$
is closed and has Haar measure zero.

**Corollary (proved here).** Suppose additionally that $k$ has odd residue degree. Let $W$ be the two-dimensional Hodge–Tate representation of $G_{\mathbb Q_p}$ obtained by extending the Lubin–Tate representation of the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, and put $\rho=W|_{G_k}$, as in [restricted two-dimensional representations](restricted-two-dimensional-representations-of-g-qp-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md). Then $\rho$ is irreducible, and
$$\{[\theta]\in\operatorname{Out}(G_k):\rho\circ\theta\text{ is Hodge–Tate}\}$$
is contained in a closed subgroup of Haar measure zero. This strengthens the existence of one automorphism destroying Hodge–Tate-ness in part 3 of that entry.

**Proof.** Use the generators and notation of [the mixing entry](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md): $\mathcal G=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle$ maps isomorphically onto $G/P$, and $P$ is topologically normally generated by $x_0,\ldots,x_d$. The surface pairs are $(x_2,x_3),\ldots,(x_{d-1},x_d)$ for odd $d$ and $(x_3,x_4),\ldots,(x_{d-1},x_d)$ for even $d$. Write $g$ for the number of pairs; $g\ge1$ in both cases.

*Step 1: topology and outer action.* Evaluation at $x_0,\ldots,x_d$ embeds $E_\varepsilon$ linearly in $\mathbb Q_p^{d+1}$: equivariance determines every value on conjugates of these generators, and continuity determines the values on their closed generated subgroup $P$. Give $E_\varepsilon$ its resulting finite-dimensional vector-space topology. For a fixed $\phi$, the map $\theta\mapsto\phi\circ\theta$ is continuous, because each coordinate is $\phi(\theta(x_i))$ and evaluation is continuous in the natural profinite topology on $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. This also gives a continuous linear action: evaluate on a basis of $E_\varepsilon$ to obtain continuity of its matrix coefficients.

Every automorphism preserves $P$ and inertia. It therefore preserves the unique quotient of order two of $G/I_k$, so $\varepsilon\circ\theta=\varepsilon$ and precomposition preserves $E_\varepsilon$. An inner automorphism given by $g\in G$ acts on $E_\varepsilon$ as the scalar $\varepsilon(g)$, so the projective action descends to $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. Both automorphism groups carry their natural profinite topologies [@kondo2025, discussion preceding Lemma 2.13, p. 17]. The stabilizer of a point in the Hausdorff space $\mathbb P(E_\varepsilon)$ is closed.

*Step 2: a finite orbit would vanish on the surface generators.* Suppose that $\mathbb Q_p\phi$ has finite orbit, and let $f$ be the row of values of $\phi$ on the $2g$ surface generators. If $f\ne0$, choose a vector $v$ of their standard integral basis with $f(v)\ne0$. For the standard nondegenerate alternating form $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$, put $\ell_v=\langle\,\cdot\,,v\rangle$. The transvection $T_v=1+v\ell_v$ is integral symplectic and $T_v^n=1+n v\ell_v$. The forms $f$ and $\ell_v$ are independent: evaluating a proposed proportionality at $v$ would contradict $f(v)\ne0$ and $\ell_v(v)=0$.

The exact surface-word automorphisms of the linked entries realize every integral symplectic matrix, fixing the tame and complementary generators; these are the automorphisms constructed in [@kondo2025, discussion after Lemma 2.15, p. 19, and Theorem 2.17, p. 20]. Choose one inducing $T_v$. On the surface values its $n$-th power acts as
$$f\longmapsto f+n f(v)\ell_v.$$
Independence shows that these rows have pairwise distinct projective classes for distinct integers $n$. This contradicts the finite orbit. Thus $\phi$ vanishes on every surface generator. The same conclusion holds for every member of its finite projective orbit.

*Step 3: mixing contradicts this vanishing.* The nonzero compact image $\phi(P)$ is $p^m\mathbb Z_p$ for some integer $m$; scale $\phi$ so that $\phi(P)=\mathbb Z_p$. Its reduction $\bar\phi:P/P^{(2)}\to\mathbb F_p$ is nonzero and $\varepsilon$-equivariant, where $P^{(2)}=P^p[P,P]$ is closed. Write $\phi_i=\bar\phi(\bar x_i)$. The class $\bar x_0$ has cyclotomic character $\omega$ in the mixing entry's Setting, so $\phi_0=0$ since $\omega\ne\varepsilon$. Step 2 kills all surface coordinates, and normal generation then forces a nonzero complementary coordinate among $\phi_1$ (odd degree) or $\phi_1,\phi_2$ (even degree).

For odd $d$, take the mixing automorphism of part 3 there with $\nu=\sigma-\omega(\sigma)$. Its formula gives
$$\bar\phi\big(\overline{\theta(x_3)}\big)=-\varepsilon(\nu^*)\varepsilon(\delta_0)\phi_1\ne0.$$
Here $\delta_0$ is the skew coefficient of part 1 there, which proves $\varepsilon(\delta_0)\ne0$, and $\varepsilon(\nu^*)=-2\omega(\sigma)\ne0$ as calculated in its proof of part 4. Hence $\phi\circ\theta$ is nonzero on the surface generator $x_3$, contradicting Step 2.

For even $d$, if $\phi_2=0$, the exact automorphism $T_1:x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$ replaces it by $\phi_2+\phi_1\ne0$, while keeping all surface values zero. Thus choose a representative in the same finite orbit with $\phi_2\ne0$. Part 2 of the mixing entry, with $\lambda=\sigma-\omega(\sigma)$, gives
$$\bar\phi\big(\overline{\theta(x_4)}\big)=-\varepsilon(\lambda^*)\phi_2=2\omega(\sigma)\phi_2\ne0,$$
again contradicting Step 2. These formulas use only the prescribed action modulo $P^{(2)}$; no control of a lift at a deeper level is required.

Every projective orbit is therefore infinite. Since $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ is profinite, a closed stabilizer of infinite index has Haar measure zero: its distinct cosets have equal measure, and there are arbitrarily many of them. This proves the Theorem.

For the Corollary, parts 1–2 of [restricted two-dimensional representations](restricted-two-dimensional-representations-of-g-qp-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md) prove irreducibility and provide a surjective $\Phi:P\to\mathbb Z_p$ in $E_\varepsilon$ such that Hodge–Tate-ness of $\rho\circ\theta$ forces $\Phi\circ\theta=c\Phi$ for $c\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$. Thus the exceptional set lies in $S_\Phi$, which the Theorem makes closed and Haar-null. $\square$

**Scope.** The hypotheses $p$ odd, $d\ge3$ and $\omega\ne\varepsilon$ are used in Step 3. This proves no statement for $\mathbb Q_3$ in dimension two, or for dyadic fields. The infinite-orbit and measure-zero assertions are deductions here from entries already on main; they are not asserted in Kondo's sources.
