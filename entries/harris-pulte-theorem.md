**Setting.** Let $K$ be a subfield of $\mathbb{C}$, let $C$ be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over $K$ of genus $g \ge 3$, let $\xi \in C(K)$, and let $\ell$ be a prime. The map $\sigma_\xi : C \to \mathrm{Jac}\, C$, $z \mapsto [z - \xi]$, is an embedding. Put $C_\xi = (\sigma_\xi)_* C$ and $C_\xi^- = i_* C_\xi$, where $i$ is the inversion of $\mathrm{Jac}\, C$. Two homologically trivial cycles enter: the $0$-cycle $(2g-2)\xi - K_C$ on $C$, with $K_C$ a canonical divisor, and the $1$-cycle $C_\xi - C_\xi^-$ on $\mathrm{Jac}\, C$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, §1, p. 1]. The second is homologically trivial because $i$ multiplies it by $-1$ but acts trivially on the cohomology group containing its class [@hainmatsumoto2005, §4.3, p. 10].

Put $H = H^1_{\text{ét}}(C_{\bar K}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(1))$ and $L = (\Lambda^3 H)(-1)$, both of weight $-1$. The $\ell$-adic Abel–Jacobi map gives classes
$$\kappa(C, \xi) \in H^1(G_K, H), \qquad \mu(C, \xi) \in H^1(G_K, H^{2g-3}_{\text{ét}}(\mathrm{Jac}\, C_{\bar K}, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(g-1))) = H^1(G_K, L)$$
of $(2g-2)\xi - K_C$ and of $C_\xi - C_\xi^-$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, §1, pp. 1–2, and §4.2, p. 9]. Wedging with the polarization embeds $H$ in $L$, and the image $\nu(C)$ of $\mu(C, \xi)$ in $H^1(G_K, L/H)$ does not depend on $\xi$. It is defined even when $C(K) = \emptyset$, as a pullback of a universal class on the moduli stack $\mathcal{M}_g$ along the classifying map [@hainmatsumoto2005, §1, p. 2; Proposition 4.7, p. 12; §10.4, p. 33].

**The class of the Galois action.** Let $\pi$ be the pro-$\ell$ completion of $\pi_1(C^{\mathrm{an}}, \xi^{\mathrm{an}})$, on which $G_K$ acts through $\rho_{C,\xi} : G_K \to \mathrm{Aut}\, \pi$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, §3.1, p. 6]. Let $\pi = L^1\pi \supseteq L^2\pi \supseteq \cdots$ be its lower central series, and $T$ the kernel of $\mathrm{Aut}\, \pi \to \mathrm{Aut}\, H_1(\pi)$.

- An element $\phi \in T$ gives the homomorphism $u \mapsto \phi(\tilde u)\tilde u^{-1} \bmod L^3\pi$ from $H_1(\pi)$ to $\mathrm{Gr}^2_L \pi$, where $\tilde u$ lifts $u$. By Labute, $\mathrm{Gr}^2_L \pi \cong \Lambda^2 H_1(\pi)/q$, with $q$ the symplectic element [@hainmatsumoto2005, §§5.1–5.2, pp. 13–14].
- Johnson's lemma, which Hain and Matsumoto prove in this pro-$\ell$ setting, says that the resulting map lands in $\Lambda^3 H(-1) = L$. This gives the Johnson homomorphism $\tau : H_1(T) \to L$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, Lemma 5.2, p. 15].
- There is a unique class $\hat m \in H^1(\mathrm{Aut}\, \pi, L)$ whose image in $H^0(\mathrm{GSp}(H), \mathrm{Hom}(H_1(T), L))$ is $2\tau$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, Proposition 5.5, p. 17]. Its pullback along $\rho_{C,\xi}$ is the class $m(C, \xi) \in H^1(G_K, L)$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, Definition 5.7, p. 17].

**Theorem (Hain–Matsumoto, the $\ell$-adic Harris–Pulte theorem).** If $g \ge 3$, then $\mu(C, \xi) = m(C, \xi)$ in $H^1(G_K, L)$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, Theorem 3, p. 4].

So a class read off from the Galois action on $\pi$ is the $\ell$-adic Abel–Jacobi class of $C_\xi - C_\xi^-$. The proof establishes the identity for the universal pointed curve over $\mathcal{M}_g^1$, using topological computations with the mapping class group, and specializes along the classifying map of $(C, \xi)$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, Theorem 8.1 and proof of Theorem 3, pp. 28–29]. There is an unpointed version. The outer action $G_K \to \mathrm{Out}\, \pi$ gives a class $n(C) \in H^1(G_K, L/H)$, and $n(C) = \nu(C)$ for $g \ge 3$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, Theorem 10.5, p. 33].

**The Hodge-theoretic original.** Let $C$ be a compact Riemann surface of genus $g \ge 3$ and $x \in C$, and let $I = I(C,x)$ be the augmentation ideal of $\mathbb{Z}\pi_1(C,x)$, so that $I/I^3$ is $J_x/J_x^3$ in the notation of the [pointed Torelli theorem](pointed-torelli-theorem-for-curves.md). Hain formulates the theorem as follows [@hain1996, §10, p. 25]. As in the source, Tate twists are not written.

- The mixed Hodge structure on $I/I^3$ is an extension $0 \to H_1(C)^{\otimes 2}/q \to I/I^3 \to H_1(C) \to 0$, where $q$ is the symplectic form. Tensor it with $H_1(C)$ and pull back along $\mathbb{Z} \to H_1(C)^{\otimes 2}$. This gives an extension $E(C,x)$ of $\mathbb{Z}$ by $H_1(C) \otimes (H_1(C)^{\otimes 2}/q)$, with class $\rho \in J(H_1(C) \otimes (H_1(C)^{\otimes 2}/q))$.
- The cycle $C_x - C_x^-$ gives $\zeta \in J(\Lambda^3 H_1(C))$.
- The map $\Phi : J(\Lambda^3 H_1(C)) \to J(H_1(C) \otimes (H_1(C)^{\otimes 2}/q))$ is induced by $\Lambda^3 H_1(C) \to H_1(C)^{\otimes 3} \to H_1(C) \otimes (H_1(C)^{\otimes 2}/q)$. The first map sends $x_1 \wedge x_2 \wedge x_3$ to $\sum_\sigma \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\, x_{\sigma(1)} \otimes x_{\sigma(2)} \otimes x_{\sigma(3)}$, over all permutations $\sigma$ of $\{1,2,3\}$.

Hain works with normal functions over a level cover of $\mathcal{M}_g^1$, whose values at $(C,x)$ are these classes.

**Theorem (Harris–Pulte, in Hain's form).** $\Phi(\zeta) = 2\rho$ [@hain1996, Theorem 10.1, p. 26].

Hain presents this as a new proof of the Harris–Pulte theorem that gives a slightly stronger result [@hain1996, §10, p. 25]. His proof shows that the two sides have the same monodromy representation, and that both vanish when $C$ is hyperelliptic and $x$ is a Weierstrass point [@hain1996, proof of Theorem 10.1, p. 26]. Hain and Matsumoto state the original as $\zeta(C,\xi) = 2\rho(C,\xi)$, leaving the comparison map implicit. They cite Theorem 11.1 of the published version of Hain's paper, and note that $2\rho(C,\xi)$ corresponds to $m(C,\xi)$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, §1, p. 4].

Hain's later survey uses an injection $A : J(\Lambda^3 H_1(C)(-1)) \to J(\mathrm{Hom}(V, H^1(C)))$, which goes in the same direction as $\Phi$. Here $V = \ker(\cup : H^1(C) \otimes H^1(C) \to H^2(C))$, called $K$ in the source, and the class $m_x$ of the extension given by closed iterated integrals of length at most $2$, which is the dual of $I/I^3$ by Chen's theorem, lies in $J(\mathrm{Hom}(V, H^1(C)))$. But the survey prints the theorem as $\nu_x = 2A(m_x)$, with $\nu_x$ the class of $C_x - C_x^-$, which applies $A$ to an element outside its domain [@hain2002, §10.4, Theorem 10.2, p. 24]. This entry therefore follows the formulation above. The survey attributes the theorem to Harris and Pulte, and says that Pulte reworked Harris's work on the harmonic volume in the language of mixed Hodge theory [@hain2002, §10.4, p. 24]. The harmonic volume is an iterated integral that Harris showed is congruent modulo $\mathbb{Z}$ to the volume of a $3$-chain bounded by the image of $C$ in a real $3$-torus [@hain2002, §5, Theorem 5.1, pp. 11–12].

**What the classes control.** Let $\mathfrak{p}(C, \xi)$ be the Lie algebra of the $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-unipotent completion of $\pi_1(C^{\mathrm{an}}, \xi^{\mathrm{an}})$. Both $G_K$ and the mapping class group $\Gamma_{C^{\mathrm{an}}, \xi^{\mathrm{an}}}$ of the pointed surface act on it, through $\rho_{C,\xi}$ and $\theta_{C,\xi}$. Write $\rho^1_C : G_K \to \mathrm{GSp}(H \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ for the action on the abelianization [@hainmatsumoto2005, §1, pp. 2–3]. Suppose that the $\ell$-adic cyclotomic character of $G_K$ has infinite image. Then the following are equivalent [@hainmatsumoto2005, Theorem 1, p. 3]:

1. the Zariski closure of the image of $\rho_{C,\xi}$ in $\mathrm{Aut}\, \mathfrak{p}(C, \xi)$ contains the image of $\theta_{C,\xi}$;
2. the same holds in $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{p}(C, \xi)/L^3)$;
3. $\rho^1_C$ is Zariski dense, and $\kappa(C, \xi)$ and $\nu(C)$ both have infinite order.

In pro-$\ell$ terms, these are equivalent to the image of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(\pi/L^{m+1}\pi)$ being open in the image of the arithmetic fundamental group $\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{M}_g^1, [C, \xi])$ of the moduli stack of pointed curves over $K$, for every $m$ or just for $m = 2$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, Theorem 9.1, p. 29]. In the unpointed version, with outer actions, only $\rho^1_C$ and $\nu(C)$ enter [@hainmatsumoto2005, Theorem 2, p. 4, and Theorem 10.6, p. 34]. Hain and Matsumoto report that a result of Matsumoto and Tamagawa implies that infinitely many curves of genus $g$ over number fields satisfy these conditions, and that it is not known whether a single number field has infinitely many such curves. The theorem as stated fails in genus $2$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, Remark 1.1 (c) and (e), pp. 3–4].

These are statements about Zariski closures in the unipotent completion. The image of $G_K$ always lies in the image of $\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{M}_g^1, [C, \xi])$. Hain and Matsumoto report that for the profinite group and affine curves of genus $g \ne 1$, Matsumoto and Tamagawa showed this inclusion is never an equality: over a number field, no nontrivial element of the geometric monodromy lies in the image of $G_K$. For the pro-$\ell$ group, equality holds for the general curve in the sense of Hilbert irreducibility [@hainmatsumoto2005, Remark 3.1, pp. 7–8].

**Example: hyperelliptic curves.** The following is checked here. Let $C$ be hyperelliptic, of genus $g \ge 3$, with hyperelliptic involution $\iota$, and let $\xi \in C(K)$ be a Weierstrass point, that is, a fixed point of $\iota$. Over $\bar K$, for every point $z$ the divisors $z + \iota(z)$ and $2\xi$ are fibres of the double cover $C \to \mathbb{P}^1$, so they are linearly equivalent, and $[\xi - z] = [\iota(z) - \xi]$. Hence $i \circ \sigma_\xi = \sigma_\xi \circ \iota$. As $\iota$ is an automorphism of $C$, this gives $C_\xi^- = (\sigma_\xi)_* \iota_* C = C_\xi$, so $C_\xi - C_\xi^- = 0$ as a cycle. Therefore:

- $\mu(C, \xi) = 0$ and $\nu(C) = 0$, and by the theorem, $m(C, \xi) = 0$.
- Condition 3 fails. So if the $\ell$-adic cyclotomic character has infinite image, the Zariski closure of the image of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Aut}\, \mathfrak{p}(C, \xi)$ does not contain the image of the mapping class group.
- Without a $K$-rational Weierstrass point, $\nu(C)$ still has finite order. Let $K' \subset \mathbb{C}$ be a finite extension of $K$ over which a Weierstrass point is defined. Since $\nu$ is pulled back from $\mathcal{M}_g$, the restriction of $\nu(C)$ to $G_{K'}$ is $\nu(C_{K'})$, which vanishes by the first point applied over $K'$. Corestriction after restriction is multiplication by $[K' : K]$, so $[K' : K]\,\nu(C) = 0$. Hence the condition on $\nu(C)$ in the unpointed version fails too, and when the cyclotomic character has infinite image, the Zariski closure of the image of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Out}\, \mathfrak{p}(C, \xi)$ does not contain the image of the mapping class group of the unpointed surface [@hainmatsumoto2005, Theorem 2, p. 4].
- On the Hodge side, the same argument gives $C_x = C_x^-$ for a Weierstrass point $x$ of a hyperelliptic compact Riemann surface, so $\zeta = 0$ and $2\rho = \Phi(\zeta) = 0$. Directly, the hyperelliptic involution $\iota$ fixes $x$. It acts by $-1$ on $H^1(C)$, as shown in the [pointed Torelli theorem](pointed-torelli-theorem-for-curves.md), and hence by $+1$ on $V \subseteq H^1(C) \otimes H^1(C)$. By functoriality it gives an automorphism of the extension $0 \to H^1(C) \to (I/I^3)^* \to V \to 0$ that is $-1$ on the subobject and the identity on the quotient, so $m_x = -m_x$ and $2m_x = 0$. Hain notes the vanishing of both sides in this case [@hain1996, proof of Theorem 10.1, p. 26].

This is consistent with Hain and Matsumoto's remark that hyperelliptic curves need a separate treatment, with classes in Bloch's higher Chow groups [@hainmatsumoto2005, Remark 1.1 (e), p. 4].

**The class $\kappa$.** Hain and Matsumoto define $\kappa$ and $\mu$ for pointed families $C \to B$ as well, as classes in $H^1_{\text{ét}}(B, -)$ [@hainmatsumoto2005, §§4.3–4.4, pp. 10–11]. The divisor class $(2g-2)\xi - K_C$ also appears Hodge-theoretically. Kaenders's computation for $X \setminus \{q\}$, extended to $q = p$, gives $\Psi(k_{pp}) = (2g-2)p - K_X$, as recorded in the [pointed Torelli theorem](pointed-torelli-theorem-for-curves.md). For the universal curve, Hain's proof that the generic curve has no sections uses the $\ell$-adic class of $(2g-2)x - \omega_{C/T}$. See [Hain's theorem on the generic curve](section-conjecture-for-universal-curves.md).
