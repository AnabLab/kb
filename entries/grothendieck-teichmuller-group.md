**Definition.** Let $\widehat{F}_2$ be the free profinite group on $x, y$, let $\widehat{F}_2'$ be its derived subgroup, and put $z = (xy)^{-1}$. For $f \in \widehat{F}_2$ and elements $a, b$ of a profinite group $G$, write $f(a, b)$ for the image of $f$ under the homomorphism $\widehat{F}_2 \to G$ with $x \mapsto a$ and $y \mapsto b$, so that $f = f(x, y)$. The profinite Grothendieck–Teichmüller group $\widehat{GT}$ is the set of pairs $(\lambda, f) \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times \times \widehat{F}_2'$ such that $x \mapsto x^\lambda$, $y \mapsto f^{-1} y^\lambda f$ defines an automorphism $F$ of $\widehat{F}_2$, and such that, with $m = (\lambda - 1)/2$,

- (I) $f(x, y) f(y, x) = 1$,
- (II) $f(x, y) x^m f(z, x) z^m f(y, z) y^m = 1$,
- (III) $f(x_{12}, x_{23}) f(x_{34}, x_{45}) f(x_{51}, x_{12}) f(x_{23}, x_{34}) f(x_{45}, x_{51}) = 1$

[@lochakschneps2006, §0]. Here (I) and (II) take place in $\widehat{F}_2$. Relation (III), the pentagon relation, takes place in the profinite completion $\widehat{K}(0,5)$ of the pure mapping class group of the sphere with $5$ marked points, which is the geometric fundamental group of the moduli space $\mathcal{M}_{0,5}$, and the $x_{i,i+1}$ for $i \in \mathbb{Z}/5$ are its standard generators [@harbaterschneps2000, §0.1], [@schneps1997, §1.1]. The element $m$ makes sense because $\lambda - 1$ is divisible by $2$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, as $\lambda$ is a unit.

The pair is determined by $F$: $F(x) = x^\lambda$ gives $\lambda$, and $F(y)$ gives $f$, using that $f \in \widehat{F}_2'$ [@harbaterschneps2000, §0.1]. Composition of automorphisms makes $\widehat{GT}$ a group, with $(\lambda, f)(\mu, g) = (\lambda\mu, f F(g))$, where $F$ belongs to $(\lambda, f)$ [@schneps1997, §1.1]. That the relations survive composition is not immediate [@schneps1997, §1.2]. The pairs satisfying (I), (II) and the automorphism condition form a group $\widehat{GT}_0$ containing $\widehat{GT}$ [@harbaterschneps2000, §0.1]. Relation (I) follows from (III), as Furusho noted [@lochakschneps2006, §0]. No effective way is known to check the automorphism condition, and it is open whether a pair satisfying (I)–(III) can give a non-invertible endomorphism [@lochakschneps2006, §0 and Question 1.1].

**Attribution and other versions.** Harbater and Schneps cite Drinfeld's 1990 paper on quasi-triangular quasi-Hopf algebras for $\widehat{GT}$ [@harbaterschneps2000, §0.1]. Schneps notes that this paper treats the discrete, pro-$\ell$ and pro-unipotent versions, all defined analogously [@schneps1997, p. 183]. The pro-unipotent version has a graded Lie algebra $\mathfrak{grt}$, defined by linearized relations [@lochakschneps2006, §0]. Its part $\mathfrak{grt}_1$ is free on generators $\sigma_3, \sigma_5, \dots$ of weights $3, 5, \dots$, by the [Deligne–Drinfeld theorem](deligne-drinfeld-conjecture.md), whose proof was completed in 2026 preprints. Drinfeld's paper has not been opened for this entry. Refinements of $\widehat{GT}$ defined by further relations are listed under the [Grothendieck–Teichmüller conjecture](grothendieck-teichmuller-conjecture.md).

**Basic properties.**

- *It is profinite.* $\widehat{GT}$ is a closed subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{F}_2)$, which is profinite as the inverse limit of the groups $\mathrm{Aut}(\widehat{F}_2/N)$ over the open characteristic subgroups $N$. By a theorem of Nikolov and Segal every automorphism of $\widehat{F}_2$ is continuous, so the definition is purely algebraic [@lochakschneps2006, §0]. Schneps credits the observation that $\widehat{GT}$ is profinite to Pop [@schneps1997, §1.4 (3)].
- *The character $\lambda$.* The map $(\lambda, f) \mapsto \lambda$ is a surjective homomorphism $\widehat{GT} \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$, whose kernel is written $\widehat{GT}^1$ [@lochakschneps2006, §0]. For the corresponding character of $\widehat{GT}_0$, Bortnovskyi, Dolgushev, Holikov and Pashkovskyi remark that, as far as they know, surjectivity is proved only through the embedding of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ below and the surjectivity of the cyclotomic character [@bdhp2024, Remark 1.3].
- *Complex conjugation.* The pair $c = (-1, 1)$ acts by $x \mapsto x^{-1}$, $y \mapsto y^{-1}$, and its normalizer in $\widehat{GT}$ is generated by $c$, as in $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Lochak and Schneps proved this, answering a question of Ihara [@schneps1997, §1.4 (1)], [@lochakschneps2006, Question 1.4]. They ask whether the conjugates of $c$ are the only nontrivial elements of finite order in $\widehat{GT}$ [@lochakschneps2006, Question 1.4].

**Characterizations by braid and mapping class groups.**

- *Braids on three strands.* Identify $\widehat{F}_2$ with the subgroup of the profinite braid group $\widehat{B}_3$ generated by $x = \sigma_1^2$ and $y = \sigma_2^2$. A pair $(\lambda, f) \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times \times \widehat{F}_2'$ that gives an automorphism of $\widehat{F}_2$ extends to an automorphism $\sigma_1 \mapsto \sigma_1^\lambda$, $\sigma_2 \mapsto f^{-1}\sigma_2^\lambda f$ of $\widehat{B}_3$ exactly when it satisfies (I) and (II). This is due to Lochak and Schneps [@schneps1997, Lemma 1].
- *Genus $0$ moduli (Harbater–Schneps).* For $n \ge 4$, let $\mathrm{Out}^\sharp_n$ be the group of outer automorphisms of $\widehat{K}(0,n)$ that are quasi-special, meaning that for some $\lambda \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$ they send the conjugacy class of each standard generator $x_{ij}$ to that of $x_{ij}^\lambda$, and symmetric, meaning that they commute with the image of the symmetric group $S_n$, which permutes the marked points. Then $\mathrm{Out}^\sharp_4 \cong \widehat{GT}_0$, and $\mathrm{Out}^\sharp_n \cong \widehat{GT}$ for $n \ge 5$ [@harbaterschneps2000, §0.1, Main Theorem]. The natural outer action of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ lies in $\mathrm{Out}^\sharp_n$, and for $n = 4, 5$ the isomorphisms match it with the embedding of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ below [@harbaterschneps2000, §0.1].
- *No conditions needed.* Hoshi, Minamide and Mochizuki prove that $\widehat{GT} \times S_{n+1} \cong \mathrm{Out}(\widehat{K}(0, n+1))$ for every $n \ge 4$, as cited by Minamide and Nakamura [@minamidenakamura2019, Theorem 1.1].
- *Braid groups and genus $1$.* Minamide and Nakamura deduce that, for $n \ge 4$, $\mathrm{Out}(\widehat{B}_n) \cong \widehat{GT} \times (1 + n(n-1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})^\times$, where the second factor is the kernel of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times \to (\mathbb{Z}/n(n-1))^\times$, and that $\widehat{GT} \cong \mathrm{Out}(\widehat{B}_n/Z_n)$, where $Z_n$ is the center. They also obtain $\widehat{GT} \cong \mathrm{Out}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1,2})$ for the profinite mapping class group of a torus with two marked points [@minamidenakamura2019, Theorems A and B and Corollary C]. The paper appeared in the American Journal of Mathematics in 2022; the locations given here are those of arXiv version 2.

**Ihara's homomorphism.** Identify $\widehat{F}_2$ with the geometric fundamental group of $\mathcal{M}_{0,4} = \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{Q}} \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ at the [tangential base point](tangential-morphisms-via-log-geometry.md) $\vec{01}$, with $x$ a small loop around $0$ and $y$ a loop around $1$ [@schneps1997, §3.1]. That this group is free profinite of rank $2$, on suitable generators of inertia groups at $0$ and $1$, is the case $(g, n) = (0, 3)$ of [SGA 1's presentation](geometric-fundamental-groups-of-curves.md). The outer action of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ preserves the conjugacy classes of inertia groups, and it lifts to the action $\sigma(x) = x^{\chi(\sigma)}$, $\sigma(y) = f_\sigma^{-1} y^{\chi(\sigma)} f_\sigma$, where $\chi$ is the cyclotomic character and $f_\sigma$ is unique once it is required to lie in $\widehat{F}_2'$. Ihara proved that $(\chi(\sigma), f_\sigma)$ satisfies (I), (II) and (III). This gives a homomorphism
$$\mathrm{Ih} : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \widehat{GT}, \qquad \sigma \mapsto (\chi(\sigma), f_\sigma).$$
Drinfeld indicated this, and Ihara gave the first complete proof [@lochakschneps2006, §1]. Bortnovskyi, Dolgushev, Holikov and Pashkovskyi write it as $\sigma \mapsto ((\chi(\sigma) - 1)/2, f_\sigma)$, recording $m$ instead of $\lambda$ [@bdhp2024, §1.3], and note that complex conjugation maps to $c = (-1, 1)$ [@bdhp2024, Remark 1.10].

*Injectivity.* This is checked here. If $\mathrm{Ih}(\sigma) = (1, 1)$, then $\sigma$ acts on $\widehat{F}_2$ as the identity. This action lifts the outer action of $\sigma$ on the geometric fundamental group of $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{Q}} \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$, which is therefore trivial, so $\sigma = 1$ by Belyi's case of the [injectivity of outer Galois representations](injectivity-of-outer-galois-representations.md). Schneps sketches the same argument [@schneps1997, §3.1].

Whether $\mathrm{Ih}$ is also surjective is the [Grothendieck–Teichmüller conjecture](grothendieck-teichmuller-conjecture.md).
