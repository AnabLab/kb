**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime. An *oriented pro-$p$ group* is a pro-$p$ group $G$ with a continuous homomorphism $\theta : G \to 1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$, called an orientation. Here $1 + 2\mathbb{Z}_2 = \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$. Efrat introduced these pairs under the name cyclotomic pro-$p$ pairs [@quadrelli2024, §3.1]. If a field $K$ contains a primitive $p$-th root of unity, its maximal pro-$p$ Galois group $G_K(p)$ carries the $p$-cyclotomic character $\theta_K$, defined by $g(\zeta) = \zeta^{\theta_K(g)}$ for every root of unity $\zeta \in K(p)$ of $p$-power order [@quadrelli2024, Example 3.1]. By Kummer theory, $G_K(p)$ is finitely generated exactly when $K^\times/K^{\times p}$ is finite [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Remark 6.1].

There are two operations on oriented pro-$p$ groups [@quadrelli2024, §3.2]:

- *Cyclotomic semidirect product.* From $(G_0, \theta)$ one forms $\mathbb{Z}_p \rtimes_\theta G_0$, in which $gag^{-1} = a^{\theta(g)}$ for $g \in G_0$ and $a \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, oriented by $\theta \circ \pi$, where $\pi$ is the projection onto $G_0$.
- *Free product.* From $(G_1, \theta_1)$ and $(G_2, \theta_2)$ one forms the free pro-$p$ product $G_1 * G_2$, oriented by the homomorphism that restricts to $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$.

**Conjecture** (Efrat). Let $\mathcal{E}_p$ be the smallest family of oriented pro-$p$ groups that is closed under these two operations and contains

1. $(F, \theta)$ for every finitely generated free pro-$p$ group $F$ and every orientation $\theta$ of it, and
2. $(G_L(p), \theta_L)$ for every finite extension $L/\mathbb{Q}_p$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity, and, if $p = 2$, the group $\mathbb{Z}/2$ with the orientation whose image is $\{\pm 1\}$.

Then the oriented pro-$p$ groups $(G_K(p), \theta_K)$, for fields $K$ that contain a primitive $p$-th root of unity and have $K^\times/K^{\times p}$ finite, are, up to isomorphism, exactly the members of $\mathcal{E}_p$ [@quadrelli2024, Conjecture 3.6]. Efrat's own recent account states an equivalent form, which he calls the Lego form, with building blocks the trivial group, $\mathbb{Z}_p$ with any orientation, the oriented $\mathbb{Z}/2$ above and the groups of $p$-adic fields [@efrat2025, Definition 8.4 and Conjecture 8.5]; free products of oriented copies of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ give the oriented free groups of item 1. His original formulation, from 1995, is a stronger arithmetical form: if $G_K(p)$ is finitely generated, it is a free pro-$p$ product of finitely many closed subgroups, each isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$, or the decomposition group of an extension to $K(p)$ of a valuation on $K$, with nontrivial inertia group, or, for $p = 2$, isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2$ [@efrat2025, p. 4 and Conjecture 8.1]. The arithmetical form implies the Lego form [@efrat2025, Corollary 8.6]. Mináč, Pasini, Quadrelli and Tân state the same conjecture, starting from the trivial group and the pairs $(\mathbb{Z}_p, \theta)$, and allowing $\mathbb{Z}_p^m$ in place of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Conjecture 4.8]. Both papers call the members of a larger family, defined in the same way but with every Demushkin group and its canonical orientation in item 2, oriented pro-$p$ groups of *elementary type* [@quadrelli2024, Definition 3.4] [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Definition 4.9]. The groups in item 2 are Demushkin groups with their canonical orientations, by [maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md) and because $\mathbb{Z}/2$ is a Demushkin group whose canonical orientation is the nontrivial one, the unique orientation of $\mathbb{Z}/2$ with image $\{\pm 1\}$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, p. 18]. Indeed, the canonical orientation of a Demushkin group $G$ has the formal Hilbert 90 property [@efrat2025, p. 24], that $H^1(G, \mathbb{Z}/p^k(1)) \to H^1(G, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ is onto for every $k \ge 1$ [@efrat2025, p. 6]. The trivial orientation of $\mathbb{Z}/2$ fails this for $k = 2$, since every homomorphism $\mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/4$ has image in $2\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ and so reduces to $0$. The nontrivially oriented $\mathbb{Z}/2$ is $G_{\mathbb{R}}(2)$ with its cyclotomic character [@efrat2025, Examples 3.3 (2)]. So $\mathcal{E}_p$ lies in the larger family, and the theorems below, stated for that family, apply to it.

One inclusion is known: every member of $\mathcal{E}_p$ occurs as some $(G_K(p), \theta_K)$, because the groups in items 1 and 2 occur and the two operations preserve this property [@quadrelli2024, p. 13] [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, p. 20]. The conjecture is the converse: there are no other finitely generated maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups. Efrat formulated it in 1995, and it parallels an elementary type conjecture for Witt rings [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, p. 20], compare [W-groups and Witt rings](w-groups-and-witt-rings.md).

**Known cases.** Let $K$ contain a primitive $p$-th root of unity, with $K^\times/K^{\times p}$ finite. Then $(G_K(p), \theta_K)$ is of elementary type in each of the following cases [@quadrelli2024, Proposition 3.7] [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Propositions 6.2 and 6.3, §6.1 and Theorem 6.5]:

- $K$ is finite, or pseudo algebraically closed, or an extension of relative transcendence degree $1$ of a pseudo algebraically closed field.
- $K$ is a local field, or an extension of transcendence degree $1$ of a local field, of characteristic not $p$.
- $K$ is an algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, by a theorem of Efrat [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, proof of Proposition 6.3]. Quadrelli states this for algebraic extensions of every global field of characteristic not $p$, citing the same paper of Efrat [@quadrelli2024, Proposition 3.7 (v) and p. 14]. For $p = 2$, Mináč, Pasini, Quadrelli and Tân state it for algebraic extensions of global fields of characteristic not $2$, citing Kozioł and Kula [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, proof of Proposition 6.3]. Efrat's own account lists algebraic extensions of global fields among the fields for which the arithmetical form is proved, citing both his 1997 paper on algebraic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$ and his 1999 paper on finitely generated pro-$p$ absolute Galois groups over global fields [@efrat2025, Example 10.1 (1) and references, p. 42].
- $K$ is $p$-rigid. For odd $p$ this is equivalent to $(G_K(p), \theta_K) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^m \rtimes (G_0, \theta_0)$ for some $m \ge 0$, with $G_0$ trivial, or $G_0 \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ and $\theta_0$ injective [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, §6.1].
- $K$ carries a $p$-henselian valuation whose residue field $\kappa$ has $(G_\kappa(p), \theta_\kappa)$ of elementary type [@quadrelli2024, Proposition 3.7 (vi)].
- $p = 2$ and $K$ is Pythagorean. For formally real Pythagorean fields with finitely many square classes, Mináč showed that the maximal pro-$2$ Galois groups are exactly the members of the smallest class containing $\mathbb{Z}/2$ with orientation of image $\{\pm 1\}$ and closed under free products and cyclotomic semidirect products with $\mathbb{Z}_2$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Theorem 6.5].

**Consequences.**

- *Koszulity.* For every oriented pro-$p$ group $(G, \theta)$ of elementary type, $H^\bullet(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ is a PBW algebra, hence Koszul [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Theorem A]. The same holds for $H^\bullet(G_K, \mathbb{F}_p)$ whenever $G_K(p)$ is of elementary type, because inflation from $G_K(p)$ to $G_K$ is then an isomorphism on mod-$p$ cohomology [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Remark 1.4]. So the conjecture implies [Positselski's conjecture](positselski-koszulity-conjecture.md) that $H^\bullet(G_K, \mathbb{F}_p)$ is Koszul [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Conjecture 1.3] for all fields $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity with $K^\times/K^{\times p}$ finite. For $(G, \theta)$ of elementary type, with $\mathrm{Im}(\theta) \subseteq 1 + 4\mathbb{Z}_2$ if $p = 2$, the quadratic dual of $H^\bullet(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ is the restricted universal enveloping algebra of the restricted Lie algebra attached to the $p$-Zassenhaus filtration of $G$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Theorem B].
- *Massey products.* If $(G, \theta)$ is of elementary type, and $\mathrm{Im}(\theta) \subseteq 1 + 4\mathbb{Z}_2$ when $p = 2$, then $G$ has the strong $n$-fold Massey vanishing property for every $n > 2$: every sequence $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_n \in H^1(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ with $\alpha_i \cup \alpha_{i+1} = 0$ for all $i$ has a defined $n$-fold Massey product containing $0$ [@quadrelli2024, Theorem 1.2 and p. 3]. So the conjecture would give this property for every finitely generated $G_K(p)$, with $\sqrt{-1} \in K$ if $p = 2$ [@quadrelli2024, Question 1.5 and p. 4]. For $p = 2$ the property fails for some fields: Wittenberg gave a number field without $\sqrt{-1}$ whose maximal pro-$2$ Galois group lacks the strong $4$-fold property, and Merkurjev and Scavia showed that every field has an extension with the same defect. In both cases the group is not finitely generated [@quadrelli2024, p. 4]. Compare [strong Massey vanishing](strong-massey-vanishing.md) and [triple Massey products in Galois cohomology](triple-massey-products-in-galois-cohomology.md).

**Evidence.**

- *Right-angled Artin groups.* For a finite simplicial graph $\Gamma$, let $G_\Gamma$ be the pro-$p$ completion of the right-angled Artin group of $\Gamma$. Snopce and Zalesskii prove that $G_\Gamma$ occurs as $G_K(p)$ for a field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity exactly when no induced subgraph of $\Gamma$ is a square or a path with four vertices, and exactly when $G_\Gamma$ can be built from $\mathbb{Z}_p$ by direct products with $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and free pro-$p$ products [@snopcezalesskii2022, Theorem 1.2, (i), (v) and (vi)]. So within this rich family, only groups of elementary type occur [@quadrelli2024, p. 2].
- *Hereditary quadraticity.* Every closed subgroup of $G_K(p)$ is again a maximal pro-$p$ Galois group [@quadrelli2024, p. 2], so by the [norm residue isomorphism theorem](norm-residue-isomorphism-theorem.md) all of them have quadratic mod-$p$ cohomology. Pro-$p$ groups with this property are called Bloch–Kato pro-$p$ groups [@snopcezalesskii2022, p. 2]. Groups of elementary type have it [@quadrelli2024, p. 2], and their finitely generated closed subgroups are again of elementary type [@quadrelli2024, Remark 3.5 (a)]. Snopce and Zalesskii write that no Bloch–Kato pro-$p$ group is known that is not of elementary type [@snopcezalesskii2022, p. 2], and Quadrelli makes the same remark for finitely generated groups [@quadrelli2024, p. 2].

**What it predicts for Demushkin groups.** It is not known whether every infinite [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) occurs as $G_K(p)$ for a field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, p. 20]. Quadrelli notes that the simplest open case is the pro-$2$ group with generators $x_1, x_2, x_3$ and the single relation $x_1^2[x_2, x_3] = 1$ [@quadrelli2024, Remark 3.3]. Efrat conjectures that if $(G_K(p), \theta_K)$ is a Demushkin group of rank at least $3$ with its canonical orientation, then it is the oriented group of a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_p)$. He calls this an open question, and notes that it would rule out Quadrelli's example and the pro-$p$ surface groups of even rank at least $4$ [@efrat2025, Conjecture 11.6 and Example 11.7, pp. 25–26]. He proves that it follows from the arithmetical form [@efrat2025, Theorems 12.5 and 12.6 (a)]. He also proves that a Demushkin group of rank at least $3$ is not a free pro-$p$ product of two nontrivial closed subgroups, and, with its canonical orientation, not a cyclotomic semidirect product $\mathbb{Z}_p \rtimes G_0$ [@efrat2025, Lemmas 11.10 and 11.11]. The proposition below derives the same prediction from the Lego form stated above, using these two facts, whose proofs it repeats, and turns it into a condition on the invariants of the classification. That derivation, the equivalence of (ii) and (iii), and the examples are checked here. I did not find them stated in the sources opened.

*Proposition.* Let $G$ be a Demushkin pro-$p$ group of rank $n \ge 3$, with canonical character $\chi$, and consider the conditions:

- (i) $G \cong G_K(p)$ for some field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity;
- (ii) $G \cong G_L(p)$ for some finite extension $L/\mathbb{Q}_p$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity;
- (iii) $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ is open in $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, and its index divides $n - 2$.

Then (ii) and (iii) are equivalent and imply (i). If the conjecture holds for $p$, all three are equivalent.

*Examples.*

- For odd $p$, $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = 1 + p^s\mathbb{Z}_p$, where $q(G) = p^s$ with $s \ge 1$ or $s = \infty$, and $p^\infty = 0$ (see the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md)). So (iii) says that $s$ is finite and $(p - 1)p^{s-1}$ divides $n - 2$, as $1 + p^s\mathbb{Z}_p$ has index $(p-1)p^{s-1}$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. For $n = 4$ this leaves only $p = 3$ and $q = 3$, the group of $\mathbb{Q}_3(\zeta_3)$ with relation $x_1^3[x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4] = 1$. Quadrelli notes that this pro-$3$ group is the only Demushkin group on $4$ generators known to occur [@quadrelli2024, Remark 3.3]. That remark has to be read for odd $p$, since the quadratic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ give pro-$2$ Demushkin groups on $4$ generators (see [maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md), Example 2). So for odd $p$ the conjecture predicts that no other $4$-generator Demushkin group occurs.
- For $n = 3$, necessarily $p = 2$, since Demushkin groups with $q \ne 2$ have even rank. Then $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\} \times (1 + 2^f\mathbb{Z}_2)$, with relation $x_1^2x_2^{2^f}[x_2, x_3] = 1$, for some $f \ge 2$ or $f = \infty$ (see the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md)). Its index in $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times = \{\pm 1\} \times (1 + 4\mathbb{Z}_2)$ is $2^{f-2}$ if $f$ is finite, and it is not open if $f = \infty$. So (iii) holds only for $f = 2$, which is the group of $\mathbb{Q}_2$. In particular the conjecture predicts that Quadrelli's example, the case $f = \infty$, does not occur, as Efrat also notes [@efrat2025, Example 11.7].
- If $q = 0$, for instance for the relation $[x_1, x_2] \cdots [x_{n-1}, x_n] = 1$ of the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface of genus $n/2 \ge 2$ (see [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md)), then $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) \subseteq \bigcap_k (1 + p^k\mathbb{Z}_p) = \{1\}$ is not open. So the conjecture predicts that these Demushkin groups do not occur, as Efrat notes for the surface groups [@efrat2025, p. 26].

*Proof.* (ii) implies (i) with $K = L$.

(ii) implies (iii). Put $L' = L \cap \mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})$. By [maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md), $G_L(p)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $[L:\mathbb{Q}_p] + 2$ whose canonical character has image $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/L')$, and by the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md) this image is an invariant of the group, so it equals $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$. It is open, of index $[L' : \mathbb{Q}_p]$, which divides $[L : \mathbb{Q}_p] = n - 2$.

(iii) implies (ii). Let $A = \mathrm{Im}(\chi)$, of index $m$ dividing $n - 2$, and let $L'$ be the fixed field of $A$ in $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})$, so that $[L' : \mathbb{Q}_p] = m$. As $A$ is open, it contains $1 + p^k\mathbb{Z}_p$ for some $k$, so $L' \subseteq \mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^k})$, which is totally ramified over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ because $\Phi_{p^k}(x + 1)$ is Eisenstein. For odd $p$, the image of the pro-$p$ group $G$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ lies in $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Remark 4.2], and these elements fix $\zeta_p$, so $\zeta_p \in L'$; for $p = 2$, $-1 \in L'$ trivially. Let $U$ be the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $(n-2)/m$, and $L = L'U$. Since $L'/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is totally ramified and $U/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is unramified and Galois, $L' \cap U = \mathbb{Q}_p$ and $[L : L'] = [U : \mathbb{Q}_p]$, so $[L : \mathbb{Q}_p] = n - 2$. Let $M = L \cap \mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})$. It is finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, hence inside some $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^j})$, hence totally ramified over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and so over $L'$. But $M \subseteq L$ and $L/L'$ is unramified, so $M = L'$. By the same entry, $G_L(p)$ is then a Demushkin group of rank $n$ whose canonical character has image $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/L') = A$. By the [classification of Demushkin groups](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), $G \cong G_L(p)$.

(i) implies (ii) under the conjecture. A Demushkin group is finitely generated, so $K^\times/K^{\times p}$ is finite by Kummer theory, and the conjecture gives $(G_K(p), \theta_K) \in \mathcal{E}_p$. All members of $\mathcal{E}_p$ are finitely generated, since the groups in items 1 and 2 are and the operations preserve this. Up to isomorphism, they are exactly the oriented groups obtained from those in items 1 and 2 by finitely many operations, because these form a family with the defining properties that lies in every such family. Choose such a construction of an oriented group isomorphic to $(G_K(p), \theta_K)$ with the fewest operations, and call its underlying group $G$.

- If $G$ comes from item 1, it is free, so $H^2(G, \mathbb{F}_p) = 0$ and $G$ is not Demushkin. If it comes from item 2, then $G \cong G_L(p)$ as in (ii), or $G \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, which has rank $1$.
- Suppose the last operation is a free product, $G = G_1 * G_2$. If one factor were trivial, the other would give a shorter construction, so both are nontrivial and have $H^1(G_i, \mathbb{F}_p) \ne 0$, since the dimension of $H^1$ is the minimal number of generators [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, §3.4, p. 17]. Restriction gives an isomorphism of graded algebras from $H^\bullet(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ onto $H^\bullet(G_1, \mathbb{F}_p) \sqcap H^\bullet(G_2, \mathbb{F}_p)$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Proposition 5.3], an algebra in which the product of two elements of positive degree from different factors is $0$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Example 2.6 (a)]. As $H^2(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ has dimension $1$, one of the $H^2(G_i, \mathbb{F}_p)$ vanishes, say for $i = 2$. Take $a \in H^1(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ corresponding to a nonzero element of $H^1(G_2, \mathbb{F}_p)$. For every $c = c_1 + c_2$ with $c_i$ in the factor of $G_i$, the product $a \cup c_1$ is $0$ and $a \cup c_2$ lies in $H^2(G_2, \mathbb{F}_p) = 0$. So $a \cup c = 0$ for all $c$, which contradicts the nondegeneracy of the cup product of a Demushkin group.
- Suppose the last operation is a cyclotomic semidirect product, $G = N \rtimes G_0$ with $N \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$. Then $H^b(N, \mathbb{F}_p)$ is $\mathbb{F}_p$ for $b = 0, 1$ and $0$ for $b \ge 2$, as $N$ is free pro-$p$. Since $\theta(g) \equiv 1 \bmod p$, conjugation by $G_0$ acts trivially on $H^1(N, \mathbb{F}_p) = \mathrm{Hom}(N, \mathbb{F}_p)$. So the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence of $1 \to N \to G \to G_0 \to 1$ has $E_2^{a,b} = H^a(G_0, \mathbb{F}_p)$ for $b = 0, 1$ and no other nonzero rows. The extension splits, so inflation $H^a(G_0, \mathbb{F}_p) \to H^a(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ is injective and no differential hits the row $b = 0$. With two rows the only possible differentials go from row $1$ to row $0$, so all vanish and $E_2 = E_\infty$. Hence $\dim H^1(G, \mathbb{F}_p) = \dim H^1(G_0, \mathbb{F}_p) + 1$, and $H^2(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ has a subquotient isomorphic to $H^1(G_0, \mathbb{F}_p)$. This agrees with the description of $H^\bullet(G, \mathbb{F}_p)$ in [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Proposition 5.8]. As $\dim H^2(G, \mathbb{F}_p) = 1$, we get $\dim H^1(G_0, \mathbb{F}_p) \le 1$, so $n = \dim H^1(G, \mathbb{F}_p) \le 2$, a contradiction.

So $G_K(p) \cong G \cong G_L(p)$ for a finite $L/\mathbb{Q}_p$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity. $\square$

The proposition says nothing about Demushkin groups of rank $2$.

**Status.** Open, as far as the sources opened here show. The most recent of them, Efrat's 2025 account, treats it as a conjecture and surveys the evidence [@efrat2025, §10], and calls the prediction for Demushkin groups an open question [@efrat2025, p. 25].
