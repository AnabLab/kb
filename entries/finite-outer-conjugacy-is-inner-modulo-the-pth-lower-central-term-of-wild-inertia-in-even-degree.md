**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the higher cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-higher-cocycle-classes-of-wild-inertia.md), which is that of [the higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md), with its lower central input, its words $w=(w_1,\dots,w_n)$, the summands $V_w\subseteq V^{\hat\otimes n}$, the coordinates $X_w$ and the rule (R). So $p$ is odd, $G=G_K$, $P$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, $P_j$ is the lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$, $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$, and $V=H/pH=L\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d$ with $L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$ and $V_k=\Lambda\bar x_k$. A word has letters in $\{0,1,\dots,d\}$, the letter $0$ standing for a slot in $L$. Assume that $d=[K:\mathbb Q_p]$ is even, so $d\ge4$. Then the complementary indices are $1$ and $2$, and the surface pairs are $(a_i,b_i)=(x_{2i+1},x_{2i+2})$ for $1\le i\le r=d/2-1$. Let $p^s$ be the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, as in [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). For a tame-trivial automorphism $\theta$, $\theta_H$ and $\theta_n$ are its actions on $H$ and on $\mathcal L_n$, and $\mathcal J_n$ is the Johnson map of the higher Johnson theorem.

*Weights.* A *weight datum* is $\ell=(W;m_1,\dots,m_r)\in\mathbb Z^{1+r}$. Give the letters the weights $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(0)=\mathrm{wt}_\ell(1)=W$, $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(2)=0$, $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(a_i)=m_i$ and $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(b_i)=W-m_i$, and put $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w)=\sum_t\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w_t)$. For a word $w$ let $n_k(w)$ be the number of slots with letter $k$, and $N(w)=n_0(w)+n_1(w)+\sum_in_{b_i}(w)$. Then
$$\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w)=W\,N(w)+\sum_{i=1}^rm_i\big(n_{a_i}(w)-n_{b_i}(w)\big).$$

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]$ even, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, and $s\ge2$, that is, $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$ contains the $p^2$-th roots of unity.

- (a) Let $2\le n\le p-1$, and let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many outer conjugates and induce the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$. Then $c_h\circ\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$ for some $h\in P_n$.
- (b) Let $3\le n\le p-1$, and let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many outer conjugates and induce the identity on $G/P_n$. Then $\mathcal J_n(\alpha)=0$, that is, $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$ for every $x\in P$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Under the same hypotheses, every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha'$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha'$ that induces the identity on $G/P_p$. Two such $\alpha'$ differ by $c_k$ with $k\in P_p$. So every outer class with finitely many conjugates lies in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_p)$.

For $p=3$, part (b) is empty and the corollary says that these classes are trivial in $\operatorname{Out}(G/P_3)$.

*Examples.* (1) Let $p\equiv1\pmod4$ and let $K\subseteq\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^2})$ be the fixed field of $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{-1}$. Then $d=p(p-1)/2$ is even. This automorphism moves $\zeta_p$, so $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $K(\mu_p)=\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^2})$, of degree $2$ over $K$. As $2$ is prime to $p$, $K(\mu_p)\subseteq K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, so $s\ge2$. (2) For any odd $p$, let $K$ be the compositum of the field in (1) with the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, so $d=p(p-1)$. Its ramification index over $\mathbb Q_p$ is $p(p-1)/2$, which $p-1$ does not divide, so $K$ does not contain the totally ramified field $\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_p)$ of degree $p-1$. As $[K(\zeta_{p^2}):K]\le2$, again $s\ge2$. For $p=3$ this gives $d=6$. On the other hand, $s=1$ whenever $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is unramified, since $K(\zeta_{p^2})/K$ then has ramification index $p(p-1)$.

**Lemma 1 (weight automorphisms; proved here).** Let $s\ge2$ and let $\ell$ be a weight datum. There is a tame-trivial $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ acting trivially on $V$ such that, for every $n\ge1$, $D_n=(\theta_n-1)/p$ is a well-defined continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant endomorphism of $\mathcal L_n$. Its reduction $\bar D_n$ on $\mathcal L_n/p=\mathcal L_n(V)$ is the restriction of $\delta_\ell=\sum_{t=1}^n1^{\otimes t-1}\otimes\Pi_\ell\otimes1^{\otimes n-t}$. Here $\Pi_\ell$ is the $\Lambda$-linear endomorphism of $V$ that multiplies $L\oplus V_1$ by $W$, $V_2$ by $0$, $V_{a_i}$ by $m_i$ and $V_{b_i}$ by $W-m_i$. So $\delta_\ell$ acts on each $V_w$ as multiplication by $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w)$.

*Proof.* Apply [the similitude theorem](similitude-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md) with $c=(1+p)^W$ and $c_i=(1+p)^{m_i}$, so that $c/c_i=(1+p)^{W-m_i}$, and let $\theta$ be the result. It is tame-trivial. Let $\xi_k\in H$ be the class of $x_k$, and write $\mathrm{wt}(k)$ for the weight of the letter $k$, with $\mathrm{wt}(0)=W$. By that theorem, whose $P_K$ is $P$ here, $\theta(x_k)\in x_k^{(1+p)^{\mathrm{wt}(k)}}P^{p,2,s}$, where $x_k$ also denotes the image in $G$ of the generator $x_k$. The image of $P^{p,2,s}=P^{p^s}[P,P]$ in $H$ is $p^sH\subseteq p^2H$, as $s\ge2$. As $(1+p)^w\equiv1+wp\pmod{p^2}$ for $w\in\mathbb Z$,
$$\theta_H(\xi_k)\equiv\xi_k+p\,\mathrm{wt}(k)\,\xi_k\pmod{p^2H}.$$
So $\theta$ fixes every $\bar x_k$. It acts $\Lambda$-linearly on $V$, which is generated by the $\bar x_k$ (part 1 of units of the tame group algebra), so it acts trivially on $V$. Hence $D_1=(\theta_H-1)/p$ is well defined, as $H$ is torsion-free. It is continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant, and its reduction is $\Lambda$-linear and multiplies $\bar x_k$ by $\mathrm{wt}(k)$, so it is $\Pi_\ell$. For $y_1,\dots,y_n\in H$, $\theta_n[y_1,\dots,y_n]=[\theta_Hy_1,\dots,\theta_Hy_n]$ for left-normed brackets in the graded Lie ring of the lower central series. Substituting $\theta_Hy_t=y_t+pD_1y_t$ and using multilinearity,
$$\theta_n[y_1,\dots,y_n]=[y_1,\dots,y_n]+p\sum_t[y_1,\dots,D_1y_t,\dots,y_n]+p^2z$$
for some $z\in\mathcal L_n$. By the lower central input, $\mathcal L_n$ is torsion-free and topologically generated by such brackets. So $\theta_n-1$ maps $\mathcal L_n$ into the closed subgroup $p\mathcal L_n$, and $D_n$ is well defined, continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant. The image of $[y_1,\dots,y_n]$ in $\mathcal L_n(V)$ is the Lie element $[\bar y_1,\dots,\bar y_n]$. On it, $\bar D_n$ gives $\sum_t[\bar y_1,\dots,\Pi_\ell\bar y_t,\dots,\bar y_n]$, which is $\delta_\ell[\bar y_1,\dots,\bar y_n]$ because $\delta_\ell$ is a derivation of the tensor algebra. Both maps are continuous and linear, and these Lie elements span a dense subspace of $\mathcal L_n(V)$. So $\bar D_n$ is the restriction of $\delta_\ell$. On $V_w$, $\delta_\ell$ is the sum over the slots of the scalars $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w_t)$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (fixed vectors; proved here).** Let $M$ be a torsion-free $\mathbb Z_p$-module with $\bigcap_kp^kM=0$, $E$ a $\mathbb Z_p$-linear endomorphism of $M$, and $U=1+pE$. If $U^Nv=v$ with $N\ge1$ and $v\in M$, then $Ev=0$.

*Proof.* Since $\binom Nk/N=\binom{N-1}{k-1}/k$,
$$U^N-1=\sum_{k=1}^N\binom Nkp^kE^k=pN\,(1+pF)E,\qquad F=\sum_{k=2}^Na_kE^{k-1},\quad a_k=\binom{N-1}{k-1}\frac{p^{k-2}}k.$$
Each $a_k$ lies in $\mathbb Z_p$. Indeed, if $p\nmid k$ this is clear, and if $v=v_p(k)\ge1$, then $k\ge p^v\ge3^v\ge v+2$, so $k-2\ge v_p(k)$. As $M$ is torsion-free, $(U^N-1)v=0$ gives $(1+pF)y=0$ for $y=Ev$. Then $y=-pFy$. If $y=p^ky'$, then $y=p^{k+1}(-Fy')$. So $y\in\bigcap_kp^kM=0$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (counting; proved here).** Let $1\le n\le p-1$, let $w$ be a word of length $n$, and let $\kappa\in\{0,1,2\}\cup\{a_j,b_j:1\le j\le r\}$ be a letter. Suppose that $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w)\equiv\mathrm{wt}_\ell(\kappa)\pmod p$ for every weight datum $\ell$. Then all letters of $w$ are $2$, except:

- for $\kappa=2$: none;
- for $\kappa=a_j$: one letter $a_j$;
- for $\kappa=b_j$: one letter $b_j$;
- for $\kappa\in\{0,1\}$: either one letter in $\{0,1\}$, or one letter $a_i$ and one letter $b_i$ for some $i$.

*Proof.* Put $N(\kappa)=1$ for $\kappa\in\{0,1,b_j\}$ and $N(\kappa)=0$ for $\kappa\in\{2,a_j\}$, and let $\varepsilon_i\in\{-1,0,1\}$ be $1$ if $\kappa=a_i$, $-1$ if $\kappa=b_i$, and $0$ otherwise. Then $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(\kappa)=W\,N(\kappa)+\sum_im_i\varepsilon_i$. The datum $(1;0,\dots,0)$ gives $N(w)\equiv N(\kappa)$, and since $0\le N(w)\le n\le p-1$, $N(w)=N(\kappa)$. The datum with $W=0$, $m_i=1$ and the other $m$'s $0$ gives $n_{a_i}(w)-n_{b_i}(w)\equiv\varepsilon_i$. As $|n_{a_i}(w)-n_{b_i}(w)|\le p-1$, the difference is $\varepsilon_i$, or $1-p$ with $\varepsilon_i=1$, or $p-1$ with $\varepsilon_i=-1$.

- In the second case, $n_{b_i}(w)=p-1=n$, so $N(w)\ge p-1\ge2>N(a_i)=0$.
- In the third case, $n_{a_i}(w)=p-1=n$, so every letter is $a_i$ and $N(w)=0<N(b_i)=1$.

Both are impossible, so $n_{a_i}(w)-n_{b_i}(w)=\varepsilon_i$ for every $i$. If $N(\kappa)=0$, then $n_0=n_1=n_{b_i}=0$ for all $i$, so $n_{a_i}(w)=\varepsilon_i$, which gives the cases $\kappa=2$ and $\kappa=a_j$. If $N(\kappa)=1$, exactly one slot carries a letter in $\{0,1,b_1,\dots,b_r\}$. For $\kappa=b_j$, $n_{b_j}(w)=n_{a_j}(w)+1\ge1$, so that letter is $b_j$, and every $n_{a_i}(w)$ is $0$. For $\kappa\in\{0,1\}$, all $\varepsilon_i$ are $0$. If that letter is some $b_i$, then $n_{a_i}(w)=1$ and the other $n_{a_{i'}}(w)$ are $0$; otherwise every $n_{a_i}(w)$ and $n_{b_i}(w)$ is $0$. $\square$

*The transvection $T_1$.* By step 5 of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), there is a tame-trivial automorphism $T_1$ that fixes $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$, and acts on $H$ by $1+N$ with $N^2=0$. Its action $\bar T_1$ on $V$ maps $\mu\bar x_2$ to $\mu\bar x_2+\mu\bar x_1$ and fixes the other summands. By the rule (R), for $Y\in V^{\hat\otimes n}$ and a word $w$, the $w$-component of $\bar T_1^{\otimes n}Y$ is the sum of the $w'$-components of $Y$ over the words $w'$ obtained from $w$ by changing some, possibly none, of its letters $1$ into $2$. Here $T(w')=T(w)$, and the coordinates are added without change. $(\ast)$

**Proof of part (a).** Let $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal S$ be as in Lemma 3 of the higher cocycle theorem for this $n$, which applies as $n\ge2$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. As $n\le p-1$, $p\nmid n$. Fix $v\in\mathcal S$, and let $X\in(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma=\bigoplus_w(V_w)_\Gamma$ be the image of $\bar v\in\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma$, as in the Reductions there.

*Weights.* Fix a weight datum $\ell$, and let $\theta$ and $D_n$ be as in Lemma 1. By Lemma 3 (ii) there, $\theta_*^Nv=v$ for some $N\ge1$. As $\theta_n=1+pD_n$ with $D_n$ $\Gamma$-equivariant, $\theta_*=1+p(D_n)_*$ on $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$. This group is torsion-free by the Cohomology paragraph there. Also $\bigcap_kp^kH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)=0$, because $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}$ is injective and maps $p^kH^1$ into $p^k(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma$, and $\bigcap_kp^k(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma=0$ in the compact abelian pro-$p$ group $(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma$. By Lemma 2, $(D_n)_*v=0$. As $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}$ evaluates cocycles at $\sigma$, $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}((D_n)_*v)=(D_n)_\Gamma\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}(v)$, and modulo $p$ this gives $(\bar D_n)_\Gamma\bar v=0$ in $\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma$. By Lemma 1, $\bar D_n$ is the restriction of $\delta_\ell$, which commutes with $\Gamma$ and acts on $V_w$ by $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w)$. So the $w$-part of $X$ is killed by $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w)$ for every word $w$ and every $\ell$. If it is nonzero, then $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w)\equiv0=\mathrm{wt}_\ell(2)$ for every $\ell$, and $w=(2,\dots,2)$ by Lemma 3.

*The word $(2,\dots,2)$.* By Lemma 3 (iii) and the reduction (iii') there, $(\bar T_1^{\otimes n})_\Gamma X=X$. By $(\ast)$, the sources of $w^*=(1,2,\dots,2)$ are $w^*$ and $(2,\dots,2)$, so $X_{w^*}=X_{w^*}+X_{(2,\dots,2)}$ and $X_{(2,\dots,2)}=0$. This is step 4 of the proof there, which uses only (iii') and (R).

So $X=0$, and $v\in p\mathcal S$ by the reduction (v') there. Hence $\mathcal S=p\mathcal S$. For $u=u(\alpha)\in\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal S$ this gives $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}(u)\in\bigcap_kp^k(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma=0$, so $u=0$, and (u4) there gives $h$. $\square$

**Proof of part (b).** Let $\mathcal S_J$ be the set $\mathcal S$ of Lemma 5 of the higher Johnson theorem for this $n$, which applies as $n\ge2$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Let $\mathcal H$ be the group of continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant homomorphisms $H\to\mathcal L_n$. It is a torsion-free $\mathbb Z_p$-module with $\bigcap_kp^k\mathcal H=0$, as $\mathcal L_n$ is torsion-free and $\bigcap_kp^k\mathcal L_n=0$. Fix $J\in\mathcal S_J$, with $\Lambda$-linear reduction $\bar J:V\to\mathcal L_n(V)$.

*Weights.* Fix $\ell$, and let $\theta$, $D_1$ and $D_n$ be as in Lemma 1. Put $U(J')=\theta_nJ'\theta_H^{-1}$ for $J'\in\mathcal H$. By Lemma 5 (ii) there, $U^NJ=J$ for some $N\ge1$. From $\theta_H=1+pD_1$ we get $\theta_H^{-1}=1-pD_1\theta_H^{-1}$, so $U=1+pE$ with
$$E(J')=D_nJ'\theta_H^{-1}-J'D_1\theta_H^{-1},$$
a $\mathbb Z_p$-linear endomorphism of $\mathcal H$. By Lemma 2, $E(J)=0$. As $\theta$ acts trivially on $V$, reduction modulo $p$ gives $\bar D_n\bar J=\bar J\Pi_\ell$. Evaluating at $\bar x_k$, the $w$-component of $\bar J(\bar x_k)$ is killed by $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(w)-\mathrm{wt}_\ell(k)$, where $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(k)$ is the weight of the letter $k$. So by Lemma 3 with $\kappa=k$, $\bar J(\bar x_k)$ has components only in the words listed there.

*The transvection.* By Lemma 5 (iii) there, $(T_1)_nJ(T_1)_H^{-1}=J$, so $\bar T_1^{\otimes n}\bar J=\bar J\bar T_1$. Write $Y_w$ for the $w$-component of $Y\in V^{\hat\otimes n}$.

- *$k=a_j$ or $b_j$.* As $\bar T_1\bar x_k=\bar x_k$, $Y=\bar J(\bar x_k)$ is fixed by $\bar T_1^{\otimes n}$. Let $w'$ be one of its words. As $n\ge2$, it has a letter $2$ at some slot $q$; let $w$ be $w'$ with this letter changed to $1$. Then $w$ is not in the list, as it contains the letter $1$, so $Y_w=0$. As $w$ has exactly one letter $1$, $(\ast)$ gives $0=Y_w=Y_w+Y_{w'}$, so $Y_{w'}=0$. Hence $Y=0$.
- *$k\in\{0,1\}$.* Again $Y=\bar J(\bar x_k)$ is fixed by $\bar T_1^{\otimes n}$. Its words have, apart from letters $2$, either one letter $a_i$ and one letter $b_i$, or one letter $0$, or one letter $1$. In the first two cases choose a slot with letter $2$, which exists as $n\ge3$, and change it to $1$. The resulting $w$ has $N(w)=2$, so it is not in the list, and it has one letter $1$; as before $Y_{w'}=0$. In the third case let $y_q=Y_{w^{(q)}}$, where $w^{(q)}$ has the letter $1$ at $q$ and $2$ elsewhere. For $q\ne q'$, let $w$ have the letter $1$ at $q$ and $q'$ and $2$ elsewhere. Then $N(w)=2$, so $Y_w=0$. The words obtained from $w$ by changing letters $1$ into $2$ are $w$, $w^{(q)}$, $w^{(q')}$ and $(2,\dots,2)$, and $Y_{(2,\dots,2)}=0$ since $N=0$ there. So $y_q+y_{q'}=0$ whenever $q\ne q'$. As $n\ge3$, for distinct $q,q',q''$ this gives $y_q=-y_{q'}=y_{q''}=-y_q$, so $y_q=0$, as $p$ is odd. Hence $Y=0$.
- *$k=2$.* Now $\bar T_1^{\otimes n}\bar J(\bar x_2)=\bar J(\bar x_2+\bar x_1)=\bar J(\bar x_2)$, by the previous case. So $Y=\bar J(\bar x_2)$, whose only word is $(2,\dots,2)$, is fixed. For $w=(1,2,\dots,2)$, $(\ast)$ gives $0=Y_w=Y_w+Y_{(2,\dots,2)}$, so $Y=0$.

So $\bar J$ vanishes on every $\bar x_k$, hence on $V$, by $\Lambda$-linearity. Then $J(H)\subseteq p\mathcal L_n$, and $J=pJ'$ with $J'\in\mathcal H$. If $p^mJ=\mathcal J_n(\alpha)$, then $p^{m+1}J'=\mathcal J_n(\alpha)$, so $J'\in\mathcal S_J$. Hence $\mathcal S_J=p\mathcal S_J$, and $\mathcal J_n(\alpha)\in\bigcap_kp^k\mathcal H=0$. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** By Corollary 1 of [the degree-one Johnson theorem in degrees three and four](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-in-degrees-three-and-four.md), which needs only degree at least $3$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, $\alpha=c_{g_2}\circ\alpha_2$ with $\alpha_2$ inducing the identity on $G/P_2$ and on $P/P_3$. Suppose $2\le n\le p-1$ and $\alpha=c_{g_n}\circ\alpha_n$, with $\alpha_n$ inducing the identity on $G/P_n$ and $P/P_{n+1}$. Then $\alpha_n$ represents the class of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates. Part (a) gives $h\in P_n$ with $c_h\alpha_n$ inducing the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$. If $n+1\le p-1$, part (b) for $n+1\ge3$ shows that $c_h\alpha_n$ is trivial on $P/P_{n+2}$; put $\alpha_{n+1}=c_h\alpha_n$ and $g_{n+1}=g_nh^{-1}$. For $n=p-1$ put $\alpha'=c_h\alpha_{p-1}$, which induces the identity on $G/P_p$, and $g=g_{p-1}h^{-1}$.

If $c_k\alpha'$ also induces the identity on $G/P_p$, then so does $c_k$, and $k\in P_p$ by Lemma 5 (o) of the higher Johnson theorem. Conversely, $c_k$ with $k\in P_p$ induces the identity on $G/P_p$. As $P$ is characteristic in $G$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), so is $P_p$. So $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_p)$ is defined, and it maps the class of $\alpha$ to the trivial class. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* The similitudes with parameters $(1+p)^W$ and $(1+p)^{m_i}$ act trivially on $V$. When $s\ge2$ they are controlled on $H/p^2H$, where they multiply each generator class by $1+p$ times its weight. Finite outer conjugacy makes the class of $\alpha$ invariant under a power of each of them. Lemma 2 turns this into invariance under the weight derivation $\delta_\ell$ modulo $p$. Below degree $p$, the weights of a word determine the counts of its letters. Only words made of $\bar x_2$ survive, and the transvection $T_1$ removes them. The proof uses no Kondo automorphism, boundary twist, unit or mixing automorphism. It uses $[K(\mu_p):K]$ only through $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, and needs $d\ge4$ rather than $d\ge5$.
- *Comparison.* For $e=[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$ and $d\ge5$, the corollary of the higher cocycle theorem gives triviality in $\operatorname{Out}(G/P_e)$, where $e\le p-1$. For $e=2$, which includes every $K$ with $p=3$ and $\mu_3\not\subseteq K$, part (b) of [the theorem modulo the third lower central term](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-the-third-lower-central-term-of-wild-inertia.md) leaves one coordinate at level $2$ open. When $d$ is even and $s\ge2$, both are superseded by triviality in $\operatorname{Out}(G/P_p)$. In particular the coordinate $E_n$ of part (b) of the higher cocycle theorem, and the constants left when $e\mid n-1$, vanish for $n\le p-1$.
- *Where this stops.* (1) At level $p$: Lemma 1 of the higher cocycle theorem needs $p\nmid n$. In Lemma 3 with $n=p$, words with $p-1$ letters $b_j$ and one letter in $\{0,1\}$ have the weights of $a_j$ and contain no letter $2$, so $T_1$ does not reach them. (2) For $s=1$, which includes every unramified $K$, the similitude theorem controls $\theta$ only modulo $P^{(2)}$, where a similitude with parameters $\equiv1\pmod p$ is the identity, and the argument gives nothing. (3) For odd $d$ the similitude theorem is not available. (4) The case $\mu_p\subseteq K$. Innerness of a finite-conjugacy automorphism is equivalent to innerness on every $G/P_j$. This continues item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535).
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, even $[K:\mathbb Q_p]$, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $s\ge2$; $2\le n\le p-1$ in (a), $3\le n\le p-1$ in (b). Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
