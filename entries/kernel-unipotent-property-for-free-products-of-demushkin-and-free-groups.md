**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime. For a pro-$p$ group $G$, with $p$-Zassenhaus filtration $G_{(n)}$ as in [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md), and $U_n(\mathbb F_p)$ the upper unitriangular $n\times n$ matrices, $G$ has the *kernel $n$-unipotent property* if
$$G_{(n)}=\bigcap_{\rho:G\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)}\ker\rho,$$
over all continuous homomorphisms [@minactan2015kernel, Definition 1.2, p. 2]. The inclusion $\subseteq$ holds for every pro-$p$ group [@minactan2015kernel, Lemma 2.5(b), p. 6].

The free pro-$p$ product $G_1*G_2$ is the coproduct of $G_1$ and $G_2$ in the category of pro-$p$ groups, and it is the pro-$p$ completion of the abstract free product [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Definition 4.6, p. 19]. So for every pro-$p$ group $H$, restriction is a bijection from continuous homomorphisms $G_1*G_2\to H$ to pairs of continuous homomorphisms $G_i\to H$, and $G_1*G_2$ is topologically generated by the images of $G_1$ and $G_2$, since the abstract free product is dense in it. Products of $s$ factors are formed by iterating, and have the same two properties.

Call a pro-$p$ group *basic* if it is of one of the following types:

- (F) a free pro-$p$ group of finite rank $d\ge1$;
- (D) a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $d\ge3$;
- (Z) for $p=2$ only, the group $\mathbb Z/2$, with $d=1$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $G=G_1*\cdots*G_s$ be a free pro-$p$ product of finitely many basic groups, $s\ge1$.

1. If $m\ge1$ and $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$, there is a continuous homomorphism $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ with $\rho(g)\ne1$. Its superdiagonal entries $a_j=\rho(\cdot)_{j,j+1}\in H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)$ can be taken with $a_j\cup a_{j+1}=0$ for all $j$.
2. $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property for every $n\ge1$.

For $s=1$ this is the theorem for [Demushkin groups](kernel-unipotent-property-for-demushkin-groups.md) of rank at least $3$, Mináč and Tân's theorem for free pro-$p$ groups [@minactan2015kernel, Theorem 2.6(a), p. 7], or the trivial case of $\mathbb Z/2$.

**Corollaries.**

1. *Cyclotomic extensions.* For $G$ as in the theorem, every orientation $\theta:G\to1+p\mathbb Z_p$ and every abelian pro-$p$ group $A$, the group $A\rtimes_\theta G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property for every $n$. This is the theorem combined with [cyclotomic semidirect products](cyclotomic-semidirect-products-preserve-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md).
2. *Fields.* Let $G_0$ be a free pro-$p$ product of finitely many oriented pro-$p$ groups, each of which is a finitely generated free pro-$p$ group with any orientation, $G_L(p)$ with its cyclotomic character for a finite extension $L/\mathbb Q_p$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity, or, for $p=2$, $\mathbb Z/2$ with the orientation of image $\{\pm1\}$. Give $G_0$ the induced orientation $\theta$, and let $m\ge0$. Then $\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes_\theta G_0$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property for every $n$, and it is isomorphic, as an oriented group, to $(G_K(p),\theta_K)$ for some field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity. So the [kernel-unipotent conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md) holds, for every $n$, for every field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity whose $G_K(p)$ is isomorphic to such a group.

*Proof of Corollary 2.* Each $G_L(p)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $[L:\mathbb Q_p]+2\ge3$ ([maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md)), so $G_0$ is a free product of basic groups. The theorem gives the property for $G_0$, and Corollary 1 with $A=\mathbb Z_p^m$ gives it for $\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes_\theta G_0$. For realizability, Quadrelli states that the oriented groups obtained from these building blocks by free pro-$p$ products and cyclotomic semidirect products with $\mathbb Z_p$ all occur as maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups with their cyclotomic characters, because realizability is preserved by both operations [@quadrelli2024, Definition 3.4, Conjecture 3.6 and the paragraph after it, p. 13]. The group $G_0$ is obtained by free products, and $\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes_\theta G_0$ from $G_0$ by $m$ cyclotomic semidirect products with $\mathbb Z_p$, as in the proof of Corollary 3 of [the semidirect product entry](cyclotomic-semidirect-products-preserve-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md). $\square$

*New cases.* Suppose that $s\ge2$ and that some factor $G_1$ is of type (D). Write $H^i(\cdot)=H^i(\cdot,\mathbb F_p)$. Restriction to the factors is an isomorphism $H^2(G)\to\bigoplus_iH^2(G_i)$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Proposition 5.3 and Example 2.6(a), pp. 22 and 7], so $\dim H^2(G)$ is the number $e$ of factors of types (D) and (Z), as $H^2$ of a free pro-$p$ group is $0$ and $H^2(\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb F_2)\cong\mathbb F_2$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, (5.2), p. 21]. Also $\dim H^1(G)=\sum_id_i$, by the universal property.

- $G$ is not free, as $e\ge1$.
- $G$ is not Demushkin. If $e\ge2$, then $\dim H^2(G)\ne1$. If $e=1$, the factors other than $G_1$ are free, and there is one since $s\ge2$. Take a nonzero $a\in H^1(G)$ vanishing on every factor except a free one, $G_2$ say. For $c\in H^1(G)$, the restriction of $a\cup c$ to $G_i$ is $0$ for $i\ne2$, since $a$ restricts to $0$ there, and lies in $H^2(G_2)=0$ for $i=2$. So $a\cup c=0$ for all $c$, and the cup product is degenerate.
- $G$ is not of the form $\tilde G=\mathbb Z_p^{m}\rtimes_\theta G'$ with $m\ge1$, for any oriented pro-$p$ group $(G',\theta)$. Write such a group as $\mathbb Z_p\rtimes G''$ with $G''=\mathbb Z_p^{m-1}\rtimes_\theta G'$. The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence of $1\to\mathbb Z_p\to\tilde G\to G''\to1$ has two nonzero rows, and its differentials vanish because the extension splits, as computed in [the semidirect product entry](cyclotomic-semidirect-products-preserve-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md) (New cases) and in the proposition of [the elementary type entry](elementary-type-conjecture.md). So $\dim H^1(\tilde G)=\dim H^1(G'')+1$ and $\dim H^2(\tilde G)\ge\dim H^1(G'')=\dim H^1(\tilde G)-1$. For $G$, on the other hand, $\dim H^1(G)-\dim H^2(G)$ is the sum of $d_i-1$ over the factors of types (D) and (Z) and of $d_i$ over those of type (F), which is at least $d_1-1\ge2$. So $\dim H^2(G)\le\dim H^1(G)-2$.

So for $n\ge4$ the fields $K$ with $G_K(p)\cong G$ are not covered by the cases recorded in [the conjecture's entry](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md), which are free and rank-two Demushkin groups and, for odd $p$, $p$-rigid fields, whose finitely generated maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups are $\mathbb Z_p^m$ or $\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes\mathbb Z_p$ ([$p$-rigid fields](p-rigid-fields.md)); these are trivial or of the form excluded in the third bullet, with $G'$ trivial or $\mathbb Z_p$. Nor are they covered by the [Demushkin theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-demushkin-groups.md), by Corollary 3 of [the semidirect product entry](cyclotomic-semidirect-products-preserve-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md), or by the products of copies of $\mathbb Z/2$ in the sources below. The case $n=3$ is Efrat's, for all fields.

**Proof of the theorem.**

*Notation.* For each $i$ fix a minimal generating set $x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,d_i}$ of $G_i$: a basis for type (F), the generator for type (Z), and the generators of a one-relator minimal presentation for type (D), as in the proof of the [Demushkin theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-demushkin-groups.md). Let $V_i$ be the $\mathbb F_p$-space with basis $X_{i,1},\ldots,X_{i,d_i}$, $V=\bigoplus_iV_i$, and $T=\bigoplus_mT_m$ the free algebra on all $X_{i,k}$, graded by degree, so $T_m=V^{\otimes m}$. Put $U_i=V_i^*$ and $U=V^*=\bigoplus_iU_i$, and pair $T_m$ with $U^{\otimes m}$ by $\langle y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes y_m,a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m\rangle=\prod_jy_j(a_j)$. For $a\in U$ let $a^{(i)}\in U_i$ be its component.

Identify $U_i$ with $H^1(G_i)=\mathrm{Hom}(G_i,\mathbb F_p)$ by $a(X_{i,k})=a(x_{i,k})$. This is injective since the $x_{i,k}$ generate $G_i$, and onto: for types (F) and (Z) every choice of values extends to a homomorphism, and for type (D) because $H^1(G_i)\cong(G_i/(G_i)_{(2)})^*$ has dimension $d_i$ for a minimal presentation [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, (4.2), p. 9]. By the universal property, restriction identifies $H^1(G)$ with $\bigoplus_iH^1(G_i)$, hence with $U$, and then $a(x_{i,k})=a(X_{i,k})$ for $a\in U$.

For each $i$ define a bilinear form $\omega_i$ on $U_i$. For type (D), $\omega_i(a,b)=a\cup b\in H^2(G_i)$, for a fixed isomorphism $H^2(G_i)\cong\mathbb F_p$; it is nondegenerate, $d_i\ge3$, and it satisfies hypothesis (H) of [the chain theorem](isotropic-chains-detect-the-quadratic-algebra-of-a-nondegenerate-form.md), as checked in the proof of the Demushkin theorem. For type (F), $\omega_i=0$. For type (Z), $\omega_i(a,b)=a(x_{i,1})\,b(x_{i,1})$. Let $\xi_i\in V_i\otimes V_i\subseteq T_2$ be the tensor of $\omega_i$, so $\langle\xi_i,a\otimes b\rangle=\omega_i(a,b)$ for $a,b\in U_i$. Since $\xi_i\in V_i\otimes V_i$, for all $a,b\in U$
$$\langle\xi_i,a\otimes b\rangle=\omega_i(a^{(i)},b^{(i)}).$$
Thus $\xi_i=0$ for type (F), and $\xi_i=X_{i,1}^2$ for type (Z). Let $(\xi)$ be the two-sided ideal of $T$ generated by $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_s$, and $(\xi)_m=(\xi)\cap T_m$.

A *chain* of length $m$ is a sequence $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ in $U$ with $\omega_i(a_j^{(i)},a_{j+1}^{(i)})=0$ for all $i$ and all $1\le j<m$. Let $C_m\subseteq U^{\otimes m}$ be the span of the tensors $a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m$ of chains, and, for a subspace $C$ of $U^{\otimes m}$, let $C^\perp\subseteq T_m$ be its annihilator.

*Chains and cup products.* This paragraph is used only for the last sentence of part 1. By the isomorphism $H^2(G)\to\bigoplus_iH^2(G_i)$ quoted under New cases, $a\cup b=0$ in $H^2(G)$ exactly when $a^{(i)}\cup b^{(i)}=0$ in $H^2(G_i)$ for every $i$, because restriction is a ring homomorphism. This is automatic for type (F). For type (Z), $H^\bullet(\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb F_2)$ is the polynomial algebra on the nonzero class $e$ of degree $1$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, (5.2), p. 21], so $e\cup e\ne0$ and $a^{(i)}\cup b^{(i)}=0$ exactly when $\omega_i(a^{(i)},b^{(i)})=0$. So the chains are exactly the sequences with zero adjacent cup products in $H^2(G)$.

*Step 1: chains give representations.* Let $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ be a chain. For each $i$ we build a continuous homomorphism $\rho_i:G_i\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ with $\rho_i(h)_{j,j+1}=a_j(h)$ for $h\in G_i$.

- Type (D). The sequence $(a_1^{(i)},\ldots,a_m^{(i)})$ has zero adjacent cup products in $H^2(G_i)$. Step 1 of the proof of the [Demushkin theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-demushkin-groups.md), applied to the Demushkin group $G_i$, gives $\rho_i$.
- Type (F). Set $\rho_i(x_{i,k})=1+\sum_ja_j(x_{i,k})E_{j,j+1}$, where $E_{j,l}$ are the matrix units, and extend to $G_i$ by freeness.
- Type (Z), $p=2$. Put $x=x_{i,1}$ and $N=\sum_ja_j(x)E_{j,j+1}$. Then $N^2=\sum_ja_j(x)a_{j+1}(x)E_{j,j+2}=\sum_j\omega_i(a_j^{(i)},a_{j+1}^{(i)})E_{j,j+2}=0$, so $(1+N)^2=1+2N+N^2=1$. Set $\rho_i(x)=1+N$.

For unitriangular matrices, $(MM')_{j,j+1}=M_{j,j+1}+M'_{j,j+1}$, so each $h\mapsto\rho_i(h)_{j,j+1}$ is a homomorphism $G_i\to\mathbb F_p$. In types (F) and (Z) it agrees with $a_j$ on the generators, so equals $a_j$ on $G_i$. By the universal property there is a continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ restricting to $\rho_i$ on each $G_i$. Then $g\mapsto\rho(g)_{j,j+1}$ is a homomorphism $G\to\mathbb F_p$ that agrees with $a_j$ on every $G_i$, so it equals $a_j$.

*Step 2: a finite quotient.* Fix $m\ge1$. Let $S$ be the free pro-$p$ group on symbols $s_{i,k}$, mapped onto $G$ by $s_{i,k}\mapsto x_{i,k}$; this is onto because the $x_{i,k}$ generate the $G_i$, which generate $G$. As in Step 2 of the proof of the Demushkin theorem, surjections of pro-$p$ groups map Zassenhaus filtrations onto Zassenhaus filtrations, and $S_{(k)}/S_{(k+1)}$ embeds in $T_k$ by taking the degree-$k$ part of the Magnus expansion [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, (3.3) and the paragraph after it, p. 8]. So $P=G/G_{(m+1)}$ is a finite $p$-group with $P_{(k)}=G_{(k)}/G_{(m+1)}$ for $k\le m+1$. Write $\bar g$ and $\bar x_{i,k}$ for images in $P$, and $I$ for the augmentation ideal of $\mathbb F_p[P]$. By Jennings' theorem, if $y\in P_{(k)}\setminus P_{(k+1)}$ then $y-1\in I^k\setminus I^{k+1}$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 3.4, p. 9]. As there, the algebra homomorphism
$$\mu:T\to\bigoplus_{k\ge0}I^k/I^{k+1},\qquad X_{i,k}\mapsto(\bar x_{i,k}-1)+I^2,$$
is onto, because the $\bar x_{i,k}$ generate $P$. Let $K=\ker\mu$ and $K_k=K\cap T_k$.

*Step 3: chains vanish on $K$.* Let $k\le m$ and let $\rho:G\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ be continuous with superdiagonal entries $a_1,\ldots,a_k\in U$. It kills $G_{(k+1)}\supseteq G_{(m+1)}$ [@minactan2015kernel, Lemma 2.5(b), p. 6], so it factors through $P$; let $\tilde\rho:\mathbb F_p[P]\to M_{k+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ be its linear extension. Then $\tilde\rho(I)$ consists of strictly upper triangular matrices, so $\tilde\rho(I^{k+1})=0$, and $\tilde\rho(I^k)$ is supported on the entry $(1,k+1)$. That entry of a product of $k$ strictly upper triangular matrices of size $k+1$ is the product of their entries in places $(1,2),\ldots,(k,k+1)$, so the $(1,k+1)$ entry of $\tilde\rho\bigl((\bar x_{i_1,l_1}-1)\cdots(\bar x_{i_k,l_k}-1)\bigr)$ is $\prod_ja_j(x_{i_j,l_j})=\langle X_{i_1,l_1}\cdots X_{i_k,l_k},a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_k\rangle$. Hence $t\mapsto\langle t,a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_k\rangle$ factors through $\mu$ on $T_k$ and vanishes on $K_k$. With Step 1, $C_k\subseteq K_k^\perp$.

*Step 4: $(\xi)_k\subseteq K_k$ for $k\le m$.* As $K$ is an ideal, it suffices that $\mu(\xi_i)=0$ when $m\ge2$. For type (F), $\xi_i=0$. For type (Z), $\bar x_{i,1}^2=1$ in $P$, so $(\bar x_{i,1}-1)^2=2(1-\bar x_{i,1})=0$ in $\mathbb F_2[P]$. For type (D), let $P_i=G_i/(G_i)_{(m+1)}$, with augmentation ideal $I_i$, and $\mu_i:T(V_i)\to\bigoplus_kI_i^k/I_i^{k+1}$ the map of Step 2 for $G_i$. Step 4 of the proof of the Demushkin theorem gives $\mu_i(\xi_i)=0$, that is, $\xi_i$ evaluated at $X_{i,l}\mapsto\bar x_{i,l}-1$ lies in $I_i^3$. The inclusion $G_i\to G$ maps $(G_i)_{(m+1)}$ into $G_{(m+1)}$, so it induces $P_i\to P$, and the induced algebra map $\mathbb F_p[P_i]\to\mathbb F_p[P]$ sends $I_i^3$ into $I^3$ and $\bar x_{i,l}-1$ to $\bar x_{i,l}-1$. Hence $\mu(\xi_i)=0$.

*Step 5: $C_m^\perp=(\xi)_m$ for every $m\ge1$.* For $\supseteq$: if $w,w'$ are words and $\xi_i$ occupies places $j,j+1$ of $w\xi_iw'\in T_m$, then for a chain $\langle w\xi_iw',a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m\rangle$ contains the factor $\omega_i(a_j^{(i)},a_{j+1}^{(i)})=0$.

For $\subseteq$, sort tensors by colours. For $c=(c_1,\ldots,c_m)\in\{1,\ldots,s\}^m$ put $V_c=V_{c_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{c_m}\subseteq T_m$ and $U_c=U_{c_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{c_m}$. Then $T_m=\bigoplus_cV_c$, $U^{\otimes m}=\bigoplus_cU_c$, and $V_c$ pairs to zero with $U_{c'}$ for $c\ne c'$. Write $h_c$ and $t_c$ for the components of $h\in T_m$ and $t\in U^{\otimes m}$; thus $\langle h_c,t\rangle=\langle h,t_c\rangle$.

- (i) *Components of chains are chains.* If $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ is a chain, then $t_c=a_1^{(c_1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m^{(c_m)}$, viewing $a_j^{(c_j)}\in U_{c_j}\subseteq U$, and $(a_1^{(c_1)},\ldots,a_m^{(c_m)})$ is a chain. Indeed, if $c_j=c_{j+1}=i$, the only nonzero components of the pair are $a_j^{(i)},a_{j+1}^{(i)}$, and $\omega_i$ vanishes on them; if $c_j\ne c_{j+1}$, then for each $i$ one of the two vectors has zero $i$-component. So if $h\in C_m^\perp$, then every $h_c\in C_m^\perp$, because $\langle h_c,t\rangle=\langle h,t_c\rangle=0$ for every chain tensor $t$.
- (ii) *The ideal splits by colours.* $(\xi)_m$ is spanned by the elements $w\xi_iw'$ with $w,w'$ words, each of which lies in a single $V_c$. So $(\xi)_m=\bigoplus_cE_c$, where $E_c$ is the span of those lying in $V_c$.
- (iii) *Runs.* Fix $c$, and split $\{1,\ldots,m\}$ into maximal intervals $J_1,\ldots,J_r$, in order, on which $c$ is constant, with value $\kappa_q$ and length $\ell_q$ on $J_q$. Then $V_c=W_1\otimes\cdots\otimes W_r$ with $W_q=V_{\kappa_q}^{\otimes\ell_q}$, and $U_c=W_1^*\otimes\cdots\otimes W_r^*$. In an element $w\xi_iw'$ of $V_c$, the two places of $\xi_i$ carry the colour $i$, so they are adjacent places in one run $J_q$ with $\kappa_q=i$. Hence
$$E_c=\sum_{q=1}^rW_1\otimes\cdots\otimes N_q\otimes\cdots\otimes W_r,$$
where $N_q\subseteq W_q$ is the degree-$\ell_q$ part of the two-sided ideal of $T(V_{\kappa_q})$ generated by $\xi_{\kappa_q}$, which is $0$ if $\ell_q=1$.
- (iv) *Annihilators of such sums.* For subspaces $N_q\subseteq W_q$ of finite-dimensional spaces, the annihilator of $\sum_qW_1\otimes\cdots\otimes N_q\otimes\cdots\otimes W_r$ in $W_1^*\otimes\cdots\otimes W_r^*$ is $N_1^\perp\otimes\cdots\otimes N_r^\perp$. Indeed, it is $\bigcap_qW_1^*\otimes\cdots\otimes N_q^\perp\otimes\cdots\otimes W_r^*$; choose for each $q$ a basis of $W_q^*$ extending one of $N_q^\perp$, and note that each space in the intersection, and the intersection itself, is spanned by the product basis vectors it contains. By duality, $E_c$ consists of the $h\in V_c$ that annihilate $N_1^\perp\otimes\cdots\otimes N_r^\perp$.
- (v) *Factor chains.* For each $q$, $N_q^\perp\subseteq U_{\kappa_q}^{\otimes\ell_q}$ is spanned by tensors of sequences $(b_1,\ldots,b_{\ell_q})$ in $U_{\kappa_q}$ with $\omega_{\kappa_q}(b_j,b_{j+1})=0$ for all $j$. For type (D) this is part 1 of [the chain theorem](isotropic-chains-detect-the-quadratic-algebra-of-a-nondegenerate-form.md). For type (F), $N_q=0$, and $N_q^\perp$ is spanned by pure tensors, all of which qualify since $\omega=0$. For type (Z), $U_{\kappa_q}$ is spanned by $e$ with $e(x_{\kappa_q,1})=1$; if $\ell_q=1$ then $N_q^\perp=U_{\kappa_q}$ is spanned by $(e)$, and if $\ell_q\ge2$ then $N_q$ contains the only word $X_{\kappa_q,1}^{\ell_q}=X_{\kappa_q,1}^{\ell_q-2}\xi_{\kappa_q}$, so $N_q^\perp=0$.
- (vi) *Concatenation.* Concatenating such sequences for $q=1,\ldots,r$ gives a sequence in $U$ whose tensor is the tensor product of theirs, and it is a chain: inside a run the condition for $\omega_{\kappa_q}$ holds and all other components vanish, and at the junction of two runs the two vectors lie in $U_{\kappa_q}$ and $U_{\kappa_{q+1}}$ with $\kappa_q\ne\kappa_{q+1}$, so for each $i$ one of them has zero $i$-component. Hence $N_1^\perp\otimes\cdots\otimes N_r^\perp\subseteq C_m$.

Now let $h\in C_m^\perp$. By (i), each $h_c$ lies in $V_c\cap C_m^\perp$, so it annihilates $N_1^\perp\otimes\cdots\otimes N_r^\perp$ by (vi), and lies in $E_c$ by (iv). By (ii), $h=\sum_ch_c\in(\xi)_m$.

*Step 6: conclusion.* Let $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$. Then $\bar g\in P_{(m)}\setminus P_{(m+1)}$, so $\bar g-1\in I^m\setminus I^{m+1}$ by Step 2. Choose $t\in T_m$ with $\mu(t)=(\bar g-1)+I^{m+1}\ne0$; then $t\notin K_m$. Steps 3, 4 and 5 give $(\xi)_m\subseteq K_m\subseteq C_m^\perp=(\xi)_m$, so $K_m=C_m^\perp$, and some chain $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ has $\langle t,a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m\rangle\ne0$. For the representation $\rho$ of Step 1, Step 3 shows that the $(1,m+1)$ entry of $\rho(g)-1=\tilde\rho(\bar g-1)$ is this nonzero number. So $\rho(g)\ne1$, proving part 1.

For part 2, let $g\notin G_{(n)}$ and let $m$ be largest with $g\in G_{(m)}$, so $1\le m<n$. Compose the representation of part 1 with the block embedding $U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)$ in the upper left corner. With the inclusion $\subseteq$ from the setting, this proves part 2. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The graded group algebra.* The proof gives $T_m/(\xi)_m\cong I^m/I^{m+1}$ for $\mathbb F_p[G/G_{(m+1)}]$, and $T/(\xi)$ is the free product of the graded algebras $T(V_i)/(\xi_i)$. This agrees with Mináč, Pasini, Quadrelli and Tân's description of the graded group algebra of a free pro-$p$ product as the free product of those of the factors [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Proposition 5.4, p. 23], and of a Demushkin group as the quotient by the initial form of its relator [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Theorem 5.2 and (5.4), p. 22]. The proof does not use these descriptions.
- *Strong Massey vanishing.* By the paragraph on cup products, Step 1 shows that for these $G$ every sequence of classes in $H^1(G)$ with zero adjacent cup products is the superdiagonal of a unipotent representation, in every length.
- *Rank-two Demushkin factors.* They are excluded. The chain theorem fails over $\mathbb F_p$ in dimension $2$ (its entry, "Rank two fails over finite fields"), so the linear algebra of Step 5 breaks down for a long run in such a factor. A rank-two Demushkin group itself has the property, by a different argument of Mináč and Tân [@minactan2015kernel, §1, p. 3], but that argument concerns the group alone. Whether free products with a rank-two Demushkin factor have the property is not decided here.
- *What is not covered.* Free products of groups that are themselves cyclotomic semidirect products $\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes G'$ with $m\ge1$ are not treated. Their cup product pairings are degenerate in a way that this argument does not handle, so the general group of [elementary type](elementary-type-conjecture.md) is not covered.

**Sources and status.** Hamza, Maire, Mináč and Tân prove the property for the free pro-$2$ product of three copies of $\mathbb Z/2$, by choosing for each word a representation with nilpotent superdiagonal matrices, and remark that the argument extends to the pro-$2$ groups they call SAP groups [@hamzamaireminactan2025, Lemma 4.8 and Remark 4.9, p. 24]. The theorem above, with free and Demushkin factors, is proved here from the cited inputs. Mináč, Tân and Tra's 2025 preprint lists as known cases free pro-$p$ groups, odd rigid fields, Demushkin groups of rank $2$, and, for $n=4$, finitely generated Demushkin groups [@minactantra2025, §1, p. 2]; its introduction mentions no result on free products. A search through November 2026 found no statement of the theorem, but it was not exhaustive.
