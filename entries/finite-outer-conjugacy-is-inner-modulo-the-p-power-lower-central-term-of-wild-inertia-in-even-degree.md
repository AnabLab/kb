**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the theorem modulo the $p$th lower central term](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-the-pth-lower-central-term-of-wild-inertia-in-even-degree.md), with its weights. So $p$ is odd, $d=[K:\mathbb Q_p]$ is even, hence $d\ge4$, $G=G_K$, $P$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, $P_j$ is the lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$, $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$, and $V=H/pH=L\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d$. The complementary indices are $1$ and $2$, the surface pairs are $(a_i,b_i)=(x_{2i+1},x_{2i+2})$ for $1\le i\le r=d/2-1$, $p^s$ is the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, and $\Lambda_s=(\mathbb Z/p^s)[[\Gamma]]$. A weight datum $\ell=(W;m_1,\dots,m_r)\in\mathbb Z^{1+r}$ gives the letters the weights $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(0)=\mathrm{wt}_\ell(1)=W$, $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(2)=0$, $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(a_i)=m_i$, $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(b_i)=W-m_i$. For a letter $\kappa$ put $N(\kappa)=1$ if $\kappa\in\{0,1,b_1,\dots,b_r\}$ and $N(\kappa)=0$ otherwise, and let $\varepsilon_i(\kappa)$ be $1$ if $\kappa=a_i$, $-1$ if $\kappa=b_i$, and $0$ otherwise.

*Multidegrees.* A multidegree of degree $n$ is $\mathbf m=(n_0,\dots,n_d)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{d+1}$ with $\sum_kn_k=n$; a word $w$ of length $n$ in the letters $0,\dots,d$ has the multidegree whose entry $n_k$ is the number $n_k(w)$ of its slots with letter $k$. Put $N(\mathbf m)=n_0+n_1+\sum_in_{b_i}$ and $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(\mathbf m)=\sum_kn_k\,\mathrm{wt}_\ell(k)=W\,N(\mathbf m)+\sum_im_i(n_{a_i}-n_{b_i})$. Let $\mathbf 2$ be the multidegree with $n_2=n$, and $\mathbf{12}$ the one with $n_1=1$ and $n_2=n-1$. By (M2) of [the higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md), applied to the decomposition of $V$ into its summands, $\mathcal L_n(V)=\bigoplus_{\mathbf m}\mathcal L_n(V)^{\mathbf m}$, where $\mathcal L_n(V)^{\mathbf m}$ consists of the elements whose components all lie in words of multidegree $\mathbf m$. These parts are $\Gamma$-stable.

*Coefficients modulo $p^s$.* Let $A=H/p^sH$, and let $\xi_k$ be the class of $x_k$. By step 5 of the proof of [the similitude theorem](similitude-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md), $A=A_0\oplus A_1\oplus\cdots\oplus A_d$, where $A_k$ is the closed $\Lambda_s$-submodule generated by $\xi_k$, $A_0\cong\mathbb Z/p^s$, and $A_k$ is free over $\Lambda_s$ on $\xi_k$ for $k\ge1$. Each $A_k$ is $\Gamma$-stable, and $A_k$ maps onto $V_k$, and $A_0$ onto $L$, under $A\to A/pA=V$. Put $M_s=\mathcal L_n/p^s\mathcal L_n$ for the relevant $n$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]$ even, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, and $s\ge2$, that is, $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$ contains the $p^2$-th roots of unity.

- (a) Let $2\le n\le p^{s-1}-1$, and let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many outer conjugates and induce the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$. Then $c_h\circ\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$ for some $h\in P_n$.
- (b) Let $3\le n\le p^{s-1}-1$, and let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many outer conjugates and induce the identity on $G/P_n$. Then $\mathcal J_n(\alpha)=0$, that is, $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$ for every $x\in P$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Under the same hypotheses, every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha'$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha'$ that induces the identity on $G/P_{p^{s-1}}$. Two such $\alpha'$ differ by $c_k$ with $k\in P_{p^{s-1}}$. So every outer class with finitely many conjugates lies in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_{p^{s-1}})$.

For $s=2$ these are the theorem and corollary of the theorem modulo the $p$th lower central term.

*Examples.* Let $t\ge2$, let $K_0\subseteq\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^t})$ be the fixed field of $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{-1}$, and let $K=K_0\mathbb Q_{p^2}$, with $\mathbb Q_{p^2}$ the unramified quadratic extension. As $K_0$ is totally ramified of degree $p^{t-1}(p-1)/2$, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=p^{t-1}(p-1)$, which is even. The automorphism $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{-1}$ moves $\zeta_p$, so $\zeta_p\notin K_0$ and $K_0(\zeta_p)=\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^t})$. If $\zeta_p\in K$, then $K$ would contain $\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^t})\mathbb Q_{p^2}$, of degree $2p^{t-1}(p-1)>[K:\mathbb Q_p]$. So $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Also $K(\zeta_{p^t})=K(\zeta_p)$ has degree $2$ over $K$, which is prime to $p$, so it is tamely ramified and lies in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$. Hence $s\ge t$, and the corollary shows that finite-conjugacy outer classes of $G_K$ are trivial in $\operatorname{Out}(G_K/P_{p^{s-1}})$, hence in $\operatorname{Out}(G_K/P_{p^{t-1}})$, as $P_{p^{s-1}}\subseteq P_{p^{t-1}}$. So the level is unbounded over such fields.

**Lemma A (multidegrees modulo $p^s$; proved here).** Let $n\ge1$. Let $\hat T_n(A)=\varprojlim_UT_n(A/U)$, with $U$ running over the open submodules of $A$ and $T_n(X)=X^{\otimes n}$ over $\mathbb Z/p^s$, with the diagonal action of $\Gamma$. There is a continuous injective $\Gamma$-equivariant homomorphism $\iota_s:M_s\to\hat T_n(A)$ with the following properties.

- (A1) $\iota_s$ maps the class of $[\cdots[y_1,y_2],\dots,y_n]$, for $y_t\in P$, to the tensor bracket $[\cdots[\bar y_1,\bar y_2],\dots,\bar y_n]$, where $\bar y_t\in A$ is the class of $y_t$ and $[u,v]=uv-vu$. Its image is the closed span of the tensor brackets of elements of $A$.
- (A2) For every tame-trivial $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, $\iota_s\theta_n=\theta_A^{\otimes n}\iota_s$, where $\theta_A$ is the action of $\theta$ on $A$ and $\theta_n$ also denotes the action induced on $M_s$ by its action on $\mathcal L_n$.
- (A3) $\hat T_n(A)=\bigoplus_{\mathbf m}\hat T^{\mathbf m}$, where $\hat T^{\mathbf m}$ is the limit of the sums of the tensor products $A_{w_1}/U_{w_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{w_n}/U_{w_n}$ over the words $w$ of multidegree $\mathbf m$, for $U=\bigoplus_kU_k$ with $U_k\subseteq A_k$ open $\Lambda_s$-submodules. With $M_s^{\mathbf m}=\iota_s^{-1}(\hat T^{\mathbf m})$, $M_s=\bigoplus_{\mathbf m}M_s^{\mathbf m}$, each $M_s^{\mathbf m}$ is closed and $\Gamma$-stable, $\iota_s(M_s^{\mathbf m})$ is the closed span of the brackets of elements $a_t\in A_{k_t}$ with $(k_1,\dots,k_n)$ of multidegree $\mathbf m$, and reduction modulo $p$ maps $M_s^{\mathbf m}$ onto $\mathcal L_n(V)^{\mathbf m}$.
- (A4) If $\theta$ is tame-trivial and $\theta_A$ multiplies each $A_k$ by $c_k\in\mathbb Z_p$, then $\theta_n$ acts on $M_s^{\mathbf m}$ as multiplication by $\prod_kc_k^{n_k}$.
- (A5) For $0\le k\le s$, $M_s^{\mathbf m}\cap p^kM_s=p^kM_s^{\mathbf m}$, and every element of $M_s^{\mathbf m}$ killed by $p^k$ lies in $p^{s-k}M_s^{\mathbf m}$.

*Proof.* *The map.* Choose a free basis of $P$ converging to $1$, and for finite subsets $S$ of it let $F(S)$ be as in the lower central input of [the boundary-twist theorem](boundary-twists-exclude-finite-outer-conjugacy-of-wild-scalars.md), with $H_S=F(S)/F(S)_2\cong\mathbb Z_p^S$. There, by an argument that does not depend on the degree and that the higher Johnson theorem uses in every degree, $\mathcal L_j\cong\varprojlim_SF(S)_j/F(S)_{j+1}$; in finite rank $F(S)_n/F(S)_{n+1}$ is the degree-$n$ part of the free Lie algebra over $\mathbb Z_p$ on $H_S$, inside $T_n(H_S)$ over $\mathbb Z_p$, with commutator classes corresponding to tensor brackets; and through the same maps reduced modulo $p$, $\mathcal L_n/p$ is identified with the completed free Lie space $\mathcal L_n(V)$ inside $V^{\hat\otimes n}$. So these inclusions give a continuous homomorphism $\iota:\mathcal L_n\to\varprojlim_ST_n(H_S)$ that maps commutator classes to tensor brackets and whose reduction modulo $p$ is injective. If every coordinate of $\iota(x)$ lies in $p^kT_n(H_S)$, then $x\in p^k\mathcal L_n$: for $k=1$ this is the injectivity modulo $p$; for $k\ge2$ it gives $x=px'$, and then every coordinate of $\iota(x')$ lies in $p^{k-1}T_n(H_S)$, as $T_n(H_S)$ is torsion-free, so induct. Hence $\iota$ induces an injection $M_s\to\varprojlim_ST_n(H_S/p^s)$. By the lower central input with $j=1$, $H=\varprojlim_SH_S$, so the kernels of the maps $A\to H_S/p^s$ are open submodules with intersection $0$, and by compactness every open submodule of $A$ contains one of them. So $\varprojlim_ST_n(H_S/p^s)=\hat T_n(A)$, and this defines $\iota_s$.

(A1) The first assertion holds by construction. The image of $\iota_s$ is compact and contains these brackets; as $A$ is the image of $P$ and $\mathcal L_n$ is topologically generated by commutator classes, the image is the closed span of the brackets.

(A2) Both maps are continuous. On the class of $[\cdots[y_1,y_2],\dots,y_n]$ they agree, since $\theta$ maps it to the class of $[\cdots[\theta y_1,\theta y_2],\dots,\theta y_n]$ and $\theta_A$ is induced by $\theta$. These classes span a dense subgroup. The same argument with conjugation by elements of $G$ shows that $\iota_s$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant.

(A3) Every open submodule $U'$ of $A$ contains $\bigoplus_k(U'\cap A_k)$, and an open subgroup contains an open $\Lambda_s$-submodule, so the $U$ of the stated form are cofinal, and $\hat T_n(A)$ is the limit over them. For such $U$, $T_n(A/U)$ is the direct sum over the words $w$ of the stated tensor products, compatibly with the transition maps, and $\Gamma$ preserves each summand. So $\hat T_n(A)=\bigoplus_{\mathbf m}\hat T^{\mathbf m}$, with $\Gamma$-stable closed summands. A bracket of elements $a_t\in A_{k_t}$ is a signed sum of the tensors $a_{\pi(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{\pi(n)}$, so it lies in $\hat T^{\mathbf m}$ for the multidegree $\mathbf m$ of $(k_1,\dots,k_n)$. Let $B_{\mathbf m}$ be the closed span of these brackets. Since the bracket is multilinear and $A=\bigoplus_kA_k$, every bracket of elements of $A$ is a finite sum of such brackets, so the image of $\iota_s$ lies in the closed subgroup $\sum_{\mathbf m}B_{\mathbf m}$, which it contains. As $B_{\mathbf m}\subseteq\hat T^{\mathbf m}$, the image is $\bigoplus_{\mathbf m}B_{\mathbf m}$ and $B_{\mathbf m}$ is its intersection with $\hat T^{\mathbf m}$. This gives $M_s=\bigoplus_{\mathbf m}M_s^{\mathbf m}$ with $\iota_s(M_s^{\mathbf m})=B_{\mathbf m}$. The map $\hat T_n(A)\to V^{\hat\otimes n}$ induced by $A\to V$ maps $\hat T^{\mathbf m}$ into the components of multidegree $\mathbf m$, and its composite with $\iota_s$ is the reduction $M_s\to\mathcal L_n/p=\mathcal L_n(V)\subseteq V^{\hat\otimes n}$, as both send commutator classes to the brackets of their classes in $V$. So reduction maps $M_s^{\mathbf m}$ into $\mathcal L_n(V)^{\mathbf m}$, and onto it, since $M_s\to\mathcal L_n(V)$ is onto and respects both decompositions.

(A4) By (A2), as $\theta_A^{\otimes n}$ acts on $\hat T^{\mathbf m}$ by $\prod_kc_k^{n_k}$.

(A5) If $x=p^ky\in M_s^{\mathbf m}$ with $y\in M_s$, then $x$ equals its $\mathbf m$-part, which is $p^k$ times the $\mathbf m$-part of $y$. If $x\in M_s^{\mathbf m}$ and $p^kx=0$, lift $x$ to $\tilde x\in\mathcal L_n$. Then $p^k\tilde x\in p^s\mathcal L_n$, so $\tilde x\in p^{s-k}\mathcal L_n$ as $\mathcal L_n$ is torsion-free, and $x\in p^{s-k}M_s\cap M_s^{\mathbf m}=p^{s-k}M_s^{\mathbf m}$. $\square$

For $Y\in M_s$ write $Y^{\mathbf m}$ for its part in $M_s^{\mathbf m}$, and for a continuous cocycle $c:\Gamma\to\mathcal L_n$ let $c_s$ be its reduction modulo $p^s$ and $c_s^{\mathbf m}$ the $\mathbf m$-part of $c_s$, a continuous cocycle $\Gamma\to M_s^{\mathbf m}$. By (A3), the reduction of $c_s^{\mathbf m}$ modulo $p$ is the $\mathbf m$-part of the reduction $\bar c:\Gamma\to\mathcal L_n(V)$ of $c$.

**Lemma B (division; proved here).** Let $n\ge2$, $0\le k\le s-1$, $\mathbf m$ a multidegree, and $c:\Gamma\to\mathcal L_n$ a continuous cocycle. If $p^kc_s^{\mathbf m}=\delta g$ for some $g\in M_s^{\mathbf m}$, where $\delta g(\rho)=(\rho-1)g$, then the $\mathbf m$-part of $\bar c$ is a coboundary.

*Proof.* If $k=0$, reduce $c_s^{\mathbf m}=\delta g$ modulo $p$. Let $k\ge1$. First, $\mathcal L_n/p^k\mathcal L_n$ has no nonzero point fixed by $\gamma$. Indeed, let $(\gamma-1)x\in p^k\mathcal L_n$. The image of $x$ in $\mathcal L_n/p=\mathcal L_n(V)$ is fixed by $\gamma$, so it is $0$ by Lemma 1 (c) of the higher Johnson theorem, as $n\ge2$; so $x=px'$, and $(\gamma-1)x'\in p^{k-1}\mathcal L_n$ as $\mathcal L_n$ is torsion-free; induct. Now $(\rho-1)g=p^kc_s^{\mathbf m}(\rho)\in p^kM_s$ for all $\rho$, so the image of $g$ in $M_s/p^kM_s=\mathcal L_n/p^k\mathcal L_n$ is fixed by $\gamma$, hence $0$. By (A5), $g=p^kg_1$ with $g_1\in M_s^{\mathbf m}$. Then $p^k(c_s^{\mathbf m}-\delta g_1)=0$, so by (A5) the cocycle $c_s^{\mathbf m}-\delta g_1$ takes values in $p^{s-k}M_s^{\mathbf m}\subseteq pM_s^{\mathbf m}$, as $k\le s-1$. Reducing modulo $p$, the $\mathbf m$-part of $\bar c$ is $\delta$ of the reduction of $g_1$. $\square$

*Valuations.* For $w\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, $(1+p)^w-1$ has $p$-adic valuation exactly $1+v_p(w)$. Indeed, $(1+p)^{p^j}=1+p^{j+1}a_j$ with $a_j\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$ by induction on $j$: in the expansion of $(1+p^{j+1}a)^p$, the linear term is $p^{j+2}a$, the terms $\binom pip^{i(j+1)}a^i$ with $2\le i\le p-1$ have valuation at least $2j+3$, and the last has valuation $p(j+1)\ge j+3$, as $p\ge3$. For $w=p^ju$ with $p\nmid u$, $(1+p^{j+1}a_j)^u\equiv1+up^{j+1}a_j\pmod{p^{2j+2}}$.

**Proof of part (a).** Let $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal S$ be as in Lemma 3 of [the higher cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-higher-cocycle-classes-of-wild-inertia.md) for this $n$, which applies as $n\ge2$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Fix $v\in\mathcal S$ and a continuous cocycle $c$ representing it. We show that $\bar c$ is a coboundary. By (M2), it suffices to show this for each $\mathbf m$-part.

*1. Weights.* Fix a weight datum $\ell$, and let $\theta$ and $D_n$ be as in Lemma 1 of the theorem modulo the $p$th lower central term, so $\theta$ comes from the similitude theorem with the parameters $(1+p)^W$ and $(1+p)^{m_i}$. As in the proof of part (a) there, $\theta_*^Nv=v$ for some $N\ge1$ by Lemma 3 (ii) of the higher cocycle theorem, $\theta_*=1+p(D_n)_*$ on the torsion-free group $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$, and Lemma 2 there gives $(D_n)_*v=0$, so $\theta_*v=v$. So $\theta_n\circ c=c+\delta h$ for some $h\in\mathcal L_n$. By the similitude theorem, $\theta_A$ multiplies $A_k$ by $(1+p)^{\mathrm{wt}_\ell(k)}$, so by (A4) $\theta_n$ acts on $M_s^{\mathbf m}$ by $\lambda_{\mathbf m}=(1+p)^{\mathrm{wt}_\ell(\mathbf m)}$. Reducing modulo $p^s$ and taking $\mathbf m$-parts, which commutes with $\delta$ as the projections are $\Gamma$-equivariant,
$$(\lambda_{\mathbf m}-1)\,c_s^{\mathbf m}=\delta(h_s^{\mathbf m}).$$
Suppose $w=\mathrm{wt}_\ell(\mathbf m)\ne0$ and $v_p(w)\le s-2$. By the valuations, $\lambda_{\mathbf m}-1=p^ku$ with $k=1+v_p(w)\le s-1$ and $u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$, so $p^kc_s^{\mathbf m}=\delta(u^{-1}h_s^{\mathbf m})$, and by Lemma B the $\mathbf m$-part of $\bar c$ is a coboundary.

*2. Which multidegrees.* Let $\mathbf m\ne\mathbf 2$ have degree $n$. If $N(\mathbf m)\ge1$, take $\ell=(1;0,\dots,0)$: then $w=N(\mathbf m)$ with $1\le w\le n<p^{s-1}$, so $v_p(w)\le s-2$. If $N(\mathbf m)=0$, then $n_0=n_1=n_{b_i}=0$ for all $i$, and as $\mathbf m\ne\mathbf 2$, some $n_{a_i}\ge1$; take $W=0$, $m_i=1$ and the other $m$'s $0$: then $w=n_{a_i}$ with $1\le w\le n$. By step 1, the $\mathbf m$-part of $\bar c$ is a coboundary for every $\mathbf m\ne\mathbf 2$.

*3. The multidegree $\mathbf 2$.* By step 5 of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), the transvection $T_1$ is tame-trivial and acts on $H$ by $1+N$ with $N\xi_2=\xi_1$ and $N\xi_i=0$ for $i\ne2$. As $T_1$ fixes $\mathcal G$, $N$ commutes with $\Gamma$. So on $A$, $N$ maps $\mu\xi_2$ to $\mu\xi_1$ for $\mu\in\Lambda_s$ and kills $A_k$ for $k\ne2$. Let $D_N$ and $D_{N'}$ be the continuous maps of $\hat T_n(A)$ that apply $N$, respectively $N':\mu\xi_1\mapsto\mu\xi_2$ (zero on $A_k$ for $k\ne1$), in one slot at a time and add the results. They are $\Gamma$-equivariant, and they preserve $\iota_s(M_s)$: a map $D$ of this form satisfies $D[u,v]=[Du,v]+[u,Dv]$, so by induction it maps brackets of elements of $A$ to sums of such brackets. Identify $M_s$ with its image under $\iota_s$.

By Lemma 3 (iii) of the higher cocycle theorem, $(T_1)_*v=v$, so $(T_1)_n\circ c=c+\delta h'$ with $h'\in\mathcal L_n$. By (A2), $(T_1)_n$ acts on $M_s$ by $(1+N)^{\otimes n}$, the sum over all sets $Q$ of slots of the map applying $N$ in the slots of $Q$. On the summand of a word $w$, the term for $Q$ vanishes unless $w$ has the letter $2$ at every slot of $Q$, and then it maps into the summand of the word obtained by changing these letters to $1$. A word of multidegree $\mathbf{12}$ arises in this way only from itself with $Q=\emptyset$, or from $(2,\dots,2)$ with $|Q|=1$. So the $\mathbf{12}$-part of $(1+N)^{\otimes n}Y$ is $Y^{\mathbf{12}}+D_NY^{\mathbf 2}$, and
$$D_N\,c_s^{\mathbf 2}=\delta\big(h_s'^{\mathbf{12}}\big).$$
On $\hat T^{\mathbf 2}$, $D_{N'}D_N$ is multiplication by $n$: after $N$ is applied in one slot, that slot is the only one with the letter $1$, and $N'$ restores it. Applying $D_{N'}$, which maps $\hat T^{\mathbf{12}}$ into $\hat T^{\mathbf 2}$, gives $nc_s^{\mathbf 2}=\delta(D_{N'}h_s'^{\mathbf{12}})$ with $D_{N'}h_s'^{\mathbf{12}}\in M_s^{\mathbf 2}$. Write $n=p^an'$ with $p\nmid n'$; as $n<p^{s-1}$, $a\le s-2$. Then $p^ac_s^{\mathbf 2}=\delta(n'^{-1}D_{N'}h_s'^{\mathbf{12}})$, and Lemma B shows that the $\mathbf 2$-part of $\bar c$ is a coboundary.

*4. Conclusion.* So $\bar c=\delta\bar k$ with $\bar k\in\mathcal L_n(V)$. Lift $\bar k$ to $k\in\mathcal L_n$. Then $c-\delta k$ takes values in $p\mathcal L_n$, and $p^{-1}(c-\delta k)$ is a continuous cocycle, as multiplication by $p$ is a homeomorphism of $\mathcal L_n$ onto $p\mathcal L_n$. So $v\in pH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$, and $v\in p\mathcal S$ by (u3) there. Hence $\mathcal S=p\mathcal S$. For $u=u(\alpha)\in\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal S$ this gives $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}(u)\in\bigcap_kp^k(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma=0$, so $u=0$ by the Cohomology paragraph there, and (u4) there gives $h$. $\square$

**Proof of part (b).** Let $\mathcal S_J$ be the set $\mathcal S$ of Lemma 5 of the higher Johnson theorem for this $n$, which applies as $n\ge2$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, and fix $J\in\mathcal S_J$. Fix a weight datum $\ell$ and $\theta$ as in step 1. The proof of part (b) of the theorem modulo the $p$th lower central term shows, by Lemma 5 (ii) of the higher Johnson theorem and Lemma 2 there, that $E(J)=0$ for the endomorphism $E$ with $\theta_nJ'\theta_H^{-1}=J'+pE(J')$; so $\theta_nJ=J\theta_H$.

*Weights.* Fix $k\in\{0,\dots,d\}$ and put $y=J(\xi_k)\in\mathcal L_n$, where now $\xi_k$ denotes the class of $x_k$ in $H$. As $\theta_H\xi_k-(1+p)^{\mathrm{wt}_\ell(k)}\xi_k\in p^sH$ and $J$ is continuous and $\mathbb Z_p$-linear, $\theta_ny-(1+p)^{\mathrm{wt}_\ell(k)}y\in p^s\mathcal L_n$. Let $y_s$ be the class of $y$ in $M_s$. By (A4), for every $\mathbf m$,
$$(1+p)^{\mathrm{wt}_\ell(k)}\big((1+p)^{f}-1\big)\,y_s^{\mathbf m}=0,\qquad f=\mathrm{wt}_\ell(\mathbf m)-\mathrm{wt}_\ell(k).$$
If $f\ne0$ and $v_p(f)\le s-2$, then $p^{1+v_p(f)}y_s^{\mathbf m}=0$ by the valuations, so $y_s^{\mathbf m}\in pM_s^{\mathbf m}$ by (A5), and by (A3) the $\mathbf m$-part of $\bar J(\bar x_k)$ is $0$.

*Which multidegrees survive.* Let $\mathbf m$ have degree $n$ and suppose the $\mathbf m$-part of $\bar J(\bar x_k)$ is nonzero. By the weights, $f=0$ or $v_p(f)\ge s-1$ for every $\ell$; in particular $f=0$ whenever $|f|<p^{s-1}$. With $\ell=(1;0,\dots,0)$, $f=N(\mathbf m)-N(k)$ lies between $-1$ and $n\le p^{s-1}-1$, so $f=0$, that is, $N(\mathbf m)=N(k)$. With $W=0$, $m_i=1$ and the other $m$'s $0$, $f=n_{a_i}-n_{b_i}-\varepsilon_i(k)$ lies between $-n-1$ and $n+1\le p^{s-1}$, so $f=0$ unless $|f|=p^{s-1}=n+1$. That would need $|n_{a_i}-n_{b_i}|=n$ with $\varepsilon_i(k)=\mp1$: either every letter is $a_i$ and $k=b_i$, so $N(\mathbf m)=0\ne1=N(k)$, or every letter is $b_i$ and $k=a_i$, so $N(\mathbf m)=n\ne0=N(k)$. Both are excluded, so $n_{a_i}-n_{b_i}=\varepsilon_i(k)$ for every $i$. The last paragraph of the proof of Lemma 3 of the theorem modulo the $p$th lower central term uses only these equalities. So every word in which $\bar J(\bar x_k)$ has a nonzero component consists of letters $2$, except: for $k=2$, none; for $k=a_j$, one letter $a_j$; for $k=b_j$, one letter $b_j$; for $k\in\{0,1\}$, either one letter in $\{0,1\}$, or one letter $a_i$ and one letter $b_i$ for some $i$. This is the list of that Lemma 3 with $\kappa=k$.

*The transvection.* By Lemma 5 (iii) of the higher Johnson theorem, $\bar T_1^{\otimes n}\bar J=\bar J\bar T_1$. The three bullets under *The transvection* in the proof of part (b) of the theorem modulo the $p$th lower central term now apply word for word: they use only this list, the rule $(\ast)$ there, $n\ge3$, and that $p$ is odd. So $\bar J$ vanishes on every $\bar x_k$, hence on $V$ by $\Lambda$-linearity. Then $J=pJ'$ with $J'\in\mathcal S_J$, as in that proof, so $\mathcal S_J=p\mathcal S_J$ and $\mathcal J_n(\alpha)\in\bigcap_kp^k\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_n)=0$. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** By Corollary 1 of [the degree-one Johnson theorem in degrees three and four](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-in-degrees-three-and-four.md), $\alpha=c_{g_2}\circ\alpha_2$ with $\alpha_2$ inducing the identity on $G/P_2$ and on $P/P_3$. Suppose $2\le n\le p^{s-1}-1$ and $\alpha=c_{g_n}\circ\alpha_n$, with $\alpha_n$ inducing the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$. Then $\alpha_n$ represents the class of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates, and part (a) gives $h\in P_n$ with $c_h\alpha_n$ inducing the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$. If $n+1\le p^{s-1}-1$, part (b) for $n+1\ge3$ shows that $c_h\alpha_n$ is trivial on $P/P_{n+2}$; put $\alpha_{n+1}=c_h\alpha_n$ and $g_{n+1}=g_nh^{-1}$. For $n=p^{s-1}-1$, which is at least $2$, put $\alpha'=c_h\alpha_n$ and $g=g_nh^{-1}$. If $c_k\alpha'$ also induces the identity on $G/P_{p^{s-1}}$, then so does $c_k$, and $k\in P_{p^{s-1}}$ by Lemma 5 (o) of the higher Johnson theorem; conversely such $c_k$ induce the identity there. As $P$ is characteristic in $G$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), so is $P_{p^{s-1}}$, and $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_{p^{s-1}})$ maps the class of $\alpha$ to the trivial class. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* As in the case $s=2$, finite outer conjugacy makes the class of $\alpha$ invariant under a power of each weight automorphism, and Lemma 2 there upgrades this to exact invariance. The new point is to read this invariance modulo $p^s$ rather than modulo $p$. There the weight automorphism multiplies the multidegree-$\mathbf m$ part by $(1+p)^{\mathrm{wt}(\mathbf m)}$, and the factor $(1+p)^{\mathrm{wt}}-1$ can be divided out unless its valuation reaches $s$. So the weights are seen as integers modulo $p^{s-1}$, not only modulo $p$, and below degree $p^{s-1}$ they determine $N(\mathbf m)$ and the differences $n_{a_i}-n_{b_i}$. The all-$\bar x_2$ part is handled $p$-adically as well, with $T_1$ and the map $D_{N'}$, which needs only $v_p(n)\le s-2$. So no Lie projection is used and $p\mid n$ is allowed.
- *Where this stops.* (1) At degree $n=p^{s-1}$, in part (a) the multidegrees with all letters in $\{0,1\}$ have weight $Wp^{s-1}$ for every $\ell$, so $(1+p)^{\mathrm{wt}}\equiv1\pmod{p^s}$ and the weight automorphisms give no condition. In part (b), for $k=a_j$, the multidegree with $p^{s-1}-1$ letters $b_j$ and one letter $0$ has weight $\mathrm{wt}_\ell(a_j)+p^{s-1}(W-m_j)$ for every $\ell$, so again the weight automorphisms give no condition on that part of $\bar J(\bar a_j)$. (2) For $s=1$, which includes every unramified $K$, the argument gives nothing, as in the case $s=2$. (3) For odd $d$ the similitude theorem is not available. (4) The case $\mu_p\subseteq K$. Innerness of a finite-conjugacy automorphism is equivalent to innerness on every $G/P_j$, so for a fixed $K$ this remains open. This continues item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535).
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, even $[K:\mathbb Q_p]$, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $s\ge2$; $2\le n\le p^{s-1}-1$ in (a) and $3\le n\le p^{s-1}-1$ in (b). Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
