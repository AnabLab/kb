**Setting.** Use the Setting of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, $P_j$ is the closed lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$, $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$, and
$$V=H/pH=L\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d,\qquad L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0,\quad V_i=\Lambda\bar x_i\cong\Lambda\ (i\ge1),$$
with coordinates $\pi_i:V\to\Lambda$ for $i\ge1$. Also $\omega$, $\epsilon$ and $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$ are as there, and $\xi_i\in H$ is the class of $x_i$. As in [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md), let $r=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor$ and let $(a_i,b_i)$, $1\le i\le r$, be the surface pairs, with $(a_1,b_1)$ the pair of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md): $(x_2,x_3)$ for odd $d$ and $(x_3,x_4)$ for even $d$. Write $V_{(i)}=V_{a_i}\oplus V_{b_i}$ and $S=\bigoplus_iV_{(i)}$. The complementary part is $C_V=V_1$ for odd $d$ and $V_1\oplus V_2$ for even $d$. Put
$$A=L\oplus C_V,\qquad V=A\oplus S,$$
and let $\pi_A:V\to A$ be the projection along $S$. Let $\gamma\in\mathcal G$ be as in step 4 there, a generator of the Sylow pro-$p$ subgroup of the closed subgroup generated by $\sigma$. Then $\omega(\gamma)=1$, so $\lambda_0=\gamma-1\in\ker\omega$.

*Lower central input.* By [the boundary-twist theorem](boundary-twists-exclude-finite-outer-conjugacy-of-wild-scalars.md), the $\mathcal L_j$ are torsion-free compact $\mathbb Z_p$-modules, topologically generated by iterated brackets of elements of $H$, and $\mathcal L_j/p$ is the completed degree-$j$ free Lie space $\mathcal L_j(V)$, inside the completed tensor space $V^{\hat\otimes j}$ with $[u,v]=uv-vu$, with compatible brackets. The identification is proved there for every $j$; we use $j\le4$. It is functorial: an automorphism of $G$ acting on $V$ by $X$ acts on $\mathcal L_j(V)$ by the restriction $\mathcal L_j(X)$ of $X^{\otimes j}$. As $p$ is odd, $\mathcal L_2(V)$ is the space of antisymmetric tensors in $V^{\hat\otimes2}$. The bracket maps $H\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ and $\mathcal L_2\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_3)$ are injective. A tensor in $V^{\hat\otimes j}$ has a component in $V_{k_1}\hat\otimes\cdots\hat\otimes V_{k_j}$ for each choice of summands, and $V_k\hat\otimes V_l\cong\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^2]]$ through the coordinates. For $z\in\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda$ let $z^{\mathsf T}$ be its image under the flip of the factors.

*Johnson maps.* For an automorphism $\theta$ trivial on $H$, $J_1(\theta):H\to\mathcal L_2$ is $xP_2\mapsto\theta(x)x^{-1}P_3$. For $\theta$ trivial on $P/P_3$, $J(\theta):H\to\mathcal L_3$ is $xP_2\mapsto\theta(x)x^{-1}P_4$. The rules of the Setting there hold: additivity, $J_1(\beta\theta\beta^{-1})=\beta_2J_1(\theta)\beta_H^{-1}$, and $J_1(c_g)=\operatorname{ad}(gP_2)$ for $g\in P$, where $\beta_j$ is the action of $\beta$ on $\mathcal L_j$ and $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$.

*Coefficients.* For a surface index $l$, let $\iota_l:\Lambda\hat\otimes A\to\mathcal L_2(V)$ be the continuous linear map with $\iota_l(\mu\otimes c)=[\mu\bar x_l,c]$. The component of $\iota_l(\varphi)$ in $V_l\hat\otimes A\cong\Lambda\hat\otimes A$ is $\varphi$, and its other components lie in $A\hat\otimes V_l$. So $\iota_l$ is injective, and the antisymmetric tensors with components only in $V_{(i)}\hat\otimes A$ and $A\hat\otimes V_{(i)}$ are the elements $\iota_{a_i}(\varphi)+\iota_{b_i}(\varphi')$. Let $\Gamma$ act on $\Lambda\hat\otimes A$ diagonally, $\rho\cdot(\nu\otimes c)=\rho\nu\otimes\rho c$, extended continuously to an action $\mu\cdot$ of $\Lambda$, and let $R_\mu(\nu\otimes c)=\nu\mu\otimes c$. Then $\mu\cdot\iota_l(\varphi)=\iota_l(\mu\cdot\varphi)$, for the diagonal action on tensors, and $R_\rho$ commutes with $\rho'\cdot$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ and induce the identity on $G/[P,P]$. Then $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in[P,[P,P]]$ for every $x\in P$, that is, $J_1(\alpha)=0$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$, $K$ be as in the theorem. Every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha_0$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_0$ that induces the identity on $G/[P,P]$ and on $P/[P,[P,P]]$. Two such $\alpha_0$ differ by $c_h$ with $h\in[P,P]$.

**Lemma 1 (Johnson calculus; proved here).** Let $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ and $k\ge1$ with $\theta(x)x^{-1}\in P_{k+1}$ for all $x\in P$.

- (a) For $m\ge1$ and $z\in P_m$, $\theta(z)z^{-1}\in P_{m+k}$, and $z\mapsto\theta(z)z^{-1}P_{m+k+1}$ induces a continuous homomorphism $\partial^\theta_m:\mathcal L_m\to\mathcal L_{m+k}$, with $\partial^\theta_{m}[\xi,\eta]=[\partial^\theta_1\xi,\eta]+[\xi,\partial^\theta_{m-1}\eta]$ for $\xi\in H$, $\eta\in\mathcal L_{m-1}$. Modulo $p$, $\partial^\theta_m$ is the restriction to $\mathcal L_m(V)$ of the map on $V^{\hat\otimes m}$ that applies the reduction of $\partial^\theta_1$ in one slot at a time and adds the results.
- (b) Let $\alpha$ be trivial on $H$ and $T$ trivial on $P/P_3$. For $n\ge1$, $\kappa=T^n\alpha T^{-n}\alpha^{-1}$ satisfies $\kappa(y)y^{-1}\in P_4$ for all $y\in P$, and $\kappa(y)y^{-1}P_5=n\big(\partial^T_2\partial^\alpha_1-\partial^\alpha_3\partial^T_1\big)(yP_2)$.

*Proof.* (a) Let $\Sigma_m$ be the set of $z\in P_m$ with $\theta(z)z^{-1}\in P_{m+k}$. It is a closed subgroup: $\theta(zz')(zz')^{-1}=\theta(z)z^{-1}\cdot z\,\theta(z')z'^{-1}z^{-1}$, and $P_{m+k}$ is closed and normal. Modulo $[P,P_{m+k}]\subseteq P_{m+k+1}$ the conjugation by $z$ can be dropped, so on $\Sigma_m$ the map $z\mapsto\theta(z)z^{-1}P_{m+k+1}$ is a continuous homomorphism. $\Sigma_1=P$ by hypothesis. Suppose $\Sigma_m=P_m$, and let $x\in P$, $z\in P_m$, $e=\theta(x)x^{-1}\in P_{k+1}$ and $f=\theta(z)z^{-1}\in P_{m+k}$. With $[ab,c]=a[b,c]a^{-1}[a,c]$ and $[a,bc]=[a,b]\,b[a,c]b^{-1}$,
$$\theta([x,z])=[ex,fz]=e\,[x,f]\,f[x,z]f^{-1}\,e^{-1}\,[e,f]\,f[e,z]f^{-1}.$$
Work modulo $P_{m+k+2}$. Conjugation by $e$ changes an element of $P_j$ by an element of $P_{j+k+1}$, and conjugation by $f$ changes it by an element of $P_{j+m+k}$. So these conjugations can be dropped on $[x,z]\in P_{m+1}$, $[x,f]\in P_{m+k+1}$ and $[e,z]\in P_{m+k+1}$, as $m\ge1$. Also $[e,f]\in P_{m+2k+1}$, and elements of $P_{m+1}$ commute with those of $P_{m+k+1}$. Hence $\theta([x,z])[x,z]^{-1}\equiv[x,f][e,z]$, which lies in $P_{m+k+1}$. As $P_{m+1}$ is the closed subgroup generated by such $[x,z]$, $\Sigma_{m+1}=P_{m+1}$. So the homomorphism on $P_m$ kills $P_{m+1}$, which gives $\partial^\theta_m$, and the congruence gives the bracket formula. Modulo $p$ both maps in the last assertion satisfy the same recursion, as $D(\xi\eta-\eta\xi)=[D\xi,\eta]+[\xi,D\eta]$ for the slotwise map $D$; and $\mathcal L_m(V)$ is topologically spanned by the $[\xi,\eta]$ with $\xi\in V$, $\eta\in\mathcal L_{m-1}(V)$.

(b) $T^n$ satisfies the hypothesis with $k=2$, and $\partial^{T^n}_m=n\partial^T_m$, by additivity of $J$ and the recursion. Let $x\in P$, and write $\alpha(x)=jx$ and $T^n(x)=fx$ with $j\in P_2$, $f\in P_3$. Put $y=\alpha T^n(x)$, so that $y\equiv x$ modulo $P_2$ and $\kappa(y)=T^n\alpha(x)$. Then
$$\kappa(y)y^{-1}=T^n(j)\,f\,j^{-1}\alpha(f)^{-1}=f'\,[j,f]\,j'^{-1},$$
where $f'=T^n(j)j^{-1}\in P_4$ has class $n\partial^T_2(jP_3)$ and $j'=\alpha(f)f^{-1}\in P_4$ has class $\partial^\alpha_3(fP_4)$, by (a). As $[j,f]\in P_5$ and $jP_3=\partial^\alpha_1(xP_2)$, $fP_4=n\partial^T_1(xP_2)$, the assertion follows. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (degree three; proved here).**

- (a) Let $\bar x\in V$, and let $\chi:V\to\mathbb F_p$ be continuous and linear with $\chi(\bar x)=1$. If $\bar y\in\mathcal L_3(V)$ and $[\bar y,\bar x]=0$, then $\bar y=0$.
- (b) If $y\in\mathcal L_3$ and $[y,H]\subseteq p^k\mathcal L_4$, then $y\in p^k\mathcal L_3$.

*Proof.* (a) Apply $\mathrm{id}^{\otimes3}\otimes\chi$ to $\bar y\bar x-\bar x\bar y=0$: $\bar y=\bar x\,y_1$ with $y_1=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\chi)(\bar y)$. Applying $\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\chi$ to $\bar y=\bar xy_1$ gives $y_1=\bar x\,w$ with $w=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\chi)(y_1)\in V$, so $\bar y=\bar x\bar xw$. Applying $\chi\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes3}$ to $\bar y\bar x-\bar x\bar y=0$ gives $\bar y=y_2\bar x$ with $y_2=(\chi\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\bar y)=\bar xw$. So $\bar x\bar xw=\bar xw\bar x$, and applying $\chi\otimes\chi\otimes\mathrm{id}$ gives $w=\chi(w)\bar x$, so $\bar y=\chi(w)\,\bar x\bar x\bar x$. Finally $\chi^{\otimes3}$ kills each $[[u,v],z]=uvz-vuz-zuv+zvu$, hence the closed span $\mathcal L_3(V)$ of these. So $\chi(w)=\chi^{\otimes3}(\bar y)=0$ and $\bar y=0$.

(b) Let $k\ge1$. Modulo $p$, $[\bar y,\bar x_1]=0$, so $\bar y=0$ by (a) with $\chi=\epsilon\circ\pi_1$. So $y=py'$, and $[y',H]\subseteq p^{k-1}\mathcal L_4$ as $\mathcal L_4$ is torsion-free. Induct. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (invariants; proved here).**

- (a) Let $Y$ be a compact $\mathbb F_p$-vector space and $z\in\Lambda\hat\otimes Y$. If $(R_{\lambda_0}\otimes\mathrm{id})(z)=0$, then $z=0$. The same holds with the factors exchanged.
- (b) If $z\in\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda$ and $(R_\gamma\otimes R_\gamma)(z)=z$, then $z=0$.
- (c) If $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, then $V^\Gamma=0$ and $H^\Gamma=0$.

*Proof.* (a) For continuous linear $f:Y\to\mathbb F_p$, $z_f=(\mathrm{id}\otimes f)(z)\in\Lambda$ satisfies $z_f(\gamma-1)=0$, so $z_f=0$ by Lemma 2 of [the central-element construction](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md). At each finite level $\mathbb F_p[\Gamma/U]\otimes Y/Y'$, a tensor killed by $\mathrm{id}\otimes\bar f$ for every functional $\bar f$ on $Y/Y'$ is $0$. So $z=0$.

(b) The homeomorphism $h(g_1,g_2)=(g_1g_2^{-1},g_2)$ of $\Gamma\times\Gamma$ descends to $\Gamma/U\times\Gamma/U$ for open normal $U$, so it induces a continuous linear automorphism $h_*$ of $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^2]]=\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda$. As $h(g_1\rho,g_2\rho)=(g_1g_2^{-1},g_2\rho)$, $h_*(R_\rho\otimes R_\rho)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_\rho)h_*$. So $(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_{\lambda_0})(h_*z)=0$, and $h_*z=0$ by (a).

(c) By Lemma 0 of [unitarity](unitarity-of-central-scalars-on-wild-frattini.md), $V^{\ker\omega}=L$, on which $\Gamma$ acts through $\omega\ne1$. So $V^\Gamma=0$. Let $h\in H^\Gamma$ be nonzero. As $H$ is a torsion-free pro-$p$ abelian group, $\bigcap_kp^kH=0$, so $h=p^kh'$ with $h'\notin pH$, and $h'\in H^\Gamma$ by torsion-freeness. Its image in $V^\Gamma$ is nonzero, a contradiction. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (the conditions; proved here).** Let $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Let $\mathcal A$ be the set of $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates that induce the identity on $G/P_2$. Let $\mathcal S$ be the set of continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant $J:H\to\mathcal L_2$ such that $p^mJ=J_1(\alpha)$ for some $m\ge0$ and $\alpha\in\mathcal A$. For such $J$, $\alpha$ and $m$, the recursion of Lemma 1 (a), together with the fact that brackets topologically generate each $\mathcal L_j$, shows that $\partial^\alpha_2$ and $\partial^\alpha_3$ map into $p^m\mathcal L_3$ and $p^m\mathcal L_4$. Put $J^{(3)}=p^{-m}\partial^\alpha_3$; by the same recursion it depends only on $J$. Then for $J\in\mathcal S$:

- (i) $\theta_2J\theta_H^{-1}\in\mathcal S$ for every tame-trivial $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.
- (ii) If $\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is tame-trivial and $\psi_H-1$ is nilpotent, then $\psi_2J\psi_H^{-1}=J$.
- (iii) For each $1\le i\le r$, let $T=T^{(i)}_1$ be the boundary twist of the $i$-th pair and $D=J(T)$. Then $\partial^T_2J-J^{(3)}D=\operatorname{ad}(y)$ for some $y\in\mathcal L_3$.

*Proof.* Let $p^mJ=J_1(\alpha)$ with $\alpha\in\mathcal A$. Every element of $\mathcal A$ is tame-trivial.

(i) $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}$ has finitely many outer conjugates and induces the identity on $G/P_2$, as $P_2$ is characteristic. Its $J_1$ is $p^m\theta_2J\theta_H^{-1}$, which is $\Gamma$-equivariant as $\theta_H,\theta_2$ commute with $\Gamma$.

(ii) By Lemma (b) of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), $\psi^n\alpha\psi^{-n}=c_g\alpha$ with $n\ge1$ and $g\in P$. Both sides induce the identity on $G/P_2$, hence so does $c_g$. For $\tilde\rho\in G$ over $\rho\in\Gamma$, the class of $g\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}$ in $H$ is $(1-\rho)(gP_2)$, so $gP_2\in H^\Gamma=0$ by Lemma 3 (c), and $J_1(c_g)=0$. The rules give $U^nJ=J$ for the operator $U(J')=\psi_2J'\psi_H^{-1}$ on the torsion-free group $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_2)$. If $N=\psi_H-1$ has $N^e=0$, then $(\psi_2-1)^{2e-1}$ kills every bracket $[\xi,\eta]$, since $\psi_2[\xi,\eta]=[(1+N)\xi,(1+N)\eta]$; so $\psi_2-1$ is nilpotent, and $U-1$ is nilpotent as $U$ is a product of two commuting unipotent operators. If $(U-1)J\ne0$, let $q\ge1$ be largest with $(U-1)^qJ\ne0$. Then $0=(U-1)^{q-1}(U^n-1)J=\sum_{t\ge1}\binom nt(U-1)^{q-1+t}J=n(U-1)^qJ$, a contradiction. So $UJ=J$.

(iii) By Lemma 2 (a) of [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md), $T$ is tame-trivial and trivial on $P/P_3$. By Lemma (b) of abelianized wild inertia, $\kappa=T^n\alpha T^{-n}\alpha^{-1}=c_g$ with $n\ge1$ and $g\in P$. By Lemma 1 (b), $[g,P]\subseteq P_4$. Injectivity of the bracket map on $H$ gives $g\in P_2$, and then injectivity on $\mathcal L_2$ gives $g\in P_3$. So $[g,y]P_5=[gP_4,yP_2]$, and Lemma 1 (b) gives $np^m(\partial^T_2J-J^{(3)}D)=\operatorname{ad}(gP_4)$. By Lemma 2 (b), $gP_4\in np^m\mathcal L_3$, and cancelling in the torsion-free group $\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_4)$ gives the claim. $\square$

For $J\in\mathcal S$ let $\bar J:V\to\mathcal L_2(V)$ be its reduction, which is $\Lambda$-linear. By Lemma 1 (a), $J^{(3)}$ reduces to the slotwise extension $\bar J^{(3)}$ of $\bar J$ to $\mathcal L_3(V)$, and $\partial^T_2$ to the slotwise extension $\delta^{(2)}$ of the reduction $\delta$ of $D$. So Lemma 4 gives, for every $J\in\mathcal S$:

- (i') $\mathcal L_2(B)\bar J B^{-1}$ is the reduction of an element of $\mathcal S$, if $B$ is the action on $V$ of a tame-trivial automorphism;
- (ii') $\mathcal L_2(\bar\Psi)\bar J=\bar J\bar\Psi$, if $\bar\Psi$ is the action on $V$ of an automorphism $\psi$ as in (ii);
- (iii') $\delta^{(2)}\bar J-\bar J^{(3)}\delta=\operatorname{ad}(\bar y)$ on $V$ for some $\bar y\in\mathcal L_3(V)$, with $\delta$ as in (iii).

**Proof of the theorem.** Here $r\ge2$, as $d\ge5$. We show that $\bar J=0$ for every $J\in\mathcal S$. Then $J=pJ'$ with $J'$ continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant, as $\mathcal L_2$ is torsion-free and compact, and $J'\in\mathcal S$. So $J\in\bigcap_kp^k\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)=0$. Applied to $J_1(\alpha)\in\mathcal S$, this proves the theorem.

Fix $J\in\mathcal S$. By (i'), each step below also applies to $\mathcal L_2(B)\bar JB^{-1}$.

*1. Kondo's automorphisms.* Let $M$ be a unipotent element of the integral symplectic group of $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$, in the notation of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md), with $\iota(\alpha_i)=a_i$ and $\iota(\beta_i)=b_i$. By the Setting there, $M$ is induced by some $\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$, and $\psi=\Psi(\varphi)$ is tame-trivial, fixes the complementary generators, and maps $a_i,b_i$ to words in the surface generators whose exponent sums are given by $M$. So $\psi_H$ fixes $\xi_0,\xi_1$ (and $\xi_2$ for even $d$) and acts on the surface classes by $M$, and $\psi_H-1$ is nilpotent. By (ii'), $\mathcal L_2(\bar M)\bar J=\bar J\bar M$, where $\bar M$ acts on $V$ $\Lambda$-linearly by $M$ on the $\bar x_{a_i},\bar x_{b_i}$ and trivially on $A$. Hence this holds for every $M$ in the group generated by such elements. It contains:

- $u_i:a_i\mapsto a_i+b_i$ and $l_i:b_i\mapsto b_i+a_i$, other basis vectors fixed;
- $d_i=(u_il_i^{-1}u_i)^2$, which is $-1$ on $a_i,b_i$ and fixes the rest;
- for $i\ne i'$, $M_{ii'}:a_i\mapsto a_i+b_{i'}$, $a_{i'}\mapsto a_{i'}+b_i$, other basis vectors fixed. It preserves the symplectic form, as $\langle a_i+b_{i'},a_{i'}+b_i\rangle=\langle a_i,b_i\rangle+\langle b_{i'},a_{i'}\rangle=0$, and $(M_{ii'}-1)^2=0$.

(1a) $\mathcal L_2(\bar d_i)$ is $-1$ on the components with exactly one slot in $V_{(i)}$ and $+1$ on the others. As $\bar d_i\bar x_k=-\bar x_k$ for $k\in\{a_i,b_i\}$ and $\bar d_{i'}\bar x_k=\bar x_k$ for $i'\ne i$, $\bar J(\bar x_k)$ has components only in $V_{(i)}\hat\otimes A$ and $A\hat\otimes V_{(i)}$. So $\bar J(\bar x_{a_i})=\iota_{a_i}(\varphi_{aa})+\iota_{b_i}(\varphi_{ab})$ and $\bar J(\bar x_{b_i})=\iota_{a_i}(\varphi_{ba})+\iota_{b_i}(\varphi_{bb})$. For $c\in\{0,1\}$, and $c=2$ for even $d$, $\bar d_i$ fixes $\bar x_c$ for all $i$, so $\bar J(\bar x_c)$ has components only in $A\hat\otimes A$ and the $V_{(i)}\hat\otimes V_{(i)}$.

(1b) As $\mathcal L_2(\bar u_i)\iota_{a_i}(\varphi)=\iota_{a_i}(\varphi)+\iota_{b_i}(\varphi)$ and $\mathcal L_2(\bar u_i)\iota_{b_i}(\varphi)=\iota_{b_i}(\varphi)$, comparing $\bar J(\bar x_{a_i}+\bar x_{b_i})$ with $\mathcal L_2(\bar u_i)\bar J(\bar x_{a_i})$ gives $\varphi_{ba}=0$ and $\varphi_{bb}=\varphi_{aa}$. In the same way $l_i$ gives $\varphi_{ab}=0$. So $\bar J(\bar x_{a_i})=\iota_{a_i}(\varphi_i)$ and $\bar J(\bar x_{b_i})=\iota_{b_i}(\varphi_i)$. Comparing $\bar J(\bar x_{a_i}+\bar x_{b_{i'}})$ with $\mathcal L_2(\bar M_{ii'})\bar J(\bar x_{a_i})=\iota_{a_i}(\varphi_i)+\iota_{b_{i'}}(\varphi_i)$ gives $\varphi_{i'}=\varphi_i$. So there is one $\varphi\in\Lambda\hat\otimes A$ with
$$\bar J(\bar x_k)=\iota_k(\varphi)\qquad\text{for every surface index }k.\tag{1}$$

(1c) Let $c$ be complementary and $Z$ the component of $\bar J(\bar x_c)$ in $V_{(i)}\hat\otimes V_{(i)}$, with parts $Z_{aa},Z_{ab},Z_{ba},Z_{bb}$. Write $N$ for the map $\mu\bar x_{a_i}\mapsto\mu\bar x_{b_i}$, zero on the other summands, so $\bar u_i=1+N$. As $\bar u_i$ fixes $\bar x_c$ and preserves $V_{(i)}$, $(N\otimes1+1\otimes N+N\otimes N)Z=0$. Its component in $V_{b_i}\hat\otimes V_{a_i}$ is $(N\otimes1)Z_{aa}$, so $Z_{aa}=0$; likewise $l_i$ gives $Z_{bb}=0$. Let $z_{c,i}\in\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda$ be the coordinate of $Z_{ab}$; then $Z_{ba}$ has coordinate $-z_{c,i}^{\mathsf T}$, by antisymmetry. For $i\ne i'$, let $N'=\bar M_{ii'}-1$. As $\bar M_{ii'}$ fixes $\bar x_c$, $(N'\otimes1+1\otimes N'+N'\otimes N')\bar J(\bar x_c)=0$. Its component in $V_{b_{i'}}\hat\otimes V_{b_i}$ comes only from $N'\otimes1$ on the $V_{a_i}\hat\otimes V_{b_i}$ component and from $1\otimes N'$ on the $V_{b_{i'}}\hat\otimes V_{a_{i'}}$ component, by (1a). So
$$z_{c,i}=z_{c,i'}^{\mathsf T}\qquad(i\ne i').\tag{2}$$

*2. Units on the first pair.* Let $\rho\in\Gamma$. Parts 2 and 3 of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), for the unit $\lambda=\rho$, give a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $\Theta$ on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $L$ and every $V_j$ with $j\notin\{a_1,b_1\}$, and has $\Theta(\mu\bar x_{a_1})=\mu\rho\bar x_{a_1}$ and $\Theta(\mu\bar x_{b_1})=\omega(\rho)^{-1}\mu\rho\bar x_{b_1}$, as $(\rho^*)^{-1}=\omega(\rho)^{-1}\rho$. Step 1 applies to $\bar J'=\mathcal L_2(\Theta)\bar J\Theta^{-1}$, with some $\varphi'$ and $z'_{c,i}$.

- At $\bar x_{a_2}$: $\bar J'(\bar x_{a_2})=\mathcal L_2(\Theta)\iota_{a_2}(\varphi)=\iota_{a_2}(\varphi)$, so $\varphi'=\varphi$.
- At $\bar x_{a_1}$: $\Theta^{-1}\bar x_{a_1}=\rho^{-1}\bar x_{a_1}$, so $\bar J(\Theta^{-1}\bar x_{a_1})=\iota_{a_1}(\rho^{-1}\cdot\varphi)$, and $\mathcal L_2(\Theta)\iota_{a_1}(\varphi'')=\iota_{a_1}(R_\rho\varphi'')$. So $\iota_{a_1}(\varphi)=\bar J'(\bar x_{a_1})=\iota_{a_1}(R_\rho(\rho^{-1}\cdot\varphi))$, that is,
$$R_\rho\varphi=\rho\cdot\varphi\qquad(\rho\in\Gamma).\tag{3}$$
- At complementary $\bar x_c$: $\bar J'(\bar x_c)=\mathcal L_2(\Theta)\bar J(\bar x_c)$, so $z'_{c,2}=z_{c,2}$ and $z'_{c,1}=\omega(\rho)^{-1}(R_\rho\otimes R_\rho)z_{c,1}$. By (2) for $\bar J'$ and for $\bar J$, $z'_{c,1}=z'^{\mathsf T}_{c,2}=z^{\mathsf T}_{c,2}=z_{c,1}$. For $\rho=\gamma$ this is $(R_\gamma\otimes R_\gamma)z_{c,1}=z_{c,1}$, so $z_{c,1}=0$ by Lemma 3 (b), and every $z_{c,i}=z_{c,1}^{\mathsf T}=0$ by (2). With (1c),
$$\bar J(\bar x_c)\in\mathcal L_2(A)\qquad(c\text{ complementary}).\tag{4}$$

*3. A boundary twist.* Let $T=T^{(1)}_1$ and $D=J(T)$, with reduction $\delta$. By step 1 of abelianized wild inertia, $\delta$ is $\Lambda$-linear, kills $L$ and every $V_j$ with $j\notin\{a_1,b_1\}$, and $\delta(\bar a)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]$, $\delta(\bar b)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]$, where $\bar a=\bar x_{a_1}$ and $\bar b=\bar x_{b_1}$. Let $E=\delta^{(2)}\bar J-\bar J^{(3)}\delta=\operatorname{ad}(\bar y)$, by (iii').

- At $\bar x_{a_2}$: $\delta(\bar x_{a_2})=0$, and $\delta^{(2)}[\mu\bar x_{a_2},c]=[\delta(\mu\bar x_{a_2}),c]+[\mu\bar x_{a_2},\delta c]=0$ for $c\in A$. By (1), $E(\bar x_{a_2})=0$. So $[\bar y,\bar x_{a_2}]=0$, and $\bar y=0$ by Lemma 2 (a) with $\chi=\epsilon\circ\pi_{a_2}$.
- At $\bar a$: let $\mathrm{pr}=\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{b_1}\otimes\pi_A$ on $V^{\hat\otimes4}$, with values in $\Lambda^{\hat\otimes3}\hat\otimes A$, defined level by level. Both $E(\bar a)$ and $\mathrm{pr}$ are continuous and linear in $\varphi$, so take $\varphi=\mu\otimes c$, with $u=\mu\bar a$, $v=\mu\bar b$, so that $\bar J(\bar a)=uc-cu$ and $\bar J(\bar b)=vc-cv$ by (1). First, $\delta^{(2)}\bar J(\bar a)=\delta(u)c-c\,\delta(u)$ with $\delta(u)=\mu\cdot[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]$, and by property ($\kappa$1) of interval twists, $(\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{b_1})(\delta(u))=\Delta_3(\mu)\cdot(-1)$, as the word $\bar a\bar a\bar b$ has coefficient $-1$ in $2\bar a\bar b\bar a-\bar b\bar a\bar a-\bar a\bar a\bar b$. So $\mathrm{pr}(\delta^{(2)}\bar J(\bar a))=-\Delta_3(\mu)\otimes c$. Second, $\bar J^{(3)}\delta(\bar a)$ is obtained from $2\bar a\bar b\bar a-\bar b\bar a\bar a-\bar a\bar a\bar b$ by replacing one letter $\bar a$ by $uc-cu$ or one letter $\bar b$ by $vc-cv$. A word seen by $\mathrm{pr}$ has letters in $V_{a_1},V_{a_1},V_{b_1},A$, in this order. Only $-\bar a\bar a(vc)$ qualifies: every other word has $c$, $u$ or $v$, or $\bar b$, in a wrong slot. So $\mathrm{pr}(\bar J^{(3)}\delta(\bar a))=-1\otimes1\otimes\mu\otimes c$. Hence, for general $\varphi$,
$$0=\mathrm{pr}(E(\bar a))=-(\Delta_3\otimes\mathrm{id}_A)(\varphi)+(\jmath\otimes\mathrm{id}_A)(\varphi),\qquad\jmath(\mu)=1\otimes1\otimes\mu.$$
Apply $\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_A$. As $(\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\Delta_3=\mathrm{id}$ and $(\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\jmath(\mu)=\epsilon(\mu)1$, this gives $\varphi=1\otimes c_\varphi$ with $c_\varphi=(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_A)(\varphi)\in A$.
- By (3), $\rho\otimes c_\varphi=R_\rho\varphi=\rho\cdot\varphi=\rho\otimes\rho c_\varphi$, so $c_\varphi\in A\cap V^\Gamma=0$ by Lemma 3 (c). With (1),
$$\bar J(S)=0.\tag{5}$$

*4. The complementary generators.* For complementary $c$ put $Q_c=\bar J(\bar x_c)\in\mathcal L_2(A)$, by (4). Let $e$ be a surface index and $B$ a $\Lambda$-linear automorphism of $V$ that fixes $L$ and every summand of $C_V$ other than $V_1$ pointwise, with $B(\mu\bar x_1)=\mu\bar x_1+\mu\lambda_0\bar x_e$. *Claim:* if $Q\in\mathcal L_2(A)$ and $\mathcal L_2(B)Q\in\mathcal L_2(A)$, then $Q$ has no component with a slot in $V_1$. Indeed, for each summand $V_j$ of $A$ (with $V_0=L$), the component of $(B\otimes B)Q$ in $V_e\hat\otimes V_j$ is $(R_{\lambda_0}\otimes\mathrm{id})$ of the component $Q_{1j}$ of $Q$ in $V_1\hat\otimes V_j$, in coordinates. It vanishes, so $Q_{1j}=0$ by Lemma 3 (a), and $Q_{j1}=0$ by antisymmetry.

- *Even $d$.* Part 2 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), with $\lambda=\lambda_0$, gives a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $B$ on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,4$, and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda_0\bar x_3$, $B\bar x_4=\bar x_4-\lambda_0^*\bar x_2$. Then $B^{-1}\bar x_c-\bar x_c\in S$ for $c=0,1,2$, so by (5) for $\bar J$, $\bar J'=\mathcal L_2(B)\bar JB^{-1}$ has $\bar J'(\bar x_c)=\mathcal L_2(B)Q_c$, which lies in $\mathcal L_2(A)$ by (4) for $\bar J'$. By the claim with $e=3$, $Q_c\in\mathcal L_2(L\oplus V_2)$. Now let $T_1$ be the transvection of step 5 of abelianized wild inertia, fixing $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$; it is tame-trivial with $(T_1)_H=1+N$, $N\xi_2=\xi_1$, $N^2=0$. By (ii'), $(\bar T_1\otimes\bar T_1)Q_c=\bar J(\bar T_1\bar x_c)$. For $c=0,1$ this is $Q_c$, and the component of $(\bar T_1\otimes\bar T_1)Q_c-Q_c$ in $V_1\hat\otimes(L\oplus V_2)$ is $(\bar N\otimes1)$ of the component of $Q_c$ in $V_2\hat\otimes(L\oplus V_2)$. As $\bar N$ maps $V_2$ isomorphically onto $V_1$, that component vanishes, and by antisymmetry $Q_c\in L\otimes L$, so $Q_c=0$. For $c=2$, $\bar J(\bar x_2+\bar x_1)=Q_2+Q_1=Q_2$, and the same argument gives $Q_2=0$.
- *Odd $d$.* Part 3 of the mixing theorem, with $\nu=\lambda_0$, gives a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $B$ on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,3$, and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda_0\bar x_2$. Then $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\lambda_0\bar x_2$ and $B^{-1}\bar x_0=\bar x_0$. As in the even case, $\mathcal L_2(B)Q_c\in\mathcal L_2(A)$, and the claim with $e=2$ gives $Q_c\in\mathcal L_2(L)=0$.

So $\bar J$ vanishes on $S$ and on every complementary $\bar x_c$, hence on $V$ by $\Lambda$-linearity. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** By the corollary of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), $\alpha=c_g\circ\alpha_0$ with $\alpha_0$ inducing the identity on $G/[P,P]$. It represents the class of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates, and the theorem gives $J_1(\alpha_0)=0$. If $c_k\alpha_0$ is another such representative, then $c_k$ is tame-trivial, so $k\in P$ because $\Gamma$ has trivial centre, and it induces the identity on $G/P_2$, so $kP_2\in H^\Gamma=0$ as in the proof of Lemma 4 (ii). $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* $J_1(\alpha)$ is one $\Gamma$-equivariant map $H\to\mathcal L_2$. Exact automorphisms acting unipotently on $H$ must fix it. Kondo's symplectic automorphisms then force the surface generators to go to $[\bar x_k,\cdot\,]$ of one common element of $\Lambda\hat\otimes A$, and the units of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) on the first pair, used by conjugating, give relation (3). The boundary-twist equation in degree four, evaluated in the tensor pattern $\bar a\bar a\bar b\,A$, leaves only an inner derivation by an element of $A$, and (3) forces that element to be $\Gamma$-invariant, hence $0$. The mixing automorphisms and $T_1$ remove the complementary values. Finally the $p$-adic layers are peeled off one at a time, since every condition used is linear.
- *Consistency.* For even $d\ge6$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, the twist $T'$ of step 2 of [the wild-Frattini theorem](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-wild-frattini.md) induces the identity on $G/[P,P]$ and has $J_1(T')=p^sj\ne0$. So the theorem shows again that it has infinitely many outer conjugates, as [complementary twists](complementary-twists-have-infinitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) proves for every even $d\ge4$. Boundary twists have $J_1=0$, so the theorem says nothing about them; [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md) treats them.
- *What remains.* Let $\alpha_0$ be as in the corollary. For $\tilde\rho\in G$ over $\rho\in\Gamma$, $\alpha_0(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}P_3\in\mathcal L_2$ depends only on $\rho$, since $\alpha_0$ is trivial on $P/P_3$, and it is a continuous $1$-cocycle. Changing $\alpha_0$ by $c_h$ with $h\in P_2$ changes it by the coboundary of $hP_3$. Whether it is a coboundary, that is, whether $\alpha_0$ is inner modulo $[P,[P,P]]$, is not decided here. That would be the next layer of item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535). Innerness of $\alpha$ is equivalent to innerness on every $G/P_j$, by compactness, since $\bigcap_jP_j=1$.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, every $s$, including $p=3$. Steps 1 and 2 compare two surface pairs, so degrees $3$ and $4$ are not covered. When $\mu_p\subseteq K$, Lemma 3 (c) fails for $L$, and the normalization of the corollary is known only under the conditions of [the case with roots of unity](finite-outer-conjugacy-modulo-wild-commutators-when-k-contains-mu-p.md). Everything here is proved from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
