**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $D$ a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $n(D) \ge 2$, so that $D$ is infinite. Let $E$ be a pro-$p$ group containing $D$ as an open normal subgroup, and put $P = E/D$. For a subgroup $H \subseteq P$, let $E_H$ be its preimage in $E$, so that $(E_H : D) = |H|$.

Conjugation in $E$ makes $E$ act on $D$, on $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and on the group $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)$ of continuous homomorphisms, by $(x \cdot f)(d) = f(x^{-1}dx)$. Since $D$ acts trivially on the last two, $P$ acts on them. Let $V_D$ be $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ modulo its torsion subgroup. Every continuous homomorphism $D \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ factors through $V_D$, so $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p) = \mathrm{Hom}(V_D, \mathbb{Z}_p)$. As $V_D$ is free of finite rank over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, an element of $P$ acts trivially on $V_D$ if and only if it acts trivially on $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)$.

For a Demushkin group $G$, put $\delta(G) = 1$ if $q(G) = 0$ and $\delta(G) = 0$ otherwise. Since $G^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^{n(G)-1} \times \mathbb{Z}_p/q(G)\mathbb{Z}_p$ ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md)), $\mathrm{Hom}(G, \mathbb{Z}_p)$ is free of rank $n(G) - 1 + \delta(G)$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Suppose that $E$ has no element of order $p$.

1. $E$ is a Demushkin group, and so is every $E_H$, with $n(D) - 2 = |H|\,(n(E_H) - 2)$.
2. For every subgroup $H \subseteq P$, the $H$-invariants $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)^H$ have rank
$$n(E_H) - 1 + \delta(E_H) = 1 + \delta(E_H) + \frac{n(D) - 2}{|H|}.$$
Here $\delta(E_H) \le \delta(D)$, with equality if $p$ is odd.
3. If $n(D) \ge 3$, then $P$ acts faithfully on $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)$, and so on $V_D$.

The hypothesis holds, for instance, when $E$ is a Demushkin group of rank at least $2$ and $D$ is any open normal subgroup, since Demushkin groups are torsion-free ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 1). For odd $p$, the ranks in part 2 are the ranks of the $H$-invariants of $\mathbb{Z}_p^{1 + \delta(D)} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_p[P]^{n(E) - 2}$. Whether $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ is isomorphic to this module tensored with $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is not addressed here.

**Corollary 1 (outer automorphisms of order $p$).** This is proved here. Let $D$ be a Demushkin group of rank $n \ge 3$. It has trivial centre ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 4), so we identify $D$ with $\mathrm{Inn}(D) \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(D)$. If $\theta \in \mathrm{Out}(D)$ has order $p$ and acts trivially on $V_D$, then $\theta$ is the class of an automorphism $\sigma$ of $D$ with $\sigma^p = 1$.

*Proof.* Choose $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ in the class $\theta$, and let $E = D \cup D\alpha \cup \cdots \cup D\alpha^{p-1}$ be the preimage of $\langle\theta\rangle$. It is a subgroup, as $\alpha$ normalizes $D$ and $\alpha^p \in D$. It is compact, as a finite union of cosets of the compact group $D$, so it is a closed subgroup of the profinite group $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$. It contains $D$ as an open normal subgroup of index $p$, so it is a pro-$p$ group: for an open normal subgroup $U$ of $E$, the index $(E : U) = (E : DU)(D : D \cap U)$ is a power of $p$. For $\beta \in E$ and $d \in D$ we have $\beta \circ \mathrm{inn}(d) \circ \beta^{-1} = \mathrm{inn}(\beta(d))$, so conjugation in $E$ is the given action of automorphisms on $D$. Hence the generator $\theta$ of $P = E/D$ acts on $V_D$ trivially. If $E$ had no element of order $p$, part 3 would make $P$ act faithfully on $V_D$, which it does not, as $\theta \ne 1$. So $E$ has an element $\tau$ of order $p$. As $D$ is torsion-free, $\tau \notin D$, so $\tau \in D\alpha^i$ for some $1 \le i \le p - 1$. Choose $j$ with $ij \equiv 1 \pmod p$. Then $\sigma = \tau^j$ has order $p$, and it lies in $D\alpha^{ij} = D\alpha$ because $D$ is normal and $\alpha^p \in D$. So $\sigma$ lies in the class $\theta$. $\square$

**Corollary 2 (Tsujimura's question, case (a)).** This is proved here. Let $p$ be odd and $D$ a Demushkin group of rank $n \ge 3$ with $q \ne 0$, such as $G_K(p)$ for $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite with $\zeta_p \in K$ ([outer automorphisms with finitely many conjugates](outer-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-with-finitely-many-conjugates.md), Corollary 2). Let $N \ne D$ be a closed normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ isomorphic to $D$, with $N \cap D$ open in both $D$ and $N$, as in case (a) of [the reduction](demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three-are-elastic.md), Corollary 2. Then every element of order $p$ of $ND/D$ is the class of an automorphism of $D$ of order $p$, lying in $ND$ and acting trivially on $V_D$. In particular $ND$ has elements of order $p$, and none of them lies in $D$ or in $N$. So if no automorphism of $D$ of order $p$ acts trivially on $V_D$, case (a) cannot occur.

*Proof.* By the reduction, $ND/D$ is a nontrivial finite $p$-group, so it has elements of order $p$. Every element of $ND$ acts trivially on $V_D$ ([outer automorphisms with finitely many conjugates](outer-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-with-finitely-many-conjugates.md), Corollary 1). Let $\theta \in ND/D$ have order $p$. By Corollary 1, $\theta$ is the class of some $\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ with $\sigma^p = 1$, and $\sigma \ne 1$ as $\theta \ne 1$. The class $\theta$ is a coset of $D$ contained in $ND$, so $\sigma \in ND$, and $\sigma$ acts trivially on $V_D$. Finally $D$ and $N \cong D$ are torsion-free. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.**

*Part 1.* As $n(D) \ge 2$, $D$ has cohomological dimension $2$ ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md), Duality). Serre showed that if a profinite group has no element of order $p$, then its $p$-cohomological dimension equals that of each of its open subgroups [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, remark after (3.3.5), p. 174]. So $\mathrm{cd}_p E = 2$. The group $E$ is topologically finitely generated, by generators of $D$ and representatives of the cosets of $D$.

An open subgroup with the same $p$-cohomological dimension as the whole group has the same dualizing module at $p$, regarded as a module for the subgroup [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, proof of (3.4.5), p. 185] [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, proof of (3.7.2), p. 211]. An infinite Demushkin group is precisely a Poincaré group of dimension $2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, remark after (3.9.9), p. 231], so its dualizing module is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ as an abelian group [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.7.1), p. 210]. Hence the dualizing module of $E$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ as an abelian group. A topologically finitely generated profinite group of $p$-cohomological dimension $2$ with this property is a Poincaré group at $p$ of dimension $2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.7.2), (ii) implies (i), p. 211]. As $E$ is an infinite pro-$p$ group, it follows that $H^1(E, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ is finite, $H^2(E, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ has order $p$ and the cup product $H^1 \times H^1 \to H^2$ is nondegenerate [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.7.6), (i) implies (ii), p. 217], that is, $E$ is a Demushkin group.

Each $E_H$ has no element of order $p$ and contains $D$ as an open normal subgroup, so the same argument applies to it. The index formula for the open subgroup $D$ of the Demushkin group $E_H$ ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md), Duality) gives $n(D) - 2 = |H|(n(E_H) - 2)$.

*Part 2.* Fix $H$, put $m = |H|$, and let $r : \mathrm{Hom}(E_H, \mathbb{Z}_p) \to \mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)$ be restriction.

- $r$ is injective: a homomorphism vanishing on $D$ factors through the finite group $E_H/D$, and $\mathbb{Z}_p$ has no nonzero finite subgroup.
- The image of $r$ consists of $H$-invariants: for $F \in \mathrm{Hom}(E_H, \mathbb{Z}_p)$, $F(x^{-1}dx) = -F(x) + F(d) + F(x) = F(d)$.
- The image of $r$ contains $m \cdot \mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)^H$. Let $f$ be $H$-invariant, inducing $\bar f : D^{\mathrm{ab}} \to \mathbb{Z}_p$. For the transfer $\mathrm{Ver} : E_H^{\mathrm{ab}} \to D^{\mathrm{ab}}$, the composite $D^{\mathrm{ab}} \to E_H^{\mathrm{ab}} \to D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the norm of $E_H/D$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (1.5.9), p. 52], which sends the class of $d$ to the product of the classes of $xdx^{-1}$ over representatives $x$ of $E_H/D$. So $F = \bar f \circ \mathrm{Ver} \circ (E_H \to E_H^{\mathrm{ab}})$ is a continuous homomorphism with $F(d) = \sum_x f(xdx^{-1}) = m f(d)$ for $d \in D$.

Hence $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)^H$ has the same rank as $\mathrm{Hom}(E_H, \mathbb{Z}_p)$, which is $n(E_H) - 1 + \delta(E_H)$ because $E_H$ is a Demushkin group by part 1. By part 1 this equals $1 + \delta(E_H) + (n(D) - 2)/m$.

For the comparison of $\delta$: the canonical character $\chi_G$ of a Demushkin group $G$ is the character by which $G$ acts on its dualizing module, and $q(G) = 0$ exactly when $\chi_G$ is trivial, since $q(G)$ is the largest power of $p$ with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_G) \subseteq 1 + q(G)\mathbb{Z}_p$, where $p^\infty = 0$ ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md)). As the dualizing module of $D$ is that of $E_H$ regarded as a $D$-module, $\chi_D$ is the restriction of $\chi_{E_H}$. If $q(E_H) = 0$, then $\chi_{E_H} = 1$, so $\chi_D = 1$ and $q(D) = 0$. Thus $\delta(E_H) \le \delta(D)$. Now let $p$ be odd and $q(D) = 0$. Then $\chi_{E_H}$ is trivial on $D$, so its image is a finite $p$-subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, and this is trivial. Indeed, if $u \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ and $u^p = 1$, then $u \equiv u^p = 1 \pmod p$. If $u \ne 1$, write $u = 1 + p^k w$ with $k \ge 1$ and $w$ a unit. By the binomial theorem $u^p \equiv 1 + p^{k+1}w \pmod{p^{k+2}}$, because $\binom{p}{2}p^{2k}$ is divisible by $p^{2k+1}$, the higher middle terms by $p^{3k+1}$, and $p^{pk}$ by $p^{3k}$, with $p \ge 3$ and $k \ge 1$. So $u^p \ne 1$, a contradiction. Hence $\chi_{E_H} = 1$, $q(E_H) = 0$ and $\delta(E_H) = \delta(D)$.

*Part 3.* Let $H \subseteq P$ be the kernel of the action of $P$ on $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)$, and suppose $H \ne 1$, so $|H| \ge 2$. Then $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)^H = \mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)$, of rank $n(D) - 1 + \delta(D)$. By part 2 this rank is
$$1 + \delta(E_H) + \frac{n(D) - 2}{|H|} \le 1 + \delta(D) + \frac{n(D) - 2}{2} < 1 + \delta(D) + (n(D) - 2),$$
the last inequality because $n(D) \ge 3$. This is a contradiction, so $H = 1$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What is left for case (a).* For odd $p$, $q \ne 0$ and rank at least $3$, Corollary 2 reduces case (a) of [Tsujimura's question](demushkin-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md) to the existence of an automorphism of $D$ of order $p$ that acts trivially on $V_D$ and whose outer class has finitely many conjugates in $\mathrm{Out}(D)$. Whether a Demushkin group with $q \ne 0$ and odd $p$ has an automorphism of order $p$ acting trivially on $V_D$ is not known here. The theorem gives no information about such an automorphism $\sigma$, because $D\langle\sigma\rangle$ has an element of order $p$.
- *Rank $2$.* Let $p$ be odd, $E$ a Demushkin group of rank $2$ and $D \ne E$ an open normal subgroup, which is a Demushkin group of rank $2$ by the index formula. Then part 2 gives rank $1 + \delta(D)$ for the invariants of every $H$, the full rank of $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)$. The invariants of a finite group acting on a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module $L$ of finite rank form a submodule $L'$ with $pv \in L'$ only if $v \in L'$, so full rank forces $L' = L$. So $P$ acts trivially on $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Z}_p)$, and part 3 fails in rank $2$.
