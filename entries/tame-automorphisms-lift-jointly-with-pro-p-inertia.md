**Setting.** Let $k/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, let $k^{ur}\subset T=k_{tr}$ be its maximal unramified and tame extensions, and put
$$
G=G_k,\qquad S=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k^{ur}),\qquad
P=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/T),\qquad
\Gamma=G/P,\qquad I=S/P.
$$
Thus $S$ is full inertia and $I$ is tame inertia. All groups and maps are profinite and continuous, and commutator subgroups are closed. Set
$$
A=[P,P],\qquad M=P^{ab},\qquad E=G/A,\qquad
R=\ker(S\longrightarrow S(p)),\qquad Q=G/R,
$$
where $S(p)$ is the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $S$. Since $R$ is characteristic in $S$ and $S$ is normal in $G$, these are quotients of $G$. The quotient $Q$ is the maximal quotient of $G$ in which the image of $S$ is pro-$p$: any map with this property kills $R$. Equivalently, $Q=\operatorname{Gal}(k^{ur}(p)/k)$, where $k^{ur}(p)$ is the maximal pro-$p$ Galois extension of $k^{ur}$ inside $\bar k$.

Let $\bar\chi:\Gamma\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ be the mod-$p$ cyclotomic character, and define the canonical joint quotient
$$
J=G/(A\cap R)=\operatorname{Gal}(T^{ab}k^{ur}(p)/k).
$$

**Theorem (proved here).** For $\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$, the following are equivalent:

1. $\bar\chi\beta=\bar\chi$.
2. $\beta$ lifts to an automorphism of $J$ inducing the identity on $Q$.
3. $\beta$ lifts to an automorphism of $J$.

Here lifting means commuting with the quotient maps to $\Gamma$. In particular, when $\mu_p\subset k$, every tame automorphism has the lift in part 2; this applies to every dyadic field.

**Proof.** Step 1 of the [cyclotomic criterion](cyclotomic-criterion-for-tame-lifting-modulo-wild-commutators.md) gives a splitting $E=M\rtimes\Gamma$, and shows that every $\beta$ preserves $I$ and induces the identity on $\Gamma/I$. Its theorem supplies a $\beta$-semilinear topological automorphism $\varphi$ of $M$ whenever condition 1 holds. Thus
$$
\varphi(\gamma m)=\beta(\gamma)\varphi(m).
$$

*1. Fixing the inertia coinvariants.* The group $I$ is pro-prime-to-$p$. Its averaging idempotent $e_I\in\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$ is defined at each finite quotient $\Gamma/U$ by
$$
e_{I,U}=\frac1{|IU/U|}\sum_{h\in IU/U}h.
$$
These elements are compatible, central because $I$ is normal, and fixed by $\beta$. Consequently
$$
M=e_IM\oplus(1-e_I)M,\qquad M_I\cong e_IM,
$$
where $M_I$ denotes continuous coinvariants. For the second assertion, averaging kills every $(i-1)m$, and at each finite level $1-e_{I,U}$ is a linear combination of the $1-i$. Passing to inverse limits shows that the closed subgroup generated by these relations is exactly $(1-e_I)M$.

Semilinearity implies that $\varphi$ preserves both summands. On $e_IM$, the actions of $\gamma$ and $\beta(\gamma)$ agree because this summand is $I$-fixed and $\beta$ is the identity modulo $I$. Hence
$$
\psi(m)=e_Im+\varphi((1-e_I)m)
$$
is also a $\beta$-semilinear automorphism, and it is the identity on $e_IM$. It defines an automorphism
$$
b:E\longrightarrow E,\qquad (m,\gamma)\longmapsto(\psi(m),\beta(\gamma)).
$$

*2. Identifying the common quotient.* The image of $S$ in $E$ is $M\rtimes I$. Every homomorphism from this group to a pro-$p$ group kills its section $I$, and therefore kills $(1-e_I)M$. Conversely, projection onto $M_I=e_IM$ is a homomorphism to a pro-$p$ group. Thus its maximal pro-$p$ quotient is $e_IM$, with kernel $(1-e_I)M\rtimes I$.

For any epimorphism of profinite groups $H\twoheadrightarrow H'$, the kernel of $H\to H(p)$ maps onto the kernel of $H'\to H'(p)$. Indeed every map from $H'$ to a pro-$p$ group kills that image, and quotienting $H'$ by the image gives a quotient of $H(p)$, hence a pro-$p$ group. Apply this to $S\twoheadrightarrow M\rtimes I$. It identifies the image of $R$ in $E$, and gives
$$
C=G/(AR)\cong e_IM\rtimes(\Gamma/I).
$$
The map from $E$ to $C$ is $(m,\gamma)\mapsto(e_Im,\gamma I)$. Part 1 shows that $b$ induces the identity on $C$.

*3. Gluing.* There is a natural isomorphism
$$
J\cong E\times_C Q.
$$
Injectivity follows from the kernel $A\cap R$. For surjectivity, lift the $E$-coordinate of a compatible pair to $g\in G$. The discrepancy in its $Q$-coordinate lies in $\ker(Q\to C)$, which is the image of $A$. Multiplying $g$ on the left by a suitable element of $A$ corrects that discrepancy. Compactness makes the continuous bijection a homeomorphism. Since $b$ fixes $C$, the pair $(b,\operatorname{id}_Q)$ and its inverse preserve this fiber product. This proves $1\Rightarrow2$; $2\Rightarrow3$ is immediate.

Finally, the kernel of $J\to\Gamma$ is $P/(A\cap R)$, whose closed commutator subgroup is $A/(A\cap R)$. Any lift in condition 3 preserves that kernel and its commutator subgroup, and therefore descends to a lift on $E$. The cyclotomic criterion gives condition 1. $\square$

**Consequences and limit.** Every finite Galois quotient of $G$ whose inertia is a $p$-group factors through $Q$. The lift in part 2 fixes all these marked quotient maps simultaneously. This includes all finite $p$-group quotients, and also allows prime-to-$p$ unramified quotients acting on nonabelian wild inertia. Indeed $R\subseteq\ker(G\to G_k(p))$, so $J$ maps onto the [earlier joint quotient](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-joint-wild-abelian-and-pro-p-quotient.md) and recovers its lift when $\mu_p\subset k$.

For $k=U_f$, the [Frobenius shift](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md) therefore lifts jointly with all of $Q$ and $E$. The remaining kernel $A\cap R$ is not removed by this argument. The full lifting problem and the remaining dyadic Galois classification are left open.
