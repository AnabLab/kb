summary: For $\mathrm{char}\, K \ne \ell$, the norm residue map $K^M_n(K)/\ell \to H^n(K, \mu_\ell^{\otimes n})$ is bijective (Rost–Voevodsky). **Statement.** Let $K$ be a field and $\ell$ a prime different from the characteristic of $K$. The Kummer sequence $1 \to \mu_\ell \to K_{\mathrm{sep}}^\times \to K_{\mathrm{sep}}^\times \to 1$, whose second map is $x \mapsto x^\ell$, has a boundary map $K^\times \to H^1(K, \mu_\ell)$. Bass and Tate showed that $(a) \cup (1 - a) = 0$ in $H^2(K, \mu_\ell^{\otimes 2})$ for $a \ne 0, 1$, so cup products extend this map to a ring homomorphism on the [Milnor K-ring](milnor-k-ring.md). It factors through the norm residue homomorphism

$$K^M_*(K)/\ell \to H^*(K, \mu_\ell^{\otimes *})$$

[@voevodsky2003, §1]. The theorem says that this is an isomorphism in every degree $n$ [@voevodsky2011, Theorem 6.16].

**History.** In degree $1$ the map is injective, and its cokernel is the $\ell$-torsion of $H^1(K, K_{\mathrm{sep}}^\times)$, which vanishes by Hilbert's Theorem 90. Merkurjev proved degree $2$ for $\ell = 2$ in 1981, and Merkurjev and Suslin proved degree $2$ for all $\ell$ in 1982. Merkurjev and Suslin, and independently Rost, proved degree $3$ for $\ell = 2$. Kato formulated the general statement, and Bloch the case of function fields over $\mathbb{C}$ [@voevodsky2003, §1]. Voevodsky proved the case $\ell = 2$, the Milnor conjecture [@voevodsky2003, Corollary 7.5]. He then proved the general case, using a result announced by Rost and proved by Suslin and Joukhovitski [@voevodsky2011, §6]. Chebolu, Efrat and Mináč credit the general case to Rost and Voevodsky, with a patch by Weibel, and state it for every $m$ prime to the characteristic, with $\mu_m^{\otimes n}$ coefficients [@cheboluefratminac2012, §1]. This entry has checked the statement in Voevodsky's paper only for prime $m = \ell$.

**Consequences for cohomology rings.** Suppose now that $\mu_\ell \subset K$, and write $H^n(G)$ for $H^n(G, \mathbb{Z}/\ell)$ with trivial action.

1. *The ring is quadratic.* A primitive $\ell$-th root of unity $\zeta$ gives isomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}/\ell \to \mu_\ell^{\otimes n}$, $1 \mapsto \zeta^{\otimes n}$, compatible with cup products. So the theorem gives an isomorphism of graded $\mathbb{F}_\ell$-algebras $K^M_*(K)/\ell \cong H^*(G_K)$, which in degree $1$ is the Kummer isomorphism $K^\times/\ell \cong H^1(G_K)$. Now $K^M_*(K)/\ell$ is the tensor algebra over $\mathbb{F}_\ell$ on $V = K^\times/\ell$ modulo the ideal generated by the elements $a \otimes (1 - a)$ of degree $2$, and the degree-$2$ part of this ideal is exactly the kernel of $V \otimes V \to K^M_2(K)/\ell$. Transporting this, $H^*(G_K)$ is generated by $H^1(G_K)$, and it is the tensor algebra on $H^1(G_K)$ modulo the ideal generated by the kernel $R_K$ of the cup product $H^1(G_K) \otimes H^1(G_K) \to H^2(G_K)$. Chebolu, Efrat and Mináč deduce from the theorem that $H^*(G_K, \mathbb{Z}/q)$ is quadratic in their sense, for a power $q$ of $\ell$ with $\mu_q \subset K$ [@cheboluefratminac2012, §8].

2. *Detection in degrees $1$ and $2$.* Let $L$ be another field of characteristic not $\ell$ with $\mu_\ell \subset L$, and let $\psi : G_K \to G_L$ be a continuous homomorphism such that $\psi^*$ is bijective on $H^1$ and injective on $H^2$. Then $\psi^* : H^*(G_L) \to H^*(G_K)$ is an isomorphism. Indeed, $\psi^* \otimes \psi^*$ is bijective on $H^1 \otimes H^1$, and it maps $R_L$ into $R_K$ because $\psi^*$ respects cup products. If $(\psi^* \otimes \psi^*)(x) \in R_K$, then $\psi^*$ kills the cup product of $x$, so $x \in R_L$ by injectivity on $H^2$. Hence $\psi^* \otimes \psi^*$ maps $R_L$ onto $R_K$, and so induces an isomorphism of the quotients in (1), which is $\psi^*$. Chebolu, Efrat and Mináč state the algebraic step for their quadratic hulls [@cheboluefratminac2012, Lemma 3.1].

3. *Maximal pro-$\ell$ quotients.* Let $K(\ell)$ be the maximal pro-$\ell$ Galois extension of $K$, with $G_K(\ell) = \mathrm{Gal}(K(\ell)/K)$. Then inflation $H^*(G_K(\ell)) \to H^*(G_K)$ is an isomorphism. Indeed, $H^1(G_{K(\ell)}) = 0$ [@cheboluefratminac2012, Remark 8.2], so $K(\ell)^\times = (K(\ell)^\times)^\ell$ by Kummer theory. As $K^M_n(K(\ell))$ is generated by symbols, $K^M_n(K(\ell))/\ell = 0$ for $n \ge 1$, and by the theorem $H^n(G_{K(\ell)}) = 0$ for $n \ge 1$. So the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence of $1 \to G_{K(\ell)} \to G_K \to G_K(\ell) \to 1$ collapses, and its edge map, inflation, is an isomorphism. Chebolu, Efrat and Mináč make the same spectral sequence argument, and note that their Theorems A–C hold with $G_K(\ell)$ in place of $G_K$ [@cheboluefratminac2012, Remark 8.2].

**Role in the graph.** The theorem translates statements about the Milnor K-ring modulo $\ell$ into statements about Galois cohomology rings, and back. Chebolu, Efrat and Mináč use it, through the quadraticity in (1), to show that a small quotient of $G_K$ determines the whole cohomology ring [@cheboluefratminac2012, §8], as recorded in [abelian-by-central quotients determine Galois cohomology](abelian-by-central-galois-cohomology.md). For a function field $K$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic not $\ell$, one has $\mu_\ell \subset K$. So the data $K^\times/\ell$, $K^M_2(K)/\ell$ and the product used in the Milnor form of [mod-$\ell$ reconstruction from rational quotients](mod-l-reconstruction-from-rational-quotients.md) are $H^1$ and $H^2$ of $G_K$ with the cup product, by Kummer theory and the degree-$2$ case, and by (3) they are also those of its maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient. This last translation is made here, not taken from that source.
