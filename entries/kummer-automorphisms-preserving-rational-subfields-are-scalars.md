**Setting.** Fix a prime $\ell$ and $n\ge1$, and put $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n$. Let $K/k$ be a function field of transcendence degree $d\ge2$ over an algebraically closed field of any characteristic. Write $V_K=K^\times/K^{\times\ell^n}$ additively. If $k\subseteq E\subseteq K$ is relatively algebraically closed in $K$, the map $E^\times/E^{\times\ell^n}\to V_K$ is injective, since an $\ell^n$-th root in $K$ of an element of $E$ is algebraic over $E$. Write $V_E$ for its image.

For a [prime divisor](quasi-prime-divisor.md) $v$ of $K/k$, normalized by $vK=\mathbb Z$, let $U_v\subseteq V_K$ be the image of the $v$-units. As $k^\times$ is $\ell^n$-divisible, $v$ induces $\bar v:V_K\to\Lambda$, and
$$U_v=\ker\bar v.$$
Indeed, if $v(f)=\ell^nm$ and $v(\varpi)=1$, then $f\varpi^{-\ell^nm}$ is a unit. For $n=1$ the annihilator of $U_v$ in $\mathrm{Hom}(V_K,\mathbb F_\ell)$ is the line $\mathbb F_\ell\bar v$. Since $\mathbb F_\ell$ is a field, an automorphism permutes the $U_v$ exactly when its dual permutes these lines.

Let $\mathcal T_0(K)$ be the set of quotients $a/b$, where $R$ is a closed point of some model of $K/k$ with regular local ring, and $a,b\in\mathfrak m_R$ have linearly independent images in $\mathfrak m_R/\mathfrak m_R^2$.

**Statement.**

1. Let $\Psi:V_K\to V_K$ be a group automorphism such that:
   - (a) $\Psi(V_{k(t)})=V_{k(t)}$ for every $t\in\mathcal T_0(K)$;
   - (b) there is a map $\pi$ from the prime divisors of $K/k$ to themselves with $\Psi(U_v)=U_{\pi(v)}$ for every $v$.

   Then $\Psi$ is multiplication by a unique $a\in\Lambda^\times$.
2. Assume $\mathrm{char}\,k\ne\ell$. Let $L/l$ be a second such function field, and let $\alpha:K^i\to L^i$ be an isomorphism of perfect closures with $\alpha(k)=l$. Then $\alpha$ induces an isomorphism $\alpha_1:V_K\to V_L$, and a bijection $v\mapsto\alpha_*v$ of prime divisors with $\alpha_1(U_v)=U_{\alpha_*v}$. If $\alpha(k(t)^i)=P^i$, then $\alpha_1(V_{k(t)})=V_P$. Let $\Theta:V_K\to V_L$ be a group isomorphism such that $\Theta(V_{k(t)})=\alpha_1(V_{k(t)})$ for every $t\in\mathcal T_0(K)$, and such that $\Theta(U_v)=U_{\theta(v)}$ for some map $\theta$ of prime divisors. Then $\Theta=a\,\alpha_1$ for a unique $a\in\Lambda^\times$.

**Relation to the manuscript.** In the October 2026 manuscript, $n=1$, and $\Theta$ is compatible with the Milnor product [@openai2026modl, §1.1, p. 2]. Its Proposition 6.1 says that the dual of $\Theta$ matches the inertia and decomposition groups of true prime divisors [@openai2026modl, Proposition 6.1, p. 24]. Under Kummer duality the inertia of a prime divisor is the annihilator of its units, as in the description of minimized inertia in [prime divisors](quasi-prime-divisor.md), so the inertia part is (b) for $\Theta$. Its Proposition 6.3 matches the good rational subfields, and Lemma 6.4 shows that $\mathcal T_0(K)$ consists of good functions [@openai2026modl, Proposition 6.3 and Lemma 6.4, pp. 25–26]. Its Theorem 7.5 then gives $\alpha$ with $\alpha(E^i)=P^i$ for matched good subfields [@openai2026modl, Theorem 7.5, p. 31]. Part 1 is its Proposition 8.3, with the input from Proposition 6.1 made a hypothesis, and part 2 is the deduction that follows it [@openai2026modl, §8.2, pp. 33–34].

The manuscript also uses decomposition groups. It distinguishes prime divisors by the rank of their restriction to a curve subfield (Lemma 8.1), and builds a separating subfield (Lemma 8.2) [@openai2026modl, Lemmas 8.1–8.2, p. 33]. Here only the unit groups $U_v$ enter: the separating function in Step 1 is built with $v_2(t)=1$.

**Proof.** *Preliminaries.*

- (P1) Let $t=a/b\in\mathcal T_0(K)$ at $R$. As $\mathcal O_R$ is regular, its associated graded ring is a polynomial ring over $k$ in $d$ variables. So the $\mathfrak m_R$-adic order $v_R$ is a valuation, trivial on $k$, with residue field $k(\mathbb P^{d-1})$. By the [characterization](quasi-prime-divisor.md) of prime divisors by residue transcendence degree, it is a prime divisor. The residue $\bar a/\bar b$ of $t$ is a ratio of independent linear forms, an affine coordinate of the purely transcendental field $k(\mathbb P^{d-1})$. So $v_R(g(t))=0$ for every nonzero $g\in k[T]$, and $v_R$ is trivial on $k(t)$. It is then trivial on the relative algebraic closure $E'$ of $k(t)$ in $K$, whose value group is torsion and hence zero. So reduction embeds $E'$ into $k(\mathbb P^{d-1})$, with image algebraic over $k(\bar t)$. As $k(\bar t)$ is relatively algebraically closed there, and $k(t)$ already maps onto it, $E'=k(t)$ [@openai2026modl, proof of Lemma 6.4, pp. 25–26].

  In characteristic $p>0$ it follows that $t\notin K^p$: a $p$-th root of $t$ would be algebraic over $k(t)$ without lying in it. So $1,t,\dots,t^{p-1}$ are linearly independent over $kK^p=K^p$. That is, $t$ is $p$-independent over $k$ [@stacks2026, Definition 15.47.1, Tag 07P1], and so $dt\ne0$ in $\Omega_{K/k}$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 15.47.2, Tag 07P2]. In characteristic $0$, $dt\ne0$ for every $t\notin k$.
- (P2) *Points are detected with multiplicity one.* Keep $t=a/b$ at $R$, and let $x\in k$. Since $a-xb\in\mathfrak m_R\setminus\mathfrak m_R^2$, the ring $\mathcal O_R/(a-xb)$ is regular, hence a domain. So $(a-xb)$ is a height-one prime of $\mathcal O_R$, the generic point of a prime divisor $D_x$ on a normal neighbourhood of $R$, and $\mathrm{ord}_{D_x}(a-xb)=1$. The image of $b$ in $\mathfrak m_R/\mathfrak m_R^2$ is not a multiple of that of $a-xb$, so $b\notin(a-xb)$ and $\mathrm{ord}_{D_x}(b)=0$. Thus $\mathrm{ord}_{D_x}(t-x)=1$. Likewise $V(b)$ gives a prime divisor $D_\infty$ with $\mathrm{ord}_{D_\infty}(1/t)=1$. A valuation $v$ trivial on $k$ with $v(t-x)=1$ has $v(t-y)=0$ for $y\ne x$, so it restricts to $\mathrm{ord}_x$ on $k(t)$, and similarly at $\infty$ [@openai2026modl, proof of Lemma 6.4, p. 26].
- (P3) *Generation.* For $f\in K^\times$, choose $R$ with $f$ a unit at $R$, and $a,b$ as above. Then $fa\equiv f(R)a$ modulo $\mathfrak m_R^2$, so $t=a/b$ and $ft=fa/b$ lie in $\mathcal T_0(K)$, and $[f]=[ft]-[t]$. So the $V_{k(t)}$ with $t\in\mathcal T_0(K)$ span $V_K$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 6.4, p. 26].
- (P4) *Order valuations.* Let $Q$ be a closed point of a smooth model at which $t$ is regular and $dt(Q)\ne0$. As $f\mapsto df(Q)$ identifies $\mathfrak m_Q/\mathfrak m_Q^2$ with $\Omega\otimes\kappa(Q)$, we get $t-t(Q)\notin\mathfrak m_Q^2$. So the order valuation $v_Q$, a prime divisor as in (P1), has $v_Q(t-t(Q))=1$.

*Step 1: $\pi$ is the identity.* We first show that for prime divisors $v_1\ne v_2$ there is $t\in\mathcal T_0(K)$ with $v_1$ trivial on $k(t)$ and $v_2(t)=1$. Given this, $V_{k(t)}\subseteq U_{v_1}$, so by (a) and (b), $V_{k(t)}=\Psi(V_{k(t)})\subseteq U_{\pi(v_1)}$. But $[t]\in V_{k(t)}$ is not in $U_{v_2}$, since $\bar v_2([t])=1$. So $\pi(v_1)\ne v_2$ for every $v_2\ne v_1$.

To build $t$, realize $v_i$ as $\mathrm{ord}_{D_i}$ on a normal projective model $X_i$: shrink a normal model on which $v_i$ is a Weil divisor to an affine open meeting it, close it up in projective space, and normalize. Let $X$ be the normalization of the closure of the graph of $X_1\dashrightarrow X_2$. The centre $Z_i$ of $v_i$ on $X$ maps to the generic point of $D_i$, so $k(D_i)\subseteq\kappa(Z_i)\subseteq Kv_i$. All three have transcendence degree $d-1$, so $Z_i$ is a prime divisor of $X$. Its local ring is a discrete valuation ring dominated by $\mathcal O_{v_i}$, hence equal to it. Rename $Z_i$ as $D_i$ [@openai2026modl, proof of Lemma 8.2, p. 33].

Choose distinct closed points $P,Q\in D_1\setminus D_2$ (possible as $d-1\ge1$), a closed point $S\in D_2\setminus D_1$, and a regular closed point $R\notin D_1\cup D_2$. Let $\mathrm{Spec}\,A\subseteq X$ be an affine open containing $P,Q,R,S$, and let $\mathfrak p_i\subset A$ be the prime of $D_i$. Choose $x_2\in A$ with $v_2(x_2)=1$, a numerator of a uniformizer of $A_{\mathfrak p_2}$. The ideals $\mathfrak q_2=\{g\in A:v_2(g)\ge2\}$, $\mathfrak m_P$, $\mathfrak m_Q$ and $\mathfrak m_R^2$ have radicals $\mathfrak p_2,\mathfrak m_P,\mathfrak m_Q,\mathfrak m_R$, which are pairwise comaximal since $P,Q,R$ are distinct and not on $D_2$. Choose $\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak m_R$ with independent images in $\mathfrak m_R/\mathfrak m_R^2$, which has dimension $d\ge2$. By the Chinese remainder theorem there are $a,b\in A$ with

| | $\mathfrak q_2$ | $\mathfrak m_P$ | $\mathfrak m_Q$ | $\mathfrak m_R^2$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $a\equiv$ | $x_2$ | $0$ | $1$ | $\alpha$ |
| $b\equiv$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ | $\beta$ |

Then $t=a/b\in\mathcal T_0(K)$, at $R$. Next, $v_2(a)=1$ and $v_2(b)=0$, so $v_2(t)=1$. As $b(P)=1$ and $a(Q)=1$, neither $a$ nor $b$ lies in $\mathfrak p_1$. So $t$ is a $v_1$-unit, and its residue in $k(D_1)$ takes the values $0$ at $P$ and $1$ at $Q$. Hence the residue is not in $k$, so it is transcendental over the algebraically closed field $k$. Then every nonzero $g(t)$ with $g\in k[T]$ has nonzero residue $g(\bar t)$, so $v_1$ is trivial on $k(t)$.

*Step 2: $\Psi$ is a scalar on each $V_{k(t)}$.* Fix $t\in\mathcal T_0(K)$ and $E=k(t)$, and let $\Psi_E$ be the restriction of $\Psi$, an automorphism of $V_E$ by (a). For $x\in\mathbb P^1(k)$ the order $\mathrm{ord}_x$ induces a surjection $V_E\to\Lambda$, with kernel $N_x$. The map $\delta=(\mathrm{ord}_x)_x:V_E\to\bigoplus_{x}\Lambda$ is injective. Indeed, if every order of $f\in E^\times$ is divisible by $\ell^n$, then $\mathrm{div}\,f=\ell^n\,\mathrm{div}\,g$ on $\mathbb P^1$, and $f/g^{\ell^n}$ is a constant, which is an $\ell^n$-th power.

By (P2) there is, for each $x$, a prime divisor $v$ restricting to $\mathrm{ord}_x$ on $E$, so $U_v\cap V_E=N_x$. By Step 1, $\Psi(U_v)=U_v$, so $\Psi_E(N_x)=N_x$. Hence $\mathrm{ord}_x\circ\Psi_E$ vanishes on $N_x$. It therefore factors through $\mathrm{ord}_x$, as $\mathrm{ord}_x\circ\Psi_E=a_x\,\mathrm{ord}_x$ with $a_x\in\Lambda$. Principal divisors on $\mathbb P^1$ have degree $0$, so for every $f\in V_E$,
$$0=\sum_x\mathrm{ord}_x(\Psi_Ef)=\sum_xa_x\,\mathrm{ord}_x(f).$$
Taking $f=[t-c]$ gives $a_c=a_\infty$ for every $c\in k$. Call the common value $a_E$. Then $\delta(\Psi_Ef)=\delta(a_Ef)$, so $\Psi_E=a_E\,\mathrm{id}$. Since $\Psi_E$ is onto and $\mathrm{ord}_0[t]=1$, $[t]\notin\ell V_E$, so $a_E\in\Lambda^\times$ [@openai2026modl, proof of Proposition 8.3, p. 34].

*Step 3: the scalars agree.* Let $t,s\in\mathcal T_0(K)$. By (P1), $dt$ and $ds$ are nonzero, so on some smooth model there is a dense open set on which $t,s$ are regular and $dt,ds$ vanish nowhere. Take a closed point $Q$ in it. By (P4), $v_Q(t-t(Q))=v_Q(s-s(Q))=1$. By Step 1, $\Psi$ preserves $U_{v_Q}=\ker\bar v_Q$. As $\bar v_Q$ is onto, $\bar v_Q\circ\Psi=c\,\bar v_Q$ for some $c\in\Lambda$. Evaluating at $[t-t(Q)]$ and using Step 2 gives $a_{k(t)}=c$, and likewise $a_{k(s)}=c$ [@openai2026modl, proof of Proposition 8.3, p. 34].

*Step 4.* With $a$ the common scalar, $\Psi=a\,\mathrm{id}$ on every $V_{k(t)}$, and these span $V_K$ by (P3). The scalar is unique because $V_K$ has elements of order $\ell^n$. This proves 1.

*Proof of 2.* Let $p$ be the characteristic exponent, so $p$ is invertible in $\Lambda$. The inclusion $K\subseteq K^i$ induces an isomorphism $V_K\to V_{K^i}$ [@openai2026modl, §1.1, p. 2]. Concretely, if $x\in K^i$ has $x^{p^r}\in K$ and $cp^r\equiv1$ modulo $\ell^n$, then $[x]=c[x^{p^r}]$. The same formula shows that for relatively algebraically closed $E$, the image of $(E^i)^\times$ corresponds to $V_E$. Moreover, the image of $\mathcal O_w^\times$ corresponds to $U_v$, where $w$ is the unique extension of $v$ to $K^i$. Define $\alpha_1$ through these isomorphisms.

The extension $w$ is trivial on $k$, and its residue field is purely inseparable over $Kv$. Conversely, any valuation of $K^i$ trivial on $k$ with residue transcendence degree $d-1$ restricts to a prime divisor of $K/k$, by the [characterization](quasi-prime-divisor.md) of prime divisors by residue transcendence degree. As $\alpha$ preserves this description and $\mathrm{trdeg}(L/l)=d$, it gives the bijection $\alpha_*$ with $\alpha_1(U_v)=U_{\alpha_*v}$. If $\alpha(k(t)^i)=P^i$, then $\alpha_1(V_{k(t)})=V_P$ by the same correspondence. Now $\Psi=\alpha_1^{-1}\Theta$ satisfies (a), and (b) with $\pi=\alpha_*^{-1}\circ\theta$. So $\Psi=a\,\mathrm{id}$ by part 1. $\square$

**Pro-$\ell$ analogue.** For $\ell$-adic characters, the same argument, with decomposition groups and all relatively algebraically closed rational subfields, is carried out in [global scalar rigidity for pro-$\ell$ rational quotients](global-scalar-rigidity-for-pro-l-rational-quotients.md). That entry supplies the scalar step after [simultaneous values from pro-$\ell$ rational quotients](simultaneous-values-from-pro-l-rational-quotients.md).

**Checks.**

- *Hypothesis (a) is needed.* For $K=k(x,y)$ and $\alpha$ the automorphism swapping $x$ and $y$, the map $\alpha_1$ satisfies (b), by the proof of 2. But $\alpha_1[x]=[y]$ is not a multiple of $[x]$, as the orders along $x=0$ and $y=0$ show. So, by part 1, $\alpha_1$ violates (a).
- *Transcendence degree $2$ is needed.* For $K=k(t)$, $\mathcal T_0(K)$ is empty, since cotangent spaces have dimension one, so (a) is empty. Also $V_K$ is the group of degree-zero divisors on $\mathbb P^1(k)$ with coefficients in $\Lambda$, and the prime divisors are the points. Any permutation of $\mathbb P^1(k)$ induces an automorphism satisfying (b), and it is not a scalar unless it is the identity.
- *Characteristic.* Part 1 does not use $\mathrm{char}\,k\ne\ell$. Part 2 does, because in characteristic $\ell$ the group $(K^i)^\times$ is $\ell$-divisible.
