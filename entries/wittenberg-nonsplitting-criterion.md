**Setting.** Let $k$ be a number field with set of places $\Omega$, and let $X$ be a smooth projective geometrically irreducible curve over $k$. Manin's pairing
$$\Big(\prod_{v \in \Omega} \mathrm{Pic}(X \otimes_k k_v)\Big) \times \mathrm{Br}(X) \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$$
is the sum of the local pairings, and its left kernel contains the diagonal image of $\mathrm{Pic}(X)$. A subgroup $B \subseteq \mathrm{Br}(X)$ is *responsible for a Brauer–Manin obstruction to divisors of degree $1$* if no element of $\prod_v \mathrm{Pic}^1(X \otimes_k k_v)$ is orthogonal to $B$ [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, p. 2]. For $N \ge 1$, write $c$ for cycle class maps $\mathrm{Pic} \to H^2(-, \mu_N)$ and $p : H^2(X, \mu_N) \to \mathrm{Br}(X)$ for the natural map. Given a subgroup $\Gamma \subseteq H^2(X, \mu_N)$, Wittenberg considers two hypotheses [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, pp. 2–3]:

- (BM) $\mathrm{Pic}^1(X \otimes_k k_v) \neq \emptyset$ for every $v$, and $p(\Gamma)$ is responsible for a Brauer–Manin obstruction to divisors of degree $1$ on $X$.
- (T) For every place $v$ dividing $N$ and every $\gamma \in \Gamma$, the image $\gamma_v$ of $\gamma$ in $H^2(X \otimes_k k_v, \mu_N)$ can be written $\gamma_{v,0} + c(\gamma_{v,1})$ with $\gamma_{v,0} \in H^2(k_v, \mu_N)$ and $\gamma_{v,1} \in \mathrm{Pic}(X \otimes_k k_v)_{\mathrm{tors}}$.

(BM) implies $\mathrm{Pic}^1(X) = \emptyset$, so $X(k) = \emptyset$.

**Theorem (Wittenberg).** If (BM) and (T) hold, then $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$ has no section [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, Théorème 2.1, p. 3]. This holds even when $X$ has genus $1$ [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, p. 3].

In practice: if $A \in \mathrm{Br}(X)$ is responsible for a Brauer–Manin obstruction to divisors of degree $1$, it suffices to find $N$ and a lift $\gamma \in H^2(X, \mu_N)$ of $A$ whose image in $H^2(X \otimes_k k_v, \mu_N)$, for each $v$ dividing $N$, lies in the subgroup generated by $H^2(k_v, \mu_N)$ and $c(\mathrm{Pic}(X \otimes_k k_v)_{\mathrm{tors}})$ [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, p. 3].

*Proof outline* [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, pp. 3–5].

- Suppose $s$ is a section. (BM) forces genus at least $1$, so $X$ is a $K(\pi, 1)$, and $s$ has a cycle class $\alpha \in H^2(X, \mu_N)$ in the sense of Esnault and Wittenberg, with image $1$ in $H^2(X \otimes_k \bar k, \mu_N) = \mathbb{Z}/N$.
- At a place $v$ not dividing $N$, $s$ restricts to a section over $k_v$ whose cycle class is the image $\alpha_v$ of $\alpha$. By Esnault and Wittenberg's local results, $\alpha_v = c(D_v)$ for some $D_v \in \mathrm{Pic}^1(X \otimes_k k_v)$ (Proposition 2.2). At places dividing $N$, choose any $D_v \in \mathrm{Pic}^1(X \otimes_k k_v)$.
- Cup product followed by the Hochschild–Serre edge map gives a pairing $\langle -, - \rangle$ on $H^2(X \otimes_k K, \mu_N)$ with values in $\mathrm{Br}(K)$, compatible with Manin's local pairing (Lemma 2.3). For every $v$ and $\gamma \in \Gamma$, $\langle \alpha_v, \gamma_v \rangle = \langle c(D_v), \gamma_v \rangle$ (Proposition 2.4). At $v$ dividing $N$ this uses (T): $p(\alpha_v)$ is infinitely divisible, because $\alpha$ lifts to $\varprojlim_m H^2(X, \mu_m)$, so it is orthogonal to torsion divisor classes, and classes from the base pair trivially with classes that vanish geometrically.
- Global reciprocity for $\langle \alpha, \gamma \rangle \in \mathrm{Br}(k)$ then makes $(D_v)_v$ orthogonal to $p(\gamma)$ for every $\gamma \in \Gamma$, contradicting (BM).

Wittenberg stresses that, for the curves below, the only global arithmetic input is the reciprocity law for the Brauer group of $\mathbb{Q}$, while Harari and Szamuely's method needs fine information on the Jacobian, such as the finiteness of its Tate–Shafarevich group and its rank [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, pp. 1–2].

**The Reichardt–Lind and Schinzel curves.** Both are smooth projective curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with real points and $p$-adic points for every $p$, but no rational point and no divisor of degree $1$ [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, p. 1].

- The Reichardt–Lind curve has genus $1$. It is the intersection in $\mathbb{P}^3$ of the quadrics $w^2 = xz$ and $2y^2 = x^2 - 17z^2$, and the smooth compactification of the affine curve $2y^2 = w^4 - 17$ [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, pp. 1 and 6].
- The Schinzel curve is the plane quartic $x^4 - 17z^4 = 2(y^2 + 4z^2)^2$, of genus $3$ [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, p. 1].

*Theorem (Wittenberg).* For both curves, $\pi_1(X) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has no section [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, Théorème 1.1, p. 1]. For the Schinzel curve, even the geometrically abelianized sequence does not split [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, Remarque 3.5, p. 8].

*How the criterion applies* [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, §3, pp. 5–8].

- The map $[x : y : z] \mapsto [xz : x^2 : y^2 + 4z^2 : z^2]$ sends the Schinzel curve to the Reichardt–Lind curve, and the fundamental exact sequence is functorial, so it suffices to treat the Reichardt–Lind curve. As checked here, the map lands on that curve: with $(w, x, y, z) = (XZ, X^2, Y^2 + 4Z^2, Z^2)$ one has $w^2 = xz$, and $2y^2 = X^4 - 17Z^4 = x^2 - 17z^2$ by the equation of the Schinzel curve. The same functoriality for the abelianized sequence, which equals the full one in genus $1$, gives the abelianized statement.
- On the Reichardt–Lind curve, the divisor of $y$ is $P - 2Q$ with closed points $P$ and $Q$ of degrees $4$ and $2$. With $N = 2$, $\gamma$ is the unique class in $H^2(X, \mathbb{Z}/2)$ restricting to $[y] \cup [17]$ on $X \setminus \{P\}$. Its image in $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ is the quaternion algebra $(y, 17)$, which gives the classical Brauer–Manin obstruction; in genus $1$, $\mathrm{Pic}^1(X_K) = X(K)$, so this is an obstruction to divisors of degree $1$, and (BM) holds.
- For (T) at $v = 2$: $\gamma$ vanishes geometrically (Proposition 3.2); geometrically trivial classes that vanish on the complement of $P$ come from torsion divisor classes, because $P$ is the $2$-torsion of an elliptic curve structure on $X$ (Proposition 3.4); and $\gamma$ vanishes on the complement of $P$ over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, because $17$ is a square in $\mathbb{Q}_2$.

**Significance.**

- *The Reichardt–Lind curve.* Stix proved that the affine Reichardt–Lind curve has no section and asked whether the projective curve has one [@stix2011bm, Question 34, p. 23]; see [descent obstructions](descent-obstructions-for-galois-sections.md). Wittenberg's theorem answers this question [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, p. 1]. The section conjecture makes no prediction in genus $1$, and indeed some genus-one curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with points everywhere locally do have sections; see [genus-one curves](galois-sections-of-genus-one-curves.md).
- *The Schinzel curve.* It satisfies the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) in a way that no local obstruction explains. Wittenberg calls it the second known example, after the curve of Harari, Szamuely and Flynn (see [abelianized sections](abelianized-galois-sections.md)), of a smooth projective curve of genus at least $2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ with adelic points and no section [@wittenberg2012reichardtlind, p. 1]. That statement is as of 2010.
