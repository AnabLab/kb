**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of degree $d$, and $G = G_K$, with inertia group $I_K$ and wild inertia group $P_K$. Let $\chi : G \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ be the cyclotomic character and $\omega : G \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ its reduction modulo $p$, the action on $\mu_p$, so that $\ker\omega = G_{K(\mu_p)}$. For $n \ge 1$ let $K_n \subset \bar K$ be the unramified extension of $K$ of degree $n$, and put $L_n = K_n(\mu_p)$. Let $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ be the maximal unramified extension of $K$, put $\tilde K = K^{\mathrm{ur}}(\mu_p) = \bigcup_{n \ge 1} L_n$, and let $\Omega_K$ be the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $\tilde K$. For a finite extension $L/K$ let $L(p)$ be its maximal pro-$p$ extension, so that $\mathrm{Gal}(L(p)/L) = G_L(p)$, and put $V_L = G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathbb{Q}_p$.

Every automorphism $\sigma$ of $G$ preserves $I_K$ and $P_K$, preserves the Frobenius element of $G/I_K \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, which generates it topologically, and satisfies $\chi \circ \sigma = \chi$ ([invariants recovered from $G_K$](local-field-galois-invariants.md)). So $\sigma$ induces the identity on $G/I_K$, and $\omega \circ \sigma = \omega$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Suppose $d \le p - 2$, and let $\sigma$ be an automorphism of $G_K$ of finite order $p^a$.

1. For every $n$ prime to $p$, $\sigma$ maps $G_{L_n}$ onto itself, acts trivially on $V_{L_n}$, and induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(L_n(p)/K)$.
2. $\Omega_K = \bigcup_{p \nmid n} L_n(p)$, and $\sigma$ maps $G_{\Omega_K}$ onto itself and induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$. Since $K(p)$, $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and $K(\mu_{p^\infty})$ lie in $\Omega_K$, $\sigma$ induces the identity on $G_K(p)$, on $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ur}}/K)$ and on $\mathrm{Gal}(K(\mu_{p^\infty})/K)$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here. Suppose $d \le p - 2$.

1. *Outer automorphisms.* $G_{\Omega_K}$ is characteristic in $G_K$, so there are homomorphisms $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K) \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K))$ and $\mathrm{Out}(G_K) \to \mathrm{Out}(\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K))$. Every element of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ of order $p$ lies in the kernel of the second.
2. *Tsujimura's question.* Let $N$ be a rival copy of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ of type (a) in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 2, with $M = N \cap G_K$, and suppose that $p$ divides $(G_K : M)$. Then the automorphism of order $p$ in $NG_K$, lying in neither $G_K$ nor $N$, that the Corollary of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) provides induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$.

**Proof of part 1.** Fix $n$ prime to $p$, write $L = L_n$, and let $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$.

*Step 1: $\sigma$ preserves $G_L$ and acts trivially on $\Gamma$.* We have $G_L = G_{K(\mu_p)} \cap G_{K_n}$. The first group is $\ker\omega$, preserved because $\omega \circ \sigma = \omega$. The second is the inverse image of $n\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ under $G \to G/I_K \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, preserved because $\sigma$ preserves $I_K$ and induces the identity on $G/I_K$. Restriction embeds $\Gamma$ into $\mathrm{Gal}(K(\mu_p)/K) \times \mathrm{Gal}(K_n/K)$. On the first factor $\sigma$ induces the identity, since $\omega$ embeds it into $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ and $\omega \circ \sigma = \omega$. On the second factor, a quotient of $G/I_K$, it induces the identity. So $\sigma$ acts trivially on $\Gamma$. In particular $\Gamma$ is abelian, and its order $N$ divides $(p - 1)n$, so $p \nmid N$.

*Step 2: the action on $V_L$ commutes with $\Gamma$.* Let $A$ be the automorphism of $V_L$ induced by $\sigma$, so $A^{p^a} = 1$. An element $\gamma \in \Gamma$ acts on $G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}$ by conjugation by any lift $s \in G$, and on $V_L$ accordingly. By Step 1, $\sigma(s) = sh$ with $h \in G_L$, and conjugation by $h$ is trivial on $G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Since $\sigma(sxs^{-1}) = \sigma(s)\sigma(x)\sigma(s)^{-1}$ for $x \in G_L$, this gives $A \circ \gamma = \gamma \circ A$.

*Step 3: $A$ is trivial on the inertia part.* Let $I \subseteq V_L$ be the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-span of the image of the inertia group $I_L = I_K \cap G_L$, which $\sigma$ preserves, so $A(I) = I$. By facts (i) and (ii) of [Lubin–Tate representations of non-Galois fields](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), applied to $L$, the map $\log_L \circ \mathrm{rec}_L^{-1}$ identifies $I$ with $L_+$, as in the Setting of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md). This identification is $\Gamma$-equivariant: local reciprocity commutes with conjugation, $\mathrm{rec}_L(\gamma(x)) = s\,\mathrm{rec}_L(x)\,s^{-1}$ for a lift $s$ of $\gamma$, as recalled in the proof of part 1 (a) of [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on norm-one units](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md), and $\log_L$ commutes with field automorphisms, being a power series with rational coefficients. By the normal basis theorem $L \cong K[\Gamma]$ as $K[\Gamma]$-modules, so $I \cong L_+ \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]^d$ as $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]$-modules.

Let $F = \mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_N)$. Since $p \nmid N$, $N$ divides $p^f - 1$ for some $f$, and $\mu_N \subseteq \mu_{p^f - 1}$ lies in the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $f$, whose residue field is $\mathbb{F}_{p^f}$ ([multiplicative groups](multiplicative-group-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). So $F/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is unramified, and every character $\varepsilon : \Gamma \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p^\times$ takes values in $F$. The elements $e_\varepsilon = N^{-1}\sum_{\gamma} \varepsilon(\gamma)^{-1}\gamma$ of $F[\Gamma]$ are orthogonal idempotents with sum $1$ and $\gamma e_\varepsilon = \varepsilon(\gamma)e_\varepsilon$, so $F[\Gamma] = \bigoplus_\varepsilon F e_\varepsilon$. Hence $I_F = I \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} F \cong F[\Gamma]^d$ is the direct sum of the subspaces $I_\varepsilon = e_\varepsilon I_F$, each of dimension $d$ over $F$. By Step 2, $A \otimes 1$ commutes with every $e_\varepsilon$, so it preserves each $I_\varepsilon$.

Fix $\varepsilon$. On $I_\varepsilon$, $A^{p^a} = 1$, so $A$ is diagonalizable over an algebraic closure of $F$, with eigenvalues $p^a$-th roots of unity, and its characteristic polynomial lies in $F[x]$ and has degree $d$. Suppose that an eigenvalue $\zeta \ne 1$ occurs, of order $p^b$ with $b \ge 1$. The extension $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is totally ramified of degree $(p-1)p^{b-1}$ ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)). The ramification index of $F(\zeta)$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is at least that of $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta)$, and its residue degree is at least that of $F$, which equals $[F : \mathbb{Q}_p]$. So $[F(\zeta) : F] \ge (p-1)p^{b-1} \ge p - 1$. The minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $F$ divides the characteristic polynomial, so $d \ge p - 1$, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence every eigenvalue is $1$, and $A$ is the identity on $I_\varepsilon$. So $A$ is the identity on $I_F$, hence on $I$.

*Step 4: $A = 1$.* The quotient $V_L/I$ is spanned by the image of $G_L/I_L$, a subgroup of $G/I_K$ on which $\sigma$ induces the identity. So $A$ is the identity on $I$ and on $V_L/I$, and $(A - 1)^2 = 0$. The minimal polynomial of $A$ divides $(x-1)^2$ and $x^{p^a} - 1$, which has no repeated roots over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, so it is $x - 1$ and $A = 1$.

*Step 5: $G_L(p)$.* As $\zeta_p \in L$, $D = G_L(p)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $[L : \mathbb{Q}_p] + 2 \ge 3$ ([maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md)). The kernel of $G_L \to D$ is characteristic in $G_L$, so $\sigma$ induces an automorphism $\bar\sigma$ of $D$ with $\bar\sigma^{p^a} = 1$. Since $D^{\mathrm{ab}} = G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$, the group $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ modulo torsion is a lattice in $V_L$, on which $\bar\sigma$ acts as $A = 1$. If $\bar\sigma \ne 1$, it has finite order $p^c > 1$ and acts trivially on $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ modulo torsion, contradicting Corollary 1 (1) of [automorphisms of order $p$ of Demushkin groups](order-p-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-act-nontrivially-modulo-torsion.md). So $\bar\sigma = 1$.

*Step 6: $\mathrm{Gal}(L(p)/K)$.* The subgroup $G_{L(p)} = \ker(G_L \to D)$ is characteristic in $G_L$, which is normal in $G$ and preserved by $\sigma$. So $G_{L(p)}$ is normal in $G$ and preserved by $\sigma$, and $\sigma$ induces an automorphism $\sigma_Q$ of $Q = G/G_{L(p)} = \mathrm{Gal}(L(p)/K)$. It is the identity on the normal subgroup $D$, by Step 5, and on $Q/D = \Gamma$, by Step 1. For $x \in Q$ put $z = x^{-1}\sigma_Q(x) \in D$. For $y \in D$, $xyx^{-1} \in D$, so
$$xyx^{-1} = \sigma_Q(xyx^{-1}) = \sigma_Q(x)\,y\,\sigma_Q(x)^{-1} = xzyz^{-1}x^{-1},$$
and $z$ centralizes $D$. A Demushkin group of rank at least $3$ has trivial centre ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 4), so $z = 1$ and $\sigma_Q = \mathrm{id}$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $\Omega'$ be the union of the $L_n(p)$ with $p \nmid n$. If $n \mid n'$, then $L_n(p)L_{n'}$ is a pro-$p$ extension of $L_{n'}$, so $L_n(p) \subseteq L_{n'}(p)$. As any two integers prime to $p$ divide their product, $\Omega'$ is a field.

*$\Omega' \subseteq \Omega_K$.* For $p \nmid n$, $L_n \subseteq \tilde K$, and $L_n(p)\tilde K/\tilde K$ is Galois with group isomorphic to a closed subgroup of the pro-$p$ group $\mathrm{Gal}(L_n(p)/L_n)$. So $L_n(p) \subseteq \Omega_K$.

*$\Omega_K \subseteq \Omega'$.* First, $\tilde K \subseteq \Omega'$. Indeed $\mu_p \subset L_1$. Write $m = p^k m'$ with $p \nmid m'$. Then $K_m = K_{p^k}K_{m'}$, where $K_{m'} \subseteq L_{m'}$, and $K_{p^k}L_{m'}/L_{m'}$ is cyclic of degree dividing $p^k$, so $K_{p^k} \subseteq L_{m'}(p)$. Now let $E/\tilde K$ be a finite Galois extension of $p$-power degree, $E = \tilde K(\theta)$, and let $g$ be the minimal polynomial of $\theta$ over $\tilde K$. Its roots lie in $E$, so they are polynomials in $\theta$ with coefficients in $\tilde K$. Choose $m'$ prime to $p$ and $k \ge 0$ such that the field $E_0 = L_{m'}K_{p^k}$ contains the coefficients of $g$ and of these polynomials. Then $E_1 = E_0(\theta)$ is a splitting field over $E_0$ of the separable polynomial $g$, which is irreducible over $E_0 \subseteq \tilde K$. So $E_1/E_0$ is Galois of degree $[E : \tilde K]$, a power of $p$. Since $E_0/L_{m'}$ is Galois of $p$-power degree, the conjugates of $E_1$ over $L_{m'}$ are Galois extensions of $E_0$ of $p$-power degree, and so is their compositum $E_2$, whose Galois group over $E_0$ embeds into a product of $p$-groups. Hence $E_2/L_{m'}$ is Galois of $p$-power degree, $E_2 \subseteq L_{m'}(p) \subseteq \Omega'$, and $\theta \in \Omega'$. So $E \subseteq \Omega'$. Since $\Omega_K$ is the union of such $E$, $\Omega_K \subseteq \Omega'$.

*The action.* By part 1, each $G_{L_n(p)}$ with $p \nmid n$ is preserved by $\sigma$, and $g^{-1}\sigma(g) \in G_{L_n(p)}$ for every $g \in G$. Hence $G_{\Omega_K} = \bigcap_{p \nmid n} G_{L_n(p)}$ is preserved by $\sigma$, and $g^{-1}\sigma(g) \in G_{\Omega_K}$ for every $g \in G$, which is the claim.

*The listed fields.* $K(p)L_1/L_1$ is a pro-$p$ extension, so $K(p) \subseteq L_1(p)$. Next, $K^{\mathrm{ur}} \subseteq \tilde K$. Finally, $\chi$ embeds $\mathrm{Gal}(K(\mu_{p^\infty})/K(\mu_p))$ into $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$, which is pro-$p$, so $K(\mu_{p^\infty}) \subseteq L_1(p)$. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** (1) The fixed fields of $I_K$ and $\ker\omega$ are $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and $K(\mu_p)$, so $G_{\tilde K} = I_K \cap \ker\omega$. Both are preserved by every automorphism of $G_K$ (Setting), so $G_{\tilde K}$ is characteristic in $G_K$. The subgroup $G_{\Omega_K}$ is the kernel of $G_{\tilde K} \to G_{\tilde K}(p)$, characteristic in $G_{\tilde K}$ and hence in $G_K$. So every automorphism of $G_K$ induces one of $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$, and inner automorphisms induce inner ones. Let $f \in \mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ have order $p$. As $1 \le d \le p - 2$, $p \nmid d$, so by part 1 of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), $f$ is the class of an automorphism $\sigma$ of order $p$. By part 2, $\sigma$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$, so $f$ maps to $1$.

(2) The Corollary of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) applies, as $p$ is odd, and gives an automorphism $\sigma \in NG_K$ of order $p$ lying in neither $G_K$ nor $N$. Part 2 applies to $\sigma$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The hypothesis $d \le p - 2$.* It is used only in Step 3, where each $I_\varepsilon$ has dimension $d$ and a $p$-power root of unity other than $1$ has degree at least $p - 1$ over $F$. For $d \ge p - 1$ the argument allows $A$ to act on some $I_\varepsilon$ through primitive $p$-th roots of unity. This range contains every $K$ with $\zeta_p \in K$, since then $p - 1 = [\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p) : \mathbb{Q}_p]$ divides the ramification index of $K$, hence $d$. So the theorem says nothing about the fields with $\zeta_p \in K$. For $p = 3$ it covers only $\mathbb{Q}_3$; for $p = 5$ the fields of degree at most $3$.
- *Comparison with $G_K(p)$.* Part 3 (a) of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) shows that automorphisms of order $p$ are trivial on $G_K(p)$ when $d \le 2$, or when $d \ge 3$ and the outer class has finitely many conjugates, using Kondo's symplectic image and an invariant linear form. Here there is no condition on conjugates, automorphisms of order $p^a$ are allowed, and the quotient $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$ is larger than $G_K(p)$; but $d \le p - 2$ is needed. Neither result contains the other: $p = 3$ with $d = 2$ is covered only there.
- *The case $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$.* Here $\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{ab}} = \mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{ur}}\,\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})$ ([automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ fix its abelianization](automorphisms-of-g-qp-fix-abelianization.md)) lies in $\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, by part 2. Whether $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ has an automorphism of order $p$, equivalently by part 1 of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ has an element of order $p$, is not decided here. Part 2 shows that such an automorphism would induce the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{\mathbb{Q}_p}/\mathbb{Q}_p)$.
- *Tame inertia.* $I_K/P_K$ is procyclic of order prime to $p$ ([finite subgroups](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), Method), so $\sigma$ acts on it by multiplication by some $u = (u_\ell) \in \prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ with $u^{p^a} = 1$. Then $u_\ell = 1$ unless $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$, as $\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ has an element of order $p$ only when $p \mid \ell - 1$. The theorem does not decide whether $u = 1$. Automorphisms of $G_K$ with $u_\ell$ a primitive $p$-th root of unity for some $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$ do exist: the automorphism of the tame quotient fixing a Frobenius lift and raising tame inertia to the power $u$ preserves the mod-$p$ cyclotomic character, since $u_{\ell'} = 1$ for every prime $\ell'$ dividing $p - 1$, so it lifts to $G_K$ ([tame lifting for odd $p$](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md)). Whether such a lift can have finite order is not known here.
