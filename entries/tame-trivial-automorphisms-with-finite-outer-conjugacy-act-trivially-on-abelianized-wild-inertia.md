**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P$ its wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$,
$$\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]],\qquad \Lambda_{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]],\qquad V=P/P^{(2)},\quad P^{(2)}=P^p[P,P].$$
Use the presentation $G=F/(\varrho)$ and the notation of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). So $F=P_F\rtimes\mathcal G$, where $\mathcal G\cong\Gamma$ is generated by $\sigma,\tau$, $P_F$ is the pro-$p$ group normally generated by $x_0,\dots,x_d$, and $F$ is free among semidirect products of $\mathcal G$ with pro-$p$ groups. The relator is
$$\varrho=R_0\,x_1^{p^s}\,C,\qquad R_0=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa,$$
where $p^s$ is the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, $C=[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d]$ for even $d$, and $C=[x_1,y_1][x_2,x_3]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d]$ with $y_1\in P_F$ for odd $d$. Wild inertia $P$ is the image of $P_F$. Let $\omega_s:\mathcal G\to(\mathbb Z/p^s)^\times$ give the action on $\mu_{p^s}$, and $\omega=\omega_1$. The same letters denote the continuous ring homomorphisms $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}\to\mathbb Z/p^s$ and $\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ that extend them. Let $\epsilon:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ be the augmentation, and $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$.

Let $P_j$ be the closed lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2=P^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$. Write $\xi_i\in H$ and $\bar x_i\in V$ for the classes of $x_i$. By part 1 of the tame-group-algebra theorem,
$$V=V_0\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d,\qquad V_0=L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0,\quad V_i=\Lambda\bar x_i\cong\Lambda\ (i\ge1),$$
with $\rho\bar x_0=\omega(\rho)\bar x_0$. Let $\pi_i:V\to\Lambda$ for $i\ge1$, and $\pi_0:V\to\mathbb F_p$, be the coordinate maps, so that $v=\sum_i\pi_i(v)\bar x_i$. The *surface pairs* $(a,b)$ are $(x_{2k},x_{2k+1})$ for odd $d$ and $(x_{2k+1},x_{2k+2})$ for even $d$, with both indices at most $d$. Each of their generators occurs in $\varrho$ only in its factor $[a,b]$. Let $S\subseteq V$ be the sum of the summands $V_a,V_b$ over all pairs, and $C_V$ the *complementary part*: $V_1$ for odd $d$ and $V_1\oplus V_2$ for even $d$. So $V=L\oplus C_V\oplus S$. Write $\bar a,\bar b$ for $\bar x_a,\bar x_b$.

An automorphism of $G$ is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$. Such an automorphism $\theta$ acts on $H$, $V$ and every $\mathcal L_j$, and these actions commute with $\Gamma$, since $\theta(\rho)\in\rho P$ and $P$ acts trivially on them. So its action $\theta_H$ on $H$ is $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$-linear, and its action on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear. By [wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md), $P$ is free pro-$p$ and characteristic, so $H$ is a torsion-free compact $\mathbb Z_p$-module and $V=H/pH$. The trivial centre $Z(\Gamma)=1$ is part 3 of the tame-group-algebra theorem. Write $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$.

*Lower central input.* From [the boundary-twist theorem](boundary-twists-exclude-finite-outer-conjugacy-of-wild-scalars.md): the $\mathcal L_j$ are torsion-free, and topologically generated by iterated brackets of elements of $H$. $\mathcal L_j/p$ is the completed degree-$j$ free Lie space $\mathcal L_j(V)$, with compatible brackets. The bracket maps $H\to\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ and $\mathcal L_2\to\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_3)$ are injective also modulo $p$. Hence they are saturated: $[h,H]\subseteq p^k\mathcal L_2$ forces $h\in p^kH$, and $[h,H]\subseteq p^k\mathcal L_3$ forces $h\in p^k\mathcal L_2$. Its step 3 gives the single-generator fact: if $\bar h\in\mathcal L_2(V)$ and $[\bar h,\bar x_1]=0$, then $\bar h=0$. View $\mathcal L_j(V)$ inside the completed tensor space, with $[u,v]=uv-vu$, so that $[[x,y],z]=xyz-yxz-zxy+zyx$.

*Projections.* For indices $i,j,k$, let $\pi_{ijk}=\pi_i\otimes\pi_j\otimes\pi_k$ on the completed $V^{\otimes3}$. It is defined level by level on finite quotients, like the maps $\kappa_i$ of [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md), and sends $v_1\otimes v_2\otimes v_3$ to $\pi_i(v_1)\otimes\pi_j(v_2)\otimes\pi_k(v_3)$. For $i,j,k\ge1$ its target is $\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^3]]$. With $\Delta_n(\rho)=\rho^{\otimes n}$ extended continuously to $\Lambda$, and $\Gamma$ acting diagonally on tensors,
$$\pi_{ijk}(\mu\cdot\zeta)=\Delta_3(\mu)\,\pi_{ijk}(\zeta)\qquad(\mu\in\Lambda,\ i,j,k\ge1),\tag{P}$$
as in ($\kappa$1) there. Also $(\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\Delta_3=(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta_3=\mathrm{id}$ and $(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon)\Delta_3=\Delta_2$.

*Johnson maps.* For an automorphism $\theta$ trivial on $P/P_3$, put $J(\theta)(xP_2)=\theta(x)x^{-1}P_4$. For $\theta$ trivial on $H$, put $J_1(\theta)(xP_2)=\theta(x)x^{-1}P_3$. These are continuous homomorphisms $H\to\mathcal L_3$ and $H\to\mathcal L_2$. They are additive in $\theta$, satisfy $J(\beta\theta\beta^{-1})=\beta_3J(\theta)\beta_H^{-1}$ and $J_1(\beta\theta\beta^{-1})=\beta_2J_1(\theta)\beta_H^{-1}$ for every automorphism $\beta$, and $J(c_g)=\operatorname{ad}(gP_3)$ for $g\in P_2$, $J_1(c_g)=\operatorname{ad}(gP_2)$ for $g\in P$. These are the rules in the Setting of [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md) and in step 2 of [the wild-Frattini theorem](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-wild-frattini.md).

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ be tame-trivial, with finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. Then $\alpha$ acts trivially on $H=P^{\mathrm{ab}}$: $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in[P,P]$ for every $x\in P$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many outer conjugates. Then $c_g\alpha$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$ and on $P^{\mathrm{ab}}$ for some $g\in G$. Any two representatives of the class of $\alpha$ that are trivial on $\Gamma$ differ by some $c_g$ with $g\in P$.

**Lemma (proved here).**

- (a) If $z\in\Lambda$ satisfies $\Delta_2(z)=z\otimes1+1\otimes z$, then $z=0$.
- (b) Let $\alpha$ be as in the theorem and $\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ tame-trivial. Then $\beta^n\alpha\beta^{-n}=c_g\alpha$ for some $n\ge1$ and $g\in P$.

*Proof.* (a) Let $U$ be an open normal subgroup of $\Gamma$, and write the image of $z$ in $\mathbb F_p[\Gamma/U]$ as $\sum_ga_gg$. Its image under $\Delta_2$ is $\sum_ga_g(g,g)$ in $\mathbb F_p[\Gamma/U\times\Gamma/U]$, while $z\otimes1+1\otimes z$ maps to $\sum_ga_g\big((g,1)+(1,g)\big)$. For $g\ne1$, the basis element $(g,1)$ has coefficient $0$ on the left and $a_g$ on the right. The basis element $(1,1)$ has coefficients $a_1$ and $2a_1$. So the image of $z$ is $0$ for every $U$, and $z=0$.

(b) The classes of $\beta^j\alpha\beta^{-j}$, $j\ge0$, are conjugates of the class of $\alpha$, so two of them agree, say for $i<j$. Conjugating by $\beta^{-i}$ gives $\beta^n\alpha\beta^{-n}=c_g\alpha$ with $n=j-i$. Then $c_g=\beta^n\alpha\beta^{-n}\alpha^{-1}$ is tame-trivial, so $gP\in Z(\Gamma)=1$. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.**

*0. The leading term.* By [the wild-Frattini theorem](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-wild-frattini.md), $\alpha$ acts trivially on $V=H/pH$, so $(\alpha_H-1)H\subseteq pH$. Suppose $\alpha_H\ne1$. As $\bigcap_kp^kH=0$, there is a largest $m\ge1$ with $(\alpha_H-1)H\subseteq p^mH$. Multiplication by $p^m$ is a continuous bijection of the torsion-free compact group $H$ onto $p^mH$, hence a homeomorphism. So
$$\alpha_H=1+p^mE$$
for a unique continuous $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$-linear $E:H\to H$. Its reduction $\bar E:V\to V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, and $\bar E\ne0$ by maximality of $m$. We derive a contradiction by showing $\bar E=0$.

For $j=2,3$, $\alpha$ acts on $\mathcal L_j$ by $\alpha_2[x,y]=[\alpha_Hx,\alpha_Hy]$ and $\alpha_3[[x,y],z]=[[\alpha_Hx,\alpha_Hy],\alpha_Hz]$. Expanding the brackets multilinearly, $\alpha_j-1$ maps these generators into $p^m\mathcal L_j$. As it is additive and continuous and $p^m\mathcal L_j$ is closed, $(\alpha_j-1)\mathcal L_j\subseteq p^m\mathcal L_j$. Torsion-freeness gives $\alpha_j=1+p^mE_j$ with $E_j$ continuous. The terms of the expansion with two or three factors $p^mE$ lie in $p^{2m}\mathcal L_j$ and $m\ge1$, so the reduction $\bar E_j$ is the derivation $\bar E^{(j)}$:
$$\bar E^{(2)}[x,y]=[\bar Ex,y]+[x,\bar Ey],\qquad \bar E^{(3)}[[x,y],z]=[[\bar Ex,y],z]+[[x,\bar Ey],z]+[[x,y],\bar Ez].$$
These agree on brackets of elements of $V$, which topologically generate $\mathcal L_j(V)$. On the tensor space, $\bar E^{(j)}$ is the sum of the maps applying $\bar E$ in one slot. Also $\alpha_H^{-1}=1-p^mE\alpha_H^{-1}$, so for every continuous homomorphism $D:H\to\mathcal L_j$,
$$\alpha_jD\alpha_H^{-1}-D=p^m(E_jD-DE)+p^{2m}Y,\qquad Y=DE^2\alpha_H^{-1}-E_jDE\alpha_H^{-1}.\tag{0}$$

*Conjugates.* Let $\theta$ be a tame-trivial automorphism with action $B$ on $V$. Then $\alpha'=\theta^{-1}\alpha\theta$ is tame-trivial, has the same finite set of outer conjugates, and $\alpha'_H=1+p^m\theta_H^{-1}E\theta_H$. So $\alpha'$ has the same $m$, and its reduced leading term is $\bar E'=B^{-1}\bar EB$. Steps 1 to 3 below apply to $\alpha'$ as well as to $\alpha$.

*1. A boundary twist.* Fix a surface pair $(a,b)$, the $l$-th in the numbering of [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md), and let $T=T^{(l)}_1$ in the notation there. It fixes $\mathcal G$ and the other $x_i$, with $T(a)=waw^{-1}$, $T(b)=wbw^{-1}$ and $w=[a,b]$. By Lemma 2 (a) and (c) there, with $I=\{l\}$ and $t=1$, $T$ is a tame-trivial automorphism, trivial on $P/P_3$, and $D=J(T)$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant, with $D(\xi_a)=[[\xi_a,\xi_b],\xi_a]$, $D(\xi_b)=[[\xi_a,\xi_b],\xi_b]$ and $D(\xi_i)=0$ for the other $i$. Its reduction $\bar D$ is $\Lambda$-linear, kills every $V_i$ with $i\notin\{a,b\}$, and has $\bar D(\bar a)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]$, $\bar D(\bar b)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]$.

By Lemma (b), $T^n\alpha T^{-n}\alpha^{-1}=c_g$ with $n\ge1$ and $g\in P$. Its inverse is $\alpha T^n\alpha^{-1}T^{-n}=c_{g_1}$ with $g_1=g^{-1}$. The left side is trivial on $P/P_3$, as $P_3$ is characteristic, so $[g_1,P]\subseteq P_3$, and injectivity of $H\to\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ gives $g_1\in P_2$. The rules for $J$ give $n(\alpha_3D\alpha_H^{-1}-D)=\operatorname{ad}(h)$ with $h=g_1P_3$. By (0), $np^m(E_3D-DE+p^mY)=\operatorname{ad}(h)$. Write $n=p^ku$ with $u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$. Saturation gives $h=p^{k+m}h'$, and cancelling $p^{k+m}$ in the torsion-free group $\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_3)$ gives $E_3D-DE+p^mY=u^{-1}\operatorname{ad}(h')$. Modulo $p$,
$$\bar E^{(3)}\bar D-\bar D\bar E=\operatorname{ad}(\bar h)\quad\text{on }V,\qquad\bar h\in\mathcal L_2(V).\tag{1}$$

*2. The block of the pair.* Since $\bar D$ is $\Lambda$-linear and kills the summands $V_i$ with $i\notin\{a,b\}$,
$$\bar D(v)=\pi_a(v)\cdot[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]+\pi_b(v)\cdot[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]\qquad(v\in V).\tag{2}$$
Expanding the two brackets: the word $\bar a\bar a\bar b$ has coefficient $-1$ in $[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]$ and $0$ in $[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]$, and the word $\bar a\bar b\bar b$ has coefficients $0$ and $1$. By (P),
$$\pi_{aab}(\bar D(v))=-\Delta_3(\pi_a(v)),\qquad \pi_{abb}(\bar D(v))=\Delta_3(\pi_b(v)).\tag{3}$$
As $\Delta_3$ is injective, being split by $\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon$, $\bar D(v)=0$ forces $\pi_a(v)=\pi_b(v)=0$.

(a) *Evaluation at $\bar x_1$.* As $x_1$ lies in no pair, $\bar D(\bar x_1)=0$, and (1) reads $[\bar h,\bar x_1]=-\bar D(\bar E\bar x_1)$. Apply $\pi_{aab}$ and $\pi_{abb}$. On the left, $[\bar h,\bar x_1]=\bar h\bar x_1-\bar x_1\bar h$ has $\bar x_1$ in its last or first slot, and $\pi_a(\bar x_1)=\pi_b(\bar x_1)=0$, so both projections vanish. By (3), $\pi_a(\bar E\bar x_1)=\pi_b(\bar E\bar x_1)=0$, so $\bar D(\bar E\bar x_1)=0$ by (2). Then $[\bar h,\bar x_1]=0$, and the single-generator fact gives $\bar h=0$:
$$\bar E^{(3)}\bar D=\bar D\bar E.\tag{4}$$

(b) *Other summands.* For $i\notin\{a,b\}$, $\bar D(\bar x_i)=0$, so (4) gives $\bar D(\bar E\bar x_i)=0$, hence $\pi_a(\bar E\bar x_i)=\pi_b(\bar E\bar x_i)=0$. By $\Lambda$-linearity, $\pi_a\circ\bar E$ and $\pi_b\circ\bar E$ vanish on $V_i$.

(c) *Evaluation at $\bar a$.* Write
$$\bar E\bar a=\mu_{aa}\bar a+\mu_{ba}\bar b+r_a,\qquad \bar E\bar b=\mu_{ab}\bar a+\mu_{bb}\bar b+r_b,$$
with $\mu$'s in $\Lambda$ and $r_a,r_b$ in the sum of the $V_i$ with $i\notin\{a,b\}$. The right side of (4) at $\bar a$ is $\mu_{aa}\cdot[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]+\mu_{ba}\cdot[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]$, by (2). The left side is
$$[[\bar E\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]+[[\bar a,\bar E\bar b],\bar a]+[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar E\bar a].$$
Expand into words. A projection $\pi_{ijk}$ sees only the words whose three letters lie in $V_i,V_j,V_k$, in this order. We compare five projections; in each line, the contributing words of the left side are listed.

- $\pi_{aab}$: the words $-\bar a(\mu_{aa}\bar a)\bar b$, $-\bar a\bar a(\mu_{bb}\bar b)$ and $-(\mu_{aa}\bar a)\bar a\bar b$. With (3),
$$\mu_{aa}\otimes1\otimes1+1\otimes\mu_{aa}\otimes1+1\otimes1\otimes\mu_{bb}=\Delta_3(\mu_{aa}).$$
Applying $\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}$ gives $\mu_{bb}=\mu_{aa}-2\epsilon(\mu_{aa})$, so $\epsilon(\mu_{bb})=-\epsilon(\mu_{aa})$. Applying $\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon$ shows that $\mu'=\mu_{aa}-\epsilon(\mu_{aa})$ satisfies $\Delta_2(\mu')=\mu'\otimes1+1\otimes\mu'$. By Lemma (a), $\mu'=0$. So $\mu_{aa}=c\in\mathbb F_p$ and $\mu_{bb}=-c$.
- $\pi_{abb}$: the words $-\bar a(\mu_{ba}\bar b)\bar b$, $+\bar a\bar b(\mu_{ba}\bar b)$ (from the first term) and $+\bar a\bar b(\mu_{ba}\bar b)$ (from the third). With (3), $-1\otimes\mu_{ba}\otimes1+2\cdot1\otimes1\otimes\mu_{ba}=\Delta_3(\mu_{ba})$. Applying $\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon$ gives $\mu_{ba}=\epsilon(\mu_{ba})=:c''\in\mathbb F_p$.
- $\pi_{aaa}$: only $[[\bar a,\mu_{ab}\bar a],\bar a]$ contributes, and the right side has no such words. So $2\cdot1\otimes\mu_{ab}\otimes1-\mu_{ab}\otimes1\otimes1-1\otimes1\otimes\mu_{ab}=0$. Applying $\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon$ gives $\mu_{ab}=\epsilon(\mu_{ab})=:c'\in\mathbb F_p$.
- $\pi_{aai}$, for $i\notin\{a,b\}$: only the word $-\bar a\bar a r_b$ contributes, and the right side has none. So $1\otimes1\otimes\pi_i(r_b)=0$, and $\pi_i(r_b)=0$.
- $\pi_{abi}$, for $i\notin\{a,b\}$: the word $\bar a\bar br_a$ occurs in $[[r_a,\bar b],\bar a]$ and in $[[\bar a,\bar b],r_a]$, each with coefficient $+1$, and nowhere else. So $2\cdot1\otimes1\otimes\pi_i(r_a)=0$, and $\pi_i(r_a)=0$, as $p$ is odd.

Hence $r_a=r_b=0$ and
$$\bar E\bar a=c\bar a+c''\bar b,\qquad\bar E\bar b=c'\bar a-c\bar b\qquad(c,c',c''\in\mathbb F_p).\tag{5}$$

*3. Transvections of the pair.* Let $t_1$ fix $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $a\mapsto ab$. It is an endomorphism of $F$ by the universal property. It fixes $w$, since $[ab,b]=ab\,b\,b^{-1}a^{-1}b^{-1}=[a,b]$, and so it fixes $\varrho$. The endomorphism $a\mapsto ab^{-1}$ also fixes $w$, as $[ab^{-1},b]=[a,b]$, and is inverse to $t_1$. So $t_1$ induces a tame-trivial automorphism of $G$. As $t_1(a)a^{-1}=aba^{-1}$, its action on $H$ is $1+N$, where $N$ is $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$-linear with $N\xi_a=\xi_b$ and $N\xi_i=0$ for $i\ne a$. Here $H$ is topologically generated by the $\Gamma$-conjugates of the $\xi_i$. So $N^2=0$, and $t_1^n$ acts by $1+nN$.

By Lemma (b), $t_1^n\alpha t_1^{-n}=c_g\alpha$ with $n\ge1$ and $g\in P$, and $c_g$ acts trivially on $H$. So $(1+nN)(1+p^mE)(1-nN)=1+p^mE$. As $(1+nN)(1-nN)=1$ and $H$ is torsion-free, $(1+nN)E(1-nN)=E$, that is, $n(NE-EN)=n^2NEN$, and $NE-EN=nNEN$. Modulo $p$, with $\bar N\bar a=\bar b$ and $\bar N$ killing the other summands, evaluate at $\bar a$ using (5):
$$c\bar b-(c'\bar a-c\bar b)=n\,c'\bar b.$$
So $c'=0$, and then $2c=0$, so $c=0$. The transvection $b\mapsto ba$ fixes $w$ as well, since $[a,ba]=[a,b]$, and in the same way gives, at $\bar b$, $-c''\bar b=n\,c''\bar a$, so $c''=0$.

*Consequence of steps 1 to 3.* As this holds for every pair, (5) and step 2 (b) give
$$\bar E(S)=0,\qquad \bar E(V)\subseteq L\oplus C_V.\tag{6}$$
By the remark on conjugates, (6) also holds for $B^{-1}\bar EB$ whenever $B$ is the action on $V$ of a tame-trivial automorphism.

*4. The cyclotomic line.* As $\bar E$ is $\Lambda$-linear, $\rho\,\bar E\bar x_0=\omega(\rho)\bar E\bar x_0$ for all $\rho$. By Lemma 0 of [unitarity](unitarity-of-central-scalars-on-wild-frattini.md), $L$ is the $\omega$-eigenspace of $V$. So $\bar E\bar x_0=e\bar x_0$ for some $e\in\mathbb F_p$.

*The element $\gamma$.* Let $\gamma\in\mathcal G$ generate the Sylow pro-$p$ subgroup of the closed subgroup generated by $\sigma$, which is infinite, as that subgroup is isomorphic to $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Then $\omega(\gamma)=1$, since $\mathbb F_p^\times$ has order prime to $p$. So $\lambda_0=\gamma-1\in\ker\omega$, and $\lambda_0^*=\gamma^{-1}-1$. By Lemma 2 of [the central-element construction](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md), applied to $\gamma$ and to $\gamma^{-1}$, right multiplication by $\lambda_0$ and left multiplication by $\lambda_0^*$ are injective on $\Lambda$.

*5. Even $d$.* Here $d\ge4$, $C_V=V_1\oplus V_2$, and the first pair is $(x_3,x_4)$. Write
$$\bar E\bar x_1=\mu_{11}\bar x_1+\mu_{21}\bar x_2+f_1\bar x_0,\qquad \bar E\bar x_2=\mu_{12}\bar x_1+\mu_{22}\bar x_2+f_2\bar x_0.$$

- *Mixing.* Part 2 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), with $\lambda=\lambda_0$, gives a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $B$ on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,4$, and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda_0\bar x_3$, $B\bar x_4=\bar x_4-\lambda_0^*\bar x_2$. So $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\lambda_0\bar x_3$. By (6), $\bar EB\bar x_4=-\lambda_0^*\bar E\bar x_2$, and (6) for $B^{-1}\bar EB$ at $\bar x_4\in S$ gives $\lambda_0^*\bar E\bar x_2=0$. Hence $\lambda_0^*\mu_{12}=\lambda_0^*\mu_{22}=0$, so $\mu_{12}=\mu_{22}=0$. Next, $B^{-1}\bar EB\bar x_1=B^{-1}\bar E\bar x_1$, by (6), and its $\bar x_3$-coordinate is $-\mu_{11}\lambda_0$. By (6) for $B^{-1}\bar EB$ it vanishes, so $\mu_{11}=0$.
- *The transvection $T_1$.* Let $T_1$ fix $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$. As $[x_1,x_2x_1]=[x_1,x_2]$, $[x_1,x_2x_1^{-1}]=[x_1,x_2]$, and $x_2$ occurs in $\varrho$ only in $[x_1,x_2]$, this gives a tame-trivial automorphism of $G$ as in step 3. It acts on $H$ by $1+N$ with $N\xi_2=\xi_1$, $N\xi_i=0$ for $i\ne2$, and $N^2=0$. As in step 3, $\bar N\bar E-\bar E\bar N=n\bar N\bar E\bar N$ for some $n\ge1$. At $\bar x_1$ this gives $\mu_{21}\bar x_1=0$. At $\bar x_2$, using $\mu_{12}=\mu_{22}=0$ and $\mu_{11}=\mu_{21}=0$, it gives $-\bar E\bar x_1=-f_1\bar x_0=0$. So $\bar E\bar x_1=0$ and $\bar E\bar x_2=f_2\bar x_0$.
- *The exact twist $T'$.* Step 2 of the wild-Frattini theorem gives an automorphism $T'$ of $G$, trivial on $H$, with $J_1(T')=p^sj$ for a continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant $j:H\to\mathcal L_2$. Its reduction $\bar\jmath$ has $\bar\jmath(\bar x_2)=[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]$ and vanishes on the other summands, as $T'$ fixes $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ with $i\ne1,2$, and $j(\xi_0)=j(\xi_1)=0$. By Lemma (b) and inversion, as in step 1, $\alpha T'^n\alpha^{-1}T'^{-n}=c_{g_1}$ with $n\ge1$ and $g_1\in P$; both $c_{g_1}$ and $T'^n$ act trivially on $H$. The rules for $J_1$ give $np^s(\alpha_2j\alpha_H^{-1}-j)=\operatorname{ad}(g_1P_2)$. By (0), $np^{s+m}(E_2j-jE+p^mY)=\operatorname{ad}(g_1P_2)$. Saturation of $H\to\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ and torsion-freeness allow cancelling $np^{s+m}$ up to a unit, as in step 1. Modulo $p$,
$$\bar E^{(2)}\bar\jmath-\bar\jmath\bar E=\operatorname{ad}(\gamma_1),\qquad\gamma_1\in V.$$
At $\bar x_1$ both terms on the left vanish, as $\bar E\bar x_1=0$, so $[\gamma_1,\bar x_1]=0$. At $\bar x_2$, the left side is $[\bar E\bar x_1,\bar x_2]+[\bar x_1,\bar E\bar x_2]-\bar\jmath(f_2\bar x_0)=f_2[\bar x_1,\bar x_0]$. Apply the coordinates of the wild-Frattini theorem for the indices $0,1,2$, and write $\pi(\gamma_1)=r_0X_0+r_1X_1+r_2X_2$. The first identity becomes $r_0[X_0,X_1]-r_2[X_1,X_2]=0$, so $r_0=r_2=0$. The second becomes $f_2[X_1,X_0]=r_1[X_1,X_2]$. As the three brackets of distinct letters are linearly independent, $f_2=0$.

So $\bar E$ vanishes on $C_V$ and on $S$, and $\bar E\bar x_0=e\bar x_0$.

*6. Odd $d$.* Here $C_V=V_1$, the first pair is $(x_2,x_3)$, and $\bar E\bar x_1=\mu\bar x_1+f\bar x_0$.

- Part 3 of the mixing theorem, with $\nu=\lambda_0$, gives a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $B$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,3$, and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda_0\bar x_2$. Then $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\lambda_0\bar x_2$, and by (6), $B^{-1}\bar EB\bar x_1=B^{-1}(\mu\bar x_1+f\bar x_0)$, whose $\bar x_2$-coordinate is $-\mu\lambda_0$. By (6) for $B^{-1}\bar EB$, $\mu\lambda_0=0$, so $\mu=0$.
- Step 3 of the wild-Frattini theorem gives $\nu'\in\ker\omega$ with $\omega(\nu'^*)\,\omega(\delta_0)\ne0$, where $\delta_0$ is the element of part 1 of the mixing theorem. Part 3 of the mixing theorem, with $\nu=\nu'$, gives a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $B'$ fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,3$ and has $B'\bar x_3=\bar x_3-\nu'^*\delta\nu'\bar x_2-\nu'^*\delta_0\bar x_1$. By (6), with $\mu=0$,
$$\bar EB'\bar x_3=-\nu'^*\delta_0\,f\bar x_0=-\omega(\nu'^*)\,\omega(\delta_0)\,f\,\bar x_0,$$
and $B'^{-1}$ fixes $\bar x_0$. By (6) for $B'^{-1}\bar EB'$ at $\bar x_3\in S$, this vanishes, so $f=0$.

So again $\bar E$ vanishes on $C_V$ and on $S$, and $\bar E\bar x_0=e\bar x_0$.

*7. The cyclotomic coefficient.* We show $e=0$.

*Two functionals.* For $k\in\{0,1\}$, let $\mathbb Z/p^s$ carry the action of $\mathcal G$ through $\omega_s$. By the universal property of $F$ there is a homomorphism $\Theta_k:F\to(\mathbb Z/p^s)\rtimes\mathcal G$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$, sends $x_k$ to $(1,1)$ and every other $x_i$ to $(0,1)$. It maps $P_F$ into $\mathbb Z/p^s$, with $\Theta_k(\rho x\rho^{-1})=\omega_s(\rho)\Theta_k(x)$, so on $P_F$ it factors through the quotient $N_s=P_F/P_F^{p^s}[P_F,P_F]$ and satisfies $\Theta_k(\lambda\tilde x)=\omega_s(\lambda)\Theta_k(\tilde x)$ for $\lambda\in(\mathbb Z/p^s)[[\mathcal G]]$. It kills $C\in[P_F,P_F]$ and $x_1^{p^s}$. Apply the Computation of the tame-group-algebra theorem with $r=s$, $N=P_F^{p^s}[P_F,P_F]$, $A=Q=N_s$, $X=1$ and $a$ the class of $x_0$, and use $(1,\tau)=1$, as in its proof of part 1. It shows that $R_0$ maps to $u_s\tilde x_0$ in $N_s$. By Lemma (b) there, $\omega_s(u_s)=0$, so $\Theta_k(R_0)=\omega_s(u_s)\Theta_k(x_0)=0$. Hence $\Theta_k(\varrho)=0$, and $\Theta_k$ factors through $G$. Restricted to $P$, it kills $[P,P]$ and induces a continuous homomorphism
$$\Psi_k:H\to\mathbb Z/p^s,\qquad \Psi_k(\xi_i)=\delta_{ik},\qquad \Psi_k(\lambda h)=\omega_s(\lambda)\Psi_k(h)\quad(\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}).$$

*The relation in $H$.* Let $N_0$ be the closed subgroup of $P_F$ generated by the $\mathcal G$-conjugates of $x_0$. Then $x_0^\sigma\in N_0$. The factor $(x_0,\tau)$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by $x_0$ and $\tau$, which is the product of its closed normal subgroup generated by the $\tau$-conjugates of $x_0$ and the closed subgroup generated by $\tau$. It maps to $1$ in $\mathcal G$, and $\mathcal G\cap P_F=1$, so $(x_0,\tau)\in N_0$. Thus $R_0\in N_0$, and its class $\bar R_0\in H$ lies in the image of $N_0$, which is the compact set $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}\xi_0$. So $\bar R_0=\vartheta\xi_0$ for some $\vartheta\in\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$. The image of $\varrho$ in $H$ is $0$, so
$$\vartheta\xi_0+p^s\xi_1=0\quad\text{in }H.\tag{7}$$
Applying $\Psi_0$ gives $\omega_s(\vartheta)=0$ in $\mathbb Z/p^s$.

*Conclusion of step 7.* By steps 4 to 6, $E\xi_0=e\xi_0+py_0$ and $E\xi_1=py_1$ for some $y_0,y_1\in H$, reading $e$ as an integer. Apply the $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$-linear map $E$ to (7), and use (7) again:
$$0=\vartheta(e\xi_0+py_0)+p^{s+1}y_1=-ep^s\xi_1+p\vartheta y_0+p^{s+1}y_1.$$
As $H$ is torsion-free, $\vartheta y_0=ep^{s-1}\xi_1-p^sy_1$. Apply $\Psi_1$. The left side gives $\omega_s(\vartheta)\Psi_1(y_0)=0$, and the right side gives $ep^{s-1}$ in $\mathbb Z/p^s$. So $p$ divides $e$, and $e=0$ in $\mathbb F_p$.

*8. Conclusion.* By steps 4 to 7, $\bar E$ vanishes on $L$, on $C_V$ and on $S$, so $\bar E=0$. This contradicts step 0. Hence $\alpha_H=1$. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** By [tame innerness](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md), $\alpha$ induces on $\Gamma$ conjugation by some $\gamma\in\Gamma$. If $g\in G$ maps to $\gamma^{-1}$, then $c_g\alpha$ is tame-trivial and represents the same outer class, so it has finitely many outer conjugates, and the theorem applies. If $c_g\alpha$ and $c_{g'}\alpha$ are both tame-trivial, then so is $c_{g'g^{-1}}$, so $g'g^{-1}\in P$ as $Z(\Gamma)=1$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What changes.* The wild-Frattini theorem gives triviality on $P/P^p[P,P]$. Here a leading-term argument handles all the $p$-adic layers of $P^{\mathrm{ab}}$ at once. At each step the leading term is a $\Lambda$-linear endomorphism $\bar E$ of $V$, so the equations are linear, unlike the multiplicative equations for the action on $V$. In step 2 the coefficient comparisons end in primitive elements of the completed group algebra, of which there are none, rather than in group-like elements. The blocks left by one boundary twist lie in $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb F_p)$, which reflects the symplectic changes of a pair that commute with its twist; the exact transvections remove them. None of the automorphisms used detects the coefficient $e$ on the cyclotomic line. It is removed instead by comparing two Kummer functionals with the abelianized relator, as in step 7.
- *What remains.* Let $\alpha$ be tame-trivial with finitely many outer conjugates. Then $\alpha$ is trivial on $P^{\mathrm{ab}}$, so $\gamma\mapsto\alpha(\tilde\gamma)\tilde\gamma^{-1}[P,P]$ is a $1$-cocycle $\Gamma\to P^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and conjugation by elements of $P$ changes it by coboundaries. Whether its class vanishes, whether the degree-one Johnson map $J_1(\alpha):P^{\mathrm{ab}}\to P_2/P_3$ has the form $\operatorname{ad}(h)$, and whether $\alpha$ itself is inner, are not decided here. They form the next layers of item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535). By [the normal-lattice theorem](finite-conjugacy-outer-classes-generate-normal-p-adic-lattices.md), a nontrivial such class would generate a normal $\mathbb Z_p^r$ in $\operatorname{Out}(G_K)$; the theorem here adds that its representatives act trivially on $P^{\mathrm{ab}}$.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$ and $d\ge3$, every $s$, including $p=3$. The cases $p=2$ and $d\le2$ are not covered: the linked inputs exclude them, and for $d\le2$ there is no surface pair. For $d=2$ the theorem and corollary hold by [the degree-two analogue](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia-in-degree-two.md), with a different proof. Everything here is proved from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
