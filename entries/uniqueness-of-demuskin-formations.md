**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd and $q = p^f$ with $f \ge 1$. Following Wingberg, let $\Gamma$ be the profinite group with generators $\sigma, \tau$ and the single defining relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, or a quotient of it whose supernatural order is divisible by $p^\infty$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, §I, p. 99]. For a profinite group $Z$ and $s \ge 1$, put $Z^0 = Z$, let $Z^i$ be the closure of $(Z^{i-1})^{p^s}[Z^{i-1}, Z]$, and let $\tilde Z$ be the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $Z$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, p. 100]. Let $X$ be a profinite group with a surjection $\varphi : X \to \Gamma$ whose kernel is pro-$p$. For an open normal subgroup $\mathcal H$ of $\Gamma$ put $X_{\mathcal H} = \varphi^{-1}(\mathcal H)$. Conjugation in $X$ makes $\Gamma/\mathcal H \cong X/X_{\mathcal H}$ act on the cohomology of $\tilde X_{\mathcal H}$, and makes $\Gamma$ act on it through $\Gamma/\mathcal H$.

**Definition (Koch).** Let $n, s \ge 1$ and let $\alpha : \Gamma \to (\mathbb{Z}/p^s)^\times$ be a character. Then $(X, \varphi)$ is a *Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n, s, \alpha$* if the following hold for every open normal subgroup $\mathcal H \subseteq \ker\alpha$, with $G = \Gamma/\mathcal H$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Definition, p. 100] [@jannsenwingberg1982, §1.1, pp. 72–73]:

1. $\tilde X_{\mathcal H}$ is a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $n|G| + 2$, and the torsion subgroup of its abelianization is cyclic of order $p^s$.
2. View $H^1(\mathcal H, \mathbb{F}_p)$ inside $H^1(\tilde X_{\mathcal H}, \mathbb{F}_p)$ by inflation along $\varphi$, and let $W$ be its orthogonal complement for the cup product pairing into $H^2(\tilde X_{\mathcal H}, \mathbb{F}_p)$, which is one-dimensional by condition 1. Then $W/H^1(\mathcal H, \mathbb{F}_p)$ is a free $\mathbb{F}_p[G]$-module of rank $n$, and is the direct sum of two totally isotropic $G$-submodules.
3. Every $\rho \in \Gamma$ acts on $H^2(\tilde X_{\mathcal H}, \mathbb{Z}/p^s)$ as multiplication by $\alpha(\rho)$.

Both sources state condition 2 with coefficients $\mathbb{F}_p$; Wingberg's unadorned $H^i$ means $H^i(\cdot, \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, p. 100]. In the proof of his Lemma 3, Wingberg deduces from condition 2, using a further result on symplectic modules, that the corresponding quotient with coefficients $\mathbb{Z}/p^s$ is a free $\mathbb{Z}/p^s[G]$-module of rank $n$ and hyperbolic [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, proof of Lemma 3, p. 105]. This entry uses that statement only through his Lemma 3.

**Theorem.**

1. *Koch.* Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite of degree $n$ with residue field of order $q$, let $T = K_{tr}$ be its maximal tamely ramified extension, $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$ and $\pi : G_K \to \Gamma$ the restriction. Let $p^s$ be the order of the group $\mu_T$ of $p$-power roots of unity in $T$, let $\chi_T : \Gamma \to (\mathbb{Z}/p^s)^\times$ be the character by which $\Gamma$ acts on $\mu_T$, and put $\alpha = \chi_T^{-1}$. Then $(G_K, \pi)$ is a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n, s, \alpha$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Bemerkung ii) after Satz 1, p. 100] [@jannsenwingberg1982, proof of Theorem 2, p. 75].
2. *Wingberg's uniqueness theorem.* If $(X, \varphi_X)$ and $(Y, \varphi_Y)$ are Demuškin formations over $\Gamma$ with the same invariants, then $X$ and $Y$ are isomorphic profinite groups [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Satz 1, p. 100].
3. *Isomorphism over $\Gamma$.* In part 2 there is an isomorphism $f : Y \to X$ with $\varphi_X \circ f = \varphi_Y$. This is proved here from Wingberg's proof.
4. *Generators.* Let $K$ be as in part 1, let $\sigma, \tau$ be any generators of $\Gamma$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, and let $\beta : \Gamma \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ be any map lifting $\alpha$. Jannsen and Wingberg's isomorphism from $G_K$ to the group presented by $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_n$ and their relations built from $\sigma, \tau, \beta$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, Theorem 2, p. 75] can be chosen to commute with the maps to $\Gamma$. So $G_K$ has such a system of generators in which the first two lift the given $\sigma, \tau$ and $x_0, \dots, x_n$ lie in the wild inertia group. This is proved here from part 3.

In part 1, Wingberg states Koch's result for the Galois group over $K$ of the maximal $p$-extension of $T$. That group is $G_K$, because $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar K/T)$ is the [wild inertia group](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md), which is pro-$p$. The group $\Gamma$ has generators $\sigma, \tau$ with the single defining relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, by Hasse and Iwasawa [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 71]. Jannsen and Wingberg's presentation is recorded in [generators and relations for $G_K$](local-galois-group-presentations.md).

**Proof of part 3.** Wingberg works with the open normal subgroups $\mathcal H \subseteq \ker\alpha$ for which $p^{2s}$ divides $(\Gamma : \mathcal H)$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, p. 103]. Call this set $\mathcal U$. It is closed under finite intersections, and it is cofinal among all open normal subgroups, because $\Gamma$ has open normal subgroups of index divisible by $p^{2s}$. For $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ and $i \ge 1$, let $M_{\mathcal H, i}$ be the set of isomorphisms $g : Y/Y^i_{\mathcal H} \to X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$ with $\bar\varphi_X \circ g = \bar\varphi_Y$, where $Y^i_{\mathcal H} = (Y_{\mathcal H})^i$ and $\bar\varphi_X : X/X^i_{\mathcal H} \to \Gamma/\mathcal H$, $\bar\varphi_Y : Y/Y^i_{\mathcal H} \to \Gamma/\mathcal H$ are induced by $\varphi_X, \varphi_Y$. These make sense because $X^i_{\mathcal H} \subseteq X_{\mathcal H} = \varphi_X^{-1}(\mathcal H)$, and likewise for $Y$.

- *$M_{\mathcal H, i}$ is nonempty.* Fix $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ and a $G$-isometry as in Wingberg's Lemma 3 [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Lemma 3, p. 104]. His Lemma 4 gives an isomorphism $\varphi_{\mathcal H, 1} : Y/Y^1_{\mathcal H} \to X/X^1_{\mathcal H}$ that fits into a commutative diagram whose right-hand vertical map is the identity of $G = \Gamma/\mathcal H$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Lemma 4, pp. 105–106]. So $\varphi_{\mathcal H, 1} \in M_{\mathcal H, 1}$. In the proof of his Satz 3, Lemmas 6 and 7 then give surjections $\varphi_{\mathcal H, i} : Y \to X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$ for all $i \ge 1$, each a solution of an embedding problem for the previous one, hence a lift of it, and starting from $Y \to Y/Y^1_{\mathcal H} \to X/X^1_{\mathcal H}$ given by $\varphi_{\mathcal H, 1}$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Lemma 6, p. 107; Lemma 7, p. 110; proof of Satz 3, pp. 111–112]. Wingberg shows that each $\varphi_{\mathcal H, i}$ induces an isomorphism $Y/Y^i_{\mathcal H} \to X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, p. 112]. Since $\varphi_{\mathcal H, i}$ lifts $\varphi_{\mathcal H, 1}$, this isomorphism commutes with the maps to $\Gamma/\mathcal H$, so it lies in $M_{\mathcal H, i}$.
- *$M_{\mathcal H, i}$ is finite.* The group $X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$ is finite. Indeed $X_{\mathcal H}/X^i_{\mathcal H}$ is pro-$p$, being built by $i$ successive extensions with abelian kernels of exponent dividing $p^s$, so it is a quotient of $\tilde X_{\mathcal H}$, which is a finitely generated pro-$p$ group by condition 1. In a finitely generated pro-$p$ group $Z$, each $Z^j$ is open: $Z/Z^1$ is a finitely generated abelian group of exponent dividing $p^s$, hence finite, so $Z^1$ is open and finitely generated, and $Z^2 \supseteq (Z^1)^1$ is open in $Z^1$ by the same argument, and so on. The same holds for $Y$, so $M_{\mathcal H, i}$ is a set of maps between finite groups.
- *Projective system.* Let $\mathcal H' \subseteq \mathcal H$ be in $\mathcal U$ and $i' \ge i$, and let $g' \in M_{\mathcal H', i'}$. Then $Y^{i'}_{\mathcal H'} \subseteq Y^{i}_{\mathcal H'} \subseteq Y^i_{\mathcal H}$, and likewise for $X$. Since $g'$ commutes with the maps to $\Gamma/\mathcal H'$, it maps $Y_{\mathcal H}/Y^{i'}_{\mathcal H'}$, the inverse image of $\mathcal H/\mathcal H'$, onto $X_{\mathcal H}/X^{i'}_{\mathcal H'}$. The $i$-th terms of the $p^s$-central series of these two groups are $Y^i_{\mathcal H}/Y^{i'}_{\mathcal H'}$ and $X^i_{\mathcal H}/X^{i'}_{\mathcal H'}$, because a continuous surjection of profinite groups maps each term of this series onto the corresponding term. An isomorphism preserves these terms, so $g'$ induces an isomorphism $Y/Y^i_{\mathcal H} \to X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$, which commutes with the maps to $\Gamma/\mathcal H$ and so lies in $M_{\mathcal H, i}$.
- *The limit.* The pairs $(\mathcal H, i)$ form a directed set, so the inverse limit of the nonempty finite sets $M_{\mathcal H, i}$ is nonempty. An element of it is a compatible family $(g_{\mathcal H, i})$. The subgroups $X^i_{\mathcal H}$ for $\mathcal H \subseteq \ker\alpha$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$ in $X$, and likewise for $Y$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, proof of Satz 3, p. 111]. Since $\mathcal U$ is cofinal, this remains true for $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$. Each $X^i_{\mathcal H}$ is open and normal in $X$, being of finite index and characteristic in the normal subgroup $X_{\mathcal H}$. So $X = \varprojlim X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$ and $Y = \varprojlim Y/Y^i_{\mathcal H}$, and the family defines a continuous homomorphism $f : Y \to X$. It is injective because each $g_{\mathcal H, i}$ is and the $Y^i_{\mathcal H}$ intersect in $1$. Its image is compact and maps onto every $X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$, so it is dense and closed, hence all of $X$. A continuous bijection of profinite groups is an isomorphism. Finally $\varphi_X \circ f$ and $\varphi_Y$ agree modulo every $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$, so they are equal. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** Jannsen and Wingberg's group is $F/(r)$. Here $F$ is the quotient of the free profinite product of $\Gamma$ with the free profinite group on $z_0, \dots, z_n$ that replaces the closed normal subgroup generated by the $z_i$ by its maximal pro-$p$ quotient, $x_i$ is the image of $z_i$, and $(r)$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by their relator $r$, built from $\sigma, \tau$ and $\beta$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, §1.2, p. 74]. Killing $x_0, \dots, x_n$ gives a canonical projection $F/(r) \to \Gamma$. By their Theorem 1, $F/(r)$ with this projection is a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with torsion $p^s$, degree $n$ and character $\alpha$, provided a condition (+) on the invariants holds [@jannsenwingberg1982, §1.2, p. 74, and Theorem 1, p. 75]. In the proof of Theorem 2 they note that (+) holds for local fields, citing Koch [@jannsenwingberg1982, proof of Theorem 2, p. 75]. By part 1, $(G_K, \pi)$ is a Demuškin formation with the same invariants, and part 3 gives an isomorphism $G_K \to F/(r)$ whose composite with the projection is $\pi$. The elements of $G_K$ corresponding to $\sigma, \tau \in F/(r)$ then restrict to $\sigma, \tau \in \Gamma$, and those corresponding to $x_0, \dots, x_n$ lie in the kernel of $\pi$, which is the wild inertia group. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Wingberg's Satz 2.* As printed, Wingberg's Satz 2 says that every isomorphism $Y/Y^2 \to X/X^2$ over $\Gamma$ lifts to an isomorphism $Y \to X$, with his indexing $Z^0 = Z$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Satz 2, pp. 100–101]. Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg state that it was stated incorrectly, and give the corrected form for automorphisms of $G_K$: the lift agrees with the given map only one step lower. They add that the corrected form is exactly what was needed for all results of Wingberg's paper and of Jannsen and Wingberg's, except the statement in Jannsen and Wingberg's §5.1 [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15) and the remark after it, p. 421]. The proof of part 3 does not use Satz 2. It uses only the existence, for each fixed $\mathcal H$, of a compatible family of isomorphisms over $\Gamma/\mathcal H$, which Wingberg's construction provides because each new map is chosen as a lift of the previous one.
- *Jakovlev.* Wingberg's proof of Lemma 3 uses results of Jakovlev on symplectic modules. Wingberg notes that those papers contain some errors, but that the statements he cites are easily checked to be correct [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, proof of Lemma 3, p. 105].
- *Use.* Part 3 is what makes re-marking arguments work: applied to $G_K$ marked by $\pi$ and by $\beta \circ \pi$ for an automorphism $\beta$ of $\Gamma$, it shows that for odd $p$ every automorphism of $\Gamma$ that preserves the mod-$p$ cyclotomic character lifts to $G_K$. See [tame automorphisms lift for odd $p$](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md).
- *The case $p = 2$.* Wingberg assumes that $p$ is odd [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, §I, p. 99]. For $p = 2$, see [generators and relations for $G_K$](local-galois-group-presentations.md) on Diekert's case and on the case where $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified.
