**Setting.** Use the Setting and notation of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ has degree $d$ and residue field of order $q$, and $G = G_K$ is identified with $F/(\varrho)$, where $F = P \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ is free among semidirect products of $\mathcal{G}$ with pro-$p$ groups, $\mathcal{G}$ is generated by $\sigma, \tau$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, and $P$ is normally generated by $x_0, \dots, x_d$. Wild inertia $P_K$ is the image of $P$, and $\mathcal{G}$ maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma = G/P_K = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$, with $\tau$ generating tame inertia. Also $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathcal{G}]]$, the character $\omega : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ giving the action on $\mu_p$ is extended to a ring homomorphism $\omega : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$, $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, $\bar P = P/P^{(2)}$, $\bar V = P_K/P_K^{(2)}$, and $u = \bar g e_h - \sigma \in \Lambda$. Write $x^y = yxy^{-1}$ and $[x, y] = xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. By part 1 there, $\bar P$ is free over $\Lambda$ on $\bar x_0, \dots, \bar x_d$, and $\bar V = \Lambda\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \Lambda\bar x_d$, where $\Lambda\bar x_i \cong \Lambda$ for $i \ge 1$ and $\rho\bar x_0 = \omega(\rho)\bar x_0$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$. With $p^s$ the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, we also use from there the $p^s$-central series $P^{p,i,s}$, the ring $\Lambda_s = (\mathbb{Z}/p^s)[[\mathcal{G}]]$, the character $\omega_s : \Lambda_s \to \mathbb{Z}/p^s$ giving the action on $\mu_{p^s}$, the map $*$ on $\Lambda_s$ with $\rho^* = \omega_s(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, and $u_s = \kappa e_h - \sigma \in \Lambda_s$. Reduction $\Lambda_s \to \Lambda$, $\lambda \mapsto \bar\lambda$, is compatible with $\omega_s, \omega$ and $*$, and $\bar u_s = u$.

Put $N_s = P/P^{p,2,s}$ and $M = P^{p,2,s}/P^{p,3,s}$, written additively, and write $\tilde x$ for the class of $x \in P$ in $N_s$. As in the proof of part 2 there, $N_s$ and $M$ are compact $\Lambda_s$-modules under conjugation, $M$ is central in $P/P^{p,3,s}$, both have exponent dividing $p^s$, and the commutator induces a continuous biadditive map $b : N_s \times N_s \to M$, $b(\tilde x, \tilde y) = [x, y]P^{p,3,s}$, with $b(\rho\tilde x, \rho\tilde y) = \rho\, b(\tilde x, \tilde y)$; also $b(\tilde y, \tilde x) = -b(\tilde x, \tilde y)$, since $[y, x] = [x, y]^{-1}$. The reduction map $N_s \to \bar P$, $\tilde x \mapsto \bar x$, maps $\lambda\tilde x$ to $\bar\lambda\bar x$.

*The relator.* For even $d$, $\varrho = (x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0, \tau)^\kappa x_1^{p^s}[x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$. For odd $d$, $\varrho = (x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0, \tau)^\kappa x_1^{p^s}[x_1, y_1][x_2, x_3]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$, and we use the presentation in the form stated by Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, with $\kappa$ their $g$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.14), pp. 419–420]. There $y_1$ is defined as follows [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 420]. Let $\alpha : \mathcal{G} \to (\mathbb{Z}/p^s)^\times$ give the action on $\mu_{p^s}$, and $\beta : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ any map lifting $\alpha$. For $\rho$ in $\mathcal{G}$ and $x \in F$ put
$$\{x, \rho\} = \big(x^{\beta(1)}\rho^2 x^{\beta(\rho)}\rho^2 \cdots x^{\beta(\rho^{p-2})}\rho^2\big)^{\pi/(p-1)},$$
where $\pi \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ has component $1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $0$ elsewhere. Let $\pi_2 \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ have component $1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$ and $0$ elsewhere, $\tau_2 = \tau^{\pi_2}$ and $\sigma_2 = \sigma^{\pi_2}$. Then
$$y_1 = x_1^{\tau_2^{p+1}}\,\{x_1, \tau_2^{p+1}\}^{\sigma_2\tau_2^{a_0}}\,\{\{x_1, \tau_2^{p+1}\}, \sigma_2\tau_2^{a_0}\}^{\sigma_2\tau_2^{b_0} + \tau_2^{(p+1)/2}},$$
where an exponent that is a sum of two elements of $\mathcal{G}$ denotes the product of the two conjugates, and $a_0, b_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$ (called $a, b$ there) are chosen so that $-\alpha(\sigma\tau^{a_0}) \bmod p$ is a square in $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ and $-\alpha(\sigma\tau^{b_0}) \bmod p$ is not. The results of the cited entry that are used below depend only on the shape of $\varrho$, and, for odd $d$, on $[x_1, y_1] \in [P, P]$, which holds because $y_1 \in P$ (part 1).

*The quadratic character.* Let $\varepsilon : \mathcal{G} \to \{\pm 1\}$ be the character with $\varepsilon(\sigma) = -1$ and $\varepsilon(\tau) = 1$, which respects $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$. It is trivial on tame inertia, and the image of $\sigma$ topologically generates $\Gamma/I = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ur}}/K)$, where $I$ is tame inertia, because $\sigma$ and $\tau$ generate $\Gamma$. So $\varepsilon$ is the character of $\Gamma$ whose kernel fixes the unramified quadratic extension $K_2$ of $K$. Extend it to a continuous ring homomorphism $\varepsilon : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$. For $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, $\varepsilon(\rho^*) = \omega(\rho)\varepsilon(\rho)^{-1} = (\omega\varepsilon)(\rho)$, so by linearity and continuity
$$\varepsilon(\lambda^*) = (\omega\varepsilon)(\lambda) \qquad (\lambda \in \Lambda). \tag{1}$$
The characters $\omega$ and $\varepsilon$ cut out $K(\mu_p)$ and $K_2$, so $\omega = \varepsilon$ exactly when $K(\mu_p) = K_2$.

*Kondo's automorphisms.* For $d \ge 3$, the surface generators, the free group $\Pi$ with its letters $\alpha_i, \beta_i$, the map $\iota : \Pi \to P$, the group $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ of automorphisms of $\Pi$ fixing the boundary word, and Kondo's homomorphism $\Psi : \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi) \to \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ are as in the Setting of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md). The superscript $\delta$ there refers to the boundary word and is unrelated to the element $\delta \in \Lambda$ of part 1 below.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *The skew part of $y_1$.* Let $d$ be odd. Then $y_1 \in P$, and there is a unique $\delta \in \Lambda$ with $\bar y_1 = \delta\bar x_1$ in $\bar P$. Put $\delta_0 = \delta - \delta^*$. Then $\varepsilon(\delta_0) \ne 0$.
2. *Even degree.* Let $d \ge 4$ be even and $\lambda \in \ker\omega$. There is an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\theta(\rho) \in \rho P_K^{(2)}$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, $\theta(x_i) \in x_iP_K^{(2)}$ for $i \ne 1, 4$, and
$$\overline{\theta(x_1)} = \bar x_1 + \lambda\bar x_3,\qquad \overline{\theta(x_4)} = \bar x_4 - \lambda^*\bar x_2 \quad\text{in } \bar V.$$
3. *Odd degree.* Let $d \ge 3$ be odd and $\nu \in \ker\omega$. There is an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\theta(\rho) \in \rho P_K^{(2)}$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, $\theta(x_i) \in x_iP_K^{(2)}$ for $i \ne 1, 3$, and
$$\overline{\theta(x_1)} = \bar x_1 + \nu\bar x_2,\qquad \overline{\theta(x_3)} = \bar x_3 - \nu^*\delta\nu\,\bar x_2 - \nu^*\delta_0\,\bar x_1 \quad\text{in } \bar V.$$
4. *No invariant line.* Let $d \ge 3$ and $\omega \ne \varepsilon$. Every automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\theta(\rho) \in \rho P_K^{(2)}$ for all $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ induces a $\Lambda$-linear automorphism $\bar\theta$ of $\bar V$. Let $\bar\Phi : \bar V \to \mathbb{F}_p$ be a nonzero continuous homomorphism with $\bar\Phi(\rho v) = \varepsilon(\rho)\bar\Phi(v)$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$. Then $\bar\Phi \circ \bar\theta \notin \mathbb{F}_p\bar\Phi$ for at least one $\theta$ in the following list: Kondo's automorphisms $\Psi(\varphi)$, $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$; for even $d$, the transvection $T_1$ and an automorphism as in part 2 with $\lambda = \sigma - \omega(\sigma)$; for odd $d$, an automorphism as in part 3 with $\nu = \sigma - \omega(\sigma)$.

Part 4 is applied in [restricted two-dimensional representations](restricted-two-dimensional-representations-of-g-qp-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), to a functional coming from the anticyclotomic character of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Kondo's automorphisms alone do not suffice there: they fix the complementary generators $x_0, x_1$ (and $x_2$ for even $d$), so they fix every such $\bar\Phi$ that vanishes on the surface generators.

**Three facts used below.**

- *(i) $p^s$-th powers.* The group $P/P^{p,3,s}$ has class at most $2$, as $[P, P] \subseteq P^{p,2,s}$ and $[P^{p,2,s}, P] \subseteq P^{p,3,s}$. In a group of class at most $2$, $(xy)^n = x^ny^n[y, x]^{n(n-1)/2}$. For $x, y \in P$ the element $[y, x]^{p^s(p^s-1)/2}$ lies in $(P^{p,2,s})^{p^s} \subseteq P^{p,3,s}$, because $p$ is odd, so that $(p^s - 1)/2$ is an integer. So $(xy)^{p^s} \equiv x^{p^s}y^{p^s}$ modulo $P^{p,3,s}$, and $w^{p^s} \in P^{p,3,s}$ for $w \in P^{p,2,s}$. Hence $x \mapsto x^{p^s}P^{p,3,s}$ induces an additive map $\wp : N_s \to M$ with $\wp(\rho\tilde x) = \rho\wp(\tilde x)$. It is continuous, so it is $\Lambda_s$-linear, as the span of $\mathcal{G}$ is dense in $\Lambda_s$.
- *(ii) Two congruences.* By Lemma (b) of the cited entry with $r = s$, $\ker\omega_s = u_s\Lambda_s$, so $(\ker\omega_s)M \subseteq u_sM$. By Lemma (c) there with $r = s$ and $N = N_s$, $b(\lambda\tilde x, \mu\tilde y) \equiv b(\tilde x, \lambda^*\mu\tilde y)$ modulo $u_sM$ for $\lambda, \mu \in \Lambda_s$.
- *(iii) Lifts into $\ker\omega_s$.* Every $\lambda \in \ker\omega$ is $\bar\lambda_s$ for some $\lambda_s \in \ker\omega_s$. Indeed, if $\lambda' \in \Lambda_s$ lifts $\lambda$, then $\omega_s(\lambda') \equiv \omega(\lambda) = 0 \pmod p$, so $\lambda_s = \lambda' - \omega_s(\lambda')\cdot 1$ lies in $\ker\omega_s$ and still lifts $\lambda$.

**Proof of part 1.**

*(a) A pro-$2$ subgroup.* The closed subgroup $I \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ generated by $\tau$ is isomorphic to $\prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and $\tau_2$ generates its Sylow pro-$2$ subgroup, which is characteristic in $I$. As $\sigma I\sigma^{-1} = I$, $\sigma$ normalizes it. The closed subgroup generated by $\sigma$ is procyclic, and $\sigma_2$ generates its Sylow pro-$2$ subgroup. So the closed subgroup $H$ generated by $\sigma_2$ and $\tau_2$ is the product of the closed subgroups they generate, an extension of a pro-$2$ group by a pro-$2$ group, hence pro-$2$. This is the argument of step (d) of [nonabelian finite subgroups](nonabelian-finite-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) for $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

*(b) $y_1 \in P$.* Let $x \in P$ and $\rho \in H$. The product inside $\{x, \rho\}$ maps to $\rho^{2(p-1)}$ in $\mathcal{G}$, and $(\rho^{2(p-1)})^{\pi/(p-1)} = \rho^{2\pi} = 1$, because $\pi$ has $2$-component $0$ and $H$ is pro-$2$. So $\{x, \rho\} \in P$. As $P$ is normal in $F$, all three factors of $y_1$ lie in $P$, and so does $y_1$.

*(c) $\delta$.* Let $F_H$ be the closed subgroup generated by $x_1$ and $H$, and $N_1$ the closed subgroup generated by the $hx_1h^{-1}$, $h \in H$. Then $N_1$ is normal in $F_H$ and $F_H = N_1H$. As $N_1 \subseteq P$ and $H \cap P = 1$, $F_H \cap P = N_1$. By its definition $y_1 \in F_H$, so $y_1 \in N_1$, whose image in $\bar P$ is the closure of the span of the $h\bar x_1$, which lies in $\Lambda\bar x_1$. So $\bar y_1 = \delta\bar x_1$ for some $\delta \in \Lambda$, unique because $\Lambda\bar x_1$ is free.

*(d) Evaluation at a character.* Let $\chi : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ be a continuous character, extended to a ring homomorphism $\Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$. Let $A_\chi = \mathbb{F}_p$, with $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ acting by $\chi(\rho)$. By the universal property of $F$ there is a continuous homomorphism $\Theta : F \to A_\chi \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, maps $x_1$ to $(1, 1)$ and maps $x_i$ to $(0, 1)$ for $i \ne 1$. On $P$ it takes values in $A_\chi$, and $\Theta(\rho x\rho^{-1}) = \chi(\rho)\Theta(x)$ for $x \in P$. So it factors through the $\Lambda$-linear map $\bar P \to A_\chi$ with $\sum_i\lambda_i\bar x_i \mapsto \chi(\lambda_1)$, and $\Theta(y_1) = \chi(\delta)$ by (c).

*Claim.* For $x \in P$ and $\rho \in H$, $\Theta(\{x, \rho\}) = \Theta(x)$ if $\omega(\rho)\chi(\rho)^2 = 1$, and $\Theta(\{x, \rho\}) = 0$ otherwise. Indeed, put $t = \Theta(x)$. In $A_\chi \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, $(a, g)(a', g') = (a + \chi(g)a', gg')$, so the product inside $\{x, \rho\}$ maps to $(St, g)$ with $g = \rho^{2(p-1)}$ and $S = \sum_{j=0}^{p-2}\beta(\rho^j)\chi(\rho)^{2j}$, read in $\mathbb{F}_p$. As $\beta$ lifts $\alpha$ and $\alpha$ reduces modulo $p$ to $\omega$, $\beta(\rho^j) \equiv \omega(\rho)^j$, so $S = \sum_{j=0}^{p-2} z^j$ with $z = \omega(\rho)\chi(\rho)^2 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$. Since $\chi(\rho)^{p-1} = 1$, $\chi(g) = 1$, so $(St, g)^n = (nSt, g^n)$ for $n \ge 1$, and by continuity $(St, g)^c = (\bar cSt, g^c)$ for $c \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, with $\bar c$ the image of $c$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p$. For $c = \pi/(p-1)$, $\bar c = (p-1)^{-1} = -1$ and $g^c = 1$ by (b). So $\Theta(\{x, \rho\}) = -St$. If $z = 1$, then $S = p - 1 = -1$. Otherwise $z^{p-1} = 1$ gives $S = (z^{p-1} - 1)/(z - 1) = 0$.

Put $\rho_1 = \tau_2^{p+1}$ and $\rho_2 = \sigma_2\tau_2^{a_0}$, and let $c_i \in \{0, 1\}$ be $1$ exactly when $\omega(\rho_i)\chi(\rho_i)^2 = 1$. As $\Theta$ is a homomorphism on $P$, $\Theta(x^g) = \chi(g)\Theta(x)$ for $g \in \mathcal{G}$, and $\{x_1, \rho_1\} \in P$, the Claim gives
$$\chi(\delta) = \Theta(y_1) = \chi(\tau_2)^{p+1} + \chi(\rho_2)\,c_1 + \big(\chi(\sigma_2\tau_2^{b_0}) + \chi(\tau_2)^{(p+1)/2}\big)\,c_1c_2. \tag{2}$$

*(e) The characters $\varepsilon$ and $\omega\varepsilon$.* By (1), $\varepsilon(\delta^*) = (\omega\varepsilon)(\delta)$, so $\varepsilon(\delta_0) = \varepsilon(\delta) - (\omega\varepsilon)(\delta)$. Let $C$ be the Sylow $2$-subgroup of $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$. For $y \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, $y^{\pi_2}$ is the component of $y$ in $C$, and $y$ is a square exactly when $y^{\pi_2} \in C^2$, since $\mathbb{F}_p^\times = C \times C'$ with $C'$ of odd order. Put $w_\sigma = \omega(\sigma_2) = \omega(\sigma)^{\pi_2}$ and $w_\tau = \omega(\tau_2)$, elements of $C$. Also $\varepsilon(\tau_2) = 1$ and $\varepsilon(\sigma_2) = -1$, as $\pi_2$ maps to $1$ in $\mathbb{Z}/2$.

For $\chi = \varepsilon$, $\omega(\rho_1)\chi(\rho_1)^2 = w_\tau^{p+1}$ and $\omega(\rho_2)\chi(\rho_2)^2 = w_\sigma w_\tau^{a_0}$. For $\chi = \omega\varepsilon$, these are $(w_\tau^{p+1})^3$ and $(w_\sigma w_\tau^{a_0})^3$. Elements of $C$ have $2$-power order, so $y^3 = 1$ only for $y = 1$, and both characters give the same $c_1$ and $c_2$: $c_1 = 1$ exactly when $w_\tau^{p+1} = 1$, and $c_2 = 1$ exactly when $w_\sigma w_\tau^{a_0} = 1$. By (2), using $(\omega\varepsilon)(\sigma_2) = -w_\sigma$ and $(\omega\varepsilon)(\tau_2) = w_\tau$,
$$\varepsilon(\delta) = 1 - c_1,\qquad (\omega\varepsilon)(\delta) = w_\tau^{p+1} - w_\sigma w_\tau^{a_0}c_1 + \big(w_\tau^{(p+1)/2} - w_\sigma w_\tau^{b_0}\big)c_1c_2,$$
since in $\varepsilon(\delta)$ the coefficient of $c_1c_2$ is $-1 + 1 = 0$.

- If $c_1 = 0$, then $\varepsilon(\delta) = 1 \ne w_\tau^{p+1} = (\omega\varepsilon)(\delta)$.
- If $c_1 = 1$ and $c_2 = 0$, then $\varepsilon(\delta) = 0$ and $(\omega\varepsilon)(\delta) = 1 - w_\sigma w_\tau^{a_0} \ne 0$.
- The case $c_1 = c_2 = 1$ does not occur. The choice of $a_0, b_0$ says that $-\omega(\sigma)\omega(\tau)^{a_0}$ is a square and $-\omega(\sigma)\omega(\tau)^{b_0}$ is not. Taking components in $C$, which contains $-1$: $-w_\sigma w_\tau^{a_0} \in C^2$ and $-w_\sigma w_\tau^{b_0} \notin C^2$. If $c_2 = 1$, the first says $-1 \in C^2$. If $c_1 = 1$, then $w_\tau^2 = w_\tau^{p+1}w_\tau^{-(p-1)} = 1$, so $w_\tau = \pm 1 \in C^2$, and then $-w_\sigma w_\tau^{b_0} = -w_\tau^{b_0 - a_0} \in C^2$, contradicting the second.

So $\varepsilon(\delta_0) \ne 0$. $\square$

**A lifting step.** Let $\psi$ be an endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and maps each $x_i$ into $P$, such that the classes of the $\psi(x_i)$ generate $\bar P$ as a $\Lambda$-module and $\psi(\varrho) \in \varrho P^{p,3,s}$. Then there is an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\theta(\rho) \in \rho P_K^{p,2,s}$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ and $\theta(x_i) \in \psi(x_i)P_K^{p,2,s}$ for every $i$, the generators being read in $G$; in particular the same holds modulo $P_K^{(2)} \supseteq P_K^{p,2,s}$. This is what the last two steps of the proof of part 2 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) show, as they use only these properties of $\psi$: $\psi$ induces an automorphism of $G/P_K^{p,3,s}$ that is the identity on the image of $\mathcal{G}$, and [Wingberg's lifting theorem for the $p^s$-central series](wingberg-lifting-for-the-p-s-central-series-of-wild-inertia.md) gives an automorphism of $G$, inducing the identity on $\Gamma$, that agrees with it modulo $P_K^{p,2,s}$.

In both proofs below, a defect $D \in u_sM$ is removed in the same way as there: choose $w_0 \in M$ with $u_sw_0 = -D$ and $z \in P^{p,2,s}$ with class $w_0$, and replace $x_0$ by $x_0z$. By the Computation of that entry, with $r = s$, $N = P^{p,3,s}$, $A = M$, $X = x_0P^{p,3,s}$ and $a = w_0$, this multiplies $(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0, \tau)^\kappa$ by $u_sw_0$ in $F/P^{p,3,s}$. All factors of $\varrho$ lie in $P$, and $M$ is central in $P/P^{p,3,s}$, so changes of the factors by elements of $M$ add up. Since $z \in P^{p,2,s} \subseteq P^{(2)}$, the class of $x_0$ in $\bar P$ is unchanged.

**Proof of part 2.** By fact (iii), choose $\lambda_s \in \ker\omega_s$ with $\bar\lambda_s = \lambda$. Choose $w, y' \in P$ with $\tilde w = \lambda_s\tilde x_3$ and $\tilde y' = -\lambda_s^*\tilde x_2$, so that $\bar w = \lambda\bar x_3$ and $\bar y' = -\lambda^*\bar x_2$. Replacing $x_1, x_4$ by $x_1w, x_4y'$ changes the factors of $\varrho$ as follows, modulo $P^{p,3,s}$, where $x_1$ occurs only in $x_1^{p^s}$ and $[x_1, x_2]$, and $x_4$ only in $[x_3, x_4]$:

- $x_1^{p^s}$ changes by $m_1 = \wp(\lambda_s\tilde x_3) = \lambda_s\wp(\tilde x_3)$, by fact (i). So $m_1 \in (\ker\omega_s)M \subseteq u_sM$.
- $[x_1, x_2]$ changes by $m_2 = b(\lambda_s\tilde x_3, \tilde x_2) \equiv b(\tilde x_3, \lambda_s^*\tilde x_2)$ modulo $u_sM$.
- $[x_3, x_4]$ changes by $m_3 = b(\tilde x_3, -\lambda_s^*\tilde x_2) = -b(\tilde x_3, \lambda_s^*\tilde x_2)$.

So $D = m_1 + m_2 + m_3 \in u_sM$. Choose $z$ as above, and let $\psi$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, maps $x_0, x_1, x_4$ to $x_0z, x_1w, x_4y'$ and fixes the other $x_i$. Then $\psi(\varrho) \equiv \varrho\cdot(u_sw_0 + D) = \varrho$ modulo $P^{p,3,s}$. The classes of the $\psi(x_i)$ in $\bar P$ are $\bar x_0$, $\bar x_1 + \lambda\bar x_3$, $\bar x_2$, $\bar x_3$, $\bar x_4 - \lambda^*\bar x_2$ and $\bar x_5, \dots, \bar x_d$, which generate $\bar P$. The lifting step gives $\theta$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** *A lift of $\delta$.* Let $N_1$ and $H$ be as in part 1 (c). The image of $N_1$ in $N_s$ is the closure of the span of the $h\tilde x_1$, $h \in H$, which lies in the compact, hence closed, submodule $\Lambda_s\tilde x_1$. As $y_1 \in N_1$, there is $\delta_s \in \Lambda_s$ with $\tilde y_1 = \delta_s\tilde x_1$. Reducing to $\bar P$ gives $\bar y_1 = \bar\delta_s\bar x_1$, so $\bar\delta_s = \delta$ by the uniqueness in part 1. Put $\delta_{0,s} = \delta_s - \delta_s^*$, so that $\bar\delta_{0,s} = \delta_0$ and $\delta_{0,s}^* = -\delta_{0,s}$.

By fact (iii), choose $\nu_s \in \ker\omega_s$ with $\bar\nu_s = \nu$. Put $\kappa' = -\nu_s^*\delta_s\nu_s$ and $\kappa'' = -\nu_s^*\delta_{0,s}$ in $\Lambda_s$, so that $\bar\kappa' = -\nu^*\delta\nu$ and $\bar\kappa'' = -\nu^*\delta_0$. Choose $w, y' \in P$ with $\tilde w = \nu_s\tilde x_2$ and $\tilde y' = \kappa'\tilde x_2 + \kappa''\tilde x_1$. Let $\psi_0$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, maps $x_1, x_3$ to $x_1w, x_3y'$ and fixes the other $x_i$. It maps $P$ into $P$, hence $P^{p,2,s}$ into $P^{p,2,s}$, and induces a $\Lambda_s$-linear map on $N_s$, so $\psi_0(y_1) \in P$ has class $\delta_s(\tilde x_1 + \nu_s\tilde x_2)$. Here $x_1$ occurs in $\varrho$ only in $x_1^{p^s}$ and in $[x_1, y_1]$, where $y_1$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by $x_1$, $\sigma$ and $\tau$, and $x_3$ occurs only in $[x_2, x_3]$. Modulo $P^{p,3,s}$:

- $x_1^{p^s}$ changes by $m_1 = \nu_s\wp(\tilde x_2)$, by fact (i); so $m_1 \in (\ker\omega_s)M \subseteq u_sM$.
- $[x_1, y_1]$ changes by $m_2 = b(\tilde x_1 + \nu_s\tilde x_2, \delta_s\tilde x_1 + \delta_s\nu_s\tilde x_2) - b(\tilde x_1, \delta_s\tilde x_1) = b(\tilde x_1, \delta_s\nu_s\tilde x_2) + b(\nu_s\tilde x_2, \delta_s\tilde x_1) + b(\nu_s\tilde x_2, \delta_s\nu_s\tilde x_2)$.
- $[x_2, x_3]$ changes by $m_3 = b(\tilde x_2, \kappa'\tilde x_2) + b(\tilde x_2, \kappa''\tilde x_1)$.

By antisymmetry and fact (ii), modulo $u_sM$,
$$b(\nu_s\tilde x_2, \delta_s\tilde x_1) = -b(\delta_s\tilde x_1, \nu_s\tilde x_2) \equiv -b(\tilde x_1, \delta_s^*\nu_s\tilde x_2),\qquad b(\nu_s\tilde x_2, \delta_s\nu_s\tilde x_2) \equiv b(\tilde x_2, \nu_s^*\delta_s\nu_s\tilde x_2),$$
$$b(\tilde x_2, \kappa''\tilde x_1) = -b(\kappa''\tilde x_1, \tilde x_2) \equiv -b(\tilde x_1, \kappa''^*\tilde x_2) = -b(\tilde x_1, \delta_{0,s}\nu_s\tilde x_2),$$
where $\kappa''^* = -\delta_{0,s}^*\nu_s = \delta_{0,s}\nu_s$, because $*$ reverses products. Adding up,
$$m_2 + m_3 \equiv b\big(\tilde x_1, (\delta_s - \delta_s^* - \delta_{0,s})\nu_s\tilde x_2\big) + b\big(\tilde x_2, (\nu_s^*\delta_s\nu_s + \kappa')\tilde x_2\big) = 0 \pmod{u_sM}.$$
So $D = m_1 + m_2 + m_3 \in u_sM$. With $z$ as above, let $\psi$ agree with $\psi_0$ except that $\psi(x_0) = x_0z$. As $\psi(y_1) = \psi_0(y_1)$, $\psi(\varrho) \equiv \varrho\cdot(u_sw_0 + D) = \varrho$ modulo $P^{p,3,s}$. The classes of the $\psi(x_i)$ in $\bar P$ are $\bar x_0$, $\bar x_1 + \nu\bar x_2$, $\bar x_2$, $\bar x_3 - \nu^*\delta\nu\,\bar x_2 - \nu^*\delta_0\,\bar x_1$ and $\bar x_4, \dots, \bar x_d$, which generate $\bar P$. The lifting step gives $\theta$. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** *$\bar\theta$ is $\Lambda$-linear.* $\theta$ preserves $P_K$, the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), hence $P_K^{(2)}$, and so induces an automorphism $\bar\theta$ of $\bar V$. For $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ write $\theta(\rho) = \rho c$ with $c \in P_K^{(2)}$. Conjugation by elements of $P_K$ is trivial on $\bar V$, so $\bar\theta(\rho v) = \rho\,\bar\theta(v)$.

*Coordinates.* Put $\varphi_i = \bar\Phi(\bar x_i)$. By continuity and equivariance, $\bar\Phi(\sum_i\lambda_i\bar x_i) = \sum_i\varepsilon(\lambda_i)\varphi_i$. As $\rho\bar x_0 = \omega(\rho)\bar x_0$, $\varepsilon(\rho)\varphi_0 = \omega(\rho)\varphi_0$ for all $\rho$, and $\omega \ne \varepsilon$ gives $\varphi_0 = 0$. Since $\bar\Phi \ne 0$, some $\varphi_i$ with $i \ge 1$ is nonzero. Suppose, for a contradiction, that $\bar\Phi \circ \bar\theta \in \mathbb{F}_p\bar\Phi$ for every $\theta$ in the list.

*Step 1: the surface generators.* Let $a_i, b_i$ be the surface generators of the Setting of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md): $(x_{2i}, x_{2i+1})$ for odd $d$ and $(x_{2i+1}, x_{2i+2})$ for even $d$, $1 \le i \le g$, with $g \ge 1$ as $d \ge 3$. For $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$, $\Psi(\varphi)$ fixes $\mathcal{G}$ and the complementary generators, and maps $a_i, b_i$ to the images under $\iota : \Pi \to P$ of $\varphi(\alpha_i), \varphi(\beta_i)$. The composite of $\iota$ with $P \to \bar V$ is a homomorphism to an abelian group, so it factors through $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$, with $[\alpha_i] \mapsto \bar a_i$ and $[\beta_i] \mapsto \bar b_i$. Let $f : \Pi^{\mathrm{ab}} \to \mathbb{F}_p$ be the homomorphism with $[\alpha_i] \mapsto \bar\Phi(\bar a_i)$, $[\beta_i] \mapsto \bar\Phi(\bar b_i)$. Then $\bar\Phi \circ \overline{\Psi(\varphi)}$ agrees with $\bar\Phi$ on the complementary $\bar x_i$, and on the surface classes it is given by $f \circ \varphi^{\mathrm{ab}}$.

Let $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ be the alternating form on $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ with $\langle[\alpha_i], [\beta_j]\rangle = \delta_{ij}$ and $\langle[\alpha_i], [\alpha_j]\rangle = \langle[\beta_i], [\beta_j]\rangle = 0$. For $v \in \Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$, $T_v(x) = x + \langle x, v\rangle v$ preserves it, since $\langle T_vx, T_vy\rangle = \langle x, y\rangle + \langle x, v\rangle\langle v, y\rangle + \langle y, v\rangle\langle x, v\rangle = \langle x, y\rangle$, and has inverse $x \mapsto x - \langle x, v\rangle v$. So $T_v \in \mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$, and $T_v = \varphi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ for some $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$, by the Setting of [even degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md). Suppose $f \ne 0$. The form is nondegenerate modulo $p$, so $v \mapsto \langle\,\cdot\,, v\rangle$ is a bijection from $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}/p$ onto $\mathrm{Hom}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}, \mathbb{F}_p)$. The classes $v$ with $\langle\,\cdot\,, v\rangle \in \mathbb{F}_pf$ form a line, of $p$ elements, while those with $f(v) \ne 0$ number $p^{2g} - p^{2g-1} \ge p(p-1) > p$. So there is $v$ with $f(v) \ne 0$ and $\langle\,\cdot\,, v\rangle \notin \mathbb{F}_pf$. Then $f \circ T_v = f + f(v)\langle\,\cdot\,, v\rangle$ is not in $\mathbb{F}_pf$, so $\bar\Phi \circ \overline{\Psi(\varphi)} \notin \mathbb{F}_p\bar\Phi$, a contradiction. Hence $f = 0$: $\bar\Phi$ vanishes on the surface classes.

*Step 2, odd $d$.* The complementary generators are $x_0, x_1$, so now $\varphi_1 \ne 0$ is the only nonzero coordinate. Let $\theta$ be as in part 3 with $\nu = \sigma - \omega(\sigma)$, which lies in $\ker\omega$. Then
$$\bar\Phi(\bar\theta(\bar x_3)) = \bar\Phi(\bar x_3 - \nu^*\delta\nu\,\bar x_2 - \nu^*\delta_0\,\bar x_1) = -\varepsilon(\nu^*)\,\varepsilon(\delta_0)\,\varphi_1,$$
while $\bar\Phi(\bar x_3) = 0$. By (1), $\varepsilon(\nu^*) = (\omega\varepsilon)(\nu) = \omega(\sigma)\varepsilon(\sigma) - \omega(\sigma) = -2\omega(\sigma) \ne 0$, and $\varepsilon(\delta_0) \ne 0$ by part 1. So $\bar\Phi \circ \bar\theta \notin \mathbb{F}_p\bar\Phi$, a contradiction.

*Step 2, even $d$.* The complementary generators are $x_0, x_1, x_2$. Let $\theta$ be as in part 2 with $\lambda = \sigma - \omega(\sigma) \in \ker\omega$. Then $\bar\Phi(\bar\theta(\bar x_4)) = \bar\Phi(\bar x_4) - \varepsilon(\lambda^*)\varphi_2 = 2\omega(\sigma)\varphi_2$, by (1) as before, while $\bar\Phi(\bar x_4) = 0$, as $x_4$ is a surface generator. So $\varphi_2 = 0$. The transvection $T_1$ of the Setting of [even degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md) fixes $\mathcal{G}$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2$, which it maps to $x_2x_1$. So $\bar\Phi(\bar T_1(\bar x_2)) = \varphi_2 + \varphi_1 = \varphi_1$, while $\bar\Phi(\bar x_2) = 0$, and therefore $\varphi_1 = 0$. Now every $\varphi_i$ vanishes, a contradiction. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The role of part 1.* In odd degree, $x_1$ enters the relation through $[x_1, y_1]$, and moving $x_1$ by $\nu\bar x_2$ creates a defect $b(\tilde x_1, \delta_{0,s}\nu_s\tilde x_2)$ that is cancelled by moving $x_3$ by $\kappa''\tilde x_1$, whose image in $\bar P$ is $-\nu^*\delta_0\bar x_1$. The new surface direction $\bar x_1$ in $\overline{\theta(x_3)}$ is seen by $\varepsilon$-twisted functionals only if $\varepsilon(\nu^*\delta_0) \ne 0$, which is where $\varepsilon(\delta_0) \ne 0$ is needed. Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's choice of $a_0, b_0$ is what excludes the remaining case. In their construction for $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $\delta$ enters only through the condition that their unit, there called $\varepsilon$ and unrelated to the character here, satisfies $\varepsilon\delta\varepsilon^* = \delta$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423], and the [nonabelian finite subgroups](nonabelian-finite-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) entry observes that only $\delta_0$ matters there.
- *The $p^s$-central series and the factor $x_1^{p^s}$.* The lifting theorem used is stated for the $p^s$-central series, so the proofs preserve the relator modulo $P^{p,3,s}$ and compute with coefficients in $\Lambda_s$; for $s = 1$ these are $P^{(3)}$ and $\Lambda$. Moving $x_1$ changes $x_1^{p^s}$ by $\wp$ of the change, for every $s$. The proofs absorb this term by taking the lifts $\lambda_s, \nu_s$ in $\ker\omega_s$, which is why $\lambda, \nu \in \ker\omega$ is assumed in parts 2 and 3.
- *What is not claimed.* Only the classes of $\theta(x_i)$ modulo $P_K^{(2)}$ are controlled, as in the cited entry, and $\lambda \mapsto \theta$ is not claimed to be a homomorphism. Part 4 excludes $\omega = \varepsilon$, where $\bar x_0$ contributes a coordinate that all the automorphisms used here fix. The case $p = 2$ is not treated.
- *Sources.* The construction and part 1 were not found in the sources opened for this entry, which are Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, Chapter VII, §5, pp. 419–423.
