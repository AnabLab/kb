**Setting.** Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field of characteristic $0$ whose residue field $k$ is perfect of characteristic $p$. Normalize $v_K(K^\times)=\mathbb Z$ and put $e=v_K(p)$. Let $X$ be a smooth proper scheme over $\mathcal O_K$, let $n\ge1$, and put
$$c_n=e\left(n+\frac1{p-1}\right)-1.$$
Let $L_n/K$ be the field cut out by the action of $G_K$ on $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\bar K},\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)$. Use Serre's [upper numbering](ramification-filtration.md).

**Theorem.**

1. $G_K^u$ acts trivially on $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\bar K},\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)$ and on $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\bar K},\mu_{p^n})$ for every $u>c_n$.
2. $v_K(\mathfrak D_{L_n/K})<e(n+1/(p-1))$.

Fontaine states both, in his numbering $G^{(u)}=G^{u-1}$, as consequences of his theorem on finite flat group schemes, and gives no proof [@fontaine1985, §2.2, Remark b), p. 526]. This is the case $i=1$ of [his conjecture](fontaine-ramification-conjecture-for-cohomology-with-good-reduction.md). The proof below writes out the deduction through the Picard scheme.

**The case $p=2$, $n=1$.** Then $c_1=2e-1$ and $\mu_2(\bar K)=\{\pm1\}$ is a trivial module. So for $K/\mathbb Q_2$ and every smooth proper $\mathcal O_K$-scheme $X$, $G_K^u$ acts trivially on $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\bar K},\mathbb F_2)$ for $u>2e-1$, and $v_K(\mathfrak D_{L_1/K})<2e$. Compare Čoupek:

- Remark 1.2(3) asserts trivial action for $\mu>1+e(1+1/(p-1))$ in the numbering $G^{(\mu)}=G^{\mu-1}$, for $i=1$ and all $e$ and $p$ [@coupek2026crystalline, Remark 1.2(3), p. 142]. At $p=2$ this is $u>2e$.
- Substituting $p=2$, $i=1$ into Theorem 1.1 gives $\alpha=2$ and $\beta=(2e-1)/4<e$, so the cutoff $u>3e$ and the different bound $1+2e+(2e-1)/4$ [@coupek2026crystalline, Theorem 1.1, p. 142].

So for schemes the cutoff of Remark 1.2(3) holds, in the stronger form $u>2e-1$. For $n=1$ and every $p$, the cutoff of part 1 is $1$ below the cutoff $u>ep/(p-1)$ of Remark 1.2(3).

**Scope.** This entry treats algebraic schemes and the algebraic étale cohomology of $X_{\bar K}$, as Fontaine does. Čoupek's theorems concern a smooth proper $p$-adic formal scheme and the étale cohomology of its adic generic fibre over $\mathbb C_K$. For the $p$-adic completion of a proper smooth scheme, matching the two groups uses the comparison of algebraic and analytic étale cohomology, which this entry does not check.

- *Projective case.* For a smooth projective variety with good reduction, Emerton and Gee embed $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\bar K},\mathbb F_p)$ into the reduction of a lattice in a crystalline representation with Hodge–Tate weights in $[-1,0]$. Their proof is an induction on dimension through smooth hypersurface sections with good reduction [@emertongee2015padic, Theorem 1.3.1 and its proof, p. 8], so it uses projectivity. The argument below needs neither projectivity nor $n=1$.
- *Formal schemes.* The argument uses Raynaud's finiteness theorem for the Picard scheme of a proper smooth scheme. For a smooth proper formal scheme that is not the completion of a scheme, the sources opened here do not settle whether $G_K^u$ acts trivially on $H^1$ for $u>2e$ at $p=2$, as Remark 1.2(3) asserts. Theorem 1.1 gives $u>3e$.

**Proof.** Write $M=H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\bar K},\mu_{p^n})$.

*Step 0 (unramified base change).* Let $K'$ be the completion of the maximal unramified extension of $K$. It is complete and discretely valued, its residue field $\bar k$ is algebraically closed, $\pi_K$ is a uniformizer of it, and $v_{K'}(p)=e$. Fix an algebraic closure $\bar K'$ of $K'$, let $\bar K$ be the algebraic closure of $K$ in it, and let $G_{K'}\to G_K$ be restriction.

Let $L/K$ be finite Galois, let $L'=LK'\subseteq\bar K'$, and let $r:\operatorname{Gal}(L'/K')\to\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ be restriction. *Claim: for $u\ge0$, $r$ maps $\operatorname{Gal}(L'/K')^u$ onto $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)^u$.* Let $K_1=L\cap K^{\mathrm{ur}}$, so that $L/K_1$ is totally ramified of some degree $e_0$. Let $\varpi$ be a uniformizer of $L$. Its minimal polynomial over $K_1$ is Eisenstein, and $\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_{K_1}[\varpi]$: in a sum $\sum_{j<e_0}c_j\varpi^j$ with $c_j\in K_1$ the terms have distinct valuations, so the sum is integral only if every $c_j$ is. Since $K_1\subseteq K'$ and $\pi_K$ is a uniformizer of both fields, the same polynomial is Eisenstein over $K'$. Hence $L'=K'(\varpi)$ is totally ramified of degree $e_0$ over $K'$, $v_{L'}$ restricts to $v_L$, and $\mathcal O_{L'}=\mathcal O_{K'}[\varpi]$ by the same argument. The map $r$ is injective, and its image fixes $K_1$, so by counting it is an isomorphism onto $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K_1)$. That group is the inertia group $G_0(L/K)$, because $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\to\operatorname{Gal}(k_{K_1}/k)$ has kernel $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K_1)$ and $L$ and $K_1$ have the same residue field. Let $s\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K_1)$. It fixes $\mathcal O_{K_1}$, and $\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_{K_1}[\varpi]$, so $s(a)-a$ is divisible by $s(\varpi)-\varpi$ for every $a\in\mathcal O_L$; likewise for $r^{-1}(s)$ on $\mathcal O_{L'}=\mathcal O_{K'}[\varpi]$. So for $x\ge0$, both $s\in G_x(L/K)$ and $r^{-1}(s)\in G_x(L'/K')$ are equivalent to $v_L(s(\varpi)-\varpi)\ge x+1$. Since $G_x(L/K)\subseteq G_0(L/K)$ for $x\ge0$, this gives $r(G_x(L'/K'))=G_x(L/K)$ for all $x\ge0$. The functions $\varphi_{L'/K'}$ and $\varphi_{L/K}$ then agree on $[0,\infty)$, and so do their inverses, which proves the claim.

Now let $N$ be a finite $G_K$-module with splitting field $L$, viewed as a $G_{K'}$-module by restriction, and let $c\ge0$. *If $G_{K'}^u$ acts trivially on $N$ for all $u>c$, then so does $G_K^u$.* Indeed, for $u>c$ the image of $G_{K'}^u$ is $\operatorname{Gal}(L'/K')^u$, by compatibility with quotients, so $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)^u=r(\operatorname{Gal}(L'/K')^u)$ acts trivially on $N$. Since $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ acts faithfully on $N$, this group is trivial, and $G_K^u$, whose image it is, acts trivially.

*Step 1 (connected pieces).* Put $R=\mathcal O_{K'}$ and $Y=X\times_{\mathcal O_K}R$, a smooth proper $R$-scheme. Its fibres are geometrically reduced, so its Stein factorization $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}R$, with $B=\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$, has $B$ finite étale over $R$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 0BUN; Theorem 03H2]. Since $R$ is henselian, $B$ is a finite product of local rings [@stacks2026, Lemma 04GG]. Each factor $B'$ is finite étale and local over $R$, so $B'\otimes_R\bar k$ is a local finite étale $\bar k$-algebra, hence equal to $\bar k$. Then $B'$ is free of rank $1$, and $B'=R$. So $Y=\coprod_jY_j$ with $\Gamma(Y_j,\mathcal O_{Y_j})=R$, and by the Stein factorization each $Y_j\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ has geometrically connected fibres [@stacks2026, Theorem 03H2]. Each $Y_j$ is defined over $R$, so $G_{K'}$ preserves each summand of $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y_{\bar K'},\mu_{p^n})=\bigoplus_jH^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y_{j,\bar K'},\mu_{p^n})$.

*Step 2 (the Picard scheme).* Fix $j$, and let $P$ be the relative Picard functor of $Y_j$ over $R$, the fppf sheafification of $T\mapsto\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{j,T})$ [@stacks2026, Definition 0B9L]. Raynaud's standing hypotheses hold: $R$ is a complete discrete valuation ring with fraction field of characteristic $0$ and algebraically closed residue field, and $Y_j$ is proper and smooth over $R$. Under them, $P$ is a group scheme, and its subgroup ${}_nP=\ker(p^n:P\to P)$ is a finite group scheme over $R$ [@raynaud1979ptorsion, §0, pp. 87–88]. Write ${}_nP=\operatorname{Spec}A$, let $A_{\mathrm{tor}}$ be the $R$-torsion of $A$, and let $J=\operatorname{Spec}(A/A_{\mathrm{tor}})$ be the scheme-theoretic closure of the generic fibre. Then $A/A_{\mathrm{tor}}$ is finite and torsion-free over the discrete valuation ring $R$, hence free, so $J$ is finite and flat. The comultiplication $A\to A\otimes_RA\to(A/A_{\mathrm{tor}})\otimes_R(A/A_{\mathrm{tor}})$ has torsion-free target, so it kills $A_{\mathrm{tor}}$; the counit and the inverse behave the same way. So $J$ is a closed subgroup scheme of ${}_nP$, finite flat and commutative, killed by $p^n$, with the same generic fibre as ${}_nP$. Raynaud proves that ${}_nP$ itself is flat when $e<p-1$ [@raynaud1979ptorsion, Theorem 1, p. 88]; passing to the closure removes any condition on $e$.

*Step 3 (points of $J$).* Put $\Omega=\bar K'$. Then $J(\Omega)={}_nP(\Omega)=P(\Omega)[p^n]$. Restricted to $\Omega$-schemes, $P$ is the Picard functor of $Y_{j,\Omega}$ over $\Omega$. The scheme $Y_{j,\Omega}$ is proper, smooth and connected, hence integral, so $\Gamma(Y_{j,\Omega},\mathcal O)$ is a finite field extension of $\Omega$, hence equal to $\Omega$. By flat base change, $\mathcal O_T\to f_{T,*}\mathcal O_{Y_{j,T}}$ is an isomorphism for every $\Omega$-scheme $T$, where $f:Y_{j,\Omega}\to\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$ is the structure map [@stacks2026, Lemma 02KH], and $Y_{j,\Omega}$ has an $\Omega$-point. Therefore $P(\Omega)=\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{j,\Omega})$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 0B9N], and
$$J(\Omega)=\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{j,\Omega})[p^n]$$
compatibly with $G_{K'}$.

*Step 4 (Kummer theory).* Since $p$ is invertible on $Y_{j,\Omega}$, the group $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y_{j,\Omega},\mu_{p^n})$ is the group of isomorphism classes of pairs $(\mathcal L,\alpha)$ with $\alpha:\mathcal L^{\otimes p^n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal O$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 040Q]. Forgetting $\alpha$ maps this group onto $\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{j,\Omega})[p^n]$. The map is injective: two choices of $\alpha$ on one $\mathcal L$ differ by a unit $u\in\Gamma(Y_{j,\Omega},\mathcal O^\times)=\Omega^\times$, and multiplication on $\mathcal L$ by a $p^n$-th root of $u$ or of $u^{-1}$ is an isomorphism between the two pairs. So $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y_{j,\Omega},\mu_{p^n})\cong J(\Omega)$ as $G_{K'}$-modules. By Fontaine's theorem over $K'$, which has perfect residue field and $v_{K'}(p)=e$, $G_{K'}^u$ acts trivially on it for every $u>c_n$.

*Step 5 (conclusion).* By Step 1, $G_{K'}^u$ acts trivially on $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y_{\bar K'},\mu_{p^n})$ for $u>c_n$. The scheme $Y_{\bar K'}=X\times_{\mathcal O_K}\bar K'$ is the base change of the proper $\bar K$-scheme $X_{\bar K}$ along an extension of algebraically closed fields, so base change gives an isomorphism $M\cong H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(Y_{\bar K'},\mu_{p^n})$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 0DDG], compatible with $G_{K'}\to G_K$. In particular $M$ is finite, by Steps 1–4. Since $c_n\ge0$, Step 0 shows that $G_K^u$ acts trivially on $M$ for $u>c_n$.

Fontaine's theorem for the finite flat group scheme $\mu_{p^n}$ over $\mathcal O_K$ shows that $G_K^u$ acts trivially on $\mu_{p^n}(\bar K)$ for $u>c_n$. On $X_{\bar K}$ the sheaf $\mu_{p^n}$ is the constant sheaf with value $\mu_{p^n}(\bar K)$. So $M\cong H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\bar K},\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)\otimes\mu_{p^n}(\bar K)$, and hence $H^1_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\bar K},\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mu_{p^n}(\bar K),M)$, $G_K$-equivariantly. For $u>c_n$, $G_K^u$ acts trivially on $\mu_{p^n}(\bar K)$ and on $M$, hence on this Hom. This proves part 1.

For part 2, part 1 gives $\operatorname{Gal}(L_n/K)^u=1$ for $u>c_n$, so in Fontaine's numbering $u_{L_n/K}\le c_n+1=e(n+1/(p-1))$. If $L_n/K$ is unramified its different is trivial. Otherwise $v_K(\mathfrak D_{L_n/K})=u_{L_n/K}-i_{L_n/K}$ with $i_{L_n/K}>0$ [@fontaine1985, Proposition 1.3, p. 518]: a nontrivial inertia element $g$ acts trivially on the residue field, so the ideal generated by all $g(x)-x$ lies in the maximal ideal. Hence $v_K(\mathfrak D_{L_n/K})<e(n+1/(p-1))$. $\square$
