**Setting.** Fix a prime $p$ and an integer $g \ge 2$. Let $M_g$ be the coarse moduli space over $\mathbb{F}_p$ of proper, smooth, geometrically connected curves of genus $g$, and for a field $k$ of characteristic $p$ put $M_{g,k} = M_g \times_{\mathbb{F}_p} k$. For a point $x$ of $M_{g,k}$, let $C_{\bar x}$ be a curve corresponding to a geometric point over $x$. The isomorphism type of its étale fundamental group $\pi_1(C_{\bar x})$, a topologically finitely generated profinite group, depends only on $x$. If $x$ lies in the closure of another point $y$, Grothendieck's specialization theory gives a continuous surjection $\mathrm{Sp}_{y,x} : \pi_1(C_{\bar y}) \to \pi_1(C_{\bar x})$ [@saiditamagawa2017, §3, p. 6]. It is an isomorphism on maximal prime-to-$p$ quotients, which are the prime-to-$p$ completion of the fundamental group of a closed surface of genus $g$. The maximal pro-$p$ quotient is free on as many generators as the $p$-rank of the Jacobian [@saiditamagawa2017, §0, p. 1, and §2, p. 4]. So at fixed genus the prime-to-$p$ quotient sees no variation. The pro-$p$ quotient records exactly the $p$-rank, which cannot increase under specialization, because $\mathrm{Sp}_{y,x}$ induces a surjection of maximal pro-$p$ quotients; where the $p$-rank drops, the group changes. These two invariants do not determine the whole group (see the finiteness paragraph below), whose full structure is not known for a single curve of genus $g \ge 2$ in characteristic $p$ [@saiditamagawa2017, §0, p. 1]. A curve over a field containing $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ is *constant* if it descends to a curve over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ [@saiditamagawa2017, §2, p. 4].

**Theorem.**

1. *Specialization to a constant curve (Tamagawa).* Let $S$ be an $\mathbb{F}_p$-scheme, $s, t$ points of $S$ with $s$ in the closure of $t$, and $X \to S$ a proper smooth curve of genus $g \ge 2$. If the geometric fibre $X_{\bar s}$ is constant and $X_{\bar t}$ is not, then the specialization homomorphism $\pi_1(X_{\bar t}) \to \pi_1(X_{\bar s})$ is not an isomorphism [@saiditamagawa2017, Theorem 2.1, p. 4]. In terms of moduli: if $x \ne y$ are points of $M_g$ and $x$ is a closed point in the closure of $y$, then $\mathrm{Sp}_{y,x}$ is not an isomorphism [@saiditamagawa2017, Theorem A, p. 2].
2. *Nonisotrivial families (Saïdi–Tamagawa).* Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p$, $S$ a connected reduced $k$-scheme of finite type, and $f : X \to S$ a proper smooth curve of genus $g \ge 2$. Call $f$ isotrivial if there are a finite extension $k'/k$, a connected $k'$-scheme $S'$ with a finite étale $k'$-morphism $S' \to S \times_k k'$, and a proper smooth curve $X'_0$ over $k'$, such that $X \times_S S' \cong X'_0 \times_{k'} S'$ over $S'$. This holds exactly when the classifying morphism $S \to M_{g,k}$ has a single point as image [@saiditamagawa2017, Definition 3.7 and Lemma 3.8, p. 7]. If $f$ is not isotrivial, then the isomorphism type of $\pi_1(X_{\bar s})$ is not constant as $s$ runs over $S$ [@saiditamagawa2017, Theorem 3.9, p. 7]. In terms of moduli, $\pi_1$ is not constant on any subvariety of $M_{g,k}$ of positive dimension, whether or not it is complete or closed [@saiditamagawa2017, Theorem 3.6, p. 7].
3. *Affine curves.* Now let $g \ge 0$, let $D \subset X$ be a relatively étale divisor of degree $n$ with $2 - 2g - n < 0$, and suppose that $(X, D)/S$ is not isotrivial, in the same sense. Then neither the tame fundamental group $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(X_{\bar s} - D_{\bar s})$ nor the étale fundamental group $\pi_1(X_{\bar s} - D_{\bar s})$ has constant isomorphism type on $S$ [@saiditamagawa2017, Theorems 3.12 and 3.13, p. 8]. For $n > 0$ the isomorphism type of the étale group depends on the choice of the geometric point $\bar s$, and there is no specialization homomorphism for it, while the tame group has neither problem [@saiditamagawa2017, §3 and Remark 3.11 (i), pp. 7–8].

On a connected subscheme of $M_{g,k}$, the group $\pi_1$ is constant exactly when every specialization homomorphism between its points is an isomorphism. This is because a topologically finitely generated profinite group is Hopfian, so a surjection between two isomorphic such groups is an isomorphism [@saiditamagawa2017, Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3, p. 6].

**History.** Pop and Saïdi proved that no nonempty open subset of $M_g$ has constant $\pi_1$, and asked whether $\mathrm{Sp}_{y,x}$ is never an isomorphism when $y \ne x$ and $x$ is closed [@popsaidi2003, Introduction, Corollary and Question, pp. 109–110] (see [the weak Isom conjecture](weak-isom-conjecture-in-positive-characteristic.md)). Part 1 answers this question affirmatively. Saïdi and Tamagawa attribute it to Tamagawa's paper on finiteness of isomorphism classes of curves, J. Algebraic Geom. 13 (2004), Theorem (8.1), which was not opened for this entry [@saiditamagawa2017, Theorem 2.1, p. 4, and References, p. 13]. Saïdi had proved part 2 for complete subvarieties of $M_{g,k}$ [@saiditamagawa2017, Theorem 3.5, p. 7].

**Finiteness over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$.** Pop and Saïdi observed that a positive answer to their question would give, by the proof of their Corollary 4.4, that for each closed point $x$ of $M_g$ only finitely many closed points $x'$ have $\pi_1(x') \cong \pi_1(x)$ [@popsaidi2003, Question, p. 110]. Yang states the finiteness for tame fundamental groups of curves of every hyperbolic type: over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$, only finitely many isomorphism classes of smooth pointed stable curves have a given tame fundamental group. He attributes this to Raynaud and to Pop and Saïdi under extra conditions, and to Tamagawa in general [@yang2018admissible, §1, p. 651]. In the same tame setting, he states the analogue of part 1 for smooth pointed stable curves: a specialization from a curve not defined over an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$ to one defined over it does not induce an isomorphism of tame fundamental groups [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 7.8 and Remark 7.8.1, p. 676]. The following consequence is checked here: the prime-to-$p$ quotient and the $p$-rank do not determine $\pi_1(C_{\bar x})$. Since $M_g \times_{\mathbb{F}_p} \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ has dimension $3g - 3 > 0$, there are infinitely many isomorphism classes of curves of genus $g$ over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$. Their prime-to-$p$ quotients are all isomorphic, and their $p$-ranks take only the $g + 1$ values $0, \dots, g$, so infinitely many of these curves share a $p$-rank. For $n = 0$ the tame fundamental group is the étale one, so by the finiteness their étale fundamental groups fall into infinitely many isomorphism classes.

**Method.** Let $X$ be a proper smooth curve over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and $X_1$ its Frobenius twist with Jacobian $J_1$. The locally exact differentials form a vector bundle of rank $p - 1$ on $X_1$, and Raynaud's theta divisor $\Theta \subset J_1$ is a divisor attached to it [@saiditamagawa2017, §1 and Theorem 1.1, p. 3]. For $N$ prime to $p$ and a point $x \in J_1$ of order $N$, the $\mu_N$-torsor over $X_1$ given by $x$ has ordinary new part of its Jacobian exactly when no multiple $ix$ with $i$ prime to $N$ lies on $\Theta$ [@saiditamagawa2017, Proposition 1.3, p. 4]. Over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p[[t]]$ the prime-to-$p$ torsion of $J_1$ is the same in both geometric fibres. Call the saturation of a set of such torsion points the set of all multiples $ix$ of its elements $x$ with $i$ prime to the order of $x$. If the specialization homomorphism were an isomorphism, the prime-to-$p$ torsion points on $\Theta$ in the two fibres would have the same saturation [@saiditamagawa2017, Lemma 2.3, p. 5]. When the generic fibre is not constant, this fails after passing to a suitable étale covering whose Galois closure has degree prime to $p$ [@saiditamagawa2017, Proposition 2.4, p. 5]. For part 2, Saïdi and Tamagawa find, after a finite étale base change, such a covering of the family whose fibre has smaller $p$-rank at some specialization of a generic point than at that generic point [@saiditamagawa2017, Theorem 3.10, p. 7]. The $p$-rank of a fibre is the number of generators of the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of its fundamental group, so the specialization homomorphism is not injective on the corresponding open subgroup [@saiditamagawa2017, proof of Theorem 3.9, p. 12].

**Consequence for the weak Isom conjecture.** This is checked here. Let $x \ne y$ be points of $M_g$ with $x$ closed and in the closure of $y$. Then $\pi_1(C_{\bar y}) \not\cong \pi_1(C_{\bar x})$ as abstract profinite groups. Indeed, if $\psi : \pi_1(C_{\bar x}) \to \pi_1(C_{\bar y})$ were an isomorphism, then $\psi \circ \mathrm{Sp}_{y,x}$ would be a continuous surjective endomorphism of the topologically finitely generated group $\pi_1(C_{\bar y})$, hence an isomorphism by the Hopfian property above. Then $\mathrm{Sp}_{y,x}$ would be injective, hence an isomorphism, contradicting part 1. So the conjecture holds for every pair of points of $M_g$ one of which is closed and lies in the closure of the other. For affine curves, Hu, Yang and Zong state and use the tame analogue in terms of moduli, deriving it from a theorem of Tamagawa. Let $q$ be a nonclosed point of the moduli space over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ of curves of type $(g,n)$ with ordered marked points, and $t$ a closed point of its closure. Then every finite quotient of the tame fundamental group of the curve at $t$ is a finite quotient of that at $q$, but not conversely [@huyangzong2024, §2.2.2, p. 8], so these groups are not isomorphic. Part 2 concerns points that need not be closed, but it does not compare two given points: it says only that $\pi_1$ is constant on no subvariety of positive dimension.

**Status.** Saïdi and Tamagawa find it quite plausible that part 1 holds without assuming that $x$ is closed, which would give part 2 at once, and that the isomorphism type of $\pi_1(C_{\bar x})$ tends to depend on the point $x$ itself [@saiditamagawa2017, §0, p. 2]. Pop and Saïdi wrote that the closedness of $x$ was essential to their methods [@popsaidi2003, Question, p. 110].

**Sources.** Saïdi and Tamagawa's paper is cited from its arXiv version 1, paginated 1–13; the journal version was not compared. Tamagawa's paper in J. Algebraic Geom. 13 (2004) was not opened, so its statements are taken from Saïdi and Tamagawa and from Yang.
