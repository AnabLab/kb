**Setting.** Use the Setting and notation of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ has degree $d$ and residue field of order $q=p^f$, $G=G_K=F/(\varrho)$ with wild inertia $P_K$ the image of $P$, $\Gamma=G/P_K=\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$ is identified with $\mathcal G$, generated by $\sigma,\tau$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^q$, and $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, with $\omega:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ the action on $\mu_p$ and $\lambda\mapsto\lambda^*$ the anti-automorphism with $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$. Also $p^s$ is the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, and $\Lambda_s$, $\omega_s$, $u_s$ and the $p^s$-central series $P^{p,i,s}$ are as there. For even $d$,
$$\varrho=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa\,x_1^{p^s}\,[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d],$$
so for $d=2$ the last factors are just $[x_1,x_2]$. By part 1 there, $\bar V=P_K/P_K^{(2)}=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0\oplus\Lambda\bar x_1\oplus\cdots\oplus\Lambda\bar x_d$, with $\Lambda\bar x_i\cong\Lambda$ for $i\ge1$ and $\lambda\bar x_0=\omega(\lambda)\bar x_0$. As in [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), put $N_s=P/P^{p,2,s}$ and $M=P^{p,2,s}/P^{p,3,s}$, written additively, write $\tilde x$ for the class of $x\in P$ in $N_s$, and let $b:N_s\times N_s\to M$ be induced by the commutator. Its fact (i) says that $x\mapsto x^{p^s}P^{p,3,s}$ induces a $\Lambda_s$-linear map $\wp:N_s\to M$, and its fact (ii) that $(\ker\omega_s)M\subseteq u_sM$.

Put
$$\Lambda^\times_1=\{\lambda\in\Lambda^\times:\omega(\lambda)=1\},$$
a subgroup of $\Lambda^\times$, as $\omega$ is a ring homomorphism.

From [surface generators modulo the Frattini subgroup](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md), called the surface-generator entry below, we use, for $m$ prime to $p$, the closed subgroup $I_m$ generated by $\tau^m$, the order $k_m$ of $q$ modulo $m$, the element $\pi_p\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ with component $1$ in $\mathbb Z_p$ and $0$ elsewhere, the central idempotent $\varepsilon_m$, $\gamma_m=\sigma^{k_m\pi_p}$ and $\eta_m=\varepsilon_m(\gamma_m-1)$, with its Lemma 1. Tame twists $\beta=\beta_{u,a}$, with $\beta(\tau)=\tau^u$ and $\beta(\sigma)=\sigma\tau^a$, the semilinear map $\bar\theta$ induced on $\bar V$, and the description of inner automorphisms of $\Gamma$ are as in the Setting of [finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md), called the degree-three entry below. The idempotents $h_O\in\mathbb F_p[[I]]\subseteq\Lambda$, for finite nonempty sets $O$ of characters $I\to\bar{\mathbb F}_p^\times$ with $O^p=O$, are those of the section *Idempotents* of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md). None of these depends on the degree.

**Lemma (units on the complementary pair; proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $d$ even and $\lambda\in\Lambda^\times_1$. There is an automorphism $\theta_\lambda$ of $G$ with $\theta_\lambda(\rho)\in\rho P_K^{(2)}$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$, $\theta_\lambda(x_i)\in x_iP_K^{(2)}$ for $i\notin\{1,2\}$, and
$$\overline{\theta_\lambda(x_1)}=\lambda\bar x_1,\qquad\overline{\theta_\lambda(x_2)}=(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_2\quad\text{in }\bar V.$$
The automorphism $\bar\psi_\lambda$ of $G/P_K^{(2)}$ induced by $\theta_\lambda$ depends only on $\lambda$. It fixes the image of $\mathcal G$ and acts on $\bar V$ $\Lambda$-linearly: as the identity on $\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$ and on $\Lambda\bar x_i$ for $i\ge3$, by $\mu\bar x_1\mapsto\mu\lambda\bar x_1$ and by $\mu\bar x_2\mapsto\mu(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_2$. Moreover $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu=\bar\psi_{\mu\lambda}$.

*Proof.* (a) *A lift with $\omega_s=1$.* Let $\lambda'\in\Lambda_s$ lift $\lambda$. Then $\omega_s(\lambda')$ reduces to $\omega(\lambda)=1$ modulo $p$, so it is a unit of $\mathbb Z/p^s$, and $\lambda_s=\omega_s(\lambda')^{-1}\lambda'$ still lifts $\lambda$ and satisfies $\omega_s(\lambda_s)=1$. As in the proof of part 2 of the units entry, a lift of a unit is a unit, and $(\lambda_s^*)^{-1}$ lifts $(\lambda^*)^{-1}$. Choose $y_1,y_2\in P$ with $\tilde y_1=\lambda_s\tilde x_1$ and $\tilde y_2=(\lambda_s^*)^{-1}\tilde x_2$, so that $\bar y_1=\lambda\bar x_1$ and $\bar y_2=(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_2$ in $\bar P=P/P^{(2)}$.

(b) *The defect.* Replace $x_1,x_2$ by $y_1,y_2$ in the factors $x_1^{p^s}$ and $[x_1,x_2]$ of $\varrho$; the other factors do not contain $x_1$ or $x_2$. In $P/P^{p,3,s}$ these factors lie in $M$, which is central there. By fact (i), $y_1^{p^s}$ and $x_1^{p^s}$ differ by $\wp(\lambda_s\tilde x_1)-\wp(\tilde x_1)=(\lambda_s-1)\wp(\tilde x_1)$, which lies in $(\ker\omega_s)M\subseteq u_sM$ by fact (ii), as $\omega_s(\lambda_s)=1$. By Lemma (c) of the units entry with $r=s$, $N=N_s$ and $\mu=(\lambda_s^*)^{-1}$, the commutators differ by
$$b(\lambda_s\tilde x_1,(\lambda_s^*)^{-1}\tilde x_2)-b(\tilde x_1,\tilde x_2)\equiv b(\tilde x_1,\lambda_s^*(\lambda_s^*)^{-1}\tilde x_2)-b(\tilde x_1,\tilde x_2)=0\pmod{u_sM}.$$
So the total change $D$ lies in $u_sM$.

(c) *Removing it.* As in the mixing theorem, choose $w_0\in M$ with $u_sw_0=-D$ and $z\in P^{p,2,s}$ with class $w_0$, and let $\psi$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$ and maps $x_0\mapsto x_0z$, $x_1\mapsto y_1$, $x_2\mapsto y_2$, fixing the other $x_i$. By the Computation of the units entry with $r=s$, $N=P^{p,3,s}$, $A=M$, $X=x_0P^{p,3,s}$ and $a=w_0$, the first two factors of $\varrho$ change by $u_sw_0$. All factors lie in $P$ and $M$ is central in $P/P^{p,3,s}$, so the changes add, and $\psi(\varrho)\equiv\varrho$ modulo $P^{p,3,s}$, the total change being $u_sw_0+D=0$.

(d) *The lift.* The classes of the $\psi(x_i)$ in $\bar P$ are $\bar x_0$, $\lambda\bar x_1$, $(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_2$ and $\bar x_i$ for $i\ge3$, which generate $\bar P$ as a $\Lambda$-module, as $\lambda$ and $\lambda^*$ are units. By the lifting step of the mixing theorem there is an automorphism $\theta_\lambda$ of $G$ with $\theta_\lambda(\rho)\in\rho P_K^{p,2,s}$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$ and $\theta_\lambda(x_i)\in\psi(x_i)P_K^{p,2,s}$ for every $i$. As $P_K^{p,2,s}\subseteq P_K^{(2)}$ and $z\in P^{(2)}$, it has the stated properties.

(e) *The induced automorphism.* As in the proof of part 3 of the units entry, $G/P_K^{(2)}$ is generated by $\bar V$ and the image of $\mathcal G$, which $\bar\psi_\lambda$ fixes. So $\bar\psi_\lambda$ commutes with conjugation by $\mathcal G$, is $\Lambda$-linear on $\bar V$ by continuity, and is determined by its values on $\bar x_0,\dots,\bar x_d$, which are as stated. Then $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu(\bar x_1)=\bar\psi_\lambda(\mu\bar x_1)=\mu\lambda\bar x_1$ and $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu(\bar x_2)=(\mu^*)^{-1}(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_2=((\mu\lambda)^*)^{-1}\bar x_2$, and $\mu\lambda\in\Lambda^\times_1$. So $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu=\bar\psi_{\mu\lambda}$. $\square$

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$, and let $\theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ have only finitely many conjugates of its class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. Then its tame twist is $q^y$ for some $y\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, and there is $g\in G_K$ such that $\mathrm{inn}(g)\circ\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$, and let $x\in\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ have finitely many conjugates.

- (a) $x$ has a representative inducing the identity on $\Gamma$, and two such representatives differ by $\mathrm{inn}(g)$ with $g\in P_K$.
- (b) If $x$ has finite order $r$ prime to $p$, then $x$ has a representative of order dividing $r$ that induces the identity on $\Gamma$.

**Proof of the Theorem.** Let $d=2$, and let $\theta$ induce $\beta=\beta_{u,a}$ on $\Gamma$, $\theta_2$ on $G/P_K^{(2)}$ and $\bar\theta$ on $\bar V$, so $\bar\theta(\lambda v)=\beta(\lambda)\bar\theta(v)$. Steps 0 (i)–(iii) of the degree-three entry concern only $\Gamma$, $\Lambda$, $\varepsilon_m$, $\eta_m$ and the fact that $\theta$ preserves the cyclotomic character, so they apply: $\beta(\varepsilon_m)=\varepsilon_m$, $\omega\circ\beta=\omega$, $\beta(\lambda^*)=\beta(\lambda)^*$ and $\beta(\eta_m)=\eta_m$.

*Step 1: commuting with the $\Theta_\nu$.* Let $\Theta_\lambda$ be the restriction of $\bar\psi_\lambda$ to $\bar V$, for $\lambda\in\Lambda^\times_1$. By the Lemma, $\mathcal P=\{\bar\psi_\lambda:\lambda\in\Lambda^\times_1\}$ is a subgroup of the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G/P_K^{(2)})$, as $\bar\psi_1=1$, and $\lambda\mapsto\bar\psi_\lambda$ is an anti-homomorphism from $\Lambda^\times_1$ onto it. Step 1 of the proof of part 1 of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) uses only these two facts, finite outer conjugacy and the trivial centre of $\Gamma$ (units entry, part 3, whose proof concerns $\Gamma$ alone). Run with $\mathcal P$, it gives a subgroup $L_0$ of finite index in $\Lambda^\times_1$ with $\Theta_\lambda\bar\theta=\bar\theta\Theta_\lambda$ for $\lambda\in L_0$. Let $e=p^be'$, with $p\nmid e'$, be the index of its normal core in $\Lambda^\times_1$. Then
$$\Theta_\nu\bar\theta=\bar\theta\,\Theta_\nu\qquad(\nu\in(\Lambda^\times_1)^e).\tag{C}$$

By Step 0 of the proof of the surface-generator entry, which uses only $\gamma=\sigma^{\pi_p}$ and its Lemma 2, $\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$ is the set of $v\in\bar V$ with $\rho v=\omega(\rho)v$ for all $\rho\in\Gamma$; as $\omega\circ\beta=\omega$, $\bar\theta$ preserves it. For $j=1,2$ write $\bar\theta(\bar x_j)=\mu_{j0}\bar x_0+\mu_{j1}\bar x_1+\mu_{j2}\bar x_2$ with $\mu_{j0}\in\mathbb F_p$ and $\mu_{j1},\mu_{j2}\in\Lambda$, so that $\bar\theta(\lambda\bar x_j)=\omega(\lambda)\mu_{j0}\bar x_0+\beta(\lambda)\mu_{j1}\bar x_1+\beta(\lambda)\mu_{j2}\bar x_2$. Put $\nu'=(\nu^*)^{-1}$. Evaluate (C) at $\bar x_1$ and at $\bar x_2$ and compare $\bar x_1$-coordinates. Since $\Theta_\nu$ maps $\mu\bar x_1\mapsto\mu\nu\bar x_1$, $\mu\bar x_2\mapsto\mu\nu'\bar x_2$ and fixes $\bar x_0$:

- (R2) $\mu_{11}\nu=\beta(\nu)\mu_{11}$, as $\bar\theta(\Theta_\nu\bar x_1)=\bar\theta(\nu\bar x_1)$;
- (R3) $\mu_{21}\nu=\beta(\nu')\mu_{21}$, as $\bar\theta(\Theta_\nu\bar x_2)=\bar\theta(\nu'\bar x_2)$.

*Step 2: $\mu_{11}$ is a unit.* First, $\omega(\eta_m)=0$: $\omega(\gamma_m)=\omega(\sigma)^{k_m\pi_p}=1$, as $\omega(\sigma)^{p-1}=1$ and $p-1$ divides $\pi_p$ in $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Fix $m$ prime to $p$ with $(p-1)\mid m$, and $c\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}$, which exists as $p\ge3$. By Lemma 1 (b) of the surface-generator entry, $1+c\eta_m$ is a central unit, and $\omega(1+c\eta_m)=1$, so $\nu=(1+c\eta_m)^e\in(\Lambda^\times_1)^e$. By Step 0 (iii) and (ii), $\beta(\nu)=\nu$ and $\beta(\nu')=(\beta(\nu)^*)^{-1}=\nu'$. By Step 2 of the proof of the surface-generator entry, $\nu'$ is central and $\nu-\nu'$ is $\eta_m^{2p^b}$ times a central unit. So (R3), which reads $\mu_{21}\nu=\nu'\mu_{21}=\mu_{21}\nu'$, gives $\eta_m^{2p^b}\mu_{21}=0$. By Lemma 1 (c) there, $\varepsilon_m\mu_{21}=0$, and letting $m\to\infty$ along multiples of $p-1$, $\mu_{21}=\lim\varepsilon_m\mu_{21}=0$ by Lemma 1 (a). So the $\bar x_1$-coordinates of $\bar\theta(\bar x_0)\in\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$ and of $\bar\theta(\bar x_2)$ vanish.

As $\bar\theta$ is surjective, $\bar x_1=\bar\theta(w)$ for some $w=c_0\bar x_0+\lambda_1\bar x_1+\lambda_2\bar x_2$, and comparing $\bar x_1$-coordinates gives $\beta(\lambda_1)\mu_{11}=1$. The finite-ring argument at the end of Step 2 of the degree-three entry, applied in every $\mathbb F_p[\Gamma/U]$, gives $\mu_{11}\beta(\lambda_1)=1$. So $\mu_{11}\in\Lambda^\times$.

*Step 3: twisted commutation.* Let $z\in\Lambda$. By Step 3 of the proof of the surface-generator entry, $1+\eta_mz$ is a unit, and $\omega(1+\eta_mz)=1+\omega(\eta_m)\omega(z)=1$. So $\nu_z=(1+\eta_mz)^e\in(\Lambda^\times_1)^e$, and $\beta(\nu_z)=(1+\eta_m\beta(z))^e$ by Step 0 (iii). Step 3 of the degree-three entry uses only these facts, its Lemma and Lemma 1 of the surface-generator entry. Run with (R2) here in place of its (R2), with $\mu_{11}$ in place of $\mu_{ss}$, it gives
$$\mu_{11}\,z^{p^b}=\beta(z)^{p^b}\,\mu_{11}\qquad(z\in\Lambda).\tag{T}$$
Explicitly: with $u_z=(1+\eta_mz)^{p^b}=1+\eta_m^{p^b}z^{p^b}$, (R2) for $\nu_z=u_z^{e'}$ reads $(\mu_{11}u_z\mu_{11}^{-1})^{e'}=u_{\beta(z)}^{e'}$. Both sides have $p^j$-th powers tending to $1$, so the Lemma of the degree-three entry gives $\mu_{11}u_z\mu_{11}^{-1}=u_{\beta(z)}$, that is, $\eta_m^{p^b}(\mu_{11}z^{p^b}-\beta(z)^{p^b}\mu_{11})=0$. Lemma 1 (c) and (a) then give (T).

*Step 4: the tame twist lies in $\overline{\langle p\rangle}$.* Let $O$ be a finite nonempty set of characters $I\to\bar{\mathbb F}_p^\times$ with $O^p=O$, and $h=h_O$. Apply (T) with $z=h$. As $h$ is idempotent and $\beta$ is a ring automorphism, $h^{p^b}=h$ and $\beta(h)^{p^b}=\beta(h)$, so $\mu_{11}h=\beta(h)\mu_{11}$. By property (ii) of tame twists, $h$ is central, so $(h-\beta(h))\mu_{11}=0$, and $h=\beta(h)$, as $\mu_{11}$ is a unit. By property (iv), $\beta(h)=h_{O'}$ with $O'=\{\chi^{u^{-1}}:\chi\in O\}$, a set of the same kind whose characters have the same orders as those in $O$. Choose $m$ prime to $p$ divisible by $p-1$ and by these orders. As in the proof of property (v), $\mathbb F_p[I/I_m]$ embeds in a finite quotient $\mathbb F_p[\Gamma/U]$ of $\Lambda$, compatibly with the maps from $\mathbb F_p[[I]]$, and $h_O$, $h_{O'}$ have images $h_{O,(m)}$, $h_{O',(m)}$ in it. So $h_{O,(m)}=h_{O',(m)}$. Evaluating at each character $\psi$ of $I/I_m$, where $h_{O,(m)}$ takes the value $1$ if $\psi\in O$ and $0$ otherwise, gives $O=O'$.

Now let $m$ be prime to $p$, let $\xi$ be a character of order $m$, which exists as $I/I_m$ is cyclic of order $m$, and take $O=\{\xi^{p^i}:i\ge0\}$. Then $\xi^{u^{-1}}\in O'=O$, so $\xi^{u^{-1}}=\xi^{p^i}$ for some $i$, and $u^{-1}\equiv p^i\pmod m$. So $u\bmod m\in\langle p\bmod m\rangle$ for every $m$ prime to $p$. As in Step 2 of the proof of part 1 of tame twists, $\overline{\langle p\rangle}$ is the intersection of the inverse images of the subgroups $\langle p\bmod m\rangle$, so $u\in\overline{\langle p\rangle}$, that is, $u=p^\nu$ for some $\nu\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ (Setting of tame twists).

*Step 5: the tame twist lies in $\overline{\langle q\rangle}$.* Steps 4 and 5 of the degree-three entry, up to the point where it invokes the odd-prime tame-twist theorem, use only (T), the unit $\mu_{ss}$, and the central idempotent $h=h_O$ for $O$ the Frobenius orbit of a character of order exactly $q-1$. With $\mu_{11}$ in place of $\mu_{ss}$ they show that $h\Lambda$ is commutative and that $u\equiv1\pmod{(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'}$. The map $\widehat{\mathbb Z}\to(\mathbb Z/(q-1))^\times$, $\nu\mapsto p^\nu\bmod(q-1)$, is the continuous extension of $n\mapsto p^n$, and $p$ has order exactly $f$ modulo $q-1=p^f-1$. So $p^\nu\equiv1$ forces $\nu\in f\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, and $u=p^{fy}=q^y$ for some $y\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.

*Step 6: the Frobenius shift.* Step 6 of the degree-three entry uses only (T) with $z=\sigma$, the commutative ring $h\Lambda$ of Step 5 and the units $h\mu_{ss}$ and $h\sigma^{p^b}$ there. With $\mu_{11}$ in place of $\mu_{ss}$ it gives $a\in(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$. Its final paragraph then applies: for $g_1\in G$ mapping to $\sigma^{-y}$, $\mathrm{inn}(g_1)\circ\theta$ induces $\beta_{q^{-y},0}\beta_{q^y,a}=\beta_{1,q^{-y}a}$, which is conjugation by some $\tau^c$ by the Setting there (*Inner automorphisms*), as $q^{-y}a\in(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$. For $g_2\in G$ mapping to $\tau^{-c}$, $\mathrm{inn}(g_2g_1)\circ\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** This is the argument of the corollary of [finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient for $\mathbb Q_p$](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-on-the-tame-quotient-for-q-p.md), which uses only its theorem, here replaced by the Theorem above. (a) The Theorem gives the representative. If $\alpha$ and $\mathrm{inn}(g)\circ\alpha$ both induce the identity on $\Gamma$, then conjugation by the image of $g$ in $\Gamma$ is trivial, so that image is central, hence trivial, and $g\in P_K$. (b) Let $\alpha$ be as in (a). As $x^r=1$, $\alpha^r=\mathrm{inn}(g)$ with $g\in G$, and $g\in P_K$ by the argument of (a). As $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ is profinite, the closure of $\langle\alpha^r\rangle$ lies in the pro-$p$ group $\mathrm{Inn}(P_K)$, the image of $P_K$. For $\pi'\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ with component $0$ in $\mathbb Z_p$ and $1$ in every other $\mathbb Z_\ell$, $\alpha'=\alpha^{\pi'}$ satisfies $(\alpha')^r=(\alpha^r)^{\pi'}=1$. As $p\nmid r$, $\pi'\equiv1\pmod r$, so $\alpha'$ represents $x^{\pi'}=x$. It lies in the closure of $\langle\alpha\rangle$, so it induces the identity on $\Gamma$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Every degree.* Together with the degree-three entry and [the case of $\mathbb Q_p$](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-on-the-tame-quotient-for-q-p.md): for every odd $p$ and every finite $K/\mathbb Q_p$, every automorphism of $G_K$ with finitely many outer conjugates is inner on $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$.
- *Method.* In degree at least $3$ the proof uses an automorphism for every unit of $\Lambda$, acting on a surface pair, which occurs in the relator only through its commutator. In degree $2$ the pair $(x_1,x_2)$ also occurs in $x_1^{p^s}$. Scaling $x_1$ by $\lambda_s$ changes $x_1^{p^s}$ by $(\lambda_s-1)\wp(\tilde x_1)$, which the relation absorbs through $x_0$ when $\omega_s(\lambda_s)=1$. This condition costs nothing in the proof: the units it uses, $1+c\eta_m$ and $1+\eta_mz$, already have $\omega=1$, because $\eta_m$ comes from the pro-$p$ part of the Frobenius.
- *The tame twist without the odd-prime theorem.* The degree-three entry obtains $u\in\overline{\langle p\rangle}$ from [the odd-prime tame-twist theorem](odd-prime-tame-twists-and-finite-outer-conjugacy.md), which is stated for degree at least $3$. Step 4 instead evaluates (T) at the central idempotents $h_O$. In degree at least $3$, (T) is proved in Step 3 of the degree-three entry before that theorem is used, so the same evaluation gives $u\in\overline{\langle p\rangle}$ there too.
- *What remains in degree two.* For degree at least $3$ the corollary of the degree-three entry also excludes every nonidentity finite-order class with finitely many conjugates. It uses the conclusions of the odd-prime theorem, whose proofs use the surface pairs, so this is not shown here for degree $2$. Corollary (b) reduces it to two cases: automorphisms of finite order prime to $p$ that are trivial on $\Gamma$, and classes of order $p$. The later layers of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535), from wild Frattini onwards, use Kondo's automorphisms, boundary twists or units on a surface pair. Whether the units of the Lemma can replace them in degree $2$ is not checked here.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$, both residue degrees $f\in\{1,2\}$ and every $s$, for the Theorem and Corollary; odd $p$ and every even degree for the Lemma. Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
