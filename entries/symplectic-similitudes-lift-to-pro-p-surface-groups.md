**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $g \ge 1$. Let $F$ be the free pro-$p$ group on $x_1, \dots, x_{2g}$, write $(a, b) = a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ and $a^b = b^{-1}ab$, and let $D = F/R$, where $R$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by
$$r = (x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{2g-1}, x_{2g}).$$
This is a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $2g$ with $q = 0$, the pro-$p$ analogue of the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface of genus $g$ (see the Examples there). By the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), every Demushkin group with $q = 0$ is isomorphic to one of these, since for $q \ne 2$ the relation can be taken to be $x_1^q(x_1, x_2) \cdots (x_{n-1}, x_n)$, and $x_1^0 = 1$.

Automorphisms are continuous. Since $r$ lies in the closed commutator subgroup, $V = D^{\mathrm{ab}} = F^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module with basis $e_1, \dots, e_{2g}$, the classes of the $x_j$. An automorphism $\alpha$ of $D$ induces $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} \in \mathrm{GL}(V)$, which we identify with its matrix in this basis, acting on column vectors. Let $\Omega$ be the alternating $2g \times 2g$ matrix with $\Omega_{2i-1, 2i} = 1$, $\Omega_{2i, 2i-1} = -1$ for $1 \le i \le g$, and all other entries $0$, and put
$$\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p) = \{A \in \mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p) : A\Omega A^{\mathsf T} = \mu\Omega \text{ for some } \mu = \mu(A) \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times\}.$$

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. For every automorphism $\alpha$ of $D$, $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} \in \mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$.
2. For every $A \in \mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ there is an automorphism $\alpha$ of $D$ with $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} = A$.

So $\alpha \mapsto \alpha^{\mathrm{ab}}$ maps $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ onto $\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. Inner automorphisms act trivially on $V$, so $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ maps onto $\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ as well.

*Corollary (scalars).* For every $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ there is an automorphism $a_\lambda$ of $D$ acting on $V$ as multiplication by $\lambda$, since $\lambda I \in \mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ with $\mu = \lambda^2$. It acts on $D_k/D_{k+1}$, for the lower central series below, as multiplication by $\lambda^k$.

**Remarks.**

- *Free groups.* For $g = 1$, $D \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^2$ and $\mathrm{GSp}_2 = \mathrm{GL}_2$, so the theorem says nothing new. For $g \ge 2$ the image is a proper subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, whereas for a free pro-$p$ group of rank $n$ every element of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ lifts [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Proposition 1.2 (i), p. 4].
- *Tsujimura's question.* In Tsujimura's proof that a free pro-$p$ group $F$ is the only closed normal copy of itself in $\mathrm{Aut}(F)$, automorphisms lifting scalar matrices are used three times: a lift of a nontrivial central element of $\mathrm{GL}(F/\Phi(F))$ for odd $p$, and lifts $a_\lambda$ of $\lambda I \in \mathrm{GL}(F^{\mathrm{ab}})$, which act on the $i$-th graded piece of the Andreadakis–Johnson filtration by $\lambda^i$ [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Proposition 3.8, p. 11, Lemma 3.11 and Proposition 3.12, pp. 12–13]. The corollary supplies such lifts for pro-$p$ surface groups. The other ingredients are listed in [the problem](demushkin-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md), and are not checked here.
- *Method.* The lifting in part 2 is successive approximation along the lower central series, as in the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md) of Demushkin groups.
- *Scope.* Demushkin groups with $q \ne 0$, whose abelianization has torsion, are not treated.
- *Sources.* The statement and proof are not taken from a source. No novelty is claimed; the literature on pro-$\ell$ surface groups was not searched for it.

**Proof.**

*Step 0: the lower central series.* For closed subgroups $X, Y$ write $(X, Y)$ for the closed subgroup generated by the $(x, y)$ with $x \in X$ and $y \in Y$. Put $D_1 = D$, let $D_{k+1}$ be the closed subgroup generated by the $(a, b)$ with $a \in D$ and $b \in D_k$, and put $\mathrm{gr}_k = D_k/D_{k+1}$. The groups $\mathrm{gr}_k$ are compact abelian pro-$p$ groups, hence $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules, and $\mathrm{gr}_1 = V$.

1. By induction and continuity of the commutator map, $D_k$ is the closure of the $k$-th term of the abstract lower central series. So the standard inclusion $(\gamma_i, \gamma_j) \subseteq \gamma_{i+j}$ for the abstract lower central series gives $(D_i, D_j) \subseteq D_{i+j}$. In particular $\mathrm{gr}_k$ is central in $D/D_{k+1}$.
2. If $U$ is an open normal subgroup of $D$, then $D_k \subseteq U$ for large $k$. Indeed $D/U$ is a finite $p$-group, hence nilpotent, and the image of $D_k$ in $D/U$ is the $k$-th term of its lower central series.
3. Let $k \ge 2$. For $a \in D$ and $b \in D_{k-1}$ the class of $(a, b)$ in $\mathrm{gr}_k$ depends only on the classes $\bar a \in V$ and $\bar b \in \mathrm{gr}_{k-1}$, and is $\mathbb{Z}_p$-bilinear in them. Write it $\{\bar a, \bar b\}$. Indeed, for $a, a_1 \in D$, $c \in D_2$, $b, b_1 \in D_{k-1}$ and $w \in D_k$, the identities $(aa_1, b) = (a, b)^{a_1}(a_1, b)$ and $(a, bb_1) = (a, b_1)(a, b)^{b_1}$ give, modulo $D_{k+1}$ and using 1,
$$(aa_1, b) \equiv (a, b)(a_1, b), \quad (a, bb_1) \equiv (a, b)(a, b_1), \quad (ac, b) \equiv (a, b), \quad (a, bw) \equiv (a, b).$$
   The last two hold because $(c, b) \in (D_2, D_{k-1}) \subseteq D_{k+1}$ and $(a, w) \in D_{k+1}$. Continuity turns additivity into $\mathbb{Z}_p$-bilinearity.
   Moreover, for any basis $v_1, \dots, v_{2g}$ of $V$, every element of $\mathrm{gr}_k$ has the form $\sum_j \{v_j, b_j\}$ with $b_j \in \mathrm{gr}_{k-1}$. Indeed, the abstract subgroup generated by the $(a, b)$ is dense in $D_k$, and writing $\bar a = \sum_j a_j v_j$ gives $\{\bar a, \bar b\} = \sum_j \{v_j, a_j\bar b\}$. So the classes of these generators lie in the set of such sums, which is a subgroup and is compact, as the image of $\mathrm{gr}_{k-1}^{2g}$ under a continuous homomorphism. A closed subgroup containing a dense subgroup of $\mathrm{gr}_k$ is all of it.
4. For $k = 2$, item 3 gives a surjective $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear map $c : \Lambda^2 V \to \mathrm{gr}_2$ with $c(v \wedge w) = \{v, w\}$; it is alternating because $(a, a) = 1$. Put $\omega = \sum_{i=1}^g e_{2i-1} \wedge e_{2i}$. The class of a product of elements of $D_2$ is the sum of their classes, so $c(\omega)$ is the class of the image of $r$ in $D$, which is $1$. Thus $c(\omega) = 0$.

For an alternating matrix $M$ put $\omega_M = \sum_{a < b} M_{ab}\, e_a \wedge e_b$, so that $\omega = \omega_\Omega$ and $M \mapsto \omega_M$ is injective. For $A \in \mathrm{GL}(V)$, the coefficient of $e_a \wedge e_b$, for $a < b$, in $\Lambda^2 A(\omega_M) = \sum_{j < l} M_{jl}\, Ae_j \wedge Ae_l$ is $\sum_{j < l} M_{jl}(A_{aj}A_{bl} - A_{bj}A_{al}) = \sum_{j \ne l} A_{aj}M_{jl}A_{bl} = (AMA^{\mathsf T})_{ab}$. So
$$\Lambda^2 A(\omega_M) = \omega_{AMA^{\mathsf T}}, \qquad \text{and } \Lambda^2 A(\omega) \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times\omega \iff A \in \mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p).$$

*Step 1: $\ker c = \mathbb{Z}_p\omega$, and part 1.* Let $\beta : V \times V \to \Lambda^2 V$ be the bilinear map with $\beta(e_a, e_b) = e_a \wedge e_b$ for $a < b$ and $\beta(e_a, e_b) = 0$ for $a \ge b$. Then $\beta(v, w) - \beta(w, v) = v \wedge w$, as both sides are bilinear and agree on basis pairs. On $H = V \times \Lambda^2 V$ put
$$(v, s)(w, t) = (v + w,\ s + t + \beta(v, w)).$$
Bilinearity of $\beta$ makes this associative, with identity $(0, 0)$ and inverse $(v, s)^{-1} = (-v, -s + \beta(v, v))$. The elements $(0, t)$ are central, and $(v, 0)(w, 0) = (w, 0)(v, 0)(0, v \wedge w)$, so the commutator of two elements with first coordinates $v$ and $w$ is $(0, v \wedge w)$. For $m \ge 1$, $p^mV \times p^m\Lambda^2 V$ is an open normal subgroup of $p$-power index: it is closed under products because $\beta(p^mV, p^mV) \subseteq p^{2m}\Lambda^2 V$, and normal because conjugating $(v, s)$ by $(u, t)$ gives $(v, s + u \wedge v)$. These subgroups form a basis of neighbourhoods of the identity, so the compact group $H$ is pro-$p$.

The closed subgroup $Z = \{0\} \times \mathbb{Z}_p\omega$ is central, so $\bar H = H/Z$ is a pro-$p$ group. Let $h : F \to \bar H$ be the continuous homomorphism with $h(x_j) = (e_j, 0)Z$. Then $h(r) = (0, \omega)Z = 1$, so $h$ factors through $D$. Its composite with the projection $\bar H \to V$, $(v, s)Z \mapsto v$, which is a homomorphism, is the abelianization map of $D$. As $\bar H$ has class at most $2$, $h(D_3) = 1$, and $h$ maps $D_2$ into $\{0\} \times \Lambda^2 V/\mathbb{Z}_p\omega$. The induced map $\mathrm{gr}_2 \to \Lambda^2 V/\mathbb{Z}_p\omega$ sends $c(v \wedge w)$ to the class of $v \wedge w$, by the commutator formula. So $\ker c \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_p\omega$, and $c(\omega) = 0$ gives equality.

Now let $\alpha$ be an automorphism of $D$. It preserves $D_2$ and $D_3$, and the induced automorphism of $\mathrm{gr}_2$ satisfies $\alpha_2 \circ c = c \circ \Lambda^2\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}}$. So $\Lambda^2\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}}$ maps $\ker c = \mathbb{Z}_p\omega$ into itself, and so does its inverse $\Lambda^2(\alpha^{-1})^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Hence $\Lambda^2\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}}(\omega) = \mu\omega$ with $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, and $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} \in \mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ by Step 0.

*Step 2: one correction.* Let $A \in \mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, and let $y_1, \dots, y_{2g} \in D$ have classes $\bar y_j = Ae_j$ in $V$. Put $\rho(y) = \prod_{i=1}^g (y_{2i-1}, y_{2i})$.

- $\rho(y) \in D_3$. Its class in $\mathrm{gr}_2$ is $\sum_i c(Ae_{2i-1} \wedge Ae_{2i}) = c(\Lambda^2 A(\omega)) = \mu(A)\, c(\omega) = 0$.
- Suppose $\rho(y) \in D_k$ with $k \ge 3$. Then there are $z_1, \dots, z_{2g} \in D_{k-1}$ with $\rho(yz) \in D_{k+1}$, where $(yz)_j = y_jz_j$.

For the second claim, let $u, u' \in D$ and $z, z' \in D_{k-1}$. Modulo $D_{k+1}$, by item 1 of Step 0:
$$(u, u'z') = (u, z')(u, u')^{z'} = (u, z')(u, u')\big((u, u'), z'\big) \equiv (u, z')(u, u'),$$
$$(uz, u'z') = (u, u'z')^z (z, u'z') \equiv (u, z')(u, u')\,(z, u'z'),$$
$$(z, u'z') = (z, z')(z, u')^{z'} \equiv (z, u').$$
Here $((u, u'), z') \in (D_2, D_{k-1})$, conjugation by $z$ changes an element of $D_2$ by an element of $(D_2, D_{k-1})$, and $(z, z') \in (D_{k-1}, D_{k-1})$ and $((z, u'), z') \in (D_k, D_{k-1})$; all of these lie in $D_{k+1}$ because $k \ge 3$. The factors $(u, z')$ and $(z, u')$ lie in $D_k$, which is central modulo $D_{k+1}$. So
$$\rho(yz) \equiv \rho(y) \prod_{i=1}^g (y_{2i-1}, z_{2i})(z_{2i-1}, y_{2i}) \pmod{D_{k+1}},$$
and in $\mathrm{gr}_k$, writing additively and using $(z, u) = (u, z)^{-1}$, the class of $\rho(yz)$ is
$$\overline{\rho(y)} + \sum_{i=1}^g \big(\{\bar y_{2i-1}, \bar z_{2i}\} - \{\bar y_{2i}, \bar z_{2i-1}\}\big).$$
The $\bar y_j = Ae_j$ form a basis of $V$, so by item 3 of Step 0 we can write $-\overline{\rho(y)} = \sum_j \{\bar y_j, b_j\}$ with $b_j \in \mathrm{gr}_{k-1}$. Choose $z_{2i} \in D_{k-1}$ with class $b_{2i-1}$ and $z_{2i-1} \in D_{k-1}$ with class $-b_{2i}$. Then the class of $\rho(yz)$ is $0$.

*Step 3: part 2.* Let $A \in \mathrm{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, and choose $y^{(3)}_j \in D$ with class $Ae_j$. By Step 2, there are $y^{(k)}_j$ for $k \ge 3$ with $\rho(y^{(k)}) \in D_k$ and $y^{(k+1)}_j \in y^{(k)}_j D_{k-1}$. By item 2 of Step 0, each sequence $(y^{(k)}_j)_k$ converges, to some $y_j$ with $y_j \in y^{(k)}_jD_{k-1}$ for all $k$, since these cosets are closed. In particular $\bar y_j = Ae_j$. By continuity $\rho(y) = \lim_k \rho(y^{(k)})$, and $\rho(y^{(k)}) \in D_k$ tends to $1$ by item 2, so $\rho(y) = 1$.

Let $\alpha : D \to D$ be the continuous homomorphism induced by $x_j \mapsto y_j$, which exists because $\rho(y) = 1$ is the image of $r$. Then $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} = A$.

- *$\alpha$ is surjective.* Suppose not. The closed subgroup $\alpha(D)$ is the intersection of the subgroups $\alpha(D)U$ for $U$ open normal, so $\alpha(D)U \ne D$ for some such $U$. A proper subgroup of the finite $p$-group $D/U$ lies in a maximal subgroup, which is normal of index $p$. Its preimage $M$ is then a normal subgroup of $D$ of index $p$ containing $\alpha(D)$. As $D/M$ is abelian of exponent $p$, $M$ contains the kernel of $D \to V/pV$, so the image of $\alpha(D)$ in $V/pV$ is a proper subgroup. But that image contains the classes of the $Ae_j$, which span $V/pV$ because $A$ is invertible.
- *$\alpha$ is injective.* $D$ is topologically generated by $2g$ elements, so for each $m$ it has only finitely many open normal subgroups of index $m$, each being the kernel of a continuous surjection onto a group of order $m$, determined by the images of the generators. As $\alpha$ is surjective, $U \mapsto \alpha^{-1}(U)$ maps this finite set injectively into itself, hence onto itself. So every open normal subgroup contains $\ker\alpha$, and $\ker\alpha = 1$.

A continuous bijection of compact Hausdorff groups is an isomorphism, so $\alpha$ is an automorphism with $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} = A$.

*The corollary.* Take $\alpha = a_\lambda$ with $a_\lambda^{\mathrm{ab}} = \lambda I$. Automorphisms commute with the brackets of Step 0, so $a_\lambda\{v, b\} = \{\lambda v, a_\lambda b\}$. By item 3 of Step 0, $\mathrm{gr}_k$ consists of sums of such brackets, so induction on $k$ shows that $a_\lambda$ acts on $\mathrm{gr}_k$ as $\lambda^k$. $\square$
