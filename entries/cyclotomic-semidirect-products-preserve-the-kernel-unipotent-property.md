**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $G$ a pro-$p$ group, with $p$-Zassenhaus filtration $G_{(n)}$ as in [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md). Thus $G_{(1)}=G$, and for $n\ge2$, $G_{(n)}$ is the closed subgroup generated by the $p$-th powers of elements of $G_{(\lceil n/p\rceil)}$ and the commutators $[x,y]$ with $x\in G_{(i)}$, $y\in G_{(j)}$, $i+j=n$ [@minactan2015kernel, §2, p. 4]. Let $U_n(\mathbb F_p)$ be the upper unitriangular $n\times n$ matrices. $G$ has the *kernel $n$-unipotent property* if
$$G_{(n)}=\bigcap_{\rho:G\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)}\ker\rho,$$
over all continuous homomorphisms [@minactan2015kernel, Definition 1.2, p. 2]. The inclusion $\subseteq$ holds for every pro-$p$ group [@minactan2015kernel, Lemma 2.5(b), p. 6].

An *orientation* of $G$ is a continuous homomorphism $\theta:G\to1+p\mathbb Z_p$ [@quadrelli2024, §3.1, p. 11]. Every continuous homomorphism $G\to\mathbb Z_p^\times$ is one: for $p=2$, $\mathbb Z_2^\times=1+2\mathbb Z_2$, and for odd $p$, $\mathbb Z_p^\times=\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb Z_p)$ and a pro-$p$ group has no nontrivial continuous homomorphism to $\mu_{p-1}$. Let $A$ be an abelian pro-$p$ group, written multiplicatively, with $b^u$ defined for $u\in\mathbb Z_p$ by continuity. The *cyclotomic semidirect product* $A\rtimes_\theta G$ is the semidirect product in which $gbg^{-1}=b^{\theta(g)}$ for $g\in G$, $b\in A$; it carries the orientation $\theta\circ\pi$, where $\pi:A\rtimes_\theta G\to G$ is the projection. Quadrelli defines it for free abelian pro-$p$ groups $A$ [@quadrelli2024, §3.2(a), p. 12]; here $A$ may be any abelian pro-$p$ group.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $(G,\theta)$ be an oriented pro-$p$ group, $A$ an abelian pro-$p$ group, $\tilde G=A\rtimes_\theta G$ and $n\ge1$. Then $\tilde G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property if and only if $G$ does.

**Corollaries.**

1. *Rigid-type groups.* For every $p$, every set $I$ and every orientation $\theta$ of $\mathbb Z_p$, the group $(\prod_I\mathbb Z_p)\rtimes_\theta\mathbb Z_p$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property for every $n$, and so does every abelian pro-$p$ group. Indeed $\mathbb Z_p$, being free pro-$p$, has the property [@minactan2015kernel, Theorem 2.6(a), p. 7], and so does the trivial group. For odd $p$ and a $p$-rigid field $F$, which by definition contains a primitive $p$-th root of unity, $G_F(p)$ is $\prod_I\mathbb Z_p$, or $(\prod_I\mathbb Z_p)\rtimes\mathbb Z_p$ with a generator $\sigma$ of the right factor acting by $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^{1+p^k}$, where $p^k$ is the largest power of $p$ with $\mu_{p^k}\subseteq F$ [@minactan2015kernel, Theorem 4.2(2), p. 11]; see [$p$-rigid fields](p-rigid-fields.md). This is the case $\theta(\sigma)=1+p^k$, so Corollary 1 recovers part 1 of [the odd rigid theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-and-massey-vanishing-for-odd-rigid-fields.md) [@minactan2015kernel, Theorem 5.2, p. 13].
2. *Demushkin groups with any orientation.* For every [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) $D$, every orientation $\theta$ of $D$, canonical or not, and every abelian pro-$p$ group $A$, the group $A\rtimes_\theta D$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property for every $n$. Every Demushkin group has it: in rank at least $3$ by [the Demushkin theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-demushkin-groups.md), in rank $2$ by Mináč and Tân, for every $p$ including $2$ [@minactan2015kernel, Proposition 5.4 and the paragraph opening its subsection, pp. 13–14], and the only Demushkin group of rank $1$ is $\mathbb Z/2$ [@minactan2015kernel, Remark 5.5, p. 14], which has it because $(\mathbb Z/2)_{(2)}=1$ and the inclusion $\mathbb Z/2\cong U_2(\mathbb F_2)\subseteq U_n(\mathbb F_2)$ is faithful. The same holds with $D$ replaced by any free pro-$p$ group, by [@minactan2015kernel, Theorem 2.6(a), p. 7].
3. *Fields.* Let $G_0$ be one of the following oriented pro-$p$ groups: a finitely generated free pro-$p$ group with any orientation; $G_L(p)$ with its cyclotomic character, for a finite extension $L/\mathbb Q_p$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity; or, for $p=2$, $\mathbb Z/2$ with the orientation of image $\{\pm1\}$. Let $m\ge0$ and $\theta$ the orientation of $G_0$. Then $\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes_\theta G_0$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property for every $n$, and it is isomorphic, as an oriented group, to $(G_K(p),\theta_K)$ for some field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity. So the [kernel-unipotent conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md) holds, for every $n$, for every field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity whose $G_K(p)$ is isomorphic to such a group.

*Proof of Corollary 3.* The group $G_0$ has the property: free groups by [@minactan2015kernel, Theorem 2.6(a), p. 7], $G_L(p)$ by the corollary of [the Demushkin theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-demushkin-groups.md), and $\mathbb Z/2$ as in Corollary 2. So the theorem with $A=\mathbb Z_p^m$ gives the property for $\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes_\theta G_0$. For realizability, $\mathbb Z_p\rtimes_{\theta\pi}(\mathbb Z_p^{m-1}\rtimes_\theta G_0)$ is $\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes_\theta G_0$, because $\theta\pi$ is trivial on $\mathbb Z_p^{m-1}$, so the new factor commutes with it, while $G_0$ acts on both through $\theta$. So $\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes_\theta G_0$ arises from $G_0$ by $m$ cyclotomic semidirect products with $\mathbb Z_p$. Quadrelli states that every oriented group built from these building blocks by cyclotomic semidirect products with $\mathbb Z_p$ and free pro-$p$ products occurs as $(G_K(p),\theta_K)$ for a field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity, because realizability is preserved by both operations [@quadrelli2024, Definition 3.4, Conjecture 3.6 and the paragraph after it, p. 13]; see also [the elementary type conjecture](elementary-type-conjecture.md). $\square$

*New cases.* Let $m\ge1$, and let $G_0$ be $G_L(p)$ as above, which is a Demushkin group of rank $[L:\mathbb Q_p]+2\ge3$, or a free group of rank at least $2$. Then for $n\ge4$ the fields of Corollary 3 are not covered by the cases recorded in [the conjecture's entry](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md), because $G=\mathbb Z_p^m\rtimes_\theta G_0$ is neither free nor Demushkin, and the rigid case recorded there is for odd $p$ only. For odd $p$, moreover, no field with this $G_K(p)$ is $p$-rigid. Write $H^i(\cdot)=H^i(\cdot,\mathbb F_p)$.

- *$\dim H^2(G)\ge2$.* Write $G=N\rtimes G_1$ with $N\cong\mathbb Z_p$ and $G_1=\mathbb Z_p^{m-1}\rtimes_\theta G_0$, as in the proof of Corollary 3. As $N$ is free pro-$p$ of rank $1$, $H^b(N)$ is $\mathbb F_p$ for $b=0,1$ and $0$ for $b\ge2$, and $G_1$ acts trivially on $H^1(N)=\mathrm{Hom}(N,\mathbb F_p)$ because orientations are $1$ modulo $p$. So the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence of $1\to N\to G\to G_1\to1$ has $E_2^{a,b}=H^a(G_1)$ for $b=0,1$ and no other nonzero rows. Its only possible differentials go from row $1$ to row $0$, and they vanish, because the extension splits, so the edge map $H^a(G_1)\to H^a(G)$, which is inflation, is injective. Hence $H^2(G)$ has a subquotient $E_\infty^{1,1}=H^1(G_1)$. As $G_0$ is a quotient of $G_1$, $\dim H^1(G_1)\ge\dim H^1(G_0)\ge2$. So $H^2(G)\ne0$ and $G$ is not free, and $\dim H^2(G)\ne1$, so $G$ is not Demushkin. The same computation appears in the proof of the proposition in [the elementary type conjecture](elementary-type-conjecture.md).
- *Not $p$-rigid, for odd $p$.* The projection $G\to G_0$ has a section, so inflation $H^*(G_0)\to H^*(G)$ is an injective ring homomorphism. Choose linearly independent $a,a'\in H^1(G_0)$ with $a\cup a'=0$: for free $G_0$, $H^2(G_0)=0$; for Demushkin $G_0$ of rank $d\ge3$, $\dim H^2(G_0)=1$, so for $a\ne0$ the kernel of $a'\mapsto a\cup a'$ has dimension at least $d-1\ge2$ and contains some $a'$ independent of $a$. Their inflations are independent in $H^1(G)$ with zero cup product, so $H^*(G)$ is not the exterior algebra on $H^1(G)$. For odd $p$, a field $F$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity is $p$-rigid exactly when $H^*(G_F(p))$ is that exterior algebra ([$p$-rigid fields](p-rigid-fields.md)), so for odd $p$ no field $F$ with $G_F(p)\cong G$ is $p$-rigid. Nothing is claimed here about $2$-rigidity.

**Proof of the theorem.**

*Step 0: three facts.* Let $H$ be a pro-$p$ group. Each fact is proved by induction from the definition, since for $n\ge2$ the indices $\lceil n/p\rceil$, $i$ and $j$ used to generate $H_{(n)}$ are smaller than $n$.

- (i) If $H'\subseteq H$ is a closed subgroup, then $H'_{(n)}\subseteq H_{(n)}$: the generators of $H'_{(n)}$ are among those of $H_{(n)}$.
- (ii) If $f:H\to H'$ is a continuous homomorphism, then $f(H_{(n)})\subseteq H'_{(n)}$: $f$ maps the generators of $H_{(n)}$ into $H'_{(n)}$, and by continuity it maps the closure of the subgroup they generate into the closure of the subgroup generated by their images.
- (iii) If $n\le p^h$, then $y^{p^h}\in H_{(n)}$ for every $y\in H$. For $h=0$, $n=1$ and this is clear. For $h\ge1$, $\lceil n/p\rceil\le p^{h-1}$, so $y^{p^{h-1}}\in H_{(\lceil n/p\rceil)}$ by induction on $h$, and its $p$-th power $y^{p^h}$ lies in $H_{(n)}$.

*Step 1: if $\tilde G$ has the property, so does $G$.* Identify $G$ with the closed subgroup $\{1\}\times G$ of $\tilde G$, so that $\pi$ restricts to the identity on it. Then $G\cap\tilde G_{(n)}=G_{(n)}$: the inclusion $\supseteq$ is (i), and if $g\in G\cap\tilde G_{(n)}$, then $g=\pi(g)\in G_{(n)}$ by (ii). So if $g\in G\setminus G_{(n)}$, then $g\notin\tilde G_{(n)}$, some continuous $\rho:\tilde G\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)$ has $\rho(g)\ne1$, and its restriction to $G$ detects $g$. The argument uses only that $G$ is a retract of $\tilde G$.

*Step 2: a representation.* For $n=1$ both groups have the property trivially, as $H_{(1)}=H$ and $U_1(\mathbb F_p)=1$. Let $n\ge2$ and let $h\ge1$ be the integer with $p^{h-1}<n\le p^h$. Let $R=\mathbb F_p[X]/(X^n)$, with basis $e_k=X^{n-k}$ for $k=1,\ldots,n$.

- (a) In $R$, $(1+X)^{p^h}=1+X^{p^h}=1$, as $p^h\ge n$. So for $c\in\mathbb Z/p^h$ multiplication by $(1+X)^c$ is a well-defined operator $T_c$, and $c\mapsto T_c$ is a homomorphism. It is injective: if $c\ne0$, write $c=p^ec'$ with $0\le e\le h-1$ and $c'$ an integer prime to $p$. Then $(1+X)^c=(1+X^{p^e})^{c'}=1+c'X^{p^e}+X^{2p^e}r$ for some $r\in R$, and $X^{p^e}\ne0$ in $R$ since $p^e\le p^{h-1}<n$, so $T_c\ne1$.
- (b) For $u\in1+p\mathbb Z_p$ choose an integer $u'\equiv u\pmod{p^h}$, and put $Y_u=(1+X)^{u'}-1$, which by (a) depends only on $u$. As $Y_u\in XR$, we have $Y_u^n=0$, so there is a unique $\mathbb F_p$-algebra endomorphism $A_u$ of $R$ with $A_u(X)=Y_u$. Since $A_u$ is an algebra map, $A_u(A_v(X))=(1+Y_u)^{v'}-1=(1+X)^{u'v'}-1=A_{uv}(X)$, so $A_uA_v=A_{uv}$, and $A_1$ is the identity. So each $A_u$ is an automorphism, and $u\mapsto A_u$ is a homomorphism $1+p\mathbb Z_p\to\mathrm{GL}(R)$ that depends only on $u$ modulo $p^h$.
- (c) Both families are upper unitriangular in the basis $(e_k)$. First, $T_1e_k=X^{n-k}+X^{n-k+1}=e_k+e_{k-1}$, with $e_0=0$. Second, $u'\equiv1\pmod p$, so $Y_u=u'X+\binom{u'}{2}X^2+\cdots=X(1+Xs)$ for some $s\in R$, and $A_u(X^j)=X^j(1+Xs)^j\in X^j+X^{j+1}R$. With $j=n-k$, this says $A_u(e_k)\in e_k+\mathrm{span}(e_1,\ldots,e_{k-1})$.
- (d) $A_uT_cA_u^{-1}=T_{uc}$. Indeed, for $f\in R$, $A_u\bigl((1+X)^cA_u^{-1}(f)\bigr)=A_u\bigl((1+X)^c\bigr)f=(1+Y_u)^cf=(1+X)^{uc}f$.

Let $G$ act on $\mathbb Z/p^h$ by $g\cdot c=\theta(g)c$, and form $(\mathbb Z/p^h)\rtimes_\theta G$. By (b)–(d),
$$\rho_n(c,g)=T_c\,A_{\theta(g)}$$
is a homomorphism $(\mathbb Z/p^h)\rtimes_\theta G\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)$: $\rho_n(c,g)\rho_n(c',g')=T_c\bigl(A_{\theta(g)}T_{c'}A_{\theta(g)}^{-1}\bigr)A_{\theta(g)}A_{\theta(g')}=T_{c+\theta(g)c'}A_{\theta(gg')}$. It is continuous, since $g\mapsto A_{\theta(g)}$ factors through $\theta$ followed by reduction modulo $p^h$. By (a) it is injective on $\mathbb Z/p^h$.

*Step 3: if $G$ has the property, so does $\tilde G$.* Let $n\ge2$ and $x\in\tilde G\setminus\tilde G_{(n)}$. Write $x=ag$ with $a\in A$ and $g\in G$.

- *Case $g\notin G_{(n)}$.* Some continuous $\rho:G\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)$ has $\rho(g)\ne1$, and $\rho\circ\pi$ is continuous on $\tilde G$ with $\rho(\pi(x))=\rho(g)\ne1$.
- *Case $g\in G_{(n)}$.* By (i), $g\in\tilde G_{(n)}$, so $a=xg^{-1}\notin\tilde G_{(n)}$. By (iii), $b^{p^h}\in\tilde G_{(n)}$ for every $b\in A$, so $a\notin A^{p^h}=\{b^{p^h}:b\in A\}$. This is a closed subgroup, the image of $A$ under the continuous endomorphism $b\mapsto b^{p^h}$. So the image of $a$ in the profinite group $A/A^{p^h}$ is nontrivial and avoids some open subgroup. The quotient by that subgroup is a finite abelian group of exponent dividing $p^h$, so it is a sum of cyclic groups of order dividing $p^h$, and projecting to a suitable factor gives a homomorphism to $\mathbb Z/p^h$ that is nonzero on the image of $a$. This gives a continuous homomorphism $\lambda:A\to\mathbb Z/p^h$ with $\lambda(a)\ne0$. By continuity $\lambda(b^u)=u\lambda(b)$ for $u\in\mathbb Z_p$, so
$$\psi:\tilde G\to(\mathbb Z/p^h)\rtimes_\theta G,\qquad (b,k)\mapsto(\lambda(b),k),$$
is a continuous homomorphism: $\psi\bigl((b,k)(b',k')\bigr)=\psi\bigl(bb'^{\theta(k)},kk'\bigr)=\bigl(\lambda(b)+\theta(k)\lambda(b'),kk'\bigr)=\psi(b,k)\psi(b',k')$. Put $\rho=\rho_n\circ\psi:\tilde G\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)$. It kills $\tilde G_{(n)}$ [@minactan2015kernel, Lemma 2.5(b), p. 6], so $\rho(g)=1$, and $\rho(x)=\rho(a)=T_{\lambda(a)}\ne1$ by Step 2.

In both cases $x\notin\bigcap_\rho\ker\rho$. With the inclusion $\subseteq$ from the setting, $\tilde G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The representation.* Since $(1+X)^{p^h}-1=X^{p^h}$ in characteristic $p$, $\mathbb F_p[X]/(X^{p^h})$ is the group algebra of the cyclic group $C=\mathbb Z/p^h$, with its generator mapped to $1+X$, and $R$ is its quotient by the $n$-th power of the augmentation ideal. Then $T_c$ is translation by $c$, and $A_u$ is induced by the automorphism $c\mapsto uc$ of $C$. Mináč and Tân build the matrices $A_u$ for $u=1+p^k$ and, at $p=2$, for $u=-(1+2^k)$ and $u=-1$, from the fact that algebra automorphisms of $R$ are induced by conjugation by upper triangular matrices [@minactan2015kernel, Lemmas 3.3–3.5, pp. 9–10]. They use them to detect elements of the finite quotients of $(\prod_I\mathbb Z_p)\rtimes\mathbb Z_p$ [@minactan2015kernel, Proposition 5.1, pp. 12–13]. The proof above packages all units $u$ into one homomorphism $u\mapsto A_u$. So it needs no computation of the orders of the $A_u$, and $G$ can be any group with the property, in place of $\mathbb Z_p$.
- *Retracts and direct products.* Step 1 shows that the property passes to retracts, for instance to free factors of a free pro-$p$ product. By (i) and (ii), $(G_1\times G_2)_{(n)}=(G_1)_{(n)}\times(G_2)_{(n)}$, so a direct product of two groups with the property has it, by projecting to a factor. Hamza, Maire, Mináč and Tân state the direct product case as a remark [@hamzamaireminactan2025, Remark 4.11, p. 25]. They prove the property for a particular semidirect product of a free pro-$2$ group by a free product of three copies of $\mathbb Z/2$, with the same case split as Step 3 [@hamzamaireminactan2025, Lemma 4.8 and Proposition 4.10, pp. 24–25].
- *What is not covered.* The theorem concerns only one of the two operations in [the elementary type conjecture](elementary-type-conjecture.md). It says nothing about free pro-$p$ products, which are needed to build the general group of elementary type, nor about actions on $A$ other than by scalars.

**Sources and status.** The theorem and Corollaries 2 and 3 are proved here from the cited inputs. I found no source stating them, in a search through November 2026 that was not exhaustive. The 2025 preprint of Mináč, Tân and Tra lists, as the known cases, free pro-$p$ groups, odd rigid fields and Demushkin groups of rank $2$, and for $n=4$ finitely generated Demushkin groups [@minactantra2025, §1, p. 2].
