**Setting.** Use the Setting and notation of [surface generators modulo the Frattini subgroup](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md), called the surface-generator entry below, which is that of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ has degree $d$, $G = G_K$ is identified with $F/(\varrho)$, $P_K$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma = G/P_K = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$ is identified with the group $\mathcal{G}$ generated by $\sigma$ and $\tau$, $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\Gamma]]$, $\omega : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$ is the ring homomorphism given by the action on $\mu_p$, and $\lambda \mapsto \lambda^*$ is the continuous anti-automorphism of $\Lambda$ with $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$ for $\rho \in \Gamma$. By part 1 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md),
$$\bar V = P_K/P_K^{(2)} = \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \Lambda\bar x_d,$$
where each $\Lambda\bar x_i$ with $i \ge 1$ is free of rank one and $\lambda\bar x_0 = \omega(\lambda)\bar x_0$ for $\lambda \in \Lambda$. For odd $d$, fix the presentation in the form used in [automorphisms mixing complementary and surface generators](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), called the mixing entry below, with Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's explicit $y_1$. By part 1 of the mixing entry and step (c) of its proof, this $y_1$ lies in $P$ and in the closed subgroup generated by $x_1$, $\sigma$ and $\tau$, so the presentation is an instance of the Setting of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). The surface-generator entry and the mixing entry both work in that Setting, so both apply to it.

Let $d \ge 3$. The complementary generators are $x_0, x_1$ for odd $d$ and $x_0, x_1, x_2$ for even $d$, and the first surface pair $(a_1, b_1)$ is $(x_2, x_3)$ for odd $d$ and $(x_3, x_4)$ for even $d$ (Setting of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md)). Let $\bar V_c$ be the sum of the $\Lambda\bar x_j$ over the complementary $x_j$ with $j \ge 1$. Every automorphism $\alpha$ of $G$ preserves $P_K$, the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), hence $P_K^{(2)}$. So it induces an automorphism $\bar\alpha$ of $\bar V$, and $\overline{\alpha\beta} = \bar\alpha\bar\beta$.

*A non-zero-divisor.* Let $\pi_p \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ have component $1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $0$ in every other $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and put $\gamma = \sigma^{\pi_p}$. As in Step 0 of the proof of the Theorem of the surface-generator entry, $\gamma$ generates a closed subgroup of $\Gamma$ isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and $\omega(\gamma) = \omega(\sigma)^{\pi_p} = 1$, as the order of $\omega(\sigma)$ divides $p - 1$. Put
$$\nu_0 = \gamma^{-1} - 1.$$
Then $\omega(\nu_0) = 0$, and $\nu_0^* = \omega(\gamma^{-1})\gamma - 1 = \gamma - 1$. Lemma 2 of the surface-generator entry, applied to $\gamma^{-1}$ and to $\gamma$, which generate the same closed subgroup, gives for $z \in \Lambda$:
$$\text{if } z\nu_0 = 0 \text{ or } \nu_0^*z = 0, \text{ then } z = 0. \tag{Z}$$

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$, and let $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ have $p$-power order, with only finitely many conjugates of its class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. Then $\theta = 1$.

More precisely, the proof shows that the complementary block $B$ of $\theta$, in the sense of the surface-generator entry, is $1$ for odd $d$ and satisfies $(B - 1)^2 = 0$ for even $d$. Part 1 of the Corollary there then gives $\theta = 1$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here. Let $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$.

1. No element of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ of $p$-power order other than $1$ has finitely many conjugates. Hence every finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has order prime to $p$.
2. Part 1 applies to every $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity $\zeta_p$, since then $d \ge p - 1 \ge 4$. So for $p \ge 5$ and $\zeta_p \in K$, $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has no finite normal subgroup of order divisible by $p$.
3. Let $N$ be a rival copy of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ of type (a) in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 2, and put $M = N \cap G_K$. Then $(G_K : M)$ is prime to $p$.

**Proof of the Theorem.** Let $p^a$ be the order of $\theta$. By the Theorem of the surface-generator entry, $\bar\theta$ is $\Lambda$-linear, $\bar\theta(\bar a_1) - \bar a_1$ and $\bar\theta(\bar b_1) - \bar b_1$ lie in $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$, and $\bar\theta(\bar V_c) \subseteq \bar V_c \oplus \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$. The complementary block $B$ is the automorphism of $\bar V_c$ that $\bar\theta$ induces modulo $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$.

*Step 1: a conjugate of $\theta$.* Let $\varphi$ be an automorphism of $G$ with $\varphi(\rho) \in \rho P_K^{(2)}$ for every $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ and $\varphi(x_0) \in x_0P_K^{(2)}$. Then $\bar\varphi$ is $\Lambda$-linear: for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ write $\varphi(\rho) = \rho c$ with $c \in P_K^{(2)}$; conjugation by elements of $P_K$ is trivial on the abelian group $\bar V$, so $\bar\varphi(\rho v) = \rho\,\bar\varphi(v)$, and $\bar\varphi$ is continuous and additive. (This is the first step of the proof of part 4 of the mixing entry.) Also $\bar\varphi$ fixes $\bar x_0$, so it maps $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ onto itself. The automorphism $\theta' = \varphi^{-1}\theta\varphi$ has order $p^a$. Its class in $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ is conjugate to that of $\theta$, so it has the same finitely many conjugates. So the Theorem of the surface-generator entry applies to $\theta'$ too, and $\overline{\theta'} = \bar\varphi^{-1}\bar\theta\bar\varphi$. Applying $\bar\varphi$ to its parts 3 and 2 for $\theta'$ gives:

- (i) $\bar\theta\bar\varphi(v) \in \bar\varphi(\bar V_c) + \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ for $v \in \bar V_c$;
- (ii) $\bar\theta\bar\varphi(v) - \bar\varphi(v) \in \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ for $v \in \{\bar a_1, \bar b_1\}$.

Below, elements of $\bar V$ are compared through their coordinates in the free summands $\Lambda\bar x_i$, $i \ge 1$, which are unique.

*Step 2: odd $d$.* Here $\bar V_c = \Lambda\bar x_1$ and $a_1 = x_2$. By the Theorem of the surface-generator entry, $\bar\theta(\bar x_1) = m\bar x_0 + \xi\bar x_1$ with $m \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and $\xi \in \Lambda$, and $B(z\bar x_1) = z\xi\bar x_1$. Also $\bar\theta(\bar x_2) = \bar x_2 + c\bar x_0$ with $c \in \mathbb{F}_p$. Let $\varphi$ be the automorphism of part 3 of the mixing entry with $\nu = \nu_0$, which is allowed as $\omega(\nu_0) = 0$. It satisfies the conditions of Step 1, and $\bar\varphi(\bar x_1) = \bar x_1 + \nu_0\bar x_2$. By $\Lambda$-linearity of $\bar\theta$ and $\omega(\nu_0) = 0$,
$$\bar\theta\bar\varphi(\bar x_1) = m\bar x_0 + \xi\bar x_1 + \nu_0\bar x_2 + \omega(\nu_0)c\,\bar x_0 = m\bar x_0 + \xi\bar x_1 + \nu_0\bar x_2.$$
By (i) this equals $z\bar\varphi(\bar x_1) + c'\bar x_0 = z\bar x_1 + z\nu_0\bar x_2 + c'\bar x_0$ for some $z \in \Lambda$ and $c' \in \mathbb{F}_p$. The coordinates at $\bar x_1$ give $z = \xi$, and those at $\bar x_2$ give $\nu_0 = \xi\nu_0$. So $(\xi - 1)\nu_0 = 0$, and $\xi = 1$ by (Z). Thus $B = 1$.

*Step 3: even $d$.* Here $\bar V_c = \Lambda\bar x_1 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_2$ and $(a_1, b_1) = (x_3, x_4)$. By the Theorem of the surface-generator entry, for $j = 1, 2$,
$$\bar\theta(\bar x_j) = m_j\bar x_0 + \beta_{j1}\bar x_1 + \beta_{j2}\bar x_2 \qquad (m_j \in \mathbb{F}_p,\ \beta_{ji} \in \Lambda),$$
so that $B(z_1\bar x_1 + z_2\bar x_2) = \sum_j z_j(\beta_{j1}\bar x_1 + \beta_{j2}\bar x_2)$, and $\bar\theta(\bar x_3) = \bar x_3 + c_3\bar x_0$, $\bar\theta(\bar x_4) = \bar x_4 + c_4\bar x_0$ with $c_3, c_4 \in \mathbb{F}_p$. Let $\varphi$ be the automorphism of part 2 of the mixing entry with $\lambda = \nu_0$, which is allowed as $\omega(\nu_0) = 0$. It satisfies the conditions of Step 1, $\bar\varphi$ fixes $\bar x_2$ and $\bar x_3$, and
$$\bar\varphi(\bar x_1) = \bar x_1 + \nu_0\bar x_3,\qquad \bar\varphi(\bar x_4) = \bar x_4 - \nu_0^*\bar x_2.$$
So the elements of $\bar\varphi(\bar V_c) + \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ are the $z_1\bar x_1 + z_2\bar x_2 + z_1\nu_0\bar x_3 + c'\bar x_0$ with $z_1, z_2 \in \Lambda$ and $c' \in \mathbb{F}_p$: their coordinate at $\bar x_3$ is their coordinate at $\bar x_1$ times $\nu_0$.

- *$v = \bar x_1$ in (i).* As $\omega(\nu_0) = 0$, $\bar\theta\bar\varphi(\bar x_1) = \bar\theta(\bar x_1) + \nu_0\bar\theta(\bar x_3) = m_1\bar x_0 + \beta_{11}\bar x_1 + \beta_{12}\bar x_2 + \nu_0\bar x_3$. So $\nu_0 = \beta_{11}\nu_0$, that is $(\beta_{11} - 1)\nu_0 = 0$, and $\beta_{11} = 1$ by (Z).
- *$v = \bar x_2$ in (i).* $\bar\theta\bar\varphi(\bar x_2) = \bar\theta(\bar x_2) = m_2\bar x_0 + \beta_{21}\bar x_1 + \beta_{22}\bar x_2$ has coordinate $0$ at $\bar x_3$. So $0 = \beta_{21}\nu_0$, and $\beta_{21} = 0$ by (Z).
- *$v = \bar b_1 = \bar x_4$ in (ii).* Using $\bar\theta(\bar x_4) = \bar x_4 + c_4\bar x_0$ and $\Lambda$-linearity,
$$\bar\theta\bar\varphi(\bar x_4) - \bar\varphi(\bar x_4) = c_4\bar x_0 - \nu_0^*\big(\bar\theta(\bar x_2) - \bar x_2\big) = \big(c_4 - \omega(\nu_0^*)m_2\big)\bar x_0 - \nu_0^*\beta_{21}\bar x_1 - \nu_0^*(\beta_{22} - 1)\bar x_2.$$
This lies in $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$, so $\nu_0^*(\beta_{22} - 1) = 0$, and $\beta_{22} = 1$ by (Z).

So $B(\bar x_1) = \bar x_1 + \beta_{12}\bar x_2$ and $B(\bar x_2) = \bar x_2$. Then $(B - 1)(\bar x_2) = 0$ and $(B - 1)(\bar x_1) = \beta_{12}\bar x_2$, so by $\Lambda$-linearity $(B - 1)^2(z_1\bar x_1 + z_2\bar x_2) = z_1\beta_{12}(B - 1)(\bar x_2) = 0$. Thus $(B - 1)^2 = 0$.

*Step 4: conclusion.* In both cases $(B - 1)^2 = 0$. As $p \ge 5$, $p - 3 \ge 2$, so $(B - 1)^{p-3} = 0$, and $\theta = 1$ by part 1 of the Corollary of the surface-generator entry. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** (1) Let $f \in \mathrm{Out}(G)$ have order $p^k$ with $k \ge 1$, and suppose it has only finitely many conjugates. Then $f_1 = f^{p^{k-1}}$ has order $p$, and its conjugates are the $p^{k-1}$-th powers of those of $f$, so there are finitely many of them. By part 3 (a) of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), which applies as $p$ is odd and $d \ge 3$, $f_1$ is the class of an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ of order $p$. The class of $\theta$ has finitely many conjugates, so $\theta = 1$ by the Theorem, and $f_1 = 1$, a contradiction. If $F$ is a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ of order divisible by $p$, it contains an element of order $p$ by Cauchy's theorem, all of whose conjugates lie in $F$. This has just been excluded.

(2) If $\zeta_p \in K$, then $d = [K:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge [\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p):\mathbb{Q}_p] = p - 1$, as the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_p$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (Eisenstein's criterion applied to $\Phi_p(x + 1)$). For $p \ge 5$ this gives $d \ge 4$.

(3) By part 2 (a) of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), $NG_K/G_K$ is a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$, isomorphic to $N/M$, and $(N : M) = (G_K : M)$. By part 1 its order is prime to $p$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Why the mixing automorphisms are needed.* Kondo's automorphisms and the automorphisms $\theta_\lambda$ of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) fix the complementary generators modulo $P_K^{(2)}$, so the surface-generator entry obtains no information on $B$ from them. An automorphism of the mixing entry moves $x_1$ into the first surface pair, and conjugating $\theta$ by it transports the control on the surface pair to the complementary block. A single such automorphism suffices. Its action on $\bar V$ has order $p$, since $(\bar\varphi - 1)^3 = 0$ and $\bar V$ has exponent $p$, so finite outer conjugacy applied to it directly might give nothing. Here the finiteness is used only inside the surface-generator entry, applied to the conjugate $\theta'$.
- *What is not used.* Of part 1 of the mixing entry only $y_1 \in P$ is used, not $\varepsilon(\delta_0) \ne 0$; nor is the image of $\bar x_3$ under the odd-degree automorphism. The entry $\beta_{12}$ of $B$ for even $d$ is not determined, and need not be, as $p \ge 5$.
- *Earlier results.* For $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$, the Theorem contains the triviality of $\theta$ on quotients of $G_K$ proved in [automorphisms with finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$, and in part 1 of the Corollary of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md). The first of these also treats $p = 3$ with $d$ odd, which is not covered here.
- *What remains of Tsujimura's question.* For $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$, part 1 of the Corollary leaves finite normal subgroups of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ of order prime to $p$, and part 3 leaves case (a) of the rival-copy dichotomy with index prime to $p$. Case (b) is untouched.
- *Not covered.* For $p = 3$, the surface-generator entry does not apply, and the exponent $p - 3$ in its criterion would be $0$. For $d \le 2$ the automorphisms $\theta_\lambda$ are not available; such $K$ do not contain $\zeta_p$ when $p \ge 5$. The case $p = 2$ is not treated. Automorphisms of finite order prime to $p$ are not considered.
