**Definition.** Let $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite and $V$ a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_p$-vector space. A continuous representation $\rho : G_k \to \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(V)$ is *Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate* if $\rho \circ \alpha$ is Hodge–Tate for every continuous automorphism $\alpha$ of $G_k$ [@hoshi2024, Definition 1.3]. An inner automorphism changes $\rho$ only up to isomorphism, so only the class of $\alpha$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ matters [@kondo2026, proof of Theorem 3.5].

**Why ask.** An automorphism $\alpha$ of $G_k$ comes from a field automorphism of $\bar k$ exactly when $\rho \circ \alpha$ is Hodge–Tate for every Hodge–Tate $\rho$ [@hoshi2021, Theorem 2.3]. Testing potentially crystalline $\rho$ is enough [@hoshi2024, Theorem 1.4]. For odd $p$, $G_k$ always has automorphisms that do not come from field automorphisms, so some potentially crystalline representation is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate [@hoshi2024, Corollary 1.5]. In this sense Hodge–Tate-ness is not determined by the topological group $G_k$ [@hoshi2021, Remark 3.15.2]. The results below say which representations show this.

**Statement.**

1. *Low dimension.* For every $p$ and $k$, every Hodge–Tate representation of dimension $1$ is Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate, and so is every reducible Hodge–Tate representation of dimension $2$ [@hoshi2024, Theorems 2.7 and 2.10]. In dimension $1$, being Hodge–Tate means agreeing on an open subgroup of inertia with a power of the cyclotomic character [@hoshi2024, Lemma 2.5], and inertia and the cyclotomic character are recovered from $G_k$.
2. *Lubin–Tate representations.* Suppose that $k$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $d > 1$, and that $p$ is odd or $\sqrt{-1} \in k$. For a uniformizer $\pi$ of $k$, let $\rho_{k,\pi} : G_k \to \mathcal{O}_k^\times \subset \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(k)$ be the composite of $G_k \to G_k^{\mathrm{ab}}$, the inverse of the reciprocity isomorphism $(k^\times)^\wedge \to G_k^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and the projection $(k^\times)^\wedge \to \mathcal{O}_k^\times$ determined by $\pi$ [@kondo2026, Definitions 2.1 and 2.3]. Up to duality, this is the representation on the Tate module of a Lubin–Tate formal group over $\mathcal{O}_k$, so it is crystalline, hence Hodge–Tate [@kondo2026, Remark 2.4]. It is abelian of dimension $d$, and irreducible [@kondo2025, proof of Theorem 1.9]: its image is all of $\mathcal{O}_k^\times$, which spans $k$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, so a stable subspace is a $k$-subspace. Give the profinite group $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ its normalized Haar measure. Then the classes $\alpha$ for which $\rho_{k,\pi} \circ \alpha$ is Hodge–Tate lie in a closed subgroup of measure zero [@kondo2026, Theorem 3.5]. In particular, $\rho_{k,\pi}$ is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate [@kondo2026, Corollary 3.6].
3. *Contrast with $\mathbb{Q}_p$.* For $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$, the Lubin–Tate character of the uniformizer $p$ is the cyclotomic character, and the Lubin–Tate characters of all uniformizers of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ are Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate [@kondo2026, Remark 3.8], as part 1 also shows. Kondo reads parts 2 and 3 together as a sharp contrast between the cyclotomic character and the other Lubin–Tate characters [@kondo2026, Abstract].

**History.** The measure-zero statement in part 2 first appears in Kondo's arXiv preprint, in every case [@kondo2026, Theorem C]. The earlier results that Kondo lists prove only the last sentence of part 2, that $\rho_{k,\pi}$ is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate, in special cases [@kondo2026, Theorem B]. Each of them constructs one explicit automorphism $\varphi$ of $G_k$, on generators, for which $\rho_{k,\pi} \circ \varphi$ is not Hodge–Tate [@kondo2026, Remark (3) after Theorem B].

- Hoshi proved that $\rho_{k,\pi}$ is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate for odd $p$ and $k$ abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of even degree [@hoshi2024, proof of Theorem 3.3]. He states this as the existence of an irreducible abelian crystalline representation of dimension $d$ that is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate [@hoshi2024, Theorem 3.3]. For quadratic $k$ this gives one of dimension $2$ [@hoshi2024, Corollary 3.4].
- Kondo proved the same for $k$ Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of even degree, also stated as an existence result [@kondo2025, Theorem 1.9 and its proof].
- According to Kondo, Nishio essentially established it for $p = 2$ with $k$ abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ and $\sqrt{-1} \in k$ [@kondo2026, Theorem B and Remark (2) after it].

The explicit method did not reach odd degree [@kondo2025, Remark 1.10]. Kondo's later argument covers odd and even degree alike but gives no explicit automorphism [@kondo2026, Remarks (1) and (2) after Theorem D].

**Method of part 2.** Each $\alpha \in \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ induces a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linear automorphism $\alpha_+$ of the additive group $k_+$, through the group-theoretic reconstruction of $k_+$ from $G_k$ [@kondo2026, §0, Groups of MLF-type].

- *A linear criterion.* For $k$ Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $\rho_{k,\pi} \circ \alpha$ is Hodge–Tate exactly when $\alpha_+ = \sum_\sigma i_\sigma \sigma$ with integers $i_\sigma$, the sum running over $\mathrm{Gal}(k/\mathbb{Q}_p)$ [@kondo2026, Theorem 2.6]. The proof uses Serre's classification of abelian Hodge–Tate representations [@kondo2026, Theorem 2.2]. Such $\alpha_+$ lie in $\mathbb{Q}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(k/\mathbb{Q}_p)]^\times$, a $p$-adic Lie group of dimension at most $d$ [@kondo2026, Lemma 1.8].
- *A large image.* The Jannsen–Wingberg relation contains a product of commutators like the boundary relation of a surface group. Automorphisms of the fundamental group of a once-punctured closed surface of genus $g = \lfloor (d-1)/2 \rfloor$ that fix the boundary loop therefore induce automorphisms of $G_k$ fixing the tame generators [@kondo2025, discussion after Lemma 2.15]. Since the mapping class group maps onto $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$, the image of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ in $\mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ contains $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ when $d \ge 3$ [@kondo2026, Lemma 3.3]. This group has dimension $2g^2 + g$. Since $d \le 2g + 2$, this exceeds $d$ once $g \ge 2$, that is, once $d \ge 5$. So the classes of the criterion have infinite index in the image, and measure zero. Degrees $3$ and $4$ use that $\mathrm{Sp}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ is not virtually abelian, and degree $2$ uses one explicit automorphism [@kondo2026, proof of Theorem 3.5].

Automorphisms of the same kinds also show that the [field automorphisms form a non-normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$](field-theoretic-subgroup-of-outer-automorphisms.md).

**The case $p = 2$ with $\sqrt{-1} \notin k$.** Part 2 assumes $\sqrt{-1} \in k$ when $p = 2$, so that Diekert's presentation applies [@kondo2026, Theorem 1.2 and Remark 3.1]. The knowledgebase's [unramified dyadic theorem](unramified-dyadic-outer-automorphisms.md), part 3, proves the same measure-zero conclusion for every finite Galois $k/\mathbb{Q}_2$ with $\sqrt{-1} \notin k$ and $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ unramified. Its proof combines Diekert's presentation in that range with a trace-hyperplane argument and Kondo's Hodge–Tate criterion; it is a separate deduction, beyond the hypotheses of Kondo's Theorem 3.5. When $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is ramified and $k \ne \mathbb{Q}_2$, no presentation of this kind is known. Roe and Turturean's presentation, which has not been refereed, covers only $\mathbb{Q}_2$, and part 2 excludes $\mathbb{Q}_2$ anyway. The same gap blocks the [2-adic Galois classification](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md).
