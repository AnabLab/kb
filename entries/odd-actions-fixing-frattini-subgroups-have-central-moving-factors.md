**Theorem (proved here).** Let a finite odd-order group $T$ act on a finite $2$-group $P$, fixing $F=\Phi(P)$ pointwise. Define
$$
C=P^T,\qquad R=[P,T]=\langle x^{-1}t(x):x\in P,\ t\in T\rangle,
\qquad V=P/F.
$$
Let $V_0=V^T$ and $V_1=\ker(\sum_{t\in T}t:V\to V)$. Then
$$
P=CR,\qquad [C,R]=1,\qquad
A=C\cap R=R\cap F=R'=\Phi(R),\qquad R/A\cong V_1.
\tag{1}
$$
Here $A$ is central in $P$ and elementary abelian. The fixed factor $C$ may have arbitrary nilpotency class. In particular
$$
P\cong(C\times R)/\{(a,a^{-1}):a\in A\},\qquad
R=[R,T],\qquad (R/\Phi(R))^T=0.
\tag{2}
$$
Every homomorphism $r:P\rtimes T\to D$ to a finite quotient of a dyadic tame Galois group kills $R$, hence factors through $(C/A)\times T$.

**Proof.** *1. Locate the fixed and moving subgroups.* Averaging on the $\mathbb F_2[T]$-module $V$ gives $V=V_0\oplus V_1$, with $V_1$ spanned by the differences $t(v)-v$. If $xF\in V_0$, write $t(x)=xf_t$ with $f_t\in F$. Since $T$ fixes $F$ pointwise, $f_{ts}=f_tf_s$. This homomorphism from an odd-order group to a $2$-group is trivial. Thus $C$ is the full inverse image of $V_0$ and contains $F$.

The subgroup $R$ is normal in $P$ and $T$-stable. For normality, writing $g_t(x)=x^{-1}t(x)$ gives
$$
y^{-1}g_t(x)y=g_t(xy)g_t(y)^{-1}.
$$
Its image in $V$ is $V_1$. Consequently $P=CR$. Writing $x=cr$ with $c\in C$ and $r\in R$ shows $x^{-1}t(x)=r^{-1}t(r)$, so $R=[R,T]$.

Each generator $x^{-1}t(x)$ centralizes $F$: for $f\in F$, pointwise fixation of $F$ gives
$$
t(x)ft(x)^{-1}=t(xfx^{-1})=xfx^{-1}.
$$
Hence $[R,F]=1$. Set $A=R\cap F$. It is central in $R$, fixed by $T$, and $R/A\cong V_1$ is elementary abelian. In particular $R'\subseteq A$ and $R^2\subseteq A$.

*2. The two factors commute.* For $c\in C$, conjugation by $c$ preserves $R$ and acts trivially on $R/A$, because $P/F$ is abelian. Therefore
$$
\lambda_c:R\longrightarrow A,\qquad
\lambda_c(r)=crc^{-1}r^{-1}
$$
is a homomorphism: its values are central in $R$. It is $T$-invariant, since $c$ and $A$ are fixed by $T$. It therefore kills every $r^{-1}t(r)$, and $R=[R,T]$ forces $\lambda_c=1$. This proves $[C,R]=1$. Since $C/F=V_0$ and $RF/F=V_1$, their intersection is $C\cap R=A$, which is now central in $P$.

*3. Identify the intersection.* On the finite abelian $2$-group $M=R/R'$, use the averaging idempotent
$$
e=|T|^{-1}\sum_{t\in T}t.
$$
The inverse of multiplication by $|T|$ exists on $M$. The exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow A/R'\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow V_1\longrightarrow0
$$
has fixed kernel and quotient with no fixed vectors. Thus $eM=A/R'$: averaging maps into this kernel and is the identity on it. But $R=[R,T]$ says that $M$ is generated by the differences $t(m)-m$, all killed by $e$. Hence $A/R'=0$.

We have proved $A=R'$, and $R/A$ elementary abelian gives $\Phi(R)=R^2R'=A$. Since $R^2\subseteq A\subseteq Z(R)$, the class-two commutator identity gives $[x,y]^2=[x^2,y]=1$. The central subgroup $R'$ is generated by these involutions, so $A$ is elementary abelian. This proves (1) and (2).

*4. Tame images.* A finite dyadic tame quotient has cyclic odd-order inertia and cyclic quotient by inertia. Every $2$-subgroup embeds in that cyclic quotient, hence is cyclic. Thus $r(R)$ is a cyclic $2$-group. Its automorphism group is a $2$-group, so conjugation by the odd-order group $r(T)$ is trivial on it. Since $R=[R,T]$, this forces $r(R)=1$. Finally $(P\rtimes T)/R\cong(C/A)\times T$. $\square$

**Checks and scope.** For a trivial action, $R=1$ and $C=P$, allowing nonabelian $F$ and arbitrary nilpotency class. If $V_0=0$, then $C=F$ and $P=FR$, so $F=\Phi(P)=\Phi(F)A$; the Frattini property forces $F=A$ and $P=R$. Thus a fixed-point-free action on the generator quotient forces the full Frattini subgroup to be central elementary abelian.

Both hypotheses on the action matter. Inversion on $C_4$ has even order and fixes $\Phi(C_4)$ pointwise, but $[C_4,T]=C_2$ is fixed, so $R=[R,T]$ fails. On the additive group $P=(\mathbb Z/4)^2$, the map $t(x,y)=(-y,x-y)$ has order three and $t-1$ has determinant $3$, hence is invertible. Thus $[P,T]=P$ and $P^T=0$, but $\Phi(P)=2P\ne0=P'$. This action does not fix $\Phi(P)$ pointwise. Preserving the Frattini subgroup as a subgroup alone is therefore insufficient. The moving factor in the theorem need not be abelian.
