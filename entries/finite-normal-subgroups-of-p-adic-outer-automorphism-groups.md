**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite of degree $d$ and $G = G_K$. For every prime $\ell$, $\mathrm{cd}_\ell\, G = 2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.1.8) (i), p. 376], and a profinite group with an element of order $\ell$ has $\mathrm{cd}_\ell = \infty$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, §3.3, p. 172]. So $G$ is torsion-free. As in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), identify $G$ with the closed normal subgroup $\mathrm{Inn}(G)$ of the profinite group $A = \mathrm{Aut}(G)$, so that $A/G = \mathrm{Out}(G)$, and conjugation by $\alpha \in A$ restricts to $\alpha$ on $G$. For $f \in \mathrm{Out}(G)$ of finite order let $E_f \subseteq A$ be the preimage of $\langle f \rangle$. It contains $G$ as an open normal subgroup with $E_f/G = \langle f \rangle$.

Let $G^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$ be the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, put $V = G^{\mathrm{ab}}(p) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathbb{Q}_p$, and let $I \subseteq V$ be the span of the image of the inertia group $I_K$. Inner automorphisms act trivially on $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, so $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ acts on $V$. Every automorphism of $G$ preserves $I_K$ and the Frobenius element of $G/I_K \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ ([invariants recovered from $G_K$](local-field-galois-invariants.md)). So $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ preserves $I$ and acts trivially on the line $V/I = (G/I_K)(p) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$. By facts (i) and (ii) of [Lubin–Tate representations of non-Galois fields](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), $\log_K \circ \mathrm{rec}_K^{-1}$ identifies $I$ with $K_+$, and $\alpha \in \mathrm{Out}(G)$ acts on $I$ as Kondo's automorphism $\alpha_+$ of $K_+$. So $\dim V = d + 1$.

*Kondo's representation.* Suppose $p$ is odd or $\sqrt{-1} \in K$, and $d \ge 3$. Write $g = (d-1)/2$ and $r = 1$ if $d$ is odd, and $g = (d-2)/2$ and $r = 2$ if $d$ is even, so $d = 2g + r$ and $g \ge 1$. In a basis $y_1, \dots, y_d$ of $K_+$ built from Jannsen–Wingberg or Diekert generators [@kondo2026, Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 1.3, p. 5], $\Phi : \mathrm{Out}(G) \to \mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, $\alpha \mapsto \alpha_+$, is a continuous homomorphism [@kondo2026, Lemma 1.6, p. 6], and its image contains the matrices $\mathrm{diag}(I_r, X)$ with $X \in \mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ [@kondo2026, Definition 3.2 and Lemma 3.3, pp. 9–10]. Neither result assumes that $K$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Euler characteristic.* Let $f \in \mathrm{Out}(G)$ have order $p$. If $E_f$ has no element of order $p$, then $\dim V^{f} = 1 + d/p$, and $I$ is a free $\mathbb{Q}_p[\langle f \rangle]$-module of rank $d/p$. In particular $p \mid d$. Hence, if $p \nmid d$, every element of order $p$ of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ is the class of an automorphism of $G_K$ of order $p$.
2. *Finitely many conjugates.* Suppose $p$ is odd or $\sqrt{-1} \in K$, and $d \ge 3$. If $f \in \mathrm{Out}(G)$ has only finitely many conjugates, for instance if $f$ lies in a finite normal subgroup, then $\Phi(f) = \mathrm{diag}(C, \mu I_{2g})$ with $C \in \mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ and $\mu \in \mathbb{Q}_p^\times$. If $f$ has finite order, $\mu$ is a root of unity.
3. *Order $p$.* Suppose $p$ is odd and $d \ge 3$, or $\sqrt{-1} \in K$ and $d \ge 6$. Let $f \in \mathrm{Out}(G)$ have order $p$ and finitely many conjugates. Then $f$ is the class of an automorphism $\sigma$ of $G$ of order $p$. If moreover $p \ge 5$, or $p = 3$ and $d$ is odd, then every such $\sigma$ acts trivially on $V$, that is, on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$. If in addition $\zeta_p \in K$, then $\sigma$ induces the identity on the maximal pro-$p$ quotient $G_K(p)$.

**Corollary (Tsujimura's question).** This is proved here. Let $K$ be as in part 3, and let $N$ be a rival copy of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ of type (a) in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 2, with $M = N \cap G_K$. If $p$ divides $(G_K : M)$, then $NG_K$ contains an automorphism $\sigma$ of $G_K$ of order $p$ that lies in neither $G_K$ nor $N$. If $p \ge 5$, or $p = 3$ and $d$ is odd, $\sigma$ acts trivially on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$, and if also $\zeta_p \in K$, it induces the identity on $G_K(p)$.

*Proof.* By part 2(a) there, $F = NG_K/G_K$ is a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ of order $(G_K : M)$. It has an element $f$ of order $p$, all of whose conjugates lie in $F$. Part 3 gives $\sigma \in E_f \subseteq NG_K$ of order $p$ with class $f$, so $\sigma \notin G_K$. Write $\sigma = g\nu$ with $g \in G_K$ and $\nu \in N$. By part 1 there, $NG_K/M$ is the direct product of $G_K/M$ and $N/M$, so $(gM)^p = 1$. If $gM = 1$, then $g \in M \subseteq N$ and $\sigma \in N$, which is impossible because $N \cong G_K$ is torsion-free. So $\sigma \notin N$. The last two claims are part 3. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** Put $C = \langle f \rangle$ and $E = E_f$, and write $U = G$ when it is viewed as the normal subgroup of $E$. Conjugation by $E$ acts on $U$ through the automorphisms in $E$, so $C$ acts on each $H^i(U, B)$ through $f$. All modules below are discrete and finite of $p$-power order.

*Step 1: finiteness.* $\mathrm{cd}_p U = 2$ and every $H^i(U, B)$ is finite [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.1.8) (i) and (iii), p. 376]. Since $E$ has no element of order $p$, Serre's theorem gives $\mathrm{cd}_p E = \mathrm{cd}_p U = 2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, remark after (3.3.5), p. 174]. The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence $H^a(C, H^b(U, B)) \Rightarrow H^{a+b}(E, B)$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (2.4.1), p. 111] gives each $H^n(E, B)$ a finite filtration whose quotients are subquotients of the finite groups $H^a(C, H^b(U, B))$, $a + b = n$, so $H^n(E, B)$ is finite. Put $\chi(E, B) = \sum_{i=0}^{2} (-1)^i \log_p \#H^i(E, B)$, and define $\chi(U, B)$ likewise. Long exact cohomology sequences, which stop at degree $2$, show that $\chi(E, -)$ is additive in short exact sequences.

*Step 2: modules on which $E$ acts through $C$.* If $E$ acts on $B$ through $E \to C$ and $\#B = p^{s}$, then $\chi(E, B) = s \cdot \chi(E, \mathbb{Z}/p)$. Indeed, the factors of a composition series of $B$ as an $E$-module are simple $C$-modules of $p$-power order, hence $\mathbb{Z}/p$ with trivial action, as $C$ is a $p$-group [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (1.6.13), p. 71]. Apply additivity.

*Step 3: $\chi(E, \mathbb{Z}/p^k) = -dk/p$.* Tate's formula gives $\chi(U, \mathbb{Z}/p^k) = \log_p \|p^k\|_K = -dk$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.3.1) and the definition before it, pp. 391–392]. The induced module $\mathrm{Ind}_E^U(\mathbb{Z}/p^k)$ is the module of maps $E/U \to \mathbb{Z}/p^k$ with $E$ acting by translation [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, §1.6, p. 62]. So $E$ acts on it through $C$, its order is $p^{kp}$, and Shapiro's lemma gives $H^i(E, \mathrm{Ind}_E^U(\mathbb{Z}/p^k)) \cong H^i(U, \mathbb{Z}/p^k)$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (1.6.4), p. 62]. By Step 2, $kp \cdot \chi(E, \mathbb{Z}/p) = -dk$. So $\chi(E, \mathbb{Z}/p) = -d/p$, and $\chi(E, \mathbb{Z}/p^k) = -dk/p$ by Step 2 again. Already $\chi(E, \mathbb{Z}/p) \in \mathbb{Z}$ forces $p \mid d$.

*Step 4: a lattice.* The $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module $\mathcal{A} = G^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$ is compact, and $\mathcal{A}/p\mathcal{A}$ is dual to the finite group $H^1(U, \mathbb{Z}/p)$. So $\mathcal{A}$ is finitely generated: lifts of generators of $\mathcal{A}/p\mathcal{A}$ generate a closed submodule $S$ with $\mathcal{A} = S + p\mathcal{A}$, and the compact pro-$p$ group $\mathcal{A}/S$ equals $p(\mathcal{A}/S)$, hence is $0$. Let $T$ be its finite torsion subgroup and $L = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}/T, \mathbb{Z}_p)$, a lattice with $C$-action and $L \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p = V^*$. Then $M_k := H^1(U, \mathbb{Z}/p^k) = \mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}, \mathbb{Z}/p^k)$ contains $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{A}/T, \mathbb{Z}/p^k) = L/p^kL$ as a $C$-submodule, with quotient $Q_k \subseteq \mathrm{Hom}(T, \mathbb{Z}/p^k)$ of order at most $\#T$. In what follows $O(1)$ means bounded independently of $k$.

- From $0 \to L \xrightarrow{p^k} L \to L/p^kL \to 0$ we get $0 \to L^C/p^kL^C \to (L/p^kL)^C \to H^1(C, L)$, and an exact sequence $H^1(C, L) \to H^1(C, L/p^kL) \to H^2(C, L)$. The groups $H^i(C, L)$, $i \ge 1$, are finitely generated over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and killed by $p$, hence finite. $L^C$ is free of rank $\dim (V^*)^C = \dim V^C$. So $\log_p \#(L/p^kL)^C = k \dim V^C + O(1)$ and $\#H^1(C, L/p^kL) = O(1)$.
- A $1$-cocycle on the cyclic group $C$ is determined by its value at a generator, so $\#H^1(C, Q_k) \le \#T$. Hence $\log_p \#M_k^C = k \dim V^C + O(1)$ and $\#H^1(C, M_k) = O(1)$.

*Step 5: counting.* Let $h^i_k = \log_p \#H^i(E, \mathbb{Z}/p^k)$, with trivial action. Then $h^0_k = k$. For $i = 1, 2$, $H^i(C, \mathbb{Z}/p^k)$ has order $p$, by periodicity [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (1.7.1), p. 74]. Tate duality gives $\#H^2(U, \mathbb{Z}/p^k) = \#H^0(K, \mu_{p^k}) \le \#\mu_{p^\infty}(K)$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.2.6), p. 380]. With Step 4, all three groups $H^0(C, H^2(U, \mathbb{Z}/p^k))$, $H^1(C, M_k)$ and $H^2(C, \mathbb{Z}/p^k)$ are $O(1)$, so $h^2_k = O(1)$ by the filtration of Step 1. The five-term exact sequence $0 \to H^1(C, \mathbb{Z}/p^k) \to H^1(E, \mathbb{Z}/p^k) \to M_k^C \to H^2(C, \mathbb{Z}/p^k)$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (1.6.7), p. 67] gives $h^1_k = k \dim V^C + O(1)$. Step 3 now reads
$$-\frac{dk}{p} = k - k \dim V^C + O(1),$$
so $\dim V^C = 1 + d/p$.

*Step 6: the module $I$.* As $C$ acts trivially on $V/I$ and taking invariants of a finite group over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is exact, $\dim I^C = d/p$. The polynomial $x^p - 1$ factors over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ as $(x - 1)\Phi_p(x)$ with $\Phi_p$ irreducible, by Eisenstein's criterion applied to $\Phi_p(x + 1)$, so $\mathbb{Q}_p[C] \cong \mathbb{Q}_p \times \mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)$. Thus $I \cong \mathbb{Q}_p^a \oplus \mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)^b$ with $a = \dim I^C = d/p$ and $a + (p-1)b = d$. So $b = d/p$, and $I \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[C]^{d/p}$.

*The last claim.* If $p \nmid d$, then $E$ has an element $t$ of finite order other than $1$. It is not in $U$, which is torsion-free, and $t^p \in U$ has finite order, so $t^p = 1$. Its class is $f^j$ with $p \nmid j$. If $jj' \equiv 1 \pmod p$, then $t^{j'}$ has order $p$ and class $f$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** The conjugacy class of $f$ is finite, so its centralizer $Z$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ is a closed subgroup of finite index, hence open and compact. Let $\mathcal{I} = \Phi(\mathrm{Out}(G))$ and let $B \subseteq \mathcal{I}$ be the group of matrices $\mathrm{diag}(I_r, X)$, $X \in \mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. Then $\Phi(Z)$ is compact, hence closed, and $(B : B \cap \Phi(Z)) \le (\mathcal{I} : \Phi(Z)) \le (\mathrm{Out}(G) : Z)$ is finite. So $B \cap \Phi(Z)$ is a closed subgroup of finite index in $B$, hence open. It consists of the $\mathrm{diag}(I_r, X)$ with $X$ in an open subgroup $W$ of $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, and $\Phi(f)$ commutes with all of them.

By the Claim in the proof of part 2 of [Lubin–Tate representations of non-Galois fields](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-span of the matrices $X - I_{2g}$ with $X \in W$ is $M_{2g}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. As $\mathrm{diag}(0, X - I_{2g}) = \mathrm{diag}(I_r, X) - I_d$, the matrix $\Phi(f)$ commutes with $\mathrm{diag}(0, Y)$ for every $Y \in M_{2g}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. Taking $Y = I_{2g}$, it commutes with $\mathrm{diag}(I_r, 0)$, so it is block diagonal, $\mathrm{diag}(C, D')$. Then $D'$ commutes with all of $M_{2g}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, so $D' = \mu I_{2g}$, and $\mu \ne 0$ as $\Phi(f)$ is invertible. If $f^m = 1$, then $\mu^m = 1$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Part 2 applies, so $\Phi(f) = \mathrm{diag}(C, \mu I_{2g})$ with $\mu^p = 1$. Suppose that $E_f$ has no element of order $p$. By part 1, $I \cong K_+$ is a free $\mathbb{Q}_p[\langle f \rangle]$-module of rank $d/p$, with $f$ acting by $\Phi(f)$. So each $p$-th root of unity in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is an eigenvalue of $\Phi(f)$ of multiplicity exactly $d/p$. But $\mu$ is an eigenvalue of multiplicity at least $2g$.

- If $p$ is odd, $\mathbb{Q}_p$ contains no primitive $p$-th root of unity, since $\Phi_p$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $p - 1 \ge 2$. So $\mu = 1$, and $2g \le d/p \le d/3$. For $d = 3$ this says $2 \le 1$, and for $d \ge 4$ it contradicts $2g \ge d - 2 > d/3$.
- If $p = 2$, then $\mu = \pm 1$ and $d$ is even, as $\sqrt{-1} \in K$, so $2g = d - 2 \le d/2$, which fails for $d \ge 6$.

So $E_f$ has an element of order $p$, and as in the last step of part 1, some element of $E_f$ of order $p$ has class $f$.

Now let $\sigma$ be any automorphism of order $p$ with class $f$, and let $p \ge 5$, or $p = 3$ and $r = 1$. Then $p$ is odd, so $\mu = 1$. As $C^p = I_r$, the eigenvalues of $C$ are $p$-th roots of unity. One other than $1$ would be a root of $\Phi_p$, which is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $p - 1 > r$, so $\Phi_p$ would divide the characteristic polynomial of $C$, of degree $r$. So all eigenvalues are $1$. As $C$ has finite order, it is diagonalizable, so $C = I_r$ and $\Phi(f) = I_d$. So $\sigma$ acts trivially on $I$ and on $V/I$. An automorphism of finite order of a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-vector space that is trivial on a subspace and on the quotient is trivial, since its minimal polynomial divides both $(x - 1)^2$ and a polynomial without repeated roots. So $\sigma$ acts trivially on $V$.

Finally let also $\zeta_p \in K$. Then $p \ge 5$, since $\zeta_3 \in K$ would make $d$ even, as $[\mathbb{Q}_3(\zeta_3) : \mathbb{Q}_3] = 2$. So $D = G_K(p)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $d + 2 \ge 3$ ([maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md)). The kernel of $G \to D$ is characteristic, so $\sigma$ induces $\bar\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ with $\bar\sigma^p = 1$. As $D^{\mathrm{ab}} = G^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$, the group $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ modulo torsion is a lattice in $V$, on which $\bar\sigma$ acts trivially. By [automorphisms of order $p$ of Demushkin groups](order-p-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-act-nontrivially-modulo-torsion.md), Theorem, $\bar\sigma = 1$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Field automorphisms.* If $K/K_0$ is cyclic of degree $p$, a generator of $\mathrm{Gal}(K/K_0)$ gives $f \in \mathrm{Out}(G)$ of order $p$, and $E_f$ is the image of $G_{K_0}$ under conjugation. This image is isomorphic to $G_{K_0}$, because $G_{K_0}$ is slim ([rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), Setting), so it is torsion-free. Part 1 then says that $K_+$ is a free $\mathbb{Q}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(K/K_0)]$-module, as the normal basis theorem also shows.
- *Primes other than $p$.* The count has no analogue for an element of prime order $\ell \ne p$. Tate's formula gives $\chi(U, \mathbb{Z}/\ell) = \log_\ell \|\ell\|_K = 0$, so the analogue of Step 3 yields $\chi(E, \mathbb{Z}/\ell) = 0$, which imposes no constraint. Nothing is said here about elements of order prime to $p$ in finite normal subgroups of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. For odd $p$ and $d \ge 3$, elements of finite order prime to $p$ do occur in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ ([finite subgroups](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md)).
- *What remains for case (a).* Whether $G_K$ has any automorphism of order $p$ is not decided here. If, for $K$ as in part 3, it has none whose class has finitely many conjugates, then every finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has order prime to $p$. The Corollary then forces $(G_K : M)$ to be prime to $p$ in case (a). For $p \ge 5$ and $\zeta_p \in K$, such an automorphism would be trivial on $G_K(p)$, so it could only be detected beyond the maximal pro-$p$ quotient. The cases $d \le 2$ for odd $p$, $d \le 4$ for $p = 2$ with $\sqrt{-1} \in K$, and $p = 2$ with $\sqrt{-1} \notin K$ are not treated, except by part 1.
