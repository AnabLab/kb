**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of degree $d$ with residue field of order $q$, $G = G_K$ with wild inertia group $P_K$, and $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K) = G/P_K$. For a pro-$p$ group $H$ let $H^{(1)} = H$ and $H^{(i+1)} = (H^{(i)})^p[H^{(i)}, H]$ (closed subgroups), so that $H^{(2)}$ is the Frattini subgroup. For $H = P_K$ this is the series $V^i$ of [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 421].

Identify $G$ with $F/(\varrho)$ as in the Setting of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md): $F = P \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, where $\mathcal{G}$ is generated by $\sigma, \tau$ with the single relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, $P$ is the pro-$p$ group normally generated by $x_0, \dots, x_d$, and $F$ is free among semidirect products of $\mathcal{G}$ with pro-$p$ groups, so that any choice of $w_0, \dots, w_d \in P$ gives a unique endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and maps $x_i$ to $w_i$. The relator $\varrho$ lies in $P$, $P_K$ is the image of $P$, and $\mathcal{G}$ maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma$, with $\tau$ generating tame inertia $I \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ (same Setting). With $x^y = yxy^{-1}$ and $[x, y] = xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$,
$$\varrho = (x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0, \tau)^\kappa\, x_1^{p^s}\, C,\qquad (x_0, \tau) = \big(x_0^{h^{p-1}}\tau x_0^{h^{p-2}}\tau \cdots x_0^{h}\tau\big)^{\pi/(p-1)},$$
where $C = [x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$ for even $d$, $C = [x_1, y_1][x_2, x_3]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$ for odd $d$ with $y_1$ in the closed subgroup generated by $x_1, \sigma, \tau$, $s \ge 1$, $\pi \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ has component $1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $0$ in every other $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and $h, \kappa \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ satisfy $\tau(\zeta) = \zeta^h$ and $\sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^\kappa$ for $p$-power roots of unity $\zeta \in K^{\mathrm{tr}}$ (same Setting, and [presentations](local-galois-group-presentations.md), condition 3, where $\kappa$ is called $g$). Let $\omega : \mathcal{G} = \Gamma \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ be the action on $\mu_p$, and put $\bar h = \omega(\tau)$ and $\bar g = \omega(\sigma)$, the residues of $h$ and $\kappa$.

For $d \ge 3$, the *surface pair* is $(x_s, x_t) = (x_3, x_4)$ for even $d$ and $(x_2, x_3)$ for odd $d$. Neither generator occurs in $\varrho$ outside the factor $[x_s, x_t]$ of $C$.

Let $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathcal{G}]] = \varprojlim_U \mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]$, and extend $\omega$ to a continuous ring homomorphism $\omega : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$. Let $\lambda \mapsto \lambda^*$ be the continuous $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear map with $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, the involution of [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 421] with $\alpha$ reduced modulo $p$. It satisfies $(\lambda\mu)^* = \mu^*\lambda^*$ and $\lambda^{**} = \lambda$, so it maps units to units. Put $\bar P = P/P^{(2)}$ and $\bar V = P_K/P_K^{(2)}$, written additively. They are compact $\Lambda$-modules, with $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ acting by conjugation; $P$ and $P_K$ act trivially, as these quotients are abelian. Write $\bar x$ for the image of $x$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Structure.* $\bar P$ is a free $\Lambda$-module on $\bar x_0, \dots, \bar x_d$, and
$$\bar V = \Lambda\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \Lambda\bar x_d,$$
where $\Lambda\bar x_i \cong \Lambda$ for $i \ge 1$, and $\Lambda\bar x_0$ is one-dimensional over $\mathbb{F}_p$, with $\mathcal{G}$ acting through $\omega$.
2. *Lifting.* Let $d \ge 3$ and $\lambda \in \Lambda^\times$. There is an automorphism $\theta_\lambda$ of $G$ with $\theta_\lambda(\rho) \in \rho P_K^{(2)}$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, $\theta_\lambda(x_i) \in x_iP_K^{(2)}$ for $i \notin \{s, t\}$, and $\overline{\theta_\lambda(x_s)} = \lambda\bar x_s$ and $\overline{\theta_\lambda(x_t)} = (\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$ in $\bar V$.
3. *The induced automorphisms.* The automorphism $\bar\psi_\lambda$ of $G/P_K^{(2)}$ induced by $\theta_\lambda$ depends only on $\lambda$. It fixes the image of $\mathcal{G}$, and acts on $\bar V$ $\Lambda$-linearly, as the identity on $\Lambda\bar x_i$ for $i \notin \{s, t\}$, by $\mu\bar x_s \mapsto \mu\lambda\bar x_s$ and by $\mu\bar x_t \mapsto \mu(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$. Moreover $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu = \bar\psi_{\mu\lambda}$, $\Gamma$ has trivial centre, and $\theta_\lambda$ is not inner if $\lambda \ne 1$.

So $\lambda \mapsto \bar\psi_\lambda$ embeds the opposite group of $\Lambda^\times$ in the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K/P_K^{(2)})$, and all $\theta_\lambda$ with $\lambda \ne 1$ are outer. The theorem is applied in [tame twists with finitely many outer conjugates](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md).

**Lemma.** This is proved here. Let $I_m \subseteq I$ be the closed subgroup generated by $\tau^m$, for $m$ prime to $p$. For $m$ prime to $p$ and divisible by $p - 1$ put $e_{(m)} = \frac1m\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\bar h^{-i}\tau^i \in \mathbb{F}_p[I/I_m]$, which makes sense because $\bar h^{p-1} = 1$. These elements are compatible, so they define $e_h \in \mathbb{F}_p[[I]]$; we use the same name for its image in $\Lambda$. Put $u = \bar g e_h - \sigma \in \Lambda$.

- (a) $e_h$ is a central idempotent of $\Lambda$, $\tau e_h = \bar h e_h$ and $\omega(e_h) = 1$.
- (b) $u \in \ker\omega$, and $\ker\omega = u\Lambda = \Lambda u$.
- (c) Let $M$ be a compact $\Lambda$-module and $b : \bar P \times \bar P \to M$ a continuous biadditive map with $b(\rho x, \rho y) = \rho b(x, y)$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$. Then $b(\lambda x, \mu y) - b(x, \lambda^*\mu y) \in uM$ for all $\lambda, \mu \in \Lambda$ and $x, y \in \bar P$.

*Proof.* Compatibility: for $m \mid m'$, the image of $e_{(m')}$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[I/I_m]$ is $\frac{1}{m'}\cdot\frac{m'}{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\bar h^{-i}\tau^i = e_{(m)}$.

(a) In $\mathbb{F}_p[I/I_m]$, $e_{(m)}^2 = \frac{1}{m^2}\sum_{i,j}\bar h^{-(i+j)}\tau^{i+j} = e_{(m)}$ and $\tau e_{(m)} = \bar h e_{(m)}$, by reindexing. Conjugation by $\sigma$ acts on $I/I_m$ by $\tau \mapsto \tau^q$, and maps $e_{(m)}$ to $\frac1m\sum_i\bar h^{-i}\tau^{qi} = e_{(m)}$, since $i \mapsto qi$ permutes $\mathbb{Z}/m$ and $\bar h^{qi} = \bar h^i$, as $q \equiv 1 \pmod{p-1}$. So the centralizer of $e_h$ in $\mathcal{G}$ is a closed subgroup containing $\sigma$ and $\tau$, hence is $\mathcal{G}$, whose span is dense in $\Lambda$; so $e_h$ is central. Finally $\omega(e_{(m)}) = \frac1m\sum_i\bar h^{-i}\bar h^i = 1$.

(b) $\omega(u) = \bar g\,\omega(e_h) - \omega(\sigma) = 0$. Let $\mathcal{I} = \ker(\Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p)$ be the augmentation ideal. It is the closure of the span of the $\rho - 1$, $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, because at each finite level the augmentation ideal is spanned by these. Let $J$ be the closure of $(\sigma - 1)\Lambda + (\tau - 1)\Lambda$. The set of $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ with $\rho - 1 \in J$ is closed, contains $\sigma$ and $\tau$, and is a subgroup, since $\rho\rho' - 1 = (\rho - 1)\rho' + (\rho' - 1)$ and $\rho^{-1} - 1 = -(\rho - 1)\rho^{-1}$. So it is $\mathcal{G}$, and $\mathcal{I} = J$. In the same way, with $\rho\rho' - 1 = \rho(\rho' - 1) + (\rho - 1)$, $\mathcal{I}$ is the closure of $\Lambda(\sigma - 1) + \Lambda(\tau - 1)$. The continuous ring automorphism $T$ of $\Lambda$ with $T(\rho) = \omega(\rho)^{-1}\rho$ satisfies $\epsilon \circ T^{-1} = \omega$, where $\epsilon$ is the augmentation, so $\ker\omega = T(\mathcal{I})$ is the closure of $(\bar g^{-1}\sigma - 1)\Lambda + (\bar h^{-1}\tau - 1)\Lambda$, and also of the corresponding left ideal. Let $x \in \ker\omega$ and write $x = xe_h + x(1 - e_h)$.

- As $e_h$ is central and $(\bar h^{-1}\tau - 1)e_h = 0$, $xe_h = e_hx$ lies in the closure of $(\bar g^{-1}\sigma - 1)e_h\Lambda$, which is compact, hence closed. Now $(\bar g^{-1}\sigma - 1)e_h = \bar g^{-1}(\sigma - \bar g)e_h = -\bar g^{-1}ue_h$. So $xe_h \in u\Lambda$, and likewise $xe_h \in \Lambda u$.
- $u(1 - e_h) = -\sigma(1 - e_h)$, and $u$, $\sigma^{-1}$, $e_h$ commute, so $1 - e_h = u\cdot(-\sigma^{-1}(1 - e_h)) = (-\sigma^{-1}(1 - e_h))\cdot u$. Hence $x(1 - e_h) = (1 - e_h)x \in u\Lambda$ and $x(1 - e_h) \in \Lambda u$.

So $\ker\omega \subseteq u\Lambda \cap \Lambda u$, and the reverse inclusions hold because $u \in \ker\omega$, a two-sided ideal.

(c) The set $uM$ is a closed subgroup of $M$, being the image of the compact group $M$. For $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ and $m \in M$, $\rho m - \omega(\rho)m = (\rho - \omega(\rho))m \in (\ker\omega)M \subseteq u\Lambda M = uM$ by (b). So for $\rho, \rho' \in \mathcal{G}$,
$$b(\rho x, \rho' y) = \rho\, b(x, \rho^{-1}\rho' y) \equiv \omega(\rho)\, b(x, \rho^{-1}\rho' y) = b(x, \rho^*\rho' y) \pmod{uM}.$$
Both $b(\lambda x, \mu y)$ and $b(x, \lambda^*\mu y)$ are continuous and biadditive in $(\lambda, \mu)$, and $\mathbb{F}_p$-bilinear, as $M$ has exponent $p$ (it is a $\Lambda$-module); they agree modulo the closed subgroup $uM$ on $\mathcal{G} \times \mathcal{G}$, whose span is dense in $\Lambda \times \Lambda$. $\square$

**Computation.** This is proved here. Let $N \subseteq P$ be a closed normal subgroup of $F$, put $Q = P/N$, and let $A \subseteq Q$ be a closed subgroup, normal in $F/N$, central in $Q$ and of exponent $p$. Write $A$ additively, as a compact $\Lambda$-module with $\rho a = \rho a\rho^{-1}$. Then for $X \in Q$ and $a \in A$, in $F/N$,
$$\big((Xa)^\sigma\big)^{-1}(Xa, \tau)^\kappa = \big(X^\sigma\big)^{-1}(X, \tau)^\kappa\cdot ua,$$
where $(Y, \tau)$ is defined for $Y \in Q$ by the formula of the Setting.

*Proof.* As $a$ is central in $Q$, $(Xa)^\sigma = X^\sigma\cdot\sigma a$, so $((Xa)^\sigma)^{-1} = (X^\sigma)^{-1}\cdot(-\sigma a)$. If $W \in F/N$ has image $\tau^j$ in $\mathcal{G}$, write $W = w\tau^j$ with $w \in Q$; then $aW = wa\tau^j = W\cdot\tau^{-j}a$. Let $Y_a = (Xa)^{h^{p-1}}\tau(Xa)^{h^{p-2}}\tau\cdots(Xa)^h\tau$, so that $Y_0$ is the element whose $\pi/(p-1)$-th power is $(X, \tau)$. Since $(Xa)^{h^j} = X^{h^j}\cdot\bar h^j a$, as $a$ commutes with $X$ and $A$ has exponent $p$, and the factor $(Xa)^{h^j}$ is followed by a word with image $\tau^j$, moving every term $\bar h^j a$ to the right gives $Y_a = Y_0\cdot b'a$ with $b' = \sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\bar h^j\tau^{-j}$. As $Y_0$ has image $\tau^{p-1}$, for every integer $n \ge 1$
$$(Y_0a')^n = Y_0^n\cdot S_na',\qquad S_n = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\tau^{-(p-1)i}\qquad (a' \in A).$$
Let $B$ be an open normal subgroup of $F/N$, and let $e = p^\alpha e'$, with $p \nmid e'$, be the exponent of $(F/N)/B$. The procyclic group $I$, of order prime to $p$, acts on the finite module $A/(A \cap B)$ through $I/I_{m'}$ for some $m'$ prime to $p$; enlarging $m'$, we may assume $(p - 1)e' \mid m'$. Choose an integer $n \ge 1$ with $m' \mid n$ and $n(p-1) \equiv 1 \pmod{p^{\alpha+1}}$. Then $n \equiv \pi/(p-1)$ modulo $e$, so $g^n \equiv g^{\pi/(p-1)}$ modulo $B$ for every $g \in F/N$. In $\mathbb{F}_p[I/I_{m'}]$, $\tau^{p-1}$ has order $k = m'/(p-1)$, which divides $n$, so $S_n = \frac nk\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\tau^{-(p-1)i} = (p-1)^{-1}E_{m'}$ with $E_{m'} = \frac1k\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\tau^{(p-1)i}$, as $n \equiv (p-1)^{-1} \pmod p$. Moreover
$$E_{m'}b' = \frac1k\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\bar h^j\tau^{(p-1)i - j} = \frac1k\sum_{c \in \mathbb{Z}/m'}\bar h^{-c}\tau^c = (p-1)\,e_{(m')},$$
because $(i, j) \mapsto (p-1)i - j$ is a bijection onto $\mathbb{Z}/m'$ and $\bar h^{j} = \bar h^{-((p-1)i-j)}$. So modulo $B$, $(X a, \tau) \equiv (Y_0 b'a)^n = Y_0^n\cdot S_nb'a \equiv (X, \tau)\cdot e_ha$. As $B$ is arbitrary, $(Xa, \tau) = (X, \tau)\cdot e_ha$. The element $(X, \tau)$ lies in $Q$, its image in $\mathcal{G}$ being $\tau^{\pi} = 1$ as $\tau$ has order prime to $p$, so $(Xa, \tau)^\kappa = (X, \tau)^\kappa\cdot\bar g e_ha$, using $\kappa \equiv \bar g \pmod p$. Multiplying the two factors gives the claim, since $ua = \bar g e_ha - \sigma a$. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** *$\bar P$ is free.* $P$ is the closed normal subgroup of $F = P \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ generated by the $x_i$, and $P$ acts trivially on $\bar P$, so the closed $\Lambda$-submodule generated by the $\bar x_i$ is $\bar P$, and $\Lambda^{d+1} \to \bar P$, $e_i \mapsto \bar x_i$, is onto (its image is compact). For an open normal subgroup $U$ of $\mathcal{G}$, let $\mathcal{G}$ act on $\mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]^{d+1}$ by left multiplication. By the universal property of $F$ there is a homomorphism $F \to \mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]^{d+1} \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and maps $x_i$ to the $i$-th basis vector $e_i$. On $P$ it is a $\mathcal{G}$-equivariant homomorphism to an elementary abelian group, so it factors through a $\Lambda$-linear map $\bar P \to \mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]^{d+1}$ with $\bar x_i \mapsto e_i$. So the composite $\Lambda^{d+1} \to \bar P \to \mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]^{d+1}$ is the reduction, and as $U$ varies these reductions separate the points of $\Lambda^{d+1}$. Hence $\Lambda^{d+1} \to \bar P$ is injective.

*The relation modulo the Frattini subgroup.* Apply the Computation with $N = P^{(2)}$, $A = Q = \bar P$, $X = 1$ and $a = \bar x_0$. Since $(1, \tau) = (\tau^{p-1})^{\pi/(p-1)} = \tau^\pi = 1$, and $x_1^{p^s}$ and $C$ lie in $P^{(2)}$, the image of $\varrho$ in $\bar P$ is $\bar\varrho = u\bar x_0$.

*$\bar V$.* As $(\varrho) \subseteq P$, $P_K = P/(\varrho)$, and the image of $P^{(i)}$ in $P_K$ is $P_K^{(i)}$, because a continuous surjection of compact groups maps the closed subgroup generated by a set onto the closed subgroup generated by its image. So $\bar V$ is the quotient of $\bar P$ by the image of $(\varrho)$, which is the closed $\Lambda$-submodule generated by $\bar\varrho$, as $(\varrho)$ is topologically generated by the conjugates $f\varrho f^{-1}$, $f \in F$. Hence $\bar V = \Lambda\bar x_0/\Lambda u\bar x_0 \oplus \bigoplus_{i \ge 1}\Lambda\bar x_i$, with $\Lambda\bar x_0/\Lambda u\bar x_0 \cong \Lambda/\Lambda u = \Lambda/\ker\omega \cong \mathbb{F}_p$, on which $\rho$ acts as $\omega(\rho)$, by Lemma (b). $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $M = P^{(2)}/P^{(3)}$, written additively. Since $[P^{(2)}, P] \subseteq P^{(3)}$ and $(P^{(2)})^p \subseteq P^{(3)}$, $M$ is central in $P/P^{(3)}$ and has exponent $p$; it is a compact $\Lambda$-module under conjugation. The commutator induces a map $b : \bar P \times \bar P \to M$, $b(\bar x, \bar y) = [x, y]P^{(3)}$: it is well defined and biadditive because $[xw, y] = x[w, y]x^{-1}[x, y]$ and $[w, y] \in P^{(3)}$ for $w \in P^{(2)}$, and $[xx', y] = x[x', y]x^{-1}[x, y]$, with the analogous identities in the second variable; it is continuous, and $\rho[x, y]\rho^{-1} = [\rho x\rho^{-1}, \rho y\rho^{-1}]$.

Choose $y_s, y_t \in P$ with $\bar y_s = \lambda\bar x_s$ and $\bar y_t = (\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$, and put $c = b(\bar y_s, \bar y_t) - b(\bar x_s, \bar x_t)$. By Lemma (c) with $\mu = (\lambda^*)^{-1}$, $c \equiv b(\bar x_s, \lambda^*(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t) - b(\bar x_s, \bar x_t) = 0$ modulo $uM$. Choose $z \in P^{(2)}$ whose class $w \in M$ satisfies $uw = -c$. Let $\psi$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, maps $x_0 \mapsto x_0z$, $x_s \mapsto y_s$, $x_t \mapsto y_t$, and fixes the other $x_i$.

*$\psi(\varrho) \equiv \varrho$ modulo $P^{(3)}$.* $\psi$ fixes $x_1$, and $x_2$ for even $d$, and $y_1$ for odd $d$, so $\psi(\varrho) = ((x_0z)^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0z, \tau)^\kappa x_1^{p^s}C'$, where $C'$ is $C$ with $[x_s, x_t]$ replaced by $[y_s, y_t]$. In $P/P^{(3)}$, all factors of $C$ and $C'$ lie in $M$, so $C' = C + c$. By the Computation with $N = P^{(3)}$, $A = M$, $X = x_0P^{(3)}$ and $a = w$, the first two factors become $(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0, \tau)^\kappa\cdot uw$. As $M$ is central in $P/P^{(3)}$ and all factors lie in $P$, $\psi(\varrho) \equiv \varrho\cdot(uw + c) = \varrho$.

*An automorphism of $G/P_K^{(3)}$.* By the proof of part 1, the inverse image of $P_K^{(3)}$ in $F$ is $(\varrho)P^{(3)}$, so $G/P_K^{(3)} = F/(\varrho)P^{(3)}$. Since $\psi$ fixes $\mathcal{G}$ and maps each $x_i$ into the normal subgroup $P$, $\psi(P) \subseteq P$, so also $\psi(P^{(3)}) \subseteq P^{(3)}$, and $\psi(f\varrho f^{-1}) = \psi(f)\psi(\varrho)\psi(f)^{-1} \in (\varrho)P^{(3)}$, so $\psi$ maps $(\varrho)P^{(3)}$ into itself and induces an endomorphism $\psi_3$ of $G/P_K^{(3)}$. The image $\psi(P)$ is a closed subgroup of $P$, stable under $\mathcal{G}$, whose image in $\bar P$ is a closed $\Lambda$-submodule containing $\bar x_0$, the $\bar x_i$ with $i \notin \{s, t\}$, $\lambda\bar x_s$ and $(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$, hence all of $\bar P$. So $\psi(P)P^{(2)} = P$, hence $\psi(P) = P$, as $P^{(2)}$ is the Frattini subgroup of $P$, and $\psi$ and $\psi_3$ are onto. A continuous surjective endomorphism $\phi$ of a topologically finitely generated profinite group $H$ is injective: $H$ has only finitely many open subgroups of each index $n$, $U \mapsto \phi^{-1}(U)$ maps them injectively, hence bijectively, to themselves, so every open subgroup contains $\ker\phi$, and their intersection is trivial. As $G/P_K^{(3)}$ is topologically generated by the images of $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_d$, $\psi_3$ is an automorphism. It fixes the images of $\sigma$ and $\tau$, so it induces the identity on $G/P_K = \Gamma$.

*The lift.* By the corrected form of Wingberg's lifting theorem [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15), p. 421], there is an automorphism $\theta_\lambda$ of $G$ that coincides with $\psi_3$ modulo $P_K^{(2)}$. It has the stated properties, because $\psi$ fixes $\mathcal{G}$ and the $x_i$ with $i \notin \{0, s, t\}$, $\psi(x_0) = x_0z$ with $z \in P^{(2)}$, and $\bar y_s = \lambda\bar x_s$, $\bar y_t = (\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** $G/P_K^{(2)}$ is generated by $\bar V$ and the image of $\mathcal{G}$, which $\bar\psi_\lambda$ fixes, so $\bar\psi_\lambda$ commutes with conjugation by $\mathcal{G}$ and is $\Lambda$-linear on $\bar V$. By part 2 it fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i \notin \{s, t\}$, and maps $\bar x_s \mapsto \lambda\bar x_s$ and $\bar x_t \mapsto (\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$; by $\Lambda$-linearity and part 1 this determines it on $\bar V$, and hence on $G/P_K^{(2)}$. In particular $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu(\bar x_s) = \bar\psi_\lambda(\mu\bar x_s) = \mu\lambda\bar x_s$ and $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu(\bar x_t) = (\mu^*)^{-1}(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t = ((\mu\lambda)^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$, so $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu = \bar\psi_{\mu\lambda}$.

*Trivial centre.* Every element of $\Gamma$ is uniquely $\tau^x\sigma^y$ with $x \in \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ and $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, since $y \mapsto \sigma^yI$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \Gamma/I = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ur}}/K)$, the image of $\sigma$ being a Frobenius. If $\tau^x\sigma^y$ is central, then $\tau = \sigma^y\tau\sigma^{-y} = \tau^{q^y}$, so $y = 0$, because $y \mapsto (x \mapsto q^y x)$ is injective from $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ to the automorphisms of $\prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.4) (ii), p. 412]. Then $\tau^x = \sigma\tau^x\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{qx}$, so $(q - 1)x = 0$ and $x = 0$, as $\prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is torsion-free.

*Not inner.* If $\theta_\lambda$ is conjugation by $g \in G$, then conjugation by the image of $g$ in $\Gamma$ is the identity, so $g \in P_K$. But conjugation by $g \in P_K$ is trivial on $\bar V$, while $\theta_\lambda$ maps $\bar x_s$ to $\lambda\bar x_s$, which differs from $\bar x_s$ for $\lambda \ne 1$, since $\Lambda\bar x_s$ is free. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Comparison with $\mathbb{Q}_p$.* For $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$, Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg twist $x_1$, which also occurs in $x_1^{p}$ and in $y_1$, by a central unit $\varepsilon$, and check that the Demuškin relation of $G_{K'}(p)$ is preserved modulo the third term for every finite tamely ramified $K'/\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 422–423]; see [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on norm-one units](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md). Here the change of $x_0$ by $z$ absorbs the defect $c$ directly in $F$, so the relator itself is preserved modulo $P^{(3)}$. This works because $u$, the coefficient with which $x_0$ enters the relation modulo commutators, generates $\ker\omega$ (Lemma (b)), and the defect of the twisted commutator lies in $(\ker\omega)M$ (Lemma (c)).
- *What is not claimed.* Only the class of $\theta_\lambda$ modulo $P_K^{(2)}$ is controlled; (7.5.15) gives no information on $\theta_\lambda$ modulo $P_K^{(3)}$, and $\lambda \mapsto \theta_\lambda$ is not claimed to be a homomorphism into $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$. For $d \le 2$ there is no surface pair, and the construction is not carried out here.
- *Taken from the sources without re-verification.* The Jannsen–Wingberg presentation, and the lifting theorem (7.5.15), which Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg attribute to Wingberg, whose own statement of it was incorrect [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15) and the remark after it, p. 421]; see [uniqueness of Demuškin formations](uniqueness-of-demuskin-formations.md) and [presentations](local-galois-group-presentations.md).
