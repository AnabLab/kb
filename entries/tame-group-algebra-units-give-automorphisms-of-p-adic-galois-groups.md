**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of degree $d$ with residue field of order $q$, $G = G_K$ with wild inertia group $P_K$, and $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K) = G/P_K$. Let $p^s$, with $s \ge 1$, be the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$. For a pro-$p$ group $H$ let $H^{(1)} = H$ and $H^{(i+1)} = (H^{(i)})^p[H^{(i)}, H]$ (closed subgroups), so that $H^{(2)}$ is the Frattini subgroup. For $H = P_K$ this is the series $V^i$ of [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 421]. Let also $H^{p,1,s} = H$ and $H^{p,i+1,s} = (H^{p,i,s})^{p^s}[H^{p,i,s}, H]$ (closed subgroups), the $p^s$-central series [@nishio2026finite, §0, p. 4]. For $s = 1$ it is the series $H^{(i)}$, and always $H^{p,2,s} \subseteq H^{(2)}$. Each $H^{(i)}$ and $H^{p,i,s}$ is characteristic in $H$.

Identify $G$ with $F/(\varrho)$ as in the Setting of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md): $F = P \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, where $\mathcal{G}$ is generated by $\sigma, \tau$ with the single relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, $P$ is the pro-$p$ group normally generated by $x_0, \dots, x_d$, and $F$ is free among semidirect products of $\mathcal{G}$ with pro-$p$ groups, so that any choice of $w_0, \dots, w_d \in P$ gives a unique endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and maps $x_i$ to $w_i$. The relator $\varrho$ lies in $P$, $P_K$ is the image of $P$, and $\mathcal{G}$ maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma$, with $\tau$ generating tame inertia $I \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ (same Setting). With $x^y = yxy^{-1}$ and $[x, y] = xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$,
$$\varrho = (x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0, \tau)^\kappa\, x_1^{p^s}\, C,\qquad (x_0, \tau) = \big(x_0^{h^{p-1}}\tau x_0^{h^{p-2}}\tau \cdots x_0^{h}\tau\big)^{\pi/(p-1)},$$
where $C = [x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$ for even $d$, $C = [x_1, y_1][x_2, x_3]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$ for odd $d$ with $y_1 \in P$ (see below), $s$ is as above, $\pi \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ has component $1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $0$ in every other $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and $h, \kappa \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ satisfy $\tau(\zeta) = \zeta^h$ and $\sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^\kappa$ for $p$-power roots of unity $\zeta \in K^{\mathrm{tr}}$ (same Setting, and [presentations](local-galois-group-presentations.md), condition 3, where $\kappa$ is called $g$). Let $\omega : \mathcal{G} = \Gamma \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ be the action on $\mu_p$, and put $\bar h = \omega(\tau)$ and $\bar g = \omega(\sigma)$, the residues of $h$ and $\kappa$.

*Why $y_1 \in P$.* Let $\pi_2 \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ have component $1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$ and $0$ in every other $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and let $H$ be the closed subgroup of $\mathcal{G}$ generated by $\tau^{\pi_2}$ and $\sigma^{\pi_2}$; it is pro-$2$, as the closed subgroup generated by $\tau^{\pi_2}$ is pro-$2$ and normal in $H$, with quotient topologically generated by the image of $\sigma^{\pi_2}$, a pro-$2$ element. Jannsen and Wingberg define $y_1$ as a product of a conjugate of $x_1$ and conjugates of elements $\{x_1, \rho\}$ and $\{\{x_1, \rho\}, \rho'\}$ with $\rho, \rho' \in H$, where $\{x, \rho\} = (x^{\beta(1)}\rho^2x^{\beta(\rho)}\rho^2\cdots x^{\beta(\rho^{p-2})}\rho^2)^{\pi/(p-1)}$ for a function $\beta : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, §1.2, pp. 74–75], and their Theorem 2 uses this relator [@jannsenwingberg1982, Theorem 2, p. 75]. For $x \in P$ and $\rho \in H$, $\{x, \rho\}$ maps to $(\rho^{2(p-1)})^{\pi/(p-1)} = 1$ in $\mathcal{G}$, because $\rho$ lies in a pro-$2$ group and $\pi/(p-1)$ has $2$-component $0$; so $\{x, \rho\} \in P$. As $P$ is normal, $y_1 \in P$. For $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$ this is part (d) of the proof in [nonabelian finite subgroups](nonabelian-finite-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md).

For $d \ge 3$, the *surface pair* is $(x_s, x_t) = (x_3, x_4)$ for even $d$ and $(x_2, x_3)$ for odd $d$. Neither generator occurs in $\varrho$ outside the factor $[x_s, x_t]$ of $C$.

Let $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathcal{G}]] = \varprojlim_U \mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]$, and extend $\omega$ to a continuous ring homomorphism $\omega : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$. Let $\lambda \mapsto \lambda^*$ be the continuous $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear map with $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, the involution of [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 421] with $\alpha$ reduced modulo $p$. It satisfies $(\lambda\mu)^* = \mu^*\lambda^*$ and $\lambda^{**} = \lambda$, so it maps units to units. Put $\bar P = P/P^{(2)}$ and $\bar V = P_K/P_K^{(2)}$, written additively. They are compact $\Lambda$-modules, with $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ acting by conjugation; $P$ and $P_K$ act trivially, as these quotients are abelian. Write $\bar x$ for the image of $x$.

*Coefficients $\mathbb{Z}/p^r$.* For $1 \le r \le s$ let $\Lambda_r = (\mathbb{Z}/p^r)[[\mathcal{G}]] = \varprojlim_U (\mathbb{Z}/p^r)[\mathcal{G}/U]$, so that $\Lambda_1 = \Lambda$. Let $\omega_r : \mathcal{G} \to (\mathbb{Z}/p^r)^\times$ give the action on $\mu_{p^r} \subseteq K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, so that $\omega_1 = \omega$, $\omega_r(\tau) = h$ and $\omega_r(\sigma) = \kappa$ modulo $p^r$, and extend it to a continuous ring homomorphism $\omega_r : \Lambda_r \to \mathbb{Z}/p^r$. Let $\lambda \mapsto \lambda^*$ also denote the continuous $\mathbb{Z}/p^r$-linear map on $\Lambda_r$ with $\rho^* = \omega_r(\rho)\rho^{-1}$; again $(\lambda\mu)^* = \mu^*\lambda^*$ and $\lambda^{**} = \lambda$. Reduction modulo $p$ is a surjective ring homomorphism $\Lambda_r \to \Lambda$ compatible with $\omega_r, \omega$ and with $*$. Since $\tau$ generates $I$, whose order is prime to $p$, $\omega_s(\tau)$ has order prime to $p$ in the cyclic group $(\mathbb{Z}/p^s)^\times$ of order $p^{s-1}(p-1)$, so $h^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p^s}$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Structure.* $\bar P$ is a free $\Lambda$-module on $\bar x_0, \dots, \bar x_d$, and
$$\bar V = \Lambda\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \Lambda\bar x_d,$$
where $\Lambda\bar x_i \cong \Lambda$ for $i \ge 1$, and $\Lambda\bar x_0$ is one-dimensional over $\mathbb{F}_p$, with $\mathcal{G}$ acting through $\omega$.
2. *Lifting.* Let $d \ge 3$ and $\lambda \in \Lambda^\times$. There is an automorphism $\theta_\lambda$ of $G$ with $\theta_\lambda(\rho) \in \rho P_K^{(2)}$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, $\theta_\lambda(x_i) \in x_iP_K^{(2)}$ for $i \notin \{s, t\}$, and $\overline{\theta_\lambda(x_s)} = \lambda\bar x_s$ and $\overline{\theta_\lambda(x_t)} = (\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$ in $\bar V$.
3. *The induced automorphisms.* The automorphism $\bar\psi_\lambda$ of $G/P_K^{(2)}$ induced by $\theta_\lambda$ depends only on $\lambda$. It fixes the image of $\mathcal{G}$, and acts on $\bar V$ $\Lambda$-linearly, as the identity on $\Lambda\bar x_i$ for $i \notin \{s, t\}$, by $\mu\bar x_s \mapsto \mu\lambda\bar x_s$ and by $\mu\bar x_t \mapsto \mu(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$. Moreover $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu = \bar\psi_{\mu\lambda}$, $\Gamma$ has trivial centre, and $\theta_\lambda$ is not inner if $\lambda \ne 1$.

So $\lambda \mapsto \bar\psi_\lambda$ embeds the opposite group of $\Lambda^\times$ in the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K/P_K^{(2)})$, and all $\theta_\lambda$ with $\lambda \ne 1$ are outer. The theorem is applied in [tame twists with finitely many outer conjugates](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md).

**Lemma.** This is proved here. Let $1 \le r \le s$. Let $I_m \subseteq I$ be the closed subgroup generated by $\tau^m$, for $m$ prime to $p$. For $m$ prime to $p$ and divisible by $p - 1$ put $e_{(m)} = \frac1m\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}h^{-i}\tau^i \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^r)[I/I_m]$, with $h$ read modulo $p^r$, which makes sense because $h^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p^r}$. These elements are compatible, so they define $e_h \in (\mathbb{Z}/p^r)[[I]]$; we use the same name for its image in $\Lambda_r$, and for every $r$, since these elements correspond under reduction. Put $u_r = \kappa e_h - \sigma \in \Lambda_r$, with $\kappa$ read modulo $p^r$, and $u = u_1 = \bar g e_h - \sigma \in \Lambda$.

- (a) $e_h$ is a central idempotent of $\Lambda_r$, $\tau e_h = h e_h$ and $\omega_r(e_h) = 1$.
- (b) $u_r \in \ker\omega_r$, and $\ker\omega_r = u_r\Lambda_r = \Lambda_r u_r$.
- (c) Let $N$ and $M$ be compact $\Lambda_r$-modules and $b : N \times N \to M$ a continuous biadditive map with $b(\rho x, \rho y) = \rho b(x, y)$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$. Then $b(\lambda x, \mu y) - b(x, \lambda^*\mu y) \in u_rM$ for all $\lambda, \mu \in \Lambda_r$ and $x, y \in N$.

For $r = 1$ these are statements about $\Lambda$, $\omega$, $u$ and $*$, and in (c) one may take $N = \bar P$.

*Proof.* Compatibility: for $m \mid m'$, the image of $e_{(m')}$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/p^r)[I/I_m]$ is $\frac{1}{m'}\cdot\frac{m'}{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}h^{-i}\tau^i = e_{(m)}$, since $h^{-i}$ depends only on $i$ modulo $p - 1$, which divides $m$.

(a) In $(\mathbb{Z}/p^r)[I/I_m]$, $e_{(m)}^2 = \frac{1}{m^2}\sum_{i,j}h^{-(i+j)}\tau^{i+j} = e_{(m)}$ and $\tau e_{(m)} = h e_{(m)}$, by reindexing. Conjugation by $\sigma$ acts on $I/I_m$ by $\tau \mapsto \tau^q$, and maps $e_{(m)}$ to $\frac1m\sum_ih^{-i}\tau^{qi} = e_{(m)}$, since $i \mapsto qi$ permutes $\mathbb{Z}/m$ and $h^{qi} = h^i$, as $q \equiv 1 \pmod{p-1}$. So the centralizer of $e_h$ in $\mathcal{G}$ is a closed subgroup containing $\sigma$ and $\tau$, hence is $\mathcal{G}$, whose span is dense in $\Lambda_r$; so $e_h$ is central. Finally $\omega_r(e_{(m)}) = \frac1m\sum_ih^{-i}h^i = 1$.

(b) $\omega_r(u_r) = \kappa\,\omega_r(e_h) - \omega_r(\sigma) = 0$. Let $\mathcal{I} = \ker(\Lambda_r \to \mathbb{Z}/p^r)$ be the augmentation ideal. It is the closure of the span of the $\rho - 1$, $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, because at each finite level the augmentation ideal is spanned by these. Let $J$ be the closure of $(\sigma - 1)\Lambda_r + (\tau - 1)\Lambda_r$. The set of $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ with $\rho - 1 \in J$ is closed, contains $\sigma$ and $\tau$, and is a subgroup, since $\rho\rho' - 1 = (\rho - 1)\rho' + (\rho' - 1)$ and $\rho^{-1} - 1 = -(\rho - 1)\rho^{-1}$. So it is $\mathcal{G}$, and $\mathcal{I} = J$. In the same way, with $\rho\rho' - 1 = \rho(\rho' - 1) + (\rho - 1)$, $\mathcal{I}$ is the closure of $\Lambda_r(\sigma - 1) + \Lambda_r(\tau - 1)$. The continuous ring automorphism $T$ of $\Lambda_r$ with $T(\rho) = \omega_r(\rho)^{-1}\rho$ satisfies $\epsilon \circ T^{-1} = \omega_r$, where $\epsilon$ is the augmentation, so $\ker\omega_r = T(\mathcal{I})$ is the closure of $(\kappa^{-1}\sigma - 1)\Lambda_r + (h^{-1}\tau - 1)\Lambda_r$, and also of the corresponding left ideal; here $\kappa^{-1}, h^{-1}$ are inverses in $(\mathbb{Z}/p^r)^\times$. Let $x \in \ker\omega_r$ and write $x = xe_h + x(1 - e_h)$.

- As $e_h$ is central and $(h^{-1}\tau - 1)e_h = 0$, $xe_h = e_hx$ lies in the closure of $(\kappa^{-1}\sigma - 1)e_h\Lambda_r$, which is compact, hence closed. Now $(\kappa^{-1}\sigma - 1)e_h = \kappa^{-1}(\sigma - \kappa)e_h = -\kappa^{-1}u_re_h$. So $xe_h \in u_r\Lambda_r$, and likewise $xe_h \in \Lambda_r u_r$.
- $u_r(1 - e_h) = -\sigma(1 - e_h)$, and $u_r$, $\sigma^{-1}$, $e_h$ commute, so $1 - e_h = u_r\cdot(-\sigma^{-1}(1 - e_h)) = (-\sigma^{-1}(1 - e_h))\cdot u_r$. Hence $x(1 - e_h) = (1 - e_h)x \in u_r\Lambda_r$ and $x(1 - e_h) \in \Lambda_r u_r$.

So $\ker\omega_r \subseteq u_r\Lambda_r \cap \Lambda_r u_r$, and the reverse inclusions hold because $u_r \in \ker\omega_r$, a two-sided ideal.

(c) The set $u_rM$ is a closed subgroup of $M$, being the image of the compact group $M$. For $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ and $m \in M$, $\rho m - \omega_r(\rho)m = (\rho - \omega_r(\rho))m \in (\ker\omega_r)M \subseteq u_r\Lambda_r M = u_rM$ by (b). So for $\rho, \rho' \in \mathcal{G}$,
$$b(\rho x, \rho' y) = \rho\, b(x, \rho^{-1}\rho' y) \equiv \omega_r(\rho)\, b(x, \rho^{-1}\rho' y) = b(x, \rho^*\rho' y) \pmod{u_rM}.$$
Both $b(\lambda x, \mu y)$ and $b(x, \lambda^*\mu y)$ are continuous and biadditive in $(\lambda, \mu)$, and $\mathbb{Z}/p^r$-bilinear, as $N$ and $M$ are $\mathbb{Z}/p^r$-modules; they agree modulo the closed subgroup $u_rM$ on $\mathcal{G} \times \mathcal{G}$, whose span is dense in $\Lambda_r \times \Lambda_r$. $\square$

**Computation.** This is proved here. Let $1 \le r \le s$. Let $N \subseteq P$ be a closed normal subgroup of $F$, put $Q = P/N$, and let $A \subseteq Q$ be a closed subgroup, normal in $F/N$, central in $Q$ and of exponent dividing $p^r$. Write $A$ additively, as a compact $\Lambda_r$-module with $\rho a = \rho a\rho^{-1}$. Then for $X \in Q$ and $a \in A$, in $F/N$,
$$\big((Xa)^\sigma\big)^{-1}(Xa, \tau)^\kappa = \big(X^\sigma\big)^{-1}(X, \tau)^\kappa\cdot u_ra,$$
where $(Y, \tau)$ is defined for $Y \in Q$ by the formula of the Setting. For $r = 1$, $A$ has exponent $p$ and the correction is $ua$.

*Proof.* As $a$ is central in $Q$, $(Xa)^\sigma = X^\sigma\cdot\sigma a$, so $((Xa)^\sigma)^{-1} = (X^\sigma)^{-1}\cdot(-\sigma a)$. If $W \in F/N$ has image $\tau^j$ in $\mathcal{G}$, write $W = w\tau^j$ with $w \in Q$; then $aW = wa\tau^j = W\cdot\tau^{-j}a$. Let $Y_a = (Xa)^{h^{p-1}}\tau(Xa)^{h^{p-2}}\tau\cdots(Xa)^h\tau$, so that $Y_0$ is the element whose $\pi/(p-1)$-th power is $(X, \tau)$. Since $(Xa)^{h^j} = X^{h^j}\cdot h^j a$, as $a$ commutes with $X$ and $A$ is a $\mathbb{Z}/p^r$-module, and the factor $(Xa)^{h^j}$ is followed by a word with image $\tau^j$, moving every term $h^j a$ to the right gives $Y_a = Y_0\cdot b'a$ with $b' = \sum_{j=1}^{p-1}h^j\tau^{-j}$. As $Y_0$ has image $\tau^{p-1}$, for every integer $n \ge 1$
$$(Y_0a')^n = Y_0^n\cdot S_na',\qquad S_n = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\tau^{-(p-1)i}\qquad (a' \in A).$$
Let $B$ be an open normal subgroup of $F/N$, and let $e = p^\alpha e'$, with $p \nmid e'$, be the exponent of $(F/N)/B$. The procyclic group $I$, of order prime to $p$, acts on the finite module $A/(A \cap B)$ through $I/I_{m'}$ for some $m'$ prime to $p$; enlarging $m'$, we may assume $(p - 1)e' \mid m'$. Choose an integer $n \ge 1$ with $m' \mid n$ and $n(p-1) \equiv 1 \pmod{p^{\alpha+r}}$. Then $n \equiv \pi/(p-1)$ modulo $e$, so $g^n \equiv g^{\pi/(p-1)}$ modulo $B$ for every $g \in F/N$. In $(\mathbb{Z}/p^r)[I/I_{m'}]$, $\tau^{p-1}$ has order $k = m'/(p-1)$, which divides $n$, so $S_n = \frac nk\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\tau^{-(p-1)i} = (p-1)^{-1}E_{m'}$ with $E_{m'} = \frac1k\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\tau^{(p-1)i}$, as $n \equiv (p-1)^{-1} \pmod {p^r}$. Moreover
$$E_{m'}b' = \frac1k\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}h^j\tau^{(p-1)i - j} = \frac1k\sum_{c \in \mathbb{Z}/m'}h^{-c}\tau^c = (p-1)\,e_{(m')},$$
because $(i, j) \mapsto (p-1)i - j$ is a bijection onto $\mathbb{Z}/m'$ and $h^{j} = h^{-((p-1)i-j)}$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p^r$, as $h^{p-1} \equiv 1$. So modulo $B$, $(X a, \tau) \equiv (Y_0 b'a)^n = Y_0^n\cdot S_nb'a \equiv (X, \tau)\cdot e_ha$. As $B$ is arbitrary, $(Xa, \tau) = (X, \tau)\cdot e_ha$. The element $(X, \tau)$ lies in $Q$, its image in $\mathcal{G}$ being $\tau^{\pi} = 1$ as $\tau$ has order prime to $p$, so $(Xa, \tau)^\kappa = (X, \tau)^\kappa\cdot\kappa e_ha$, with $\kappa$ acting on $A$ through $\mathbb{Z}/p^r$. Multiplying the two factors gives the claim, since $u_ra = \kappa e_ha - \sigma a$. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** *$\bar P$ is free.* $P$ is the closed normal subgroup of $F = P \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ generated by the $x_i$, and $P$ acts trivially on $\bar P$, so the closed $\Lambda$-submodule generated by the $\bar x_i$ is $\bar P$, and $\Lambda^{d+1} \to \bar P$, $e_i \mapsto \bar x_i$, is onto (its image is compact). For an open normal subgroup $U$ of $\mathcal{G}$, let $\mathcal{G}$ act on $\mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]^{d+1}$ by left multiplication. By the universal property of $F$ there is a homomorphism $F \to \mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]^{d+1} \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and maps $x_i$ to the $i$-th basis vector $e_i$. On $P$ it is a $\mathcal{G}$-equivariant homomorphism to an elementary abelian group, so it factors through a $\Lambda$-linear map $\bar P \to \mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]^{d+1}$ with $\bar x_i \mapsto e_i$. So the composite $\Lambda^{d+1} \to \bar P \to \mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}/U]^{d+1}$ is the reduction, and as $U$ varies these reductions separate the points of $\Lambda^{d+1}$. Hence $\Lambda^{d+1} \to \bar P$ is injective.

*The relation modulo the Frattini subgroup.* Apply the Computation with $r = 1$, $N = P^{(2)}$, $A = Q = \bar P$, $X = 1$ and $a = \bar x_0$. Since $(1, \tau) = (\tau^{p-1})^{\pi/(p-1)} = \tau^\pi = 1$, and $x_1^{p^s}$ and $C$ lie in $P^{(2)}$ (for odd $d$, because $y_1 \in P$, so $[x_1, y_1] \in [P, P]$), the image of $\varrho$ in $\bar P$ is $\bar\varrho = u\bar x_0$.

*$\bar V$.* As $(\varrho) \subseteq P$, $P_K = P/(\varrho)$, and the image of $P^{(i)}$ in $P_K$ is $P_K^{(i)}$, because a continuous surjection of compact groups maps the closed subgroup generated by a set onto the closed subgroup generated by its image. So $\bar V$ is the quotient of $\bar P$ by the image of $(\varrho)$, which is the closed $\Lambda$-submodule generated by $\bar\varrho$, as $(\varrho)$ is topologically generated by the conjugates $f\varrho f^{-1}$, $f \in F$. Hence $\bar V = \Lambda\bar x_0/\Lambda u\bar x_0 \oplus \bigoplus_{i \ge 1}\Lambda\bar x_i$, with $\Lambda\bar x_0/\Lambda u\bar x_0 \cong \Lambda/\Lambda u = \Lambda/\ker\omega \cong \mathbb{F}_p$, on which $\rho$ acts as $\omega(\rho)$, by Lemma (b). $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $N_s = P/P^{p,2,s}$ and $M_s = P^{p,2,s}/P^{p,3,s}$, written additively. Since $P^{p,2,s}$ contains $[P, P]$ and $P^{p^s}$, $N_s$ is abelian of exponent dividing $p^s$. Since $[P^{p,2,s}, P] \subseteq P^{p,3,s}$ and $(P^{p,2,s})^{p^s} \subseteq P^{p,3,s}$, $M_s$ is central in $P/P^{p,3,s}$ and has exponent dividing $p^s$. Both are compact $\Lambda_s$-modules under conjugation, as the $P^{p,i,s}$ are characteristic in $P$, hence normal in $F$, and $P$ acts trivially. Write $\tilde x$ for the class of $x \in P$ in $N_s$. As $P^{p,2,s} \subseteq P^{(2)}$, there is a reduction map $N_s \to \bar P$, $\tilde x \mapsto \bar x$, and it maps $\lambda\tilde x$ to $\bar\lambda\bar x$, where $\bar\lambda$ is the image of $\lambda \in \Lambda_s$ in $\Lambda$. The commutator induces a map $b : N_s \times N_s \to M_s$, $b(\tilde x, \tilde y) = [x, y]P^{p,3,s}$. It is well defined and biadditive because $[xw, y] = x[w, y]x^{-1}[x, y]$ with $[w, y] \in P^{p,3,s}$ for $w \in P^{p,2,s}$, and $[xx', y] = x[x', y]x^{-1}[x, y]$ with $x[x', y]x^{-1} \equiv [x', y]$, as $M_s$ is central; the second variable is analogous. It is continuous, and $\rho[x, y]\rho^{-1} = [\rho x\rho^{-1}, \rho y\rho^{-1}]$.

Choose $\lambda_s \in \Lambda_s$ with $\bar\lambda_s = \lambda$. It is a unit. Indeed, if $\mu_s$ lifts $\lambda^{-1}$, then $\lambda_s\mu_s = 1 - p\nu$ and $\mu_s\lambda_s = 1 - p\nu'$ for some $\nu, \nu' \in \Lambda_s$, and $1 - p\nu$ and $1 - p\nu'$ are units with inverses $\sum_{j < s}(p\nu)^j$ and $\sum_{j < s}(p\nu')^j$, as $p^s = 0$ in $\Lambda_s$; so $\lambda_s$ has a right and a left inverse. Also $\overline{(\lambda_s^*)^{-1}} = (\lambda^*)^{-1}$, as reduction is compatible with $*$. Choose $y_s, y_t \in P$ with $\tilde y_s = \lambda_s\tilde x_s$ and $\tilde y_t = (\lambda_s^*)^{-1}\tilde x_t$, so that $\bar y_s = \lambda\bar x_s$ and $\bar y_t = (\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$. Put $c = b(\tilde y_s, \tilde y_t) - b(\tilde x_s, \tilde x_t)$. By Lemma (c) with $r = s$, $N = N_s$, $M = M_s$ and $\mu = (\lambda_s^*)^{-1}$, $c \equiv b(\tilde x_s, \lambda_s^*(\lambda_s^*)^{-1}\tilde x_t) - b(\tilde x_s, \tilde x_t) = 0$ modulo $u_sM_s$. Choose $z \in P^{p,2,s}$ whose class $w \in M_s$ satisfies $u_sw = -c$. Let $\psi$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, maps $x_0 \mapsto x_0z$, $x_s \mapsto y_s$, $x_t \mapsto y_t$, and fixes the other $x_i$.

*$\psi(\varrho) \equiv \varrho$ modulo $P^{p,3,s}$.* $\psi$ fixes $x_1$, and $x_2$ for even $d$, and $y_1$ for odd $d$, so $\psi(\varrho) = ((x_0z)^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0z, \tau)^\kappa x_1^{p^s}C'$, where $C'$ is $C$ with $[x_s, x_t]$ replaced by $[y_s, y_t]$. In $P/P^{p,3,s}$, all factors of $C$ and $C'$ are commutators of elements of $P$, so they lie in $M_s$, and $C' = C + c$. By the Computation with $r = s$, $N = P^{p,3,s}$, $A = M_s$, $X = x_0P^{p,3,s}$ and $a = w$, the first two factors become $(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0, \tau)^\kappa\cdot u_sw$. As $M_s$ is central in $P/P^{p,3,s}$ and all factors lie in $P$, $\psi(\varrho) \equiv \varrho\cdot(u_sw + c) = \varrho$.

*An automorphism of $G/P_K^{p,3,s}$.* As $(\varrho) \subseteq P$, $P_K = P/(\varrho)$, and the image of $P^{p,3,s}$ in $P_K$ is $P_K^{p,3,s}$, because a continuous surjection of compact groups maps the closed subgroup generated by a set onto the closed subgroup generated by its image. So the inverse image of $P_K^{p,3,s}$ in $F$ is $(\varrho)P^{p,3,s}$, and $G/P_K^{p,3,s} = F/(\varrho)P^{p,3,s}$. Since $\psi$ fixes $\mathcal{G}$ and maps each $x_i$ into the normal subgroup $P$, $\psi(P) \subseteq P$, so also $\psi(P^{p,3,s}) \subseteq P^{p,3,s}$, and $\psi(f\varrho f^{-1}) = \psi(f)\psi(\varrho)\psi(f)^{-1} \in (\varrho)P^{p,3,s}$, so $\psi$ maps $(\varrho)P^{p,3,s}$ into itself and induces an endomorphism $\psi_3$ of $G/P_K^{p,3,s}$. The image $\psi(P)$ is a closed subgroup of $P$, stable under $\mathcal{G}$, whose image in $\bar P$ is a closed $\Lambda$-submodule containing $\bar x_0$, the $\bar x_i$ with $i \notin \{s, t\}$, $\lambda\bar x_s$ and $(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$, hence all of $\bar P$. So $\psi(P)P^{(2)} = P$, hence $\psi(P) = P$, as $P^{(2)}$ is the Frattini subgroup of $P$, and $\psi$ and $\psi_3$ are onto. A continuous surjective endomorphism $\phi$ of a topologically finitely generated profinite group $H$ is injective: $H$ has only finitely many open subgroups of each index $n$, $U \mapsto \phi^{-1}(U)$ maps them injectively, hence bijectively, to themselves, so every open subgroup contains $\ker\phi$, and their intersection is trivial. As $G/P_K^{p,3,s}$ is topologically generated by the images of $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_d$, $\psi_3$ is an automorphism. It fixes the images of $\sigma$ and $\tau$, so it induces the identity on $G/P_K = \Gamma$.

*The lift.* By [Wingberg's lifting theorem for the $p^s$-central series](wingberg-lifting-for-the-p-s-central-series-of-wild-inertia.md), there is an automorphism $\theta_\lambda$ of $G$, inducing the identity on $\Gamma$, that coincides with $\psi_3$ modulo $P_K^{p,2,s}$, and hence modulo $P_K^{(2)} \supseteq P_K^{p,2,s}$. It has the stated properties, because $\psi$ fixes $\mathcal{G}$ and the $x_i$ with $i \notin \{0, s, t\}$, $\psi(x_0) = x_0z$ with $z \in P^{p,2,s} \subseteq P^{(2)}$, and $\bar y_s = \lambda\bar x_s$, $\bar y_t = (\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** $G/P_K^{(2)}$ is generated by $\bar V$ and the image of $\mathcal{G}$, which $\bar\psi_\lambda$ fixes, so $\bar\psi_\lambda$ commutes with conjugation by $\mathcal{G}$ and is $\Lambda$-linear on $\bar V$. By part 2 it fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i \notin \{s, t\}$, and maps $\bar x_s \mapsto \lambda\bar x_s$ and $\bar x_t \mapsto (\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$; by $\Lambda$-linearity and part 1 this determines it on $\bar V$, and hence on $G/P_K^{(2)}$. In particular $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu(\bar x_s) = \bar\psi_\lambda(\mu\bar x_s) = \mu\lambda\bar x_s$ and $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu(\bar x_t) = (\mu^*)^{-1}(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t = ((\mu\lambda)^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$, so $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu = \bar\psi_{\mu\lambda}$.

*Trivial centre.* Every element of $\Gamma$ is uniquely $\tau^x\sigma^y$ with $x \in \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ and $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, since $y \mapsto \sigma^yI$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \Gamma/I = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ur}}/K)$, the image of $\sigma$ being a Frobenius. If $\tau^x\sigma^y$ is central, then $\tau = \sigma^y\tau\sigma^{-y} = \tau^{q^y}$, so $y = 0$, because $y \mapsto (x \mapsto q^y x)$ is injective from $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ to the automorphisms of $\prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.4) (ii), p. 412]. Then $\tau^x = \sigma\tau^x\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{qx}$, so $(q - 1)x = 0$ and $x = 0$, as $\prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is torsion-free.

*Not inner.* If $\theta_\lambda$ is conjugation by $g \in G$, then conjugation by the image of $g$ in $\Gamma$ is the identity, so $g \in P_K$. But conjugation by $g \in P_K$ is trivial on $\bar V$, while $\theta_\lambda$ maps $\bar x_s$ to $\lambda\bar x_s$, which differs from $\bar x_s$ for $\lambda \ne 1$, since $\Lambda\bar x_s$ is free. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Comparison with $\mathbb{Q}_p$.* For $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$, Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg twist $x_1$, which also occurs in $x_1^{p}$ and in $y_1$, by a central unit $\varepsilon$, and check that the Demuškin relation of $G_{K'}(p)$ is preserved modulo the third term for every finite tamely ramified $K'/\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 422–423]; see [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on norm-one units](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md). Here the change of $x_0$ by $z$ absorbs the defect $c$ directly in $F$, so the relator itself is preserved modulo $P^{p,3,s}$. This works because $u_s$, the coefficient with which $x_0$ enters the relation modulo commutators and $p^s$-th powers, generates $\ker\omega_s$ (Lemma (b)), and the defect of the twisted commutator lies in $(\ker\omega_s)M_s$ (Lemma (c)).
- *Why the $p^s$-central series.* The lifting theorem used is stated for the $p^s$-central series of $P_K$, so the relator has to be preserved modulo $P^{p,3,s}$. The quotient $M_s = P^{p,2,s}/P^{p,3,s}$ has exponent dividing $p^s$, not $p$, and this is why the Lemma and the Computation are proved with coefficients $\mathbb{Z}/p^r$. For $s = 1$, $P^{p,i,1} = P^{(i)}$, $\Lambda_1 = \Lambda$ and $u_1 = u$. The factor $x_1^{p^s}$ is fixed by $\psi$, so it imposes no condition on $\lambda$.
- *A finer statement.* The proof shows more than part 2 states: for every unit $\lambda_s \in \Lambda_s^\times$ with image $\lambda$ in $\Lambda$, $\theta_\lambda$ can be chosen so that, in $P_K/P_K^{p,2,s}$, it maps the class of $x_s$ to $\lambda_s$ times it and the class of $x_t$ to $(\lambda_s^*)^{-1}$ times it, fixes the classes of the $x_i$ with $i \notin \{s, t\}$, and induces the identity on $G/P_K$.
- *What is not claimed.* Only the class of $\theta_\lambda$ modulo $P_K^{p,2,s}$ is controlled; the lifting theorem gives no information on $\theta_\lambda$ modulo $P_K^{p,3,s}$, and $\lambda \mapsto \theta_\lambda$ is not claimed to be a homomorphism into $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$. For $d \le 2$ there is no surface pair, and the construction is not carried out here.
- *Taken from the sources without re-verification.* The Jannsen–Wingberg presentation. The lifting step is proved in [the cited entry](wingberg-lifting-for-the-p-s-central-series-of-wild-inertia.md) from Wingberg's Lemma 7, through [the one-step lifting criterion](one-step-lifting-criterion-for-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg attribute the lifting theorem to Wingberg, whose own statement of it was incorrect [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15) and the remark after it, p. 421]; see [uniqueness of Demuškin formations](uniqueness-of-demuskin-formations.md) and [presentations](local-galois-group-presentations.md).
