**Setting.** Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field of characteristic $0$, with valuation ring $\mathcal{O}_K$ and perfect residue field $k$ of characteristic exponent $p \ge 1$; for instance, a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Fix an algebraic closure $\bar K$. Let $K^{\mathrm{ur}} \subset \bar K$ be the maximal unramified extension, with valuation ring $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$, and let $I = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar K/K^{\mathrm{ur}}) \subseteq G_K$ be the inertia group. Let $\ell \ne p$ be a prime. Let $X = Y \setminus D$ be a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) of type $(g, n)$ over $K$: $Y$ is smooth, proper and geometrically connected of genus $g$, $D \subset Y$ is a reduced divisor with $n$ geometric points, the *cusps*, and $2g - 2 + n > 0$. Let $J$ be the Jacobian of $Y$, and write $\Delta_X = \pi_1(X_{\bar K})$ and $\Delta_Y = \pi_1(Y_{\bar K})$.

**Convention.** Following Mochizuki, $X$ has *stable reduction* if $Y$, marked by its cusps, extends to a stable pointed curve over $\mathcal{O}_K$, and the *stable model* of $X$ is the complement of the marked points in it [@mochizuki2004absolute, §2, p. 23]. The cusps need not be $K$-rational, so this is spelled out here with Knudsen's conditions [@knudsen1983, Definition 1.1, p. 162]. A *stable model* of $(Y, D)$ is a proper flat $\mathcal{O}_K$-scheme $\mathcal{Y}$ with a closed subscheme $\mathcal{D}$, finite étale over $\mathcal{O}_K$, and an isomorphism $(\mathcal{Y}_K, \mathcal{D}_K) \cong (Y, D)$, such that for every geometric point $\bar s$ of $\mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}_K$:

- $\mathcal{Y}_{\bar s}$ is reduced and connected, with at most ordinary double points;
- the $n$ points of $\mathcal{D}_{\bar s}$ are distinct smooth points of $\mathcal{Y}_{\bar s}$;
- each nonsingular rational component of $\mathcal{Y}_{\bar s}$ has at least $3$ points that are marked or where it meets the other components;
- $\dim H^1(\mathcal{Y}_{\bar s}, \mathcal{O}) = g$.

If $\mathcal{D}$ is a disjoint union of $n$ sections, this is an $n$-pointed stable curve in Knudsen's sense, and for $n = 0$ it is a stable curve [@knudsen1983, p. 162].

**Theorem.** The following are equivalent.

1. $X$ has stable reduction.
2. $I$ acts trivially on the set $D(\bar K)$ of cusps, and unipotently on $T_\ell J$.
3. $I$ acts trivially on the set of $\Delta_X$-conjugacy classes of inertia subgroups of cusps in $\Delta_X$, and unipotently on $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$.

For $g = 0$ the unipotence conditions are empty, and for $n = 0$ the cusp conditions are. The unipotence condition holds for one prime $\ell \ne p$ exactly when it holds for all, since by Step 0 below and [@hallenicaise2012, Theorem 3.6.5 (1), p. 9] it says that $J_{\breve K}$ has semi-abelian reduction. Each $\sigma \in I$ then satisfies $(\sigma - 1)^{2g} = 0$ on every $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$, by Cayley–Hamilton, so $I$ also acts unipotently on $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, where $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$. For $K$ finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, this form of condition 3 is the "well-known stable reduction criterion" that Mochizuki uses, without a reference, to show that the arithmetic fundamental group of $X$ determines whether $X$ has stable reduction [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 2.1 and its proof, pp. 22–23]; see [reconstruction of logarithmic special fibres](reconstruction-of-log-special-fibres-of-p-adic-curves.md).

*Example.* Let $\pi$ be a uniformizer of $K$. For every $a \in K \setminus \{0, 1\}$, the curve $\mathbb{P}^1_K \setminus \{0, 1, \infty, a\}$ has stable reduction, since its genus is $0$ and its cusps are $K$-rational. The curve $\mathbb{P}^1_K \setminus \{0, \infty, \sqrt{\pi}, -\sqrt{\pi}\}$ does not, because $K(\sqrt{\pi})/K$ is totally ramified, so inertia swaps the cusps $\pm\sqrt{\pi}$.

**Proof.** This is proved here from the cited results.

*Step 0: fields.* The ring $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is the union of the valuation rings of the finite unramified extensions of $K$ in $\bar K$. These share a uniformizer of $K$, and their residue fields run through the finite extensions of $k$ [@saitohenselianexcerpt, Proposition 1.5.3, p. 15]. So $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is a discrete valuation ring whose residue field $\bar k$ is algebraically closed, as $k$ is perfect. It is henselian. Indeed, a monic polynomial over $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$ with a simple root modulo the maximal ideal has its coefficients in the valuation ring of some finite unramified extension $K'$ of $K$ whose residue field contains that root, and the root lifts there because $K'$ is complete. Let $\breve K$ be the completion of $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$, with valuation ring $\breve{\mathcal{O}}$ and residue field $\bar k$, and embed $\bar K$ into an algebraic closure of $\breve K$. Restriction is an isomorphism $G_{\breve K} \to G_{K^{\mathrm{ur}}} = I$, because a henselian discrete valuation field and its completion have the same absolute Galois group [@saitohenselianexcerpt, Proposition 1.4.7 and its proof, pp. 13–14]. As $J[\ell^m]$ and $D$ are finite étale over $K$, the sets $J[\ell^m](\bar K)$ and $D(\bar K)$ stay the same when $\bar K$ is replaced by an algebraic closure of $\breve K$, compatibly with this isomorphism.

*Step 1: cusps.* Suppose that $(\mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{D})$ is a stable model. Since $\mathcal{O}_K$ is henselian, the finite étale $\mathcal{O}_K$-algebra of $\mathcal{D}$ is a product of local rings [@saitohenselianexcerpt, proof of Lemma 1.5.2, p. 15]. Each is a discrete valuation ring étale over $\mathcal{O}_K$ [@saitohenselianexcerpt, Corollary 1.2.4, p. 8], hence the valuation ring of a finite unramified extension of $K$ [@saitohenselianexcerpt, Lemma 1.5.2 (1), p. 15]. So the residue fields of the points of $D = \mathcal{D}_K$ are unramified over $K$, their $K$-embeddings into $\bar K$ land in $K^{\mathrm{ur}} = \bar K^{I}$, and $I$ fixes $D(\bar K)$ pointwise. Conversely, if $I$ fixes $D(\bar K)$ pointwise, these embeddings land in $\bar K^I = K^{\mathrm{ur}}$, so every cusp is rational over a finite unramified extension of $K$.

*Step 2: genus one.* Let $F$ be a complete discretely valued field with valuation ring $R$ and algebraically closed residue field $\kappa$, and let $E$ be a smooth proper geometrically connected curve of genus $1$ over $F$ with $E(F) \ne \emptyset$. Then $E$ has semistable reduction, in the sense of [@stacks2026, Tag 0CDH], if and only if $G_F$ acts unipotently on $T_\ell \operatorname{Jac}(E)$.

By [@hallenicaise2012, Theorem 3.6.5 (1), p. 9], the second condition says that $\operatorname{Jac}(E)$ has semi-abelian reduction, that is, the identity component $\mathcal{J}^0_\kappa$ of the special fibre of its Néron model has no unipotent radical [@hallenicaise2012, (3.2.2), p. 7, and (3.6.2), p. 9]. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the minimal model of $E$ over $R$ [@stacks2026, Tag 0C2W]. A point of $E(F)$ extends to a section of $\mathcal{C}$, which passes through a component of $\mathcal{C}_\kappa$ of multiplicity one because $\mathcal{C}$ is regular [@delignemumford1969, proof of Theorem 2.4, p. 89]. As $E(F) \ne \emptyset$, $E$ has index one, and Raynaud's theorem gives $\mathcal{J}^0 \cong \mathrm{Pic}^0_{\mathcal{C}/R}$ [@hallenicaise2012, (4.3.1), p. 11], hence $\mathcal{J}^0_\kappa \cong \mathrm{Pic}^0(\mathcal{C}_\kappa/\kappa)$, as in [@delignemumford1969, proof of Theorem 2.4, pp. 89–90]. If $E$ has semistable reduction, then $\mathcal{C}$ is at-worst-nodal [@stacks2026, Tag 0CDG], so this generalized Jacobian is an extension of an abelian variety by a torus [@delignemumford1969, proof of Theorem 2.4, p. 89]. Conversely, if it has no unipotent radical, the argument of [@delignemumford1969, proof of Theorem 2.4, pp. 90–91], through their Lemma 2.6 and intersection numbers on $\mathcal{C}$, shows that $\mathcal{C}_\kappa$ is reduced with at most ordinary double points, so $E$ has semistable reduction [@stacks2026, Tag 0CDG].

Deligne and Mumford work under the standing assumption $g \ge 2$ [@delignemumford1969, §2, p. 87], but the steps used here do not need it. They use that $\mathcal{C}$ is a minimal regular model with a section, Raynaud's theorem, which Halle and Nicaise state in every genus, and the adjunction formula for divisors supported on $\mathcal{C}_\kappa$. In their §2 the assumption enters through Proposition 2.3 and the descent at the end of the proof of Theorem 2.4, and neither is used here.

*Step 3: 1 implies 2.* Let $(\mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{D})$ be a stable model. Step 1 gives the cusp condition. Let $g \ge 1$. The base change $\mathcal{Y}_{\breve{\mathcal{O}}}$ is a proper model of $Y_{\breve K}$ with the same geometric special fibre as $\mathcal{Y}$, so it is at-worst-nodal, and $Y_{\breve K}$ has semistable reduction [@stacks2026, Tags 0CDG and 0CDH]. If $g \ge 2$, then $Y_{\breve K}$ has a stable model over $\breve{\mathcal{O}}$ [@stacks2026, Tag 0E8D]. So $G_{\breve K}$ acts unipotently on $T_\ell J$, by part (a) of the consequence in [stable reduction of curves](stable-reduction-of-curves.md). If $g = 1$, then $n \ge 1$, the cusps are $\breve K$-points by Step 1, and Step 2 gives the same conclusion. By Step 0, $I$ acts unipotently on $T_\ell J$.

*Step 4: 2 implies 1 over $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$.* Assume condition 2. By Step 1, the cusps are $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$-points.

First, $Y_{K^{\mathrm{ur}}}$ has a projective semistable model over $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$, one whose geometric fibres are reduced with at most ordinary double points. If $g = 0$, then $n \ge 3$, so $Y_{K^{\mathrm{ur}}}$ has a rational point and $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}}$ is such a model [@stacks2026, Tag 0CDK]. If $g \ge 1$, then $G_{\breve K}$ acts unipotently on $T_\ell J$ by Step 0. For $g \ge 2$, $Y_{\breve K}$ then has a stable model, by part (a) of the consequence in stable reduction of curves; for $g = 1$, Step 2 shows that it has semistable reduction, as $n \ge 1$ and the cusps are $\breve K$-points. In both cases the minimal model of $Y_{\breve K}$ over $\breve{\mathcal{O}}$ is at-worst-nodal [@stacks2026, Tag 0CDG] and projective [@stacks2026, proof of Tag 0CD9]. For a smooth geometrically connected projective curve over the fraction field of a discrete valuation ring, every semistable model over the completion is defined over the ring itself [@liu2006finitecovers, Corollary 1.6, p. 5]. Applied to $Y_{K^{\mathrm{ur}}}$ and $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$, this gives a model of $Y_{K^{\mathrm{ur}}}$ over $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$ with the same geometric fibres.

Second, if a curve has semistable reduction over a Dedekind scheme and $M$ is a set of rational points with $2g - 2 + |M| \ge 1$, then it has a *stable marked model* [@liu2006finitecovers, Remark 2.21, pp. 10–11]. In Liu's terms this is a marked model, that is, a projective semistable model in which the closures of the points of $M$ are disjoint sections in the smooth locus, and it is stable: its geometric fibres are connected, and each irreducible component $\Gamma$ of a geometric fibre meets the other components in at least $1 - (2p_a(\Gamma) - 2) - |M \cap \Gamma|$ points [@liu2006finitecovers, 2.19, p. 10]. Liu characterizes it by $\deg \omega(\mathcal{M})|_\Gamma > 0$ on the components $\Gamma$ of the special fibre, where $\mathcal{M}$ is the closure of $M$; over an algebraically closed residue field this is the ampleness of $\omega(\mathcal{M})$ to which his stability condition amounts. Take $M = D(K^{\mathrm{ur}})$. For a nonsingular rational component the inequality is the third condition of the convention. The fourth holds because the Euler characteristic of $\mathcal{O}$ on the fibres is constant [@stacks2026, Tag 0B9T], and $H^0$ of a connected reduced proper curve over an algebraically closed field is that field. So this gives a stable model $\mathcal{W}$ of $(Y, D)_{K^{\mathrm{ur}}}$ whose cusps are $n$ sections.

*Step 5: descent to $\mathcal{O}_K$.*

- *Uniqueness.* Let $C$ and $C'$ be $n$-pointed stable curves over a discrete valuation ring $\mathcal{O}'$. The moduli stack of $n$-pointed stable curves of genus $g$ is proper, in particular separated [@knudsen1983, Theorem 2.7, p. 179]. That is, the scheme of isomorphisms from $C$ to $C'$ is proper, equivalently finite, over $\mathcal{O}'$ [@delignemumford1969, Definition 4.7, p. 99]. By the valuative criterion, every isomorphism of the generic fibres that respects the sections extends uniquely to an isomorphism over $\mathcal{O}'$.
- *A finite level.* $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is the directed union of the valuation rings of the finite Galois unramified extensions of $K$ in $\bar K$. By limit arguments [@stacks2026, Tags 01ZM, 01ZP, 04AI and 081E], there are such an extension $K'$, over which every cusp is rational, and a closed subscheme $\mathcal{W}'$ of a projective space over $\mathcal{O}' = \mathcal{O}_{K'}$, flat over $\mathcal{O}'$, with $n$ sections and an isomorphism of its generic fibre with $(Y, D)_{K'}$, whose base change to $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is $\mathcal{W}$ with its sections and generic identification. The geometric fibres of $\mathcal{W}'$ are those of $\mathcal{W}$, so $\mathcal{W}'$ is an $n$-pointed stable curve.
- *Galois descent.* Let $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(K'/K)$. Then $\mathcal{O}'$ is étale over $\mathcal{O}_K$ [@saitohenselianexcerpt, Lemma 1.5.2 (1), p. 15], so $\mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}' \to \mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}_K$ is finite locally free and surjective, and $\mathcal{O}' \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_K} \mathcal{O}' \cong \prod_{\gamma \in \Gamma} \mathcal{O}'$. For $\gamma \in \Gamma$, the pullback $\gamma^* \mathcal{W}'$ along $\gamma : \mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}' \to \mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}'$ is again an $n$-pointed stable curve. Its generic fibre is identified with $Y_{K'}$, since $Y$ is defined over $K$, with the sections permuted as $\gamma$ permutes $D(K')$. By uniqueness there is a unique isomorphism $\varphi_\gamma : \gamma^* \mathcal{W}' \to \mathcal{W}'$ extending the identity of $Y_{K'}$. Uniqueness also gives $\varphi_{\gamma\delta} = \varphi_\gamma \circ \gamma^* \varphi_\delta$, so the $\varphi_\gamma$ form a descent datum. It is effective because $\mathcal{W}'$ is projective over $\mathcal{O}'$ [@stacks2026, Tag 0CCJ]. This gives an $\mathcal{O}_K$-scheme $\mathcal{Y}$ with $\mathcal{Y}_{\mathcal{O}'} \cong \mathcal{W}'$, and $\mathcal{Y}$ is proper and flat [@stacks2026, Tags 02L1 and 02L2]. The union of the sections is stable under the $\varphi_\gamma$, so it descends to a closed subscheme $\mathcal{D} \subseteq \mathcal{Y}$ [@stacks2026, Tag 03I0], which is finite étale over $\mathcal{O}_K$ [@stacks2026, Tags 02LA and 02VN]. The identification of generic fibres descends to $(\mathcal{Y}_K, \mathcal{D}_K) \cong (Y, D)$ [@stacks2026, Tag 02W0]. Geometric points of $\mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}_K$ lift to $\mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}'$, so $(\mathcal{Y}, \mathcal{D})$ has the geometric fibres of $\mathcal{W}'$, and it is a stable model of $(Y, D)$.

*Step 6: 2 is equivalent to 3.* For a cusp $x$, the inertia subgroups of $\Delta_X$ at the cusps of the universal covering over $x$ form one conjugacy class [@mochizuki2004absolute, §1.3, p. 18]. The map sending $x$ to this class is a bijection from $D(\bar K)$ onto the set of conjugacy classes of inertia subgroups of cusps [@mochizuki2004absolute, proof of Lemma 1.3.9, p. 20, and proof of Lemma 2.1, p. 23]. It is $G_K$-equivariant by transport of structure: an element of $\pi_1(X)$ over $\sigma \in G_K$ carries the inertia subgroups over $x$ to those over $\sigma(x)$. For the Tate modules, $H^1(Y_{\bar K}, \mu_{\ell^m})$ is canonically $\mathrm{Hom}(\Delta_Y, \mu_{\ell^m}(\bar K))$ [@milne2013lec, Example 11.3, p. 78], and is canonically $\mathrm{Pic}^0(Y_{\bar K})[\ell^m]$ through the Kummer sequence [@stacks2026, Tag 03RQ], which is $J[\ell^m](\bar K)$ [@milne2013lec, proof of Proposition 14.1, p. 90]. Both identifications are natural, hence $G_K$-equivariant. In the limit, $T_\ell J \cong \mathrm{Hom}(\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell, \mathbb{Z}_\ell(1))$ as $G_K$-modules, and $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is free of rank $2g$ by consequence 2 in [geometric fundamental groups of curves](geometric-fundamental-groups-of-curves.md). The group $I$ acts trivially on $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$: $x^{\ell^m} - 1$ has distinct roots in $\bar k$, which lift to $\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{ur}}$ because it is henselian. So as $I$-modules, $T_\ell J$ is the $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-dual of $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$. An element $\sigma$ acts on the dual by the transpose of $\sigma^{-1}$, which is unipotent exactly when $\sigma$ is. $\square$

**What this settles.** The "Anabelian form" paragraph of [stable reduction of curves](stable-reduction-of-curves.md) left three steps unchecked: the comparison of $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}}$ with Tate modules, which is Step 6; the identification of $G_{\breve K}$ with inertia, which is Step 0; and the descent from $\breve{\mathcal{O}}$ to $\mathcal{O}_K$, which is Steps 4 and 5. It also left the cusps and the compactifications of genus $0$ and $1$, which are Steps 1, 2 and 4. The case where $K$ has characteristic $p > 0$ is not treated here.
