**Finite reconstruction (proved here).** Let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension of nonarchimedean local fields with finite residue fields, with group $G$. Normalize each field's valuation to have value group $\mathbb Z$. For every subgroup $H\le G$, supply the integer
$$
\Delta(H)=v_K\bigl(\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal O_{L^H}/\mathcal O_K)\bigr).
$$
The finite group with this function on its subgroups determines all its lower and upper [ramification groups](ramification-filtration.md), as follows. Put
$$
I=\bigcap_{\Delta(H)=0}H,\qquad f=[G:I].\tag{1}
$$
Then $I$ is inertia and $f=f(L/K)$. For $g\ne1$, put $C=\langle g\rangle$, $m=|C|$, and compute
$$
i(g)=-\frac{1}{f\,\varphi(m)}
\sum_{D\le C}\mu([C:D])\,|D|\,\Delta(D),\tag{2}
$$
where $\mu$ and $\varphi$ are the integer Mobius and Euler functions. This gives
$$
i(g)=\min_{a\in\mathcal O_L}v_L(g(a)-a),\qquad
G_j=\{1\}\cup\{g\ne1:i(g)\ge j+1\}\quad(j\ge0),
$$
and $G_{-1}=G$. Herbrand's change of numbering then gives the upper groups. In particular, the discriminants must be attached to their subgroups; an unlabelled list is not the input.

**Proof.** Write $\mathfrak D_{M/F}$ for the different of a finite separable local extension and $\delta(M/F)=v_M(\mathfrak D_{M/F})$. We use the following checked facts: the discriminant is the ideal norm of the different [@sutherland2025different, Theorem 12.17, p. 6]; its vanishing exponent characterizes an unramified extension [@sutherland2025different, Theorem 12.19, p. 7]; differents multiply in towers [@sutherland2025different, Proposition 12.28, p. 10]; and for $\mathcal O_M=\mathcal O_F[\alpha]$, the different is generated by the derivative of the minimal polynomial at $\alpha$ [@sutherland2025different, Proposition 12.24, p. 9]. The norm of the maximal ideal of $M$ is the $f(M/F)$-th power of that of $F$: the quotient by that ideal has length $f(M/F)$ over $\mathcal O_F$. Hence the first fact gives
$$
v_F\bigl(\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal O_M/\mathcal O_F)\bigr)
=f(M/F)\delta(M/F).
$$

*Recover inertia.* Its fixed field $T=L^{G_0}$ is the largest unramified subextension, and $[T:K]=f(L/K)$ [@milne2020ant, Theorem 7.58(a), p. 131]. Thus $\Delta(H)=0$ exactly when $L^H\subseteq T$, equivalently $G_0\subseteq H$. The intersection in (1) equals $G_0$, since $H=G_0$ occurs in it.

*The different as a sum of displacements.* Set $i_G(g)=\min_{a\in\mathcal O_L}v_L(g(a)-a)$ for $g\ne1$. Outside inertia it is zero, by the definition of the residue action. For $g\in G_0\setminus\{1\}$ and a uniformizer $\pi$ of $L$, it equals $v_L(g\pi-\pi)$. Indeed, $\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_T[\pi]$ [@milne2020ant, Remark 7.56, p. 130], and $g$ fixes every coefficient, so each polynomial displacement is divisible by $g\pi-\pi$, with equality attained at $\pi$. If $P$ is the minimal polynomial of $\pi$ over $T$, then
$$
P'(\pi)=\prod_{g\in G_0\setminus\{1\}}(\pi-g\pi).
$$
The derivative formula and the tower $L/T/K$, with $T/K$ unramified, therefore give $\delta(L/K)=\sum_{g\ne1}i_G(g)$. Apply the same argument to the Galois extension $L/L^H$, whose group is $H$. Its displacement function is the restriction of $i_G$, since its definition uses only $\mathcal O_L$, $v_L$ and the automorphism. Consequently
$$
\delta(L/L^H)=\sum_{h\in H\setminus\{1\}}i_G(h).
$$
This applies to nonnormal $H$ as well.

*Normalize the tower identity.* For $E=L^H$, transitivity gives
$$
\delta(L/K)=\delta(L/E)+e(L/E)\delta(E/K).
$$
Multiply by $f=f(L/K)$. Since $f(L/K)=f(L/E)f(E/K)$ and $e(L/E)f(L/E)=|H|$, the preceding formulas yield
$$
\Delta(1)-|H|\Delta(H)
=f\sum_{h\in H\setminus\{1\}}i_G(h).\tag{3}
$$

*Invert on cyclic subgroups.* Generators of one cyclic subgroup have the same $i_G$: membership in each $G_j$ depends only on whether their cyclic subgroup lies in $G_j$. For a nontrivial cyclic $D$ write this common value as $i_D$. Partitioning a cyclic $C$ into the generators of its subgroups rewrites (3) as
$$
\Delta(1)-|C|\Delta(C)
=f\sum_{1\ne D\le C}\varphi(|D|)i_D.
$$
Ordinary Mobius inversion on the divisors of $m=|C|$ recovers $f\varphi(m)i_C$ by summing the left side over $D\le C$ with coefficient $\mu([C:D])$. For $m>1$ the coefficients sum to zero, cancelling $\Delta(1)$ and giving (2). This proves the reconstruction. $\square$

**A numerical geometricity criterion (proved here).** Let $K,K'$ be finite extensions of the same $\mathbb Q_p$. For an open subgroup $U\le G_K$ define
$$
\Delta_K(U)=v_K\bigl(\operatorname{disc}(\mathcal O_{\bar K^U}/\mathcal O_K)\bigr).
$$
An outer class of continuous isomorphisms $\alpha:G_K\xrightarrow{\sim}G_{K'}$ comes from a unique $\mathbb Q_p$-isomorphism of fields exactly when
$$
\Delta_K(U)=\Delta_{K'}(\alpha(U))\qquad\text{for every open }U\le G_K.\tag{4}
$$
This condition is independent of the representative of the outer isomorphism, since conjugate subgroups have isomorphic fixed fields over the base.

*Proof.* For any open normal $N\le G_K$, the subgroups of $G_K/N$ correspond to the open $U\supseteq N$. Condition (4) identifies all their discriminant data with those for $G_{K'}/\alpha(N)$. The finite reconstruction identifies their upper filtrations. Taking inverse limits gives preservation of the upper filtration on the absolute groups. [Mochizuki's local theorem](mochizuki-local-fields-theorem.md) supplies the unique field isomorphism [@mochizuki1997, Theorem 4.2]. Conversely, a field isomorphism preserves normalized valuations, rings of integers and trace pairings, hence every discriminant exponent in (4). $\square$

**Checks.** For an unramified extension every $\Delta(H)$ is zero, so (1) gives $I=1$ and (2) gives $i(g)=0$.

For a check with residue degree greater than one and nonnormal subgroups, let $L$ be the splitting field of $X^3-2$ over $K=\mathbb Q_2$. The cube roots of unity generate the unramified quadratic extension (their polynomial reduces to the irreducible $X^2+X+1$), and the cubic polynomial is Eisenstein there. Thus $G=S_3$, $I=C_3$ and $f=2$. Both the cubic field and $L$ over the unramified quadratic field have different exponent $2$, from the derivative $3\alpha^2$. Therefore
$$
\Delta(1)=4,\qquad \Delta(C_2)=2,\qquad
\Delta(C_3)=\Delta(S_3)=0.
$$
Formula (2) gives $i(g)=0$ on transpositions and $i(g)=1$ on nonidentity $3$-cycles, as required for this tame extension. In the wild quadratic example $\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt{-1})/\mathbb Q_2$, write $i=\sqrt{-1}$. The polynomial of $i-1$ is the Eisenstein polynomial $X^2+2X+2$, so $\mathcal O_L=\mathbb Z_2[i]$. Its discriminant exponent is $2$; (2) gives $i(g)=2$ and lower break $1$.

**Scope.** These are deductions from standard different formulas and the linked theorem, with no novelty claim beyond the knowledgebase. The data include non-Galois intermediate fields and the numerical exponents as additional structure. The criterion uses every finite extension; no finite collection of discriminants or recovery of them from the unadorned absolute Galois group is asserted.
