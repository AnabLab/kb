**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite of degree $d$, inside a fixed algebraic closure. Let $K(p)$ be the largest Galois extension of $K$ whose Galois group is a pro-$p$ group, and put $G_K(p) = \mathrm{Gal}(K(p)/K)$, the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $G_K$. If $\zeta_p \in K$, let $q$ be the largest power of $p$ with $\mu_q \subset K$. Put $K' = K \cap \mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})$. Since $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is abelian, every subfield of it is normal over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, so $K'$ does not depend on how $K$ is embedded. The map $a \mapsto \sigma_a$, where $\sigma_a(\zeta) = \zeta^a$, identifies $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q}_p)$ [@labute1967, §5, p. 131].

**Structure.**

1. If $\zeta_p \notin K$, which forces $p$ odd, then $G_K(p)$ is a free pro-$p$ group of rank $d + 1$. This is due to Šafarevič [@serre1963, Théorème 4.1].
2. If $\zeta_p \in K$, then $G_K(p)$ is a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) with $n = d + 2$ and $q(G_K(p)) = q$ [@serre1963, Théorème 4.2]. By local class field theory its abelianization is the pro-$p$ completion of $K^\times$, namely $\mathbb{Z}/q \times \mathbb{Z}_p^{d+1}$ [@labute1967, §5, p. 130]. Its canonical character is the cyclotomic character on $\mu_{p^\infty} \subset K(p)$, so $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/K')$ [@labute1967, §5, p. 131].

Labute derives part 1 from Šafarevič and part 2 following Serre [@labute1967, §5, pp. 130–131]. The [classification of Demushkin groups](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md) then gives one relation on $d + 2$ generators $x_1, \dots, x_{d+2}$, with $(x, y) = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$:

- If $q \ne 2$: $x_1^q(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{d+1}, x_{d+2}) = 1$, due to Demushkin [@labute1967, Theorem 7].
- If $q = 2$ and $d$ is odd: $x_1^2 x_2^4 (x_2, x_3)(x_4, x_5) \cdots (x_{d+1}, x_{d+2}) = 1$, due to Serre [@labute1967, Theorem 8]. For $K = \mathbb{Q}_2$ this is the single relation $x_1^2x_2^4(x_2, x_3) = 1$ on three generators, which Serre also states, with commutators written as $xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$ [@serre1963, Corollaire 4.4].
- If $q = 2$ and $d$ is even: $x_1^{2+2^f}(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{d+1}, x_{d+2}) = 1$ if $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = U_2^{[f]}$, and $x_1^2(x_1, x_2)\, x_3^{2^f}(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{d+1}, x_{d+2}) = 1$ if $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\} \times U_2^{(f)}$, with $f \ge 2$ [@labute1967, Theorem 9]. Here $U_2^{(f)} = 1 + 2^f\mathbb{Z}_2$, and $U_2^{[f]}$ is the closed subgroup generated by $-1 + 2^f$.

**Isomorphism criterion.** Let $K, L$ be finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Then $G_K(p) \cong G_L(p)$ if and only if $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] = [L:\mathbb{Q}_p]$ and one of the following holds:

- neither $K$ nor $L$ contains $\zeta_p$, or
- both contain $\zeta_p$, and $K' = L'$.

If $\zeta_p \in K$ and $q_K \ne 2$, the condition $K' = L'$ is equivalent to $q_K = q_L$. Labute states the "if" direction: $G_K(p)$ is completely determined by $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p]$ and $K'$ [@labute1967, p. 108]. The proof below also gives the converse.

*Proof.* Suppose first that the conditions hold. If neither field contains $\zeta_p$, both groups are free pro-$p$ of rank $d + 1$. If both do, both are Demushkin groups of rank $d + 2$ with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/K') = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/L')$, so they are isomorphic by the classification.

Conversely, let $G_K(p) \cong G_L(p)$. A free pro-$p$ group $G$ has $H^2(G, \mathbb{Z}/p) = 0$ [@serre1963, 2.2], and a Demushkin group has $H^2(G, \mathbb{Z}/p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$. So $\zeta_p \in K$ if and only if $\zeta_p \in L$. The dimension of $H^1(G, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ is $d + 1$ in the first case and $d + 2$ in the second, so the degrees agree. If both fields contain $\zeta_p$, then $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ is an invariant of the group [@labute1967, Corollary to Theorem 4]. So $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/K') = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/L')$, and $K' = L'$ by Galois theory.

For the last claim, let $q_K \ne 2$. Then $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = 1 + q_K\mathbb{Z}_p$ [@labute1967, p. 106]. Its fixed field is $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{q_K})$, because $\sigma_a$ fixes $\mu_{q_K}$ exactly when $a \equiv 1 \bmod q_K$. So $K' = \mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{q_K})$. If $q_L = q_K$, the same argument gives $L' = K'$. If $K' = L'$, the two groups have the same $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$, and $q$ is determined by $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ [@labute1967, Corollary to Theorem 4], so $q_L = q_K$. $\square$

**Consequences.**

- *What $G_K(p)$ sees.* It determines the degree, whether $\zeta_p \in K$, and, if so, the field $K'$. By the criterion it carries no other information about $K$. By contrast, $G_K$ determines $K \cap \mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{ab}}$, by the forward direction of the [classification of $p$-adic fields with isomorphic absolute Galois groups](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), and $K'$ is the part of it inside $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})$.
- *Odd $p$.* Here $q$ is never $2$, so $G_K(p)$ is determined by its abelianization $\mathbb{Z}/q \times \mathbb{Z}_p^{d+1}$, where $q = 1$ when $\zeta_p \notin K$. Indeed the abelianization gives $d$ and $q$, hence whether $\zeta_p \in K$, and then $K' = \mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_q)$. The same holds for $p = 2$ when $\sqrt{-1} \in K$.
- *$p = 2$ and $d$ odd.* Then $q = 2$, since $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})$ has degree $2$. Also $K' = \mathbb{Q}_2$, since $[K':\mathbb{Q}_2]$ divides $d$ and is a power of $2$, as $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ is a pro-$2$ group. So $G_K(2)$ depends only on $d$.
- *The ramification filtration is essential.* By [Abrashkin's theorem](abrashkin-local-grothendieck-theorem.md), an isomorphism of these groups that preserves the ramification filtrations comes from a field isomorphism. The examples below show that without the filtrations very little survives.

**Examples.**

1. *$p \ge 5$, $K$ quadratic.* Then $\zeta_p \notin K$, since $[\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p):\mathbb{Q}_p] = p - 1 > 2$ by Eisenstein's criterion. So $G_K(p)$ is free of rank $3$ for every quadratic $K$. Yet the absolute Galois groups of distinct quadratic extensions are pairwise non-isomorphic, because $G_K$ determines $K \cap \mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{ab}} = K$.
2. *$p = 2$, $K$ quadratic.* The following facts are checked here. A unit of $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is a square exactly when it is $\equiv 1 \bmod 8$, since $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times = \{\pm 1\} \times U_2^{(2)}$ and $\log$ maps $U_2^{(f)}$ isomorphically onto $2^f\mathbb{Z}_2$ for $f \ge 2$ [@labute1967, Remarks after the Corollary to Theorem 4]. So the seven quadratic extensions are $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{a})$ for $a \in \{-1, \pm 2, \pm 5, \pm 10\}$. Since $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ has exactly three open subgroups of index $2$, the field $\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$ has exactly three quadratic subfields. They are $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})$, $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{2})$ with $\sqrt{2} = \zeta_8 + \zeta_8^{-1}$, and $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-2})$ with $\sqrt{-2} = \zeta_8 + \zeta_8^3$. The classes give four isomorphism types of $G_K(2)$:
   - $a \in \{\pm 5, \pm 10\}$: $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{a})$ is not one of the three cyclotomic quadratic fields, so $K' = \mathbb{Q}_2$ and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \mathbb{Z}_2^\times = \{\pm 1\} \times U_2^{(2)}$. The relation is $x_1^2(x_1, x_2)\, x_3^4(x_3, x_4) = 1$.
   - $a = 2$: $\sigma_c(\zeta_8 + \zeta_8^{-1}) = \zeta_8^c + \zeta_8^{-c}$ equals $\sqrt{2}$ exactly when $c \equiv \pm 1 \bmod 8$. So $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\} \times U_2^{(3)}$, and the relation is $x_1^2(x_1, x_2)\, x_3^8(x_3, x_4) = 1$.
   - $a = -2$: $\sigma_c$ fixes $\zeta_8 + \zeta_8^3$ exactly when $c \equiv 1, 3 \bmod 8$. This group is $U_2^{[2]}$, the closed subgroup generated by $3$, because $3^2 = 9$ topologically generates $U_2^{(3)}$. The relation is $x_1^6(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) = 1$. Labute's example on p. 132 gives this relation for $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-2})$. It names the subgroup $U_2^{[3]}$, but by the definition on p. 107 that subgroup has index $4$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$, and the exponent $6 = 2 + 2^2$ matches $U_2^{[2]}$.
   - $a = -1$: $q = 4$, since $[\mathbb{Q}_2(\zeta_8):\mathbb{Q}_2] = (\mathbb{Z}_2^\times : U_2^{(3)}) = 4$. The relation is $x_1^4(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) = 1$.

   The four images of $\chi$ are distinct, so these are four pairwise non-isomorphic groups. The seven fields have pairwise non-isomorphic absolute Galois groups, as in Example 1. And the first three types all have abelianization $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2^3$, so for $q = 2$ the abelianization does not determine $G_K(2)$. For instance, $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{5})}(2) \cong G_{\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-5})}(2)$, although one field is unramified over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ and the other is ramified. By Abrashkin's theorem, no isomorphism between these two groups preserves the ramification filtrations.
