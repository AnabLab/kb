**Setting.** Fix a prime $\ell$ and $n\ge1$, and put $\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n$. Let $K/k$ be a function field of transcendence degree $d\ge2$ over an algebraically closed field with $\mathrm{char}\,k\ne\ell$, and let $p$ be the characteristic exponent. Write $V_K=K^\times/K^{\times\ell^n}$ additively, $K^i$ for the perfect closure, and $\mathcal T_0(K)$ for the set of ratios of transverse parameters, as in [Kummer automorphisms preserving rational subfields](kummer-automorphisms-preserving-rational-subfields-are-scalars.md). The inclusion $K\subseteq K^i$ induces an isomorphism $V_K\to V_{K^i}$ [@openai2026modl, §1.1, p. 2]. Through it, an automorphism $\beta$ of $K^i$ induces an automorphism $\beta_1$ of $V_K$, and an isomorphism $\alpha:K^i\to L^i$ induces $\alpha_1:V_K\to V_L$.

**Statement.**

1. Let $\beta$ be an automorphism of $K^i$ with $\beta(k)=k$ and $\beta_1=a\,\mathrm{id}$ for some $a\in\Lambda$. Then $\beta=\mathrm{Frob}^m$, that is $x\mapsto x^{p^m}$, for some $m\in\mathbb Z$, and $a=p^m$. In characteristic $0$, $\beta$ is the identity.
2. Let $L/l$ be a function field over an algebraically closed field, and let $\alpha,\alpha':K^i\to L^i$ be isomorphisms with $\alpha(k)=\alpha'(k)=l$ and $\alpha_1\in\Lambda^\times\alpha'_1$. Then $\alpha=\mathrm{Frob}^m\circ\alpha'$ for some $m\in\mathbb Z$. So the canonical map $\mathrm{Isom}^i_F(K,L)\to\mathrm{Isom}(V_K,V_L)/\Lambda^\times$ is injective.
3. The canonical maps in the [mod-$\ell$ Bogomolov–Pop conjecture](mod-l-bogomolov-pop-conjecture.md) and in the [Bogomolov–Pop conjecture](bogomolov-pop-conjecture.md) are injective, for every pair of fields that they concern.

Part 1 is Proposition 8.4 of the October 2026 mod-$\ell$ manuscript, and part 2 with $n=1$ is the injectivity step of its proof of Theorem 1.1 [@openai2026modl, Proposition 8.4 and proof of Theorem 1.1, pp. 34–35]. The manuscript shows that $\beta$ preserves the perfection of each good subfield using its Lemma 5.2 on curve subfields. Step 2 below replaces this with a direct argument. Injectivity is the direction that the Lean formalization of the pro-$\ell$ manuscript covers, as recorded in [the manuscripts note](manuscripts-claiming-bogomolov-pop.md). No novelty is claimed here, and the literature was not searched for this exact statement.

**Proof of 1.** Note that $a$ is a unit, since $\beta_1$ is onto and $V_K\ne\ell V_K$. Fix $t\in\mathcal T_0(K)$ and $E=k(t)$. By the preliminaries of [the companion entry](kummer-automorphisms-preserving-rational-subfields-are-scalars.md), $E$ is relatively algebraically closed in $K$ and $dt\ne0$ in $\Omega_{K/k}$. For relatively algebraically closed $F$, write $V_F\subseteq V_K$ for the image of $F^\times$. Under $V_K\cong V_{K^i}$, $V_F$ corresponds to the image of $(F^i)^\times$, because $[x]=c[x^{p^r}]$ when $x^{p^r}\in F$ and $cp^r\equiv1$ modulo $\ell^n$.

*Step 1: a curve subfield with the same classes.* The field $E^i$ is relatively algebraically closed in $K^i$: if $z\in K^i$ is algebraic over $E^i$, then some $z^{p^r}\in K$ is algebraic over $E$, so lies in $E$. Hence $\beta(E^i)$ is relatively algebraically closed in $K^i$ too. Put $P=\beta(E^i)\cap K$. Then $P$ is relatively algebraically closed in $K$, since anything in $K$ algebraic over $P$ lies in $\beta(E^i)$. It has $P^i=\beta(E^i)$, since $z\in\beta(E^i)$ has some $z^{p^r}\in P$. So $P$ has transcendence degree $1$. As $\beta_1$ maps the image of $(E^i)^\times$ onto that of $(P^i)^\times$, we get $V_P=aV_E=V_E$.

*Step 2: $P=E$.* Suppose not. Then $E$ and $P$ are algebraically independent: if $P$ were algebraic over $E$, relative algebraic closedness would give $P\subseteq E$, and then $E\subseteq P$. Choose $u\in P\setminus k$, so $t,u$ are algebraically independent. Choose a smooth affine model $U$ on which $t,u$ are regular and $dt$ vanishes nowhere. Then $\phi=(t,u):U\to\mathbb A^2$ is dominant.

Let $x\in t(U)$. At each closed point $Q$ of $Z_x=V(t-x)\subseteq U$, $t-x\notin\mathfrak m_Q^2$, since $dt(Q)\ne0$. So $\mathcal O_Q/(t-x)$ is regular, hence a domain. Each irreducible component $D$ of $Z_x$ is therefore a prime divisor with $\mathrm{ord}_D(t-x)=1$, because $t-x$ generates the prime of $D$ in $\mathcal O_Q$ for $Q\in D$. So $[t-x]\in V_E$ is not in the image $U_D$ of the $\mathrm{ord}_D$-units, as $\mathrm{ord}_D(t-x)=1$ is prime to $\ell$. Since $V_P=V_E$, also $V_P\not\subseteq U_D$, so $\mathrm{ord}_D$ is nontrivial on $P$. Hence it is nontrivial on $k(u)$, over which $P$ is algebraic. As $u$ is regular along $D$, some $u-y$ with $y\in k$ has positive order, so $u$ is constant on $D$.

Thus $\phi$ maps each of the finitely many components of $Z_x$ to a point, and $\phi(U)$ meets each line $\{x\}\times\mathbb A^1$ in a finite set. But $\phi(U)$ is constructible and dense, so it contains a dense open subset of $\mathbb A^2$. That open set meets all but finitely many of these lines in infinite sets, a contradiction. So $P=E$, and $\beta(E^i)=E^i$.

*Step 3: $\beta$ on $E^i$.* Put $\rho=\beta|_k$ and $h=\beta(t)$. Then $k(h)^i=\beta(E^i)=E^i$. Choose $N$ with $q=h^{p^N}\in k(t)$, and let $e$ be maximal with $q\in k(t^{p^e})$, so that $q=q_0(t^{p^e})$ with $q_0\notin k(X^p)$. In characteristic $0$, take $N=e=0$. Then $z=t^{p^e}$ is separable over $k(q)$. Indeed, $k(z)$ is purely inseparable of some degree $p^j$ over the separable closure $F$ of $k(q)$ in it. So $z^{p^j}\in F$, and comparing degrees gives $F=k(z^{p^j})$. As $q\in F$, maximality of $e$ forces $j=0$. It is also purely inseparable over $k(q)$, since $t\in k(q)^i$. So $k(q)=k(z)$, and $q_0$ is a Möbius transformation $M$. Hence
$$h=M(z)^{1/p^N}.$$
Let $H$ be the bijection $y\mapsto M(y^{p^e})^{1/p^N}$ of $\mathbb P^1(k)$.

Extend each order $\mathrm{ord}_y$ on $E$ to $E^i$ with values in $\mathbb Z[1/p]$. Reduction modulo $\ell^n$ makes sense on $\mathbb Z[1/p]$, and gives the orders on $V_E$. For $c\in k$, the function $M(z)-\rho(c)^{p^N}$ has a simple zero at $z=M^{-1}(\rho(c)^{p^N})$ and a simple pole at $z=M^{-1}(\infty)$ as a function of $z$. As $t^{p^e}-z_0=(t-z_0^{1/p^e})^{p^e}$, it follows that
$$\mathrm{div}\bigl(h-\rho(c)\bigr)=p^{e-N}\Bigl([H^{-1}(\rho(c))]-[H^{-1}(\infty)]\Bigr).$$
On the other hand, $\beta_1[t-c]=[h-\rho(c)]$ equals $a[t-c]$, whose orders are $a$ at $c$, $-a$ at $\infty$, and $0$ elsewhere. Both order vectors are nonzero, as $a$ and $p$ are units. Comparing supports gives $\{H^{-1}(\rho(c)),H^{-1}(\infty)\}=\{c,\infty\}$ for every $c\in k$. Using two values of $c$ shows $H^{-1}(\infty)=\infty$, and hence $H(c)=\rho(c)$ for every $c\in k$. In particular $H$ fixes $0$, $1$ and $\infty$, so $M$ does, and $M$ is the identity. So $h=t^{p^m}$ and $\rho(c)=c^{p^m}$ with $m=e-N$. In characteristic $0$, $h=t$ and $\rho$ is the identity.

*Step 4.* Distinct powers of Frobenius differ on the infinite field $k$, so $m$ is determined by $\rho$ and is the same for every $t\in\mathcal T_0(K)$. So $\beta$ and $\mathrm{Frob}^m$ agree on $k$ and on $\mathcal T_0(K)$, which generate $K$ by (P3) of the companion entry [@openai2026modl, Lemma 6.4, p. 26]. They agree on $K$, hence on $K^i$, since $\beta(z)^{p^r}=\beta(z^{p^r})$. Finally, $\mathrm{Frob}^m$ acts on $V_K$ as $p^m$, so $a=p^m$. $\square$

**Proof of 2.** If $\alpha_1=a\alpha'_1$, then $\beta=\alpha'^{-1}\circ\alpha$ preserves $k$ and has $\beta_1=a\,\mathrm{id}$. By part 1, $\beta=\mathrm{Frob}^m$, so $\alpha=\alpha'\circ\mathrm{Frob}^m=\mathrm{Frob}^m\circ\alpha'$, as field isomorphisms commute with $p$-th powers. $\square$

**Proof of 3.** If $\mathrm{Isom}^i_F(K,L)$ is nonempty, then $L/l$ has the same characteristic and transcendence degree as $K/k$, so part 2 applies; otherwise there is nothing to prove. With $n=1$, part 2 shows that the manuscript's map $\mathrm{Isom}^i_F(K,L)\to\mathrm{Isom}_M(V_K,V_L)/\mathbb F_\ell^\times$ is injective. Composed with the bijection of [the Milnor–Galois comparison](milnor-and-galois-forms-of-mod-l-bogomolov-pop.md), it is Topaz's map to $\overline{\mathrm{Isom}}^c(G^a_L,G^a_K)$, which is therefore injective. That map factors through the map of the pro-$\ell$ conjecture, as recorded in [the mod-$\ell$ conjecture](mod-l-bogomolov-pop-conjecture.md), so the latter is injective too. $\square$

**Checks.** In characteristic $0$, the conclusion says that the only automorphism of $K$ preserving $k$ and acting on $V_K$ by a scalar is the identity. For example, $x\mapsto-x$ on $k(x,y)$ sends $[x]$ to $[-x]=[x]$ but $[x-1]$ to $[x+1]$, which is not a multiple of $[x-1]$. In characteristic $p$, $\mathrm{Frob}$ acts as $p$, a unit modulo $\ell^n$, so the conclusion cannot be strengthened to $\beta=\mathrm{id}$. The hypothesis $\mathrm{char}\,k\ne\ell$ is needed to define $\beta_1$, since $(K^i)^\times$ is $\ell$-divisible in characteristic $\ell$.

**Scope.** This rebuilds §8.3 of the manuscript and the injectivity part of its proof of Theorem 1.1, and no gap was found there. Unlike the existence part, this injectivity does not depend on the manuscript's earlier sections.
