**Setting.** Let $K$ be a number field and $S$ a finite set of places of $K$. Faltings works with abelian varieties over $K$ that have good reduction outside $S$, and with polarizations of degree $d > 0$, which he calls *$d$-fach polarisiert* [@faltings1983, §1, p. 349]. After a finite extension of $K$, such an abelian variety admits an isogeny of degree $\sqrt d$ relating it to a principally polarized one, which is how his proofs reduce to $d = 1$ [@faltings1983, proof of Korollar 3, p. 362].

**Theorem** (Faltings).

1. *Isogeny classes.* For each $g$, only finitely many isogeny classes of abelian varieties of dimension $g$ over $K$ have good reduction outside $S$ [@faltings1983, §6, Satz 5, p. 362].
2. *Shafarevich conjecture for abelian varieties.* For each $g$ and each $d > 0$, there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of abelian varieties of dimension $g$ over $K$ with a polarization of degree $d$ and good reduction outside $S$ [@faltings1983, §6, Satz 6, p. 363].
3. *Tate modules up to isomorphism.* Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $K$ and $d > 0$. There are only finitely many isomorphism classes of abelian varieties $B$ over $K$ with a polarization of degree $d$ such that $T_\ell(A) \cong T_\ell(B)$ for every prime $\ell$ [@faltings1983, §5, Korollar 3, p. 362].
4. *Shafarevich conjecture for curves.* For each $g \ge 2$, there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of smooth curves of genus $g$ over $K$ with good reduction outside $S$ [@faltings1983, §6, Korollar 1, p. 365].
5. *Mordell conjecture.* If $X$ is a smooth curve of genus $g \ge 2$ over $K$, then $X(K)$ is finite [@faltings1983, §6, Satz 7, p. 365].

Faltings names item 2 the Shafarevich conjecture, and notes that the Mordell conjecture was already known to follow from it, by Parshin [@faltings1983, §1, p. 349].

**Proof outline.** This follows Faltings.

- *Heights.* Faltings attaches a modular height $h(A)$ to a semiabelian variety over the ring of integers with proper generic fibre, as the normalized Arakelov degree of its metrized line bundle of invariant differentials of top degree [@faltings1983, §3, Definition, p. 354]. For each $c$, only finitely many such varieties with a principal polarization of the generic fibre have $h(A) \le c$, up to isomorphism [@faltings1983, §3, Satz 1, pp. 356–357]. For an $\ell$-divisible subgroup $G$ of the generic fibre, with $G_n$ the kernel of $\ell^n$ on $G$, Satz 2 asserts $h(A/G_n) = h(A)$ for all $n$ [@faltings1983, §4, Satz 2, p. 358]. Faltings adds in proof that Gabber pointed out that the argument gives only that the sequence $h(A/G_n)$ is eventually constant, and that this suffices [@faltings1983, Zusatz bei der Korrektur, p. 366].
- *Item 1.* By the Weil conjectures, for $v \notin S$ the local $L$-factor $L_v(A, s)$ has only finitely many possibilities. Fix a prime $\ell$. By Hermite–Minkowski [@faltings1983, §3, Lemma 4, p. 357] there is a finite Galois extension $K'/K$ containing every extension of degree at most $\ell^{8g^2}$ unramified outside $\ell$ and $S$. By Chebotarev, choose places $v_1, \dots, v_r$ whose Frobenius elements meet every conjugacy class of $\mathrm{Gal}(K'/K)$. If $A_1$ and $A_2$ have the same $L$-factors at these places, consider the $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-algebra $M$ generated by the image of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{End}(T_\ell(A_1)) \times \mathrm{End}(T_\ell(A_2))$. It is free of rank at most $8g^2$. The induced homomorphism $G_K \to (M/\ell M)^\times$ has image of order at most $\ell^{8g^2}$, generating $M/\ell M$, and it factors through $\mathrm{Gal}(K'/K)$. So its image consists of images of Frobenius elements at the $v_i$, and by Nakayama these Frobenius elements generate $M$. Hence the two traces agree on all of $M$, which is what Faltings needs for $A_1$ and $A_2$ to be isogenous [@faltings1983, proof of Satz 5, pp. 362–363]. The last step uses the semisimplicity of rational Tate modules and the fact that they determine isogeny classes; see [Faltings' Tate-module theorems](faltings-tate-isogeny-theorem.md).
- *Item 2.* By item 1, all the abelian varieties in question may be taken isogenous to one fixed $A$. After a finite extension of $K$, one may assume semiabelian reduction everywhere, and, as in the proof of item 3, that $d = 1$. The number $\exp(2[K:\mathbb{Q}](h(B) - h(A)))$ is rational. Faltings bounds the exponents of the primes $\ell \le N$ in it, using that the Galois-stable lattices in $T_\ell(A) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ fall into finitely many isomorphism classes, which comes from semisimplicity. For $\ell > N$ he shows that $\ell$-power isogenies do not change the height, using Raynaud's results on finite flat group schemes of type $(p, \dots, p)$ and the Weil conjectures. So the heights $h(B)$ are bounded, and Satz 1 gives finiteness [@faltings1983, proof of Satz 6, pp. 363–365].
- *Item 4.* Faltings's proof is the single word "Torelli", an appeal to Torelli's theorem to pass from item 2 for Jacobians, with their canonical principal polarizations, to curves [@faltings1983, §6, proof of Korollar 1, p. 365].
- *Item 5.* Faltings quotes Parshin's construction. After enlarging $K$, there is an unramified covering $\phi : X_1 \to X$ of degree $m > 2$. By Lemma 4 there is a finite extension $K_1 \supseteq K$ such that for every $x \in X(K)$, the fibre $\phi^{-1}(x)$ consists of $m$ distinct $K_1$-rational points. Choose $y \in \phi^{-1}(x)$, put $D = \phi^{-1}(x) \setminus \{y\}$, and map $X_1 \setminus D$ to the generalized Jacobian $A$ of $(X_1, D)$. Multiplication by $2$ on $A$ induces a covering $Y(x) \to X_1$ ramified exactly over $D$. The curve $Y(x)$ has bad reduction only at places dividing $2$, places of bad reduction of $X_1$, and places where $\phi$ ramifies in the fibre modulo $v$. These are finitely many places, so there are only finitely many possibilities for $Y(x)$; this is where item 4, over $K_1$, enters. The same holds for $Y(x) \to X_1 \to X$, which is ramified exactly over $x$, and the finiteness of $X(K)$ follows [@faltings1983, §6, proof of Satz 7, p. 365].

**Siegel's theorem.** Faltings remarks that the same method proves Siegel's theorem on integral points, with no diophantine approximation [@faltings1983, §6, Bemerkungen 1, p. 365]. For the thrice-punctured line, [Kim's unipotent method](kim-unipotent-proof-of-siegel-theorem.md) gives another such proof, through fundamental groups.

**Scope.** These statements are for number fields. Several anabelian arguments use the finiteness of $X(K)$ for every field $K$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$. Koenigsmann, for instance, applies "Faltings" to fields finite or finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ [@koenigsmann2005, proof of Lemma 1.7, p. 8]. The 1983 paper does not treat fields of positive transcendence degree, and no source for that extension was opened for this entry.

**Uses in the graph.**

- *The neighbourhood criterion.* In [Tamagawa's theorem on affine curves](tamagawa-grothendieck-conjecture-for-affine-curves.md), a section is geometric exactly when every finite étale neighbourhood of it has a rational point. Over fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, the proof needs the finiteness of the rational points of the proper neighbourhoods, which makes them compact. Over number fields this is item 5; for the other fields see the scope paragraph above. This is what reduces the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) to its weak form.
- *From sections to birational sections.* The same compactness, through Koenigsmann's Lemma 1.7, shows that the section conjecture implies the [birational section conjecture](birational-section-conjecture.md); see the section conjecture entry.
- *Finiteness for a given pro-$\ell$ kernel.* Hoshi proves that over a number field only finitely many hyperbolic curves of a given type have outer Galois representations unramified outside a given finite set of primes. He combines the Oda–Tamagawa good reduction criterion with a theorem of Faltings and Shafarevich for the compactifications of genus at least $1$, of which item 4 is the case of genus at least $2$, and with the theorems of Mahler and Siegel and of Faltings and Mordell for the cusps [@hoshi2011monodromic, proof of Theorem A.3, p. 98]. See [pro-$\ell$ kernels of genus-zero curves](pro-l-kernels-determine-monodromically-full-genus-zero-curves.md).
- *Selmer sections.* For number fields with no CM subfield, the [finiteness of local images of Selmer sections](finiteness-of-local-images-of-selmer-sections.md) contains item 5, because the local image contains $Y(K)$.
