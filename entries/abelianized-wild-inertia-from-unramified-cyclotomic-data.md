**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, with $\mu_p\subset K$, degree $n$, and residue field of order $q$. Put $P_K=\mathrm{Gal}(\bar K/K_{tr})$, $\Gamma_K=\mathrm{Gal}(K_{tr}/K)$ and
$$
M_K=P_K^{ab},\qquad E_K=G_K/[P_K,P_K].
$$
Commutator subgroups are closed. Let $N_K$ be the kernel of the unramified projection $\Gamma_K\to\widehat{\mathbb Z}\to\mathbb Z_p$, put $R_K=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma_K]]$, and let $e_K\in R_K$ be the central averaging idempotent of the pro-prime-to-$p$ group $N_K$. Thus $e_KR_K\cong\Lambda_K=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma_K/N_K]]$. Write $\gamma_K$ for the image of arithmetic Frobenius in $\Gamma_K/N_K$.

Let $p^{s_K}=|\mu_{p^\infty}(K^{ur})|$, and let Frobenius act on this finite group by $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{g_K}$. Set
$$
J_K=(p^{s_K},\gamma_K-\widetilde g_K)\subset\Lambda_K,
$$
where $\widetilde g_K\in\mathbb Z_p$ is any lift of $g_K\in(\mathbb Z/p^{s_K})^\times$. The ideal is independent of the lift. These are the data denoted $s'$ and $g_K$ in [the wild commutator entry](p-adic-galois-groups-modulo-wild-commutators.md).

**Theorem (proved here).** There is an isomorphism of compact $R_K$-modules
$$
M_K\cong
\Lambda_K^{n-1}\oplus J_K\oplus\big((1-e_K)R_K\big)^n,
$$
where the first two summands have action through $\Gamma_K/N_K$. Moreover $E_K\cong M_K\rtimes\Gamma_K$. Consequently, for finite $K,L/\mathbb Q_p$ containing $\mu_p$,
$$
E_K\cong E_L
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n_K=n_L,\quad q_K=q_L,\quad s_K=s_L,\quad
g_K\equiv g_L\pmod{p^{s_K}}.
$$

**Ramified fourth roots (proved here).** Suppose $p=2$ and adjoining $i$, with $i^2=-1$, ramifies over each of $K$ and $L$. Then
$$
E_K\cong E_L
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[K:\mathbb Q_2]=[L:\mathbb Q_2]\ \text{and}\ f_K=f_L,
$$
where $f_K,f_L$ are residue degrees. In particular, this quotient cannot distinguish any of the pairs left open in the ramified case of the [absolute Galois classification](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md).

**Proof.** Fix $K$ and suppress its subscripts. Steps 2–4 of [the tame lifting proof](tame-automorphisms-lift-modulo-wild-commutators.md) establish the following inputs for every $\mu_p\subset K$: the extension $1\to M\to E\to\Gamma\to1$ splits; $e$ is the central averaging idempotent of $N$; and $Q=(1-e)M$ is projective. Applying $1-e$ to the exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow R^{n+1}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow0
$$
from [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Theorem 7.4.5(i), p. 408] and splitting the resulting sequence gives
$$
Q\oplus(1-e)R\cong\big((1-e)R\big)^{n+1}.
$$
The cited proof uses local duality and the Euler characteristic formula to establish projectivity; its hypothesis $\mu_p\subset K$ is retained here.

*Cancellation.* We claim $Q\cong((1-e)R)^n$. For an open normal $U\subset\Gamma$ and $a\ge1$, put $F_{a,U}(Y)=Y/(p^aY+I(U)Y)$, where $I(U)$ is the closed ideal generated by $u-1$, $u\in U$. Applying $F_{a,U}$ to the stable isomorphism and cancelling the common finite summand gives
$$
F_{a,U}(Q)\cong F_{a,U}\big(((1-e)R)^n\big)
$$
by [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Theorem 5.6.10(i), p. 327]. The isomorphisms at each level form a nonempty finite set. An isomorphism at a finer level descends to every coarser level, since it preserves the submodules generated by $p^a$ and $I(U)$. Every finite collection of compatibility conditions is therefore satisfied by descending an isomorphism from a common finer level. Compactness gives compatible isomorphisms at all levels. Both modules are finitely generated over $R$, hence are inverse limits of these finite quotients, so $Q\cong((1-e)R)^n$. This is the compactness argument used in [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, proof of Proposition 5.6.11, p. 328].

*Identifying $eM$.* Let $E_N$ be the inverse image of $N$ in $E$. A splitting identifies $E_N=M\rtimes N$. Every continuous homomorphism from $E_N$ to a pro-$p$ group kills the section $N$, because $N$ is pro-prime-to-$p$. It therefore factors through the coinvariants
$$
M_N=M/\overline{\langle (h-1)m:h\in N,\ m\in M\rangle}.
$$
Conversely, projection $M\rtimes N\to M_N$ is a continuous homomorphism to an abelian pro-$p$ group. Thus $M_N$ is the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $E_N^{ab}$, namely $X_K$ in the wild commutator entry. Averaging gives $M_N\cong eM$: in each finite quotient $1-e$ is a linear combination of $1-h$, $h\in N$, so $\ker(e:M\to eM)$ is exactly the closed subgroup of coinvariant relations. Theorem 1 of that entry now gives
$$
eM\cong X_K\cong\Lambda^{n-1}\oplus J_K.
$$
Together with cancellation and the splitting, this proves the asserted module and group decompositions.

*Classification.* Necessity is Theorem 2 of the wild commutator entry. For sufficiency, equal $q$ identifies the tame groups by matching inertia generators and Frobenius lifts in their presentations $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^q$. This identifies $N$, $e$, $R$, $\Lambda$ and $\gamma$. Equal $n,s,g$ then identifies every summand in the displayed module decomposition, hence the semidirect products. If adjoining $i$ ramifies, $\mu_{2^\infty}(K^{ur})=\mu_2$: a higher $2$-power root would put $i$ in $K^{ur}$. Thus $s_K=1$, $g_K=1$ and $J_K=(2,\gamma_K-1)$ for every such field; $q_K=2^{f_K}$ gives the stated specialization. The pairs left open in the absolute Galois classification have equal degree and maximal abelian subfield, hence equal residue degree: their maximal unramified subfields lie in, and can be recovered from, that abelian subfield. $\square$

**Example (proved here).** Take $K=\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt2)$ and $L=\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt{-2})$. Both have degree $2$ and residue degree $1$, by the Eisenstein polynomials $x^2-2$ and $x^2+2$. They are distinct because $-1$ is not a square in $\mathbb Q_2$ (an odd square is $1$ modulo $8$). Their common compositum with $\mathbb Q_2(i)$ is $\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_8)=\mathbb Q_2(i,\sqrt2)$: $(1+i)/\sqrt2$ is a primitive eighth root, and $\zeta_8+\zeta_8^{-1}=\sqrt2$ for a suitable choice. This extension is totally ramified of degree $4$, since $(x+1)^4+1$ is Eisenstein at $2$. Hence adjoining $i$ ramifies over both fields, and the theorem gives $E_K\cong E_L$. But $G_K\not\cong G_L$: the two fields are distinct abelian extensions of $\mathbb Q_2$, so part 1 of the absolute Galois classification distinguishes their maximal abelian subfields.

**Scope.** These are deductions from the two linked proofs, not statements attributed to the book. They classify $G_K/[P_K,P_K]$, and do not decide the question about full absolute Galois groups. The example shows that this quotient can discard information retained by $G_K$.
