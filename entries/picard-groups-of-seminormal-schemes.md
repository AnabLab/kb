**Theorem.** Let $k$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb Q$, and let $X$ be a seminormal $k$-scheme of finite type. Then $\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong F\oplus T$ with $F$ free abelian and $T$ finite.

A ring $A$ is seminormal if for all $x,y\in A$ with $x^3=y^2$ there is a unique $a\in A$ with $x=a^2$ and $y=a^3$. A scheme is seminormal if it is covered by spectra of such rings [@stacks2026, Definitions 29.48.1 and 29.48.3, Tag 0EUK]. Then the ring of every affine open is seminormal, and so is every open subscheme [@stacks2026, Lemma 29.48.4, Tag 0EUP].

**Consequences.**

- *Torsion.* $\operatorname{Pic}(X)_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong T$ is finite. So no nonzero torsion element is divisible by every positive integer: if $x=|T|y$ is torsion, then so is $y$, so $y\in T$ and $x=0$. For the same reason $\varprojlim_n\operatorname{Pic}(X)[n]=0$.
- *Étale neighbourhoods.* A scheme étale over a seminormal $k$-scheme of finite type is seminormal [@berens2025, Lemma 1.7, p. 6], so the theorem applies to every such scheme of finite type.
- *Status.* Guralnick–Jaffe–Raskind–Wiegand proved the theorem for seminormal $X$ that are also $S_2$ [@guralnickjafferaskindwiegand1996, Theorem 6.5, p. 25]. Carlson–Haine–Wolf explain that adapting Voevodsky's argument to seminormal $X$ needs only that $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ have no infinitely divisible torsion elements. They know this for $S_2$ schemes, and without $S_2$ they know neither a proof nor a counterexample [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Introduction and footnote 1, pp. 2–3]. Berens also does not know whether Picard groups of seminormal $k$-schemes have bounded torsion [@berens2025, Remark 1.11, p. 7]. The proof below is new here; none of the opened sources contains it.
- *Reconstruction.* Berens reproves étale reconstruction for seminormal $S_2$ schemes by Voevodsky's method. His proof says that it suffices for $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ to have bounded torsion, and gets this from the $S_2$ case above [@berens2025, Theorem 1.10 and its proof, pp. 6–7]. With the theorem and his Lemma 1.7, that step no longer needs $S_2$. The conclusion itself was already known by other methods [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Theorem 0.3, p. 2]; see [the Carlson–Stix entry](etale-topos-reconstruction-over-sub-p-adic-fields.md). Only the Picard input is checked here, not the rest of Voevodsky's argument.
- *Sharpness.* Reduced does not suffice. The one-dimensional domain $A=\mathbb Q+(x-1)^2\mathbb Q[x,x^{-1}]$ has $\operatorname{Pic}(A)\cong\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ [@guralnickjafferaskindwiegand1996, Example 6.2, p. 24]. An open subscheme of the [reduced R1 counterexample to Voevodsky's printed theorem](voevodsky-reconstruction-fails-for-r1-schemes.md) also has $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ in its Picard group.

**Why $S_2$ was needed before.** The $S_2$ proof uses the Milnor square of the conductor, whose corner $Z$ below can be a non-seminormal scheme with large Picard group. It removes the non-normal locus $Q$ of $Z$, which has codimension at least $2$ in $X$, so that $Z$ becomes normal; this needs $\operatorname{Pic}(X)\to\operatorname{Pic}(X\smallsetminus Q)$ to be injective [@guralnickjafferaskindwiegand1996, proof of Theorem 6.5, pp. 25–26], which holds for $S_2$ schemes [@berens2025, Lemma 1.9, p. 6]. Here $Z$ and its preimage are replaced by their seminormalizations instead. The square is then no longer a Milnor square, but Step 3 shows that it is still cartesian, and Step 4 needs only that.

**The class $\mathcal C$.** Let $\mathcal C$ be the class of abelian groups isomorphic to $F\oplus T$ with $F$ free and $T$ finite. These closure properties are proved here.

1. *Subgroups.* Let $H\subseteq F\oplus T$. Then $H_{\mathrm{tors}}\subseteq T$ is finite, and $x\mapsto|T|x$ embeds $H/H_{\mathrm{tors}}$ in $F$. Subgroups of free abelian groups are free, so $H/H_{\mathrm{tors}}$ is free, $H\to H/H_{\mathrm{tors}}$ splits, and $H\in\mathcal C$.
2. *Extensions.* Let $0\to A\to G\to B\to0$ be exact with $A,B\in\mathcal C$, and write $B=F_B\oplus T_B$. The preimage $G'$ of $F_B$ is an extension of a free group by $A$, so $G'\cong A\oplus F_B\in\mathcal C$, and $N=[G:G']=|T_B|$. The torsion of $G$ is finite, because $0\to A_{\mathrm{tors}}\to G_{\mathrm{tors}}\to B_{\mathrm{tors}}$ is exact. The map $x\mapsto Nx$ embeds $G/G_{\mathrm{tors}}$ in $G'/G'_{\mathrm{tors}}$, which is free. So $G\in\mathcal C$ as in 1.
3. *Quotients by finitely generated subgroups.* Let $G=F\oplus T$, let $\Lambda\subseteq G$ be finitely generated, and let $p:G\to F$ be the projection. The generators of $p(\Lambda)$ involve only finitely many elements of a basis of $F$, so $F=F_0\oplus F_1$ with $F_0$ of finite rank and $p(\Lambda)\subseteq F_0$. Then $F/p(\Lambda)\cong F_0/p(\Lambda)\oplus F_1$ is in $\mathcal C$. The sequence $0\to T/(T\cap\Lambda)\to G/\Lambda\to F/p(\Lambda)\to0$ is exact, so $G/\Lambda\in\mathcal C$ by 2.

**Inputs.** For a field $K$ and a $K$-scheme $Y$ of finite type, let $\Lambda_Y$ be the integral closure of $K$ in $\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}$. Then $\Lambda_Y$ is a finite-dimensional $K$-algebra, and $\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)_{\mathrm{red}}^\times/\Lambda_Y^\times$ is a finitely generated free abelian group [@guralnickjafferaskindwiegand1996, Proposition 1.6, p. 4]. For $k$ finitely generated over $\mathbb Q$:

- if $Y$ is a normal $k$-scheme of finite type, then $\operatorname{Pic}(Y)$ is finitely generated [@guralnickjafferaskindwiegand1996, Proposition 6.1, p. 24];
- if $\Lambda$ is a finite-dimensional reduced $k$-algebra with $k$-subalgebras $E_1,E_2$, then $\Lambda^\times/E_1^\times E_2^\times\in\mathcal C$ [@guralnickjafferaskindwiegand1996, Theorem 6.4(2), p. 25]. The separability that part (2) asks for is automatic in characteristic $0$. The authors quote this from a paper of Colliot-Thélène, Guralnick and Wiegand, which was not opened here.

**Lemma.** Let $R\subseteq D\subseteq S$ be rings, with $R$ noetherian and $D$ finite over $R$. Suppose that $\operatorname{Spec}D\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is bijective and induces isomorphisms on residue fields, and that $R$ is seminormal in $S$: every $b\in S$ with $b^2,b^3\in R$ lies in $R$. Then $D=R$.

*Proof.* First, $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is seminormal in $S_{\mathfrak p}$ for every prime $\mathfrak p$ of $R$. Let $b\in S$ and $s\notin\mathfrak p$ with $(b/s)^2=r/t$ and $(b/s)^3=r'/t'$ in $S_{\mathfrak p}$. Then $u(tb^2-s^2r)=0$ and $u'(t'b^3-s^3r')=0$ in $S$ for some $u,u'\notin\mathfrak p$. With $w=stut'u'$ we get $(wb)^2=(t'u')^2s^2tu\cdot us^2r$ and $(wb)^3=(stu)^3t'^2u'^2\cdot u's^3r'$, both in $R$. So $wb\in R$, and $b/s=wb/ws\in R_{\mathfrak p}$.

Now suppose $D\ne R$. The conductor $\mathfrak c=\{r\in R:rD\subseteq R\}$ is an ideal of both $R$ and $D$, and $\mathfrak c\ne R$. Let $\mathfrak p$ be a minimal prime over $\mathfrak c$. Since $D/R$ is a finite $R$-module, $\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak p}$ is the conductor of $D_{\mathfrak p}$ into $R_{\mathfrak p}$, so $D_{\mathfrak p}\ne R_{\mathfrak p}$.

- By the bijection on spectra, the integral extension $D_{\mathfrak p}$ of $R_{\mathfrak p}$ has a unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak q$. Its residue field is that of $R_{\mathfrak p}$, so $D_{\mathfrak p}=R_{\mathfrak p}+\mathfrak q$.
- A prime of $D_{\mathfrak p}$ containing $\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak p}$ lies over a prime of $R_{\mathfrak p}$ containing $\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak p}$. That is $\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}$, by minimality, so the prime is $\mathfrak q$. As $D_{\mathfrak p}$ is noetherian, $\mathfrak q^N\subseteq\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak p}\subseteq R_{\mathfrak p}$ for some $N$.
- If $\mathfrak q\subseteq R_{\mathfrak p}$, then $D_{\mathfrak p}=R_{\mathfrak p}$. So the least $n$ with $\mathfrak q^n\subseteq R_{\mathfrak p}$ is at least $2$. Pick $y\in\mathfrak q^{n-1}\smallsetminus R_{\mathfrak p}$. Then $y^2\in\mathfrak q^{2n-2}$ and $y^3\in\mathfrak q^{3n-3}$ both lie in $\mathfrak q^n\subseteq R_{\mathfrak p}$, which contradicts the first paragraph. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** This is proved here, by induction on $\dim X$; the empty scheme is trivial.

1. *The conductor.* The normalization $\nu:\widetilde X\to X$ is finite, and $\widetilde X$ is normal of finite type over $k$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 33.27.1, Tag 0BXR]. Over an affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ we have $\nu^{-1}(U)=\operatorname{Spec}A'$, where $A'$ is the integral closure of $A$ in its total ring of fractions $Q(A)$, the product of the residue fields at the minimal primes [@stacks2026, Lemma 29.55.3, Tag 035P]. Let $I\subseteq\mathcal O_X$ be the annihilator of $\nu_*\mathcal O_{\widetilde X}/\mathcal O_X$, so that $I(U)=\{a\in A:aA'\subseteq A\}$. It is an ideal of both $A$ and $A'$: if $c\in I(U)$ and $b\in A'$, then $cb\in A$ and $cbA'\subseteq cA'\subseteq A$.
   - *$A$ is seminormal in $A'$.* If $c,v$ in a reduced ring satisfy $c^2=v^2$ and $c^3=v^3$, then $c^2v=v^3$ and $cv^2=c^3=v^3$, so $(c-v)^3=0$ and $c=v$. If $b\in A'$ has $b^2,b^3\in A$, the seminormality of $A$ gives $a\in A$ with $a^2=b^2$ and $a^3=b^3$, so $b=a\in A$.
   - *$I$ is radical in $A'$.* Let $s\in A'$ with $s^2\in I$. For $t\in A'$, both $(st)^2=s^2t^2$ and $(st)^3=s^2\cdot st^3$ lie in $I\subseteq A$, so $st\in A$, and hence $s\in I$. If $s^n\in I$ with $n\ge2$, then $(s^m)^2\in I$ for $m=\lceil n/2\rceil<n$, so $s^m\in I$, and so on down to $s\in I$.
   - *The corners.* So $R=A/I$ and $S=A'/I$ are reduced, $R\subseteq S$ is finite, and $R$ is seminormal in $S$: if $\bar b^2,\bar b^3\in R$, then $b^2,b^3\in A+I=A$, so $b\in A$. Let $Z\subseteq X$ and $E=\nu^{-1}(Z)\subseteq\widetilde X$ be the closed subschemes cut out by $I$. Over $U$ their rings are $R$ and $A'\otimes_AR=A'/IA'=S$, so both are reduced.
   - *Dimension.* For a minimal prime $\mathfrak p$ of $A$, the ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is a field and $A'_{\mathfrak p}\subseteq Q(A)_{\mathfrak p}=A_{\mathfrak p}$, so $\mathfrak p\notin\operatorname{Supp}(A'/A)=V(I)$. Thus $Z$ contains no generic point of $X$, and $\dim Z<\dim X$.
2. *Seminormalizations.* Let $Z^{\mathrm{sn}}\to Z$ and $E^{\mathrm{sn}}\to E$ be the seminormalizations. They are universal homeomorphisms that induce isomorphisms on residue fields, their sources are seminormal, and there is a unique $h:E^{\mathrm{sn}}\to Z^{\mathrm{sn}}$ compatible with $E\to Z$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 29.48.7, Tag 0EUS]. Seminormality of $Z^{\mathrm{sn}}$ is part (4) of that lemma applied to $Z^{\mathrm{sn}}\to Z$ itself. Universal homeomorphisms are integral [@stacks2026, Lemma 29.46.5, Tag 04DF], so over $U$ these schemes are $\operatorname{Spec}R^{\mathrm{sn}}$ and $\operatorname{Spec}S^{\mathrm{sn}}$, and $h$ gives $\varphi:R^{\mathrm{sn}}\to S^{\mathrm{sn}}$. Let $\psi:S\to S^{\mathrm{sn}}$ be the structure map. Seminormal rings are reduced [@stacks2026, Lemma 29.48.5, Tag 0EUQ].
   - *Injectivity.* A ring map whose map on spectra is surjective has nilpotent kernel. $\operatorname{Spec}S^{\mathrm{sn}}\to\operatorname{Spec}S$ is surjective, and $S$ is reduced, so $\psi$ is injective. $\operatorname{Spec}S^{\mathrm{sn}}\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is surjective, by lying over for $R\subseteq S$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 10.36.17, Tag 00GQ], and it factors through the bijection $\operatorname{Spec}R^{\mathrm{sn}}\to\operatorname{Spec}R$. So $\operatorname{Spec}S^{\mathrm{sn}}\to\operatorname{Spec}R^{\mathrm{sn}}$ is surjective, and $\varphi$ is injective.
   - *Finite type.* The minimal primes $\mathfrak q_i$ of the reduced ring $R^{\mathrm{sn}}$ lie over the minimal primes $\mathfrak p_i$ of $R$, with $\kappa(\mathfrak q_i)=\kappa(\mathfrak p_i)$. So $R^{\mathrm{sn}}$ embeds in $\prod_i\kappa(\mathfrak p_i)=Q(R)$ compatibly with $R$, and it is integral over $R$. It therefore lies in the integral closure of $R$ in $Q(R)$, which is finite over $R$ by the references in Step 1. So $Z^{\mathrm{sn}}$ is of finite type over $k$, of dimension $\dim Z<\dim X$, and likewise for $E^{\mathrm{sn}}$.
3. *A cartesian square.* The map $A\to A'\times_{S^{\mathrm{sn}}}R^{\mathrm{sn}}$, $a\mapsto(a,\bar a)$, is bijective. It is injective because $A\subseteq A'$. Let $(b,g)$ satisfy $\psi(\bar b)=\varphi(g)$, and put $D=\psi^{-1}(\varphi(R^{\mathrm{sn}}))$. Then $R\subseteq D\subseteq S$, and $D$ is finite over $R$. Through $\varphi^{-1}\psi$ we have $R\subseteq D\subseteq R^{\mathrm{sn}}$, with $R^{\mathrm{sn}}$ integral over $R$. So $\operatorname{Spec}D\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is surjective by lying over, and injective because $\operatorname{Spec}R^{\mathrm{sn}}\to\operatorname{Spec}D$ is surjective by lying over while $\operatorname{Spec}R^{\mathrm{sn}}\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is bijective. Its residue field extensions lie inside those of $R^{\mathrm{sn}}$ over $R$, which are trivial. The Lemma gives $D=R$. So $\bar b=\bar a$ for some $a\in A$, hence $b-a\in I\subseteq A$ and $b\in A$, and $\varphi(g)=\varphi(\bar a)$ gives $g=\bar a$.
4. *The kernel embeds in a unit group.* Let $i:Z^{\mathrm{sn}}\to X$, $j:E^{\mathrm{sn}}\to X$ and $p:E^{\mathrm{sn}}\to\widetilde X$ be the evident maps, so that $j=\nu p=ih$. Put
   $$W=\frac{\mathcal O(E^{\mathrm{sn}})^\times}{p^*\mathcal O(\widetilde X)^\times\cdot h^*\mathcal O(Z^{\mathrm{sn}})^\times},\qquad K_0=\ker\bigl(\operatorname{Pic}X\to\operatorname{Pic}\widetilde X\times\operatorname{Pic}Z^{\mathrm{sn}}\bigr).$$
   For $L\in K_0$, choose generators $\beta$ of $\nu^*L$ and $\gamma$ of $i^*L$. Then $p^*\beta=u\,h^*\gamma$ for a unique $u\in\mathcal O(E^{\mathrm{sn}})^\times$. Its class $\theta(L)\in W$ does not depend on the choices, and $\theta$ is a homomorphism, by taking tensor products of generators.
   - *$\theta$ is injective.* If $\theta(L)=1$, rescale $\beta$ and $\gamma$ by units so that $p^*\beta=h^*\gamma$. On an affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with a generator $\tau$ of $L|_U$, write $\beta=b\tau$ and $\gamma=r\tau$, with $b\in A'^\times$ and $r\in(R^{\mathrm{sn}})^\times$. Then $\psi(\bar b)=\varphi(r)$, so by Step 3, $(b,r)$ comes from some $a\in A$. It is a unit, since $(b^{-1},r^{-1})$ also comes from $A$.
   - *Gluing.* So $a\tau$ generates $L|_U$. Replacing $\tau$ by $c\tau$ with $c\in A^\times$ divides $a$ by $c$, so $a\tau$ does not depend on $\tau$. These sections are compatible with restriction to smaller affine opens, so they glue to a generator of $L$, and $L\cong\mathcal O_X$.
5. *$W\in\mathcal C$.* By the units input, $\mathcal O(E^{\mathrm{sn}})^\times=\Lambda^\times\times\mathbb Z^m$ with $\Lambda=\Lambda_{E^{\mathrm{sn}}}$, after splitting off the free quotient. Likewise $\mathcal O(\widetilde X)^\times=\Lambda_{\widetilde X}^\times F_1$ and $\mathcal O(Z^{\mathrm{sn}})^\times=\Lambda_{Z^{\mathrm{sn}}}^\times F_2$ with $F_1,F_2$ finitely generated.
   - The images $E_1=p^*\Lambda_{\widetilde X}$ and $E_2=h^*\Lambda_{Z^{\mathrm{sn}}}$ consist of elements integral over $k$, so they are $k$-subalgebras of $\Lambda$.
   - Each is the image of a finite product of fields, so $p^*(\Lambda_{\widetilde X}^\times)=E_1^\times$ and $h^*(\Lambda_{Z^{\mathrm{sn}}}^\times)=E_2^\times$.
   - Hence $W$ is the quotient of $(\Lambda^\times/E_1^\times E_2^\times)\oplus\mathbb Z^m$ by the finitely generated image of $F_1$ and $F_2$. The first summand lies in $\mathcal C$ by the subalgebra input, so $W\in\mathcal C$ by properties 2 and 3.
6. *Conclusion.* $\operatorname{Pic}\widetilde X$ is finitely generated by the normal-scheme input, and $\operatorname{Pic}Z^{\mathrm{sn}}\in\mathcal C$ by induction, since $Z^{\mathrm{sn}}$ is seminormal of finite type and smaller dimension. So the image of $\operatorname{Pic}X$ in their product lies in $\mathcal C$, by properties 1 and 2. By Steps 4 and 5 and property 1, $K_0\in\mathcal C$. By property 2, $\operatorname{Pic}X\in\mathcal C$. $\square$

The hypothesis on $k$ enters only through the two inputs from section 6 of Guralnick–Jaffe–Raskind–Wiegand.
