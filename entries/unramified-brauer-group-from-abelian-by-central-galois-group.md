**Setting.** Let $K/k$ be a function field, with $k$ algebraically closed and $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}(k)$. Write $G_K$ for the absolute Galois group and $C=\Pi^c_K$ for its [maximal pro-$\ell$ class-two quotient](abelian-by-central-galois-group.md). Choose compatible roots of unity to identify $\mu_{\ell^\infty}$ with the trivial discrete module $M=\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$.

For a pro-$\ell$ group $P$, define its Bogomolov multiplier by
$$
B_0(P)=\ker\left(H^2_{\mathrm{cont}}(P,M)\longrightarrow\prod_B H^2_{\mathrm{cont}}(B,M)\right),
$$
where $B$ ranges over closed abelian subgroups topologically generated by at most two elements. This is the bicyclic-restriction definition, extended to pro-$\ell$ groups [@bogomolovtschinkel2012, §7, pp. 33–34].

**Theorem (Bogomolov).** Inflation along $G_K\to C$ identifies
$$
B_0(C)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\operatorname{Br}_{\mathrm{nr}}(K/k)\{\ell\}\subseteq H^2(G_K,M)=\operatorname{Br}(K)\{\ell\}.
$$
Here $\{\ell\}$ denotes the $\ell$-primary subgroup, and unramified means zero residue at every divisorial valuation trivial on $k$. The original theorem identifies the field invariant with the corresponding subgroups of the cohomology of both $G_K$ and its class-two quotient [@bogomolov1992abelian, Theorem 13.2 and preceding paragraph, pp. 60–61]. The pro-$\ell$ formulation is stated explicitly in [@bogomolovtschinkel2012, Theorem 22, p. 35].

**Mechanism.** The proof compares the closed spans of three sorts of relations in the exterior square of the abelianized Galois group: divisorial inertia–decomposition pairs, commuting pairs in the full group, and pairs commuting in the class-two quotient [@bogomolov1992abelian, proof of Theorem 13.2, pp. 60–61]. Thus the unramified subgroup is recognized by group cohomology and subgroup restrictions, without supplying a model of $K/k$.

**Scope.** The theorem concerns the entire pro-$\ell$ class-two quotient and divisible $\ell$-primary coefficients. For arbitrary finite groups, inflation from the class-two quotient gives only an injection of Bogomolov multipliers in general [@bogomolovtschinkel2012, §7, equation (7.1), pp. 33–34; introduction, p. 5].
