**Statement.** Let $G, G'$ be [Demushkin groups](demushkin-group.md) with ranks $n, n'$ and canonical characters $\chi, \chi'$. Then $G \cong G'$ if and only if $n = n'$ and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \mathrm{Im}(\chi')$ as subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. The "if" direction is [@labute1967, p. 107, Remark (3)], and the "only if" direction holds because $n$ and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ are invariants of the group [@labute1967, Corollary to Theorem 4].

**Normal forms.** Let $F$ be the free pro-$p$ group on $x_1, \dots, x_n$, and write $(x, y) = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. Put $U_p^{(f)} = 1 + p^f\mathbb{Z}_p$, and let $U_2^{[f]}$ be the closed subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ generated by $-1 + 2^f$ [@labute1967, pp. 106–107].

1. *$q \ne 2$ (Demushkin).* Then $n$ is even, and $G \cong F/(r)$ with
   $$r = x_1^q(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{n-1}, x_n).$$
   Conversely, for every even $n$ and every $q = p^g$ with $g \ge 1$ or $g = \infty$, this relation defines a Demushkin group with these invariants, and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = U_p^{(g)}$ [@labute1967, p. 106]. Serre gives the same statement [@serre1963, Théorème 3.1].
2. *$q = 2$ and $n$ odd (Serre).* Then $r = x_1^2 x_2^{2^f}(x_2, x_3)(x_4, x_5) \cdots (x_{n-1}, x_n)$ for some $f \ge 2$ or $f = \infty$. Every such relation defines a Demushkin group with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\} \times U_2^{(f)}$ [@labute1967, p. 107]. Serre states this for $n \ge 3$, with $k = 2^f$ or $k = 0$ in place of $2^f$ [@serre1963, Théorème 3.2].
3. *$q = 2$ and $n$ even (Labute).* Let $A = \mathrm{Im}(\chi)$. Then [@labute1967, Theorem 1]:
   $$r = x_1^{2+2^f}(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{n-1}, x_n) \quad \text{if } (A : A^2) = 2, \text{ where } A = U_2^{[f]} \text{ and } f \ge 2 \text{ or } f = \infty,$$
   $$r = x_1^2(x_1, x_2)\, x_3^{2^f}(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{n-1}, x_n) \quad \text{if } (A : A^2) = 4, \text{ where } A = \{\pm 1\} \times U_2^{(f)} \text{ and } f \ge 2.$$
   Every relation of the first form with $n \ge 2$, and of the second form with $n \ge 4$, defines a Demushkin group with the stated $A$ [@labute1967, Theorem 1].

**Why $\chi$ is needed.** For $q = 2$ the invariants $n$ and $q$ no longer determine $G$, apart from the trivial case $n = 1$ where $G = \mathbb{Z}/2$ [@serre1963, p. 147]. Serre classified the case $q = 2$ with $n$ odd and asked whether, for $n$ even and $p = 2$, Demushkin groups are still characterized by $n$ and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ [@serre1963, 10.1]. Labute's Theorem 1 answers this, and he notes that Demushkin proved it independently. Labute also reports that Theorem 2 of Demushkin's paper on this case is incorrect [@labute1967, p. 108]. In fact Labute proves more: two Demushkin relations with the same $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ differ by an automorphism of $F$ [@labute1967, Theorem 2].

**Method.** One first puts the relation in normal form modulo the third term of a filtration of $F$, using that the cup product is nondegenerate. Then one corrects it by successive approximation along the filtration [@serre1963, §6]. For $p \ne 2$ and $q \ne 0$ the graded object is a free Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Z}/q[\pi]$, where $\pi$ comes from the $q$-th power map [@serre1963, §6]. For $q = 2$ squaring no longer corresponds to multiplication by $\pi$, and Serre uses $\chi$ to tell the resulting normal forms apart [@serre1963, §7].

The main application is to [maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md).
