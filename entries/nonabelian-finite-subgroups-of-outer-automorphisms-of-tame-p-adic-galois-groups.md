**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd. Use the notation of [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on norm-one units](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md), proof of part 2, which follows Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 419–423]: $G = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, $V \subset G$ the wild inertia group, $V^2 = V^p[V,V]$ and $V^3 = (V^2)^p[V^2,V]$, $T$ the maximal tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $\mathcal{G} = G/V = \mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_p)$ with generators $\sigma, \tau$ such that $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^p$, where $\tau$ generates tame inertia, a splitting $G = V \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, and a surjection $F = P_2 \rtimes \mathcal{G} \to G$, where $P_2$ is the free pro-$p$ $\mathcal{G}$-operator group on $x_0, x_1$. Let $\omega : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ give the action on $\mu_p$, so that $\omega(\sigma) = 1$ and $\omega(\tau)$ has order $p - 1$. Let $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathcal{G}]]$, with the continuous $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear anti-automorphism $*$ given by $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$. Levels $K'$, the groups $\Gamma' = \mathrm{Gal}(K'/\mathbb{Q}_p)$, and the elements $\kappa_{K'}, \lambda_{K'} \in \mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$ are as in that entry.

Use also the notation of step (a) of the proof of [finite abelian subgroups](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md): the characters $\xi$ of tame inertia with values in $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$, their Frobenius orbits $O(\xi) = \{\xi^{p^k} : k \ge 0\}$, the involution $\iota(O) = \{\omega\xi^{-1} : \xi \in O\}$ on orbits, the idempotents $t_\xi$, and the central idempotents $T_O \in \Lambda$. By properties 1–5 there, the $T_O$ are compatible across levels, orthogonal, with sum $1$ at each level, $\tau T_O = \sum_{\xi \in O}\xi(\tau)t_\xi$, $T_O^* = T_{\iota(O)}$, and $\lambda_{K'} = T_{\{\omega\}}$ at every level $K'$.

The group $\mathcal{G}$ acts on $P_2$ by $\rho \cdot x = \rho x \rho^{-1}$, which makes the Frattini quotient $P_2/\Phi(P_2) = \Lambda\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1$ a free left $\Lambda$-module. In Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's notation, $x^\rho = \rho x\rho^{-1}$ and $y_1$ is the element in the relation of [the Jannsen–Wingberg presentation](local-galois-group-presentations.md) [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.14), pp. 419–420]. They let $\delta \in \Lambda$ be the element with $y_1 \equiv \delta\cdot\bar x_1$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423]; part (d) of the proof checks that $y_1 \in P_2$. Put
$$\delta_0 = \delta - \delta^*.$$
Let $\pi_p, \pi_2 \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ be the idempotents with $\pi_p\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{Z}_p$ and $\pi_2\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{Z}_2$, and put $\pi' = 1 - \pi_p$, $\sigma_2 = \sigma^{\pi_2}$, $\tau_2 = \tau^{\pi_2}$ and $\sigma_{p'} = \sigma^{\pi'}$. For $c \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, $\sigma^c\tau\sigma^{-c} = \tau^{p^c}$ with $p^c \in \prod_{r \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_r^\times$ defined by continuity.

**Theorem.** This is proved here, by running a construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg with noncentral units and applying a splitting theorem of Nishio.

1. *Unitary units.* Let $\mathcal{U}$ be the set of $v \in \Lambda^\times$ such that $v^*\delta_0 v = \delta_0$ and $\lambda_{K'}v = \lambda_{K'}$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$ for every level $K'$. Then $\mathcal{U}$ is a subgroup of $\Lambda^\times$. For every $v \in \mathcal{U}$ there is an automorphism $\theta_v$ of $G$ that induces the identity on $G/V$ and induces on $V/V^2$ the $\Lambda$-linear map with $\bar x_0 \mapsto \bar x_0$ and $\bar x_1 \mapsto v\cdot\bar x_1$. The automorphism $\Phi(v)$ of $G/V^2$ induced by $\theta_v$ depends only on $v$, and $\Phi : \mathcal{U} \to \mathrm{Aut}(G/V^2)$ is injective with $\Phi(v)\Phi(v') = \Phi(v'v)$.
2. *Frobenius groups.* Let $\ell \ne p$ be an odd prime such that the order $f$ of $p$ modulo $\ell$ is odd, at least $3$, and prime to $p$. Then $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has a subgroup isomorphic to the nonabelian group $\mathbb{Z}/\ell \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/f$, in which a generator of $\mathbb{Z}/f$ acts on $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$ as multiplication by $p$, whose elements all induce the identity on $G/V$. It maps isomorphically onto a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G)$.
3. *Existence.* For every odd $p$ there is such a prime $\ell$. For instance $(p, \ell, f) = (3, 11, 5)$, $(5, 31, 3)$ and $(7, 19, 3)$ qualify. So $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ has nonabelian finite subgroups for every odd $p$.
4. *Tame fields.* Let $K \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ be a finite tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Restriction to $G_K$ embeds the subgroup of part 2 in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$, and the image maps isomorphically onto a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$.

**Context.** The finite subgroups of $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ found earlier in the knowledgebase are abelian: they come from central units of $\Lambda$ ([finite abelian subgroups](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md), [groups of order prime to $p$](prime-to-p-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). Nishio's hyperoctahedral subgroups need $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 3$ ([finite subgroups of outer automorphism groups](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md)). The new observation is that in Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's computation the centrality of their unit $\varepsilon$ enters only through $\varepsilon\delta\varepsilon^* = \delta$ and $\varepsilon\lambda_{K'} = \lambda_{K'}$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423], and that only the skew part $\delta_0$ of $\delta$ matters. The element $y_1$ is built from $x_1$ and the $2$-parts $\sigma_2, \tau_2$ only. On the block of $\Lambda$ cut out by a Frobenius orbit of odd length of characters of odd order, $\tau_2$ acts trivially and $\sigma_2$ is central, so every unit of that block, paired with its adjoint on the block $\iota(O)$, is unitary. Such a block maps onto a matrix algebra $\mathrm{M}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$, which contains the Frobenius group. In fact the block contains a copy of $\mathrm{M}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$ with the same identity, and this gives [every finite group of order prime to $p$](prime-to-p-finite-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md).

- By consequence 1 of the [Nishio entry](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), no nontrivial element of the image of these groups in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ lies in a quasi-geometric subgroup, in particular in any conjugate of $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$. For $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$ every nontrivial finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ fails to be quasi-geometric, by consequence 2 there.
- Whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ has elements of order $p$ is not settled here; the splitting step cannot produce them, because the kernel it splits off is pro-$p$. The case $p = 2$ is not covered, because the construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg assumes $p$ odd.
- *Literature.* The statement was not found in the sources opened for this entry, which include Nishio's paper on finite subgroups.

**Proof.**

*(a) Graded pieces and two identities.* Let $K'$ be a level, $F_{K'}$ the inverse image of $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K')$ in $F$, $F_{K'}(p)$ its maximal pro-$p$ quotient with $p$-central series $F_{K'}(p)^i$, and $\mathrm{gr}^i = F_{K'}(p)^i/F_{K'}(p)^{i+1}$ for $i = 1, 2$, written additively. Conjugation by $\mathcal{G} \subset F$ makes $\mathrm{gr}^1$ and $\mathrm{gr}^2$ left $\Lambda$-modules. Elements of $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K')$ lie in $F_{K'}$ and act by inner automorphisms, so the action factors through $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$, and $\mathrm{gr}^2$ is an $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space. As $P_2 \subseteq F_{K'}$, there is a $\Lambda$-linear map $P_2/\Phi(P_2) \to \mathrm{gr}^1$. The commutator $[y, z] = yzy^{-1}z^{-1}$ induces a biadditive map $\mathrm{gr}^1 \times \mathrm{gr}^1 \to \mathrm{gr}^2$ with $[y, z] = -[z, y]$ and $\rho\cdot[y, z] = [\rho\cdot y, \rho\cdot z]$. As $p$ is odd, the $p$-th power map $P : \mathrm{gr}^1 \to \mathrm{gr}^2$ is additive, and it commutes with conjugation, so it is $\Lambda$-linear. Write $\kappa\lambda = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$; it satisfies $\kappa\lambda\rho = \omega(\rho)\kappa\lambda$ for $\rho \in \Gamma'$ (the companion entry, proof of part 2 (d)).

- (i) For $a \in \mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$ and $y, z \in \mathrm{gr}^1$, $\kappa\lambda\cdot[a\cdot y, z] = \kappa\lambda\cdot[y, a^*\cdot z]$. Indeed $[\rho y\rho^{-1}, z] = \rho[y, \rho^{-1}z\rho]\rho^{-1}$, so $[\rho\cdot y, z] = \rho\cdot[y, \rho^{-1}\cdot z]$, and multiplying by $\kappa\lambda$ gives $\omega(\rho)\kappa\lambda\cdot[y, \rho^{-1}\cdot z] = \kappa\lambda\cdot[y, \rho^*\cdot z]$. The general case follows by linearity. This is the identity of the companion entry.
- (ii) If $c \in \mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$ satisfies $c^* = c$, then $\kappa\lambda\cdot[y, c\cdot y] = 0$. Indeed, by (i), $\kappa\lambda\cdot[c\cdot y, y] = \kappa\lambda\cdot[y, c\cdot y]$, while $[c\cdot y, y] = -[y, c\cdot y]$. So $2\kappa\lambda\cdot[y, c\cdot y] = 0$, and $p$ is odd.

*(b) Proof of part 1.* If $v, w \in \mathcal{U}$, then $(vw)^*\delta_0(vw) = w^*(v^*\delta_0v)w = \delta_0$ and $\lambda_{K'}vw = \lambda_{K'}w = \lambda_{K'}$. Multiplying $v^*\delta_0v = \delta_0$ on the left by $(v^*)^{-1} = (v^{-1})^*$ and on the right by $v^{-1}$ gives $(v^{-1})^*\delta_0v^{-1} = \delta_0$, and $\lambda_{K'} = \lambda_{K'}v$ gives $\lambda_{K'}v^{-1} = \lambda_{K'}$. So $\mathcal{U}$ is a subgroup.

Let $v \in \mathcal{U}$. As Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg do for $\varepsilon$, define an endomorphism $\psi$ of $F$ by fixing $\sigma, \tau, x_0$ and sending $x_1$ to an element of $P_2$ with class $v\cdot\bar x_1$, a product of conjugates of powers of $x_1$ in some order. It is an automorphism, because it is one modulo the Frattini subgroup, $v$ being a unit [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422]. It fixes $\mathcal{G}$ pointwise, so it preserves $P_2$ and each $F_{K'}$, satisfies $\psi(\rho\cdot z) = \rho\cdot\psi(z)$, and induces on $P_2/\Phi(P_2)$ the $\Lambda$-linear map $\bar x_0 \mapsto \bar x_0$, $\bar x_1 \mapsto v\cdot\bar x_1$.

Let $K'$ be a level. By Jannsen and Wingberg, as quoted by Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, the Demuškin relation $r_{K'}$ of $G_{K'}(p)$, as a quotient of $F_{K'}(p)$, satisfies
$$r_{K'} \equiv (x_0^{-\sigma}\langle x_0, \tau\rangle)^{\kappa\lambda}\,(x_1^p)^{\kappa\lambda}\,[x_1, y_1]^{\kappa\lambda} \bmod F_{K'}(p)^3,$$
which is the first line of their computation with $\varepsilon = 1$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423]. Apply $\psi$ to the three factors.

- The first is a word in $x_0$ and elements of $\mathcal{G}$, all fixed by $\psi$.
- The second has class $\kappa\lambda\cdot P(\bar x_1)$ in $\mathrm{gr}^2$. Its image has class $\kappa\lambda\cdot P(v\cdot\bar x_1) = \kappa\lambda v\cdot P(\bar x_1) = \kappa\lambda\cdot P(\bar x_1)$, since $\lambda_{K'}v = \lambda_{K'}$.
- The classes of $\psi(x_1)$ and $\psi(y_1)$ in $\mathrm{gr}^1$ are $v\cdot\bar x_1$ and $\delta v\cdot\bar x_1$. By (i), the image of the third factor has class $\kappa\lambda\cdot[v\cdot\bar x_1, \delta v\cdot\bar x_1] = \kappa\lambda\cdot[\bar x_1, v^*\delta v\cdot\bar x_1]$. The element $c = v^*\delta v - \delta$ satisfies $c - c^* = v^*\delta_0v - \delta_0 = 0$, so by (ii) this equals $\kappa\lambda\cdot[\bar x_1, \delta\cdot\bar x_1]$, the class of $[x_1, y_1]^{\kappa\lambda}$.

So $\psi(r_{K'}) \equiv r_{K'}$ modulo $F_{K'}(p)^3$ for every level $K'$, which is the congruence Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg prove for their $\varepsilon$. Their argument then goes through unchanged: $\psi$ induces an automorphism of each $G_{K'}(p)/G_{K'}(p)^3$, hence, in the limit, an automorphism of $G/V^3$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, and by (7.5.15) there is $\theta_v \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ that agrees with it modulo $V^2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15), p. 421, and p. 423].

So $\Phi(v)$ is the automorphism of $G/V^2 = (V/V^2) \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ induced by $\psi$. It fixes the image of $\mathcal{G}$, so its restriction to $V/V^2$ is $\Lambda$-linear, and it maps $\bar x_0 \mapsto \bar x_0$ and $\bar x_1 \mapsto v\cdot\bar x_1$. As $V/V^2 = \Lambda\bar x_0 + \Lambda\bar x_1$ and the image of $\mathcal{G}$ generate $G/V^2$, $\Phi(v)$ depends only on $v$. Both $\Phi(v)\Phi(v')$ and $\Phi(v'v)$ fix $\mathcal{G}$ and $\bar x_0$, and $\Phi(v)\Phi(v')(\bar x_1) = \Phi(v)(v'\cdot\bar x_1) = v'v\cdot\bar x_1$, so they agree. If $\Phi(v) = 1$, then $(v - 1)\cdot\bar x_1 = 0$, so $v = 1$, because $\bar x_1$ generates a free $\Lambda$-module in $V/V^2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423]. $\square$

*(c) A block on which the $2$-parts are central.* Let $\ell$ and $f$ be as in part 2. Then $\ell \nmid p - 1$, since $f > 1$. Choose a character $\xi$ of tame inertia of order $\ell$, and put $O = O(\xi)$. Since $\xi^{p^k} = \xi$ exactly when $p^k \equiv 1 \pmod \ell$, $O$ has $f$ elements, and their values at $\tau$ lie in $\mathbb{F}_{p^f}$. The characters in $\iota(O)$ have order $\ell(p-1)$, because $\gcd(\ell, p - 1) = 1$. So $O$, $\iota(O)$, $\{1\}$ and $\{\omega\}$ are four distinct orbits. Write $R = T_O\Lambda$, a ring with identity $T_O$, and $z \mapsto T_Oz$ for the continuous ring homomorphism $\Lambda \to R$.

- *$T_O\sigma^f$ is central in $R$.* As $\sigma^f\tau\sigma^{-f} = \tau^{p^f}$ and $\xi'(\tau)^{p^f} = \xi'(\tau)$ for $\xi' \in O$, we get $T_O\tau^{p^f} = \sum_{\xi' \in O}\xi'(\tau)^{p^f}t_{\xi'} = T_O\tau$, so $T_O\sigma^f$ commutes with $T_O\tau$. It commutes with $T_O\sigma$, and $R$ is topologically spanned by products of $T_O\sigma^{\pm 1}$ and $T_O\tau^{\pm 1}$.
- *$T_O\tau_2 = T_O$.* The values $\xi'(\tau)$, $\xi' \in O$, have odd order $\ell$, and $\pi_2 \equiv 0$ modulo $\ell$, so $\xi'(\tau)^{\pi_2} = 1$.
- *$T_O\sigma_2$ is central in $R$.* As $f$ is odd, $\pi_2 = f\cdot c'$ with $c' \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, whose $2$-component is $1/f$ and whose other components are $0$. So $\sigma_2 = (\sigma^f)^{c'}$ is a limit of powers of $\sigma^f$.

Let $H$ be the closed subgroup of $\mathcal{G}$ generated by $\sigma_2$ and $\tau_2$, and $\Lambda_H \subseteq \Lambda$ the image of $\mathbb{F}_p[[H]]$, a closed subring stable under $*$, since $h^* = \omega(h)h^{-1}$. Every $h \in H$ is a limit of words in $\sigma_2^{\pm 1}, \tau_2^{\pm 1}$, so $T_Oh$ is a limit of powers of $T_O\sigma_2$. Hence $T_O\Lambda_H$ lies in the centre of $R$, which is closed.

*(d) $\delta$ lies in $\Lambda_H$.* Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg give
$$y_1 = x_1^{\tau_2^{p+1}}\,\{x_1, \tau_2^{p+1}\}^{\sigma_2\tau_2^a}\,\{\{x_1, \tau_2^{p+1}\}, \sigma_2\tau_2^a\}^{\sigma_2\tau_2^b + \tau_2^{(p+1)/2}},$$
with integers $a, b$, where $\{x, \rho\} = (x^{\beta(1)}\rho^2x^{\beta(\rho)}\rho^2\cdots x^{\beta(\rho^{p-2})}\rho^2)^{\pi_p/(p-1)}$ for a function $\beta : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, and an exponent that is a sum of group elements denotes the product of the corresponding conjugates [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 420]. The group $H$ is pro-$2$: the closed subgroup generated by $\tau_2$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_2$ and normalized by $\sigma_2$, and the closed subgroup generated by $\sigma_2$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_2$. Let $x \in P_2$ and $\rho \in H$. The product inside $\{x, \rho\}$ maps to $\rho^{2(p-1)}$ in $\mathcal{G}$, and $(\rho^{2(p-1)})^{\pi_p/(p-1)} = 1$ because $\rho$ lies in a pro-$2$ group and $\pi_p/(p-1)$ has $2$-component $0$. So $\{x, \rho\} \in P_2$. Hence each factor of $y_1$ lies in $P_2$, and so does $y_1$.

Let $F_H \subseteq F$ be the closed subgroup generated by $x_1$ and $H$, so $y_1 \in F_H$, and let $N_1$ be the closed subgroup generated by the $hx_1h^{-1}$, $h \in H$. It is normalized by $H$ and contains $x_1$, so it is normal in $F_H$, and $F_H = N_1H$. As $N_1 \subseteq P_2$ and $H \cap P_2 = 1$ in $F = P_2 \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, $F_H \cap P_2 = N_1$. So $y_1 \in N_1$. The image of $N_1$ in $P_2/\Phi(P_2)$ is the closure of the subgroup generated by the $h\cdot\bar x_1$, which is $\Lambda_H\cdot\bar x_1$, since $\mathbb{F}_p[[H]]$ is compact. So $\delta\cdot\bar x_1 \in \Lambda_H\cdot\bar x_1$, and $\delta \in \Lambda_H$ because $\Lambda\bar x_1$ is free. As $\Lambda_H$ is stable under $*$, $\delta_0 \in \Lambda_H$, and by (c) $T_O\delta_0$ is central in $R$.

*(e) A Frobenius group of units of the block.* Let $S$ be the closed subgroup of $\mathcal{G}$ generated by $\sigma_{p'}^f$. It is procyclic of order prime to $p$, so $\mathbb{F}_p[[S]]$ contains the averaging idempotent $e_S$, the limit of $\frac1n\sum_{s \in S/S^n}s$ over $n$ prime to $p$, with $se_S = e_S$ for $s \in S$. Its image in $\Lambda$ is again denoted $e_S$. Since $\sigma_{p'}^f = (\sigma^f)^{\pi'}$, (c) shows that $T_Os$ is central in $R$ for $s \in S$. For $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$ this gives $T_O\rho^{-1}s\rho = (T_O\rho^{-1})(T_Os)(T_O\rho) = T_Os$, and passing to the limit, $\rho^{-1}(T_Oe_S)\rho = T_Oe_S$. So $e_1 = T_Oe_S$ is a central idempotent of $\Lambda$. Put
$$a = e_1\tau, \qquad b = e_1\sigma_{p'},$$
units of the ring $e_1\Lambda$, with identity $e_1$.

- $a^\ell = e_1$, since $T_O\tau^\ell = T_O$; and $b^f = e_1\sigma_{p'}^f = e_1$, since $\sigma_{p'}^f \in S$.
- $bab^{-1} = e_1\tau^{p^{\pi'}} = a^p$. Indeed, $T_O\tau^m$ depends only on $m$ modulo $\ell$, and $p^{\pi'} \equiv p \pmod \ell$, because $c \mapsto p^c \bmod \ell$ factors through $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}/f\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $\pi' \equiv 1 \pmod f$, as $f$ is prime to $p$.

So $\alpha \mapsto a$, $\beta \mapsto b$ defines a homomorphism to $(e_1\Lambda)^\times$ from the group $\Pi = \langle \alpha, \beta \mid \alpha^\ell, \beta^f, \beta\alpha\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-p}\rangle$, which is the semidirect product $\mathbb{Z}/\ell \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/f$ of order $\ell f$, as $p^f \equiv 1 \pmod \ell$. It is injective. To see this, let $\mathcal{N}$ be the closed normal subgroup of $\mathcal{G}$ generated by $\tau^\ell$ and $\sigma^f$. Then $\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N} \cong \Pi$, with $\tau, \sigma \mapsto \alpha, \beta$, because $\sigma^f$ is central modulo $\tau^\ell$. Under $\Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p[\Pi]$, $e_S \mapsto 1$, and $T_O$ maps to $\bar T_O = \sum_{\xi' \in O}\bar t_{\xi'}$ with $\bar t_{\xi'} = \frac1\ell\sum_{i<\ell}\xi'(\tau)^{-i}\alpha^i$, by grouping exponents modulo $\ell$ as in property 5 of the finite-abelian entry. So the composite $\Pi \to \mathbb{F}_p[\Pi]$ is $g \mapsto \bar T_O g$, as $\sigma_{p'} \mapsto \beta^{\pi'} = \beta$. Suppose $\bar T_O\alpha^i\beta^j = \bar T_O$, and compute in $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p[\Pi]$, where the $\bar t_{\xi'}$ are nonzero orthogonal idempotents commuting with $\alpha$, with $\alpha\bar t_{\xi'} = \xi'(\tau)\bar t_{\xi'}$. Conjugation by $\beta$ maps $\bar t_{\xi'}$ to $\bar t_{\xi'^{p'}}$ with $pp' \equiv 1 \pmod \ell$, as in property 2 there. If $j \not\equiv 0 \pmod f$, then $\beta^j\bar t_\xi\beta^{-j} = \bar t_{\xi''}$ with $\xi'' \ne \xi$, so multiplying the assumed equality by $\bar t_\xi$ on both sides gives $\bar t_\xi = \xi(\tau)^i\,\bar t_\xi\bar t_{\xi''}\beta^j = 0$, which is false. If $j \equiv 0$, then $\bar T_O\alpha^i = \sum_{\xi' \in O}\xi'(\tau)^i\bar t_{\xi'}$ equals $\bar T_O$ only if $\ell \mid i$. So $\langle a, b\rangle \cong \Pi$. It is nonabelian, since $p \not\equiv 1 \pmod \ell$, and its order $\ell f$ is prime to $p$.

*(f) Unitary units from the block.* As $*$ is an anti-automorphism, $e_1^* = e_S^*T_{\iota(O)}$ is a central idempotent, and $e_1e_1^* = 0$ because $T_OT_{\iota(O)} = 0$. For $g \in \langle a, b\rangle$ put
$$v_g = (1 - e_1 - e_1^*) + g + (g^*)^{-1},$$
where $g^*$ is a unit of $e_1^*\Lambda$. Then $v_g$ is a unit, with inverse $(1 - e_1 - e_1^*) + g^{-1} + g^*$, and $g \mapsto v_g$ is an injective homomorphism, because $(gh)^* = h^*g^*$. Moreover $v_g \in \mathcal{U}$.

- *Unitarity.* Here $v_g^* = (1 - e_1 - e_1^*) + g^* + g^{-1}$. Decompose along the central orthogonal idempotents $e_1$, $e_1^*$ and $1 - e_1 - e_1^*$. On the third, $v_g$ and $v_g^*$ are $1$. On $e_1$, $v_g^*\delta_0v_g$ is $g^{-1}(e_1\delta_0)g$, which is $e_1\delta_0$, because $e_1\delta_0 = e_S\cdot T_O\delta_0$ is central in $R$ by (c) and (d), and $g \in R$. On $e_1^*$, it is $g^*(e_1^*\delta_0)(g^*)^{-1}$. Applying $*$ to $g(e_1\delta_0) = (e_1\delta_0)g$ and using $(e_1\delta_0)^* = \delta_0^*e_1^* = -e_1^*\delta_0$ shows that $g^*$ commutes with $e_1^*\delta_0$. So $v_g^*\delta_0v_g = \delta_0$.
- *The level condition.* At a level $K'$, $\lambda_{K'} = T_{\{\omega\}}$, and $T_{\{\omega\}}T_O = T_{\{\omega\}}T_{\iota(O)} = 0$ as $\{\omega\} \ne O, \iota(O)$. So $\lambda_{K'}e_1 = \lambda_{K'}e_1^* = 0$, and $\lambda_{K'}v_g = \lambda_{K'}$.

The same computation gives $v_g\delta_0v_g^* = \delta_0$ and $v_g\lambda_{K'} = \lambda_{K'}$, so the conclusion does not depend on whether exponents are read as a left or a right action.

*(g) Proof of part 2.* Let $Y = \{v_g : g \in \langle a, b\rangle\} \cong \Pi$. By part 1, $\Phi(Y)$ is a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G/V^2)$, and $g \mapsto \Phi(v_{g^{-1}})$ is an isomorphism from $\langle a, b\rangle$ onto it. Every element of $\Phi(Y)$ is induced by an automorphism of $G$. Every automorphism of $G$ preserves $V$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), hence $V^2$. Let $\Delta \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ be the subgroup of automorphisms inducing an element of $\Phi(Y)$ on $G/V^2$; it maps onto $\Phi(Y)$, a group of order $\ell f$ prime to $p$. Nishio's Corollary 1.4, (ii) implies (i), with $(J, P, i, s) = (G, V, 2, 1)$, so that $P^{p,2,1} = V^2$ and the automorphisms preserving $V$ form all of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$, applies. Its proof splits $1 \to \mathrm{Aut}_{G/V^2}(G) \to \Delta \to \Phi(Y) \to 1$, whose kernel is pro-$p$ by his Theorem 1.2 [@nishio2026finite, Theorem 1.2, p. 6; Corollary 1.4 and its proof, p. 7]. This is step (d) of the finite-abelian entry. It gives a subgroup $\Gamma \subseteq \Delta$ isomorphic to $\Pi$, whose elements induce elements of $\Phi(Y)$ on $G/V^2$, hence the identity on $G/V$. By consequence 1 of the [Nishio entry](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), the finite group $\Gamma$ maps isomorphically into $\mathrm{Out}(G)$. $\square$

*(h) Proof of part 4.* This is step (f) of the finite-abelian entry. As $K$ is tamely ramified, $V \subseteq G_K$, and each $\gamma \in \Gamma$ satisfies $\gamma(g) \in gV$, so $\gamma(G_K) = G_K$. If $\gamma$ is the identity on $G_K$, then for $g \in G$ the element $g^{-1}\gamma(g)$ centralizes the open subgroup $G_K \cap g^{-1}G_Kg$, so it is $1$ because $G$ is slim, and $\gamma = 1$. The image of $\Gamma$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ is finite, so it maps isomorphically into $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ by consequence 1 of the Nishio entry. $\square$

*(i) Proof of part 3.* Let $f = 3$ if $p \ne 3$ and $f = 5$ if $p = 3$, so that $f$ is an odd prime different from $p$. Put $N = (p^f - 1)/(p - 1) = 1 + p + \dots + p^{f-1}$. It is odd, as a sum of $f$ odd numbers, and $N > f$. If $f \mid N$, then $p^f \equiv 1 \pmod f$, while $p^f \equiv p$ by Fermat, so $p = 1 + fk$ for an integer $k$; then $p^i \equiv 1 + ifk \pmod{f^2}$, and $N \equiv f + fk\cdot\frac{f(f-1)}{2} \equiv f \pmod{f^2}$, as $f$ is odd. So $f^2 \nmid N$, and $N$ has a prime factor $\ell \ne f$. Then $\ell$ is odd and $\ell \ne p$, and the order of $p$ modulo $\ell$ divides the prime $f$. If it were $1$, then $p \equiv 1 \pmod \ell$, so $N \equiv f \pmod \ell$ and $\ell \mid f$, which is false. So the order is $f$, which is odd, at least $3$ and prime to $p$. In the examples, $3^5 = 243 = 22\cdot 11 + 1$, $5^3 = 125 = 4\cdot 31 + 1$ and $7^3 = 343 = 18\cdot 19 + 1$, and $11 \nmid 3^1 - 1$, $31 \nmid 5 - 1$, $19 \nmid 7 - 1$. $\square$
