**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite, with residue field of order $q$, and $G = G_K$. As in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), $G$ is centre-free, elastic and topologically finitely generated, and it is identified with $\mathrm{Inn}(G)$, a closed normal subgroup of the profinite group $A = \mathrm{Aut}(G)$; conjugation by $\alpha \in A$ restricts to $\alpha$ on $G$. A *rival copy* is a closed normal subgroup $N \ne G$ of $A$ with $N \cong G$. Put $M = N \cap G$.

Let $I \supseteq P$ be the inertia and wild inertia subgroups of $G$, $\Gamma = G/P = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$ and $I_\Gamma = I/P$. Choose a Frobenius lift $\sigma$ and a topological generator $\tau$ of $I_\Gamma$. By the Lemma in [the Frobenius-shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md) and its proof, $x \mapsto \tau^x$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell \to I_\Gamma$, every element of $\Gamma$ is $\tau^a\sigma^b$ with $b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, and $q^b = 1$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ only for $b = 0$. A closed subgroup $X$ of $I_\Gamma$ corresponds to a closed $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$-submodule of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, which is the product of its $\ell$-components $X_\ell \subseteq \mathbb{Z}_\ell$, each $0$ or $\ell^k\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ (multiply by the idempotents of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$). Put $S(X) = \{\ell : X_\ell \ne 0\}$. For a set $S$ of primes other than $p$ and $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, let $q_S^y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ have $\ell$-component $q^y$ for $\ell \in S$ and $1$ for $\ell \notin S$.

Write $y_G : G \to G/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ for the map sending Frobenius to $1$. As $I_\Gamma \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$ as a module over $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ur}}/K)$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)) and $I_\Gamma$ is abelian, conjugation by $g \in G$ acts on $I_\Gamma$ by $t \mapsto t^{q^{y_G(g)}}$. Every $\alpha \in A$ preserves $I$ and $P$ ([invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md)), so it acts on the procyclic group $I_\Gamma$ by $t \mapsto t^{u_\alpha}$ for a unique $u_\alpha \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$, its *tame twist*. It also maps Frobenius in $G/I$ to Frobenius (same entry), which generates $G/I$ topologically, so $\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/I$. Call $\alpha$ *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$, and let $A_{\mathrm{tt}}$ be the closed normal subgroup of such automorphisms. Let $V = P/\Phi(P)$, where $\Phi(P)$ is the closure of $P^p[P, P]$.

For $N \cong G$, fix an isomorphism $\theta : N \to G$ and put $I_N = \theta^{-1}(I)$, $P_N = \theta^{-1}(P)$ and $y_N = y_G \circ \theta$. These do not depend on $\theta$: two choices differ by an automorphism of $G$, which preserves $I$ and $P$ and is the identity on $G/I$. Transporting the facts above, conjugation by $n \in N$ acts on $I_N/P_N$ by $t \mapsto t^{q^{y_N(n)}}$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $N$ be a rival copy of $G = G_K$, and $M = N \cap G$.

1. *Normal pieces.* $P_N$ and $I_N$ are normal in $A$. Put $Q = P_N \cap P$ and $J = I_N \cap I$. Then
$$Q = P_N \cap G = P \cap N = M \cap P = M \cap P_N, \qquad J = I_N \cap G = I \cap N = M \cap I = M \cap I_N.$$
2. *Commutators.* $[N, G] \subseteq J$, $[P_N, G] \subseteq Q$ and $[N, P] \subseteq Q$. So every element of $P_N$ is tame-trivial, and conjugation by elements of $P$ induces the identity on $N/P_N$.
3. *Layers of $M$.* $Q \ne 1$, and $Q$ is not topologically finitely generated and has infinite index in $G$ and in $N$. The group $J/Q$ is procyclic of order prime to $p$, and maps isomorphically onto the closed subgroup $\bar J = JP/P$ of $I_\Gamma$ and onto $JP_N/P_N \subseteq I_N/P_N$. The group $M/J$ embeds in $G/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and in $N/I_N \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$.
4. *Action on the tame quotient.* Put $S = S(\bar J)$. Every $n \in N$ induces on $\Gamma$ an automorphism $\gamma \mapsto \gamma'$ with $\gamma'\gamma^{-1} \in \bar J$ for all $\gamma$, and its tame twist is $u_n = q_S^{y_N(n)}$. For every $\ell \in S$, $\bar J_\ell \supseteq (q - 1)\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. Symmetrically, conjugation by $g \in G$ acts on $I_N/P_N$ by $t \mapsto t^{q_S^{y_G(g)}}$.
5. *Dichotomy.* The following are equivalent: (a) $S = \varnothing$; (b) $M \subseteq P$; (c) $N \subseteq A_{\mathrm{tt}}$. In that case $M = Q$, and $NG/P$ is the internal direct product of $\Gamma = G/P$ and its centralizer $NP/P \cong N/Q \cong NG/G$. If $S \ne \varnothing$, then $MP/P \supseteq \bar J$ contains every $\tau^x$ with $x_\ell \in (q-1)\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ for $\ell \in S$ and $x_\ell = 0$ for $\ell \notin S$, that is, the $S$-part of the closed commutator subgroup $[\Gamma, \Gamma] = \overline{\langle \tau^{q-1} \rangle}$.
6. *Images in $\mathrm{Out}(G)$.* $NG/G$ is a closed normal prosolvable subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G)$. $P_NG/G \cong P_N/Q$ is a closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ consisting of classes of tame-trivial automorphisms, and it is trivial exactly when $P_N \subseteq P$.

**Corollary 1 (open meetings).** This is proved here. Suppose $M$ is open in $G$, and write $M = G_{K'}$. Then $S$ contains every prime $\ell \ne p$, and the maximal tamely ramified subextension $K' \cap K^{\mathrm{tr}}$ of $K'/K$ is abelian over $K$ with ramification index dividing $q - 1$. If $(G : M)$ is prime to $p$, then $K'/K$ itself is tamely ramified and abelian with ramification index dividing $q - 1$.

The last sentence is Corollary 2 of [central scalars](central-scalars-in-finite-outer-conjugacy-on-wild-frattini.md), which assumes $p$ odd and $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 3$ and uses finite outer conjugacy with lifted automorphisms. Here it holds for every $p$ and every degree, and the tame part holds without the index condition.

**Lemma (prime-to-$p$ detection).** This is proved here. Let $H$ be a closed subgroup of $A$ of order prime to $p$, that is, every open normal subgroup of $H$ has index prime to $p$. If every element of $H$ is tame-trivial and acts trivially on $V$, then $H = 1$.

Part 2 of [finite-order detection](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-on-the-frattini-quotient-of-wild-inertia.md) proves the same conclusion for a single automorphism of finite order, for odd $p$, without tame-triviality. The Lemma allows elements of infinite order, such as a generator of a copy of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$.

**Corollary 2 (the tame-trivial case).** This is proved here. Let $n_0 \in I_N$ map to a topological generator of $I_N/P_N$, let $\pi' \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ have component $0$ at $p$ and $1$ at every $\ell \ne p$, and let $C$ be the closed subgroup generated by $n_0^{\pi'}$. Then $C$ maps isomorphically onto $I_N/P_N \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. If $S = \varnothing$, then $C \cap G = 1$, so $C$ embeds in $\mathrm{Out}(G)$; $C$ acts trivially on $V/V_Q$, where $V_Q = Q\Phi(P)/\Phi(P)$, and faithfully on $V_Q$. In particular $V_Q \ne 0$, that is, $Q \not\subseteq \Phi(P)$.

**Proof of part 1.** $P$ and $I$ are normal in $A$, because conjugation by $\alpha \in A$ restricts to $\alpha$ on $G$, and $\alpha$ preserves $P$ and $I$. Conjugation by $\alpha$ restricts to a continuous automorphism $c_\alpha$ of the closed normal subgroup $N$, and $\theta c_\alpha \theta^{-1}$ is an automorphism of $G$, which preserves $P$ and $I$. So $c_\alpha$ preserves $P_N$ and $I_N$.

The subgroup $P_N \cap G$ is normal in $A$, hence in $G$, and pro-$p$, being closed in $P_N \cong P$. As $P$ is the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), $P_N \cap G \subseteq P$, so $P_N \cap G = Q$. In the same way $P \cap N$ is a closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $N$, and $P_N$ is the largest one, transporting by $\theta$; so $P \cap N = Q$. Then $M \cap P = N \cap P = Q$ and $M \cap P_N = G \cap P_N = Q$.

Let $J_0 = I_N \cap G$, normal in $A$. Then $J_0 \cap P_N = G \cap P_N = Q$, and $J_0 \cap P = I_N \cap P \subseteq N \cap P = Q$, while $Q \subseteq J_0 \cap P$ as $Q \subseteq P_N \subseteq I_N$. So $J_0/Q$ maps injectively into the procyclic group $I_N/P_N$, and is procyclic. Its image $J_0P/P \cong J_0/(J_0 \cap P) = J_0/Q$ in $\Gamma$ is closed, normal and procyclic. As $I_\Gamma$ is the largest closed normal procyclic subgroup of $\Gamma$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), $J_0P/P \subseteq I_\Gamma$, so $J_0 \subseteq I$ and $I_N \cap G = I_N \cap I$. Exchanging the roles of $G$ and $N$: $(I \cap N) \cap P = Q$ and $(I \cap N) \cap P_N = I \cap P_N \subseteq G \cap P_N = Q$, so $(I \cap N)/Q$ embeds in $I/P$ and is procyclic; its image in $N/P_N$ is a closed normal procyclic subgroup, so it lies in $I_N/P_N$, transporting by $\theta$, and $I \cap N = I \cap I_N$. Finally $M \cap I = N \cap I = J$ and $M \cap I_N = G \cap I_N = J$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** For $n \in N$ and $g \in G$, the element $ngn^{-1}g^{-1}$ of $A$ is $n(g)g^{-1}$. It lies in $M$ ([rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 1), and in $I$, because $n$ induces the identity on $G/I$ (Setting). So $[N, G] \subseteq M \cap I = J$. Since $P_N$, $P$, $G$ and $N$ are normal in $A$, $[P_N, G] \subseteq P_N \cap G = Q$ and $[N, P] \subseteq N \cap P = Q$. For $x \in P_N$, $x(g)g^{-1} \in Q \subseteq P$, so $x$ is tame-trivial. For $z \in P$ and $n \in N$, $znz^{-1}n^{-1} \in Q \subseteq P_N$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** If $Q = 1$, then $[P_N, G] = 1$, so for $x \in P_N$ and $g \in G$, $\mathrm{inn}(x(g)) = x\,\mathrm{inn}(g)\,x^{-1} = \mathrm{inn}(g)$, and $x(g) = g$ as $G$ is centre-free. Then $P_N = 1$, which is false, as $P_N \cong P$ is free pro-$p$ and not finitely generated ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). So $Q$ is a nontrivial closed normal subgroup of $G$. It lies in $P$, which has infinite index since $\Gamma$ is infinite, and in $P_N$, which has infinite index in $N$. By elasticity, a topologically finitely generated nontrivial closed normal subgroup of $G$ is open, so $Q$ is not topologically finitely generated.

In the proof of part 1, $J/Q$ embeds as a closed subgroup of $I_N/P_N \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, so it is procyclic of order prime to $p$, and $J/Q \to JP/P$ and $J/Q \to JP_N/P_N$ are isomorphisms, since $J \cap P = J \cap P_N = Q$ and continuous bijections of compact groups are homeomorphisms. As $M \cap I = M \cap I_N = J$, $M/J$ embeds in $G/I$ and in $N/I_N$. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** Let $n \in N$ and $y = y_N(n)$. For $g \in G$, $n(g)g^{-1} \in J$ by part 2, so the automorphism of $\Gamma$ induced by $n$ is the identity modulo $\bar J$.

*Two descriptions of one conjugation.* Let $j \in J$ and $c = njn^{-1} \in J$. Inside $N$, conjugation by $n$ acts on $I_N/P_N$ by $t \mapsto t^{q^y}$, so $cP_N = (jP_N)^{q^y}$. Inside $G$, $c = n(j)$, so $cP = (jP)^{u_n}$. The isomorphisms $J/Q \to JP_N/P_N$ and $J/Q \to \bar J$ of part 3 commute with raising to powers in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, by continuity, so both say something about $cQ \in J/Q$: $cQ = (jQ)^{q^y} = (jQ)^{u_n}$. Transported to $\bar J \subseteq I_\Gamma \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, this reads $(q^y - u_n)x = 0$ for every $x \in \bar J$. For $\ell \in S$ choose $x \in \bar J$ with $x_\ell \ne 0$; since $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is a domain, the $\ell$-component of $u_n$ is $q^y$.

*The other primes.* Let $\tilde\tau \in I$ map to $\tau$. By part 2, $n(\tilde\tau)\tilde\tau^{-1} \in J$, and its image in $I_\Gamma$ is $\tau^{u_n - 1}$. So $u_n - 1 \in \bar J$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. For $\ell \notin S$, $\bar J_\ell = 0$, so the $\ell$-component of $u_n$ is $1$. Hence $u_n = q_S^y$. For $\ell \in S$, choose $n$ with $y_N(n) = 1$, which exists as $N/I_N \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$; then $q - 1$ is the $\ell$-component of $u_n - 1 \in \bar J$, so $(q - 1)\mathbb{Z}_\ell \subseteq \bar J_\ell$.

The symmetric statement follows by the same argument with $G$ and $N$ exchanged: for $g \in G$, conjugation by $g$ on $I_N/P_N$ is $t \mapsto t^{v}$ for some $v \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$, and on $J/Q \subseteq I/P$ it is $t \mapsto t^{q^{y_G(g)}}$; also $[G, N] \subseteq J$ gives $v - 1 \in JP_N/P_N$. The set $S$ is the same, since it is the set of primes at which the procyclic group $J/Q$ is nontrivial. $\square$

**Proof of part 5.** First, $Z(\Gamma) = 1$: a central element commutes with $\tau$, so it lies in $I_\Gamma$ by the Lemma in [the Frobenius-shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md), say $\tau^a$, and $\tau^a = \sigma\tau^a\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{qa}$ gives $(q - 1)a = 0$ in the torsion-free group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, so $a = 0$. Put $\bar M = MP/P$. Since $P \subseteq I$, $MP \cap I = (M \cap I)P$, so $\bar M \cap I_\Gamma = \bar J$.

(a) $\Rightarrow$ (b). If $S = \varnothing$ then $\bar J = 1$, so $\bar M$ embeds in $\Gamma/I_\Gamma \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and is procyclic. Being closed and normal in $\Gamma$, it lies in $I_\Gamma$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), so $\bar M = \bar M \cap I_\Gamma = 1$ and $M \subseteq P$.

(b) $\Rightarrow$ (c). Then $[N, G] \subseteq J \subseteq M \subseteq P$, so $n(g) \in gP$ for all $n \in N$, $g \in G$.

(c) $\Rightarrow$ (a). For $m \in M$, the inner automorphism $m$ is tame-trivial, so the image of $m$ in $\Gamma$ is central, hence trivial. So $M \subseteq P$, $J \subseteq P$, $\bar J = 1$ and $S = \varnothing$.

In this case $M = M \cap P = Q$. Let $H = NG$, a closed normal subgroup of $A$, and $\bar H = H/P$, which contains the normal subgroups $\Gamma = G/P$ and $\bar N = NP/P$. Elements of $\bar N$ centralize $\Gamma$, as elements of $N$ are tame-trivial. $\bar N \cap \Gamma = (NP \cap G)/P = MP/P = 1$, and $\bar H = \Gamma\bar N$. So $\bar H$ is the internal direct product of $\Gamma$ and $\bar N$. If $\gamma x$, with $\gamma \in \Gamma$ and $x \in \bar N$, centralizes $\Gamma$, then $\gamma \in Z(\Gamma) = 1$; so $\bar N$ is the centralizer of $\Gamma$. Also $\bar N \cong N/(N \cap P) = N/Q$, and $NG/G \cong N/M = N/Q$ ([rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), proof of part 2).

If $S \ne \varnothing$, part 4 gives $\bar J_\ell \supseteq (q-1)\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ for $\ell \in S$, and $\bar M \supseteq \bar J$. Finally, $\Gamma/\overline{\langle\tau^{q-1}\rangle}$ is abelian, as $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q \equiv \tau$ there, and $\tau^{q-1} = \sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}\tau^{-1}$; so $[\Gamma, \Gamma] = \overline{\langle\tau^{q-1}\rangle}$, which corresponds to $(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_\ell (q-1)\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. $\square$

**Proof of part 6.** $NG/G \cong N/M$ ([rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), proof of part 2), a quotient of the prosolvable group $N \cong G_K$ (same proof), so it is prosolvable; it is closed and normal in $\mathrm{Out}(G) = A/G$ since $NG$ is closed and normal in $A$. The product $P_NG$ is closed, as the image of the compact set $P_N \times G$, and normal in $A$. The natural map $P_N/(P_N \cap G) \to P_NG/G$ is a continuous bijection of compact groups, and $P_N \cap G = Q$ by part 1, so $P_NG/G \cong P_N/Q$, which is pro-$p$. Its elements are classes of elements of $P_N$, which are tame-trivial by part 2. It is trivial exactly when $P_N \subseteq G$, that is, when $P_N = P_N \cap G \subseteq P$. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 1.** As $M$ is open in $G$, $J = M \cap I$ is open in $I$, so $\bar J$ is open in $I_\Gamma$ and every $\bar J_\ell$ is nonzero. By part 4, $\bar J \supseteq \prod_\ell (q - 1)\mathbb{Z}_\ell = (q-1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, which corresponds to $[\Gamma, \Gamma]$ by the end of the proof of part 5. The field $K' \cap K^{\mathrm{tr}}$ is the fixed field of $MP$, and its Galois group over $K$ is $G/MP \cong \Gamma/\bar M$, which is abelian because $\bar M \supseteq \bar J \supseteq [\Gamma, \Gamma]$. Its ramification index is $(I : I \cap MP) = (I_\Gamma : \bar M \cap I_\Gamma) = (I_\Gamma : \bar J)$, which divides $(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' : (q - 1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}') = q - 1$, as $q - 1$ is prime to $p$. If $(G : M)$ is prime to $p$, then the image of $P$ in the finite group $G/M$ is a $p$-group whose order divides $(G : M)$, so $P \subseteq M$, and $K' \subseteq K^{\mathrm{tr}}$. $\square$

**Proof of the Lemma.** For $k \ge 1$ let $U_k$ be the intersection of the open subgroups of $G$ of index at most $k$. As $G$ is topologically finitely generated, there are finitely many of them, so $U_k$ is open and characteristic, and every open normal subgroup contains some $U_k$, so $\bigcap_k U_k = 1$. Fix $U = U_k$ and put $\bar G = G/U$ and $\bar P = PU/U$, a normal $p$-subgroup of $\bar G$. The image $H_U$ of $H$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(\bar G)$ is a finite continuous quotient of $H$, since the topology of $A$ is defined by the characteristic open subgroups of $G$ ([rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), Setting), so $|H_U|$ is prime to $p$.

*$H_U$ acts trivially on $\bar P$.* The Frattini subgroup $\Phi(\bar P)$ is the image of $\Phi(P)$, so $\bar P/\Phi(\bar P)$ is a quotient of $V$, and $H_U$ acts trivially on it. If the image of $H_U$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(\bar P)$ were nontrivial, it would contain an element $\beta$ of prime order $\ell \ne p$, acting trivially on $\bar P/\Phi(\bar P)$. The counting argument in the proof of Lemma A of [pro-$p$ surface groups](pro-p-surface-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md) shows that no such $\beta \ne 1$ exists: $\beta$ permutes the tuples $(z_1, \dots, z_r)$ with $z_i \in y_i\Phi(\bar P)$, where the $y_i$ map to a basis of $\bar P/\Phi(\bar P)$; there are $|\Phi(\bar P)|^r$ of them, a number prime to $\ell$, so $\beta$ fixes one, which generates $\bar P$ by the Frattini argument.

*$H_U$ is trivial.* $H_U$ also acts trivially on $\bar G/\bar P$, a quotient of $\Gamma$. Let $h$ be an automorphism of $\bar G$ that is trivial on $\bar P$ and on $\bar G/\bar P$, and put $\delta_h(g) = h(g)g^{-1} \in \bar P$. For $z \in \bar P$, $h(gzg^{-1}) = gzg^{-1}$ and also $h(gzg^{-1}) = h(g)zh(g)^{-1}$, so $\delta_h(g)$ centralizes $\bar P$ and lies in $Z(\bar P)$. If $h'$ is another such automorphism, then $hh'(g) = h(\delta_{h'}(g)g) = \delta_{h'}(g)\delta_h(g)g$, because $h$ fixes $\delta_{h'}(g) \in \bar P$. So $h \mapsto \delta_h$ is an injective homomorphism from the group of such automorphisms to the group of maps $\bar G \to Z(\bar P)$ under pointwise multiplication, which is abelian of exponent dividing that of the $p$-group $Z(\bar P)$. So that group of automorphisms is a $p$-group, and its subgroup $H_U$, of order prime to $p$, is trivial.

So every $h \in H$ induces the identity on every $G/U_k$, hence $h = 1$. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 2.** $C$ is the image of the closed subgroup $\pi'\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ under $k \mapsto n_0^k$, so it is procyclic of order prime to $p$, and $C \cap P_N = 1$. The group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ acts on the pro-(prime-to-$p$) group $I_N/P_N$ through $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, where $\pi'$ maps to $1$, so $n_0^{\pi'}$ maps to the same generator as $n_0$, and $C \to I_N/P_N$ is an isomorphism.

Let $S = \varnothing$. Then $C \cap G \subseteq M = Q \subseteq P_N$ by part 5, so $C \cap G = 1$. The action of $A$ on $G$ is continuous, so the kernel $C_0$ of the action of $C$ on $V$ is closed. Its elements are tame-trivial by part 5 and act trivially on $V$, so $C_0 = 1$ by the Lemma. By part 2, $c(z)z^{-1} \in Q$ for $c \in C$ and $z \in P$, so $C$ acts trivially on $V/V_Q$. If $V_Q = 0$, $C$ would act trivially on $V$, contradicting $C_0 = 1$, as $C \ne 1$. Let $c \in C$ act trivially on $V_Q$. Then $c$ acts on $V$ as $1 + \delta$ with $\delta(V) \subseteq V_Q$ and $\delta(V_Q) = 0$, so $c^p$ acts as $(1 + \delta)^p = 1$, since $V$ is an $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space and $\delta^2 = 0$. As $p$ is invertible in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, $c$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by $c^p$, which acts trivially on $V$. So $c \in C_0 = 1$, and $C$ acts faithfully on $V_Q$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The mechanism.* The common inertia $J$ is normal in $A$, and $J/Q$ embeds both in tame inertia of $G$ and in tame inertia of $N$. Conjugation by $n \in N$ acts on $J/Q$ by the cyclotomic power $q^{y_N(n)}$ seen from $N$, and by the tame twist $u_n$ seen from $G$. Since $[N, G]$ lies in $J$, $u_n$ is also $1$ modulo $\bar J$. Comparing the two at each prime gives part 4. No finite outer conjugacy is used.
- *What remains.* Neither case of part 5 is excluded. If $S = \varnothing$, then $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ contains the closed normal subgroup $NG/G \cong N/Q$, all of whose elements are classes of tame-trivial automorphisms. It maps onto $N/P_N \cong \Gamma$, and its image of tame inertia of $N$ contains a copy of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ acting faithfully on $V_Q$, by Corollary 2. Ruling this out would need information on normal subgroups of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ made of tame-trivial classes, which the existing results obtain only under finite outer conjugacy. If $S \ne \varnothing$, then $M$ contains elements of $I \setminus P$ that generate closed subgroups of order prime to $p$, and each element of $N$ acts on the $\ell$-part of tame inertia like the corresponding power of Frobenius for $\ell \in S$ and trivially for $\ell \notin S$.
- *Case (a).* For odd $p$ and $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 3$, rival copies with open intersection are already excluded by [the odd-prime theorem](odd-prime-tame-twists-and-finite-outer-conjugacy.md). Corollary 1 is a constraint for $p = 2$ and for degree at most $2$, where that theorem does not apply.
- *Scope.* Everything here holds for every prime $p$ and every finite $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$. It is proved from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
