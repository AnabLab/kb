**Theorem (proved here).** Let $k/\mathbb Q_2$ have degree $d$, and let $\pi:G_k\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma$ be its tame quotient. Let a finite odd-order group $T$ act on a finite $2$-group $P$ with central Frattini subgroup. Put $H=P\rtimes T$. For every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$ and finite epimorphisms $q:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow D$, $r:H\twoheadrightarrow D$,
$$
\#\{F:G_k\twoheadrightarrow H:rF=q\pi\}
=\#\{F:G_k\twoheadrightarrow H:rF=q\alpha\pi\}.
\tag{1}
$$
All maps are continuous, and counts distinguish actual maps, including conjugate maps. No bound on the exponent of $\Phi(P)$ is imposed.

**Proof.** *1. Remove nontrivial central action layer by layer.* Write $Z=\Phi(P)$ additively, and set
$$
e=|T|^{-1}\sum_{t\in T}t\in\operatorname{End}(Z),\qquad
W=(1-e)Z.
$$
As multiplication by odd $|T|$ is invertible on $Z$, averaging gives $Z=Z^T\oplus W$ and $W^T=0$. Filter $W$ by
$$
0=W_0\subseteq W_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq W_m=W,
\qquad W_i=W[2^i].
$$
Each $W_i/W_{i-1}$ is elementary abelian and is central in $P/W_{i-1}$. It lies in $\Phi(P/W_{i-1})$, since Frattini subgroups commute with quotients: both sides are generated by squares and commutators. Each layer has zero $T$-fixed subgroup. Indeed averaging makes taking invariants exact on finite abelian $2$-groups with $T$-action: average any lift of an invariant element to get an invariant lift. The subgroups $W_i$ have no invariants because they lie in $W$.

Apply the [elementary central-action reduction](nontrivial-central-action-in-dyadic-lift-counts.md) to each layer. Every tame marking kills $W$, and each epimorphism to $H/W$ has exactly
$$
\prod_{i=1}^m|W_i/W_{i-1}|^{d+1}=|W|^{d+1}
\tag{2}
$$
surjective lifts to $H$. This multiplier is independent of the marking. Thus it suffices to prove (1) after replacing $P$ by $P/W$, whose central Frattini subgroup $Z/W$ is fixed by $T$.

*2. Separate the fixed factor without bounding its exponent.* Apply the [moving-factor decomposition](elementary-moving-factor-for-central-frattini-2-groups.md). With its notation,
$$
P=CR,\quad [C,R]=1,\quad A=C\cap R=\Phi(R),\quad
U=R/A,\quad Q=C/A,
$$
where $T$ fixes $C$ pointwise, $A$ is elementary abelian, and $U^T=0$. Write $a=\dim A$ and $u=\dim U$. The central product gives a central extension
$$
1\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow H
\longrightarrow Q\times(U\rtimes T)\longrightarrow1.
\tag{3}
$$
Fix epimorphisms $\beta:G_k\twoheadrightarrow Q$ and $\epsilon:G_k\twoheadrightarrow T$. Let $c_\beta\in H^2(k,A)=A$ be the obstruction to lifting $\beta$ through $C\to Q$, using the local invariant and $\mathbb F_2=\mu_2$.

Let $(J_d,b_d)$ be the paired augmentation module in the [odd-order cup-module theorem](odd-order-equivariant-cup-module-over-dyadic-fields.md). Commutators of $R$ define $B:U\times U\to A$. For a basis $v_i$ and dual basis $\xi_i$, put
$$
\Omega(f)=\sum_{i<j}B(v_i,v_j)b_d(f(\xi_i),f(\xi_j)),\qquad
N_d(c)=\#\{f:U^*\hookrightarrow J_d\text{ $T$-linear}:\Omega(f)=c\}.
\tag{4}
$$
The contraction is independent of the basis: pulling back $b_d$ gives an alternating form on $U^*$, hence an element of $\bigwedge^2U$, to which $B$ is applied. The number of surjections to $H$ above the fixed pair $(\beta,\epsilon)$ is
$$
M(\beta,\epsilon)=2^{u+a(d+2)}N_d(c_\beta).
\tag{5}
$$
In particular it is independent of $\epsilon$.

Here is the obstruction calculation, including why the larger fixed factor causes no change. Let $L$ be cut out by $\epsilon$. A map to $U\rtimes T$ above $\epsilon$ is a cocycle. Inflation-restriction identifies its class with a $T$-linear map $U^*\to H^1(L,\mathbb F_2)$; its image lies in the nontrivial summand, isometric to $J_d$. Positive-degree cohomology of the odd-order group $T$ with $2$-primary coefficients vanishes [@milnecft, II, Corollary 1.31, p. 70; Remark 1.35(a), p. 72; continuous-cohomology discussion, p. 88]. The restriction to $G_L$ surjects onto $U$ exactly when this map is injective. Each class has $2^u$ cocycle representatives, since $U^T=0$.

Choose lifts $t_i\in R$ of $v_i$. The ordered section of $R\to U$ has factor set
$$
F_R(x,y)=\sum_i t_i^2x_iy_i+
\sum_{i<j}B(v_i,v_j)x_jy_i\quad\text{in }A.
$$
On restriction to $G_L$, square terms disappear because the cup form on the nontrivial summand is alternating. The remaining term is $\Omega(f)$. Restriction on $H^2(k,A)$ is injective, since corestriction after restriction is multiplication by odd $|T|$ [@milnecft, II, Proposition 1.30, p. 70; continuous-cohomology discussion, p. 88]. Under the local identifications with $A$ it is the identity, coordinate by coordinate. The two factors $C,R$ commute, so the factor set of (3) is the sum of their factor sets, with no mixed term. The lift obstruction is therefore $c_\beta+\Omega(f)$.

Changing a cocycle representative amounts to conjugation by $U$, which lifts to conjugation by $R$ and fixes $Q$. Each unobstructed actual quotient map has $|H^1(k,A)|=2^{a(d+2)}$ lifts [@milne2006adt, I, Corollary 2.3, p. 28; Theorem 2.8, p. 31]. These facts give (5).

To check surjectivity in (5), let a lift have image $J$, and assume $f$ is injective. Its image from $G_L$ in $P/\Phi(P)=V_0\oplus V_1$ is $T$-stable: $G_L$ is normal in $G_k$, and conjugation on this quotient acts through the surjection $\epsilon$ to $T$. The image projects onto $V_1$ by injectivity. It projects onto $V_0$ as well: $\beta(G_L)=Q$, because $Q/\beta(G_L)$ is a quotient of the odd-order group $T$ and a $2$-group. Averaging splits the image into its fixed and moving parts, so these two projections force it to be all of $V_0\oplus V_1$. Hence $(J\cap P)\Phi(P)=P$, and the Frattini property gives $J\cap P=P$. Its $T$ projection is already onto, so $J=H$. Conversely, a surjection onto $H$ must surject onto $U\rtimes T$, forcing injectivity of $f$. This proves (5) with its stated surjectivity condition.

*3. Preserve the full tame marking.* The decomposition theorem gives $r(R)=1$. Thus $r$ factors through $Q\times T$, and its commuting images have coprime orders. Write
$$
D=D_2\times D_o,\qquad r=(r_2,r_o),\qquad q=(q_2,q_o),
$$
where $r_2:Q\twoheadrightarrow D_2$ and $r_o:T\twoheadrightarrow D_o$. A tame automorphism fixes the unramified quotient pointwise, as in the [finite-quotient criterion](finite-quotient-criterion-for-frobenius-shift-lifting.md). Since $q_2$ kills the odd-order tame inertia image, $q_2\alpha=q_2$.

The first count in (1) is the sum of (5) over
$$
\beta:G_k\twoheadrightarrow Q,\quad r_2\beta=q_2\pi,
\qquad
\epsilon_0:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow T,\quad r_o\epsilon_0=q_o,
$$
with $\epsilon=\epsilon_0\pi$. All odd-order quotients kill wild inertia, so every relevant $\epsilon$ occurs. Precomposition $\epsilon_0\mapsto\epsilon_0\alpha$ bijects these markings with those for $q_o\alpha$; the allowed $\beta$ are unchanged. Equation (5) gives equal summands. This proves (1) in the fixed-central case, and (2) proves it in general. $\square$

**A nonabelian example beyond exponent two (proved here).** Let $c$ generate $C_8$, let $z$ be the central involution of $Q_8$, and set
$$
P=(C_8\times Q_8)/\langle(c^4,z)\rangle.
$$
Both factors embed, have common subgroup $\langle c^4\rangle=\langle z\rangle$, and commute. Squares and commutators show that $\Phi(P)=\langle c^2\rangle\cong C_4$, which is central. Let $T=C_3$ act trivially on $C_8$ and cycle the quaternion directions. Then $|P\rtimes T|=96$, and the theorem covers all its tame markings. The decomposition has $C=C_8$, $R=Q_8$, $A=C_2$, and $Q=C_4$. Its fixed-factor obstruction is the one for $C_8\to C_4$; (5) retains every fibre, including cancellation against a nonzero quaternion obstruction.

**Scope.** This extends the [elementary-abelian Frattini theorem](dyadic-central-frattini-targets-give-no-count-obstruction.md) to arbitrary central Frattini subgroups. It keeps the entire fixed-factor map, so it asserts neither degree-only counts for that factor nor such a formula for arbitrary proper markings. Targets with noncentral Frattini subgroup, targets outside the stated semidirect-product class, and the full Frobenius-shift lifting and dyadic classification problems remain open here.
