**Setting.** Let $k$ be a number field with completions $k_v$, and let $X$ be a geometrically connected variety over $k$. Write $\pi_1(X/k)$ for the extension
$$1 \to \pi_1(X_{\bar k}) \to \pi_1(X) \to G_k \to 1,$$
and $\mathrm{Sect}(X/k)$ for its set of sections up to conjugation by $\pi_1(X_{\bar k})$. Since $\pi_1(X_{k_v}) = \pi_1(X) \times_{G_k} G_{k_v}$, a section $s$ restricts to a local section $s \otimes k_v \in \mathrm{Sect}(X_{k_v}/k_v)$ [@stix2011bm, §2.1, p. 3]. A point $P_v \in X(k_v)$ gives a local section $s_{P_v}$.

Stix calls a family $(s_v) \in \prod_v \mathrm{Sect}(X_{k_v}/k_v)$ *adelic* if, for every continuous homomorphism $\varphi$ from $\pi_1(X)$ to a finite group, $\varphi \circ s_v$ is unramified for almost all $v$. The restrictions of a global section form an adelic family, and so do the sections of an adelic point [@stix2011bm, §3.2 and Proposition 10, p. 8]. If $X$ is proper, every family is adelic [@stix2011bm, Proposition 11, p. 8].

**Theorem 1: interpolation (Harari–Stix).** Let $S$ be a set of places of $k$ of Dirichlet density $0$, for instance a finite set. Suppose that $P_v \in X(k_v)$ is given for every $v \notin S$. Then the following are equivalent [@hararistix2012, Theorem 2.1, p. 11]:

1. For every finite $k$-group scheme $G$ and every class $\alpha \in H^1(X, G)$, the family $(P_v^*\alpha)_{v \notin S}$ lies in the diagonal image of $H^1(k, G)$ in $\prod_{v \notin S} H^1(k_v, G)$.
2. The same holds for every finite constant $k$-group scheme $G$.
3. There is a section $s$ of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$ with $s \otimes k_v = s_{P_v}$ in $\mathrm{Sect}(X_{k_v}/k_v)$ for every $v \notin S$.

Condition 1 says, in Stoll's terms, that $(P_v)$ survives every $X$-torsor under a finite group scheme [@hararistix2012, Remark (1), p. 12]. Take $S = \emptyset$ and $X$ proper, so that families of local points are adelic points. Then an adelic point survives every finite descent obstruction exactly when its local sections all come from one global section. In particular, if such an adelic point exists, then $\pi_1(X/k)$ splits. Betts and Stix take from Harari and Stix that the finite descent locus is the image of the sections that come from local points at every place, and use this to show the following. For a curve of genus at least $2$ over a number field with no CM subfield, the projection of the finite descent locus to each $X(k_v)$ with $v$ finite is finite [@bettsstix2025, Theorem B and §1, p. 2]. See [finiteness of the local images of Selmer sections](finiteness-of-local-images-of-selmer-sections.md).

The theorem is stated more generally for the quotient of $\pi_1(X)$ by a closed subgroup $\bar U$ of $\pi_1(X_{\bar k})$ that is normal in $\pi_1(X)$. Conditions 1 and 2 then range over the classes $\alpha$ whose geometric part is trivial on $\bar U$, and condition 3 asks for a section of the quotient extension, with the local equalities up to conjugation by $\pi_1(X_{\bar k})/\bar U$ [@hararistix2012, Theorem 2.1, p. 11]. If $\bar U$ is the closed commutator subgroup, so that the quotient is the geometrically abelianized extension, the classes in condition 1 are those of torsors with abelian geometric monodromy [@hararistix2012, Remark (3), p. 12]. Completions may be replaced by henselizations throughout [@hararistix2012, Remark (4), p. 12].

The proof is group-theoretic. The equivalences hold for any family of fields $k_i$ over $k$ such that the conjugates of the images of the maps $G_{k_i} \to G_k$ have dense union [@hararistix2012, Corollary 1.7, p. 10]. For the completions at the places outside $S$, this density follows from the Chebotarev density theorem [@hararistix2012, proof of Theorem 2.1, p. 11]. Without such a condition the interpolation can fail: if every $k_i$ is $\bar k$, conditions like 1 and 2 hold trivially, but the extension need not split [@hararistix2012, Remark (1), p. 6].

**Theorem 2: a Brauer–Manin condition (Stix).** Let $\alpha \in H^2(\pi_1(X), \mu_n)$. For a local section $s_v$, the pullback $s_v^*\alpha$ lies in $H^2(k_v, \mu_n) = \mathrm{Br}(k_v)[n]$. For an adelic family it vanishes at almost all $v$ [@stix2011bm, Proposition 12, p. 9], so
$$\langle \alpha, (s_v) \rangle = \sum_v \mathrm{inv}_v(s_v^*\alpha) \in \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$$
is defined. If $(s_v)$ is the family of restrictions of a global section, then $\langle \alpha, (s_v) \rangle = 0$ for every $n$ and every $\alpha$. This follows from the exact sequence $0 \to \mathrm{Br}(k) \to \bigoplus_v \mathrm{Br}(k_v) \to \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ [@stix2011bm, Theorem 13, p. 9]. On the sections of an adelic point, the pairing is the usual Brauer–Manin pairing with the image of $\alpha$ in $\mathrm{Br}(X)[n]$ [@stix2011bm, (3.3), p. 9]. For a smooth geometrically connected curve, an adelic point is orthogonal to the Brauer group exactly when its family of sections passes this test [@stix2011bm, Proposition 14, p. 9]. (At the infinite places, adelic points are taken up to connected components here [@stix2011bm, §3.1, p. 7].)

Harari and Stix add an abelian form. Let $X$ be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve. If the geometrically abelianized extension has a section whose restriction to each $k_v$ is the section of a point $P_v \in X(k_v)$, then $(P_v)$ is orthogonal to the Brauer group [@hararistix2012, Corollary 3.1, p. 13].

**Conditional consequences.** Stix considers the following hypotheses [@stix2011bm, §4.1, p. 12]:

- (LSC), the $p$-adic section conjecture: the section conjecture holds over every finite extension of every $\mathbb{Q}_p$. See the local fields paragraph of the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md).
- (BM): for smooth projective geometrically connected curves over number fields, the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to the existence of rational points.

If (LSC) and (BM) hold, then the section conjecture holds for every smooth projective geometrically connected curve of genus at least $2$ over a number field [@stix2011bm, Theorem 25, pp. 14–15]. Stix notes that Wittenberg observed this independently [@stix2011bm, p. 14]. The proof has two steps. By (LSC) and the real section conjecture, adelic points correspond bijectively to adelic families of sections, so by Theorem 2 the adelic points orthogonal to the Brauer group correspond to the families that pass the test [@stix2011bm, Proposition 23, p. 14]. Under (BM), let $s$ be a section whose local restrictions come from an adelic point. The adelic point lifts to every finite étale neighbourhood of $s$, and by Theorem 2 the lift is orthogonal to the Brauer group of the neighbourhood. So each neighbourhood has a rational point, and the usual limit argument gives a rational point that induces $s$ [@stix2011bm, Proposition 24 and its proof, p. 14]. Stix refers to Stoll for an analogue with the finite descent obstruction in place of the Brauer–Manin obstruction [@stix2011bm, p. 15]. Under (LSC) for $X$ and finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich group of its Jacobian, a curve of genus at least $1$ with adelic points and a section has index $1$ [@stix2011bm, Theorem 22, p. 14].

**The Reichardt–Lind curve.** Let $U$ be the affine curve $2Y^2 = Z^4 - 17$, $Y \neq 0$, over $\mathbb{Q}$, and $X$ its smooth projective model, given in $\mathbb{P}^3$ by $2T^2 = A^2 - 17B^2$ and $AB = C^2$. Then $X$ has genus $1$, and its class is a nontrivial element of the Tate–Shafarevich group of its Jacobian, the elliptic curve $y^2 = x(x^2 + 17)$. So $X$ has points everywhere locally but no rational point, as Lind (1940) and Reichardt (1942) found. The complement $X \setminus U$ consists of two closed points, with residue fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[4]{17})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, so $U$ is a hyperbolic curve of type $(1, 6)$ [@stix2011bm, §5.1, pp. 15–16]. It has no rational points and no rational cusps, so the section conjecture for affine curves predicts that $\pi_1(U/\mathbb{Q})$ has no section. Stix proves this.

- *Theorem* [@stix2011bm, Theorem 27, p. 17]. $\pi_1(U/\mathbb{Q})$ does not split. More precisely, the geometrically pro-$2$ extension of $X$ has no section whose restrictions to $\mathbb{Q}_2$ and $\mathbb{Q}_{17}$ lift to the geometrically pro-$2$ fundamental group of $U$.
- *Proof idea* [@stix2011bm, proof of Theorem 27, pp. 17–18]. The cup product of the Kummer classes of $Y$ and $17$ on $U$ extends to a class $\alpha$ on the geometrically pro-$2$ fundamental group of $X$. For a section $s$, the local terms of $\langle \alpha, (s \otimes \mathbb{Q}_v) \rangle$ vanish away from $2$, $17$ and $\infty$. At $\infty$, the real section conjecture places $s \otimes \mathbb{R}$ at a real point, which can be moved into $U(\mathbb{R})$. At these three places the term is the quaternion algebra $(y_v, 17)$, where $y_v$ is the value of $Y$ at the lifted section. It is split at $2$ and $\infty$, where $17$ is a square. At $17$, evaluating units at sections compatibly with norms shows that $2y_{17}^2$ is a norm from $\mathbb{Q}_{17}(\sqrt[4]{17})$, and this forces $\mathrm{inv}_{17}(y_{17}, 17) = 1/2$. That contradicts Theorem 2.
- *Why it is new evidence.* $U$ has adelic points, so its lack of sections is not explained by local obstructions such as those of [period and index](section-conjecture-period-index.md) or the real section conjecture. Stix also notes that Harari and Szamuely's method does not apply, because the Jacobian has rank $0$ [@stix2011bm, pp. 2 and 17].
- *Abelian is enough here.* Even the geometrically abelianized extension of $U$ does not split [@stix2011bm, Corollary 33, p. 22].
- *More examples.* The same argument rules out sections for infinitely many arithmetic twists $\ell T^2 = A^2 - pB^2$, $AB = C^2$ (with the corresponding open curves), for instance $(\ell, p) = (2, 41)$, $(6, 73)$, $(11, 97)$ and $(19, 17)$ [@stix2011bm, §5.5, pp. 18–19], and for every rational fibre of an isotrivial family of twists found by Elkies [@stix2011bm, Proposition 28, p. 19].

Stix asks whether $\pi_1(X/\mathbb{Q})$ splits for the projective curve $X$ [@stix2011bm, Question 34, p. 23]. Since $X$ has genus $1$, the section conjecture makes no prediction for it. Wittenberg later proved that it does not split, by pairing Brauer classes with the cycle class of a section; see [Wittenberg's criterion](wittenberg-nonsplitting-criterion.md).

**Limits of the method.** For the Selmer curve $S : 3X^3 + 4Y^3 + 5Z^3 = 0$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, the Tate–Shafarevich group of the Jacobian is finite, so a locally constant Brauer class explains the absence of rational points, and Stix constructs one [@stix2011bm, p. 24 and Theorem 43, p. 28]. Yet some adelic section of $\pi_1(S/\mathbb{Q})$ passes the test of Theorem 2 for every class in $H^2(\pi_1(S), \mu_3)$ whose image in $\mathrm{Br}(S)$ is locally constant [@stix2011bm, Theorem 44, p. 28]. The reason is that at $3$, local sections vary in a larger space than the sections of $3$-adic points do [@stix2011bm, §7.4 and proof of Theorem 44, pp. 25–29]. Stix leaves open whether $\pi_1(S/\mathbb{Q})$ splits [@stix2011bm, Question 45, p. 29]. Çiperiani and Stix later showed that it does, because the class of $S$ lies in the maximal divisible subgroup of the Weil–Châtelet group of its Jacobian. So the absence of rational points on $S$ is not reflected in the absence of sections; see [genus-one curves](galois-sections-of-genus-one-curves.md).

**Topology of sections.** For a proper geometrically connected variety over a number field, $\mathrm{Sect}(X/k)$ is a profinite set in a natural topology, whose open sets come from finite étale neighbourhoods of sections [@stix2011bm, §2.3.1 and Proposition 7, pp. 5–6]. Stix warns that a countable profinite set need not be finite, so this does not reprove Faltings's theorem from the section conjecture [@stix2011bm, Remark 8, p. 6].

**Birational sections.** Harari and Stix apply Theorem 1 and the abelian form of Theorem 2 to sections of $G_{k(X)} \to G_k$. See [birational sections and the Brauer–Manin obstruction](birational-sections-and-the-brauer-manin-obstruction.md).
