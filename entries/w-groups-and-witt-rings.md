**Setting.** Let $F$ be a field of characteristic not $2$, and let $G_F(2)$ be the Galois group of its quadratic closure, which is the maximal pro-$2$ quotient of $G_F$. The *W-group* of $F$ is
$$\mathcal{G}_F = G_F(2)/G_F(2)^4[G_F(2)^2, G_F(2)],$$
the quotient by the closed subgroup generated by fourth powers and by commutators of squares with arbitrary elements [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, Definition 2.1]. Put $F^{(2)} = F(\sqrt{a} : a \in F^\times)$, and let $F^{(3)}$ be the compositum of the fields $F^{(2)}(\sqrt{y})$ with $y \in F^{(2)}$ and $F^{(2)}(\sqrt{y})/F$ Galois. Then $\mathcal{G}_F = \mathrm{Gal}(F^{(3)}/F)$. Its Frattini subgroup $\Phi(\mathcal{G}_F)$ is $\mathrm{Gal}(F^{(3)}/F^{(2)})$, which is elementary abelian and central, and $E = \mathcal{G}_F/\Phi(\mathcal{G}_F) = \mathrm{Gal}(F^{(2)}/F)$ is the Pontryagin dual of $F^\times/F^{\times 2}$. The group $\mathcal{G}_F$ is finite exactly when $F^\times/F^{\times 2}$ is [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, §2, p. 6]. As $\Phi(\mathcal{G}_F)$ is central with abelian quotient, $\mathcal{G}_F$ has nilpotency class at most $2$, and its exponent divides $4$.

*Comparison with other quotients.* This is checked here. Write $G = G_F(2)$, $G^2$ for the closed subgroup generated by squares, and $N = G^4[G^2, G]$. Since $[x, y] = x^{-2}(xy^{-1})^2y^2$, the commutators lie in $G^2$. Modulo $N$, squares are central of order at most $2$, so the image of $G^2$ is elementary abelian, and the squares of elements of $G^2$ lie in $N$. As $G^4 \subseteq (G^2)^2$, this gives $N = (G^2)^2[G^2, G]$. Since $[G, G]G^2 = G^2$, this is the subgroup $G^{(3)}$ in the mod-$2$ form of the [abelian-by-central Galois group](abelian-by-central-galois-group.md). So $\mathcal{G}_F$ is that group $G^c$ for $\ell = 2$, and also the quotient $G^{c,1}$ for $\ell = 2$ in [abelian-by-central quotients determine Galois cohomology](abelian-by-central-galois-cohomology.md). Chebolu, Efrat and Mináč likewise identify their quotient $G_F^{[3]}$ for $q = 2$ with the W-group [@cheboluefratminac2012, p. 2].

**Statement.** Let $k_*F = K^M_*(F)/2$ be the mod-$2$ [Milnor K-ring](milnor-k-ring.md), a commutative graded $\mathbb{F}_2$-algebra generated by $k_1F = F^\times/F^{\times 2}$. The ring $H^*(E, \mathbb{F}_2)$ is the polynomial ring on $H^1(E, \mathbb{F}_2) = k_1F$. Write $l(a)$ for the class of $a$ in $k_1F$, and let $I_F \subseteq H^2(E, \mathbb{F}_2)$ be the subspace spanned by the products $l(a)l(1 - a)$ for $a \ne 0, 1$. Then $k_*F$ is the polynomial ring on $k_1F$ modulo the ideal generated by $I_F$ [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, p. 10]. Let $L$ be another field of characteristic not $2$.

1. *Structure.* The transgression $\delta : H^1(\Phi(\mathcal{G}_F), \mathbb{F}_2) \to H^2(E, \mathbb{F}_2)$ of the central extension $1 \to \Phi(\mathcal{G}_F) \to \mathcal{G}_F \to E \to 1$ is injective with image $I_F$, and $\mathcal{G}_F$ is determined by this subspace [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, Theorem 3.7, Lemma 3.3 and Remark 3.4]. The authors describe $\mathcal{G}_F$ as the "minimal" extension of $E$ in which the relations of Milnor K-theory hold [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, p. 3].
2. *Milnor K-theory.* $\mathcal{G}_F \cong \mathcal{G}_L$ exactly when $k_*F \cong k_*L$, since both are determined by the same subspace $I_F$ [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, Corollary 3.9 and p. 11].
3. *Witt rings.* If the Witt rings of quadratic forms satisfy $W(F) \cong W(L)$, then $\mathcal{G}_F \cong \mathcal{G}_L$, and "the converse holds provided $F$ and $L$ have the same level if $\langle 1, 1\rangle_F$ is universal" [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, Corollary 3.9]. The level $s(F)$ is the least $m$ such that $-1$ is a sum of $m$ squares in $F$, or $\infty$ if there is none [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, Remark 2.3]. The form $\langle 1, 1\rangle_F$ is universal when every element of $F^\times$ is a sum of two squares. So if some element of $F^\times$ is not a sum of two squares, the W-group determines the Witt ring. Adem, Karagueuzian and Mináč deduce part 3 from part 2 and the main theorem of Mináč and Spira (*Witt rings and Galois groups*, Ann. of Math. 144 (1996), 35–60), which has not been opened for this entry.
4. *Cohomology.* Inflation along $G_F \to \mathcal{G}_F$ maps the subring of $H^*(\mathcal{G}_F, \mathbb{F}_2)$ generated by $H^1$ isomorphically onto $H^*(G_F, \mathbb{F}_2)$ [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, Theorem 3.14]. The whole ring $H^*(\mathcal{G}_F, \mathbb{F}_2)$ can be substantially larger [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, p. 3]. Chebolu, Efrat and Mináč extend part 4 to $\mathbb{Z}/q$ coefficients as their Theorem A [@cheboluefratminac2012, p. 2].
5. *Orderings.* For an involution $\sigma \in \mathcal{G}_F$ not in $\Phi(\mathcal{G}_F)$, put $P_\sigma = \{f \in F : \sigma(\sqrt{f}) = \sqrt{f}\}$. Mináč and Spira showed that $P_\sigma$ is an ordering of $F$, and that $\sigma \mapsto P_\sigma$ gives a one-to-one correspondence between classes of such involutions and orderings of $F$ [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, proof of Theorem 2.2]. If $|\mathcal{G}_F| > 2$, then $F$ is not formally real exactly when every element of order $2$ in $\mathcal{G}_F$ is central [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, Theorem 2.2]. If $F$ is formally real, $\Phi(\mathcal{G}_F)$ has index $2$ in every maximal elementary abelian subgroup of $\mathcal{G}_F$ [@ademkaragueuzianminac1999, Theorem 2.4].

**The orderings, counted.** This is checked here, given that each $P_\sigma$ is an ordering. Since $\Phi(\mathcal{G}_F)$ fixes $F^{(2)}$, the set $P_\sigma$ depends only on the image $\bar\sigma$ of $\sigma$ in $E$. It also determines $\bar\sigma$, because $\sigma(\sqrt{f}) = -\sqrt{f}$ for $f \in F^\times \setminus P_\sigma$. Every ordering $P$ arises. Indeed, let $R$ be a real closure of $(F, P)$ in an algebraic closure $\bar F$. Then $\bar F = R(\sqrt{-1})$, and the nonzero squares of $R$ meet $F$ in $P \setminus \{0\}$. These are standard facts of Artin–Schreier theory, used here without being checked in a source. Let $\sigma$ be the image in $\mathcal{G}_F$ of the generator of $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar F/R)$. Then $\sigma^2 = 1$, and $\sigma$ fixes $\sqrt{f}$ exactly when $\sqrt{f} \in R$, so $P_\sigma = P$. As $-1 \notin P$, $\sigma$ moves $\sqrt{-1} \in F^{(2)}$, so $\sigma$ is an involution outside $\Phi(\mathcal{G}_F)$. Hence the orderings of $F$ correspond bijectively to the elements of $E$ that are images of involutions outside $\Phi(\mathcal{G}_F)$. As the Frattini subgroup is characteristic, the abstract group $\mathcal{G}_F$ determines the number of orderings of $F$. For a number field, whose orderings correspond to its real places by a standard fact not checked in a source here, the W-group therefore determines the number of real places, as $G_K$ does by [involutions and real places](involutions-and-real-places.md).

**Example: the level condition is needed.** This is checked here. For an odd prime power $q$, $G_{\mathbb{F}_q}(2) \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, so $\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{F}_q} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2/4\mathbb{Z}_2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/4$. Thus $\mathbb{F}_3$ and $\mathbb{F}_5$ have isomorphic W-groups. Their Witt rings are not isomorphic. In $W(F)$, $1 + 1$ is the class of $\langle 1, 1\rangle$. Over $\mathbb{F}_5$, $1^2 + 2^2 = 0$, so $\langle 1, 1\rangle$ is isotropic, hence hyperbolic, and $1 + 1 = 0$ in $W(\mathbb{F}_5)$. Over $\mathbb{F}_3$, $-1$ is not a square, so $\langle 1, 1\rangle$ is anisotropic, and $1 + 1 \ne 0$ in $W(\mathbb{F}_3)$. These use the standard facts that an isotropic nondegenerate binary form is hyperbolic and that a nonzero anisotropic form has nonzero class in the Witt ring. The example fits part 3: over a finite field of odd order $q$, every element is a sum of two squares, because $x^2$ and $a - y^2$ each take $(q + 1)/2$ values, so $\langle 1, 1\rangle$ is universal, while $s(\mathbb{F}_5) = 1$ and $s(\mathbb{F}_3) = 2$.

**Role in the graph.** The W-group is a small quotient of $G_F$. Yet it determines the mod-$2$ Galois cohomology ring, by parts 2 and 4 and the [norm residue isomorphism theorem](norm-residue-isomorphism-theorem.md), as well as the number of orderings and, apart from the level condition, the Witt ring. It does not determine the field, as $\mathbb{F}_3$ and $\mathbb{F}_5$ show. For $\ell = 2$, the mod-$\ell$ group $G^c_K$ in [mod-$\ell$ reconstruction from rational quotients](mod-l-reconstruction-from-rational-quotients.md) is the W-group, by the comparison above. Topaz defines $G^c_K$ with a separate case for $\ell = 2$, and his Theorem A requires only $\mathrm{char}\, K \ne \ell$ [@topaz2016, §1.2 and Theorem A]. So for a function field $K$ of transcendence degree at least $5$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic not $2$, the W-group together with the rational quotients determines $K$ up to purely inseparable extensions.
