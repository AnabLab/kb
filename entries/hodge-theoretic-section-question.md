**Setting.** For a smooth connected complex variety $X$, let $\mathrm{VMHS}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)$ be the category of admissible graded-polarizable $\mathbb{Z}$-variations of mixed Hodge structure on $X$. It is an abelian tensor category with unit the constant variation $\mathbb{Z}(0)$, and functors between such categories are taken to be exact additive $\otimes$-functors [@xu2025, §5.1, p. 17]. Let $f : X \to B$ be a smooth projective morphism with connected fibres between smooth connected varieties. An algebraic section $s$ gives $s^* : \mathrm{VMHS}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X) \to \mathrm{VMHS}_{\mathbb{Z}}(B)$ with $s^* \circ f^* = \mathrm{id}$, and a functor $F$ is called a section of $f^*$ if $F \circ f^*$ is isomorphic to the identity [@xu2025, Definition 5.1.2].

**Question (Xu).** [@xu2025, Question 5.1.3]

1. *Injectivity.* If $s_1 \neq s_2$ are algebraic sections, can $s_1^*$ and $s_2^*$ be isomorphic?
2. *Surjectivity.* Is every section of $f^*$ isomorphic to $s^*$ for some algebraic section $s$?

$\mathrm{VMHS}_{\mathbb{Z}}$ is not Tannakian, so here the question is asked of functors directly [@xu2025, p. 5]. It is an analogue of Grothendieck's [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md), with sections of $f^*$ in place of sections of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$.

**Tannakian form.** In a second paper Xu modifies the formulation [@xu2025exact, §6, p. 19]. There $\mathrm{VMHS}(X)$ consists of admissible graded-polarizable $\mathbb{Q}$-variations whose underlying local systems are defined over $\mathbb{Z}$. With a base point it is a neutral Tannakian category, with Tannakian group $\pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(X))$ [@xu2025exact, Definition 1.1]. The local systems underlying such variations and their subquotients form a Tannakian category $\mathrm{LS}^{\mathrm{Hdg}}(X)$, and André's split exact sequence
$$1 \to \pi_1(\mathrm{LS}^{\mathrm{Hdg}}(X)) \to \pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(X)) \to \pi_1(\mathrm{MHS}) \to 1$$
plays the role of the fundamental exact sequence of étale fundamental groups [@xu2025exact, p. 2 and p. 19]. For $f$ as above and $b \in B$, the sequence $\pi_1(\mathrm{LS}^{\mathrm{Hdg}}(X_b)) \to \pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(X)) \to \pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(B)) \to 1$ is exact [@xu2025exact, Theorem 1.4], though in general not left exact [@xu2025exact, Theorem 1.5 (1)]. A map of Tannakian groups is *Hodge theoretic* if it induces a map of André's sequences [@xu2025exact, Definition 6.1]. The question asks when the map $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}$, from algebraic sections to Hodge theoretic splittings of $\pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(X)) \to \pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(B))$ up to conjugation, is a bijection [@xu2025exact, Question 6.2].

**Known.**

- *Injectivity for families of curves.* Let $f$ be a family of smooth projective curves of genus $g \ge 2$ over a smooth connected $B$, not necessarily proper. If $s_1^* \cong s_2^*$ on $\mathrm{VMHS}_{\mathbb{Z}}(X)$, then $s_1 = s_2$ [@xu2025, Proposition 5.2.1 and Theorem 1.0.13]. This applies to Kodaira fibrations [@xu2025, Corollary 5.2.7]. Unlike the [topological analogue](topological-sections-of-kodaira-fibrations.md), it needs no hypothesis on the monodromy. A proof is below.
- *Étale obstruction.* Let $f$ be a smooth projective map between smooth connected complex quasi-projective varieties. If $\pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(X)) \to \pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(B))$ has a Hodge theoretic splitting, then $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X) \to \pi_1^{\text{ét}}(B)$ splits [@xu2025exact, Theorem 6.3]. The splitting gives a tensor functor on $\mathrm{LS}^{\mathrm{Hdg}}$, and by Nori's criterion, that a local system $\mathbb{V}$ has finite monodromy exactly when $P(\mathbb{V}) \cong Q(\mathbb{V})$ for some distinct $P, Q \in \mathbb{N}[x]$, tensor functors preserve local systems of finite monodromy [@xu2025exact, Lemma 6.4]. These form a Tannakian category with group $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}$. By an observation of André, the condition that the splitting be Hodge theoretic is not needed [@xu2025exact, Remark 6.7].
- *Universal curves.* Let $g \ge 3$ and let $U$ be an open subset of $\mathcal{M}_g$, with preimages in the universal curve and in the moduli space of degree $1$ line bundles on it. For both families, there is no Hodge theoretic splitting, and $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}$ is a bijection [@xu2025exact, Corollary 6.6]. The proof combines the étale obstruction with the non-splitting of the étale sequences over the generic point of $\mathcal{M}_g$, citing Hain and Li–Litt–Salter–Srinivasan [@xu2025exact, proof of Corollary 6.6]. Xu calls this an affirmative answer "in a trivial way" [@xu2025exact, p. 4]. For the first formulation, he reports Hain's private indication that the proof of Hain's theorem on [universal curves](section-conjecture-for-universal-curves.md) adapts to give bijectivity for $B = \mathcal{M}_g$ with $g > 4$ [@xu2025, p. 6]. No written proof of that was opened.

*Proof of injectivity.* This follows Xu, with his auxiliary section taken to be $s_1$. For a curve $C$ of positive genus and points $x \neq y$, the relative homology $H_1(C, \{x, y\})$ is a mixed Hodge structure, an extension
$$0 \to H_1(C, \mathbb{Z}) \to H_1(C, \{x, y\}) \to \mathbb{Z}(0) \to 0.$$
Such extensions are classified by the Jacobian of $C$, and the class of this one is the Abel–Jacobi image of $y - x$ [@xu2025, Proposition-Definition 5.2.2, Proposition 5.2.3 and Theorem 5.2.4]. It is non-zero, since Abel–Jacobi is injective on a curve of positive genus. Xu's construction, applied with auxiliary section $s_1$, gives an admissible variation $\mathcal{J}$ on $X$ whose fibre at $y \in X_b$ is $H_1(X_b, \{s_1(b), y\})$ for $y \neq s_1(b)$, and the split extension $H_1(X_b) \oplus \mathbb{Z}(0)$ at $y = s_1(b)$ [@xu2025, proof of Proposition 5.2.1 and Lemma 5.2.6, pp. 19–20]. So every fibre of $s_1^*\mathcal{J}$ is split. If $s_1 \neq s_2$, choose $b$ with $s_1(b) \neq s_2(b)$. The fibre of $s_2^*\mathcal{J}$ at $b$ is then a non-split extension. An isomorphism $s_1^* \cong s_2^*$ would give an isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures between the two fibres at $b$. Such an isomorphism preserves the weight filtrations, whose $W_{-1}$ is $H_1(X_b)$ in both, so the image of the summand $\mathbb{Z}(0)$ would split the second extension. $\square$

Xu takes an arbitrary auxiliary section $s_0$ and concludes from the injectivity of the period map. With $s_0 = s_1$ the comparison is between a split and a non-split extension, so it does not depend on how the isomorphism acts on the graded pieces $H_1(X_b)$ and $\mathbb{Z}(0)$. Admissibility of $\mathcal{J}$ is automatic when $B$ is proper, and for general $B$ Xu sketches a proof, citing Hain [@xu2025, Lemma 5.2.6].

**Speculative.** The non-geometric splittings of [Kodaira fibrations](topological-sections-of-kodaira-fibrations.md) are natural test cases for existence. Their profinite completions split the sequence of étale fundamental groups, so the étale obstruction does not exclude them. Xu asks whether every group-theoretic splitting of $\pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(X)) \to \pi_1(\mathrm{VMHS}(B))$ is automatically Hodge theoretic, and says he does not believe it but has no counterexample [@xu2025exact, Remark 6.7]. Nothing is claimed here about either question.

**Status.** Open in general. Both papers are preprints whose latest arXiv versions are dated October 2025. Ferrario's 2020 ETH thesis, *A complex analogue of Grothendieck's section conjecture*, which the second paper cites [@xu2025exact, §6, p. 19], could not be opened for this entry. A literature search in November 2026 found no further answer, but that search was not exhaustive.
