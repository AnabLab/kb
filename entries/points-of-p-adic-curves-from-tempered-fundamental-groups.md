**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $\Sigma$ a set of primes with at least two elements that contains $p$. Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field of characteristic $0$ with residue characteristic $p$, and $\Omega$ the $p$-adic completion of $\bar K$. For a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) $X$ over $\bar K$ or $\Omega$, write $\Pi^{\mathrm{tp}}_X$ for its $\Sigma$-tempered fundamental group. This topological group is defined in earlier work in combinatorial anabelian geometry, with properties taken from André. For $X$ over $\bar K$, its pro-$\Sigma$ completion is the geometric pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental group $\Delta^\Sigma_X$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Notations and Conventions, pp. 14–15]. Write $\tilde X \to X$ for the corresponding universal pro-$\Sigma$ covering, so that $\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde X/X) = \Delta^\Sigma_X$. For $X$ over $K$, write $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X$ for the geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental group, the quotient of $\pi_1(X)$ by the kernel of $\pi_1(X_{\bar K}) \to \Delta^\Sigma_{X_{\bar K}}$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, p. 2].

**Types of points.** Let $X$ be proper over $K$, $\tilde X \to X$ a universal geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ covering, and $\tilde X^{\mathrm{an}}$ the inverse limit of the topological Berkovich spaces of its finite subcoverings. The points of $\tilde X^{\mathrm{an}}$ are the primitive $p$-valuations of the function field $K(\tilde X)$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Proposition 2.3 (vii), p. 38]. A point is of

- *type 1* if it is the valuation given by a point of $\tilde X(\Omega)$;
- *type 2* if it is given by an inverse system of valuations of irreducible components of special fibres of semistable models of the finite subcoverings;
- *type 3* if it arises in a specified way from a node of such a special fibre;
- *type 4* otherwise.

The four types are disjoint. The type of a point of $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ is defined through a lift, and it does not depend on $\Sigma$ or on the lift [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Definition 3.1, p. 73, and Remark 3.1.1, p. 74]. The authors present this as the well-known classification of the points of Berkovich curves [@mochizukitsujimura2024, §3, p. 72].

**Decomposition groups detect points of type 1.** Keep $X$ proper over $K$, and let $\ell \in \Sigma \setminus \{p\}$. Let $x \in X^{\mathrm{an}}$, and let $D_x$ be a decomposition group of $x$ in the geometric $\Sigma$-tempered fundamental group of $X$. Then [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Proposition 3.9, p. 85]:

1. If $x$ is of type 1, then $D_x$ is trivial.
2. If $x$ is of type 4, then an open subgroup of $D_x$ surjects continuously onto $\mathbb{Z}_p$. In particular, $D_x$ is nontrivial.
3. If $x$ is of type 2 or 3 and $X$ satisfies $\Sigma$-[resolution of nonsingularities](resolution-of-nonsingularities.md) ($\Sigma$-RNS), then an open subgroup of $D_x$ surjects continuously onto $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$.
4. If $X$ satisfies $\Sigma$-RNS, then $x$ is of type 1 if and only if $D_x$ is trivial.

Part 2 needs no resolution hypothesis. Its proof characterizes points of type 1 by an unbounded module of differentials [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Proposition 3.8, p. 81]. It then uses units built in the proof of resolution of nonsingularities, which become $p$-divisible in the field fixed by the kernels of all maps to $\mathbb{Z}_p$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, proof of Proposition 3.9, pp. 86–88].

*The role of $p$.* This is checked here from the cited statements. Assume $\Sigma$-RNS. In the geometric pro-$\ell$ fundamental groups of suitable coverings, the decomposition group of a strongly verticial chain is a pro-$\ell$ surface group, that of a weakly verticial chain is trivial, and that of a strongly edge-like chain is $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Proposition 3.5 (i), pp. 78–79]. The chains of these three kinds are pairwise disjoint and exhaust all chains [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Proposition 3.3 (ii), p. 76]. The primitive strongly verticial and the primitive strongly edge-like chains correspond to the points of types 2 and 3 [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Proposition 3.4 (iii), p. 77]. Since the primitive chains correspond to all points of $\tilde X^{\mathrm{an}}$, the points of types 1 and 4 are exactly the primitive weakly verticial chains. So for $\ell \neq p$ the pro-$\ell$ decomposition groups do not separate the points of type 4 from those of type 1, and part 2 separates them by a quotient $\mathbb{Z}_p$. This fits the requirement that $\Sigma$ contain $p$.

**Theorem C (Mochizuki–Tsujimura, preprint).** Let $X$ be a hyperbolic curve over $\bar K$ that satisfies $\Sigma$-RNS, a condition defined for curves over complete discretely valued fields. Then the set $\tilde X(\Omega)$, with its action of $\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde X/X)$, can be reconstructed from the topological group $\Pi^{\mathrm{tp}}_X$, in a purely group-theoretic way and functorially with respect to isomorphisms of topological groups. Hence so can the quotient $X(\Omega) = \tilde X(\Omega)/\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde X/X)$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Corollary 3.10, p. 88]. Every hyperbolic curve over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ is defined over a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and satisfies $\Sigma$-RNS, by the theorem in [resolution of nonsingularities](resolution-of-nonsingularities.md). So this applies to it with $\Omega = \mathbb{C}_p$, which is the form stated in the Introduction [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, Theorem C, p. 6].

*Proof outline.* For proper $X$, an isomorphism of $\Sigma$-tempered fundamental groups induces a homeomorphism of the pro-Berkovich spaces, compatible with the actions [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Corollary 2.5 (i), pp. 54–55]. The authors compare this with homeomorphisms in Lepage's work on Mumford curves [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Remark 2.5.1, p. 56]. The points of type 1 are then picked out by part 4 above. For general $X$, the cuspidal inertia subgroups and the genus are recovered from $\Pi^{\mathrm{tp}}_X$ by a generalization of an earlier result of Mochizuki. The proper case is then applied to the smooth compactifications of the finite étale Galois coverings of genus at least $2$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, proof of Corollary 3.10, p. 88].

*What is reconstructed.* This is checked here. On its own, the $\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde X/X)$-set $\tilde X(\Omega)$ carries no information up to isomorphism. Write $\Delta = \mathrm{Gal}(\tilde X/X)$. For each finite étale Galois subcovering $Z \to X$ of $\tilde X \to X$, each fibre of $Z(\Omega) \to X(\Omega)$ is a torsor under $\mathrm{Gal}(Z/X)$. Passing to the inverse limit, each fibre of $\tilde X(\Omega) \to X(\Omega)$ is a torsor under $\Delta$. So $\tilde X(\Omega) \cong \Delta \times X(\Omega)$ as $\Delta$-sets, and $X(\Omega)$ has the cardinality of $\Omega$ for every curve $X$. Thus any two such sets are isomorphic once their groups are identified. The content of Theorem C is the functoriality: an isomorphism of tempered groups induces a definite bijection of points. The next two statements use exactly this.

**Theorem B (Mochizuki–Tsujimura, preprint).** Let $X^\dagger, X^\ddagger$ be hyperbolic curves over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, $x^\dagger \in X^\dagger(\mathbb{C}_p)$ and $x^\ddagger \in X^\ddagger(\mathbb{C}_p)$, and $X^\dagger_{x^\dagger} = X^\dagger_{\mathbb{C}_p} \setminus \{x^\dagger\}$, $X^\ddagger_{x^\ddagger} = X^\ddagger_{\mathbb{C}_p} \setminus \{x^\ddagger\}$. Let $\sigma_X : X^\dagger \to X^\ddagger$ be an isomorphism over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, inducing $\sigma : \Pi^{\mathrm{tp}}_{X^\dagger} \to \Pi^{\mathrm{tp}}_{X^\ddagger}$ up to inner automorphisms. If an isomorphism $\tilde\sigma : \Pi^{\mathrm{tp}}_{X^\dagger_{x^\dagger}} \to \Pi^{\mathrm{tp}}_{X^\ddagger_{x^\ddagger}}$ of topological groups is compatible with $\sigma$ under the surjections induced by the open immersions $X^\dagger_{x^\dagger} \hookrightarrow X^\dagger_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ and $X^\ddagger_{x^\ddagger} \hookrightarrow X^\ddagger_{\mathbb{C}_p}$, then $x^\ddagger = \sigma_X(x^\dagger)$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, Theorem B, p. 5]. So a point of $X(\mathbb{C}_p)$ is determined by the tempered group of the curve punctured there, together with its map to the tempered group of $X$. The paper proves this for complete discretely valued $K$ and curves satisfying $\Sigma$-RNS [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Corollary 2.5 (ii), p. 55]. With resolution of nonsingularities, it generalizes a theorem of Tsujimura, and its proof is a more sophisticated version of his argument [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Remark 2.5.1, p. 56].

**Decomposition groups of closed points.** For $\square \in \{\dagger, \ddagger\}$, let $K^\square$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_{p^\square}$, $X^\square$ a hyperbolic curve over $K^\square$, and $\Sigma^\square$ a set of primes containing $p^\square$. Suppose some prime $\ell$ lies in both $\Sigma^\dagger \setminus \{p^\dagger\}$ and $\Sigma^\ddagger \setminus \{p^\ddagger\}$. Let $\sigma : \Pi^{(\Sigma^\dagger)}_{X^\dagger} \to \Pi^{(\Sigma^\ddagger)}_{X^\ddagger}$ be an isomorphism of profinite groups. Then:

1. $p^\dagger = p^\ddagger$ and $\Sigma^\dagger = \Sigma^\ddagger$, and $\sigma$ maps the geometric subgroup onto the geometric subgroup and induces an isomorphism $G_{K^\dagger} \to G_{K^\ddagger}$.
2. $\sigma$ induces a bijection between the sets of decomposition groups of closed points of $X^\dagger$ and of $X^\ddagger$.

This is [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Theorem 3.11, p. 89] in the case $L^\square = K^\square$. That this case applies is checked here. The field $K^\square$ is trivially a tamely ramified Galois extension of itself of ramification index $1$, prime to $\ell$, and it is its own completion. Part 2 of the theorem has two hypotheses. The first is a condition on images of inertia, which part 1 of the theorem provides when every pro-$\ell$ closed subgroup of the kernel of the $\ell$-adic cyclotomic character on the absolute Galois group of the residue field is trivial. That holds for finite residue fields [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Remark 3.11.1, p. 91]. The second is $\Sigma$-RNS, which holds by the theorem in [resolution of nonsingularities](resolution-of-nonsingularities.md) with $L = K^\square$. The theorem itself allows tamely ramified extensions $L^\square$ of $K^\square$ that may be infinite, and then concerns closed points of $X^\square$ over the completion of $L^\square$.

*Proof outline.* Part 1 combines earlier results on geometric fundamental groups cited there. For part 2, results of combinatorial anabelian geometry show that $\sigma$ comes, up to inner automorphisms, from an isomorphism of geometrically $\Sigma$-tempered fundamental groups. Theorem C then gives a bijection $\tilde X^\dagger(\Omega^\dagger) \to \tilde X^\ddagger(\Omega^\ddagger)$ compatible with the actions of the two groups $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}$. A generalization of Tate's theorem on the Galois invariants of $\Omega$ identifies the decomposition groups of closed points among the stabilizers [@mochizukitsujimura2024, proof of Theorem 3.11, pp. 89–91]. Combined with Mochizuki's point-theoretic theorem, this gives the [absolute Grothendieck conjecture over $p$-adic fields](absolute-grothendieck-conjecture-for-p-adic-curves.md) [@mochizukitsujimura2024, proof of Theorem 3.12, p. 91]. The authors say that this reconstruction of the decomposition groups of closed points "may be thought of as a sort of a weak version of the Section Conjecture" [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, pp. 6–7].

**Relation to sections.** This is checked here. Let $K$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $X$ a hyperbolic curve over $K$, $\Sigma$ any nonempty set of primes, and $s$ a section of $\mathrm{pr} : \Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X \to G_K$. Then $s$ comes from a $K$-rational point if and only if $s(G_K)$ is a decomposition group of a closed point.

- Recall the decomposition groups. For a closed point $x$, choose a point $\tilde x$ of $\tilde X$ over $x$, where $\tilde X \to X$ is a universal geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ covering. Its stabilizer $D_{\tilde x}$ in $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X$ is a decomposition group of $x$, and the decomposition groups of $x$ are the conjugates of $D_{\tilde x}$. The geometric subgroup acts freely on the points over $x$, so $\mathrm{pr}$ maps $D_{\tilde x}$ injectively onto $G_{K(x)}$, for the embedding $K(x) \subseteq \bar K$ given by $\tilde x$. If $x \in X(K)$, the inverse of $\mathrm{pr} : D_{\tilde x} \to G_K$ is the section $s_{\tilde x}$ given by $\tilde x$, and the sections that come from $x$ are these $s_{\tilde x}$.
- If $s$ comes from $x \in X(K)$, then $s = s_{\tilde x}$ for some $\tilde x$, so $s(G_K) = D_{\tilde x}$.
- Conversely, suppose $s(G_K) = D_{\tilde x}$. Then $G_K = \mathrm{pr}(s(G_K)) = G_{K(x)}$, so $K(x) = K$ and $x$ is $K$-rational. Since $\mathrm{pr} \circ s$ is the identity, $\mathrm{pr}$ restricts to a bijection from $s(G_K)$ onto $G_K$ whose inverse is $s$. So a section is determined by its image, and $s = s_{\tilde x}$.

So, when $\Sigma$ contains $p$ and at least one other prime and $X$ is proper, the $p$-adic analogue of the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) for $X$ asks that the image of every section lie in a set of subgroups that can be reconstructed from the profinite group $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X$ alone [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, p. 6]. For affine $X$, the analogue must also allow the cuspidal sections, whose images lie in decomposition groups at $K$-rational cusps, as in the paragraph on affine curves in the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) entry. For proper $X$, Mochizuki and Tsujimura also characterize the sections that come from rational points as those whose images are maximal stabilizers of points of $\tilde X^{\mathrm{an}}$. See [Berkovich points fixed by sections](berkovich-points-fixed-by-p-adic-sections.md).
