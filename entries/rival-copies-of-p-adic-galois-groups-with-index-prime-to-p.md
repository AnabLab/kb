**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of degree $d$ with residue field of order $q$, and $G = G_K$. As in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), identify $G$ with $\mathrm{Inn}(G)$, a closed normal subgroup of $A = \mathrm{Aut}(G)$; conjugation by $\alpha \in A$ restricts to $\alpha$ on $G$. A *rival copy* is a closed normal subgroup $N \ne G$ of $A$ with $N \cong G$. Put $M = N \cap G$. It is of *type (a)* if $M$ is open in $G$; then $M$ is open in $N$, $M = G_L$ for a finite Galois extension $L/K$, $m := (G : M) = (N : M) \ge 2$, and $F = NG/G$ is a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ of order $m$ (same entry, part 2 (a)). Also $M$ is normal in $A$ (same entry, part 1).

Write $P_K$ for the wild inertia of $G$ and $\Gamma_K = G/P_K = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$, with the generators $\sigma, \tau$, the relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$ and the normal form $\tau^x\sigma^y$ ($x \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$) of the Setting of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md); tame inertia is $I_K/P_K = \overline{\langle\tau\rangle} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. For a profinite group $Q \cong G_{K'}$, with $K'$ finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, write $I_Q$ and $P_Q$ for the closed subgroups that correspond to inertia and wild inertia under an isomorphism $Q \cong G_{K'}$. They do not depend on the isomorphism, as isomorphisms of absolute Galois groups of $p$-adic fields carry inertia to inertia and wild inertia to wild inertia ([invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md)). By [wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md), $P_Q$ is the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $Q$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p$ be odd and $d \ge 3$, and let $N$ be a rival copy of type (a) whose index $m = (G_K : M)$ is prime to $p$.

1. *Common wild inertia.* $P_N = P_K = P_M \subseteq M$. So $L \subseteq K^{\mathrm{tr}}$: $L/K$ is tamely ramified.
2. *Splitting of the tame quotient.* Put $P = P_K$, $H = NG_K$ and $\bar H = H/P$, which contains $\Gamma_K = G_K/P$, $\Gamma_N = N/P$ and $\Gamma_L = M/P = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/L)$. Let $C$ be the centralizer of $\Gamma_K$ in $\bar H$. Then $\bar H = \Gamma_K \times C$, and $C \cong NG_K/G_K$.
3. *$N$ as a twisted diagonal.* There is a continuous surjective homomorphism $\chi : \Gamma_K \to C$ with kernel $\Gamma_L$ such that $\Gamma_N = \{k\chi(k) : k \in \Gamma_K\}$. In particular $NG_K/G_K \cong C \cong \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$.
4. *Lifts of finite order.* Every element of $NG_K/G_K$ of order $k$ is the class of an automorphism $c'$ of $G_K$ of order $k$ that induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$. If $k > 1$, then $c'$ lies in neither $G_K$ nor $N$, and acts nontrivially on $P_K/P_K^{(2)}$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here. Let $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$. By part 3 of the Corollary of [finite normal subgroups of order prime to $p$](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups-have-order-prime-to-p.md), every rival copy of type (a) has index prime to $p$, so parts 1 to 4 apply to it. Hence, if $G_K$ has a rival copy of type (a), then $G_K$ has an automorphism of finite order prime to $p$, other than the identity, that induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$ and whose class lies in a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$.

**Lemma.** This is proved here. Every open subgroup $W$ of $\Gamma_K$ has trivial centralizer in $\Gamma_K$. The same holds in every group isomorphic to $\Gamma_K$.

*Proof.* $W$ contains an open normal subgroup of $\Gamma_K$, with finite quotient, so $\tau^r, \sigma^s \in W$ for some integers $r, s \ge 1$. Let $x = \tau^a\sigma^y$ commute with $W$. Then $\tau^r = x\tau^rx^{-1} = \tau^{rq^y}$, so $r(q^y - 1) = 0$ in the torsion-free group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and $q^y = p^{fy} = 1$, where $q = p^f$. The map $\nu \mapsto p^\nu$ is injective (Setting of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md)), so $fy = 0$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, and $y = 0$. Then $\tau^a = \sigma^s\tau^a\sigma^{-s} = \tau^{aq^s}$ gives $a(q^s - 1) = 0$, so $a = 0$. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** The image of $P_K$ in the finite group $G_K/M$ is a quotient of a pro-$p$ group, so its order is a power of $p$; it divides $m$, which is prime to $p$. So $P_K \subseteq M$. As $P_K$ is a closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $M$, $P_K \subseteq P_M$. Conversely $P_M$ is characteristic in $M$, which is normal in $G_K$, so $P_M$ is a closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G_K$, and $P_M \subseteq P_K$. Thus $P_K = P_M$. The same argument in $N \cong G_K$, using $(N : M) = m$, gives $P_N = P_M$. Since $P_K \subseteq G_L$, the field $L$ lies in the fixed field $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$ of $P_K$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** The subgroup $P$ is normal in $A$, since every automorphism of $G_K$ preserves $P_K$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)); so $\Gamma_K$, $\Gamma_N$ and $\Gamma_L$ are normal subgroups of $\bar H$, and $\Gamma_K \cap \Gamma_N = (G_K \cap N)/P = \Gamma_L$. For $h \in H$, the automorphism of $\Gamma_K$ induced by the automorphism $h$ of $G_K$ is conjugation by $\bar h$ in $\bar H$.

*Tame twists of elements of $N$.* Let $n \in N$. The automorphism $n$ of $G_K$ preserves $I_K$ and $P_K$ ([invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md)) and acts on $I_K/P$ by $t \mapsto t^{u}$ for some $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$, its tame twist. On the other hand, conjugation by $n$ in $A$ restricts to an inner automorphism of $N$. Choose an isomorphism $\vartheta : N \to G_K$; it maps $I_N$ and $P_N$ onto $I_K$ and $P_K$. If the image of $\vartheta(n)$ in $\Gamma_K$ is $\tau^x\sigma^y$, conjugation by $\vartheta(n)$ acts on $I_K/P_K$ by $t \mapsto t^{q^y}$, as $\sigma^y\tau\sigma^{-y} = \tau^{q^y}$ and $I_K/P_K$ is abelian. So conjugation by $n$ acts on $I_N/P_N = I_N/P$ by $t \mapsto t^{q^y}$. Now $M = G_L$ is open in $G_K$ and in $N$. The inertia group of a finite extension $L$ of $K$ is $I_K \cap G_L$, the subgroup fixing $LK^{\mathrm{ur}} = L^{\mathrm{ur}}$. Also $\vartheta(M) = G_{L'}$ for a finite extension $L'/K$, so $I_M = \vartheta^{-1}(I_K \cap G_{L'}) = I_N \cap M$. So $I_M = I_K \cap M = I_N \cap M$, and $I_M/P$ is an open subgroup of $I_K/P$, containing $\tau^e$ for some $e \ge 1$. Both formulas describe conjugation by $n$ in $A$ on $I_M/P$, so $\tau^{eu} = \tau^{eq^y}$, and $u = q^y$, as $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ is torsion-free.

*The splitting.* The class of $n$ lies in the finite normal subgroup $F$ of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$, so it has finitely many conjugates. By the Theorem of [Frobenius shifts](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md), there is $g \in G_K$ such that $\mathrm{inn}(g) \circ n$, which is the element $gn$ of $H$, induces the identity on $\Gamma_K$; that is, $\overline{gn} \in C$. Every element of $H$ is $g'n$ with $g' \in G_K$ and $n \in N$, and $\overline{g'n} = \overline{g'g^{-1}}\cdot\overline{gn} \in \Gamma_KC$. So $\bar H = \Gamma_KC$. The centralizer $C$ of the normal subgroup $\Gamma_K$ is normal in $\bar H$, and $C \cap \Gamma_K$ is the centre of $\Gamma_K$, which is trivial (part 3 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). So $\bar H$ is the internal direct product $\Gamma_K \times C$, and $C \cong \bar H/\Gamma_K \cong H/G_K = NG_K/G_K$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** An element of $C \cap \Gamma_N$ centralizes $\Gamma_L$, which is open in $\Gamma_N$, of index $(N : M) = m$. As $\Gamma_N \cong \Gamma_K$, the Lemma gives $C \cap \Gamma_N = 1$. So the projection $\pi : \bar H = \Gamma_K \times C \to \Gamma_K$ is injective on $\Gamma_N$. It is the identity on $\Gamma_L \subseteq \Gamma_K$, so $\pi(\Gamma_N)$ contains $\Gamma_L$ with index $(\Gamma_N : \Gamma_L) = m = (\Gamma_K : \Gamma_L)$, and $\pi(\Gamma_N) = \Gamma_K$. Let $\chi : \Gamma_K \to C$ be the projection to $C$ composed with the inverse of $\pi|_{\Gamma_N}$, a continuous homomorphism. Then $\Gamma_N = \{k\chi(k) : k \in \Gamma_K\}$, and $\ker\chi = \Gamma_N \cap \Gamma_K = \Gamma_L$. The image of $\chi$ is the image of $\Gamma_N$ in $\bar H/\Gamma_K \cong C$, which is everything because $\bar H = \Gamma_K\Gamma_N$. So $C \cong \Gamma_K/\Gamma_L \cong G_K/M = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** Let $f \in NG_K/G_K$ have order $k$, and let $c \in C$ correspond to $f$ under $C \cong \bar H/\Gamma_K \cong NG_K/G_K$; so $c$ has order $k$, which divides $m$ and is prime to $p$. Choose $\tilde c \in H$ with image $c$. Let $\pi' \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ have component $0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $1$ in every other $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and put $c' = \tilde c^{\pi'}$, a power taken in the procyclic closed subgroup of the profinite group $A$ generated by $\tilde c$. Then $\tilde c^k \in P$, which is pro-$p$, so the closed subgroup generated by $\tilde c^k$ is pro-$p$ and $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ acts on it through $\mathbb{Z}_p$, where $\pi'$ maps to $0$; hence $c'^k = (\tilde c^k)^{\pi'} = 1$. The image of $c'$ in $\bar H$ is $c^{\pi'} = c$, because $c$ has order $k$ and $\pi' \equiv 1 \pmod k$. So $c'$ has order exactly $k$, as an element of $A$, that is, as an automorphism of $G_K$. It induces on $\Gamma_K$ conjugation by $c \in C$, which is the identity. Its class $c'G_K$ corresponds to $c$, so it is $f$.

Let $k > 1$. Then $c' \notin G_K$ and $c' \notin N$, because $G_K$ is torsion-free ([finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), Setting) and so is $N \cong G_K$. If $c'$ acted trivially on $P_K/P_K^{(2)}$, it would be the identity by part 2 of [detection on the Frattini quotient](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-on-the-frattini-quotient-of-wild-inertia.md), as it has finite order; it does not. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What remains of case (a).* For $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$, the Corollary reduces case (a) of the [rival-copy dichotomy](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md) to automorphisms of $G_K$ of finite order prime to $p$ that are trivial on the tame quotient and whose classes lie in a finite normal subgroup. Automorphisms of this kind without the condition on the class exist: for every odd $p$ and every $K$, there is an involution of $G_K$ that induces the identity on $G_K/P_K$ and is not inner ([centralizers of involutions](centralizers-of-involutions-in-p-adic-galois-automorphism-groups.md), part 2). Whether one of them, or any automorphism of finite order prime to $p$ trivial on $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$, has finitely many outer conjugates is not decided here. Such an automorphism acts $\Lambda$-linearly on $P_K/P_K^{(2)}$, with $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)]]$, and nontrivially, by the argument of part 4.
- *The shape of a rival copy.* By parts 2 and 3, modulo the common wild inertia, $N$ is obtained from $G_K$ by twisting with a homomorphism $\chi$ onto a finite group $C$ of order prime to $p$ that centralizes $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$. The lifts $c'$ of part 4 commute with $G_K$ modulo $P_K$.
- *Without the index condition.* Part 1 uses that $m$ is prime to $p$, and the rest of the proof uses part 1. For $p = 3$ this is not known for every rival copy of type (a), and for $d \le 2$ the Theorem of [Frobenius shifts](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md) is not available.
