**Fields and topology.** A two-dimensional local field of characteristic $p$ is a complete discrete valuation field $K$ whose residue field $K^1$ is a complete discrete valuation field with finite residue field $k$. Then $K\cong k((\tau_0))((t_0))$, so elements of $K$ are double Laurent series, and the *$P$-topology* is the topology in which these double series converge. Every finite separable extension $E\subset K_{\mathrm{sep}}$ of $K$ is again such a field, with compatible $P$-topology, and the groups $\Gamma_E=\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{sep}}/E)$ are exactly the open subgroups of $\Gamma_K$. Through Artin–Schreier–Witt duality, the maximal abelian quotient $\Gamma_E^{\mathrm{ab}}$ receives the dual topology, also called the $P$-topology. In dimension one this extra structure is just the profinite topology [@abrashkin2003twodim, Introduction, p. 8].

**$F$-structure and ramification.** The ramification filtration needs a further choice, an *$F$-structure*: a subfield $K_c\subset K$ of one-dimensional constants, meaning a one-dimensional subfield that is algebraically closed in $K$ and closed in the $t_0$-adic topology. The filtration is a decreasing family of normal subgroups $\Gamma_K^{(j)}$ indexed by $J(2)=J_1\sqcup J_2$, where $J_1=\{j\in\mathbb Q: j\ge0\}$, $J_2=\{j\in\mathbb Q^2: j\ge(0,0)\}$ is ordered lexicographically, and every element of $J_1$ is smaller than every element of $J_2$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, Introduction, p. 9]. The $J_1$ part comes from the classical ramification filtration of $\Gamma_{K_c}=\mathrm{Gal}(K_{c,\mathrm{sep}}/K_c)$, and the $J_2$ part filters $\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{sep}}/KK_{c,\mathrm{sep}})$ [@abrashkin2004towards, Introduction, p. 294]. For $K=k((\tau_0))((t_0))$, the choice $K_c=k((t_0))$ is the *standard* $F$-structure [@abrashkin2003twodim, §3, p. 21]. A finite extension $E$ carries the induced $F$-structure, given by the algebraic closure $E_c$ of $K_c$ in $E$, and it is standard when $E$ has local parameters $t_0,\tau_0$ with $t_0\in E_c$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, §5.1, p. 38].

**The fields the group sees.** Let $R(K_c)$ be the $t_0$-adic completion of the maximal purely inseparable extension of $K_c$, and let $R_c(K)$ be the $t_0$-adic completion of the compositum $K\cdot R(K_c)$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, §2.1, p. 15]. Let $R_c(K)_{\mathrm{alg}}$ be the $t_0$-adic closure of the compositum of $R(K_c)$ with an algebraic closure $\bar K$. Then $\Gamma_K=\mathrm{Aut}(R_c(K)_{\mathrm{alg}}/R_c(K))$. Let $\mathrm{Iso}_P(K_c,K,\bar K)$ be the group of $P$-continuous automorphisms $\psi$ of $R_c(K)_{\mathrm{alg}}$ such that $\psi(R_c(K))=R_c(K)$ and $\psi(K_c)=K_c$. Each such $\psi$ acts on $\Gamma_K$ by conjugation, giving an automorphism $\psi^*$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, Introduction, p. 9].

**Theorem (Abrashkin).** Let $p\ge3$, and let $K$ be a two-dimensional local field of characteristic $p$ with any $F$-structure $K_c$. Let $\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{rf}P}(\Gamma_K)$ be the group of continuous automorphisms $\theta$ of $\Gamma_K$ such that:

1. $\theta(\Gamma_K^{(j)})=\Gamma_K^{(j)}$ for every $j\in J(2)$;
2. for every open subgroup $H\subset\Gamma_K$, the induced map $H^{\mathrm{ab}}\to\theta(H)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $P$-continuous.

Then $\psi\mapsto\psi^*$ is an isomorphism of groups $\mathrm{Iso}_P(K_c,K,\bar K)\to\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{rf}P}(\Gamma_K)$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, Introduction, Theorem, p. 9]. The assumption $p\ge3$ stands throughout the paper [@abrashkin2003twodim, Introduction, p. 8]. The proof is carried out for an arbitrary $F$-structure [@abrashkin2003twodim, §5, p. 38].

**What is and is not recovered.** The theorem requires $\psi(R_c(K))=R_c(K)$, not $\psi(K)=K$. Abrashkin explains that the ramification filtration and the $P$-topologies do not reconstruct $K$ itself, only its compositum with the maximal inseparable extension of $K_c$, because the $J_2$ part of the filtration is in effect defined over an algebraic closure of the constant field [@abrashkin2004towards, Introduction, p. 294]. The statement also concerns automorphisms of $\Gamma_K$ for a single field $K$. It does not by itself compare the Galois groups of two different fields, although its proof works throughout with isomorphisms $\Gamma_E\to\Gamma_{E'}$ between corresponding open subgroups [@abrashkin2003twodim, §5, pp. 38–41].

**Method.** For the standard $F$-structure, the proof rests on an explicit description of the ramification ideals in the Lie algebras that nilpotent Artin–Schreier theory attaches to the quotients $\Gamma_K/\Gamma_K^{p^M}C_3(\Gamma_K)$, where $C_3$ denotes the closed subgroup of commutators of order $3$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, §§3.1–3.2, pp. 21–22]. That description is the main result of a companion paper [@abrashkin2004towards, Abstract, p. 293]. Automorphisms compatible with this filtration whose abelianizations are $P$-continuous are then strongly restricted on $\Gamma_K(p)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, Introduction, p. 9].

Given $\theta$, the Galois extensions $E/K$ for which both $E$ and the field $E'$ with $\theta(\Gamma_E)=\Gamma_{E'}$ have standard $F$-structure form a cofinal family [@abrashkin2003twodim, §5.1, Proposition 5.1, p. 39]. For each such $E$, the [one-dimensional theorem](abrashkin-local-grothendieck-theorem.md) applied to the first residue fields gives a $P$-continuous automorphism $\psi_E$ of $R_c(K)_{\mathrm{alg}}$ with $\psi_E(R_c(E))=R_c(E')$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, §5.2, Proposition 5.2, p. 39]. Using the $P$-continuity of the induced isomorphism $\Gamma_{E,1}^{\mathrm{ab}}\to\Gamma_{E',1}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ of maximal abelian quotients of exponent $p$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, §5.2, p. 39], Abrashkin shows that the restriction of $\psi_E$ to $R_c(E)$ induces $\theta$ on $\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, §5.3, Proposition 5.4, p. 40]. These restrictions are compatible as $E$ grows [@abrashkin2003twodim, Proposition 5.5, p. 41], so they glue to an automorphism $\psi$ of $R_c(K)_{\mathrm{alg}}$ preserving $R_c(K)$ and $R(K_c)$. Finally, the one-dimensional theorem for $K_c$ and the triviality of the centralizer of $\Gamma_{K_c}$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, §2.3, Proposition 2.5, p. 20] give $\psi(K_c)=K_c$ [@abrashkin2003twodim, §5.4, p. 42].

**Higher dimensions.** Abrashkin writes that the constructions and statements of the paper generalize directly to local fields of any dimension [@abrashkin2003twodim, Introduction, p. 9], and that the method for the ramification filtration does too [@abrashkin2004towards, Introduction, p. 294]. No written proof for dimension $N\ge3$ was found, either in the 2004 and 2024 papers cited here or in a literature search. In 2024 Abrashkin writes that he expects techniques from his later work on the ramification filtration to "develop more substantial and clear approach to the proof of the local analog of the Grothendieck conjecture for all higher local fields" [@abrashkin2024galois, §0.4(d), p. 677].

**Later restatement.** Abrashkin's 2005 preprint restates the result as Theorem 5, for any dimension $N$ and for an isomorphism between the Galois groups of two fields. There $P$-continuity is assumed only modulo $p^M$ and only for finite extensions with standard $F$-structure. The preprint says that the case $N=2$ was proved in this paper and that general $N$ "can be done along the same lines". The Remark after Theorem 5 notes the two changes of hypotheses: the restriction to standard $F$-structures, and $P$-continuity required only on the abelianized Galois groups of maximal $p$-extensions rather than on the full abelianizations [@abrashkin2007higher, §9.2, Theorem 5 and Remark, p. 38]. The Remark does not discuss the passage from automorphisms of one Galois group to isomorphisms between two. The [mixed-characteristic higher-dimensional theorem](higher-dimensional-local-grothendieck-theorem.md) applies Theorem 5, in the same dimension, to two characteristic-$p$ fields produced by the field-of-norms construction [@abrashkin2007higher, proof of Theorem 6, §9.3.3, pp. 39–40].

**Source version.** Locations in the 2003 paper refer to the Russian original. Its English translation appeared in the Proceedings of the Steklov Institute of Mathematics 241 (2003), 2–34.
