**Statement.** For $i = 1, 2$, let $F_i$ be a number field and let $\tilde F_i/F_i$ be a solvably closed Galois extension, meaning that $\tilde F_i$ has no nontrivial abelian extensions. Put $Q_i = \mathrm{Gal}(\tilde F_i/F_i)$. Let $\Sigma_i$ be a nonempty set of primes, let $X_i$ be a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $F_i$, and let $\Delta_{X_i}$ be the maximal pro-$\Sigma_i$ quotient of $\pi_1(X_i \otimes_{F_i} \bar F_i)$. Suppose that the outer action $G_{F_i} \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_{X_i})$ factors through $Q_i$, so that $Q_i$ is *$\Sigma_i$-compatible* with $X_i$. Since $\Delta_{X_i}$ is slim, pulling back $1 \to \Delta_{X_i} \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Delta_{X_i}) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_{X_i}) \to 1$ along $Q_i \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_{X_i})$ gives an exact sequence $1 \to \Delta_{X_i} \to \Pi_{X_i} \to Q_i \to 1$, and $\Pi_{X_i}$ is a quotient of $\pi_1(X_i)$ [@mochizuki2006solvably, Definitions 1 and 2, Lemma 3.2]. Let $\tilde X_i \to X_i$ be the pro-finite étale covering determined by $\Pi_{X_i}$. Then passing to fundamental groups gives a bijection between the isomorphisms of topological groups $\Pi_{X_1} \to \Pi_{X_2}$ and the compatible pairs of isomorphisms of schemes $\tilde X_1 \to \tilde X_2$, $X_1 \to X_2$ [@mochizuki2006solvably, Theorem 3.4].

**The usual form.** This is checked here. Let $\Sigma$ be a nonempty set of primes, let $X_i$ be a hyperbolic curve over a number field $F_i$, and let $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_{X_i}$ be the quotient of $\pi_1(X_i)$ by the kernel $N_i$ of $\pi_1(X_i \otimes_{F_i} \bar F_i) \to \Delta_{X_i}$. For $\Sigma$ the set of all primes, $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_{X_i} = \pi_1(X_i)$. Then the natural map
$$\mathrm{Isom}(X_1, X_2) \to \mathrm{OutIsom}(\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_{X_1}, \Pi^{(\Sigma)}_{X_2})$$
is bijective. Here $\mathrm{Isom}$ is the set of isomorphisms of schemes, with no base field fixed, and $\mathrm{OutIsom}$ is the set of isomorphisms of profinite groups modulo inner automorphisms of the target.

*Proof.* Take $\tilde F_i = \bar F_i$, which is solvably closed. Then $Q_i = G_{F_i}$ is $\Sigma$-compatible with $X_i$, and $\Pi_{X_i} = \Pi^{(\Sigma)}_{X_i}$. Indeed, an element of $\pi_1(X_i)$ in the kernel of $\pi_1(X_i) \to \Pi_{X_i}$ lies in $\pi_1(X_i \otimes_{F_i} \bar F_i)$ and acts trivially on $\Delta_{X_i}$, so its image in $\Delta_{X_i}$ is central, hence trivial, and the kernel is $N_i$. An isomorphism of schemes $\phi : X_1 \to X_2$ lies over an isomorphism $F_1 \to F_2$, because $F_i$ is the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ in the function field of the geometrically integral curve $X_i$. So $\phi$ maps $\pi_1(X_1 \otimes_{F_1} \bar F_1)$ onto $\pi_1(X_2 \otimes_{F_2} \bar F_2)$, up to conjugacy, and hence maps $N_1$ onto $N_2$, which are characteristic subgroups. Therefore $\phi$ lifts to an isomorphism $\tilde\phi : \tilde X_1 \to \tilde X_2$. Two lifts differ by an element $\gamma$ of $\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde X_2/X_2) = \Pi_{X_2}$, and replacing $\tilde\phi$ by $\gamma \circ \tilde\phi$ composes the induced isomorphism $g \mapsto \tilde\phi g \tilde\phi^{-1}$ with conjugation by $\gamma$. So the bijection of the theorem passes to the quotients on both sides.

*Consequences.* If $\pi_1(X_1) \cong \pi_1(X_2)$ as profinite groups, then $X_1 \cong X_2$ as schemes, and so $F_1 \cong F_2$. Taking $X_1 = X_2 = X$ gives $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X) \cong \mathrm{Aut}(X)$, where $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$ is the group of automorphisms of the scheme $X$.

**Mochizuki's proof.** Mochizuki says that the result is, in essence, an immediate corollary of classical facts about Galois groups of number fields, Uchida's theorem, and his own pro-$\Sigma$ theorem over nonarchimedean local fields [@mochizuki2006solvably, Abstract]. The proof has three steps.

1. *Geometric subgroups are characteristic.* Every isomorphism $\Pi_{X_1} \to \Pi_{X_2}$ maps $\Delta_{X_1}$ onto $\Delta_{X_2}$, and $\Sigma_1 = \Sigma_2$ [@mochizuki2006solvably, Proposition 3.3]. In the first of Mochizuki's two proofs, the image of $\Delta_{X_1}$ in $Q_2$ is a topologically finitely generated closed normal subgroup, hence trivial [@mochizuki2006solvably, proof of Proposition 3.3]. The reason is that if $\tilde F/F$ is Galois and $\tilde F$ has no cyclic extensions of degree $p$ for some prime $p$, then every topologically finitely generated closed normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde F/F)$ is trivial. Mochizuki proves this using Hilbertianity, following Fried and Jarden [@mochizuki2006solvably, Theorem 2.4 and its proof]. For $\tilde F = \bar F$ there is also a direct description: $\Delta_X$ is the maximal topologically finitely generated closed normal subgroup of $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X$ [@mochizuki2012, Theorem 2.6 (vi)].
2. *Uchida's theorem.* The induced isomorphism $Q_1 \to Q_2$ comes from a unique isomorphism $\tilde F_1 \to \tilde F_2$ mapping $F_1$ onto $F_2$ [@mochizuki2006solvably, Theorem 3.1]. This is [Uchida's theorem for solvably closed extensions](uchida-solvably-closed-theorem.md).
3. *The relative theorem.* The isomorphism $\Pi_{X_1} \to \Pi_{X_2}$ now lies over a field isomorphism, and Theorem A of [Mochizuki's Hom theorem](mochizuki-hom-theorem-for-hyperbolic-curves.md) gives the unique compatible pair of isomorphisms of schemes [@mochizuki2006solvably, proof of Theorem 3.4]. Number fields are sub-$p$-adic for every prime $p$. Theorem A is stated for profinite and for geometrically pro-$p$ groups, and the profinite case is derived from the pro-$p$ case by passing to all finite étale coverings [@mochizuki1999, Remark after Theorem 16.5]. Mochizuki applies Theorem A to geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ groups without further comment. The same passage to coverings, for a prime $p \in \Sigma$ and the coverings given by open subgroups of $\Pi_{X_2}$, appears to give this case, but this entry has not written it out.

In the second proof of step 1, cohomological dimensions show that $\Sigma_1 = \Sigma_2$ and that $X_1$ is affine exactly when $X_2$ is. When $\Sigma_i$ is the set of all primes and $X_i$ is affine, Matsumoto's [injectivity theorem](injectivity-of-outer-galois-representations.md) forces $\tilde F_i = \bar F_i$ [@mochizuki2006solvably, proof of Proposition 3.3].

**Comparison with $p$-adic fields.** Over a finite extension $k$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, isomorphisms between such fundamental groups still preserve the geometric subgroups [@mochizuki2012, Corollary 2.8 (ii)]. What fails is step 2, since $G_k$ has automorphisms that do not come from fields (see the [naive local Neukirch–Uchida theorem](naive-local-neukirch-uchida.md)). The [$p$-adic absolute theorem](absolute-grothendieck-conjecture-for-p-adic-curves.md) of Mochizuki and Tsujimura needed new methods, and as recorded in that entry it is stated for $\Sigma$ with at least two elements containing the residue characteristics, the geometrically pro-$p$ case being open. Over number fields, $\Sigma$ may be a single prime.

**Smaller Galois groups.** The theorem applies to groups much smaller than $\pi_1(X)$. Let $\Sigma = \{r\}$ for a prime $r$, and let $X$ be a hyperbolic curve over a number field $F$. Then there is a finite Galois extension $F_1/F$ such that the maximal solvable extension $F_1^{\mathrm{sol}}$ of $F_1$ is $\Sigma$-compatible with $X$ [@mochizuki2006solvably, Proposition 4.1 (ii)]. The reason is that the kernel of $\mathrm{Out}(\Delta) \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Delta^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{F}_r)$ is pro-$r$ for every topologically finitely generated pro-$r$ group $\Delta$ [@mochizuki2006solvably, Proposition 4.1 (i)]. So $X$ is determined, as a scheme, by an extension of $\mathrm{Gal}(F_1^{\mathrm{sol}}/F)$ by its pro-$r$ geometric fundamental group: a hyperbolic curve over a number field with a compatible group of this kind isomorphic to it is isomorphic to $X$. For once-punctured elliptic curves, Mochizuki also finds such extensions that are linearly disjoint from fields generated by $\ell$-torsion points, under conditions on $\ell \notin \Sigma$ [@mochizuki2006solvably, Propositions 4.3 and 4.4].

**Belyi cuspidalization.** A hyperbolic orbicurve is *of strictly Belyi type* if it is defined over a number field and isogenous to a hyperbolic curve of genus $0$ [@mochizuki2013, Definition 3.5]. Two hyperbolic orbicurves are *isogenous* if some hyperbolic orbicurve admits finite étale morphisms to both [@mochizuki2013, §0, p. 177].

*Examples.* These are checked here.

- A hyperbolic curve of genus $0$ over a number field is of strictly Belyi type.
- Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over a number field $k$, with origin $O$ and $2$-torsion subscheme $E[2]$. Multiplication by $2$ restricts to a finite étale morphism $E \setminus E[2] \to E \setminus \{O\}$, because $[2]^{-1}(O) = E[2]$. The involution $-1$ acts freely on $E \setminus E[2]$, since its fixed points are $E[2]$, and the quotient is $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ minus the image of $E[2]$, which is a hyperbolic curve of type $(0, 4)$. So $E \setminus \{O\}$ is of strictly Belyi type.
- No proper hyperbolic curve is of strictly Belyi type. A finite étale morphism between orbicurves is finite and surjective, so its source is proper exactly when its target is. A hyperbolic curve of genus $0$ is not proper.

*The algorithm.* Let $X$ be a hyperbolic orbicurve of strictly Belyi type over a sub-$p$-adic field $k$, for instance a number field. There is a functorial group-theoretic algorithm that constructs the "NF-portion of the function field" of $X$ from the extension $1 \to \Delta_X \to \pi_1(X) \to G_k \to 1$ [@mochizuki2015, Theorem 1.9]. Write $\bar k_{\mathrm{NF}}$ for the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\bar k$. The algorithm has five steps [@mochizuki2015, Theorem 1.9 (a)–(e)].

1. Belyi cuspidalization constructs the surjections $\pi_1(U) \to \pi_1(Y)$, where $Y$ is a finite étale covering of $X$ and $U \subseteq Y$ is obtained by removing finitely many points defined over $\bar k_{\mathrm{NF}}$, together with the decomposition groups of these points [@mochizuki2013, Corollary 3.7].
2. It identifies the inertia groups of the removed points with a cyclotome built from $\pi_1(U)$.
3. It constructs the classes in $H^1$ of $\pi_1(U)$ that come from principal divisors supported on the cusps.
4. It recognizes the Kummer classes of rational functions and constants defined over $\bar k_{\mathrm{NF}}$, giving the multiplicative groups of $\bar k_{\mathrm{NF}}$ and of the function fields over $\bar k_{\mathrm{NF}}$ of suitable coverings.
5. It recovers addition from divisors and evaluation at points, by an argument that Mochizuki finds implicit in Uchida's work on function fields [@mochizuki2015, Proposition 1.3 and the sentence before it].

The algorithm is functorial for open injective homomorphisms of such extensions and for change of base field [@mochizuki2015, Theorem 1.9]. When $k$ is a number field or a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, its input can be the profinite group $\pi_1(X)$ alone [@mochizuki2015, Remark 1.9.2], because the geometric subgroup is then characterized group-theoretically [@mochizuki2012, Theorem 2.6 (v), (vi)]. In general the algorithm does not construct $\bar k$ itself [@mochizuki2015, Remark 1.9.3]. For a number field $k$, though, $\bar k_{\mathrm{NF}} = \bar k$, so the algorithm constructs $\bar k$ as a field.

*Meaning.* Mochizuki regards the functoriality of the algorithm under isomorphisms as a new proof of the absolute version over number fields, for which he cites Theorem 3.4 above. This proof does not depend logically on the Neukirch–Uchida theorem. To his knowledge it is the first proof of a consequence of Neukirch–Uchida that is logically independent of it and constructs the number fields explicitly [@mochizuki2015, Remark 1.9.5 (i)]. Since the algorithm needs strictly Belyi type, the new proof covers only such curves. The same algorithm works over a number field and over its nonarchimedean completions, compatibly with base change. Mochizuki contrasts this with the proof of Neukirch–Uchida, which uses how primes decompose in extensions of the global field [@mochizuki2015, Remark 1.9.5 (ii)]. The only non-elementary ingredient is Belyi cuspidalization, which depends on Mochizuki's Hom theorem. Dropping it gives a very elementary algorithm in the birational setting: it constructs the function field of a curve over a Kummer-faithful field, a class that includes sub-$p$-adic fields, from the Galois group of the function field together with its projection to $G_k$ [@mochizuki2015, Introduction, p. 943, Remark 1.5.4 (i) and Theorem 1.11].
