**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd and $K/\mathbb Q_p$ finite of even degree $d\ge4$. Use the Setting of [the shear theorem](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-wild-frattini.md), which takes its presentation from [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). So $G=G_K=F/(\varrho)$; $\mathcal G\cong\Gamma=G/P$ is generated by $\sigma,\tau$; $P$ is wild inertia, which is free pro-$p$ and characteristic ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)); $p^s$ is the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$; $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, with augmentation $\operatorname{aug}$ and augmentation ideal $\mathfrak a=\ker\operatorname{aug}$; $\omega:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ is the character on $\mu_p$; $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$; and, as $d$ is even,
$$\varrho=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa\,x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d].$$
By part 1 of the tame-group-algebra theorem, $V=P/P^{(2)}=L\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^d\Lambda\bar x_i$ with $L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$ and each $\Lambda\bar x_i$ free of rank one. For $i\ge1$ let $\mathrm{pr}_i:V\to\Lambda$ be the coefficient of $\bar x_i$; it is continuous and $\Lambda$-linear. Part 3 there gives $Z(\Gamma)=1$. An automorphism of $G$ is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$; it then acts $\Lambda$-linearly on $V$.

For $\rho\in\Gamma$ we have $\omega(\rho^*)=1=\operatorname{aug}(\rho)$ and $\operatorname{aug}(\rho^*)=\omega(\rho)$. By linearity and continuity, $\omega(\lambda^*)=\operatorname{aug}(\lambda)$ and $\operatorname{aug}(\lambda^*)=\omega(\lambda)$ for all $\lambda\in\Lambda$. As $*$ is an involution, it maps $\ker\omega$ bijectively onto $\mathfrak a$.

*Lower central input.* As in [the boundary-twist theorem](boundary-twists-exclude-finite-outer-conjugacy-of-wild-scalars.md), let $P_j$ be the lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$ and $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$. Its lower central input gives the following. The $\mathcal L_j$ are torsion-free. $\mathcal L_2/p$ is the completed degree-two free Lie space $\mathcal L_2(V)$ on $V=H/p$, functorially in $V$; that is, $\mathcal L_2(V)=\varprojlim_U\mathcal L_2(V/U)$ over the open subspaces $U$ of $V$, and $\mathcal L_2(E)=\Lambda^2E$ for a finite-dimensional space $E$. The map $h\mapsto\operatorname{ad}(h)=[h,\cdot\,]$ from $H$ to $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ is injective, also modulo $p$. Conjugation by lifts of elements of $\Gamma$ makes $\mathcal L_2(V)$ a compact $\Lambda$-module with $\rho\cdot[u,v]=[\rho u,\rho v]$.

*Degree-one Johnson maps.* By step 2 of the shear theorem, for an automorphism $\theta$ of $P$ acting trivially on $H$, $J_1(\theta)(xP_2)=\theta(x)x^{-1}P_3$ is a continuous homomorphism $H\to\mathcal L_2$. Moreover $J_1(\theta\theta')=J_1(\theta)+J_1(\theta')$; $J_1(\beta\theta\beta^{-1})=\beta_2J_1(\theta)\beta_H^{-1}$ for every automorphism $\beta$ of $P$, with induced actions $\beta_H$ on $H$ and $\beta_2$ on $\mathcal L_2$; and $J_1(c_g)=\operatorname{ad}(gP_2)$ for $g\in P$, where $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$. Automorphisms of $G$ preserve $P$, and these rules are applied to their restrictions. If an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ fixes $\mathcal G$ pointwise, then $J_1(\theta)$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant, since $\theta(\rho x\rho^{-1})(\rho x\rho^{-1})^{-1}=\rho\,\theta(x)x^{-1}\rho^{-1}$.

*Mixing automorphisms.* For $\lambda\in\ker\omega$, part 2 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md) gives an automorphism $\theta_\lambda$ of $G$ with $\theta_\lambda(\rho)\in\rho P^{(2)}$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$, so that $\theta_\lambda$ is tame-trivial. It fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\notin\{1,4\}$, and $\bar\theta_\lambda(\bar x_1)=\bar x_1+\lambda\bar x_3$, $\bar\theta_\lambda(\bar x_4)=\bar x_4-\lambda^*\bar x_2$ in $V$. Fix one such $\theta_\lambda$ for each $\lambda$. For $\mu\in\ker\omega$ let $N_\mu:V\to V$ be the $\Lambda$-linear map with $N_\mu(\bar x_1)=\mu\bar x_3$, $N_\mu(\bar x_4)=-\mu^*\bar x_2$, and $N_\mu(\bar x_i)=0$ for the other $i$, including $i=0$. Then $\bar\theta_\lambda=1+N_\lambda$, as both sides are $\Lambda$-linear and agree on generators. The image of $N_\lambda$ lies in $\Lambda\bar x_2\oplus\Lambda\bar x_3$, which every $N_\mu$ kills, so $N_\mu N_\lambda=0$. Also $N_\lambda+N_\mu=N_{\lambda+\mu}$.

**Complementary twists.** For $1\le k\le d/2$ and $t\in\mathbb Z_p$, put
$$w_k=x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\cdots[x_{2k-1},x_{2k}],$$
the initial segment of $\varrho$ after its torsion part. Let $T_{k,t}$ fix $\mathcal G$ and the $x_i$ with $i=0$ or $i>2k$, and send $x_i$ to $w_k^tx_iw_k^{-t}$ for $1\le i\le2k$. For $k=1$ and $t=1$ this is the twist $T'$ of step 2 of the shear theorem.

**Lemma 1 (proved here).**

- (a) $T_{k,t}$ induces an automorphism of $G$, and $T_{k,t}T_{k,t'}=T_{k,t+t'}$.
- (b) $T_{k,t}$ is tame-trivial and acts trivially on $H$, hence on $V$.
- (c) There is a continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant homomorphism $j_k:H\to\mathcal L_2$ with $j_k(\bar x_i)=[\bar x_1,\bar x_i]$ for $2\le i\le2k$ and $j_k(\bar x_i)=0$ for $i\in\{0,1\}$ and $i>2k$, and $J_1(T_{k,t})=tp^sj_k$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ be tame-trivial and act trivially on $H$. Suppose
$$J_1(\alpha)=p^m\delta+\operatorname{ad}(h_0)$$
with $m\ge0$, $h_0\in H$, and $\delta:H\to\mathcal L_2$ continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant. Let $\bar\delta:V\to\mathcal L_2(V)$ be the reduction of $\delta$ modulo $p$, and put $c_\delta=c(\bar\delta(\bar x_2))\in\Lambda$, with the contraction $c$ of Lemma 2 below. If $\lambda,\lambda'\in\ker\omega$ and $\theta_\lambda\alpha\theta_\lambda^{-1}$ and $\theta_{\lambda'}\alpha\theta_{\lambda'}^{-1}$ have the same class in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$, then
$$(\lambda-\lambda')^*c_\delta=0.$$
Consequently the class of $\alpha$ has at least $|\mathfrak a c_\delta|$ conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$, and infinitely many if $c_\delta\ne0$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $1\le k\le d/2$ and $t\in\mathbb Z_p\setminus\{0\}$. Let $B$ be a tame-trivial automorphism of $G$ acting trivially on $P/P_3$. Examples are $B=1$ and any product of boundary twists: the boundary twist of a surface pair $(a,b)$, from step 1 of the boundary-twist theorem and step 1 of the shear theorem, fixes $\mathcal G$ and acts trivially on $P/P_3$, because $[a,b]\in P_2$. Then $\lambda\mapsto[\theta_\lambda T_{k,t}B\theta_\lambda^{-1}]$ is injective on the infinite group $\ker\omega$, so the class of $T_{k,t}B$ has infinitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. In particular $T_{k,t}$ is not inner, and $t\mapsto[T_{k,t}]$ is an injective homomorphism $\mathbb Z_p\to\operatorname{Out}(G)$; each $T_{k,t}$ acts trivially on $\Gamma$ and on $V$.

**Lemma 2 (a contraction; proved here).** Put
$$b(u,v)=\mathrm{pr}_1(u)\operatorname{aug}(\mathrm{pr}_2v)-\mathrm{pr}_1(v)\operatorname{aug}(\mathrm{pr}_2u)\qquad(u,v\in V).$$
There is a unique continuous linear map $c:\mathcal L_2(V)\to\Lambda$ with $c([u,v])=b(u,v)$. It satisfies:

- (i) $c(\nu\cdot\xi)=\nu\,c(\xi)$ for $\nu\in\Lambda$ and $\xi\in\mathcal L_2(V)$;
- (ii) $c([\gamma,\bar x_4])=0$ for $\gamma\in V$;
- (iii) $c\circ\mathcal L_2(1+N_\mu)=c$ for $\mu\in\ker\omega$;
- (iv) $c([\bar x_1,\bar x_2])=1$.

*Proof.* The map $b$ is continuous, $\mathbb F_p$-bilinear and alternating. Let $J$ be an open subspace of $\Lambda$, and $b_J:V\times V\to\Lambda/J$ the composite with the projection. The preimage of $0$ under $b_J$ is open and contains $\{0\}\times V$. As $V$ is compact, the tube lemma gives an open neighbourhood $U'$ of $0$ with $U'\times V$ in this preimage, and $U'$ contains an open subspace $U$, since $V$ is a profinite $\mathbb F_p$-vector space. As $b$ is alternating, $b_J$ also vanishes on $V\times U$. So $b_J$ induces a linear map $\Lambda^2(V/U)\to\Lambda/J$, $\bar u\wedge\bar v\mapsto b_J(u,v)$, and the same holds for every smaller open subspace. These maps are compatible as $U$ and $J$ shrink, and their limit is a continuous linear map $c:\mathcal L_2(V)\to\Lambda$ with $c([u,v])=b(u,v)$. It is unique, because the brackets span a dense subspace of $\mathcal L_2(V)$.

For (i), let $\rho\in\Gamma$. The $\mathrm{pr}_i$ are $\Lambda$-linear and $\operatorname{aug}(\rho\lambda)=\operatorname{aug}(\lambda)$, so $b(\rho u,\rho v)=\rho\,b(u,v)$; that is, $c(\rho\cdot\xi)=\rho\,c(\xi)$ for brackets $\xi$, hence for all $\xi$ by linearity and continuity. Both sides of (i) are continuous in $\nu$ and agree on $\Gamma$, whose span is dense in $\Lambda$. For (ii), $\mathrm{pr}_1(\bar x_4)=\mathrm{pr}_2(\bar x_4)=0$. For (iii), put $\bar\psi=1+N_\mu$. For $v\in V$, $\mathrm{pr}_1(N_\mu v)=0$ and $\mathrm{pr}_2(N_\mu v)=-\mathrm{pr}_4(v)\mu^*$, so $\operatorname{aug}(\mathrm{pr}_2(N_\mu v))=-\operatorname{aug}(\mathrm{pr}_4v)\,\omega(\mu)=0$. Hence $\mathrm{pr}_1\circ\bar\psi=\mathrm{pr}_1$ and $\operatorname{aug}\circ\mathrm{pr}_2\circ\bar\psi=\operatorname{aug}\circ\mathrm{pr}_2$, so $b(\bar\psi u,\bar\psi v)=b(u,v)$. Since $\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)[u,v]=[\bar\psi u,\bar\psi v]$, the maps $c\circ\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)$ and $c$ agree on brackets, hence everywhere. For (iv), $b(\bar x_1,\bar x_2)=1\cdot1-0=1$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (proved here).** If $0\ne e\in\Lambda$, then $\mathfrak a e$ is infinite.

*Proof.* Since $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p\cdot1+\mathfrak a$, we have $\Lambda e=\mathbb F_pe+\mathfrak ae$. If $\mathfrak ae$ were finite, $\Lambda e$ would be a finite subset of $\Lambda$ stable under left multiplication by $\Gamma$. This action is continuous, so an open subgroup $U$ of $\Gamma$ fixes $e$. Let $\pi_p\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ have $p$-component $1$ and all other components $0$, and put $\gamma_p=\sigma^{\pi_p}$. Killing $\tau$ in the presentation of $\mathcal G$ leaves the free profinite group on $\sigma$, so the closed subgroup generated by $\sigma$ maps onto $\Gamma/I\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, where $I$ is tame inertia. The closed subgroup generated by $\gamma_p$ is the image of $\pi_p\widehat{\mathbb Z}\cong\mathbb Z_p$ under $n\mapsto\sigma^n$. So it is pro-$p$, and it maps onto the factor $\mathbb Z_p$ of $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, so it is infinite. Its intersection with $U$ is open in it, so $\gamma_p^{p^n}\in U$ for some $n$. Then $\gamma_p^{p^n}$ also generates an infinite pro-$p$ closed subgroup and $(\gamma_p^{p^n}-1)e=0$. Lemma 2 of [the central-element construction](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md) gives $e=0$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Proof of Lemma 1.** (a) The group $F$ is free among semidirect products of $\mathcal G$ with pro-$p$ groups, and $w_k^tx_iw_k^{-t}$ lies in the pro-$p$ part, so $T_{k,t}$ defines an endomorphism of $F$ fixing $\mathcal G$. Each of $x_1,\dots,x_{2k}$ is conjugated by $w_k^t$, and $w_k$ is a word in them. So $T_{k,t}(w_k)=w_k^tw_kw_k^{-t}=w_k$, and $T_{k,t}(w_k^{t'})=w_k^{t'}$ for $t'\in\mathbb Z_p$ by continuity. The remaining factors $(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa$ and $[x_{2k+1},x_{2k+2}]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d]$ of $\varrho$ involve only $\mathcal G$ and fixed generators. Hence $T_{k,t}(\varrho)=\varrho$. For $1\le i\le2k$,
$$T_{k,t}T_{k,t'}(x_i)=T_{k,t}(w_k^{t'}x_iw_k^{-t'})=w_k^{t'}w_k^tx_iw_k^{-t}w_k^{-t'}=T_{k,t+t'}(x_i),$$
and both sides fix the other generators. So $T_{k,t}T_{k,t'}=T_{k,t+t'}$ on $F$. As $T_{k,0}=1$, each $T_{k,t}$ is an automorphism of $F$ with inverse $T_{k,-t}$. It maps $(\varrho)$, the closed normal subgroup generated by $\varrho$, onto itself, so it induces an automorphism of $G$.

(b) $T_{k,t}$ fixes $\mathcal G$, so it is tame-trivial and preserves $P$, the image of the closed normal subgroup generated by the $x_i$. For $i\le2k$, $T_{k,t}(x_i)x_i^{-1}=[w_k^t,x_i]\in P_2$, and the other $x_i$ are fixed. As $T_{k,t}$ fixes $\mathcal G$, its action on $H$ commutes with $\Gamma$. It fixes each $\bar x_i$, and these generate $H$ as a topological $\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$-module (step 2 of the shear theorem). So it acts trivially on $H$.

(c) The group $P/P_3$ has class at most two, so commutators induce a biadditive map $H\times H\to\mathcal L_2$, and $[y^t,x]\equiv[y,x]^t$ modulo $P_3$ for $t\in\mathbb Z_p$. As $w_k\in x_1^{p^s}P_2$ and $[P_2,P]\subseteq P_3$, for $1\le i\le2k$
$$J_1(T_{k,t})(\bar x_i)=[w_k^t,x_i]P_3=t\,[x_1^{p^s},x_i]P_3=tp^s[\bar x_1,\bar x_i],$$
written additively. This vanishes for $i=1$, and $J_1(T_{k,t})$ vanishes at the other $\bar x_i$ since $T_{k,t}$ fixes them. $J_1(T_{k,t})$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant, as $T_{k,t}$ fixes $\mathcal G$. Its values at the generators lie in the closed $\Gamma$-stable subgroup $p^s\mathcal L_2$, so its image does. Multiplication by $p^s$ is a homeomorphism of the torsion-free compact group $\mathcal L_2$ onto $p^s\mathcal L_2$. So $J_1(T_{k,1})=p^sj_k$ for a continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant $j_k$ with the stated values. Finally $J_1(T_{k,t})$ and $tp^sj_k$ are continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant homomorphisms that agree on the $\bar x_i$, so they are equal. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** Choose $g_0\in P$ with $g_0P_2=h_0$, and put $\alpha'=\alpha c_{g_0}^{-1}$. It is tame-trivial, acts trivially on $H$, and $J_1(\alpha')=J_1(\alpha)-J_1(c_{g_0})=p^m\delta$. Also $\theta_\lambda\alpha'\theta_\lambda^{-1}=\theta_\lambda\alpha\theta_\lambda^{-1}c_{\theta_\lambda(g_0)}^{-1}$, so all classes in the statement are unchanged when $\alpha$ is replaced by $\alpha'$.

Suppose the classes of $\theta_\lambda\alpha'\theta_\lambda^{-1}$ and $\theta_{\lambda'}\alpha'\theta_{\lambda'}^{-1}$ agree. Put $\psi=\theta_{\lambda'}^{-1}\theta_\lambda$ and $\mu=\lambda-\lambda'\in\ker\omega$. Then $\psi\alpha'\psi^{-1}=\alpha'c_g$ for some $g\in G$. The automorphism $c_g=\alpha'^{-1}\psi\alpha'\psi^{-1}$ is tame-trivial, so $gP\in Z(\Gamma)=1$ and $g\in P$. Both $\alpha'$ and $\psi\alpha'\psi^{-1}$ act trivially on $H$, and the rules for $J_1$ give
$$p^m\big(\psi_2\delta\psi_H^{-1}-\delta\big)=\psi_2J_1(\alpha')\psi_H^{-1}-J_1(\alpha')=J_1(c_g)=\operatorname{ad}(h),\qquad h=gP_2.$$
So $[h,H]\subseteq p^m\mathcal L_2$. If $m\ge1$, injectivity of $\operatorname{ad}$ modulo $p$ gives $h\in pH$. Write $h=ph_1$. Then $[h_1,H]\subseteq p^{m-1}\mathcal L_2$, as $\mathcal L_2$ is torsion-free. Repeating gives $h=p^mh'$ with $h'\in H$; for $m=0$ take $h'=h$. As $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ is torsion-free,
$$\psi_2\delta\psi_H^{-1}-\delta=\operatorname{ad}(h').\tag{1}$$
Reduce (1) modulo $p$. The action $\psi_H$ reduces to the action $\bar\psi$ of $\psi$ on $V$. Since $\psi_2[x,y]=[\psi x,\psi y]$, functoriality in the lower central input shows that $\psi_2$ reduces to $\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)$. Both $\bar\psi$ and $\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)$ are $\Lambda$-linear, since $\psi$ is tame-trivial. The map $\delta$ reduces to $\bar\delta$, which is $\Lambda$-linear because $\delta$ is continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant. With $\gamma\in V$ the residue of $h'$, this gives
$$\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)\circ\bar\delta\circ\bar\psi^{-1}-\bar\delta=\operatorname{ad}(\gamma)\quad\text{on }V.\tag{2}$$
By the Setting, $\bar\psi=(1-N_{\lambda'})(1+N_\lambda)=1+N_\mu$ and $\bar\psi^{-1}=1-N_\mu$. So $\bar\psi^{-1}\bar x_4=\bar x_4+\mu^*\bar x_2$. Evaluating (2) at $\bar x_4$ gives
$$\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)\bar\delta(\bar x_4)-\bar\delta(\bar x_4)+\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)\big(\mu^*\cdot\bar\delta(\bar x_2)\big)=[\gamma,\bar x_4].$$
Apply $c$. By Lemma 2 (iii), the first two terms cancel. By (iii) and (i), the third becomes $\mu^*c(\bar\delta(\bar x_2))=\mu^*c_\delta$. By (ii), the right side vanishes. So $\mu^*c_\delta=0$.

For the count, let $A=\{\mu\in\ker\omega:\mu^*c_\delta=0\}$. By what was just shown, $\lambda$ in different cosets of $A$ give different classes $[\theta_\lambda\alpha\theta_\lambda^{-1}]$. The map $\mu\mapsto\mu^*c_\delta$ is additive with kernel $A$. Its image is $\mathfrak ac_\delta$, as $*$ maps $\ker\omega$ onto $\mathfrak a$. So the number of classes is at least $|\ker\omega/A|=|\mathfrak ac_\delta|$, which is infinite if $c_\delta\ne0$, by Lemma 3. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** Put $\alpha=T_{k,t}B$. As $B$ acts trivially on $P/P_3$, it acts trivially on $H$ and $J_1(B)=0$. So $\alpha$ is tame-trivial, acts trivially on $H$, and $J_1(\alpha)=J_1(T_{k,t})=tp^sj_k$ by Lemma 1. Write $tp^s=p^mu$ with $u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$, and take $\delta=uj_k$ and $h_0=0$. Then $\bar\delta(\bar x_2)=\bar u[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]$, and $c_\delta=\bar u\ne0$ by Lemma 2 (iv). So $A=\{\mu\in\ker\omega:\mu^*=0\}=0$, and the theorem makes $\lambda\mapsto[\theta_\lambda\alpha\theta_\lambda^{-1}]$ injective on $\ker\omega$. This group is infinite, being of index $p$ in the infinite group $\Lambda$. An inner automorphism has a single outer conjugate, so $T_{k,t}$ is not inner for $t\ne0$. By Lemma 1 (a), $t\mapsto[T_{k,t}]$ is then an injective homomorphism. Lemma 1 (b) gives the last assertion. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The case $k=d/2$.* In $G$, the relation $\varrho=1$ gives $w_{d/2}=\big((x_0,\tau)^\kappa\big)^{-1}x_0^\sigma$. Since $T_{d/2,t}$ fixes $w_{d/2}$, the automorphism $T_{d/2,t}c_{w_{d/2}^{-t}}$ fixes $x_1,\dots,x_d$ and conjugates $x_0$ and every element of $\mathcal G$ by $w_{d/2}^{-t}$. It has the same outer class as $T_{d/2,t}$, so for $t\ne0$ it too has infinitely many outer conjugates.
- *What the coefficient measures.* $c_\delta$ keeps the $\bar x_1$-coefficient of the $\Lambda\bar x_1\wedge\Lambda\bar x_2$ part of $\bar\delta(\bar x_2)$, after applying $\operatorname{aug}$ to the $\bar x_2$-coefficient. Equivariant inner terms do not affect it. Indeed, if $\operatorname{ad}(\gamma)$ is $\Lambda$-linear on $V$, then $\gamma$ is fixed by $\Gamma$ by injectivity of $\operatorname{ad}$, so $\mathrm{pr}_1(\gamma)=0$ by Lemma 2 of the central-element construction, and $c([\gamma,\bar x_2])=\mathrm{pr}_1(\gamma)=0$.
- *What is not covered.* The theorem uses only the mixing automorphisms of part 2, and says nothing when $c_\delta=0$. A boundary twist acts trivially on $P/P_3$, so its $J_1$ vanishes and the theorem does not apply to it. For instance, for $T_{1,1}T_{2,-1}$ the decomposition $J_1=p^s(j_1-j_2)$, with $\delta=j_1-j_2$, gives $c_\delta=0$. For odd $d$, the factor $x_1^{p^s}[x_1,y_1]$ involves $y_1$, which is built from tame conjugates of $x_1$, so conjugating $x_1$ by it does not preserve $\varrho$, and no exact analogue of $T_{k,t}$ is given here. For $d=2$, $T_{1,t}$ exists but there is no surface pair, hence no mixing automorphism. The case $p=2$ is not treated.
- *Scope.* Whether every tame-trivial automorphism with finitely many outer conjugates is inner remains open; this is item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535). The results here are proved from the linked entries on `main`. No novelty is claimed, and the sources behind those entries were not reopened.
