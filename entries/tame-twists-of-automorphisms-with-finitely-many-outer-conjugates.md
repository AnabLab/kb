**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of degree $d \ge 3$ with residue field of order $q$, $G = G_K$ with wild inertia $P_K$, and $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K) = G/P_K$. Use the notation of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md): $G = F/(\varrho)$ with $\mathcal{G} = \Gamma$ generated by $\sigma, \tau$, tame inertia $I = \overline{\langle\tau\rangle} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ with closed subgroups $I_m = \overline{\langle\tau^m\rangle}$, $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\Gamma]]$, the character $\omega : \Gamma \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ given by the action on $\mu_p$, the involution $\lambda \mapsto \lambda^*$ with $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, the $\Lambda$-module $\bar V = P_K/P_K^{(2)}$, the surface pair $(x_s, x_t)$, and for $\lambda \in \Lambda^\times$ the automorphism $\bar\psi_\lambda$ of $G/P_K^{(2)}$ induced by the automorphism $\theta_\lambda$ of $G$ constructed there.

Every $\eta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ preserves $P_K$ and $I_K$ ([invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md)), so it induces an automorphism $\beta$ of $\Gamma$ with $\beta(\tau) = \tau^u$ for a unit $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$, the *tame twist* of $\eta$; in the notation of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md), $\beta = \beta_{u,a}$ with $\beta_{u,a}(\sigma) = \sigma\tau^a$. Let $\beta_\Lambda$ be the continuous ring automorphism of $\Lambda$ extending $\beta$. Since $P_K$ is characteristic ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)) and $P_K^{(2)}$ is characteristic in $P_K$, $\eta$ induces an automorphism $\eta_2$ of $G/P_K^{(2)}$ and an additive automorphism $\bar\eta$ of $\bar V$. As $\eta_2(g)$ maps to $\beta(\bar g)$ in $\Gamma$ and $P_K$ acts trivially on $\bar V$, $\bar\eta(\rho v) = \beta(\rho)\bar\eta(v)$ for $\rho \in \Gamma$, hence $\bar\eta(\lambda v) = \beta_\Lambda(\lambda)\bar\eta(v)$ for $\lambda \in \Lambda$.

The powers $p^\nu$, $\nu \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, are defined in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ by continuity. The map $\nu \mapsto p^\nu$ is injective [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.4) (ii) with $f = 1$, p. 412], so its image, which is the closure $\overline{\langle p\rangle}$ of the subgroup generated by $p$, is isomorphic to $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and torsion-free.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$, and let $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ be an automorphism whose class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has only finitely many conjugates, with tame twist $u$.

1. $u \in \overline{\langle p\rangle}$: $\theta$ acts on tame inertia by $t \mapsto t^{p^\nu}$ for some $\nu \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$.
2. If $\theta$ has finite order, then $\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$.

Part 1 cannot be strengthened to $u = 1$: conjugation by a lift of $\sigma$ is inner and has tame twist $q$. Part 1 needs no condition on the order of $\theta$. So for every automorphism of $\Gamma$ with tame twist outside $\overline{\langle p\rangle}$ that lifts to $G_K$, for instance the finite-order tame automorphisms with $u \ne 1$ that lift by [tame automorphisms for odd $p$](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md), every lift has infinitely many outer conjugates.

**Corollary.** This is proved here. Let $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$, and write $d = 2g + r$ with $r \in \{1, 2\}$.

1. Let $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ have order $p^a$, with finitely many outer conjugates. Then $\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$, on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,m}/K)$ for every $m$ prime to $p$ with $r\cdot\mathrm{ord}_m(q) < p - 1$, including $m = q - 1$. Here $\Omega_{K,m}$ is the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $T_m(\mu_p)$, and $T_m$ is the extension of $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ of degree $m$ inside $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, as in [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md).
2. Every element of order $p$ of a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ is the class of an automorphism of order $p$, and every such automorphism is as in part 1.
3. *Tsujimura's question.* In case (a) of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 2, with $p$ dividing $(G_K : N \cap G_K)$, the automorphism of order $p$ in $NG_K$ given by the Corollary of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) is as in part 1.
4. *What a counterexample needs.* If $\theta \ne 1$ is as in part 1, it is the identity on $\Gamma$, and there is $m$ prime to $p$ with $r\cdot\mathrm{ord}_m(q) \ge p - 1$ such that $\theta$ is not the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,m}/K)$.

**Idempotents.** Let $X$ be the set of continuous homomorphisms $\chi : I \to \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$. Each has finite image, of order prime to $p$, and $v \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ acts on $X$ by $\chi^v(t) = \chi(t^v)$. For a finite set $O \subseteq X$ with $O^p = O$, choose $m$ prime to $p$, divisible by $p - 1$ and by the orders of all $\chi \in O$, and put
$$h_{O,(m)} = \frac1m\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\Big(\sum_{\chi \in O}\chi(\tau)^{-i}\Big)\tau^i.$$
The inner sums are permuted by $x \mapsto x^p$, so they lie in $\mathbb{F}_p$, and $h_{O,(m)} \in \mathbb{F}_p[I/I_m]$. As $p \nmid m$, $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p[I/I_m] \cong \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p[x]/(x^m - 1)$ is the product of copies of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ indexed by the characters $\psi$ of $I/I_m$, and $\psi(h_{O,(m)}) = \sum_{\chi \in O}\frac1m\sum_i(\psi\chi^{-1})(\tau)^i = 1$ if $\psi \in O$ and $0$ otherwise. The same evaluation shows that the $h_{O,(m)}$ are compatible as $m$ varies, so they define $h_O \in \mathbb{F}_p[[I]]$, and the following hold, each checked by evaluating characters at a level $m$ where every character involved factors.

- (i) $h_O$ is idempotent and $h_Oh_{O'} = h_{O \cap O'}$.
- (ii) $h_O$ is central in $\Lambda$: conjugation by $\sigma$ acts on $I/I_m$ by $\tau \mapsto \tau^q$, so it maps $h_O$ to $h_{O'}$ with $O' = \{\chi : \chi^q \in O\}$, which is $O$ because $\chi \mapsto \chi^q$ permutes the finite set $O$.
- (iii) $h_O^* = h_{O^\vee}$ with $O^\vee = \{\omega_I\chi^{-1} : \chi \in O\}$, where $\omega_I = \omega|_I \in X$ has order dividing $p - 1$: on $\mathbb{F}_p[I/I_m]$, $(\tau^i)^* = \omega(\tau)^i\tau^{-i}$, so $\psi(x^*) = (\omega_I\psi^{-1})(x)$.
- (iv) If $\eta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has tame twist $u$, then $\beta_\Lambda(h_O) = h_{O'}$ with $O' = \{\chi^{u^{-1}} : \chi \in O\}$: $\beta$ maps $I_m$ onto itself and acts on $I/I_m$ by $\tau \mapsto \tau^u$, so $\psi(\beta_\Lambda(x)) = \psi^u(x)$.
- (v) $h_O \ne 0$ in $\Lambda$ if $O \ne \emptyset$. Indeed, let $n$ be the order of $q$ modulo $m$. Then $\sigma^n\tau\sigma^{-n} = \tau^{q^n} \in \tau I_m$, and $\sigma\tau^m\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{qm}$, so $U = I_m\overline{\langle\sigma^n\rangle}$ is a closed normal subgroup of $\Gamma$ of finite index. Every element of $\Gamma$ is uniquely $\tau^x\sigma^y$ with $x \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ and $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ ([units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), proof of part 3), so $U \cap I = I_m$. So $I/I_m$ embeds in the finite group $\Gamma/U$, and $\mathbb{F}_p[I/I_m]$ embeds in $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U]$.

**Proof of part 1.** Write $\theta_2$, $\bar\theta$ and $\beta$ for the automorphisms induced by $\theta$, as in the Setting.

*Step 1: commuting with many $\bar\psi_\lambda$.* Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G/P_K^{(2)})$; it contains the image of $\mathrm{Inn}(G)$, which is $\mathrm{Inn}(G/P_K^{(2)})$, as a normal subgroup. The classes of the $\eta_2\theta_2\eta_2^{-1}$, $\eta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$, modulo $\mathrm{Inn}(G/P_K^{(2)})$ form a finite set $\mathcal{C}$, the image of the finite set of conjugates of the class of $\theta$. By part 3 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), $\mathcal{P} = \{\bar\psi_\lambda : \lambda \in \Lambda^\times\}$ is a subgroup of $\mathcal{A}$, which acts on $\mathcal{C}$ by conjugation. The stabilizer $\mathcal{P}_0$ of the class of $\theta_2$ has finite index in $\mathcal{P}$. As $\lambda \mapsto \bar\psi_\lambda$ is an anti-homomorphism onto $\mathcal{P}$, $L_0 = \{\lambda : \bar\psi_\lambda \in \mathcal{P}_0\}$ is a subgroup of finite index of $\Lambda^\times$. For $\lambda \in L_0$, $\bar\psi_\lambda\theta_2\bar\psi_\lambda^{-1} = \theta_2 c_x$ for conjugation $c_x$ by some $x \in G/P_K^{(2)}$. Both sides induce $\beta$ on $\Gamma$, as $\bar\psi_\lambda$ induces the identity there, so conjugation by the image of $x$ in $\Gamma$ is the identity, and $x \in \bar V$, as $\Gamma$ has trivial centre (same entry, part 3). Conjugation by $x \in \bar V$ is trivial on the abelian group $\bar V$. Hence, writing $\Theta_\lambda$ for the restriction of $\bar\psi_\lambda$ to $\bar V$,
$$\Theta_\lambda\bar\theta = \bar\theta\,\Theta_\lambda \qquad (\lambda \in L_0).$$

*Step 2: choice of characters.* Suppose $u \notin \overline{\langle p\rangle}$. As $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times = \varprojlim(\mathbb{Z}/m)^\times$ over $m$ prime to $p$, $\overline{\langle p\rangle}$ is the intersection of the inverse images of the subgroups $\langle p \bmod m\rangle$, so there is $m_0$ prime to $p$ with $u \bmod m_0 \notin \langle p \bmod m_0\rangle$. Choose distinct primes $\ell_1, \ell_2, \dots$ not dividing $p(p-1)m_0$, put $M_j = m_0\ell_j$, let $\chi_j \in X$ have order exactly $M_j$, which exists as $I/I_{M_j}$ is cyclic of order $M_j$, and put $O_j = \{\chi_j^{p^i} : i \ge 0\}$ and $h_j = h_{O_j}$. By (iv), $\beta_\Lambda(h_j) = h_{O_j'}$ with $O_j' = \{\chi^{u^{-1}} : \chi \in O_j\}$.

- (a) For $j \ne j'$, $O_j \cap O_{j'} = \emptyset$, as the orders differ. So $h_jh_{j'} = 0$, and $h_j^*h_{j'}^* = (h_{j'}h_j)^* = 0$.
- (b) $O_j' \cap O_j = \emptyset$: otherwise $\chi_j^{u^{-1}p^a} = \chi_j^{p^b}$ for some $a, b$, so $u^{-1}p^a \equiv p^b \pmod{M_j}$, as $\chi_j$ has order $M_j$, and $u \equiv p^{a-b} \pmod{m_0}$, a contradiction.
- (c) For $j \ne j'$, $O_j' \cap O_{j'}^\vee = \emptyset$: the characters in $O_j'$ have order $M_j$, as $u^{-1}p^a$ is a unit modulo $M_j$, which is divisible by $\ell_j$, while those in $O_{j'}^\vee$ have order dividing the least common multiple of $p - 1$ and $M_{j'}$, which is prime to $\ell_j$.

So by (i) and (iii), $\beta_\Lambda(h_j)h_j = 0$ and $\beta_\Lambda(h_j)h_{j'}^* = 0$ for $j \ne j'$.

*Step 3: an eigenspace.* Fix $c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ of order at least $3$, which exists as $p \ge 5$. The elements $\lambda_j = 1 + (c - 1)h_j$ are central units of $\Lambda$, with inverse $1 + (c^{-1} - 1)h_j$. Since $L_0$ has finite index, $\lambda_jL_0 = \lambda_{j'}L_0$ for some $j \ne j'$, and then
$$\nu = \lambda_j^{-1}\lambda_{j'} = 1 + (c^{-1} - 1)h_j + (c - 1)h_{j'} \in L_0,$$
by (a). It is central, and $(\nu^*)^{-1} = 1 + (c - 1)h_j^* + (c^{-1} - 1)h_{j'}^*$, by (a) again. By part 1 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), $\bar V = W \oplus \Lambda\bar x_s \oplus \Lambda\bar x_t$, where $W$ is the sum of the other summands and $\Lambda\bar x_s \cong \Lambda\bar x_t \cong \Lambda$. By its part 3, $\Theta_\nu$ is the identity on $W$, multiplication by $\nu$ on $\Lambda\bar x_s$ and by $(\nu^*)^{-1}$ on $\Lambda\bar x_t$. Decomposing $\Lambda$ with the orthogonal central idempotents $h_j, h_{j'}$, and with $h_j^*, h_{j'}^*$, $\nu$ acts on $\Lambda\bar x_s$ with eigenvalues $c^{-1}, c, 1$ on $h_j\Lambda\bar x_s$, $h_{j'}\Lambda\bar x_s$ and the rest, and $(\nu^*)^{-1}$ acts on $\Lambda\bar x_t$ with eigenvalues $c, c^{-1}, 1$ on $h_j^*\Lambda\bar x_t$, $h_{j'}^*\Lambda\bar x_t$ and the rest. As $c^{-1} \notin \{1, c\}$, the $c^{-1}$-eigenspace of $\Theta_\nu$ is
$$E = h_j\Lambda\bar x_s \oplus h_{j'}^*\Lambda\bar x_t.$$

*Step 4: contradiction.* By Step 1, $\bar\theta$ commutes with $\Theta_\nu$, so $\bar\theta(E) \subseteq E$. Let $v = h_j\bar x_s \in E$, which is nonzero by (v), as $\Lambda\bar x_s$ is free. Then $\bar\theta(v) = \beta_\Lambda(h_j)\bar\theta(\bar x_s)$ lies in $E \cap \beta_\Lambda(h_j)\bar V$, which is $\beta_\Lambda(h_j)E$ because $E$ is a $\Lambda$-submodule and $\beta_\Lambda(h_j)$ is a central idempotent. By Step 2, $\beta_\Lambda(h_j)E = \beta_\Lambda(h_j)h_j\Lambda\bar x_s \oplus \beta_\Lambda(h_j)h_{j'}^*\Lambda\bar x_t = 0$. So $\bar\theta(v) = 0$, contradicting the injectivity of $\bar\theta$. Hence $u \in \overline{\langle p\rangle}$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $\theta$ have order $n$. By part 1, $u = p^\nu$ with $\nu \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, and $u^n = 1$, so $p^{n\nu} = 1$ and $n\nu = 0$ by injectivity of $\nu \mapsto p^\nu$, hence $\nu = 0$ and $u = 1$. So $\beta = \beta_{1,a}$, and by the composition rule $\beta_{u,a}\beta_{u',a'} = \beta_{uu',\,a + ua'}$ ([tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md), Setting), $\beta^n = \beta_{1,na} = 1$, so $na = 0$ in the torsion-free group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ and $a = 0$. Thus $\beta$ is the identity. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** (1) By part 2, $\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$, so its tame twist is $u = 1$. The hypothesis (H1) of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md) holds, so by its Corollary 1, as $u \equiv 1 \pmod{q-1}$, $\theta$ is the identity on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,q-1}/K)$, and by its part 3, as $u \equiv 1$ modulo every $m$, it is the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,m}/K)$ whenever $r\cdot\mathrm{ord}_m(q) < p - 1$.

(2) Elements of a finite normal subgroup have finitely many conjugates. By part 3 (a) of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), an element of order $p$ with finitely many conjugates is the class of an automorphism of order $p$, and every such automorphism has a class with the same conjugates, so part 1 applies.

(3) The automorphism given by the Corollary of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) has order $p$ and its class lies in the finite normal subgroup $NG_K/G_K$ of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$, so part 1 applies.

(4) By part 2, $\theta$ is the identity on $\Gamma$, so the first alternative of Corollary 2 of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md), $u \ne 1$, does not occur, and the second is the statement. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Status of the open cases.* Corollary 2 of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md) left two ways for an automorphism of order $p$ with finitely many outer conjugates to be nontrivial: a tame twist at a prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$, or nontriviality on a level $\Omega_{K,m}$ with $r\cdot\mathrm{ord}_m(q) \ge p - 1$. For $p \ge 5$ the first is now excluded. The second is not addressed here.
- *The method.* The conjugating automorphisms are the $\theta_\nu$ for central units $\nu$ of $\Lambda$ built from idempotents of characters of tame inertia. They lift the scaling by $\nu$ of one surface generator modulo the Frattini subgroup of $P_K$, which needs the lifting theorem of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15), p. 421], as explained in [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). Only their action on $\bar V$ is used.
- *Not covered.* For $p = 3$ the only choice is $c = -1$, and the $c^{-1}$-eigenspace then also contains $h_j^*\Lambda\bar x_t$, which $\beta_\Lambda(h_j)$ need not kill; whether part 1 holds for $p = 3$ is not decided here. For $d \le 2$ the automorphisms $\theta_\lambda$ are not available. The case $p = 2$ is not treated.
