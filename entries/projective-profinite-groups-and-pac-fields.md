**Definitions.** A field is *pseudo-algebraically closed* (PAC) if every nonempty geometrically integral variety over it has a rational point. A profinite group $P$ is *projective* if every finite embedding problem
$$
\varphi:P\longrightarrow A,\qquad \alpha:B\twoheadrightarrow A
$$
has a continuous weak solution $\gamma:P\to B$ with $\alpha\gamma=\varphi$. The groups $A,B$ are finite; $\gamma$ need not be surjective.

**Characterization.** For any profinite group $P$, the following are equivalent:

1. $P$ is projective.
2. $\operatorname{cd}(P)\le1$ [@harbater2009, §2, p. 3].
3. $P$ is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of a free profinite group [@koenigsmann2000relative, Proposition 1.4(1), (4), (5), pp. 8–10, with no local subgroups].
4. $P\cong G_F$ for some PAC field $F$ [@koenigsmann2000relative, Abstract, p. 1].

Here cohomological dimension is for continuous cohomology with all discrete torsion modules, not just trivial coefficients. For item 3, specialize Koenigsmann's relative characterization to an empty family of local subgroups: the local conditions disappear and the free product in (5) is just the free profinite group. The image of the continuous embedding is closed because $P$ is compact. Item 4 is the Ax–Lubotzky–van den Dries characterization, as stated in the cited abstract; no perfectness hypothesis on $F$ is imposed.

**Projective need not mean free profinite, checked here.** Fix a prime $p$. To solve a finite embedding problem for $P=\mathbb Z_p$, replace $A$ by the cyclic $p$-group $C=\varphi(P)$ and $B$ by $E=\alpha^{-1}(C)$. A Sylow $p$-subgroup $S$ of $E$ maps onto $C$: its image has index dividing $[E:S]$, which is prime to $p$, in the $p$-group $C$. Choose $b\in S$ mapping to $\varphi(1)$. Since $b$ has $p$-power order, $1\mapsto b$ extends to a continuous homomorphism $\mathbb Z_p\to B$, solving the problem. Thus $\mathbb Z_p$ is projective and occurs as the absolute Galois group of a PAC field. It is not free profinite: for any prime $r\ne p$ it has no quotient $\mathbb Z/r$, whereas every nontrivial free profinite group has such a quotient by mapping one free generator to $1$ and all others to $0$.

**PAC itself is not determined by the group, checked here.** The procyclic group $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ is projective: in any finite embedding problem choose $b\in B$ lifting $\varphi(1)$; the map $1\mapsto b$ extends continuously from $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ to the finite cyclic group generated by $b$, giving a weak solution. Thus item 4 supplies a PAC field $F$ with $G_F\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Choose a prime $q$ different from $\operatorname{char}(F)$. The union of the cyclic extensions $\mathbb F_{q^n}/\mathbb F_q$, with compatible Frobenius generators, also gives $G_{\mathbb F_q}\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Yet $\mathbb F_q$ is not PAC: the nonempty geometrically integral affine variety
$$
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_q[t,1/(t^q-t)]
$$
has no $\mathbb F_q$-point. Hence isomorphic absolute Galois groups need not preserve either the PAC property or the characteristic. The characterization asserts existence of a PAC realization, not that every field with a projective absolute Galois group is PAC.

**Role and source check.** The [Shafarevich conjecture](shafarevich-conjecture.md) requires freeness beyond projectivity. The characterization above explains the distinction.
