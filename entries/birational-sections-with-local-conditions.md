**Setting.** Let $k$ be a number field and $X$ a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $k$, with smooth projective completion $\bar X$ and function field $K$ [@stix2015birational, §1.3, p. 2]. Write $\mathrm{Sect}(X/k)$ for the set of sections of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$ up to conjugation by $\pi_1(X_{\bar k})$, and $\kappa : X(k) \to \mathrm{Sect}(X/k)$ for the map sending a rational point to its section [@stix2015birational, §2.1, p. 2]. Stix uses the following kinds of sections.

- *Birationally liftable.* The generic point induces $G_K \to \pi_1(X)$, which carries sections of $G_K \to G_k$ to sections of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$. The sections obtained this way are the birationally liftable ones [@stix2015birational, §2.1.1, p. 3]. A section of $G_K \to G_k$ induces a birationally liftable section of $\pi_1(U) \to G_k$ for every open $U \subseteq X$.
- *Cuspidal.* For $a \in \bar X(k) \setminus X(k)$, the decomposition group of a prolongation of the valuation of $a$ maps onto $G_k$, and the sequence splits. Composing the splittings with the map to $\pi_1(X)$ gives the cuspidal sections centred in $a$. They are birationally liftable [@stix2015birational, §2.1.3, p. 3].
- *Selmer.* A section $s$ is Selmer if, for every place $v$, its restriction $s_v$ to $G_{k_v}$ is cuspidal or comes from a point of $X(k_v)$. It then has a unique adelic point $\underline{x}(s) = (x_v)_v$ in $\bar X(\mathbb{A}_k)_\bullet$, the modified adelic points, whose component at an infinite place $v$ lies in $\pi_0(\bar X(k_v))$ [@stix2015birational, §2.2.1, p. 3]. For proper $X$ this is the notion used by [Betts and Stix](finiteness-of-local-images-of-selmer-sections.md).
- *Adelic.* A Selmer section is adelic if $\underline{x}(s)$ lies in $X(\mathbb{A}_k)_\bullet$ [@stix2015birational, §2.2.2, p. 4]. Fix a model of $\bar X$ and $X$ with good reduction over a dense open subset of $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathfrak{o}_k$. A place $v$ there is *integral* for $\underline{x}(s)$ if the closure of $x_v$ avoids the boundary, *degenerate* if $x_v \in X(k_v)$ but its closure meets the boundary in the special fibre, and *cuspidal* if $x_v \in (\bar X \setminus X)(k_v)$. The remaining places, including the infinite ones, are *bad* [@stix2015birational, §4.1, p. 6]. Then $X(\mathbb{A}_k)_\bullet$ consists of the adelic points for which all but finitely many places are integral, no place is cuspidal, and the components at bad places lie in $X(k_v)$ [@stix2015birational, Remark 9 (2), p. 6].
- *Birationally adelic.* A section of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$ is birationally adelic if it lifts to a section of $G_K \to G_k$ whose induced section of $\pi_1(U) \to G_k$, for every open $U \subseteq X$, is adelic for $U$ or cuspidal [@stix2015birational, §2.2.3, p. 4].

By Koenigsmann, if $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$ has a birationally liftable section, then $\bar X(k_v) \neq \emptyset$ for every place $v$, and every birationally liftable section is Selmer [@stix2015birational, Proposition 1, p. 4]. See [birational sections over local fields](birational-sections-over-local-fields.md).

**Theorem (Stix).**

- *B: integrality at density one.* Let $s$ be a birationally liftable section of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$, for any number field $k$. Then either $x_v$ is integral for a set of places $v$ of Dirichlet density $1$, or $s$ is cuspidal [@stix2015birational, Theorem B, p. 1]. This combines a dichotomy, that either the integral places have density $1$ or all but finitely many places are cuspidal or degenerate [@stix2015birational, Corollary 15, p. 13], with the theorem that a birationally liftable section of the second kind is cuspidal [@stix2015birational, Theorem 20, p. 15].
- *A: birationally adelic sections.* If $k$ is totally real or imaginary quadratic, then $\kappa$ and the inclusion of the cuspidal sections give a bijection
  $$\mathrm{Sect}^{\mathrm{cusp}}(X/k) \sqcup X(k) \to \mathrm{Sect}^{\mathrm{ba}}(X/k)$$
  onto the birationally adelic sections [@stix2015birational, Theorem A, p. 1, and Corollary 7, p. 6].
- *C: adelic sections and descent.* Let $k$ be $\mathbb{Q}$ or imaginary quadratic, and suppose that $X$ has a global unit that is not constant. Then the maps
  $$X(k) \to \mathrm{Sect}^{\mathrm{adelic}}(X/k) \to X(\mathbb{A}_k)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}desc}}$$
  are bijective, where the last set consists of the modified adelic points that survive every finite descent obstruction [@stix2015birational, Theorem C, p. 2, and Corollary 6, p. 6]. Stix calls this a strong approximation result for rational points on hyperbolic curves [@stix2015birational, Abstract, p. 1].

Adelic sections are never cuspidal [@stix2015birational, Corollary 5, p. 5]. Indeed, by functoriality every local point of a cuspidal section centred in $a$ is $a$ itself, so every place that is not bad is cuspidal.

*Example, checked here.* The unit $t$ on $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ is not constant, so Theorem C applies. Every adelic section of $\pi_1(X) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ comes from a unique $a \in \mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0, 1\}$, and every modified adelic point of $X$ that survives all finite descent obstructions is rational. Theorem C says nothing about proper curves, which have no nonconstant units. For them, the equality of the finite descent locus with the rational points is Stoll's conjecture; see [Betts–Stix](finiteness-of-local-images-of-selmer-sections.md) for what is known.

**What is left for the birational section conjecture.** Stix writes that Theorem A shows that assuming birational adelicity is almost enough to prove the birational section conjecture, depending on $k$, and that Theorem B shows that this hypothesis is almost true [@stix2015birational, §1.1, p. 1]. The gap is the following, as checked here. Let $k$ be totally real or imaginary quadratic, and let $s$ be a birationally liftable section that is neither cuspidal nor in the image of $\kappa$. By Theorem A, every lift of $s$ has an open $U \subseteq X$ on which the induced section $s_U$ is neither adelic nor cuspidal. Now $s_U$ is a birationally liftable section of $\pi_1(U) \to G_k$, and it is Selmer, so by Theorem B for $U$ the local points are integral with respect to $U$ at a set of places of density $1$. So $\underline{x}(s_U)$ fails to lie in $U(\mathbb{A}_k)_\bullet$ only on a set of places of density $0$: either infinitely many places are not integral, or some place is cuspidal or bad with its component outside $U$. Showing that this cannot happen, for some lift of $s$, would prove, over these fields, that every birationally liftable section of a hyperbolic curve is cuspidal or comes from a rational point. A single cuspidal place already prevents $s_U$ from being adelic, so Theorem A as stated does not give infinitely many non-integral places.

**Proof of Theorem A.** A Selmer section has *finite support* if the Zariski closure of the images of its local points $x_v$ is finite over $k$. Stoll proved that if $Z$ is a proper closed subscheme of a smooth projective curve of positive genus, then the adelic points that survive every finite descent obstruction and lie in $Z$ at a set of places of density $1$ are exactly the points of $Z(k)$. Using this, Stix shows that the non-cuspidal Selmer sections with finite support are exactly the sections of rational points [@stix2015birational, Theorem 4 and Corollary 5, p. 5]. It remains to show that birationally adelic sections have finite support. Shrinking $X$, one finds a nonconstant map $f$ from an open $U \subseteq X$ to a torus $T$ over $k$ whose adelic sections are exactly its rational points. Then the section $f_* s$ of $\pi_1(T) \to G_k$ comes from a point of $T(k)$, and the support of $s$ lies in the preimage of that point under $f$, which is finite [@stix2015birational, proofs of Corollaries 6 and 7, p. 6]. The torus is $\mathbb{G}_m$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and imaginary quadratic fields [@stix2015birational, Proposition 2, p. 4]. Over a totally real field $k$, it is the norm-one torus of $k' = k(\sqrt{a})$ with $a \in k$ totally negative, and $f = (g + \sigma(\alpha))/(g + \alpha)$ for a nonconstant $g \in k(X)$, $\alpha \in k' \setminus k$ and $\sigma$ the generator of $\mathrm{Gal}(k'/k)$ [@stix2015birational, Proposition 3, p. 5, and proof of Corollary 7, p. 6]. Its norm is $\sigma(f) f = 1$.

**Why $\mathbb{G}_m$ only works over $\mathbb{Q}$ and imaginary quadratic fields.** This is checked here from Stix's proof of Proposition 2. Identify $\mathrm{Sect}(\mathbb{G}_m/k)$ with the profinite completion $\widehat{k^*}$ by Kummer theory. The proof uses the hypothesis on $k$ only in its last sentence. Before that, for any number field $k$, it computes the set of adelic sections of $\mathbb{G}_m$ as $k^* \cdot \widehat{\mathfrak{o}_k^*}$ inside $\widehat{k^*}$ [@stix2015birational, proof of Proposition 2, p. 4]. This set equals $k^*$ only if $\mathfrak{o}_k^*$ is finite, that is, by Dirichlet's unit theorem, only for $\mathbb{Q}$ and imaginary quadratic fields.

*Proof.* Suppose $\mathfrak{o}_k^*$ is infinite, and write $\mathfrak{o}_k^* = \mu \times E$ with $\mu$ the roots of unity and $E$ free of rank $r \ge 1$ with basis $\varepsilon_1, \dots, \varepsilon_r$. If $u \in \mathfrak{o}_k^*$ is an $n$-th power $y^n$ in $k^*$, then $y$ has valuation $0$ everywhere, so $y \in \mathfrak{o}_k^*$. Hence $\mathfrak{o}_k^*/\mathfrak{o}_k^{*n} \to k^*/k^{*n}$ is injective for every $n$, and passing to the limit embeds $\widehat{\mathfrak{o}_k^*} \cong \mu \times \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^r$ in $\widehat{k^*}$. The same argument shows that $\bigcap_n k^{*n} \subseteq \bigcap_n \mathfrak{o}_k^{*n} = 1$, so $k^* \to \widehat{k^*}$ is injective. Each finite place $v$ gives maps $k^*/k^{*n} \to \mathbb{Z}/n$, hence a continuous extension $\widehat{k^*} \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ of $v$, which vanishes on $\widehat{\mathfrak{o}_k^*}$. So an element of $k^*$ lying in $\widehat{\mathfrak{o}_k^*}$ has valuation $0$ at every finite place and lies in $\mathfrak{o}_k^*$, which corresponds to $\mu \times \mathbb{Z}^r$. For $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \setminus \mathbb{Z}$, the element $\varepsilon_1^a$ corresponds to $(1, a, 0, \dots, 0)$, so it does not lie in $k^*$, although it lies in $k^* \cdot \widehat{\mathfrak{o}_k^*}$. $\square$

So over a real quadratic field, for instance, $\varepsilon_1^a$ is an adelic section of $\mathbb{G}_m$ that comes from no rational point. This is why the totally real case uses the norm-one torus, for which Stix shows that the norm $\mathfrak{o}_{k'}^* \to \mathfrak{o}_k^*$ is an isomorphism up to torsion [@stix2015birational, proof of Proposition 3, p. 5]. The source gives no version of Theorem A over fields that are neither totally real nor imaginary quadratic, such as cubic fields with a complex place.

**Proof of Theorem B.**

- *Representations from families.* For a family of elliptic curves $E \to X$ and a section $s$, put $\rho_{s,E/X,\ell} = \rho_{E/X,\ell} \circ s : G_k \to \mathrm{GL}(T_\ell(E_{\bar x}))$. Its determinant is the cyclotomic character, and for $s = \kappa(a)$ it is the Galois representation of the fibre $E_a$ [@stix2015birational, §4.2, p. 7]. If $E/X$ has bad semistable reduction at every point of $\bar X \setminus X$ and $s$ is Selmer, the local behaviour at $v$ depends on its type. At an integral place the trace of Frobenius $a_v$ is an integer independent of $\ell$ with $|a_v| \le 2\sqrt{N(v)}$. At degenerate and cuspidal places the representation is upper triangular with diagonal $\delta_v \varepsilon, \delta_v$ for an unramified quadratic character $\delta_v$, and $a_v = \delta_v(\mathrm{Frob}_v)(N(v) + 1)$ [@stix2015birational, §4.3, pp. 7–8].
- *A dichotomy.* Suppose that $E/X$ has bad and not even potentially good reduction at the points of $\bar X \setminus X$, and that $s$ is Selmer. Then $\underline{x}(s)$ is asymptotically integral, and exactly one of the following holds: the integral places have density $1$; or $\rho_{s,E/X,\ell}$ is upper triangular with diagonal $\delta\varepsilon, \delta$ for a quadratic character $\delta$ independent of $\ell$ and ramified at most at the bad places, and all but finitely many places are cuspidal or degenerate [@stix2015birational, Theorem 10, p. 9]. The proof bounds, by the Chebotarev density theorem, the density of cuspidal and degenerate places by the proportion of elements of the mod-$\ell$ image with split characteristic polynomial and an eigenvalue $\pm 1$. Using the classification of subgroups of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_\ell)$ with full determinant, Stix shows that this proportion becomes arbitrarily small as $\ell$ varies unless the mod-$\ell$ images are reducible in a special way [@stix2015birational, proof of Theorem 10 and Lemmas 11–14, pp. 9–13]. In that remaining case $a_v = \pm(N(v) + 1)$ at every place that is not bad, which the Hasse bound excludes at integral places [@stix2015birational, proof of Theorem 10, p. 10].
- *Enough families.* The open sets $\beta^{-1}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0,1,\infty\})$, for finite maps $\beta : \bar X \to \mathbb{P}^1$ that send $\bar X \setminus X$ into $\{0,1,\infty\}$ and are étale over $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, form a basis of the topology of $X$, by Mochizuki's improvement of Belyi's theorem. Stix pulls back the Legendre family $y^2 = x(x-1)(x-\lambda)$, which has bad reduction exactly at $0$, $1$ and $\infty$ [@stix2015birational, proof of Corollary 15, p. 13].
- *Recognizing the cusp.* In the second case, Stix compares maximal unipotent subgroups of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_2)$. The $2$-adic monodromy of the Legendre family is congruent to $1$ modulo $2$, since the $2$-torsion points of the family are sections over the base. So conjugation by it cannot exchange the maximal unipotent subgroups attached to the cusps $0$, $1$, $\infty$ [@stix2015birational, Lemma 19, pp. 14–15]. At a cuspidal or degenerate place $v$, let $y_v$ be the boundary point that the closure of $x_v$ meets in the special fibre. Using several maps $\beta$, this forces $y_v$ to be one point $y$, independent of $v$. Then the residue field of $y$ has a place of degree $1$ over almost every place of $k$, so $y$ is $k$-rational [@stix2015birational, proof of Theorem 20 and Lemma 21, p. 16].

*Check of the monodromy, done here.* Stix takes the topological monodromy $x \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $y \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $z \mapsto \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ 2 & -3 \end{pmatrix}$ on loops around $0$, $1$, $\infty$ with $xyz = 1$ from Stiller [@stix2015birational, Lemma 16, p. 14]. Multiplying out gives $xy = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $xyz = 1$. The matrix $z$ has trace $-2$ and determinant $1$ and is not $-1$, so its Jordan form is $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$, as Stix states [@stix2015birational, Remark 17, p. 14]. The fixed lines of $x$, $y$ and $z^2 = \begin{pmatrix} -3 & 4 \\ -4 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$ are spanned by $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$, which reduce to three distinct points of $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{F}_2)$, as the proof of Lemma 19 uses.

Stix contrasts this description of cuspidal sections through $\mathrm{GL}_2$ with Nakamura's characterization through weights [@stix2015birational, §1.1, p. 1].
