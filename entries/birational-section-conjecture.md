---
title: Birational section conjecture
kind: problem
summary: For a smooth complete curve $X$ over a field $K$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, every section of the restriction map $G_{K(X)} \to G_K$ should have image in a decomposition group at a $K$-rational point of $X$. The analogue holds over $\mathbb{R}$ and finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$.
tags: [function-fields, number-fields, galois-groups, section-conjecture]
---

**Setting.** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$ and $X$ a smooth complete geometrically integral curve over $K$, with function field $K(X)$. Fix a separable closure $\Omega$ of $K(X)$, and let $\bar K$ be the algebraic closure of $K$ in $\Omega$. Restriction to $\bar K$ gives a surjection $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K} : G_{K(X)} \to G_K$ with kernel $G_{\bar K(X)}$ [@koenigsmann2005, §1.1, p. 3].

A point $P \in X(\bar K)$ defines a valuation of $K(X)$, and each prolongation of it to $\Omega$ has a decomposition group $D_P \subseteq G_{K(X)}$. If $P$ is $K$-rational, the inertia subgroup of $D_P$ has complements, and each complement is the image of a section of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$. These sections are called geometric, and they need not all be conjugate [@koenigsmann2005, §1.4, p. 5]. A section $s$ is geometric exactly when $s(G_K) \subseteq D_P$ for some $P \in X(\bar K)$, and then $P$ is $K$-rational [@koenigsmann2005, Observation 1.3, p. 5].

**Conjecture.** If $K$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, then every section of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ is geometric.

This is Koenigsmann's birational section property for every smooth complete curve over $K$ [@koenigsmann2005, Definition 1.4, p. 6], and it is the birational form of Grothendieck's section conjecture [@koenigsmann2005, abstract]. Bresciani states it in the same way for all smooth geometrically connected curves, with no hyperbolicity assumption, unlike the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) for étale fundamental groups [@bresciani2023, §1, p. 1].

**Sections and points.** When $K$ is not separably closed, as here, conjugates of decomposition groups at distinct points of $X$ meet trivially [@koenigsmann2005, p. 6]. So the image of a geometric section lies in decomposition groups at only one point, and the conjecture gives a map from sections onto $X(K)$ whose fibres are the families of sections attached to each point. Koenigsmann describes the expected correspondence as one between $K$-rational points and "certain canonical families of conjugacy classes of sections" [@koenigsmann2005, §1.2, p. 4]. In particular, the conjecture predicts that $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ has a section only if $X(K) \ne \emptyset$.

**Reduction to the projective line.** For a fixed $K$, the conjecture for $\mathbb{P}^1$ implies it for every curve over $K$. Bresciani calls the corresponding reduction well known [@bresciani2023, §1.4, p. 3]. The argument is written out here.

*Proof.* Choose $t \in K(X)$ not in $K$. Then $K(X)/K(t)$ is finite, $G_{K(X)}$ is an open subgroup of $G_{K(t)}$, and $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ is the restriction of $\mathrm{pr}_{\mathbb{P}^1/K}$ to it, since both restrict to $\bar K$. So a section $s$ of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ is also a section of $\mathrm{pr}_{\mathbb{P}^1/K}$. If the conjecture holds for $\mathbb{P}^1$, then $s(G_K)$ lies in the decomposition group $D_w \subseteq G_{K(t)}$ of a prolongation $w$ to $\Omega$ of the valuation of a point $Q \in \mathbb{P}^1(K)$. Hence $s(G_K) \subseteq D_w \cap G_{K(X)}$, which is the decomposition group of $w$ over $K(X)$. The restriction of $w$ to $K(X)$ is a nontrivial valuation that is trivial on $K$, so it is the valuation of a point $P \in X(\bar K)$ above $Q$. By the characterization above, $s$ is geometric. $\square$

So the conjecture for $K$ says that every section of $G_{K(t)} \to G_K$ has image in a decomposition group at $t = a$ for some $a \in K$, or at $t = \infty$.

**A group-theoretic form.** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$ that is neither separably closed nor real closed, and that either has no nontrivial henselian valuation, or has a henselian valuation of rank one and mixed characteristic $(0, p)$ with $p$ dividing the order of $G_K$. Koenigsmann shows that a section $s$ of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ is then geometric exactly when $s(G_K)$ normalizes a closed subgroup $C \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ of $G_{K(X)}$ with $C \cap s(G_K) = 1$, so that the subgroup generated by $C$ and $s(G_K)$ is the semidirect product $C \rtimes s(G_K)$ [@koenigsmann2005, Theorem 4.1, p. 15]. The condition $C \cap s(G_K) = 1$ is part of the criterion: without it, $C = s(G_K)$ would qualify for every section whenever $G_K \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. He states that these hypotheses hold for all sub-$p$-adic fields [@koenigsmann2005, §4.1, p. 14], and so for all fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$. For these fields the conjecture is therefore equivalent to a statement about profinite groups: for every section $s$, some closed subgroup $C \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ of $G_{K(X)}$ with $C \cap s(G_K) = 1$ is normalized by $s(G_K)$. The proof constructs a henselian valuation on the fixed field of $C \rtimes s(G_K)$, using his form of the [Galois characterization of henselianity](galois-characterization-of-henselianity.md) [@koenigsmann2005, proof of Theorem 4.1, p. 15].

The rational points themselves are always visible in the group. For the same fields, $P \mapsto D_P$ is a bijection from $X(K)$ to the conjugacy classes of maximal "hensel subgroups" of $G_{K(X)}$ that map onto $G_K$ [@koenigsmann2005, Corollary 4.3, p. 16]. A profinite group is a hensel group if some Sylow subgroup contains a nontrivial normal abelian subgroup but no procyclic open subgroup [@koenigsmann2005, p. 16]. The conjecture says that the image of every section lies in one of these subgroups.

**Known results.**

- *Abelian quotient.* After abelianizing the geometric kernel, a section exists exactly when $X$ has a divisor of degree $1$, over number fields if the Jacobian has finite Tate–Shafarevich group, and unconditionally over $p$-adic fields. See [abelian birational sections](abelian-birational-sections.md) for Esnault–Wittenberg's theorem and the distinction from rational points.
- *Local fields.* Over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and over $\mathbb{R}$, every section is geometric. Over every field elementarily equivalent to a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and over every real closed field, a section exists only if $X(K) \ne \emptyset$. See [Koenigsmann's theorem on birational sections over local fields](birational-sections-over-local-fields.md). Over the countable fields $\mathbb{Q}_p \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ and $\mathbb{R} \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, by contrast, every curve with a rational point has a section that is not geometric [@koenigsmann2005, Proposition 3.1, p. 13]. So the conjecture cannot be extended to all fields.
- *Local points.* If $K$ is a number field and $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ has a section, then $X$ has a point over every completion of $K$, by the same entry. Using Koenigsmann's results, a section $s$ gives a point $x_v \in X(K_v)$ for every place $v$ [@bresciani2023, §1.1, p. 2].
- *Stix.* Let $K$ be a number field and $U \subseteq X$ a hyperbolic open subset. A section $s$ of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ induces a section $s_U$ of $\pi_1(U) \to G_K$, whose restriction to each completion comes from a point of $U(K_v)$ or is cuspidal [@stix2015birational, Proposition 1, p. 4]. Stix proves two results on these local points [@stix2015birational, Theorems A and B, p. 1]. First, unless $s_U$ is cuspidal, the local points are integral on a model of $U$ at a set of places of Dirichlet density $1$. Second, if $K$ is totally real or imaginary quadratic and, for every open $V \subseteq U$, the induced section of $\pi_1(V) \to G_K$ is cuspidal or has its local points in the adelic points of $V$, then $s_U$ is cuspidal or comes from a point of $U(K)$. See [birational sections with local conditions](birational-sections-with-local-conditions.md) for the precise statements and for the gap between them. Bresciani states both results in terms of the set of places where the local points are not integral, and notes that bridging the two results would prove the conjecture for totally real and imaginary quadratic fields [@bresciani2023, §1.1, p. 2].
- *Hoshi.* Let $K$ be $\mathbb{Q}$ or an imaginary quadratic field. A section of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ is geometric exactly when its local points lie in a closed subscheme of $X$, finite over $K$, at all but finitely many primes [@hoshi2014conditional, Theorem A, p. 4]. So over these fields the conjecture is equivalent to two conditions on the birational sections of $\mathbb{P}^1_K$: their local points avoid three rational points, and for some prime $\ell$ the induced pro-$\ell$ section of $\mathbb{P}^1_K \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ is cuspidal or unramified almost everywhere [@hoshi2014conditional, Corollary 4.6, pp. 31–32]. Hoshi writes that it was not clear to him whether these conditions always hold [@hoshi2014conditional, p. 6]. Over any number field, a section of the maximal geometrically prosolvable quotient of $G_{K(X)}$ over $G_K$ is geometric exactly when its local points lie in such a subscheme outside a set of primes of Dirichlet density $0$ [@hoshi2014conditional, Theorem 4.1, p. 23]. See [Hoshi's criteria over small number fields](birational-sections-over-small-number-fields.md), which also compares them with Stix's results.
- *Global obstructions.* If $K$ is a number field and $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ has a section, then $X$ has an adelic point orthogonal to the Brauer group. As a consequence, the conjecture holds for every $X$ over $K$ with a nonconstant morphism to an abelian variety whose Mordell–Weil and Tate–Shafarevich groups over $K$ are finite [@hararistix2012, Theorems 3.2 and 3.3, pp. 13–15]. See [birational sections and the Brauer–Manin obstruction](birational-sections-and-the-brauer-manin-obstruction.md).
- *Reduction to number fields.* Saïdi and Tyler show that the conjecture for number fields implies it for all fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ [@bresciani2023, §1.1, p. 2].
- *Stronger hypotheses.* For a smooth curve $X$, not necessarily complete, over $K$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, Bresciani proves that a section of the étale fundamental group of $X$ over $K$ is geometric or cuspidal exactly when its base change to $K(t)$, for an indeterminate $t$, lifts to a birational section over $K(t)$ [@bresciani2023, Theorem A, p. 2]. He also shows that the birational section conjecture is equivalent to an interpolation statement for sections over families of open subsets of $\mathbb{P}^1$ [@bresciani2023, Theorem C, p. 3].
- *Local–global principle for covers.* Let $K$ be a number field. Koenigsmann shows that if every section is geometric for every smooth complete curve over $K$, then some curve over $K$ has a cover over $\bar K$ that is defined over every henselization and every real closure of $K$, but not compatibly over $K$ [@koenigsmann2005, Proposition 2.9, p. 12]. He notes that Dèbes proved a local-global principle for Galois covers, and that for "mere covers" the principle was expected to fail, although no counterexample was known [@koenigsmann2005, §2.3.2, p. 12].

**Status.** Open over number fields in general, as far as the sources opened here show, although it is known for the curves in the item on global obstructions above. Bresciani's 2023 paper treats it as open and lists the partial results above [@bresciani2023, §1.1, p. 2]. Later literature has not been checked for this entry.

**Context.** For $K$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, Pop proved that $G_{K(X)}$ determines $X$ up to $K$-birational equivalence [@koenigsmann2005, §1.2, p. 4]. See also [Pop's theorem for finitely generated fields](pop-mochizuki-finitely-generated-fields.md). Koenigsmann notes that in all such results the Galois data encode the $K$-rational points, and that the section conjecture says that "the group theoretic code for $K$-rational points of $X$ is as simple as possible" [@koenigsmann2005, §1.2, p. 4].

**Sources not opened.** The paper of Saïdi and Tyler is reported here through Bresciani's account.
