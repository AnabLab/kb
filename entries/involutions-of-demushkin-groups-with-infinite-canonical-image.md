**Setting.** For a pro-$2$ [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) $B$, put $V_B = B^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathrm{torsion}$ and $K_B = \ker(B \to V_B)$, and let $\chi_B$ be its canonical character. All homomorphisms are continuous. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the class of pro-$2$ Demushkin groups $B$ with $n(B) \ge 3$ and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_B)$ infinite.

Since $q(B)$ is the largest power of $2$ with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_B) \subseteq 1 + q(B)\mathbb{Z}_2$, with $2^\infty = 0$ [@labute1967, p. 106], every $B \in \mathcal{C}$ has $q(B) \ne 0$. As $B^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^{n(B)-1} \times \mathbb{Z}_2/q(B)\mathbb{Z}_2$ [@labute1967, p. 106], $V_B$ is free of rank $n(B) - 1$, and every homomorphism $B \to \mathbb{Z}_2$ factors through $V_B$.

By the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), with $(x, y) = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ and $F$ free pro-$2$ on $x_1, \dots, x_n$, a group $B \in \mathcal{C}$ of rank $n$ is isomorphic to $F/(r)$ with $r$ of one of the following forms [@labute1967, pp. 106–107 and Theorem 1].

- (I) $r = x_1^m(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n)$ with $n$ even and either $m = q(B) = 2^s$ with $2 \le s < \infty$, or $q(B) = 2$ and $m = 2 + 2^f$ with $2 \le f < \infty$. Then $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_B)$ is $1 + 2^s\mathbb{Z}_2$, respectively the closed subgroup generated by $-1 + 2^f$.
- (II) $q(B) = 2$, $n$ odd, $r = x_1^2x_2^{2^f}(x_2, x_3)(x_4, x_5)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n)$ with $2 \le f < \infty$, and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_B) = \{\pm 1\} \times (1 + 2^f\mathbb{Z}_2)$.
- (III) $q(B) = 2$, $n$ even, $r = x_1^2(x_1, x_2)x_3^{2^f}(x_3, x_4)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n)$ with $2 \le f < \infty$, and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_B) = \{\pm 1\} \times (1 + 2^f\mathbb{Z}_2)$.

Labute's list also allows $f = \infty$ in (II) and in (I) with $q(B) = 2$, where $m = 2 + 2^f$ becomes $2$. Those groups have $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\}$, which is finite, so they are not in $\mathcal{C}$; nor are the groups with $q = 0$. In (I)–(III), $n \ge 4$ when $n$ is even, since $n \ge 3$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $D \in \mathcal{C}$. If $\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ satisfies $\sigma^2 = 1$ and acts trivially on $V_D$, then $\sigma = 1$. Consequently the kernels of $\mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V_D)$ and $\mathrm{Out}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V_D)$ contain no element $\ne 1$ of finite $2$-power order, and every finite $2$-subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ or of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ acts faithfully on $V_D$.

For $q(D)$ divisible by $4$ this is [the theorem for $q$ divisible by four](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-q-divisible-by-four.md). The new case is $q(D) = 2$ with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_D)$ infinite.

**Corollary ($2$-adic fields).** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite. Then $G_K(2)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $[K:\mathbb{Q}_2] + 2 \ge 3$ ([maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md)), and its canonical character is the cyclotomic character, with image $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})/K')$, where $K' = K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$ [@labute1967, §5, p. 131]. This image is open in $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$, so it is infinite, and $G_K(2) \in \mathcal{C}$. So the theorem applies to $G_K(2)$ for every finite $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$. When $\sqrt{-1} \notin K$, for instance for $K = \mathbb{Q}_2$, where $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}(2)$ has the single relation $x_1^2x_2^4(x_2, x_3)$ on three generators [@labute1967, Theorem 8, p. 132], it was not covered by the earlier entries.

**Proof.**

*Step 1: the class is stable.* Let $B \in \mathcal{C}$ and let $U \subseteq B$ be open. Then $U$ is a Demushkin group with $n(U) - 2 = (B : U)(n(B) - 2) \ge 1$ ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md), Duality). Its canonical character is the restriction of $\chi_B$, because the dualizing module of $U$ is that of $B$ regarded as a $U$-module ([torsion-free extensions](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), proof of part 2). As $\chi_B(U)$ has index at most $(B : U)$ in $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_B)$, it is infinite. So $U \in \mathcal{C}$, and in particular $q(U) \ne 0$.

Now let $E$ be a pro-$2$ group without elements of order $2$ that contains some $U \in \mathcal{C}$ as an open normal subgroup. By parts 1 and 2 of [the torsion-free extension theorem](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), applied to $U \subseteq E$ with $H = E/U$, $E$ is a Demushkin group with $\delta(E) \le \delta(U) = 0$, and
$$\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_2}\mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Q}_2)^{E/U} = 1 + \frac{n(U) - 2}{(E : U)}. \tag{1}$$
Here $E$ acts on $\mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Q}_2) = \mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Z}_2) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_2$ through conjugation, and $U$ acts trivially.

*Step 2: propagation.* Let $B \in \mathcal{C}$, let $\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(B)$ satisfy $\tau^2 = 1$ and act trivially on $V_B$, and let $U \subseteq B$ be open with $K_B \subseteq U$. We show that $\tau(U) = U$ and that $\tau$ acts trivially on $V_U$.

As $K_B \supseteq (B, B)$, $U$ is normal in $B$. For $x \in B$, $\tau(x)x^{-1} \in K_B \subseteq U$, so $\tau(U) \subseteq U$, with equality as $\tau^2 = 1$, and $\tau$ acts trivially on $B/U$. Let $E = B \rtimes \langle t \rangle$ with $t^2 = 1$ and $txt^{-1} = \tau(x)$. It is a pro-$2$ group containing $U$ as a normal subgroup, and $G = E/U = (B/U) \times \langle \bar t \rangle$ with $\bar t$ of order $2$. Put $a = (B : U)$, so $|G| = 2a$, and $M = \mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Q}_2)$, on which $G$ acts. Let $\psi$ be the character of $G$ on $M$, so $\psi(1) = n(U) - 1$.

(i) *Involutions of $E$ map to $\bar t$.* $B$ is torsion-free ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 1), so an element of order $2$ of $E$ has the form $xt$ with $x \in B$, and $1 = (xt)^2 = x\tau(x)$. Since $\tau$ acts trivially on $V_B$, the class $[x]$ satisfies $2[x] = 0$, so $[x] = 0$ and $x \in K_B \subseteq U$.

(ii) *Cyclic subgroups avoiding $\bar t$.* Let $C \subseteq G$ be cyclic with $\bar t \notin C$, and let $E_C$ be its preimage in $E$. By (i), $E_C$ has no element of order $2$. A nontrivial element of finite order in a pro-$2$ group has a power of order $2$, so $E_C$ has no torsion, and (1) gives $\dim M^C = 1 + (n(U) - 2)/|C|$.

(iii) *$M^G$.* Restriction $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Q}_2) \to M$ is injective, because a homomorphism vanishing on $U$ factors through the finite group $B/U$. Its image lies in $M^{B/U}$. By (1) for $U \subseteq B$ and the index formula, $\dim M^{B/U} = 1 + (n(B) - 2) = n(B) - 1 = \dim \mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Q}_2)$, so restriction is onto $M^{B/U}$. It is compatible with the actions of $t$, and $\tau$ acts trivially on $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Q}_2) = \mathrm{Hom}(V_B, \mathbb{Q}_2)$. So $\bar t$ fixes $M^{B/U}$, and $M^G = M^{B/U}$ has dimension $n(B) - 1$.

(iv) *Odd powers.* If $g \in G$ and $k$ is odd, then $\psi(g^k) = \psi(g)$. Let $g$ have order $2^b$. The polynomial $\Phi_{2^b}(X + 1) = (X + 1)^{2^{b-1}} + 1$ is Eisenstein at $2$ for $b \ge 1$, so $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2(\zeta_{2^b})/\mathbb{Q}_2)$ contains an automorphism $\gamma$ with $\gamma(\zeta) = \zeta^k$ for every $2^b$-th root of unity $\zeta$. The eigenvalues of $g$ are such roots of unity, so $\psi(g^k) = \gamma(\psi(g)) = \psi(g)$, as $\psi(g) \in \mathbb{Q}_2$.

(v) *$\psi(g) = 1$ for $g \notin \{1, \bar t\}$.* Induct on the order $2^b \ge 2$ of $g$, and put $C = \langle g \rangle$. First, $\bar t \notin C$: write $g = (\beta, \bar t^i)$; if $g^k = \bar t$, then $ik$ is odd, so $k$ is odd, and $\beta^k = 1$ forces $\beta = 1$ since $\beta$ has $2$-power order, so $g = \bar t$. By (ii), $|C| \dim M^C = 2^b + n(U) - 2$. The $2^{b-1}$ generators of $C$ have character value $\psi(g)$ by (iv). The other elements form the subgroup of index $2$, which does not contain $\bar t$; apart from $1$ they have smaller order, so value $1$ by induction. Hence
$$2^b + n(U) - 2 = \sum_{c \in C}\psi(c) = (n(U) - 1) + (2^{b-1} - 1) + 2^{b-1}\psi(g),$$
and $\psi(g) = 1$.

(vi) *Conclusion.* By (iii) and (v),
$$2a(n(B) - 1) = \sum_{g \in G}\psi(g) = (n(U) - 1) + \psi(\bar t) + (2a - 2).$$
With $n(U) - 1 = a(n(B) - 2) + 1$ this gives $\psi(\bar t) = n(U) - 1 = \dim M$. An involution of a $\mathbb{Q}_2$-vector space is diagonalizable with eigenvalues $\pm 1$, so $\bar t$ acts trivially on $M$. Thus $\tau$ acts trivially on $\mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Z}_2)$, which is the full $\mathbb{Z}_2$-dual of the free module $V_U$, and so trivially on $V_U$.

*Step 3: an index-two cover that kills every mod-$2$ character.* Let $A = \mathbb{Z}_2[c]/(c^2 - 1)$, free over $\mathbb{Z}_2$ on $1, c$, and $\Gamma = A \rtimes \langle c \rangle$, where $\langle c \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ acts on $A$ by multiplication. Write elements as $(a, \varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon \in \{1, c\}$, so $(a, \varepsilon)(b, \eta) = (a + \varepsilon b, \varepsilon\eta)$. Direct computation gives
$$(a, \varepsilon)^{-1} = (-\varepsilon a, \varepsilon), \qquad (a, c)^2 = ((1 + c)a, 1), \qquad (a', c)(a, 1)(a', c)^{-1} = (ca, 1), \tag{2}$$
$$\big((a, \varepsilon), (b, \eta)\big) = \big(\varepsilon(\eta - 1)a + \eta(1 - \varepsilon)b,\ 1\big). \tag{3}$$
Let $\pi : \Gamma \to \langle c \rangle$ be the projection and $\lambda : A \to \mathbb{Z}_2$ the coefficient of $1$. For $1 \le k \le n$ let $\theta_k : F \to \mathbb{F}_2$ send $x_k$ to $1$ and the other $x_i$ to $0$.

*Claim.* Let $B \in \mathcal{C}$. Then $B$ has an open normal subgroup $B_1$ of index $2$ with $K_B \subseteq B_1$, such that every homomorphism $f : B \to \mathbb{F}_2$ vanishes on $K_{B_1}$.

*Proof.* Present $B = F/(r)$ as in (I)–(III). Since $n(B) \ge 3$, the generators used below exist: $x_3$ and $x_4$ in (I), and $x_3$ in (II). Define $\Psi : F \to \Gamma$ on generators as follows, sending every generator not listed to $(0, 1)$.

| Form | $j$ | $\Psi(x_1)$ | $\Psi(x_j)$ | Other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (I) | $3$ | $(c - 1, 1)$ | $(0, c)$ | $\Psi(x_4) = (m, 1)$ |
| (II) | $2$ | $(c - 1, 1)$ | $(0, c)$ | $\Psi(x_3) = (2, 1)$ |
| (III) | $2$ | $(c - 1, 1)$ | $(0, c)$ | none |

Each factor of $r$ maps into the abelian subgroup $A \times \{1\}$, so $\Psi(r)$ is the sum of their images. In (I), $x_1^m \mapsto m(c - 1)$, $(x_1, x_2) \mapsto 0$ and $(x_3, x_4) \mapsto m(1 - c)$ by (3), and the other commutators map to $0$. In (II), $x_1^2 \mapsto 2(c - 1)$, $x_2^{2^f} \mapsto 0$ since $(0, c)^2 = (0, 1)$, and $(x_2, x_3) \mapsto 2(1 - c)$ by (3), and the other commutators map to $0$. In (III), $x_1^2 \mapsto 2(c - 1)$ and $(x_1, x_2) \mapsto (c - 1)^2 = 2 - 2c$ by (3) and $c^2 = 1$, and the remaining factors map to $0$. In each case $\Psi(r) = (0, 1)$, so $\Psi$ induces a homomorphism $B \to \Gamma$, still written $\Psi$.

Now $\pi \circ \Psi = \theta_j$, identifying $\langle c \rangle$ with $\mathbb{F}_2$. Let $B_1 = \ker\theta_j$. Write $e_i$ for the class of $x_i$ in $F^{\mathrm{ab}} = \mathbb{Z}_2^n$, so that $B^{\mathrm{ab}} = \mathbb{Z}_2^n/\mathbb{Z}_2\bar r$ with $\bar r$ the image of $r$. In (I), $\bar r = me_1$, so the torsion subgroup $T$ of $B^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is generated by the class of $e_1$. In (II) and (III), $\bar r = 2(e_1 + 2^{f-1}e_k)$ with $k = 2$, respectively $k = 3$. As $e_1 + 2^{f-1}e_k$ is part of a basis of $\mathbb{Z}_2^n$, $T$ has order $2$, generated by its class. In each case $\theta_j$ vanishes on the generator of $T$, in (II) and (III) because $f \ge 2$, so $K_B$, the preimage of $T$, lies in $B_1$. On the other hand $\theta_1$ takes the value $1$ on the generator of $T$. A homomorphism $B \to \mathbb{Z}_2$ kills $T$, so $\theta_1$ is not the reduction of one.

$B_1$ is topologically generated by $x_k$ and $x_jx_kx_j^{-1}$ for $k \ne j$, together with $x_j^2$. Indeed, let $S$ be the closed subgroup they generate. Conjugation by $x_j$ maps each generator into $S$, so $x_jSx_j^{-1} \subseteq S$, and then $x_j^{-1}Sx_j = x_j^{-2}(x_jSx_j^{-1})x_j^2 \subseteq S$ as $x_j^2 \in S$. The other $x_k$ lie in $S$, so $S$ is normal in $B$. Then $B/S$ is generated by the image of $x_j$, whose square is trivial, so $(B : S) \le 2$. As $S \subseteq B_1$ and $(B : B_1) = 2$, $S = B_1$.

$\Psi$ maps $B_1$ into $A \times \{1\} \cong A$, so $h = \lambda \circ \Psi|_{B_1} : B_1 \to \mathbb{Z}_2$ is a homomorphism. By (2), and since $\Psi(x_j) = (0, c)$, it takes the values $\lambda(a_k)$ on $x_k$, the coefficient of $c$ in $a_k$ on $x_jx_kx_j^{-1}$, and $0$ on $x_j^2$, where $\Psi(x_k) = (a_k, 1)$. With $a_1 = c - 1$, this is $-1$ on $x_1$ and $1$ on $x_jx_1x_j^{-1}$. The only other nonzero $a_k$ is $m$ in (I) or $2$ in (II), which gives the even values $m, 0$ or $2, 0$. So $h$ reduces modulo $2$ to the homomorphism that is $1$ on $x_1$ and $x_jx_1x_j^{-1}$ and $0$ on the other generators, which is $\theta_1|_{B_1}$, as $j \ne 1$.

Every homomorphism $B_1 \to \mathbb{Z}_2$ factors through $V_{B_1}$, so $h$ vanishes on $K_{B_1}$, and hence so does $\theta_1$. The same holds for the reduction of every homomorphism $B \to \mathbb{Z}_2$, restricted to $B_1$. These reductions form a subspace of dimension $n - 1$ in the $n$-dimensional space $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{F}_2)$: $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Z}_2)$ is free of rank $n - 1$, and a homomorphism to $\mathbb{Z}_2$ with zero reduction is twice a homomorphism. As $\theta_1$ is not in this subspace, together they span $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{F}_2)$. $\square$

For (I) with $m = q(B)$ this is the construction in [the theorem for $q$ divisible by four](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-q-divisible-by-four.md), Step 3.

*Step 4: cofinality and conclusion.* Call an open subgroup $U$ of $D$ *reachable* if there is a chain $D = U_0 \supseteq U_1 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq U_k = U$ in which each $U_{i+1}$ is open in $U_i$ and contains $K_{U_i}$. All terms lie in $\mathcal{C}$ by Step 1.

*Reachable subgroups are cofinal.* We show, by induction on $(B : W)$, that for $B \in \mathcal{C}$ and $W$ open and normal in $B$ there is such a chain starting at $B$ and ending inside $W$. If $W = B$, there is nothing to do. Otherwise take $B_1$ from Step 3. If $W \subseteq B_1$, apply induction to $W \subseteq B_1$, which has smaller index, and put $B$ in front of the chain. If not, then $B_1W = B$. Choose a nonzero homomorphism $f : B \to \mathbb{F}_2$ vanishing on $W$, which exists as $B/W$ is a nontrivial finite $2$-group, and put $B_2 = B_1 \cap \ker f$. Since $f(B_1) = f(B_1W) = f(B) \ne 0$, $B_2$ has index $2$ in $B_1$. It contains $K_{B_1}$ by Step 3. Also $W \cap B_1 \subseteq B_2$ is normal in $B_2$, and $(B_2 : W \cap B_1) = 2(B : W)/4 < (B : W)$, as $(W : W \cap B_1) = 2$. Apply induction to $W \cap B_1 \subseteq B_2$, and put $B \supseteq B_1$ in front. For an arbitrary open subgroup of $D$, apply this to its normal core in $D$.

*Conclusion.* Fix a reachable chain. By induction on $i$ we show that $\sigma(U_i) = U_i$, that $\sigma$ acts trivially on $V_{U_i}$, and that $d_x = x\sigma(x)^{-1} \in K_{U_i}$ for every $x \in D$. For $i = 0$ this is the hypothesis. Given it for $i$, Step 2, applied to $B = U_i$, $\tau = \sigma|_{U_i}$ and $U = U_{i+1}$, gives the first two assertions for $i + 1$. Next, $d_x \in K_{U_i} \subseteq U_{i+1}$ and $d_x\sigma(d_x) = x\sigma(x)^{-1}\sigma(x)x^{-1} = 1$. In $V_{U_{i+1}}$, on which $\sigma$ acts trivially, this says $2[d_x] = 0$, so $[d_x] = 0$ and $d_x \in K_{U_{i+1}}$. Hence $d_x$ lies in every reachable subgroup, so in every open normal subgroup of $D$ by cofinality. So $d_x = 1$ for all $x$, and $\sigma = 1$.

*The consequences.* An element $\ne 1$ of finite $2$-power order in the kernel for $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ has a power of order $2$ in the kernel, which is impossible. Let $\theta \in \mathrm{Out}(D)$ have order $2$ and act trivially on $V_D$, where $D$ is centre-free ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 4) and identified with $\mathrm{Inn}(D)$. By Corollary 1 of [the torsion-free extension theorem](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), with $p = 2$, $\theta$ is the class of some $\sigma$ with $\sigma^2 = 1$. Inner automorphisms act trivially on $V_D$, so $\sigma$ does too, so $\sigma = 1$ and $\theta = 1$, a contradiction. Taking powers again excludes all elements $\ne 1$ of finite $2$-power order in the kernel for $\mathrm{Out}(D)$. Finally, the kernel of the action of a finite $2$-subgroup on $V_D$ is a finite $2$-group inside one of these kernels, so it is trivial. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Tsujimura's question at $p = 2$.* In case (a) of [the reduction](demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three-are-elastic.md), Corollary 2, which holds for every $p$, $ND/D$ is a nontrivial finite normal $2$-subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$. For $D \in \mathcal{C}$ the theorem shows that it acts faithfully on $V_D$. So case (a) would be excluded for $D \in \mathcal{C}$ if every finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ acted trivially on $V_D$. This is proved for odd $p$ in [the finite-conjugacy theorem](outer-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-with-finitely-many-conjugates.md), and not here for $p = 2$. For $p = 2$ it is proved for every $D \in \mathcal{C}$ by [the finite-conjugacy theorem including $p = 2$](outer-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-with-finitely-many-conjugates-including-p-2.md), part 3, for normal form (I), and by [the theorem for nonprocyclic dyadic character](finite-normal-subgroups-of-demushkin-outer-automorphism-groups-with-nonprocyclic-dyadic-character.md) for (II) and (III); Corollary 3 of the former combines these with the theorem here to exclude case (a) for every $D \in \mathcal{C}$. See [the problem](demushkin-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md).
- *Why the image must be infinite.* Steps 1 and 2 need $\delta = 0$ for every torsion-free finite extension of every open subgroup. If $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_D)$ is finite, then it is $1$ or $\{\pm 1\}$, and $\ker\chi_D$ is an open subgroup with trivial canonical character, that is, with $q = 0$. The theorem says nothing about these groups. For rank $2$ the statement fails, by the example at the end of [the theorem for $q$ divisible by four](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-q-divisible-by-four.md).
- *Odd-order torsion.* The theorem says nothing about elements of odd order in the two kernels.
- *Sources.* The statement and proof are not taken from a source, and no literature search was made for the case $q = 2$. The method is that of [the theorem for $q$ divisible by four](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-q-divisible-by-four.md) and of [the odd-prime theorem](order-p-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-act-nontrivially-modulo-torsion.md).
