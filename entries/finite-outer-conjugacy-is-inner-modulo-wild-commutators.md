**Setting.** Use the Setting of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P$ is its wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$, and
$$H=P^{\mathrm{ab}}=P/P_2,\qquad P_2=[P,P],\qquad V=H/pH=P/P^p[P,P].$$
Also $G=F/(\varrho)$, where $F=P_F\rtimes\mathcal G$ is free among semidirect products of $\mathcal G\cong\Gamma$ with pro-$p$ groups, $\mathcal G$ is generated by $\sigma,\tau$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^q$, and
$$\varrho=R_0\,x_1^{p^s}\,C,$$
where $R_0$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by $x_0$ and $\mathcal G$, and $C$ is a product of commutators of elements of $P_F$ (for odd $d$ this uses $y_1\in P_F$). We write $\sigma,\tau$ also for their images in $\Gamma$. Let $I\subseteq\Gamma$ be tame inertia, the closed subgroup generated by $\tau$; it is isomorphic to $\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$ ([tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), Setting). Let $\omega:\Gamma\to\mathbb F_p^\times$ give the action on $\mu_p\subseteq K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, extended to $\Lambda$, and let $\epsilon:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ be the augmentation. Then $\omega=1$ exactly when $\mu_p\subseteq K$.

The *surface indices* are the indices of the surface pairs: $2,\dots,d$ for odd $d$ and $3,\dots,d$ for even $d$. The other indices, $0,1$ and, for even $d$, $2$, are *complementary*. An automorphism is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$, and $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$. A tame-trivial $\theta$ acts $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$-linearly on $H$, by $\theta_H$.

*Coinvariants.* For a compact $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$-module $M$, let $I_\Gamma M$ be the closure of the subgroup generated by the $(\rho-1)m$ with $\rho\in\Gamma$, $m\in M$, and $M_\Gamma=M/I_\Gamma M$. Let $\bar e_i\in H_\Gamma$ be the class of $x_i$. A tame-trivial $\theta$ induces a $\mathbb Z_p$-linear map $\theta_*$ of $H_\Gamma$, since $\theta_H(I_\Gamma H)\subseteq I_\Gamma H$.

*The cocycle.* Let $\alpha$ be tame-trivial and trivial on $H$. For $\gamma\in\Gamma$ choose $g\in G$ over $\gamma$ and put
$$c_\alpha(\gamma)=\alpha(g)g^{-1}P_2\in H.$$
This lies in $H$ since $\alpha(g)g^{-1}\in P$, and does not depend on $g$: for $x\in P$, $\alpha(gx)(gx)^{-1}=\alpha(g)g^{-1}\cdot g\,\alpha(x)x^{-1}g^{-1}$, and $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in P_2$. The same identity for $x\in G$ gives $c_\alpha(\gamma\delta)=c_\alpha(\gamma)+\gamma c_\alpha(\delta)$, so $c_\alpha$ is a continuous $1$-cocycle. Put
$$u(\alpha)=c_\alpha(\sigma)+I_\Gamma H\in H_\Gamma.$$
By definition, $c_\alpha=0$ exactly when $\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/P_2$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ be tame-trivial with finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. By [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md) it is trivial on $H$, so $u(\alpha)$ is defined.

- (a) $u(\alpha)$ lies in the $\mathbb Z_p$-span of the $\bar e_i$ with complementary $i$.
- (b) If $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, then $u(\alpha)=0$, and there is $g\in P$ such that $c_g\circ\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/[P,P]$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Suppose $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha_0$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_0$ inducing the identity on $G/[P,P]$. In particular $\alpha$ induces the identity on $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and every outer class with finitely many conjugates lies in the kernel of the map $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/[P,P])$.

**Lemma 1 (proved here).** *Structure of $H_\Gamma$.*

- (a) There is $\theta_0\in\mathbb Z_p$ such that $\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}\to H_\Gamma$, $(c_i)\mapsto\sum_ic_i\bar e_i$, is onto with kernel $\mathbb Z_p r$, where $r=\theta_0e_0+p^se_1$.
- (b) $H_\Gamma/pH_\Gamma=V_\Gamma$, and $\theta_0\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$ if and only if $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. In that case $\bar e_1,\dots,\bar e_d$ is a basis of $V_\Gamma$ and $\bar e_0=0$ there. For $\lambda\in\Lambda$, the class of $\lambda\bar x_i$ in $V_\Gamma$ is $\epsilon(\lambda)\bar e_i$.
- (c) For $2\le k\le d$ there is a homomorphism $\Phi_k:H_\Gamma\to\mathbb Z_p$ with $\Phi_k(\bar e_i)=\delta_{ik}$ for all $i$. If $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, there is also $\Phi_1$ with $\Phi_1(\bar e_1)=1$, $\Phi_1(\bar e_0)=-p^s\theta_0^{-1}$ and $\Phi_1(\bar e_k)=0$ for $k\ge2$, and $(\Phi_1,\dots,\Phi_d):H_\Gamma\to\mathbb Z_p^d$ is an isomorphism. Then $v=\sum_{k=1}^d\Phi_k(v)\bar e_k$ for all $v\in H_\Gamma$.

*Proof.* (a) Let $Q$ be the quotient of $P_F$ by the closed subgroup generated by $[P_F,P_F]$ and the $\rho x\rho^{-1}x^{-1}$ with $\rho\in\mathcal G$, $x\in P_F$. It is abelian, and conjugation by $F$ acts trivially on it. $P_F$ is the closed normal subgroup of $F$ generated by the $x_i$, so $Q$ is topologically generated by their classes $[x_i]$. By the universal property of $F$ there is a homomorphism $\Theta:F\to\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}\times\mathcal G$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$ and maps $x_i$ to $(e_i,1)$. It maps $P_F$ into $\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}$, a closed normal subgroup containing the images of the $x_i$. There, $\mathcal G$ acts trivially, so $\Theta$ induces $\bar\Theta:Q\to\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}$ with $[x_i]\mapsto e_i$. The $\mathbb Z_p$-linear map $e_i\mapsto[x_i]$ is inverse to it, as both composites fix topological generators. So $\bar\Theta$ is an isomorphism.

$\Theta$ maps the closed subgroup generated by $x_0$ and $\mathcal G$ into $\mathbb Z_pe_0\times\mathcal G$, and $R_0=\varrho\,(x_1^{p^s}C)^{-1}\in P_F$, so $\Theta(R_0)=\theta_0e_0$ for some $\theta_0\in\mathbb Z_p$. Also $\Theta(C)=0$ and $\Theta(x_1^{p^s})=p^se_1$, so $\Theta(\varrho)=r$. Let $N$ be the closed normal subgroup of $F$ generated by $\varrho$, so that $P=P_F/N$. The closed commutator subgroup $[P,P]$ is the image of $[P_F,P_F]$, so $H_\Gamma$ is the quotient of $Q$ by the image of $N$. Since $N$ is topologically generated by the $f\varrho f^{-1}$, which all have class $[\varrho]$ in $Q$, that image is $\mathbb Z_p[\varrho]$, and $\bar\Theta$ maps it onto $\mathbb Z_pr$. This proves (a).

(b) $H_\Gamma/pH_\Gamma=H/(pH+I_\Gamma H)=V/I_\Gamma V=V_\Gamma$. By part 1 of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), $V=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0\oplus\bigoplus_{i\ge1}\Lambda\bar x_i$, with $\rho\bar x_0=\omega(\rho)\bar x_0$ and $\Lambda\bar x_i\cong\Lambda$. Each $\rho\bar x_i-\bar x_i$ lies in $I_\Gamma V$, so by continuity the class of $\lambda\bar x_i$ is $\epsilon(\lambda)\bar e_i$. Coinvariants commute with direct sums. $\Lambda_\Gamma=\Lambda/\ker\epsilon\cong\mathbb F_p$, since $\ker\epsilon$ is the closure of the span of the $(\rho-1)\Lambda$, as in the proof of Lemma (b) of that entry. $(\mathbb F_p\bar x_0)_\Gamma$ is $0$ if some $\omega(\rho)\ne1$, and is $\mathbb F_p$ if $\omega=1$. So $\dim V_\Gamma$ is $d$ if $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $d+1$ otherwise. By (a), $V_\Gamma\cong\mathbb F_p^{d+1}/\mathbb F_p\bar\theta_0e_0$, of dimension $d$ exactly when $\theta_0\notin p\mathbb Z_p$. When $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, the $d$ elements $\bar e_1,\dots,\bar e_d$ span $V_\Gamma$, as $\bar e_0=0$ there, so they form a basis.

(c) The $k$-th coordinate kills $r$ for $k\ge2$. If $\theta_0$ is a unit, the map $\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}\to\mathbb Z_p^d$ with $e_0\mapsto-p^s\theta_0^{-1}f_1$ and $e_k\mapsto f_k$ for $k\ge1$ kills $r$ and is onto. If $\sum_ic_ie_i$ is in its kernel, then $c_k=0$ for $k\ge2$ and $c_1=p^s\theta_0^{-1}c_0$, so $\sum_ic_ie_i=c_0\theta_0^{-1}r$. So its kernel is $\mathbb Z_pr$, and by (a) it induces the isomorphism $(\Phi_1,\dots,\Phi_d)$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (proved here).** *Cocycles with trivial class in $H_\Gamma$.* Let $c:\Gamma\to H$ be a continuous $1$-cocycle with $c(\sigma)\in I_\Gamma H$. Then there is $h\in H$ with $c(\gamma)=(\gamma-1)h$ for all $\gamma\in\Gamma$.

*Proof.* (i) *Finite levels.* For an open subgroup $U$ of $H$, its stabilizer in $\Gamma$ is open, so the intersection of its finitely many translates $\rho U$ is an open $\Gamma$-stable subgroup inside $U$. So the open $\Gamma$-stable subgroups $U$ form a basis at $0$, closed under finite intersections, and $\bigcap U=0$. Fix such a $U$; then $H/U$ is a finite $p$-group. Let $I_U$ be the kernel of the action of $I$ on $H/U$. It is open in $I$, and on it $c$ modulo $U$ is a homomorphism into a finite $p$-group. Every finite quotient of $I_U$ has order prime to $p$, so $c(I_U)\subseteq U$. Every open subgroup of $I\cong\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$ is the closure of $\langle\tau^m\rangle$ for some $m$ prime to $p$. So $\tau^m$ acts trivially on $H/U$ and $c(\tau^m)\in U$ for some $m=m_U$ prime to $p$.

(ii) *The value at $\tau$.* Let $S=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}c(\tau^i)$. Summing $c(\tau^{i+1})=c(\tau)+\tau c(\tau^i)$ over $0\le i<m$ gives $S+c(\tau^m)=mc(\tau)+\tau S$, so $h_U=-m^{-1}S$ satisfies $c(\tau)-(\tau-1)h_U\in U$. The sets $\{h\in H:c(\tau)-(\tau-1)h\in U\}$ are closed and nonempty, and they shrink with $U$. By compactness they have a common point $h_1$, and then $c(\tau)=(\tau-1)h_1$.

(iii) *Normalizing.* For $h\in H$, $\delta h(\gamma)=(\gamma-1)h$ is a cocycle, with $\delta h(\sigma)\in I_\Gamma H$. Put $c'=c-\delta h_1$. Then $c'(\tau)=0$ and $c'(\sigma)\in I_\Gamma H$. The set where $c'$ vanishes is a closed subgroup, since $c'(\gamma\delta)=c'(\gamma)+\gamma c'(\delta)$ and $c'(\gamma^{-1})=-\gamma^{-1}c'(\gamma)$, so $c'$ vanishes on $I$. Then
$$0=c'(\tau^q)=c'(\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1})=c'(\sigma)+\sigma\big(c'(\tau)+\tau c'(\sigma^{-1})\big)=(1-\tau^q)c'(\sigma).$$
As $q$ is a power of $p$, $\tau^q$ topologically generates $I$, so $c'(\sigma)\in H^I$.

(iv) *The value at $\sigma$.* The set of $\rho\in\Gamma$ with $(\rho-1)H\subseteq J=(\sigma-1)H+(\tau-1)H$ is a closed subgroup containing $\sigma$ and $\tau$, because $J$ is compact and $(\rho\rho'-1)x=(\rho-1)\rho'x+(\rho'-1)x$ and $(\rho^{-1}-1)x=-(\rho-1)\rho^{-1}x$. So $I_\Gamma H=J$, and $c'(\sigma)=(\sigma-1)a+(\tau-1)b$ for some $a,b\in H$. For $U$ and $m$ as in (i), let $e_m=m^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\tau^i$, acting on $H/U$. Then $e_m(\tau-1)=(\tau-1)e_m=m^{-1}(\tau^m-1)=0$ there. Also $e_m$ fixes the class of $c'(\sigma)\in H^I$. And $\sigma e_m=e_m\sigma$ on $H/U$: as operators $\sigma\tau^i=\tau^{qi}\sigma$, $\tau^m$ acts trivially, and $i\mapsto qi$ permutes $\mathbb Z/m$. Applying $e_m$ to the classes, any lift $h$ of $e_m(a+U)$ satisfies $c'(\sigma)-(\sigma-1)h\in U$ and $(\tau-1)h\in U$. By compactness, as in (ii), some $h_2$ satisfies $c'(\sigma)=(\sigma-1)h_2$ and $(\tau-1)h_2=0$.

(v) The cocycles $c'$ and $\delta h_2$ agree at $\sigma$ and at $\tau$. The set where two continuous cocycles agree is a closed subgroup, and $\sigma,\tau$ generate $\Gamma$. So $c'=\delta h_2$, and $c=\delta(h_1+h_2)$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (proved here).** *Transport.* Let $\alpha$ be tame-trivial and trivial on $H$.

- (a) For $g\in P$ with class $\bar g\in H$, $c_g\alpha$ is tame-trivial and trivial on $H$, and $c_{c_g\alpha}(\gamma)=c_\alpha(\gamma)+(1-\gamma)\bar g$. So $u(c_g\alpha)=u(\alpha)$.
- (b) For tame-trivial $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}$ is tame-trivial and trivial on $H$, and $c_{\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}}=\theta_H\circ c_\alpha$. So $u(\theta\alpha\theta^{-1})=\theta_*u(\alpha)$.
- (c) If $\alpha$ has finitely many outer conjugates and $t\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is tame-trivial, then $t_*^n\,u(\alpha)=u(\alpha)$ for some $n\ge1$.

*Proof.* (a) For $x\in G$ over $\gamma$, $c_g\alpha(x)x^{-1}=g\cdot\alpha(x)x^{-1}\cdot xg^{-1}x^{-1}$, whose class in $H$ is $\bar g+c_\alpha(\gamma)-\gamma\bar g$. (b) As $\theta$ is tame-trivial, $\theta^{-1}(x)$ lies over $\gamma$. Write $\alpha(\theta^{-1}(x))=y\,\theta^{-1}(x)$ with $y\in P$ of class $c_\alpha(\gamma)$. Then $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}(x)x^{-1}=\theta(y)$, of class $\theta_H(c_\alpha(\gamma))$. (c) By Lemma (b) of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), $t^n\alpha t^{-n}=c_g\alpha$ for some $n\ge1$ and $g\in P$. Apply (b) with $\theta=t^n$, and (a). $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** Write $u=u(\alpha)$.

*Conjugates.* Let $\theta$ be tame-trivial. Then $\alpha'=\theta^{-1}\alpha\theta$ is tame-trivial and trivial on $H$. Its outer class is conjugate to that of $\alpha$, so it has the same finite set of outer conjugates. By Lemma 3 (b), $u(\alpha')=\theta_*^{-1}u$. So whatever is proved below for every $\alpha$ satisfying the hypotheses holds for $\alpha'$ as well.

*1. Surface coordinates.* Let $(a,b)$ be a surface pair. The transvection $t$ of step 3 of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md) fixes $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $a\mapsto ab$. It is a tame-trivial automorphism of $G$ with $t_H\xi_a=\xi_a+\xi_b$ and $t_H\xi_i=\xi_i$ for $i\ne a$, where $\xi_i\in H$ is the class of $x_i$. So $t_*v=v+\Phi_a(v)\bar e_b$ for $v\in H_\Gamma$: both sides are $\mathbb Z_p$-linear and agree on the $\bar e_i$, which generate $H_\Gamma$ by Lemma 1 (a). As $\Phi_a(\bar e_b)=0$, $t_*^nv=v+n\Phi_a(v)\bar e_b$. By Lemma 3 (c), $n\Phi_a(u)\bar e_b=0$ for some $n\ge1$. Applying $\Phi_b$ gives $n\Phi_a(u)=0$, so $\Phi_a(u)=0$. The transvection $b\mapsto ba$ of the same step gives $\Phi_b(u)=0$ in the same way. Write $u=\sum_ic_i\bar e_i$ by Lemma 1 (a). Then $c_k=\Phi_k(u)=0$ for every surface index $k$, which proves (a).

From now on let $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, so that $\theta_0\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$ and $\Phi_1$ exists, by Lemma 1.

*2. The index $2$ for even $d$.* The transvection $T_1$ of step 5 there fixes $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$, and is a tame-trivial automorphism with $(T_1)_H\xi_2=\xi_2+\xi_1$. As in step 1, $(T_1)_*^nv=v+n\Phi_2(v)\bar e_1$, and Lemma 3 (c) gives $n\Phi_2(u)\bar e_1=0$ for some $n\ge1$. Applying $\Phi_1$ gives $\Phi_2(u)=0$.

By steps 1 and 2 and Lemma 1 (c), $u=\Phi_1(u)\,\bar e_1$, for every $\alpha$ satisfying the hypotheses.

*3. The index $1$.* As $\omega\ne1$, choose $\rho_0\in\Gamma$ with $\omega(\rho_0)\ne1$, and put $\lambda=\rho_0-\omega(\rho_0)\in\Lambda$. Then $\omega(\lambda)=0$ and $\epsilon(\lambda)=1-\omega(\rho_0)\ne0$.

- *Even $d$.* Part 2 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), with this $\lambda$, gives an automorphism $\theta$ with $\theta(\rho)\in\rho P^p[P,P]$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$, so $\theta$ is tame-trivial. Its action $B$ on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, with $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda\bar x_3$, $B\bar x_4=\bar x_4-\lambda^*\bar x_2$ and $B\bar x_i=\bar x_i$ otherwise. As $B\bar x_3=\bar x_3$, $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\lambda\bar x_3$. Modulo $p$, $\theta_H^{-1}$ is $B^{-1}$, so on $H_\Gamma/pH_\Gamma=V_\Gamma$ the map $\theta_*^{-1}$ induces the map induced by $B^{-1}$. By Lemma 1 (b), this sends $\bar e_1$ to $\bar e_1-\epsilon(\lambda)\bar e_3$ in the basis $\bar e_1,\dots,\bar e_d$, and $\Phi_3$ reduces to the $\bar e_3$-coordinate. So $\Phi_3(\theta_*^{-1}\bar e_1)\equiv-\epsilon(\lambda)\not\equiv0\pmod p$ is a unit. Now $3$ is a surface index, so step 1 applied to $\alpha'=\theta^{-1}\alpha\theta$ gives
$$0=\Phi_3(u(\alpha'))=\Phi_3\big(\theta_*^{-1}(\Phi_1(u)\bar e_1)\big)=\Phi_1(u)\,\Phi_3(\theta_*^{-1}\bar e_1).$$
Hence $\Phi_1(u)=0$.
- *Odd $d$.* Part 3 of the mixing theorem, with $\nu=\lambda$, gives a tame-trivial $\theta$ whose action $B$ on $V$ has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda\bar x_2$, $B\bar x_3=\bar x_3-\lambda^*\delta\lambda\,\bar x_2-\lambda^*\delta_0\,\bar x_1$ and $B\bar x_i=\bar x_i$ otherwise. As $B\bar x_2=\bar x_2$, $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\lambda\bar x_2$. As in the even case, $\Phi_2(\theta_*^{-1}\bar e_1)\equiv-\epsilon(\lambda)$ is a unit, and $2$ is a surface index, so step 1 for $\theta^{-1}\alpha\theta$ gives $\Phi_1(u)=0$.

*4. Conclusion.* So $u=0$, that is, $c_\alpha(\sigma)\in I_\Gamma H$. By Lemma 2, $c_\alpha(\gamma)=(\gamma-1)h$ for some $h\in H$ and all $\gamma$. Choose $g\in P$ with class $h$. By Lemma 3 (a), $c_{c_g\alpha}(\gamma)=(\gamma-1)h+(1-\gamma)h=0$, so $c_g\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in[P,P]$ for every $x\in G$. This proves (b). $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** By [tame innerness](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md), there is $g_0\in G$ such that $c_{g_0}\alpha$ is tame-trivial. It represents the outer class of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates. By the theorem, $\alpha_0=c_gc_{g_0}\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/[P,P]$ for some $g\in P$, and $\alpha=c_{(gg_0)^{-1}}\alpha_0$. As $[P,P]\subseteq[G,G]$, $\alpha_0$ induces the identity on $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and so does every inner automorphism. Since $P$ is characteristic ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), so is $[P,P]$, and $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/[P,P])$ is defined; the class of $\alpha$ maps to the class of $\alpha_0$, which is trivial. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* The cocycle class is the single element $u(\alpha)$ of $H_\Gamma$, which is free of rank $d$ when $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, with coordinates $\Phi_1,\dots,\Phi_d$. An exact automorphism acting unipotently on $H_\Gamma$ has to fix $u(\alpha)$, by finite outer conjugacy, and the exact transvections kill every coordinate except $\Phi_1$. A tame-trivial automorphism fixing $x_1$ modulo $P_2$ fixes $\bar e_1$, so it gives no condition on $\Phi_1(u)\bar e_1$. The mixing automorphisms move $x_1$, but are known only modulo the Frattini subgroup of $P$. Their actions on $V_\Gamma$ have order $p$, so finite conjugacy under them gives nothing modulo $p$. Instead, step 3 uses them to conjugate $\alpha$ and applies step 1 to the conjugate. Since $u(\alpha)=\Phi_1(u)\bar e_1$ and $\Phi_3\circ\theta_*^{-1}$ is $\mathbb Z_p$-linear, only the reduction of $\theta_*^{-1}$ modulo $p$ is needed.
- *When $\mu_p\subseteq K$.* Then $\omega=1$, so $\ker\omega=\ker\epsilon$, and the mixing automorphisms induce the identity on $V_\Gamma$, so step 3 gives nothing. Also $\theta_0\in p\mathbb Z_p$, so $H_\Gamma$ may have torsion. If $\theta_0=0$, then $\bar e_1$ is torsion, and step 2 gives only $p^s\mid n\Phi_2(u)$. Part (a) still holds, but whether $u(\alpha)=0$ is not decided here.
- *What remains.* Let $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, and let $\alpha_0$ be a representative trivial on $G/P_2$, as in (b). Its degree-one Johnson map $J_1(\alpha_0):H\to P_2/P_3$, $xP_2\mapsto\alpha_0(x)x^{-1}P_3$, is then $\Gamma$-equivariant. Indeed, for $\rho\in G$ write $\alpha_0(\rho)=c\rho$ with $c\in P_2$, and let $z=\rho x\rho^{-1}$ and $y=\rho\,\alpha_0(x)x^{-1}\rho^{-1}$. Then $\alpha_0(z)z^{-1}=c\,yz\,c^{-1}z^{-1}\equiv y$ modulo $P_3$, as $[P_2,P]\subseteq P_3$. Whether $J_1(\alpha_0)$ is $\operatorname{ad}(h)$ for some $h\in H$, and whether $\alpha_0$ is inner, are the next layers of item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535).
- *Related results.* By part 1 of [finite-order detection modulo wild commutators](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-modulo-wild-commutators.md), an automorphism of finite order that is inner on $G/[P,P]$ is trivial. With the corollary, for $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, an automorphism of finite order with finitely many outer conjugates is trivial. This is consistent with the corollary of [tame innerness](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md), which shows for every $K$ that a nonidentity outer class of finite order has infinitely many conjugates. Likewise, for $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, the normal lattices of [the normal-lattice theorem](finite-conjugacy-outer-classes-generate-normal-p-adic-lattices.md) lie in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/[P,P])$.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, $d\ge3$, every $s$, including $p=3$, and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ for part (b). Part (a) holds for every such $K$. The case $p=2$ and $d\le2$ are excluded by the linked inputs. Everything here is proved from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
