**Setting.** Let $R$ be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field $K$ and residue field $k$, and let $p \ge 1$ be the characteristic exponent of $k$. Let $C$ be a smooth proper geometrically connected curve over $K$ of genus $g \ge 2$, with Jacobian $J$. For a prime $\ell \ne p$, let $\rho_\ell : G_K \to \mathrm{GL}(T_\ell J)$ be the $\ell$-adic representation, where $G_K = \mathrm{Gal}(K^s/K)$ for a separable closure $K^s$.

**Definitions (Deligne–Mumford).** A *stable curve* of genus $g$ over a scheme $S$ is a proper flat morphism whose geometric fibres are reduced, connected and $1$-dimensional, have only ordinary double points, have $\dim H^1(\mathcal{O}) = g$, and in which every nonsingular rational component meets the other components in more than $2$ points [@delignemumford1969, Definition 1.1, p. 76]. The curve $C$ has *stable reduction* if there is a stable curve over $R$ with generic fibre $C$ [@delignemumford1969, Definition 2.2, p. 88]. Such a stable model is unique up to canonical isomorphism [@delignemumford1969, Lemma 1.12 with its proof and the remark after it, p. 84]. An abelian variety $A/K$ has *semi-abelian reduction* if the identity component of the special fibre of its Néron model is an extension of an abelian variety by a torus [@hallenicaise2012, (3.6.2), p. 9].

**Theorem.**

1. *Stable reduction theorem.* There is a finite extension $L/K$ such that $C_L$ has a stable model over the integral closure of $R$ in $L$ [@delignemumford1969, Corollary 2.7, p. 92]. Deligne and Mumford state this for every discrete valuation ring, although the section containing it assumes an algebraically closed residue field [@delignemumford1969, §2, p. 87]. The Stacks project proves the semistable form for every discrete valuation ring, with $L/K$ separable of degree at most $B_g = (2g-2)(\ell_2^{2g})!$, where $\ell_1 < \ell_2$ are the first two primes above $768g$ [@stacks2026, Tags 0CDN and 0CEJ]. There, semistable reduction means that some proper model is at-worst-nodal, or equivalently that every minimal model is [@stacks2026, Tags 0CDG and 0CDH].
2. *Jacobian criterion (Deligne–Mumford).* Assume that $k$ is algebraically closed. Then $C$ has stable reduction if and only if the special fibre of the Néron model of $J$ has no unipotent radical [@delignemumford1969, Definition 2.1, p. 88, and Theorem 2.4, p. 89]. The same section shows that $C$ has stable reduction exactly when its minimal regular model $\mathcal{C}$ has reduced special fibre with only ordinary double points [@delignemumford1969, Definition 2.2 and Proposition 2.3, pp. 88–89]. For the easy direction of the criterion, Raynaud's theorem then identifies the identity component of the Néron model with $\mathrm{Pic}^0(\mathcal{C}/R)$, so its special fibre is the generalized Jacobian of $\mathcal{C}_k$, an extension of an abelian variety by a torus [@delignemumford1969, Theorem 2.5 and proof of Theorem 2.4, p. 89].
3. *Grothendieck's criterion, as stated by Halle and Nicaise.* Assume that $R$ is complete and $k$ is separably closed, so that $G_K$ is the inertia group [@hallenicaise2012, (2.2.1)–(2.2.2), p. xii], and let $A/K$ be an abelian variety. Then:
   - (a) $A$ has semi-abelian reduction if and only if $G_K$ acts unipotently on $T_\ell A$;
   - (b) there is a unique minimal finite extension $L$ of $K$ in $K^s$ such that $A_L$ has semi-abelian reduction, and $L/K$ is Galois;
   - (c) semi-abelian reduction is preserved by every finite separable extension of $K$ [@hallenicaise2012, Theorem 3.6.5, p. 9].

   In their proof, $L$ is the fixed field of the set of $\sigma \in G_K$ that act unipotently on $T_\ell A$, which they show is an open normal subgroup [@hallenicaise2012, proof of Theorem 3.6.5, p. 9]. They state the theorem for semi-abelian varieties and deduce it from Grothendieck's semistable reduction theorem in SGA 7, Exposé IX, which was not opened here.

**Consequence.** This is proved here. Assume that $R$ is complete and $k$ is algebraically closed.

- (a) $C$ has stable reduction if and only if $\rho_\ell(\sigma)$ is unipotent for every $\sigma \in G_K$. This holds for one prime $\ell \ne p$ exactly when it holds for every such prime.
- (b) There is a finite Galois extension $L_C/K$ in $K^s$, independent of $\ell$, such that for every finite separable extension $K'/K$ in $K^s$, the curve $C_{K'}$ has stable reduction if and only if $L_C \subseteq K'$.

*Proof.* (a) Since $k$ is algebraically closed, the identity component of the special fibre of the Néron model of $J$ is an extension of an abelian variety by $T \times U$, with $T$ a torus and $U$ unipotent, by Chevalley's theorem [@hallenicaise2012, (3.2.2), p. 7]. Both "no unipotent radical" in part 2 and "semi-abelian reduction" in part 3 say that $U = 0$. So parts 2 and 3(a), applied to $A = J$, give the equivalence. Whether $C$ has stable reduction does not involve $\ell$.

(b) Let $K'/K$ be finite separable inside $K^s$. Then $K'$ is complete, and its residue field is a finite extension of $k$, hence equal to $k$. So (a) applies over $K'$: $C_{K'}$ has stable reduction if and only if every element of $\rho_\ell(G_{K'})$ is unipotent. Let $I' \subseteq G_K$ be the set of $\sigma$ with $\rho_\ell(\sigma)$ unipotent, an open normal subgroup by the proof of part 3, and let $L_C$ be its fixed field. Then $C_{K'}$ has stable reduction if and only if $G_{K'} \subseteq I'$, that is, $L_C \subseteq K'$. This property determines $L_C$ as the smallest such $K'$, so $L_C$ does not depend on $\ell$. $\square$

By part 3(b) and the description of $L$ in its proof, $L_C$ is the minimal extension over which $J$ acquires semi-abelian reduction.

**Example.** This is proved here. Assume that $R$ is complete, $k$ is algebraically closed and $p \notin \{2, 5\}$, so that $\mathrm{char}\, K \notin \{2, 5\}$. Let $\pi$ be a uniformizer, and let $C$ be the smooth projective curve over $K$ with affine equation $y^2 = x^5 - \pi$. Put $K' = K(\varpi)$ with $\varpi^{10} = \pi$. Then $C_{K''}$ has stable reduction, for a finite separable extension $K''/K$ in $K^s$, if and only if $K' \subseteq K''$. So $L_C = K'$, of degree $10$ over $K$, and $C$ has no stable model over $K(\sqrt{\pi})$ or $K(\pi^{1/5})$. Over $K'$ the curve even has good reduction.

*Proof.*

1. *The field.* As $x^{10} - 1$ is separable over $k$ and $k$ is algebraically closed, Hensel's lemma puts all $10$th roots of unity in $K$. The polynomial $x^{10} - \pi$ is Eisenstein and separable, and its roots are the $\zeta\varpi$ with $\zeta^{10} = 1$. So $K'/K$ is Galois of degree $10$, and $\sigma \mapsto \zeta_\sigma = \sigma(\varpi)/\varpi$ is an isomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(K'/K) \to \mu_{10}(K)$.
2. *A curve with good reduction.* Let $C_0$ be the smooth projective curve $Y^2 = X^5 - 1$ over $K$. Glue $\mathrm{Spec}\, R[X,Y]/(Y^2 - X^5 + 1)$ and $\mathrm{Spec}\, R[U,V]/(V^2 - U + U^6)$ along $X = 1/U$, $Y = V/U^3$; on the overlap, $V^2 = U^6(X^5 - 1) = U - U^6$. Each chart is free of rank $2$ over the corresponding chart of $\mathbb{P}^1_R$, so the glued scheme $\mathcal{C}_0$ is finite and flat over $\mathbb{P}^1_R$, hence proper and flat over $R$. Its fibres are smooth, by the Jacobian criterion, as $2$ and $5$ are invertible in $k$ and in $K$. On the first chart, $2Y = 5X^4 = 0$ forces $X = Y = 0$, which is not on the curve. On the second, a singular point would have $V = 0$, $6U^5 = 1$ and $U = U^6$; then $U \ne 0$, so $U^5 = 1$ and $6 = 1$, which is impossible as $p \ne 5$. In each geometric fibre both charts are irreducible, since $X^5 - 1$ and $U - U^6$ have simple roots and so are not squares, and they meet; so the fibre is irreducible. It is a double cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ branched at the five roots of $X^5 = 1$ and at $U = 0$. As $p \ne 2$, Riemann–Hurwitz gives $2g - 2 = 2 \cdot (-2) + 6$, so $g = 2$. Hence $\mathcal{C}_0$ is a smooth proper model whose geometric fibres are smooth connected curves of genus $2$, which is a stable curve. By (a), every element of $\rho_{C_0,\ell}(G_K)$ is unipotent.
3. *Twisting.* The map $\varphi(x, y) = (x\varpi^{-2}, y\varpi^{-5})$ is an isomorphism $C_{K'} \to (C_0)_{K'}$, because $\varpi^{10} = \pi$. In particular $C$ has genus $2$, and $C_{K'}$ has good reduction. For $\zeta \in \mu_{10}(K)$, the map $\beta_\zeta(X, Y) = (\zeta^2 X, \zeta^5 Y)$ is an automorphism of $C_0$ over $K$. For $\sigma \in G_K$ and $P = (x, y) \in C(K^s)$,
   $$\varphi(\sigma P) = (\sigma(x)\varpi^{-2}, \sigma(y)\varpi^{-5}) = \beta_{\zeta_\sigma}\bigl(\sigma(\varphi(P))\bigr),$$
   since $\sigma(\varpi) = \zeta_\sigma\varpi$. Passing to divisor classes, $\varphi_*\rho_{C,\ell}(\sigma)\varphi_*^{-1} = (\beta_{\zeta_\sigma})_*\,\rho_{C_0,\ell}(\sigma)$ on $T_\ell J_{C_0}$.
4. *Jordan decomposition.* Here $(\beta_{\zeta_\sigma})_*$ has finite order, so it is semisimple on $T_\ell J_{C_0} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$, and it commutes with the unipotent $\rho_{C_0,\ell}(\sigma)$ because $\beta_{\zeta_\sigma}$ is defined over $K$. By uniqueness of the Jordan decomposition, $\rho_{C,\ell}(\sigma)$ is unipotent if and only if $(\beta_{\zeta_\sigma})_* = 1$. For $\zeta \ne 1$, $\beta_\zeta$ is not the identity, since $\zeta^2 = \zeta^5 = 1$ forces $\zeta = 1$. A nontrivial automorphism of a smooth proper curve of genus $g \ge 2$ over an algebraically closed field acts nontrivially on $T_\ell$ of its Jacobian: otherwise the Lefschetz–Weil formula would count $2 - 2g < 0$ fixed points, which is the argument in [@delignemumford1969, proof of Lemma 1.14, p. 85]. So $\rho_{C,\ell}(\sigma)$ is unipotent exactly when $\zeta_\sigma = 1$, that is, when $\sigma \in G_{K'}$. Thus $I' = G_{K'}$ in the proof of (b), and $L_C = K'$. $\square$

So the semisimple part of $\rho_{C,\ell}(\sigma)$ is $\varphi_*^{-1}(\beta_{\zeta_\sigma})_*\varphi_*$, a homomorphism of $G_K$ with kernel $G_{K'}$: stable reduction fails over $K$ because of this finite twist, and over $K'$ the curve reaches good reduction, not merely stable reduction.

**Anabelian form.** For a hyperbolic curve $X$ over a finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with compactification $Y$ and geometric fundamental group $\Delta_X$, Mochizuki uses the "well-known stable reduction criterion": $X$ has stable reduction exactly when the inertia subgroup of $G_K$ acts unipotently on $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, where $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and trivially on the set of conjugacy classes of inertia groups of cusps in $\Delta_X$. He notes that this condition is group-theoretic, and concludes that an isomorphism of the arithmetic fundamental groups of two such curves preserves stable reduction [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 2.1 and its proof, pp. 22–23]; see [reconstruction of logarithmic special fibres](reconstruction-of-log-special-fibres-of-p-adic-curves.md). Mochizuki gives no reference. The consequence above is the proper case over a complete field with algebraically closed residue field, with $T_\ell J$ in place of the pro-$\ell$ part of $\Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}}$. For a complete nonsingular curve over a separably closed field and a rational point $P$, the Abel–Jacobi map based at $P$ identifies the abelianized fundamental group of the curve with $\pi_1$ of its Jacobian [@milne1986jacobian, Proposition 9.1, p. 30]. Three standard steps are not checked here: that the pro-$\ell$ part of $\pi_1(J_{\bar K})$ is $T_\ell J$, Galois-equivariantly; that $G_{\widehat{K^{\mathrm{ur}}}}$ is the inertia group of $K$; and the descent of stable models from the completion of the maximal unramified extension to $\mathcal{O}_K$. The cusp condition is not treated here either.

Over a complete field with algebraically closed residue field, stable reduction of a proper curve of genus at least $2$ is thus detected by abelian data. For good reduction, abelian data do not suffice; see the [Oda–Tamagawa criterion](oda-good-reduction-criterion.md).
