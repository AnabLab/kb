**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $H=P^{\mathrm{ab}}=P/P_2$, $\xi_i\in H$ is the class of $x_i$, and $\bar e_i\in H_\Gamma=H/I_\Gamma H$ is the class of $x_i$. For a tame-trivial $\alpha$ that is trivial on $H$, $c_\alpha(\gamma)=\alpha(g)g^{-1}P_2$ for $g$ over $\gamma$, and $u(\alpha)=c_\alpha(\sigma)+I_\Gamma H\in H_\Gamma$. By Lemma 1 (a) there, $(c_i)\mapsto\sum_ic_i\bar e_i$ maps $\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}$ onto $H_\Gamma$ with kernel $\mathbb Z_pr$, where $r=\theta_0e_0+p^se_1$ and $\theta_0$ is defined in its proof by $\Theta(R_0)=\theta_0e_0$. For $2\le k\le d$, $\Phi_k:H_\Gamma\to\mathbb Z_p$ is the homomorphism of Lemma 1 (c) there, with $\Phi_k(\bar e_i)=\delta_{ik}$ for all $i$. Let $T_H$ be the torsion subgroup of $H_\Gamma$.

Assume $\mu_p\subseteq K$, and use the notation of [wild Frobenius shifts](wild-frobenius-shifts-when-k-contains-mu-p.md). So $p^m$ and $p^s$ are the numbers of $p$-power roots of unity in $K$ and in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, with $1\le m\le s$. The elements $h,\kappa\in\mathbb Z_p$ of the presentation satisfy $\tau(\zeta)=\zeta^h$ and $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^\kappa$ for the $p$-power roots of unity $\zeta\in K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, and congruences (i) $h\equiv1\pmod{p^s}$ and (ii) $\kappa\equiv1\pmod{p^m}$ hold. Also $k=\mathbb Z/p^m$, and $f,\chi_0,\dots,\chi_d\in H^1(G,k)$ are the characters of Step 3 there, dual to $\sigma,x_0,\dots,x_d$: $\chi_i(\sigma)=0$ and $\chi_i(x_j)=\delta_{ij}$. Finally $A_m$ and $\nu:A_m\to k$ are as in part 1 there. Since $[\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p):\mathbb Q_p]=p-1$ divides $d$, the degree $d$ is even, so $d\ge4$, the surface indices are $3,\dots,d$, and
$$\varrho=R_0\,x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d],\qquad R_0=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa.$$
Put $h'=(h+h^2+\cdots+h^{p-1})/(p-1)\in\mathbb Z_p$. Let $\Lambda_s$, $\omega_s$ and $\lambda\mapsto\lambda^*$ be as in [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), and let $\mathrm{aug}_s:\Lambda_s\to\mathbb Z/p^s$ be the augmentation, the continuous ring homomorphism with $\rho\mapsto1$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ be tame-trivial with finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. By [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md) it is trivial on $H$, so $u=u(\alpha)$ is defined. As in the proof of the corollary of [wild Frobenius shifts](wild-frobenius-shifts-when-k-contains-mu-p.md), $\alpha\in A_m$, so $\nu(\alpha)\in k$ is defined.

- (a) Write $u=\sum_{i=0}^dc_i\bar e_i$ with $c_i\in\mathbb Z_p$. Then $c_i=0$ for $3\le i\le d$, $c_1\equiv c_2\equiv0\pmod{p^m}$, and $c_0\equiv\nu(\alpha)\pmod{p^m}$. If $\theta_0\ne0$, then also $c_2=0$.
- (b) Suppose that $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ is unramified. Then $\theta_0=p^ma_0$ with $a_0\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$, and $T_H$ is cyclic of order $p^m$, generated by $\bar t=a_0\bar e_0+p^{s-m}\bar e_1$. Moreover $u=c\,\bar t$ for some $c\in\mathbb Z_p$ with
$$c\,a_0\equiv\nu(\alpha)\pmod{p^m},\qquad p^{s-m}c\in p^m\mathbb Z_p.$$
In particular $u\in T_H$, $p^{\min(m,s-m)}u=0$, and $u=0$ if and only if $\nu(\alpha)=0$.
- (c) Suppose that $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ is ramified and $\theta_0\ne0$. Then $\bar e_0$ is not torsion, and $u=c\,\bar e_0$ for a unique $c\in\mathbb Z_p$, with $c\equiv\nu(\alpha)\pmod{p^m}$.

The $c_i$ in (a) are determined by $u$ only modulo $\mathbb Z_p(\theta_0,p^s,0,\dots,0)$. The conditions in (a) do not depend on the choice, since $\theta_0\equiv p^s\equiv0\pmod{p^m}$ by Lemma A (1).

**Corollary (proved here).** Suppose that $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ is unramified. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$, and let $\nu(\alpha)$ be as in the corollary of [wild Frobenius shifts](wild-frobenius-shifts-when-k-contains-mu-p.md).

1. The automorphism $\bar\alpha$ of $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ induced by $\alpha$ satisfies $\bar\alpha(z)-z\in(G^{\mathrm{ab}})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ for every $z\in G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, written additively.
2. The following are equivalent: (i) $\nu(\alpha)=0$; (ii) $\alpha$ induces the identity on $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$; (iii) $\alpha=c_g\circ\alpha_0$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_0$ inducing the identity on $G/[P,P]$.

So in this case the conclusion of the corollary of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), proved there for $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, holds for $\alpha$ exactly when $\nu(\alpha)=0$.

**Lemma A (proved here).**

1. $\theta_0=\kappa h'-1$, and $\theta_0\in p^m\mathbb Z_p$. Put $a_0=\theta_0/p^m$ and $r_0=a_0e_0+p^{s-m}e_1$, so that $r=p^mr_0$, and let $\bar t\in H_\Gamma$ be the class of $r_0$.
2. $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ is unramified if and only if $s>m$. In that case $v_p(\kappa-1)=m$ and $a_0\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$.
3. $T_H$ is cyclic of order $p^m$, generated by $\bar t$. If $s>m$, then $H_\Gamma=T_H\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^d\mathbb Z_p\bar e_i$, with $\bar e_1,\dots,\bar e_d$ free. If $s=m$, then $H_\Gamma=\mathbb Z_p\bar e_0\oplus T_H\oplus\bigoplus_{i=2}^d\mathbb Z_p\bar e_i$, with $\bar e_0,\bar e_2,\dots,\bar e_d$ free.
4. If $\theta_0\ne0$, then $a\bar e_1\ne0$ for every nonzero $a\in\mathbb Z_p$.

*Proof.* (1) In the proof of Lemma 1 (a) of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), $\Theta:F\to\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}\times\mathcal G$ is the continuous homomorphism to the direct product that is the identity on $\mathcal G$ and maps $x_i$ to $(e_i,1)$. In a direct product with $\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}$, $(v,\gamma)^n=(nv,\gamma^n)$ for $n\in\mathbb Z$, so by continuity $(v,\gamma)^c=(c_pv,\gamma^c)$ for $c\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, where $c_p$ is the image of $c$ in $\mathbb Z_p$. Now $\Theta(x_0^\sigma)=(e_0,1)$, and the product $x_0^{h^{p-1}}\tau x_0^{h^{p-2}}\tau\cdots x_0^h\tau$ maps to $((h^{p-1}+\cdots+h)e_0,\tau^{p-1})$. For $c=\pi/(p-1)$, $c_p=(p-1)^{-1}$, and $(\tau^{p-1})^c=\tau^\pi=1$, because $\tau$ generates $I\cong\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$ and $\pi$ has component $0$ at every $\ell\ne p$. So $\Theta((x_0,\tau))=(h'e_0,1)$ and $\Theta(R_0)=(-e_0+\kappa h'e_0,1)$, which gives $\theta_0=\kappa h'-1$. By (i), $h^j\equiv1\pmod{p^s}$, so $h'\equiv(p-1)/(p-1)=1\pmod{p^s}$. With (ii) and $m\le s$, $\theta_0=(\kappa-1)h'+(h'-1)\equiv0\pmod{p^m}$.

(2) Let $\zeta$ be a primitive $p^{m+1}$-th root of unity. If $s>m$, then $\zeta\in K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, so $K(\zeta)/K$ is tamely ramified, and its ramification index is prime to $p$. Its Galois group fixes $\mu_{p^m}\subseteq K$, so it embeds in the kernel of $(\mathbb Z/p^{m+1})^\times\to(\mathbb Z/p^m)^\times$, which has order $p$. The ramification index divides the degree, which divides $p$, so it is $1$. Conversely, if $K(\zeta)/K$ is unramified, then $\zeta\in K^{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $s\ge m+1$.

Let $s>m$. Then $\zeta$ is among the $p$-power roots of unity of $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, on which $\tau$ acts trivially by (i). The elements of $\Gamma$ fixing $\zeta$ form a closed subgroup containing $\tau$. If it contained $\sigma$, it would be $\Gamma$, which $\sigma$ and $\tau$ generate topologically, and then $\zeta\in K$ by Galois theory, contradicting the definition of $m$. So $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^\kappa\ne\zeta$, that is, $\kappa\not\equiv1\pmod{p^{m+1}}$. With (ii), $v_p(\kappa-1)=m$. As $h'\in1+p^s\mathbb Z_p$ with $s\ge m+1$, $v_p((\kappa-1)h')=m$ and $v_p(h'-1)\ge m+1$, so $v_p(\theta_0)=m$ and $a_0$ is a unit.

(3) If $s>m$, the $e_0$-coordinate $a_0$ of $r_0$ is a unit by (2). If $s=m$, its $e_1$-coordinate is $1$. A vector of $\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}$ whose $j$-th coordinate is a unit forms a basis together with the $e_i$ for $i\ne j$. Take $j=0$ if $s>m$ and $j=1$ if $s=m$, and let $Q=\bigoplus_{i\ne j}\mathbb Z_pe_i$. Then $\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}=\mathbb Z_pr_0\oplus Q$, and by Lemma 1 (a) of the cocycle theorem
$$H_\Gamma\cong\mathbb Z_p^{d+1}/\mathbb Z_pp^mr_0=(\mathbb Z/p^m)\bar t\oplus Q.$$
As $Q$ is torsion-free, $T_H=(\mathbb Z/p^m)\bar t$, of order $p^m$. This is the decomposition stated in each case.

(4) If $a\bar e_1=0$, then $ae_1=b(\theta_0e_0+p^se_1)$ for some $b\in\mathbb Z_p$. Comparing $e_0$-coordinates gives $b\theta_0=0$, so $b=0$ and $a=0$. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.**

*(a), surface coordinates.* By part (a) of the theorem of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), $u$ lies in the $\mathbb Z_p$-span of $\bar e_0,\bar e_1,\bar e_2$. So for $3\le i\le d$, $c_i=\Phi_i(u)=0$.

*(a), congruences.* Let $0\le i\le d$. The character $\chi_i:G\to k$ is a continuous homomorphism to an abelian group. On $P$ it kills $[P,P]$, so it induces a homomorphism $H\to k$. This kills each $(\rho-1)y$, as $\chi_i(gyg^{-1})=\chi_i(y)$, and so by continuity it kills $I_\Gamma H$. Hence it induces $\bar\chi_i:H_\Gamma\to k$, with $\bar\chi_i(\bar e_j)=\chi_i(x_j)=\delta_{ij}$, and $\bar\chi_i(u)=\chi_i(\alpha(\sigma)\sigma^{-1})$. So $\bar\chi_i(u)=c_i\bmod p^m$. By part 1 of [wild Frobenius shifts](wild-frobenius-shifts-when-k-contains-mu-p.md), evaluated at $\sigma$ with $f(\sigma)=1$, $\chi_i(\alpha(\sigma)\sigma^{-1})=\nu(\alpha)\chi_i(x_0)=\nu(\alpha)\delta_{i0}$. Hence $c_0\equiv\nu(\alpha)$ and $c_i\equiv0$ for $i\ge1$, modulo $p^m$.

*(a), the index $2$.* As in step 2 of the proof in [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), the transvection $T_1$, which fixes $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$, is a tame-trivial automorphism with $(T_1)_H\xi_2=\xi_2+\xi_1$ and $(T_1)_H\xi_i=\xi_i$ for $i\ne2$. So $(T_1)_*v=v+\Phi_2(v)\bar e_1$ for $v\in H_\Gamma$, as both sides are $\mathbb Z_p$-linear and agree on the generators $\bar e_i$, and as $\Phi_2(\bar e_1)=0$, $(T_1)_*^nv=v+n\Phi_2(v)\bar e_1$ for $n\ge1$. By Lemma 3 (c) there, $n\Phi_2(u)\bar e_1=0$ for some $n\ge1$. If $\theta_0\ne0$, Lemma A (4) gives $n\Phi_2(u)=0$, so $c_2=\Phi_2(u)=0$.

*(b).* Let $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ be unramified. By Lemma A (2), $s>m$, $v_p(\kappa-1)=m$, $a_0\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$ and $\theta_0\ne0$. The assertions about $T_H$ are Lemma A (3). By (a), $u=c_0\bar e_0+c_1\bar e_1$. As $\bar t=a_0\bar e_0+p^{s-m}\bar e_1$,
$$u=c\,\bar t+c'\,\bar e_1,\qquad c=a_0^{-1}c_0,\quad c'=c_1-a_0^{-1}p^{s-m}c_0.$$

*A mixing automorphism.* Let $\lambda_s=\sigma-\kappa\in\Lambda_s$, with $\kappa$ read modulo $p^s$. Then $\omega_s(\lambda_s)=\omega_s(\sigma)-\kappa=0$, and $\mathrm{aug}_s(\lambda_s)=1-\kappa$. Its reduction $\lambda=\sigma-\omega(\sigma)$ lies in $\ker\omega$. Run the proof of part 2 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md) with this $\lambda$, taking $\lambda_s$ as the lift in $\ker\omega_s$ that its fact (iii) allows; the rest of that proof uses only $\lambda_s\in\ker\omega_s$. It gives an endomorphism $\psi$ of $F$, the identity on $\mathcal G$, with $\psi(x_0)=x_0z$ for some $z\in P_F^{p,2,s}$, $\psi(x_1)=x_1w$ with $w\in P_F$ of class $\lambda_s\tilde x_3$ in $P_F/P_F^{p,2,s}$, $\psi(x_4)=x_4y'$, and $\psi(x_i)=x_i$ for $i\notin\{0,1,4\}$. Its lifting step gives an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\theta(\rho)\in\rho P^{p,2,s}$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$ and $\theta(x_i)\in\psi(x_i)P^{p,2,s}$ for every $i$, where $P^{p,2,s}$ is the closure of $P^{p^s}[P,P]$. So $\theta$ is tame-trivial, $\theta(x_3)\in x_3P^{p,2,s}$ and $\theta(x_1)\in x_1wP^{p,2,s}$.

*Its action on $H_\Gamma$ modulo $p^s$.* The image of $P^{p,2,s}$ in $H$ is $p^sH$, so $P/P^{p,2,s}=H/p^sH$, a module over $\Lambda_s$, on which $\theta$ acts $\Lambda_s$-linearly. In it, $\theta_H(\xi_3)\equiv\xi_3$ and $\theta_H(\xi_1)\equiv\xi_1+\lambda_s\xi_3$. Pass to $H/(p^sH+I_\Gamma H)=H_\Gamma/p^sH_\Gamma$. There the class of $\rho y-y$ is $0$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$, so by linearity and continuity the class of $\mu y$ is $\mathrm{aug}_s(\mu)$ times the class of $y$, for $\mu\in\Lambda_s$. Hence
$$\theta_*(\bar e_3)\equiv\bar e_3,\qquad\theta_*(\bar e_1)\equiv\bar e_1+(1-\kappa)\bar e_3\pmod{p^sH_\Gamma}.$$
The automorphism $\theta_*$ of $H_\Gamma$ has inverse $(\theta^{-1})_*$, and both map $p^sH_\Gamma$ onto itself. Applying $\theta_*^{-1}$ to $\theta_*(\bar e_1-(1-\kappa)\bar e_3)\equiv\bar e_1$ gives $\theta_*^{-1}(\bar e_1)\equiv\bar e_1-(1-\kappa)\bar e_3$ modulo $p^sH_\Gamma$. As $\Phi_3(p^sH_\Gamma)\subseteq p^s\mathbb Z_p$,
$$\Phi_3(\theta_*^{-1}\bar e_1)\equiv\kappa-1\pmod{p^s}.$$
Since $v_p(\kappa-1)=m<s$, $\Phi_3(\theta_*^{-1}\bar e_1)\ne0$.

*Conjugating.* $\alpha'=\theta^{-1}\alpha\theta$ is tame-trivial, and its outer class is conjugate to that of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates. By Lemma 3 (b) of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), applied to $\theta^{-1}$, it is trivial on $H$ and $u(\alpha')=\theta_*^{-1}u$. As $3$ is a surface index, part (a) of that theorem for $\alpha'$ gives $\Phi_3(\theta_*^{-1}u)=0$. Now $\theta_*^{-1}\bar t$ lies in $T_H$, and $\Phi_3$ vanishes on $T_H$ because $\mathbb Z_p$ is torsion-free. So
$$0=\Phi_3(\theta_*^{-1}u)=c\,\Phi_3(\theta_*^{-1}\bar t)+c'\,\Phi_3(\theta_*^{-1}\bar e_1)=c'\,\Phi_3(\theta_*^{-1}\bar e_1),$$
and $c'=0$. Hence $u=c\,\bar t\in T_H$.

*The constraints on $c$.* As $c'=0$, the representation $u=c_0\bar e_0+c_1\bar e_1$ has $c_0=a_0c$ and $c_1=p^{s-m}c$. By (a), $a_0c\equiv\nu(\alpha)$ and $p^{s-m}c\equiv0$ modulo $p^m$. Since $\bar t$ has order $p^m$ and $a_0$ is a unit, $u=0$ if and only if $c\in p^m\mathbb Z_p$, if and only if $\nu(\alpha)=0$. Finally $p^mu=0$, and $p^{s-m}u=(p^{s-m}c)\bar t=0$.

*(c).* Let $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ be ramified, so that $s=m$ by Lemma A (2), and let $\theta_0\ne0$. By Lemma A (3), $\bar e_0$ spans a free direct summand, so it is not torsion. By (a), $u=c_0\bar e_0+c_1\bar e_1$ with $c_1=p^mc_1'$ for some $c_1'\in\mathbb Z_p$. As $s=m$, $\bar t=a_0\bar e_0+\bar e_1$ and $p^m\bar t=0$, so $p^m\bar e_1=-p^ma_0\bar e_0=-\theta_0\bar e_0$. Hence $u=c\,\bar e_0$ with $c=c_0-\theta_0c_1'$, and $c\equiv c_0\equiv\nu(\alpha)\pmod{p^m}$, because $\theta_0\in p^m\mathbb Z_p$. As $\bar e_0$ is not torsion, $c$ is unique. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** By [tame innerness](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md), $\alpha_1=c_{g_0}\circ\alpha$ is tame-trivial for some $g_0\in G$. It represents the outer class of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates, and by the corollary of [wild Frobenius shifts](wild-frobenius-shifts-when-k-contains-mu-p.md) $\nu(\alpha)=\nu(\alpha_1)$. By (b), $u(\alpha_1)=c\,\bar t\in T_H$.

(1) Inner automorphisms induce the identity on $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, so $\bar\alpha=\bar\alpha_1$. Write $[y]$ for the image of $y\in G$ in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and put $\delta(y)=[\alpha_1(y)]-[y]$. This is a continuous homomorphism $G\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$. It vanishes on $P$, since $\alpha_1(x)x^{-1}\in[P,P]\subseteq[G,G]$ for $x\in P$. It vanishes at $\tau$: the profinite abelian group $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the product of its pro-$p$ part $A_p$ and its prime-to-$p$ part $A_{p'}$. The elements $[\tau]$ and $[\alpha_1(\tau)]$ generate closed subgroups that are continuous images of $I\cong\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$, so they lie in $A_{p'}$. But $\alpha_1(\tau)\tau^{-1}\in P$, whose image lies in $A_p$, so $\delta(\tau)\in A_p\cap A_{p'}=0$. Finally, $\delta(\sigma)$ is the image of $c_{\alpha_1}(\sigma)$ under the map $H\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ induced by $P\to G$. This map kills $I_\Gamma H$, so $\delta(\sigma)$ is the image of $u(\alpha_1)=c\,\bar t$, which has order dividing $p^m$. The kernel of $\delta$ is a closed normal subgroup containing $P$ and $\tau$, and the quotient of $G$ by the closed normal subgroup generated by $P$ and $\tau$ is $\Gamma/I$, topologically generated by the image of $\sigma$. So $\delta(G)$ lies in the closure of the subgroup generated by $\delta(\sigma)$, which is finite. Since $G\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is onto, $\bar\alpha(z)-z\in\langle\delta(\sigma)\rangle\subseteq(G^{\mathrm{ab}})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ for every $z$.

(2) (iii) implies (ii): inner automorphisms and $\alpha_0$ induce the identity on $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, as $[P,P]\subseteq[G,G]$. (ii) implies (i): then $\chi_0\circ\alpha_1=\chi_0$, and part 1 of [wild Frobenius shifts](wild-frobenius-shifts-when-k-contains-mu-p.md) gives $\nu(\alpha_1)\chi_0(x_0)f=0$, so $\nu(\alpha)=\nu(\alpha_1)=0$. (i) implies (iii): by (b), $u(\alpha_1)=0$. As in step 4 of the proof of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), Lemma 2 there gives $h\in H$ with $c_{\alpha_1}(\gamma)=(\gamma-1)h$, and Lemma 3 (a) there shows that $\alpha_0=c_g\circ\alpha_1$ induces the identity on $G/[P,P]$ for any $g\in P$ of class $h$. Then $\alpha=c_{(gg_0)^{-1}}\circ\alpha_0$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What is new.* Part (b) of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md) kills the coordinate of $u(\alpha)$ on $\bar e_1$ when $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, by conjugating with a mixing automorphism built from $\lambda\in\ker\omega$ with $\epsilon(\lambda)\ne0$ in $\mathbb F_p$. For $\mu_p\subseteq K$ every such $\lambda$ has $\epsilon(\lambda)=0$. The proof of (b) works instead with coefficients $\mathbb Z/p^s$: the lift $\sigma-\kappa\in\ker\omega_s$ has augmentation $1-\kappa$ of valuation exactly $m<s$. This is visible modulo $p^s$, the precision with which Wingberg's lifting theorem determines the mixing automorphism.
- *What remains when $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ is unramified.* The whole obstruction to innerness modulo $[P,P]$ is then $\nu(\alpha)$. [Wild Frobenius shifts](wild-frobenius-shifts-when-k-contains-mu-p.md) shows that $\nu$ cannot be detected through conjugation alone, because conjugation scales it by a unit. Whether $\nu(\alpha)=0$ for every finite-conjugacy $\alpha$ is not decided here. By (b), $u(\alpha)$ has order dividing $p^{\min(m,s-m)}$. For example it has order at most $p$ when $s=m+1$.
- *When $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ is ramified.* Then $s=m$, as for $K=\mathbb Q_p(\mu_{p^m})$, whose extension by $\mu_{p^{m+1}}$ is totally ramified of degree $p$. Then $\kappa\equiv h\equiv1\pmod{p^s}$, so $\omega_s$ is trivial and $\mathrm{aug}_s$ vanishes on $\ker\omega_s$. So an automorphism built as in the proof of (b), from any lift in $\ker\omega_s$, acts as the identity on $H_\Gamma/p^sH_\Gamma$: it moves $\bar e_1$ by $\mathrm{aug}_s(\lambda_s)\bar e_3=0$ and $\bar e_4$ by $-\mathrm{aug}_s(\lambda_s^*)\bar e_2=-\omega_s(\lambda_s)\bar e_2=0$ there. Part (c) reduces this case, when $\theta_0\ne0$, to one invariant $c\in\mathbb Z_p$ that lifts $\nu(\alpha)$. The automorphisms constructed through Wingberg's lifting theorem are known only modulo $P^{p,2,s}$, and whether $c=0$ is not decided here. The case $\theta_0=0$, possible only when $s=m$, gives only $u\in\mathbb Z_p\bar e_0+p^m\mathbb Z_p\bar e_2$, by (a), as $p^m\bar e_1=0$ there.
- *Examples with $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ unramified.* Let $L=\mathbb Q_p(\mu_{p^2})$, let $U$ be the unramified extension of degree $p$ of $\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p)$, and let $M=LU$. As $L/\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p)$ is totally ramified and $U/\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p)$ is unramified, both of degree $p$, they are linearly disjoint, and $\mathrm{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p))\cong(\mathbb Z/p)^2$. Let $K$ be the fixed field of a subgroup of order $p$ other than $\mathrm{Gal}(M/L)$ and $\mathrm{Gal}(M/U)$. Then $K\ne L$, so $\mu_{p^2}\not\subseteq K$ and $m=1$. Also $K\ne U$, so $KU=M$, which is unramified over $K$ and contains $\mu_{p^2}$. So $K(\mu_{p^2})=M$ is unramified over $K$, and (b) and the corollary apply to this $K$, of degree $p(p-1)$.
- *A subgroup of index dividing $p^{\min(m,s-m)}$ (proved here).* Let $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ be unramified. For tame-trivial $\alpha,\beta$ trivial on $H$, $c_{\alpha\beta}(\gamma)$ is the class of $\alpha(\beta(g)g^{-1})\cdot\alpha(g)g^{-1}$, which is $c_\beta(\gamma)+c_\alpha(\gamma)$ because $\alpha$ is trivial on $H$. So $u(\alpha\beta)=u(\alpha)+u(\beta)$. The outer classes with finitely many conjugates form a subgroup $Z$ of $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. Each has a tame-trivial representative, by [tame innerness](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md), and two of them differ by $c_g$ with $g\in P$, as $\Gamma$ has trivial centre ([the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), part 3). By Lemma 3 (a) of the cocycle theorem, $u$ is therefore a well-defined homomorphism on $Z$. By (b), it takes values in $p^{\max(0,2m-s)}T_H$, which is cyclic of order $p^{\min(m,s-m)}$, and by the corollary its kernel is the set of classes in $Z$ that become trivial in $\operatorname{Out}(G/[P,P])$. So these classes form a subgroup of $Z$ of index dividing $p^{\min(m,s-m)}$.
- *Scope and sources.* Odd $p$, every finite $K/\mathbb Q_p$ containing $\mu_p$ with $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$, every $m$ and $s$. Part (b) and the corollary need $K(\mu_{p^{m+1}})/K$ unramified.
