**Definitions.** Use the [Bloch–Kato property](bloch-kato-pro-p-group.md) of hereditary quadratic mod-$p$ cohomology. A pro-$p$ group $G$ is *locally powerful* if every finitely generated closed subgroup $H$ is powerful, meaning

$$
\overline{[H,H]}\subseteq
\begin{cases}
H^p,&p\ne2,\\
H^4,&p=2,
\end{cases}
$$

where $H^m$ denotes the closed subgroup generated by $m$th powers [@quadrelli2012blochkato, §1–3.1, pp. 1–4]. For a continuous orientation $\theta:G\to\mathbb Z_p^\times$, call $(G,\theta)$ *$\theta$-abelian* when

$$
ghg^{-1}=h^{\theta(g)}\qquad(g\in G,\ h\in\ker\theta).
$$

Then $\ker\theta$ is abelian and $G$ is metabelian. The trivial orientation gives the abelian case [@quadrelli2012blochkato, §1, pp. 1–2].

**Theorem (Quadrelli).** Let $p$ be odd and $G$ Bloch–Kato. The following are equivalent:

1. $G$ contains no closed nonabelian free pro-$p$ subgroup.
2. $G$ is locally powerful.
3. Some continuous orientation $\theta:G\to\mathbb Z_p^\times$ makes $(G,\theta)$ $\theta$-abelian.

No finite-generation hypothesis is needed. If $G$ is finitely generated, these conditions are also equivalent to $G$ being $p$-adic analytic [@quadrelli2012blochkato, Theorem 4.6, pp. 9–10]. In that case they are equivalent to

$$
\operatorname{cd}(G)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)
\quad\text{and, equivalently,}\quad
H^*(G,\mathbb F_p)\cong\Lambda^*H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)
$$

[@quadrelli2012blochkato, Corollary 4.8, p. 10].

**How the free subgroup is detected.** In Quadrelli's proof, a finitely generated closed subgroup $K$ that is not powerful has a nonzero relation in the kernel of its degree-two cup product. Restricting to a suitable subgroup $S$ generated by two members of a minimal generating set of $K$ makes cup product zero on $S$. Hereditary quadraticity then gives $H^2(S,\mathbb F_p)=0$, and $S$ is free pro-$p$ of rank two [@quadrelli2012blochkato, proof of Theorem 4.6, p. 10]. This explains the role of hereditary quadraticity in detecting a free subgroup.

**At $p=2$.** The equivalence of conditions 1–3 holds for a torsion-free Bloch–Kato pro-$2$ group if its Bockstein $\beta:H^1(G,\mathbb F_2)\to H^2(G,\mathbb F_2)$ is zero [@quadrelli2012blochkato, Theorem 4.11, p. 11]. Here $\beta(a)=a\cup a$ [@quadrelli2012blochkato, §2, p. 2]. The odd-prime theorem does not assert these equivalences for arbitrary pro-$2$ groups.

**Galois form (Ware).** Let $F$ have characteristic different from $p$ and contain a primitive $p$th root of unity, and also $\sqrt{-1}$ if $p=2$. For $G=G_F(p)$, either $(G,\theta_F)$ is $\theta_F$-abelian for the actual cyclotomic character, or $G$ contains a closed nonabelian free pro-$p$ subgroup. This holds without finite generation [@quadrelli2022smooth, Theorem 4.6]. The [norm residue theorem](norm-residue-isomorphism-theorem.md) supplies the Bloch–Kato property [@quadrelli2012blochkato, §1–2, pp. 1–3]. The Galois statement specifies the cyclotomic orientation; the abstract theorem only asserts the existence of an orientation.
