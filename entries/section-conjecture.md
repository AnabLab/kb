summary: For a proper hyperbolic curve $X$ over a field $K$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, every section of $\pi_1(X) \to G_K$ should come, up to conjugacy, from a unique $K$-rational point. Injectivity is known and surjectivity is open. The pro-$p$ analogue fails.

**Setting.** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$ and $X$ a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $K$, with fundamental group $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X = \pi_1(X)$ and geometric fundamental group $\Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_X$, so that there is an exact sequence
$$1 \to \Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_X \to \Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X \to G_K \to 1.$$
A $K$-rational point $x$ gives, by functoriality, a section of $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X \to G_K$ whose image is the decomposition group of a point above $x$ in the universal pro-étale cover of $X$. Because of base points and the choice of that point, only its class up to conjugation by $\Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_X$ is well defined [@stix2010, (1.2), p. 1]. Write $\mathrm{Sect}(G_K, \Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X)$ for the set of continuous sections modulo conjugation by $\Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_X$, and
$$\sigma_X : X(K) \to \mathrm{Sect}(G_K, \Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X)$$
for the resulting map. A section is *geometric* if its class lies in the image of $\sigma_X$ [@saidi2012, Definition 2.2, p. 6].

**Conjecture (Grothendieck).** If $K$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ and $X$ is a proper hyperbolic curve over $K$, that is, a proper curve of genus at least $2$, then $\sigma_X$ is bijective.

This is the statement in Hoshi [@hoshi2010, Introduction, p. 830] and Saïdi [@saidi2012, §2, p. 6]. Stix states it for number fields [@stix2010, Conjecture 1, p. 1]. Esnault and Wittenberg [@esnaultwittenberg2010, §1, p. 1] and Koenigsmann [@koenigsmann2005, §1.3, p. 5] attribute it to Grothendieck's letter to Faltings of 27 June 1983, published in *Geometric Galois Actions 1*, London Math. Soc. Lecture Note Ser. 242 (1997), pp. 49–58. That letter has not been opened for this entry.

Mochizuki phrases the conjecture loosely through the map of his injectivity theorem, saying that it should be bijective "for instance when $X_K$ is proper" and needs slight modification when $X_K$ is affine [@mochizuki1999, Remark after Theorem 19.1, p. 92]. That map uses the geometrically pro-$p$ quotient $\Pi_X$ of $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X$, and read literally in that form the statement is false, by Hoshi's theorem below. The sources above all use the profinite group.

**Affine curves.** Suppose that $X$ is not proper, with smooth compactification $\bar X$. A $K$-rational point of $\bar X \setminus X$, a cusp, also gives sections of $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X \to G_K$, whose images lie in decomposition groups at that point [@koenigsmann2005, Remark 1.2 (3), p. 5]. Stix describes them as "an abundance of additional cuspidal sections". He says that apart from the modification they force, the conjecture stays the same, with the condition on the genus replaced by negative Euler characteristic [@stix2010, (1.2), p. 1], which is hyperbolicity [@koenigsmann2005, §1.2, p. 3]. In Koenigsmann's formulation, a section is a *branch section* if its image lies in a decomposition group at a point of $\bar X \setminus X$, and the conjecture asserts that $\sigma_X$ is a bijection onto the classes of non-branch sections [@koenigsmann2005, Definition 1.1, pp. 4–5].

**Equivalent forms.**

- *Surjectivity.* The map $\sigma_X$ is known to be injective whenever $K$ is sub-$p$-adic for some $p$, which includes every field finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$. See [injectivity in the section conjecture](section-conjecture-injectivity.md). So the conjecture says exactly that every section is geometric [@saidi2012, §2, p. 6].
- *Sections as the only obstruction.* A section $s$ has a system of neighbourhoods: a tower of geometrically connected finite étale covers $X_i[s] \to X$ over $K$, whose fundamental groups are open subgroups of $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X$ that contain $s(G_K)$ and intersect in $s(G_K)$ [@saidi2012, §2.4, pp. 7–8]. If $K$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, or finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, then $s$ is geometric exactly when $X_i[s](K) \neq \emptyset$ for every $i$. Saïdi attributes this to Tamagawa [@saidi2012, Lemma 2.5, p. 9]. Each $X_i[s]$ is again a proper hyperbolic curve over $K$, by the Riemann–Hurwitz formula, and $s$ restricts to a section of its fundamental group. So the conjecture over $K$ is equivalent to its weak form: for every proper hyperbolic curve $X$ over $K$, if $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X \to G_K$ has a section, then $X(K) \neq \emptyset$ [@saidi2012, p. 9]. Esnault and Wittenberg state the same equivalence, citing Koenigsmann [@esnaultwittenberg2010, §1, p. 1].

**Relation to the birational conjecture.** The section conjecture implies the [birational section conjecture](birational-section-conjecture.md). Esnault and Wittenberg call the latter "a consequence of Grothendieck's section conjecture" [@esnaultwittenberg2010, §2, p. 3]. For a proper curve $X$ of genus at least $2$, they explain that under the section conjecture a section of $G_{K(X)} \to G_K$ forces $X(K) \neq \emptyset$, because it induces a section of $\pi_1(X) \to G_K$ [@esnaultwittenberg2010, §1, p. 1]. The full argument is as follows.

Fix $K$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, a smooth complete curve $X$ over $K$, and a section $s$ of $G_{K(X)} \to G_K$, and let $F$ be the fixed field of $s(G_K)$ in a separable closure $\Omega$ of $K(X)$. For each finite extension $F'$ of $K(X)$ inside $F$, the field $K$ is algebraically closed in $F'$, so $F'$ is the function field of a smooth complete curve $X'$ over $K$, and $s$ is also a section of $G_{F'} \to G_K$. It therefore induces a section of $\pi_1(X') \to G_K$ [@koenigsmann2005, Remark 1.6 (1), p. 6]. If $X'$ has genus at least $2$, the section conjecture gives $X'(K) \neq \emptyset$. Koenigsmann's proof of his Lemma 1.7 shows that this suffices. The curves $X'$ of genus at least $2$ are cofinal among all the $X'$, and their sets of $K$-points are finite by Faltings' theorem, so the inverse limit of these sets is nonempty. A point of the limit is a $K$-rational place of $F$ [@koenigsmann2005, Lemma 1.7 and its proof, pp. 7–8].

That such a place makes $s$ geometric is checked here. Since $s(G_K) \cap G_{\bar K(X)} = 1$, the field $F \bar K$ is all of $\Omega$, so every finite extension of $F$ lies in $FL$ for some finite Galois extension $L/K$. Moreover $[FL : F] = [L : K]$, because restriction $G_F \to G_K$ is an isomorphism. The residue field of every prolongation of the place to $FL$ contains $L$, so by the fundamental inequality the prolongation is unique. Hence $F$ is henselian at the place, and $s(G_K) = G_F$ lies in the decomposition group in $G_{K(X)}$ of its prolongation to $\Omega$. The place restricts to a nontrivial place of $K(X)$ that is trivial on $K$, so this is a decomposition group at a point of $X$, and $s$ is geometric [@koenigsmann2005, Observation 1.3, p. 5].

For a fixed curve, Koenigsmann notes that in general neither conjecture implies the other, since $G_{K(X)} \to \pi_1(X)$ need not split [@koenigsmann2005, Remark 1.6 (1), p. 6]. In the other direction, Saïdi notes that the birational conjecture together with a positive answer to his cuspidalisation problem, which asks whether every section of $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X \to G_K$ lifts to a section of $G_{K(X)} \to G_K$, would give the section conjecture [@saidi2012, Remark 4.4 (ii), p. 19].

**Evidence and related results.**

- *Real places.* Mochizuki formulated and proved that, for every hyperbolic curve over $\mathbb{R}$, proper or affine, section classes correspond bijectively to the connected components of its real points [@mochizuki2003topics, Corollary 3.13, pp. 142–143]. Stix credits Mochizuki with this earlier result [@stix2010, (A.1), p. 12] and gives another proof for smooth projective curves of genus at least one, including genus one [@stix2010, Theorem 26, p. 14]. See [the real section theorem](real-section-conjecture.md) for the precise statements and cohomological criteria. Since a section over a number field restricts to a section over each completion [@saidi2012, §2.3, p. 7], a curve over a number field without points over some real completion has no sections, and the conjecture holds for it trivially. An example is the curve $X^{2n} + Y^{2n} + Z^{2n} = 0$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ for $n \ge 2$ [@stix2010, (7.3) and Example 19, p. 11].
- *$p$-adic places.* Let $K$ be finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $X$ a smooth projective curve of positive genus over $K$ whose fundamental group sequence splits. Then the period and index of $X$ are powers of $p$, and they are equal if $p$ is odd. Over $\mathbb{Q}$, a section forces period and index to be equal [@stix2010, Theorems 2 and 3, p. 2]. Stix notes that in light of the section conjecture one expects both to be $1$ [@stix2010, §1, p. 2]. He uses this to construct curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with real points but no sections, the sections being obstructed at a $p$-adic place [@stix2010, (7.4) and Example 20, p. 11].
- *Global obstructions.* Over a number field, the restrictions of a global section to the completions pass a Brauer–Manin test, and the local sections of an adelic point come from one global section exactly when the point survives every finite descent obstruction. With this, Stix proves that the affine Reichardt–Lind curve $2Y^2 = Z^4 - 17$, $Y \neq 0$, over $\mathbb{Q}$ has no sections, although it has adelic points. He also shows that the conjecture over number fields would follow from its $p$-adic version together with the conjecture that the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to rational points on curves [@stix2011bm, Theorems 25 and 27, pp. 14–17]. See [descent and Brauer–Manin obstructions for Galois sections](descent-obstructions-for-galois-sections.md).
- *Local images of Selmer sections.* Call a section *Selmer* if its restriction to every decomposition group is the section of a local point. Let $K$ be a number field with no CM subfield and $X$ a proper hyperbolic curve over $K$. Betts and Stix prove that the Selmer sections give only finitely many points of $X(K_v)$ at each finite place $v$, as the conjecture and Faltings' theorem predict. So the finite descent locus has finite projection to each $X(K_v)$. The proof extends Lawrence and Venkatesh's method of $p$-adic period maps [@bettsstix2025, Theorems A and B, pp. 1–2]. See [finiteness of the local images of Selmer sections](finiteness-of-local-images-of-selmer-sections.md).
- *Adelic sections of affine curves.* Let $K$ be $\mathbb{Q}$ or an imaginary quadratic field, and $X$ a hyperbolic curve over $K$ with a nonconstant global unit, such as $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$. Stix proves that every section whose restrictions to the completions come from the components of one adelic point of $X$ comes from a unique rational point, and that every adelic point surviving all finite descent obstructions is rational [@stix2015birational, Theorem C, p. 2]. See [birational sections with local conditions](birational-sections-with-local-conditions.md), which also records his results for sections that lift to the function field.
- *Generic curves.* Let $k$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ and $g \ge 5$. The generic curve over the function field of the moduli space $\mathcal{M}_g$ over $k$, or of a level covering of it, has no rational points, and its fundamental group sequence does not split. So the conjecture holds for it, with both sides empty. This is Hain's theorem, recorded with its sources in [the entry on the generic curve](section-conjecture-for-universal-curves.md).
- *Other fields.* The analogue fails over finite fields, where sections always exist because $G_K$ is free, but some proper hyperbolic curves have no rational points [@saidi2012, §2, p. 6]. Over a separably closed field, $\sigma_X$ is surjective but not injective [@koenigsmann2005, Remark 1.2 (1), p. 5]. If $K$ is $p$-adically closed and a proper relatively algebraically closed subfield of a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, then $\sigma_X$ is not surjective for any proper hyperbolic curve $X$ over $K$ with $X(K) \neq \emptyset$ [@koenigsmann2005, Proposition 2.3 (2), p. 9]. Koenigsmann's wording there says "any smooth complete curve", but his abstract restricts to genus greater than $1$, and his proof applies Mochizuki's injectivity theorem to points of $X$ over the finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ that contains $K$, which needs $X$ hyperbolic [@koenigsmann2005, proof of Proposition 2.3, pp. 9–10]. For $X = \mathbb{P}^1_K$ the statement would be false, since $\Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_X = 1$ and the single section comes from any $K$-point. Koenigsmann names the field of $p$-adic algebraic numbers as a field over which the conjecture fails [@koenigsmann2005, abstract]. Such a field is sub-$p$-adic, so $\sigma_X$ is injective there for hyperbolic $X$, and the conjecture cannot be extended from finitely generated fields to all sub-$p$-adic fields.
- *Local fields.* Koenigsmann conjectures that the analogue holds for smooth complete curves of genus at least $2$ over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@koenigsmann2005, §2.1, p. 9], and Saïdi states the same $p$-adic version [@saidi2012, §2.3, p. 7]. The birational analogue over these fields is a theorem: see [Koenigsmann's theorem on birational sections over local fields](birational-sections-over-local-fields.md). Pop and Stix prove that every section of a hyperbolic curve over a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ lies in the decomposition group of a valuation of the function field prolonging the $p$-adic valuation, which reduces the $p$-adic version to excluding valuations that do not come from rational points [@popstix2017, Theorem 26 and §6.1, pp. 18 and 21]. See [the valuative section theorem](valuative-section-theorem-for-p-adic-curves.md).
- *The pro-$p$ version fails.* Replace $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X$ by its geometrically pro-$p$ quotient $\Pi_X$. The map from $X(K)$ to sections of $\Pi_X \to G_K$ modulo $\Delta_X$ is still injective over sub-$p$-adic fields [@mochizuki1999, Theorem 19.1, p. 92]. It is not surjective in general. Hoshi constructs nongeometric pro-$p$ sections of proper hyperbolic curves over number fields and over $p$-adic local fields [@hoshi2010, Theorem A, pp. 831–832]. For example, for a regular prime $p > 3$ the Fermat curve of exponent $p$ acquires such sections over suitable finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ inside the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ unramified outside $p$, and over their completions above $p$ [@hoshi2010, Theorem A, p. 832]. For a regular prime $p > 7$, the same curve over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ has infinitely many conjugacy classes of pro-$p$ sections [@hoshi2010, Theorem B, p. 832]. Hoshi points out that, by injectivity, finiteness of the set of conjugacy classes of sections would imply finiteness of $X(K)$, and that Theorem B shows this approach to Mordell's conjecture fails for the pro-$p$ group [@hoshi2010, Introduction, p. 832]. Saïdi explains that Hoshi's method relies on the image of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Out}(\Delta_X)$ being free pro-$p$, and is unlikely to work for the profinite group, where the outer representation is injective for hyperbolic curves over number fields and $p$-adic fields [@saidi2012, Remark 2.6 (ii), p. 10].

**Status.** Open, as far as the sources opened here show. Hoshi writes that surjectivity "remains unsolved" [@hoshi2010, Introduction, p. 830], and Saïdi that the conjecture "is still widely open" [@saidi2012, §0, p. 2]. These sources date from 2008 to 2010, and later literature, including Stix's monograph *Rational points and arithmetic of fundamental groups* (Lecture Notes in Math. 2054, 2013), has not been checked for this entry. A [manuscript dated 6 October 2026](manuscript-claiming-p-adic-section-conjecture.md) claims the full $p$-adic analogue and a global consequence under a finite-cover descent equality [@openai2026padicsections, Theorem 1.1, p. 2 and Theorem 8.2, p. 20]. Its completion argument has not been independently verified here, so the local statement is recorded as a claim.

**Sources not opened.** Grothendieck's letter to Faltings and Tamagawa's paper containing the neighbourhood criterion, which Saïdi cites as Proposition 2.8 (iv). These are reported here through the sources that cite them.
