**Setting.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with inertia group $I_k \subseteq G_k$. Two characters of $G_k$ with values in the same abelian group are *inertially equivalent* if they agree on an open subgroup of $I_k$. For a finite extension $E/\mathbb{Q}_p$ and a field embedding $\sigma : E \to k$, let $\chi_\sigma : G_k \to E^\times$ be the composite of $G_k \to G_k^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong (k^\times)^\wedge$, the projection to $\mathcal{O}_k^\times$ given by a uniformizer, inversion, and the norm $\mathcal{O}_k^\times \to \mathcal{O}_E^\times$ along $\sigma$. Its inertial equivalence class does not depend on the uniformizer [@mochizuki2012, Definition 3.1 (i), (ii)].

- A character $\rho : G_k \to E^\times$ is of *qLT-type* if, for some open subgroup $H \subseteq G_k$ with fixed field $k_H$ and some embedding $\sigma : E \to k_H$, the restriction $\rho|_H$ is inertially equivalent to $\chi_\sigma$ [@mochizuki2012, Definition 3.1 (iii)]. Characters arising from Lubin–Tate groups are of qLT-type [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.1.1].
- Let $1 \to \Delta \to \Pi \to G_k \to 1$ be the extension given by a hyperbolic curve $X$ over $k$, with $\Pi = \pi_1(X)$. It is of *A-qLT-type* if the following holds for every open subgroup $H \subseteq G_k$ and every character $\rho : H \to F^\times$ of qLT-type, where $F$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ whose conjugates over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ all lie in $k_H$. There is an open subgroup $J$ of the inverse image of $I_k \cap H$ in $\Pi$, with image $J_G$ in $G_k$ and with $J_\Delta = J \cap \Delta$, such that $F$, with $J_G$ acting through $\rho$, is isomorphic to a subquotient of the $J_G$-module $J_\Delta^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ [@mochizuki2012, Definition 3.1 (v)]. Mochizuki paraphrases this as saying that every Lubin–Tate character on the inertia subgroup of an open subgroup of $G_k$ arises, after restriction to a smaller open subgroup, from a subquotient of the $p$-adic Tate module of the Jacobian of a finite étale cover of $X$ [@mochizuki2015, Appendix, (CM5), pp. 1153–1154].
- For extensions $\Pi_i \to G_{k_i}$ of this kind, a continuous homomorphism $\psi : \Pi_1 \to \Pi_2$ is *semi-absolute* if it has open image and maps $\Delta_1$ into $\Delta_2$ [@mochizuki2012, Definition 2.4]. It then induces an open homomorphism $\phi : G_{k_1} \to G_{k_2}$. Such a $\phi$ is *geometric* if it arises from an isomorphism of fields $\bar k_2 \to \bar k_1$ mapping $k_2$ into $k_1$ [@mochizuki2012, Definition 3.1 (iv)].

Mochizuki states the results below for hyperbolic orbicurves and, more generally, for extensions of AFG-type. They are stated here for hyperbolic curves.

**Statement.** Let $k_1, k_2$ be finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_{p_1}, \mathbb{Q}_{p_2}$, let $X_1, X_2$ be hyperbolic curves over them, and let $\psi : \pi_1(X_1) \to \pi_1(X_2)$ be semi-absolute, inducing $\phi : G_{k_1} \to G_{k_2}$.

1. If $\pi_1(X_2)$ is of A-qLT-type, then $\phi$ is geometric [@mochizuki2012, Theorem 3.5 (iii)].
2. If $\pi_1(X_2)$ is of A-qLT-type, then $\psi$ arises from a unique dominant morphism of schemes $X_1 \to X_2$ lying over a morphism $\mathrm{Spec}\, k_1 \to \mathrm{Spec}\, k_2$ [@mochizuki2012, Corollary 3.8, condition (g)].
3. *Tamagawa's criterion.* If $X_2$ has a finite étale cover $Y \to X_2$ by a hyperbolic curve $Y$ with a dominant $k_2$-morphism from $Y$ to the projective line minus three points over $k_2$, then $\pi_1(X_2)$ is of A-qLT-type [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1] [@mochizuki2015, Appendix, (CM5)].

So parts 1 and 2 apply whenever $X_2$ satisfies the hypothesis of part 3. Part 2 also holds under other conditions on $\phi$, among them that $\phi$ is an isomorphism preserving the ramification filtration [@mochizuki2012, Corollary 3.8, conditions (a)–(h)].

**Proof outline.** For part 1, Mochizuki argues as follows [@mochizuki2012, proof of Theorem 3.5 (iii)]. By Tate's theorem on $p$-divisible groups, for every open subgroup $J$ of $\pi_1(X_1)$, the module $J_\Delta^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ is Hodge–Tate with weights in $\{0, 1\}$. Together with the A-qLT property of $\pi_1(X_2)$, this shows that $\phi$ is of *01-qLT-type*: restricting along $\phi$ turns characters of qLT-type on open subgroups of $G_{k_2}$ into Hodge–Tate characters with weights in $\{0, 1\}$ [@mochizuki2012, Definition 3.1 (iv)]. By part (i) of the same theorem, such a $\phi$ is geometric (compare the [Hodge–Tate criteria](hodge-tate-geometricity-criterion.md)). Part 2 follows from part 1 and the relative theorem [@mochizuki1999, Theorem A], as in [Mochizuki's Hom theorem](mochizuki-hom-theorem-for-hyperbolic-curves.md) [@mochizuki2012, proof of Corollary 3.8].

Part 3 is a result communicated to Mochizuki by Tamagawa, who derives it from the following statement $(\ast^{\mathrm{CM}})$: for every character $\rho : G_k \to E^\times$ of qLT-type there is an abelian variety $A$ with complex multiplication over a finite extension $k_A$ of $k$ such that $\rho|_{G_{k_A}}$ is inertially equivalent to a character whose module is a subquotient of the $p$-adic Tate module of $A$ [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1]. The derivation runs as follows. Such an $A$ is defined over a number field, so it is a quotient of the Jacobian of a proper curve $Z$ over a number field. By [Belyi's theorem](belyi-theorem.md), an open subscheme of $Z$ is a finite étale cover of the projective line minus three points. So the Galois module comes from the Tate module of the Jacobian of a finite étale cover of the tripod, hence from covers of $Y$ and $X_2$ [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1]. Mochizuki later verified $(\ast^{\mathrm{CM}})$ in an appendix on complex multiplication, and concluded that Tamagawa's criterion holds [@mochizuki2015, Introduction, p. 945, and Appendix, (CM5)].

**Function fields.** Let $K_1, K_2$ be function fields of transcendence degree at least $1$ over finite extensions $k_1, k_2$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with $k_i$ algebraically closed in $K_i$, and let $\Delta_i$ be the kernel of $G_{K_i} \to G_{k_i}$. Then every open homomorphism $G_{K_1} \to G_{K_2}$ that restricts to an open homomorphism $\Delta_1 \to \Delta_2$ arises from a unique embedding of fields $K_2 \to K_1$ inducing a finite embedding $k_2 \to k_1$ [@mochizuki2012, Corollary 3.9]. Mochizuki first stated this assuming Tamagawa's criterion or $(\ast^{\mathrm{CM}})$, and later noted that his verification of it makes the corollary unconditional [@mochizuki2015, Introduction, p. 945]. The proof uses that every such function field contains the function field of a tripod [@mochizuki2012, proof of Corollary 3.9].

**What the hypotheses do.**

- *Semi-absoluteness cannot be dropped.* The corresponding absolute statement, for arbitrary open homomorphisms, is false in general [@mochizuki2012, Introduction, item (3)]. Starting from any hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field and passing to a suitable finite étale cover, one gets a curve whose $\pi_1$ surjects, through the dual graph of the special fibre of its stable model, onto a free profinite group of any prescribed finite rank. Hence it surjects onto the fundamental group of any hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field, which is topologically finitely generated, and such a surjection is open but does not map $\Delta_1$ into $\Delta_2$ [@mochizuki2012, Example 2.13].
- *Isomorphisms are automatically semi-absolute.* Every isomorphism $\pi_1(X_1) \to \pi_1(X_2)$ maps $\Delta_1$ onto $\Delta_2$ [@mochizuki2012, Corollary 2.8 (ii)]. So part 2 applies to isomorphisms, and Mochizuki counts it, with Tamagawa's criterion, among the absolute isomorphism versions of the Grothendieck conjecture over $p$-adic fields that rely on Belyi maps [@mochizuki2013, Remark 2.10.1]. The isomorphism version for all hyperbolic curves over $p$-adic fields was later proved by other means, in a preprint; see [the absolute Grothendieck theorem over $p$-adic fields](absolute-grothendieck-theorem-for-p-adic-curves.md).
- *Which curves are covered.* Mochizuki asks whether every hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field is of A-qLT-type; see [the A-qLT problem](a-qlt-problem-for-p-adic-hyperbolic-curves.md). A positive answer would remove the hypothesis of part 3 from part 2.

**Relation to Hodge–Tate representations.** Compare [Hodge–Tate representations not preserved by automorphisms](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md). There, a single Lubin–Tate representation of $G_k$ stays Hodge–Tate only under a measure-zero set of outer automorphisms. Here, realizing Lubin–Tate characters inside the geometric fundamental group, where Hodge–Tate weights are controlled by Tate's theorem, forces every semi-absolute $\psi$ to induce a geometric $\phi$.
