**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$. Use the Setting and notation of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) for $d=2$. So $G=G_K=F/(\varrho)$ with $F=P\rtimes\mathcal G$, wild inertia $P_K$ is the image of $P$, $\mathcal G$ maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma=G/P_K=\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$, and with $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$,
$$\varrho=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa\,x_1^{p^s}\,[x_1,x_2].$$
Also $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, and $\omega:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ is the ring homomorphism extending the action of $\Gamma$ on $\mu_p$. Let $I\subseteq\Gamma$ be tame inertia, the closed subgroup generated by $\tau$, and $\Lambda_I\subseteq\Lambda$ the closure of the span of $I$. By part 1 there,
$$\bar V=P_K/P_K^{(2)}=L\oplus\Lambda\bar x_1\oplus\Lambda\bar x_2,\qquad L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0,$$
with $\Lambda\bar x_j\cong\Lambda$ for $j=1,2$ and $\lambda\bar x_0=\omega(\lambda)\bar x_0$. So every $v\in\bar V$ is uniquely $c\bar x_0+\lambda_1\bar x_1+\lambda_2\bar x_2$ with $c\in\mathbb F_p$ and $\lambda_j\in\Lambda$. Call $c$ the $\bar x_0$-coordinate and $\lambda_j$ the $\bar x_j$-coordinate of $v$. The map $\pi:\bar V\to\mathbb F_p$ taking the $\bar x_0$-coordinate satisfies $\pi(\lambda v)=\omega(\lambda)\pi(v)$, as the decomposition is one of $\Lambda$-modules.

Every automorphism of $G$ maps $P_K$ onto itself ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), so it induces an additive automorphism of $\bar V$. If $\eta\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$, the induced map $\bar\eta$ is $\Lambda$-linear. Indeed, for $\rho\in\mathcal G$, $\eta(\rho)\in\rho P_K$, and $P_K$ acts trivially on $\bar V$, so $\bar\eta(\rho v)=\rho\,\bar\eta(v)$; and $\bar\eta$ is continuous.

*Transvections.* For $k\in\mathbb Z$ let $T_k$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$, maps $x_2$ to $x_2x_1^k$ and fixes $x_0,x_1$. Since
$$[x_1,x_2x_1^k]=x_1x_2x_1^kx_1^{-1}x_1^{-k}x_2^{-1}=[x_1,x_2],$$
$T_k$ fixes $\varrho$ and induces an endomorphism of $G$, again written $T_k$. As $T_kT_{-k}$ and $T_0=\mathrm{id}$ agree on the generators, $T_k$ is an automorphism with inverse $T_{-k}$. These are the transvections of the Setting of [the even-degree entry](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md), whose construction uses only the last two factors of the relator. $T_k$ is the identity on the image of $\mathcal G$, so it induces the identity on $\Gamma$, and $\bar T_k$ is the $\Lambda$-linear map fixing $\bar x_0$ and $\bar x_1$ and sending $\bar x_2$ to $\bar x_2+k\bar x_1$.

The [degree-two tame-quotient theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-on-the-tame-quotient-in-degree-two.md) is called the degree-two entry below, and [surface generators modulo the Frattini subgroup](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md) the surface-generator entry.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$. Let $\theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ have finite $p$-power order, and suppose that its class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has only finitely many conjugates. Then $\theta=1$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$.

1. No element of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ of $p$-power order other than $1$ has finitely many conjugates. Hence every finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has order prime to $p$.
2. Let $N$ be a rival copy of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ of type (a) in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 2, and put $M=N\cap G_K$. Then $(G_K:M)$ is prime to $p$.

**Proof of the Theorem.** Suppose $\theta\ne1$, of order $p^a$ with $a\ge1$, and put $\alpha=\theta^{p^{a-1}}$, of order $p$. The conjugates of the class of $\alpha$ are the $p^{a-1}$-th powers of those of the class of $\theta$, so there are finitely many. We show $\alpha=1$, a contradiction.

*Step 1: $\alpha$ is trivial on $\Gamma$.* By the Theorem of the degree-two entry there is $g\in G$ such that $\mathrm{inn}(g)\circ\alpha$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$. So $\alpha$ induces on $\Gamma$ conjugation by the image $\gamma$ of $g^{-1}$. As $\alpha^p=1$, conjugation by $\gamma^p$ is the identity on $\Gamma$, so $\gamma^p$ is central in $\Gamma$, hence trivial, because $\Gamma$ has trivial centre (part 3 of the units entry). $\Gamma$ is torsion-free: it is isomorphic to the image of $\mathcal G$ in $G$, a closed subgroup of $G$, and $G$ is torsion-free (Setting of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md)). So $\gamma=1$, and $\alpha$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$.

So $A=\bar\alpha$ is $\Lambda$-linear, and $A^p=1$. By Step 1 of the proof in the degree-two entry, $A$ maps $L$ onto itself, so $A(\bar x_0)=\epsilon\bar x_0$ with $\epsilon\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ and $\epsilon^p=1$. As $\mathbb F_p^\times$ has order $p-1$, $\epsilon=1$. For $j=1,2$ write
$$A(\bar x_j)=c_j\bar x_0+a_{j1}\bar x_1+a_{j2}\bar x_2\qquad(c_j\in\mathbb F_p,\ a_{ji}\in\Lambda).$$
These are the coefficients $\mu_{j0},\mu_{j1},\mu_{j2}$ of the degree-two entry for the automorphism $\alpha$, whose tame twist $\beta$ is the identity. By $\Lambda$-linearity, $A(\lambda\bar x_j)=\omega(\lambda)c_j\bar x_0+\lambda a_{j1}\bar x_1+\lambda a_{j2}\bar x_2$.

*Step 2: $a_{21}=0$, $a_{12}=0$ and $a_{22}=a_{11}$.* Step 2 of the proof in the degree-two entry uses only that the class of the automorphism has finitely many conjugates. Applied to $\alpha$, it gives $a_{21}=0$, and it shows that $a_{11}$ is a unit of $\Lambda$. For $k\in\mathbb Z$, the automorphism $\alpha_k=T_k\alpha T_k^{-1}$ has class conjugate to that of $\alpha$, so its class has the same finitely many conjugates. It induces the identity on $\Gamma$, so $\bar\alpha_k=\bar T_kA\bar T_{-k}$ is $\Lambda$-linear. Step 2 of the degree-two entry, applied to $\alpha_k$, says that the $\bar x_1$-coordinate of $\bar\alpha_k(\bar x_2)$ is $0$. Now
$$A(\bar T_{-k}\bar x_2)=A(\bar x_2-k\bar x_1)=(c_2-kc_1)\bar x_0-ka_{11}\bar x_1+(a_{22}-ka_{12})\bar x_2,$$
using $a_{21}=0$, and applying $\bar T_k$ gives
$$\bar\alpha_k(\bar x_2)=(c_2-kc_1)\bar x_0+\big(k(a_{22}-a_{11})-k^2a_{12}\big)\bar x_1+(a_{22}-ka_{12})\bar x_2.$$
So $k(a_{22}-a_{11})-k^2a_{12}=0$ for every $k$. Adding the equations for $k=1$ and $k=-1$ gives $2a_{12}=0$, and subtracting them gives $2(a_{22}-a_{11})=0$. As $p$ is odd, $a_{12}=0$ and $a_{22}=a_{11}$.

*Step 3: $a_{11}=1$.* As the tame twist of $\alpha$ is the identity, the twisted commutation (T) of Step 3 of the degree-two entry reads $a_{11}z^{p^b}=z^{p^b}a_{11}$ for every $z\in\Lambda$, for some $b\ge0$. By Lemma 4 (a) of the surface-generator entry, every element of $\Lambda_I$ is the $p^b$-th power of an element of $\Lambda_I$. So $a_{11}$ commutes with every element of $\Lambda_I$. Next, $A(\bar x_1)=c_1\bar x_0+a_{11}\bar x_1$ by Step 2. As $A(L)=L$ and $A(\lambda\bar x_1)\in L+\lambda a_{11}\bar x_1$, induction gives $A^n(\bar x_1)\in L+a_{11}^n\bar x_1$ for $n\ge1$. Since $A^p=1$ and $\Lambda\bar x_1$ is free, $a_{11}^p=1$. Put $z=a_{11}-1$. It commutes with $\Lambda_I$, and $z^p=a_{11}^p-1=0$, as $\Lambda$ has characteristic $p$. Lemma 3 of the surface-generator entry gives $z=0$. So $a_{11}=a_{22}=1$.

Thus $A(\bar x_0)=\bar x_0$, $A(\bar x_1)=\bar x_1+c_1\bar x_0$ and $A(\bar x_2)=\bar x_2+c_2\bar x_0$. By $\Lambda$-linearity, $(A-1)(c\bar x_0+\lambda_1\bar x_1+\lambda_2\bar x_2)=(\omega(\lambda_1)c_1+\omega(\lambda_2)c_2)\bar x_0$. So $(A-1)\bar V\subseteq L$, and $A-1$ kills $L$.

*Step 4: the shear vanishes.* Let $E=K(\mu_p)$, and let $E(p)$ be its maximal pro-$p$ extension. Part 1 of the Theorem of the even-degree entry, with $n=1$, so that $K_1=K$ and $L_1=E$, applies to $\alpha$: its order is a power of $p$ and its class has finitely many conjugates. So $\alpha$ maps $G_{E(p)}$ onto itself and $\alpha(g)\in gG_{E(p)}$ for every $g\in G$.

The extension $E/K$ has degree dividing $p-1$, so it is tamely ramified, and $G_E\supseteq P_K$. Its image in $\Gamma$ is $\Gamma_E=\ker(\omega|_\Gamma)$. Let $\mathcal G_E\subseteq G$ be the image of the elements of $\mathcal G$ that map into $\Gamma_E$. As the image of $\mathcal G$ is a complement to $P_K$ in $G$, every element of $G_E$ is uniquely $v\gamma$ with $v\in P_K$ and $\gamma\in\mathcal G_E$. Define $\chi:G_E\to\mathbb F_p$ by $\chi(v\gamma)=\pi(\bar v)$. It is continuous. It is a homomorphism: $(v\gamma)(v'\gamma')=(v\cdot\gamma v'\gamma^{-1})(\gamma\gamma')$, and $\pi$ of the class of $v\cdot\gamma v'\gamma^{-1}$ is $\pi(\bar v)+\omega(\gamma)\pi(\bar v')=\pi(\bar v)+\pi(\bar v')$, as $\omega(\gamma)=1$. It is surjective, as $\chi(x_0)=1$. So its kernel is an open normal subgroup of $G_E$ of index $p$, and it contains $G_{E(p)}$, the intersection of the open normal subgroups of $G_E$ of $p$-power index.

Let $v\in P_K$. Then $\alpha(v)\in P_K$ and $\alpha(v)\in vG_{E(p)}$, so $\chi(\alpha(v))=\chi(v)$, that is, $\pi(A\bar v)=\pi(\bar v)$. So $\pi((A-1)\bar v)=0$. By Step 3, $(A-1)\bar v\in L$, on which $\pi$ is injective. Hence $(A-1)\bar v=0$. So $A=1$: $\alpha$ induces the identity on $P_K/P_K^{(2)}$. As $\alpha$ has finite order, part 2 of [detection on the Frattini quotient](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-on-the-frattini-quotient-of-wild-inertia.md) gives $\alpha=1$. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** (1) Let $f\in\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ have order $p^k$ with $k\ge1$, and suppose it has finitely many conjugates. Then $f_1=f^{p^{k-1}}$ has order $p$, and its conjugates are the $p^{k-1}$-th powers of those of $f$, so there are finitely many. As $p\nmid2=[K:\mathbb Q_p]$, part 1 of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) shows that $f_1$ is the class of an automorphism $\theta$ of $G_K$ of order $p$. Its class has finitely many conjugates, so $\theta=1$ by the Theorem, and $f_1=1$, a contradiction. If a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ had order divisible by $p$, it would contain an element of order $p$ by Cauchy's theorem, all of whose conjugates lie in it. This has just been excluded.

(2) By part 2 (a) of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), $NG_K/G_K$ is a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$, isomorphic to $N/M$, and $(N:M)=(G_K:M)$. By part 1 its order is prime to $p$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* The argument follows the outline proposed in [research issue 614](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/614). In degree $2$ there is no surface pair, so the mixing automorphisms used for degree at least $3$ in [the analogous entry](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups-have-order-prime-to-p.md) are not available. The transvections $T_k$ preserve the relator exactly. Conjugating by them turns the single vanishing $\mu_{21}=0$ of the degree-two entry into the vanishing of $a_{12}$ and the equality $a_{22}=a_{11}$. Steps 1 to 3 leave a possible shear $\bar x_j\mapsto\bar x_j+c_j\bar x_0$ into the cyclotomic line $L$. Step 4 detects it with a character of $G_{K(\mu_p)}$ that is injective on $L$.
- *The case $p\ge5$.* After Step 3, $(A-1)^2=0$, and $p-2\ge2$. So part 1 of [detection on the Frattini quotient](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-on-the-frattini-quotient-of-wild-inertia.md) already gives $\alpha=1$, without Step 4. For $p=3$ that part needs $A=1$, which is what Step 4 supplies.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$ and every quadratic $K/\mathbb Q_p$, ramified or unramified, with every $s$. This includes $K=\mathbb Q_3(\mu_3)$, where $E=K$ and $\Gamma_E=\Gamma$. With the corollary of [finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md) for degree at least $3$, part 1 of the Corollary holds for every odd $p$ and every $K$ of degree at least $2$.
- *What remains.* In degree $2$, by Corollary (b) of the degree-two entry, a nontrivial finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ would have to come from automorphisms of finite order prime to $p$ that are trivial on $\Gamma$. These are not treated here, and neither are classes of infinite order. Also not covered are $K=\mathbb Q_p$, where there is no second complementary generator and the twisted commutation (T) used in Step 3 is not recorded, and $p=2$.
- *Sources.* Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
