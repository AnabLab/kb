**Setting.** Use the Setting and notation of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), with $d=[K:\mathbb Q_p]$ even. So $p$ is odd and $G=G_K=F/(\varrho)$ with $F=P\rtimes\mathcal G$, where $\mathcal G$ is generated by $\sigma,\tau$ and $P$ is normally generated by $x_0,\dots,x_d$. Any choice of $w_0,\dots,w_d\in P$ gives a unique endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$ and maps $x_i$ to $w_i$. Wild inertia $P_K$ is the image of $P$, and $\mathcal G$ maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma=G/P_K$. Also $p^s$ is the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, and $P^{p,i,s}$, $\Lambda_s$, $\omega_s$, $\lambda\mapsto\lambda^*$ and $u_s\in\Lambda_s$ are as there. For even $d$,
$$\varrho=A(x_0)\,x_1^{p^s}\,[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d],\qquad A(X)=(X^\sigma)^{-1}(X,\tau)^\kappa,$$
where $(X,\tau)$ is given by the formula there for $X$ in $P$ or in a quotient of $P$ by a closed normal subgroup of $F$. Put $r=d/2-1$ and $(a_i,b_i)=(x_{2i+1},x_{2i+2})$ for $1\le i\le r$, the surface pairs.

As in the proof of part 2 there, put $N_s=P/P^{p,2,s}$ and $M_s=P^{p,2,s}/P^{p,3,s}$, written additively, and write $\tilde x$ for the class of $x\in P$ in $N_s$. Both are compact $\Lambda_s$-modules under conjugation by $\mathcal G$. The group $Q=P/P^{p,3,s}$ has class at most $2$, and $M_s$ is central in it. The commutator induces a continuous biadditive map $b:N_s\times N_s\to M_s$, $b(\tilde x,\tilde y)=[x,y]P^{p,3,s}$, with $b(\rho\tilde x,\rho\tilde y)=\rho\,b(\tilde x,\tilde y)$ and $b(\tilde y,\tilde x)=-b(\tilde x,\tilde y)$. Let $H=P_K/[P_K,P_K]$, written additively, and $V=H/pH=P_K/P_K^{(2)}$, called $\bar V$ there. As $P_K^{p,2,s}=P_K^{p^s}[P_K,P_K]$, $P_K/P_K^{p,2,s}=H/p^sH$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=d$ even, and $c,c_1,\dots,c_r\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$. There is an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\theta(\rho)\in\rho P_K^{p,2,s}$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$ and
$$\theta(x_0)\in x_0^cP_K^{p,2,s},\quad\theta(x_1)\in x_1^cP_K^{p,2,s},\quad\theta(x_2)\in x_2P_K^{p,2,s},\quad\theta(a_i)\in a_i^{c_i}P_K^{p,2,s},\quad\theta(b_i)\in b_i^{c/c_i}P_K^{p,2,s}$$
for $1\le i\le r$. So $\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$, and it acts $\Lambda_s$-linearly on $H/p^sH$, multiplying the classes of $x_0,x_1,x_2,a_i,b_i$ by $c,c,1,c_i,c/c_i$. On $V$ it acts $\Lambda$-linearly, multiplying $\bar x_0,\bar x_1,\bar x_2,\bar a_i,\bar b_i$ by the residues of these numbers. If $c\not\equiv1$ or some $c_i\not\equiv1$ modulo $p$, then $\theta$ is not inner. More generally, $\theta$ is not inner unless $c\equiv c_1\equiv\cdots\equiv c_r\equiv1\pmod{p^s}$.

**Proof.** *1. Homogeneity of $A$.* Let $X\in Q$, and let $R\subseteq Q$ be the closed subgroup generated by the conjugates $\rho X\rho^{-1}$, $\rho\in\mathcal G$, in $F/P^{p,3,s}$. Let $C_X\subseteq M_s$ be the closed subgroup generated by the elements $b(\lambda\tilde X,\mu\tilde X)$, $\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_s$, where $\tilde X$ is the image of $X$ in $N_s$. *Claim:* $A(X)$ and $A(X^c)$ lie in $R$, and $A(X^c)A(X)^{-c}\in C_X$.

$R$ is normalized by $\mathcal G$. Put $Y=X^{h^{p-1}}\tau X^{h^{p-2}}\tau\cdots X^h\tau$, so that $(X,\tau)=Y^{\pi/(p-1)}$. Moving each $\tau$ to the right gives $Y=y\,\tau^{p-1}$ with $y\in R$, and then $Y^n=y_n\tau^{(p-1)n}$ with $y_n\in R$ for $n\ge1$. Choose integers $n_k\to\pi/(p-1)$ in $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Then $Y^{n_k}\to(X,\tau)$ and $\tau^{(p-1)n_k}\to\tau^\pi=1$, as $\tau$ has order prime to $p$. So $y_{n_k}\to(X,\tau)$, and $(X,\tau)\in R$ because $R$ is closed. Also $X^\sigma\in R$, so $A(X)\in R$. The same holds for $X^c$, as the conjugates of $X^c$ lie in $R$.

The closure $\bar R$ of $[R,R]$ is normal in the compact group $R\mathcal G$, and $R\cap\mathcal G=1$, as $R\subseteq Q$. So $E=R\mathcal G/\bar R$ is the semidirect product of $R^{\mathrm{ab}}=R/\bar R$, written additively, by $\mathcal G$. Write its elements as pairs $(a,g)$, with $(a,g)(a',g')=(a+g\cdot a',gg')$. Let $\xi$ be the image of $X$. Then $X^k$ maps to $(k\xi,1)$ for $k\in\mathbb Z_p$. So $Y$ maps to $(\beta\xi,t)$ with $t=\tau^{p-1}$ and $\beta=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}h^j\tau^{p-1-j}$, and $Y^n$ maps to $(S_n\beta\xi,t^n)$ with $S_n=\sum_{i<n}t^i$. The first coordinate $E\to R^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is continuous, so $(X,\tau)$ maps to $(\ell,1)$ with $\ell=\lim_kS_{n_k}\beta\xi$. With $X^c$ in place of $X$, every first coordinate is multiplied by $c$, so $(X^c,\tau)$ maps to $(c\ell,1)$. Hence $A(X)$ maps to $(\kappa\ell-\sigma\xi,1)$, and $A(X^c)$ maps to $c$ times this. So $A(X^c)A(X)^{-c}\in\bar R$.

As $Q$ has class at most $2$, $[z,z']=b(\tilde z,\tilde z')$ for $z,z'\in R$. The image of $R$ in $N_s$ is the closed subgroup generated by the $\rho\tilde X$, which lies in $\Lambda_s\tilde X$. So $[R,R]\subseteq C_X$, and $\bar R\subseteq C_X$ as $C_X$ is closed.

*2. Absorbing $C_{x_0}$.* Let $B=\{b(u_s\tilde x_0,\tilde y):\tilde y\in N_s\}$, the image of the compact group $N_s$ under a continuous homomorphism. Then $u_sM_s+B$ is a closed subgroup of $M_s$. *Claim:* $C_X\subseteq u_sM_s+B$ for $X=x_0P^{p,3,s}$.

Let $\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_s$. By Lemma (c) there, with $r=s$, $N=N_s$ and $M=M_s$, $b(\lambda\tilde x_0,\mu\tilde x_0)\equiv b(\tilde x_0,\nu\tilde x_0)$ modulo $u_sM_s$, with $\nu=\lambda^*\mu$. Write $\nu=\omega_s(\nu)\cdot1+\nu_1$ with $\nu_1\in\ker\omega_s$. By Lemma (b) there, $\ker\omega_s=\Lambda_su_s$, so $\nu_1=\mu_1u_s$ for some $\mu_1\in\Lambda_s$. As $b$ is $\mathbb Z/p^s$-bilinear, $b(\tilde x_0,\omega_s(\nu)\tilde x_0)=\omega_s(\nu)b(\tilde x_0,\tilde x_0)=0$. By antisymmetry and Lemma (c), used twice and with $(\mu_1u_s)^*=u_s^*\mu_1^*$,
$$b(\tilde x_0,\mu_1u_s\tilde x_0)=-b(\mu_1u_s\tilde x_0,\tilde x_0)\equiv-b(\tilde x_0,u_s^*\mu_1^*\tilde x_0)\equiv-b(u_s\tilde x_0,\mu_1^*\tilde x_0)\pmod{u_sM_s}.$$
So every generator of $C_X$ lies in the closed subgroup $u_sM_s+B$.

*3. The endomorphism $\psi$.* Let $X=x_0P^{p,3,s}$. By steps 1 and 2, $A(X^c)A(X)^{-c}=u_sm+b(u_s\tilde x_0,\tilde y)$ for some $m\in M_s$ and $\tilde y\in N_s$. Choose $z\in P^{p,2,s}$ with class $-m$, and $y\in P$ with class $\tilde y$. Let $\psi$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$ and maps
$$x_0\mapsto x_0^cz,\quad x_1\mapsto x_1^c,\quad x_2\mapsto x_2,\quad a_i\mapsto a_i^{c_i},\quad b_i\mapsto b_i^{c/c_i}\quad(1\le i\le r).$$

*$\psi(\varrho)\in(\varrho)P^{p,3,s}$.* Work modulo $P^{p,3,s}$, and let $m_\varrho=x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2]\prod_i[a_i,b_i]$, an element of $P^{p,2,s}$, so of $M_s$. In the class-$2$ group $Q$, $[x^k,x'^l]=[x,x']^{kl}$ for integers $k,l$, and by continuity for $k,l\in\mathbb Z_p$. So $\psi(m_\varrho)=(x_1^{p^s})^c[x_1,x_2]^c\prod_i[a_i,b_i]^c=m_\varrho^c$, as these factors lie in the abelian group $M_s$. As $\psi$ is continuous and fixes $\sigma,\tau$, $\psi(A(x_0))=A(\psi(x_0))$. By the Computation there, with $r=s$, $N=P^{p,3,s}$, $A=M_s$, $X^c$ in place of $X$ and $a$ the class of $z$, $A(X^cz)=A(X^c)\cdot(-u_sm)$. As $m_\varrho$ is central and $Q$ is pro-$p$, $\varrho^c=A(X)^cm_\varrho^c$. So, writing $M_s$ additively,
$$\psi(\varrho)\varrho^{-c}=A(X^c)A(X)^{-c}-u_sm=b(u_s\tilde x_0,\tilde y).$$
The class of $\varrho$ in $N_s$ is $u_s\tilde x_0$: $m_\varrho\in P^{p,2,s}$, and by the Computation with $r=s$, $N=P^{p,2,s}$, $A=Q=N_s$, $X=1$ and $a=\tilde x_0$, the class of $A(x_0)$ is $u_s\tilde x_0$, as $(1,\tau)=\tau^\pi=1$ (proof of part 1 there). So $b(u_s\tilde x_0,\tilde y)$ is the class of $[\varrho,y]$, and $\psi(\varrho)\equiv[\varrho,y]\varrho^c$ modulo $P^{p,3,s}$, with $[\varrho,y]$ and $\varrho^c$ in $(\varrho)$.

*4. The automorphism.* As in the last two steps of the proof of part 2 there, $\psi(P)\subseteq P$, $\psi(P^{p,3,s})\subseteq P^{p,3,s}$, and $\psi$ maps $(\varrho)P^{p,3,s}$ into itself. So it induces an endomorphism $\psi_3$ of $G/P_K^{p,3,s}=F/(\varrho)P^{p,3,s}$. The image of $\psi(P)$ in $\bar P=P/P^{(2)}$ contains $\bar c\bar x_0$, as $z\in P^{p,2,s}\subseteq P^{(2)}$, and $\bar c\bar x_1$, $\bar x_2$, $\bar c_i\bar a_i$ and $\overline{c/c_i}\,\bar b_i$, where bars denote residues. These residues are nonzero, so by part 1 there these elements generate $\bar P$ as a $\Lambda$-module. Hence $\psi(P)P^{(2)}=P$, so $\psi(P)=P$, and $\psi_3$ is onto. As there, it is an automorphism of the topologically finitely generated group $G/P_K^{p,3,s}$, and it induces the identity on $\Gamma$. By [Wingberg's lifting theorem for the $p^s$-central series](wingberg-lifting-for-the-p-s-central-series-of-wild-inertia.md), some automorphism $\theta$ of $G$, inducing the identity on $\Gamma$, induces the same automorphism of $G/P_K^{p,2,s}$ as $\psi_3$. As $\psi$ fixes $\mathcal G$ and $z\in P^{p,2,s}$, $\theta$ has the stated properties.

*5. The actions.* For $\tilde\rho\in G$ over $\rho\in\Gamma$, $\theta(\tilde\rho)\in\tilde\rho P_K$ and $P_K$ acts trivially on $H$, so $\theta$ commutes with the action of $\Gamma$ on $H/p^sH$; being continuous, it is $\Lambda_s$-linear. The class of $x^k$ is $k$ times the class of $x$, which gives the action on $H/p^sH$, and reduction modulo $p$ gives the action on $V$. If $\bar c\ne1$, then $\bar c\bar x_1\ne\bar x_1$, and if $\bar c=1$ but $\bar c_i\ne1$, then $\bar c_i\bar a_i\ne\bar a_i$, as $\Lambda\bar x_1$ and $\Lambda\bar a_i$ are free (part 1 there). An inner automorphism inducing the identity on $\Gamma$ is conjugation by some $g$ with $gP_K$ central in $\Gamma$, so $g\in P_K$ by part 3 there, and then it acts trivially on $H$. So $\theta$ is not inner in that case. For the general statement, $H/p^sH=N_s/\Lambda_su_s\tilde x_0$, as $P_K=P/(\varrho)$ and the class of $\varrho$ is $u_s\tilde x_0$. The proof of part 1 there with $\mathbb Z/p^s$ in place of $\mathbb F_p$ shows that $N_s$ is free over $\Lambda_s$ on the $\tilde x_i$, and $\Lambda_s/\Lambda_su_s=\Lambda_s/\ker\omega_s\cong\mathbb Z/p^s$. So $H/p^sH\cong\mathbb Z/p^s\oplus\Lambda_s^d$ on the classes of $x_0,x_1,\dots,x_d$, and $\theta$ acts trivially on it only if all of $c$, $c_i$ and $c/c_i$ are $1$ modulo $p^s$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Constant units.* For $c=1$, $\theta$ multiplies the $i$th pair by $c_i$ and $c_i^{-1}$ modulo $P_K^{p,2,s}$. Modulo $P_K^{(2)}$, and for the first pair, this is the action of $\theta_\lambda$ of part 2 there for the constant unit $\lambda=\bar c_1$, since $\lambda^*=\lambda$ for constant $\lambda$. The new information is the control modulo $P_K^{p,2,s}$, which for $s\ge2$ sees $H/p^2H$. [Weight automorphisms](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-the-pth-lower-central-term-of-wild-inertia-in-even-degree.md) use exactly this.
- *The cyclotomic line.* On $L=\Lambda\bar x_0\cong\mathbb F_p$, on which $\Gamma$ acts through $\omega$, $\theta$ acts by $\bar c$, which can be any element of $\mathbb F_p^\times$. Modulo $L$ it acts by $\bar c$ on $\Lambda\bar x_1$ and by $1$ on $\Lambda\bar x_2$, so not by a central unit when $\bar c\ne1$. This is consistent with [unitarity of central scalars](unitarity-of-central-scalars-on-wild-frattini.md), which concerns central actions. Compare [central actions moving the cyclotomic line](nontrivial-cyclotomic-line-actions-on-wild-frattini.md), which realizes the nonidentity squares by finite-order automorphisms acting centrally modulo $L$, for $p\ge5$.
- *Even degree.* The relator is multiplied by $c$ because $x_0$ and $x_1$ enter it to first order, through $A(x_0)$ and $x_1^{p^s}$, while $x_2$ is fixed and absorbs the scaling in $[x_1,x_2]$. For odd $d$ the relator is $A(x_0)x_1^{p^s}[x_1,y_1][x_2,x_3]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d]$, with $y_1$ in the closed subgroup generated by conjugates of $x_1$ ([mixing automorphisms](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), part 1). There $[x_1,y_1]$ is quadratic in $x_1$, and step 3 does not apply. Whether such automorphisms exist for odd $d$ is not decided here.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, every even $d$, every $s\ge1$, all $c,c_i\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$. Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
