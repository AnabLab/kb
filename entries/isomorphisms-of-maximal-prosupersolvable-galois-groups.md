**Setting.** Notation is as in [centre and automorphisms of the maximal prosupersolvable Galois group](automorphisms-of-maximal-prosupersolvable-galois-groups.md). Fix $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. For a number field $K \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ and a prime $\ell$:

- $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ is the maximal prosupersolvable extension of $K$, and $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}} = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ss}}/K)$.
- $E_\ell(K)$ is the maximal abelian extension of $K$ of exponent dividing $\ell - 1$, so $E_2(K) = K$.
- $\Omega_\ell(K)$ is the maximal pro-$\ell$ extension of $E_\ell(K)$, and $\Gamma_\ell(K) = \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K)/K)$.
- $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell)$ is the maximal abelian pro-$\ell$ extension of $K$.
- $A_\ell(K) = \Omega_\ell(K) \cap K^{\mathrm{ab}}$. It is Galois over $K$ with abelian group.

All these fields are determined by $K$ inside $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. So an automorphism of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ that maps $K_1$ onto $K_2$ maps each of them for $K_1$ onto the corresponding one for $K_2$. An *isomorphism of extensions* $\Omega_\ell(K_1)/K_1 \to \Omega_\ell(K_2)/K_2$ is a field isomorphism $\tau$ with $\tau(K_1) = K_2$. It induces $x \mapsto \tau x \tau^{-1}$ from $\Gamma_\ell(K_1)$ to $\Gamma_\ell(K_2)$, and similarly for isomorphisms $K_1^{\mathrm{ss}} \to K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $K, K_1, K_2$ be number fields.

1. *Fibre product.* $A_\ell(K) = K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell) \, E_\ell(K)$. Restriction identifies $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ with the group of families $(x_\ell)_\ell \in \prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K)$ for which some $a \in \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K)$ satisfies $x_\ell|_{A_\ell(K)} = a|_{A_\ell(K)}$ for every $\ell$.
2. *Isomorphisms.* Let $\mathcal{T}(K_1, K_2)$ be the set of families $(\tau_\ell)_\ell$ of isomorphisms of extensions $\tau_\ell : \Omega_\ell(K_1)/K_1 \to \Omega_\ell(K_2)/K_2$ whose restrictions to $K_1$ are all equal. For such a family, $(x_\ell)_\ell \mapsto (\tau_\ell x_\ell \tau_\ell^{-1})_\ell$ maps $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$ onto $G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{ss}}$, in the description of part 1. This gives a bijection from $\mathcal{T}(K_1, K_2)$ to the set of isomorphisms $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}} \to G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{ss}}$, compatible with composition.
3. *Which isomorphisms come from fields.* Let $\alpha : G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}} \to G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{ss}}$ correspond to $(\sigma_\ell)_\ell$. Then $\alpha$ is induced by a field isomorphism $K_1^{\mathrm{ss}} \to K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}$ if and only if some isomorphism $s : K_1^{\mathrm{ab}} \to K_2^{\mathrm{ab}}$ satisfies $s|_{A_\ell(K_1)} = \sigma_\ell|_{A_\ell(K_1)}$ for every $\ell$.
4. *Outer automorphisms.* $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}) \cong \bigl(\prod_\ell \mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell(K)/K)\bigr) \rtimes \mathrm{Aut}(K)$. Here $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$ acts on each $\mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell(K)/K)$ by conjugation with any extension to $E_\ell(K)$. The classes of automorphisms induced by field automorphisms of $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ form exactly the complement $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$.
   - By part 2, every $u = (u_\ell)_\ell \in \prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K)$ acts on $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ by conjugation.
   - Let $a \in \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K)$ be the element with $a|_{K^{\mathrm{ab}}(m)} = u_m|_{K^{\mathrm{ab}}(m)}$ for every prime $m$. Then the class of conjugation by $u$ in the normal subgroup is $\bigl(u_\ell|_{E_\ell(K)} \cdot (a|_{E_\ell(K)})^{-1}\bigr)_\ell$.

For odd $\ell$ the group $\mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell(K)/K)$ is nontrivial, since $E_\ell(K)$ contains every quadratic extension of $K$. So the normal subgroup is a product of infinitely many nontrivial groups, and $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}})$ is uncountable for every $K$. For $K = \mathbb{Q}$, $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ss}}) \cong \prod_\ell \mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell(\mathbb{Q})/\mathbb{Q})$ is abelian.

**Comparison with the earlier entry.** [Part 4 of the earlier entry](automorphisms-of-maximal-prosupersolvable-galois-groups.md) conjugates only the factor $\Gamma_2(K)$ by some $g$, that is, $u = (g, 1, 1, \dots)$. By the formula in part 4 here, its class is $\bigl((a|_{E_\ell(K)})^{-1}\bigr)_\ell$, where $a$ acts as $g$ on $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)$ and trivially on $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(m)$ for odd $m$. That is trivial exactly when $g$ is trivial on $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)$, because $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)$ lies in the compositum of the $E_\ell(K)$ for odd $\ell$, as Step 9 there shows. This recovers the embedding $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)/K) \to \mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}})$ found there, and part 4 here shows that it is far from all of $\mathrm{Out}$. Conjugating one factor $\Gamma_\ell(K)$ for odd $\ell$ also gives outer automorphisms.

For [the prosupersolvable problem](prosupersolvable-neukirch-uchida-problem.md), the theorem says exactly how far functoriality fails. The isomorphisms between the quotients are as rigid as possible on each $\Omega_\ell$. But the field isomorphisms $\sigma_\ell$ only have to agree on $K_1$, while a field isomorphism of $K_1^{\mathrm{ss}}$ would make them agree on all of $K_1^{\mathrm{ab}}$.

**Proof.** The Galois theory of composita is used without a source, in two forms. For a Galois extension $L/K$ and an algebraic extension $F/K$, restriction is an isomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(LF/F) \to \mathrm{Gal}(L/L \cap F)$. An isomorphism between subfields of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ extends to an automorphism of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. Two cited inputs come from entries in the graph.

- (I1) $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ is the compositum of the fields $\Omega_\ell(K)$. A finite Galois extension $M/K$ lies in $\Omega_\ell(K)$ if and only if $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)$ has a normal Sylow $\ell$-subgroup $P$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)/P$ abelian of exponent dividing $\ell - 1$. This is part 1 of [the earlier entry](automorphisms-of-maximal-prosupersolvable-galois-groups.md).
- (I2) Let $\alpha : G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}} \to G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{ss}}$ be an isomorphism. For every $\ell$ there is an isomorphism of extensions $\sigma_\ell : \Omega_\ell(K_1)/K_1 \to \Omega_\ell(K_2)/K_2$ with $\alpha(g)|_{\Omega_\ell(K_2)} = \sigma_\ell \, g|_{\Omega_\ell(K_1)} \, \sigma_\ell^{-1}$ for all $g$. The restrictions $\sigma_\ell|_{K_1}$ do not depend on $\ell$. This is parts 1–3 of [the prosupersolvable quotient determines a number field](prosupersolvable-quotient-determines-number-field.md).

*Step 1: the fields $A_\ell(K)$.* Write $\Omega_\ell, E_\ell, A_\ell$ for $\Omega_\ell(K), E_\ell(K), A_\ell(K)$.

- *$K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell) E_\ell \subseteq A_\ell$.* $E_\ell$ is abelian over $K$ and lies in $\Omega_\ell$. $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell) E_\ell / E_\ell$ is a pro-$\ell$ extension, so $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell) \subseteq \Omega_\ell$.
- *$A_\ell \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell) E_\ell$.* Let $M \subseteq A_\ell$ be finite over $K$, with abelian group $Q = Q_\ell \times Q_{\ell'}$, where $Q_\ell$ is the Sylow $\ell$-subgroup. By (I1), $Q/Q_\ell \cong Q_{\ell'}$ has exponent dividing $\ell - 1$. So the fixed field of $Q_\ell$ lies in $E_\ell$, and the fixed field of $Q_{\ell'}$, of $\ell$-power degree, lies in $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell)$. As $Q_\ell \cap Q_{\ell'} = 1$, $M$ is their compositum.
- *Products.* A finite subextension of $E_\ell/K$ has degree whose prime factors divide $\ell - 1$, so $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell) \cap E_\ell = K$. Hence restriction is an isomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(A_\ell/K) \to \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell)/K) \times \mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell/K)$. Likewise $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the compositum of the $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(m)$, since a finite abelian group is the product of its Sylow subgroups. Finite subextensions of different $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(m)$ have coprime degrees, so restriction is an isomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K) \to \prod_m \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(m)/K)$.

*Step 2: $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ss}}/K^{\mathrm{ab}}) \to \prod_\ell \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K^{\mathrm{ab}})$ is an isomorphism.*

- *Each factor is pro-$\ell$.* $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K^{\mathrm{ab}}) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell/A_\ell)$, which is a closed subgroup of the pro-$\ell$ group $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell/E_\ell)$, since $E_\ell \subseteq A_\ell$.
- *Injective.* $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ is the compositum of the $\Omega_\ell K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, by (I1).
- *Surjective.* Let $S$ be a finite set of primes, and for $\ell \in S$ let $L_\ell \subseteq \Omega_\ell K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ be finite Galois over $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$. The degrees $[L_\ell : K^{\mathrm{ab}}]$ are powers of distinct primes, and each divides the degree of the compositum $L$ of the $L_\ell$. So $[L : K^{\mathrm{ab}}] \ge \prod_{\ell \in S} [L_\ell : K^{\mathrm{ab}}]$, and the injection $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K^{\mathrm{ab}}) \to \prod_{\ell \in S} \mathrm{Gal}(L_\ell/K^{\mathrm{ab}})$ is bijective. Hence the image is dense, and it is closed by compactness.

*Step 3: part 1.* Restriction $r : G_K^{\mathrm{ss}} \to \prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K)$ is injective by (I1). Let $F$ be the set of families described in part 1. Clearly $r(g) \in F$, with $a = g|_{K^{\mathrm{ab}}}$. Conversely let $(x_\ell)_\ell \in F$, with $a$ as in part 1.

- Choose $g_0 \in G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ with $g_0|_{K^{\mathrm{ab}}} = a$. Then $y_\ell = (g_0|_{\Omega_\ell})^{-1} x_\ell$ is trivial on $A_\ell = \Omega_\ell \cap K^{\mathrm{ab}}$.
- By Step 2 and the isomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K^{\mathrm{ab}}) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell/A_\ell)$, some $h \in \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ss}}/K^{\mathrm{ab}})$ has $h|_{\Omega_\ell} = y_\ell$ for every $\ell$.
- Then $r(g_0 h) = (x_\ell)_\ell$.

So $r(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}) = F$. From now on we identify $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ with $F$.

*Step 4: part 2.* Let $(\tau_\ell)_\ell \in \mathcal{T}(K_1, K_2)$ with common restriction $t : K_1 \to K_2$, and write $\Phi(\tau)$ for the map $(x_\ell)_\ell \mapsto (\tau_\ell x_\ell \tau_\ell^{-1})_\ell$. It is an isomorphism of profinite groups $\prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K_1) \to \prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K_2)$.

- *$\Phi(\tau)$ maps $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$ into $G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{ss}}$.* Extend $t$ to an automorphism $T$ of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. Then $u_\ell = T^{-1}\tau_\ell$ is an automorphism of $\Omega_\ell(K_1)$ fixing $K_1$, so $u_\ell \in \Gamma_\ell(K_1)$. Let $(x_\ell)_\ell \in G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$, with $a$ as in part 1. As $\mathrm{Gal}(A_\ell(K_1)/K_1)$ is abelian, $u_\ell x_\ell u_\ell^{-1}$ and $x_\ell$ agree on $A_\ell(K_1)$. So $\tau_\ell x_\ell \tau_\ell^{-1} = T (u_\ell x_\ell u_\ell^{-1}) T^{-1}$ agrees on $A_\ell(K_2) = T(A_\ell(K_1))$ with $T a T^{-1}$. This is an element of $\mathrm{Gal}(K_2^{\mathrm{ab}}/K_2)$ that does not depend on $\ell$.
- *Onto.* The family $(\tau_\ell^{-1})_\ell \in \mathcal{T}(K_2, K_1)$ gives the inverse map. Clearly $\Phi(\tau') \circ \Phi(\tau) = \Phi(\tau'\tau)$ when the composite is defined.
- *Every isomorphism arises.* Let $\alpha$ be an isomorphism, and take $(\sigma_\ell)_\ell \in \mathcal{T}(K_1, K_2)$ from (I2). The formula in (I2) says that $\Phi(\sigma)$ and $\alpha$ have the same restriction to every $\Omega_\ell(K_2)$, so $\Phi(\sigma) = \alpha$ by part 1.
- *Injective.* Suppose $\Phi(\tau) = \Phi(\tau')$. Since $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}} \to \Gamma_\ell(K_1)$ is surjective, $\tau_\ell$ and $\tau'_\ell$ induce the same map $\Gamma_\ell(K_1) \to \Gamma_\ell(K_2)$. So $w = \tau_\ell^{-1}\tau'_\ell$ is an automorphism of $\Omega_\ell(K_1)$ with $w(K_1) = K_1$ that commutes with $\Gamma_\ell(K_1)$. Hence $w$ maps the fixed field of each closed subgroup $U \subseteq \Gamma_\ell(K_1)$ to the fixed field of $w U w^{-1} = U$.
  - Every quadratic extension $K_1(\sqrt b)$ lies in $\Omega_\ell(K_1)$: for $\ell = 2$ because $\Omega_2(K_1)$ is the maximal pro-$2$ extension, and for odd $\ell$ because $2 \mid \ell - 1$. So $K_1(\sqrt{w(b)}) = K_1(\sqrt b)$, that is, $w(b)/b \in K_1^{\times 2}$, for all $b \in K_1^\times$.
  - By Step 7 of [the earlier entry](automorphisms-of-maximal-prosupersolvable-galois-groups.md), $w$ fixes $K_1$. So $w$ is a central element of $\Gamma_\ell(K_1)$, and $w = 1$ by part 2 there.

*Step 5: part 3.*

- *Only if.* Suppose $\lambda : K_1^{\mathrm{ss}} \to K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}$ induces $\alpha$. Extending $\lambda$ to $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ shows $\lambda(\Omega_\ell(K_1)) = \Omega_\ell(K_2)$, and $(\lambda|_{\Omega_\ell(K_1)})_\ell \in \mathcal{T}(K_1, K_2)$ maps to $\alpha$. By injectivity in part 2, $\sigma_\ell = \lambda|_{\Omega_\ell(K_1)}$. Take $s = \lambda|_{K_1^{\mathrm{ab}}}$.
- *If.* Let $s$ be as in part 3, and extend it to an automorphism $T$ of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. Then $T(K_1) = \sigma_\ell(K_1) = K_2$, so $T$ maps $K_1^{\mathrm{ss}}$ onto $K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}$. Now $u_\ell = T^{-1}\sigma_\ell$ lies in $\Gamma_\ell(K_1)$ and is trivial on $A_\ell(K_1)$, because $T$ and $\sigma_\ell$ agree there. So $(u_\ell)_\ell$ satisfies the condition of part 1 with $a = 1$, and it equals $r(g)$ for some $g \in G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$. The isomorphism $\lambda = T|_{K_1^{\mathrm{ss}}} \circ g$ satisfies $\lambda|_{\Omega_\ell(K_1)} = T u_\ell = \sigma_\ell$ for every $\ell$. So the isomorphism induced by $\lambda$ is $\Phi(\sigma) = \alpha$.

*Step 6: part 4.* By part 2 with $K_1 = K_2 = K$, $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}})$ is isomorphic to the group $\mathcal{A} = \mathcal{T}(K, K)$. Conjugation by $g \in G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ corresponds to $r(g)$, so the inner automorphisms correspond to $F$.

- *The extension.* Sending a family to its common restriction gives a homomorphism $\rho : \mathcal{A} \to \mathrm{Aut}(K)$. It is surjective: extend $t \in \mathrm{Aut}(K)$ to $T \in \mathrm{Aut}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})$ and take $(T|_{\Omega_\ell(K)})_\ell$. Its kernel is all of $\prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K)$, since a family of elements of the groups $\Gamma_\ell(K)$ needs no compatibility to lie in $\mathcal{A}$. As $F \subseteq \ker \rho$, there is an exact sequence $1 \to \prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K)/F \to \mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}) \to \mathrm{Aut}(K) \to 1$.
- *The kernel.* Restriction maps $\prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K)$ onto $\prod_\ell \mathrm{Gal}(A_\ell/K)$. By part 1, $F$ is the preimage of the image $\Delta$ of $a \mapsto (a|_{A_\ell})_\ell$ on $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K)$. Using Step 1, write elements of $\prod_\ell \mathrm{Gal}(A_\ell/K)$ as $(b_\ell, e_\ell)_\ell$ with $b_\ell \in \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell)/K)$ and $e_\ell \in \mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell/K)$. Let $a_b \in \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K)$ be the element with restrictions $(b_\ell)_\ell$. Then $\psi(b, e) = (e_\ell \cdot (a_b|_{E_\ell})^{-1})_\ell$ is a continuous homomorphism onto $\prod_\ell \mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell/K)$, since all these groups are abelian.
  - $\psi(\Delta(a)) = 1$, because $a_b = a$ for $b = (a|_{K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\ell)})_\ell$.
  - Conversely, $\psi(b, e) = 1$ means $e_\ell = a_b|_{E_\ell}$ for all $\ell$, that is, $(b, e) = \Delta(a_b)$.
  - So $\ker \psi = \Delta$, and $u \mapsto \psi((u_\ell|_{A_\ell})_\ell)$ identifies $\prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K)/F$ with $\prod_\ell \mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell/K)$. This is the formula in part 4.
- *The complement.* Let $N$ be the group of automorphisms $\sigma$ of $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ with $\sigma(K) = K$. Then $\sigma \mapsto (\sigma|_{\Omega_\ell})_\ell$ is a homomorphism $N \to \mathcal{A}$, injective by (I1). It corresponds to the action of field automorphisms, and its image $N'$ contains $F$.
  - $\rho$ maps $N'$ onto $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$, by the same extension argument as for $\rho$ itself.
  - $N' \cap \ker \rho$ is the image of $\{\sigma \in N : \sigma|_K = 1\} = G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$, which is $F$.
  - So $N'/F$ is a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}})$ that $\rho$ maps isomorphically onto $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$. By construction it is the set of classes of field-induced automorphisms.
- *The action.* Let $\sigma \in N$ restrict to $t$ on $K$, and let $u \in \prod_\ell \Gamma_\ell(K)$. Then $\sigma u \sigma^{-1} = (\sigma u_\ell \sigma^{-1})_\ell$. Since $\sigma$ maps each $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(m)$, $E_\ell$, $A_\ell$ and $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ onto itself, restriction and $\psi$ commute with conjugation by $\sigma$. On the abelian groups $\mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell/K)$, conjugation by $\sigma$ depends only on $t$. $\square$

**Status of the inputs.** The existence of the family $(\sigma_\ell)_\ell$ in (I2), and so the surjectivity in part 2, rests on Karshon and Shusterman's Theorem 9.4, adapted in [the entry cited for (I2)](prosupersolvable-quotient-determines-number-field.md). That input is an arXiv preprint of January 2026 and has not been independently audited here. The rest of the proof uses only (I1), part 2 and Step 7 of [the earlier entry](automorphisms-of-maximal-prosupersolvable-galois-groups.md), and Galois theory. This covers part 1, the injectivity in part 2, and Step 6 apart from the identification of $\mathcal{A}$ with all of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}})$. So without that input, $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}})$ still contains $\bigl(\prod_\ell \mathrm{Gal}(E_\ell(K)/K)\bigr) \rtimes \mathrm{Aut}(K)$ as described, and the input is needed only for equality and for part 3.
