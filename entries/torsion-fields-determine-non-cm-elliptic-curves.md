**Theorem.** Let $K$ be a number field, and let $E_1, E_2$ be elliptic curves over $K$, neither of which has complex multiplication over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. Then $E_1 \cong E_2$ over $K$ if and only if $K(E_1[N]) = K(E_2[N])$ for every $N \ge 1$ [@mochizuki2003topics, Theorem 1.1, p. 122].

Here $K(E[N])$ is the field generated by the coordinates of the $N$-torsion points, that is, the fixed field of the kernel of $G_K \to \mathrm{Aut}(E[N])$. The fields are compared inside one algebraic closure of $K$; since they are Galois over $K$, the condition does not depend on that choice [@mochizuki2003topics, remark after Theorem 1.1, p. 123]. Since $E[N]$ is the direct sum of the $E[p^n]$ for the prime powers $p^n$ exactly dividing $N$, the field $K(E[N])$ is the compositum of the fields $K(E[p^n])$. So it suffices to ask for equality when $N$ is a prime power.

Mochizuki makes no claim of essential originality for this result. He offers it as a prototype of the Tate and Grothendieck conjectures, in which the isomorphism class of a curve is recovered from Galois-theoretic information [@mochizuki2003topics, Introduction, p. 120, and §1, p. 122].

**Over $\mathbb{Q}$.** There is a finite set $\mathfrak{CM} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ such that two elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $j$-invariants outside $\mathfrak{CM}$ are isomorphic over $\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if $\mathbb{Q}(E_1[N]) = \mathbb{Q}(E_2[N])$ for all $N$ [@mochizuki2003topics, Corollary 1.2, p. 125]. It is the set of $j$-invariants of elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ with complex multiplication. Following an unpublished manuscript of Serre, Mochizuki lists its $13$ elements: $0$, $2^6 3^3$, $(2 \cdot 3 \cdot 11)^3$, $(2^2 \cdot 5)^3$, $2^4 3^3 5^3$, $-3 \cdot 2^{15} 5^3$, $-3^3 5^3$, $(3 \cdot 5 \cdot 17)^3$, $-2^{15}$, $-(2^5 \cdot 3)^3$, $-(2^6 \cdot 3 \cdot 5)^3$, $-(2^5 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 11)^3$ and $-(2^6 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot 23 \cdot 29)^3$ [@mochizuki2003topics, remark after Corollary 1.2, pp. 125–126]. In particular every curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ with non-integral $j$-invariant is covered.

**Proof outline** [@mochizuki2003topics, proof of Theorem 1.1, pp. 123–125]. Write $K[N]$ for the common field $K(E_i[N])$, put $K[p^\infty] = \bigcup_n K[p^n]$ and $\Gamma[p^n] = \mathrm{Gal}(K[p^\infty]/K[p^n])$. Let $GL_2^{[n]}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ and $PGL_2^{[n]}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ be the subgroups of elements congruent to $1$ modulo $p^n$.

1. *Isomorphism over $\bar K$.* Fix a prime $p$. Faltings' semisimplicity and Tate theorems and the absence of CM give an $n$ such that each $T_pE_i$ induces an isomorphism $\beta_i : \Gamma[p^n] \to GL_2^{[n]}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. The induced automorphism $\alpha$ of $PGL_2^{[n]}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ preserves every $PGL_2^{[m]}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, because the fields $K[p^m]$ are shared. By Lie theory, after enlarging $n$, it is conjugation by some $A \in PGL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ [@mochizuki2003topics, Lemma 1.3, p. 126]. This gives a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear isomorphism $\psi : T_pE_1 \to T_pE_2$ with $\psi(\sigma t) = \lambda_\sigma \sigma(\psi(t))$ for a character $\lambda$ of $\Gamma[p^n]$. Both determinants are the cyclotomic character, so $\lambda^2 = 1$. Over an extension $K'$ of $K[p^n]$ of degree at most $2$, the Tate modules are therefore isomorphic, and Faltings' theorem gives an element of $\mathrm{Hom}_{K'}(E_1, E_2) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p$ that is an isomorphism on Tate modules. Without CM, $\mathrm{Hom}_{\bar K}(E_1, E_2)$ is free of rank at most $1$. So it is free of rank $1$, with a generator $\epsilon$ that is an isomorphism on $p$-power torsion for every $p$, hence an isomorphism.
2. *Descent to $K$.* Take $p \ge 5$ unramified in $K$ and such that both $p$-adic representations map $\mathrm{Gal}(K[p^\infty]/K)$ isomorphically onto $GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. Mochizuki obtains such $p$ from mod-$\ell$ versions of semisimplicity and the Tate conjecture. Composing $\beta_1 \beta_2^{-1}$ with conjugation by $A^{-1}$ gives an automorphism of $GL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ that induces the identity on $PGL_2^{[n]}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. By a lemma of Tamagawa it then induces the identity on $PGL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ [@mochizuki2003topics, Lemma 1.4, p. 127]. So it has the form $\sigma \mapsto \lambda(\sigma)\sigma$, where $\lambda$ factors through the determinant, because $SL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ has no abelian quotients for $p \ge 5$, and $\lambda^2 = 1$. Hence $E_1 \cong E_2$ over the quadratic extension $K_p$ of $K$ cut out by the cyclotomic character modulo squares. For two such primes $p \ne p'$ the fields $K_p$ and $K_{p'}$ are linearly disjoint, by ramification, so $G_K$ fixes $\epsilon$.

Mochizuki notes that the proof uses the rigidity of the simple $p$-adic Lie group $PGL_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, which abelian groups such as $\mathbb{Z}_p$ lack, and that this is why CM is excluded [@mochizuki2003topics, second remark after the proof of Theorem 1.1, p. 125].

**What the hypotheses do.** The examples below are checked here. They show that the theorem fails for CM curves, and that its condition cannot be weakened in the ways listed.

*A twist criterion.* Let $E : y^2 = f(x)$ over $K$, with $f$ a monic cubic. Let $d \in K^\times$ be a non-square, $\chi_d$ the character of $K(\sqrt d)/K$, and $E^{(d)} : y^2 = d^3 f(x/d)$ the quadratic twist. Then $\phi(x, y) = (x/d,\, y/(d\sqrt d))$ is an isomorphism $E^{(d)} \to E$ over $K(\sqrt d)$, and ${}^\sigma\phi = [\chi_d(\sigma)] \circ \phi$ for $\sigma \in G_K$. Hence $\phi(\sigma P) = \chi_d(\sigma)\, \sigma(\phi(P))$, so $\sigma$ fixes $E^{(d)}[N]$ pointwise exactly when it acts on $E[N]$ as multiplication by $\chi_d(\sigma)$. Comparing kernels gives:

- if $N \le 2$, then $K(E^{(d)}[N]) = K(E[N])$, because $-1 = 1$ on $E[N]$;
- if $N \ge 3$, then $K(E^{(d)}[N]) = K(E[N])$ if and only if every $\sigma \in G_K$ that acts on $E[N]$ as $1$ or as $-1$ fixes $\sqrt d$. Indeed, such a $\sigma$ with $\chi_d(\sigma) = -1$ lies in exactly one of the two kernels; if there is none, the kernels agree.

1. *Complex multiplication.* Let $E : y^2 = x^3 + 1$ and $E' : y^2 = x^3 - 27$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then $E' = E^{(-3)}$, both have $j = 0$, and $\mathbb{Q}(E[N]) = \mathbb{Q}(E'[N])$ for every $N$, but $E \not\cong E'$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
   - *Equal fields.* Let $\zeta$ be a primitive cube root of unity and $u(x, y) = (\zeta x, y)$. Suppose $N \ge 3$ and $\sigma$ acts on $E[N]$ as $\varepsilon = \pm 1$. For $P \in E[N]$, $\varepsilon\, u(P) = \sigma(u(P)) = {}^\sigma u(\sigma P) = \varepsilon\, {}^\sigma u(P)$, so ${}^\sigma u = u$ on $E[N]$. If $\sigma(\zeta) \ne \zeta$, then ${}^\sigma u = u^2$, so $u$ would fix $E[N]$ pointwise. But $u$ fixes only $O$ and $(0, \pm 1)$, while $E[N]$ has $N^2 \ge 9$ points. So $\sigma$ fixes $\zeta$, hence $\sqrt{-3}$, and the criterion applies.
   - *Not isomorphic.* On $y^2 = x^3 + B$, a point of order $3$ is a flex, so its tangent meets the curve there with multiplicity $3$; comparing coefficients of $x^2$ forces $x = 0$ or $x^3 = -4B$. On $E$ the points $(0, \pm 1)$ have order $3$. On $E'$ these conditions give $y^2 = -27$ or $x^3 = 108$, so $E'$ has no rational point of order $3$.
   - For instance $\mathbb{Q}(E[3]) = \mathbb{Q}(E'[3]) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \sqrt{-3})$, from the points with $x = 0$ or $x^3 = -4B$, although $E[3]$ and $E'[3]$ are not isomorphic Galois modules. The curves become isomorphic over the CM field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$.
2. *A single prime is not enough, even without CM.* Let $p$ be a prime, and put $d = (-1)^{(p-1)/2} p$ if $p$ is odd and $d = -1$ if $p = 2$, so that $\sqrt d \in \mathbb{Q}(\mu_p)$, respectively $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_4)$. If $\sqrt d \notin K$, then $K(E^{(d)}[p^n]) = K(E[p^n])$ for every elliptic curve $E$ over $K$ and every $n$.
   - *Proof.* The case $p^n \le 2$ is covered above. Otherwise, an element $\sigma$ acting on $E[p^n]$ as $\pm 1$ has determinant $1$ there. The determinant is the cyclotomic character [@mochizuki2003topics, proof of Theorem 1.1, p. 123], so $\sigma$ fixes $\mu_{p^n}$, which contains $\mu_p$, respectively $\mu_4$, and so fixes $\sqrt d$.
   - For odd $p$ unramified in $K$, the field $K(\sqrt d)$ is the field $K_p$ of step 2 above. So the $p$-power torsion fields alone cannot exclude a twist by $K_p$, and the descent in step 2 needs two primes.
   - *Example.* $E : y^2 = x(x-9)(x-25)$ has $j = 481^3/(3^4 5^4)$. This is not an integer, hence not in the list above, so $E$ has no CM. Its twist $E^{(-3)} : y^2 = x(x+27)(x+75)$ has the same $3^n$-torsion fields for all $n$. On $E$, the points $(15, \pm 30i)$ and $(-15, \pm 120i)$ halve $(0,0)$, and $(9 + 12i, \pm(-48 + 36i))$ and $(9 - 12i, \pm(-48 - 36i))$ halve $(9,0)$. Together with $E[2] \subseteq E(\mathbb{Q})$ they generate $E[4]$, so $\mathbb{Q}(E[4]) = \mathbb{Q}(i)$. On $E^{(-3)}$ the halves of $(0,0)$ are $(45, \pm 360\sqrt 3)$ and $(-45, \pm 90\sqrt 3)$. So $\sqrt 3 \in \mathbb{Q}(E^{(-3)}[4])$, the $4$-torsion fields differ, and the curves are not isomorphic over $\mathbb{Q}$.
3. *Finite levels, not towers.* Let $\varphi : E_1 \to E_2$ be an isogeny over $K$ of degree $m$. For $\gcd(N, m) = 1$ it restricts to a Galois-equivariant bijection $E_1[N] \to E_2[N]$, so $K(E_1[N]) = K(E_2[N])$. Now let $p$ be any prime, $p^v$ the exact power of $p$ dividing $m$, and $\hat\varphi$ the dual isogeny, so that $\varphi \circ \hat\varphi = [m]$. Every point of $E_2[p^n]$ is $mR$ for some $R \in E_2[p^{n+v}]$, because $m/p^v$ is invertible modulo $p^{n+v}$, and $mR = \varphi(\hat\varphi(R))$. So $E_2[p^n] \subseteq \varphi(E_1[p^{n+v}])$ and $K(E_2[p^n]) \subseteq K(E_1[p^{n+v}])$, and symmetrically. Hence $K$-isogenous curves have the same field $K(E[p^\infty])$ for every $p$, and the same field generated by all torsion points.
   - *Example.* The map $(x, y) \mapsto (y^2/x^2,\, y(1 - x^2)/x^2)$ is a $2$-isogeny over $\mathbb{Q}$ from $E : y^2 = x^3 + x^2 + x$ to $E'' : y^2 = x^3 - 2x^2 - 3x$. Their $j$-invariants $2048/3$ and $35152/9$ are distinct and not integers, hence not in the list above. So neither curve has CM, and they are not isomorphic even over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$.
   - Their $N$-torsion fields agree for odd $N$, and their $2$-power torsion towers have the same union. But $\mathbb{Q}(E[2]) = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, since $x^2 + x + 1$ is irreducible, while $\mathbb{Q}(E''[2]) = \mathbb{Q}$, since $x^2 - 2x - 3 = (x-3)(x+1)$.
   - So the condition in the theorem cannot be weakened to equality of the fields generated by all torsion points, or by all $p$-power torsion points for each $p$, or to equality for odd $N$ only.

**In the graph.** The entry on [pro-$\ell$ kernels of monodromically full genus-$0$ curves](pro-l-kernels-determine-monodromically-full-genus-zero-curves.md) records that Hoshi presents his theorem there as a genus-$0$ analogue of this one. There a single field, the one cut out by the pro-$\ell$ outer representation, suffices. For elliptic curves the corresponding abelian field $K(E[\ell^\infty])$, which appears for abelian varieties in [the $\ell$-moderate points problem](l-moderate-points-and-pro-l-kernel-fields.md), does not, by examples 2 and 3. The theorem also contrasts with [injectivity of outer Galois representations](injectivity-of-outer-galois-representations.md). For a hyperbolic curve over a number field, the kernel of the whole profinite outer representation is trivial and carries no information. Here the information is in the kernels at finite abelian levels.
