**Setting.** Let $D$ be a pro-$2$ [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $n \ge 3$ whose canonical character $\chi : D \to \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ has image $\{\pm 1\}$. Then $q(D) = 2$, because $q$ is the largest power of $2$ with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) \subseteq 1 + q\mathbb{Z}_2$ ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md)). Write $V_D = D^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathrm{torsion}$, free of rank $n - 1$. All homomorphisms are continuous, and $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Q}_2)$ means continuous homomorphisms from a profinite group $B$ to the additive group of $\mathbb{Q}_2$; for $B = D$ these all factor through $V_D$.

By the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), with $(a, b) = a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ and $F$ free pro-$2$ on $x_1, \dots, x_n$, such a $D$ is isomorphic to $F/(r)$ with
$$r = x_1^2(x_2, x_3)(x_4, x_5)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n) \quad (n \text{ odd}), \qquad r = x_1^2(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n) \quad (n \text{ even}),$$
the forms of items 2 and 3 with $f = \infty$. Only the odd form is used below.

*The orientation subgroup.* Uniqueness of the canonical character gives $\chi \circ \alpha = \chi$ for every $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$: the character $\chi \circ \alpha$ has the surjectivity property that characterizes $\chi$, because $\alpha$ induces isomorphisms between the cohomology groups for $\chi$ and for $\chi \circ \alpha$ compatible with reduction. Put $D^+ = \ker\chi$, an open subgroup of index $2$ with $\alpha(D^+) = D^+$ for every $\alpha$. Put $W = (D^+)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and $W_{\mathbb{Q}} = W \otimes \mathbb{Q}_2$. Every $x \in D \setminus D^+$ acts on $W$ by conjugation in the same way, since $D^+$ acts trivially; call this involution $J$, and let $W_+$ and $W_-$ be its $(+1)$- and $(-1)$-eigenspaces in $W_{\mathbb{Q}}$.

*The commutator line.* Let $b : \Lambda^2 W \to \mathrm{gr}_2 D^+ = D^+_2/D^+_3$ be $v \wedge w \mapsto [v, w]$, the class of the commutator of lifts. It is natural: for an automorphism $\beta$ of $D^+$ with action $\beta_*$ on $W$, $b \circ \Lambda^2(\beta_*) = \mathrm{gr}_2(\beta) \circ b$. For $\varphi \in W_{\mathbb{Q}}^*$ let $\iota_\varphi : \Lambda^2 W_{\mathbb{Q}} \to W_{\mathbb{Q}}$ be the contraction $v \wedge w \mapsto \varphi(v)w - \varphi(w)v$. Call $\omega \in \Lambda^2 W_{\mathbb{Q}}$ *nondegenerate* if $\varphi \mapsto \iota_\varphi\omega$ is injective.

**Lemma A ($q = 0$).** This is proved here. Let $p$ be a prime and $S$ a pro-$p$ surface group of genus $g \ge 2$, that is, a Demushkin group with $q = 0$ and rank $2g \ge 4$. An automorphism of $S$ of finite order acting trivially on $S^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is trivial.

*Proof.* Let $K_i$ be the kernel of $\mathrm{Aut}(S) \to \mathrm{Aut}(S/S_{i+1})$, as in [the lower central series of pro-$p$ surface groups](lower-central-series-of-pro-p-surface-groups.md), part 4; that entry's $S$ is ours by the classification. Let $\kappa$ be an automorphism as in the statement; then $\kappa \in K_1$. If $\kappa \ne 1$, part 4(d) gives a largest $i$ with $\kappa \in K_i$. The image of $\kappa$ in the torsion-free group $K_i/K_{i+1}$ (part 4(a)) is nonzero and of finite order, which is impossible. $\square$

For $q = 0$, $V_S = S^{\mathrm{ab}}$, so Lemma A is the involution statement below in that case, for every $p$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $D$ be as in the setting.

1. *The orientation subgroup.* $D^+$ is a Demushkin group with $q = 0$ and rank $2n - 2$, so $W$ is free of rank $2n - 2$. Restriction is an isomorphism from $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Q}_2)$ onto the $J$-invariant elements of $\mathrm{Hom}(D^+, \mathbb{Q}_2) = W_{\mathbb{Q}}^*$, and $\dim W_+ = \dim W_- = n - 1$. The kernel of $b$ is a direct summand of $\Lambda^2 W$ of rank $1$, spanned by a nondegenerate $\omega$, and $\Lambda^2(J)\omega = -\omega$. Hence $\omega \in W_+ \wedge W_-$, and $\varphi \mapsto \iota_\varphi\omega$ maps the functionals vanishing on $W_-$ isomorphically onto $W_-$.
2. *Automorphisms acting by scalars.* For every automorphism $\beta$ of $D^+$ there is $\mu(\beta) \in \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ with $\Lambda^2(\beta_*)\omega = \mu(\beta)\omega$, and $\mu$ is multiplicative. If $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ acts on $V_D$ as multiplication by $c \in \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$, then $\alpha|_{D^+}$ acts on $W_{\mathbb{Q}}$ as $c$ on $W_+$ and as $c^{-1}\mu(\alpha|_{D^+})$ on $W_-$.
3. *Involutions.* If $\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ satisfies $\sigma^2 = 1$ and acts trivially on $V_D$, then $\sigma = 1$. Consequently the kernels of $\mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V_D)$ and of $\mathrm{Out}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V_D)$ contain no element $\ne 1$ of finite $2$-power order.

**Combined statement.** Let $D$ be any pro-$2$ Demushkin group of rank at least $3$. Its canonical image is a closed subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times = \{\pm 1\} \times (1 + 4\mathbb{Z}_2)$, so it is infinite, $\{1\}$ or $\{\pm 1\}$. In the first case [the theorem for infinite canonical image](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-infinite-canonical-image.md) applies; in the second $q = 0$ and Lemma A applies; in the third part 3 applies. So no involution $\ne 1$ of $D$ acts trivially on $V_D$.

**Proof of part 1.**

*Structure of $D^+$.* By the index formula $n(D^+) - 2 = 2(n - 2)$, so $n(D^+) = 2n - 2 \ge 4$. The canonical character of an open subgroup is the restriction of $\chi$ ([torsion-free extensions](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), proof of part 2), so $\chi_{D^+} = 1$ and $q(D^+) = 0$. Hence $W \cong \mathbb{Z}_2^{2n-2}$ ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md)), and $D^+$ is isomorphic to the pro-$2$ surface group $S$ of genus $n - 1$ of [the lower central series entry](lower-central-series-of-pro-p-surface-groups.md), by the classification.

*Restriction.* $D$ is torsion-free ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 1). Part 2 of [the torsion-free extension theorem](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), for the open normal subgroup $D^+$ of $D$ and $H = D/D^+$, says that the $J$-invariants of $\mathrm{Hom}(D^+, \mathbb{Z}_2)$ have rank $n(D) - 1 + \delta(D) = n - 1$, as $q(D) \ne 0$. Restriction from $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Q}_2)$, of dimension $n - 1$, is injective, because a homomorphism vanishing on $D^+$ factors through a finite group, and its image is $J$-invariant. So it is an isomorphism onto the invariants. The invariant functionals are those vanishing on $W_-$, so $\dim W_+ = n - 1$ and $\dim W_- = (2n - 2) - (n - 1) = n - 1$.

*The kernel of $b$.* Fix an isomorphism $\theta : D^+ \to S$; it induces isomorphisms on $\Lambda^2$ of the abelianizations and on $\mathrm{gr}_2$ commuting with the two maps $b$. For $S$, part 1 of the lower central series entry identifies $\mathrm{gr}\,S$ with $L/(\rho)$, where $L$ is the free Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Z}_2$ on $X_1, \dots, X_{2n-2}$ and $\rho = \sum_i [X_{2i-1}, X_{2i}]$. Its degree-$2$ part $L_2$ is free with basis $[X_i, X_j]$, $i < j$, which identifies it with $\Lambda^2$ of the degree-$1$ part, and the degree-$2$ part of the ideal $(\rho)$ is $\mathbb{Z}_2\rho$. So $\ker b_S = \mathbb{Z}_2\rho$, a direct summand of rank $1$, and $\rho = \sum_i \xi_{2i-1} \wedge \xi_{2i}$ is nondegenerate: $\iota$ sends the dual basis vectors $\xi_{2i-1}^*, \xi_{2i}^*$ to $\xi_{2i}, -\xi_{2i-1}$. Transporting by $\theta$, $\ker b = \mathbb{Z}_2\omega$ with $\omega$ nondegenerate.

*The sign of $J$.* As $x^2 \in D^+$ acts trivially on $W$, $J^2 = 1$; so $\Lambda^2(J)\omega = \epsilon\omega$ with $\epsilon = \pm 1$, by naturality of $b$ applied to conjugation by $x$. Write
$$\Lambda^2 W_{\mathbb{Q}} = \Lambda^2 W_+ \oplus (W_+ \wedge W_-) \oplus \Lambda^2 W_-,$$
on whose summands $\Lambda^2(J)$ acts by $+1, -1, +1$.

- *$n$ even.* If $\epsilon = 1$, then $\omega = \omega_+ + \omega_-$ with $\omega_\pm \in \Lambda^2 W_\pm$. For $\varphi$ vanishing on $W_-$, $\iota_\varphi\omega = \iota_\varphi\omega_+ \in W_+$. So $\varphi \mapsto \iota_\varphi\omega_+$ is an injective linear map from $W_+^*$ to $W_+$ given by an alternating form, which forces $\dim W_+$ to be even. But $\dim W_+ = n - 1$ is odd. So $\epsilon = -1$.
- *$n$ odd.* Use the presentation with $r = x_1^2C$, $C = (x_2, x_3)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n)$. Let $\theta_k \in \mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{F}_2)$ send $x_k$ to $1$ and the other generators to $0$, and let $\bar\chi \in \mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{F}_2)$ be $\chi$ read in $\{\pm 1\} \cong \mathbb{F}_2$, so $\ker\bar\chi = D^+$ and $\bar\chi \circ \alpha = \bar\chi$ for all $\alpha$. For a pair $(a, b) = (x_{2i}, x_{2i+1})$, $i \ge 1$, the substitutions $L : b \mapsto ab$ and $R : a \mapsto ba$, fixing the other generators, fix $r$, since $(a, ab) = (a, b) = (ba, b)$ and $x_1$ does not occur in the pair. They are automorphisms of $F$, with inverses $b \mapsto a^{-1}b$ and $a \mapsto b^{-1}a$, so they define automorphisms of $D$. For $v = \sum_k c_k\theta_k$ one computes $v \circ L = v + c_a\theta_b$ and $v \circ R = v + c_b\theta_a$. A vector fixed by all of them has $c_k = 0$ for $k \ge 2$. As $\bar\chi \ne 0$ is fixed, $\bar\chi = \theta_1$ and $D^+ = \ker\theta_1$. As in Step 3 of [the theorem for infinite canonical image](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-infinite-canonical-image.md), $\ker\theta_1$ is topologically generated by $x_1^2$ and the elements $u_k = x_k$, $u'_k = x_1x_kx_1^{-1}$ for $2 \le k \le n$. In $D$, $x_1^2 = C^{-1}$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by the $u_k$, so the $2n - 2$ elements $u_k, u'_k$ generate $D^+$, and their classes $\bar u_k, \bar u'_k$ form a basis of $W$. Let $C'$ be $C$ with every $x_k$ replaced by $u'_k$. Then $C' = x_1Cx_1^{-1} = x_1^{-2} = C$. Both are products of commutators in $D^+$, so $b$ sends
$$\omega_0 = \sum_{i=1}^{(n-1)/2}\big(\bar u'_{2i} \wedge \bar u'_{2i+1} - \bar u_{2i} \wedge \bar u_{2i+1}\big)$$
to the class of $C'C^{-1} = 1$. So $\omega_0 \in \ker b$, and $\omega_0 \ne 0$, so $\mathbb{Q}_2\omega = \mathbb{Q}_2\omega_0$. Conjugation by $x_1$ sends $u_k$ to $u'_k$ and $u'_k$ to $x_1^2u_kx_1^{-2}$, so $J$ swaps $\bar u_k$ and $\bar u'_k$, and $\Lambda^2(J)\omega_0 = -\omega_0$. So $\epsilon = -1$.

So $\omega \in W_+ \wedge W_-$. For $\varphi$ vanishing on $W_-$ and $v \in W_+$, $w \in W_-$, $\iota_\varphi(v \wedge w) = \varphi(v)w \in W_-$. So $\varphi \mapsto \iota_\varphi\omega$ is an injective map from an $(n - 1)$-dimensional space to $W_-$, of dimension $n - 1$, hence an isomorphism. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** For an automorphism $\beta$ of $D^+$, naturality of $b$ shows that $\Lambda^2(\beta_*)$ preserves $\ker b = \mathbb{Z}_2\omega$, so it acts on this rank-$1$ summand by a unit $\mu(\beta)$, multiplicatively in $\beta$.

Let $\alpha$ act on $V_D$ as $c$, and put $\beta = \alpha|_{D^+}$. For $x \in D \setminus D^+$, $\beta \circ (\text{conjugation by } x) \circ \beta^{-1}$ is conjugation by $\alpha(x) \in D \setminus D^+$ on $D^+$, so $\beta_*$ commutes with $J$ and preserves $W_\pm$. For $f \in \mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Q}_2)$, $f \circ \alpha = cf$, as $f$ factors through $V_D$. Restricting, $g \circ \beta_* = cg$ for every functional $g$ vanishing on $W_-$, by part 1. So for $v \in W_+$, $\beta_*v - cv$ lies in $W_-$, and also in $W_+$; hence $\beta_*v = cv$. Let $T$ be the restriction of $\beta_*$ to $W_-$. For $\varphi$ vanishing on $W_-$, $v \in W_+$ and $w \in W_-$,
$$\iota_\varphi\big(\Lambda^2(\beta_*)(v \wedge w)\big) = \iota_\varphi(cv \wedge Tw) = c\,\varphi(v)\,Tw = cT\big(\iota_\varphi(v \wedge w)\big).$$
Applying $\iota_\varphi$ to $\Lambda^2(\beta_*)\omega = \mu(\beta)\omega$ gives $cT(\iota_\varphi\omega) = \mu(\beta)\iota_\varphi\omega$. By part 1 the vectors $\iota_\varphi\omega$ fill $W_-$, so $T = c^{-1}\mu(\beta)$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** If $\sigma = 1$ there is nothing to show, so suppose $\sigma \ne 1$. Then $\sigma$ is not inner: $D$ is centre-free ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 4), so $\sigma = c_d$ would give $c_{d^2} = 1$, $d^2 = 1$, and $d = 1$ as $D$ is torsion-free. Identify $D$ with $\mathrm{Inn}(D) \subseteq A = \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, so that $\alpha c_d\alpha^{-1} = c_{\alpha(d)}$.

*Two facts.* (i) If $\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ has finite order and $\tau|_{D^+}$ acts trivially on $W$, then $\tau = 1$. Indeed $\tau|_{D^+} = 1$ by Lemma A, as $D^+$ is a pro-$2$ surface group of genus $n - 1 \ge 2$. Then for $d \in D$ and $u \in D^+$, $\tau(d)u\tau(d)^{-1} = \tau(dud^{-1}) = dud^{-1}$, so $d^{-1}\tau(d)$ centralizes the nontrivial normal subgroup $D^+$ of $D$, and is $1$ by part 4 of [the subgroups entry](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md). (ii) By part 2 with $c = 1$, $\sigma|_{D^+}$ acts on $W_{\mathbb{Q}}$ as $1$ on $W_+$ and as $\mu = \mu(\sigma|_{D^+})$ on $W_-$, and $\mu^2 = \mu(\sigma^2|_{D^+}) = 1$. So $\sigma|_{D^+}$ acts on $W$ as $1$ or as $J$.

*If it acts as $1$*, then $\sigma = 1$ by (i), a contradiction.

*If it acts as $J$*, let $E = D \cup D\sigma$, a closed subgroup of $A$ containing $D$ with index $2$, hence a pro-$2$ group. $D^+$ is normal in $E$, and $E/D^+$ has order $4$. It is elementary abelian: for $x \in D \setminus D^+$, $x^2 \in D^+$, $\sigma^2 = 1$, and $\sigma x\sigma^{-1} = \sigma(x) \in xD^+$ because $\chi(\sigma(x)) = \chi(x)$. Let $P = D^+ \cup D^+x\sigma$, a subgroup of index $2$ in $E$. Conjugation by an element of $D^+x\sigma$ acts on $W$ as $J \cdot J = 1$.

- If $P$ has no element of order $2$, it has no nontrivial element of finite order, as it is a pro-$2$ group. Part 3 of [the torsion-free extension theorem](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), applied to $D^+ \subseteq P$ with $n(D^+) \ge 3$, says that $P/D^+$ acts faithfully on $V_{D^+} = W$. But its nontrivial element acts trivially. Contradiction.
- Otherwise some $\tau \in D^+x\sigma$ has order $2$. Write $\tau = z\sigma$ with $z \in D^+x \subseteq D \setminus D^+$. As an automorphism of $D$, $\tau = c_z \circ \sigma$, and $\tau|_{D^+}$ acts on $W$ as $J \cdot J = 1$. By (i), $\tau = 1$, so $\sigma = z^{-1} \in D$ is inner. Contradiction.

So $\sigma = 1$.

*The kernels.* An element $\ne 1$ of finite $2$-power order in the kernel for $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ has a power of order $2$ there, which is excluded. If $\vartheta \in \mathrm{Out}(D)$ has order $2$ and acts trivially on $V_D$, then by Corollary 1 of [the torsion-free extension theorem](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), with $p = 2$, $\vartheta$ is the class of an automorphism of order $2$, which acts trivially on $V_D$ because inner automorphisms do, and this is excluded. Taking powers excludes every element $\ne 1$ of finite $2$-power order. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Why the method of the infinite case fails.* That proof needs every torsion-free finite extension of every open subgroup to have $q \ne 0$. Here $D^+$ has $q = 0$, so it fails at once. The proof above uses $D^+$ instead: it is a surface group, whose Andreadakis kernel is torsion-free (Lemma A), and $J$ reverses the sign of the commutator line, so an involution trivial on $V_D$ acts on $W$ as $1$ or as $J$.
- *Tsujimura's question.* The theorem is an input for case (a) of [the reduction](demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three-are-elastic.md), Corollary 2, for the groups with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\}$; see [finite normal subgroups and rival copies for these groups](rival-copies-of-demushkin-groups-with-canonical-image-of-order-two.md). By itself it does not exclude case (a).
- *Odd-order elements.* Nothing is said here about elements of odd order in the two kernels.
- *Sources.* The statement and proof are not taken from a source, and no literature search was made. No novelty is claimed.
