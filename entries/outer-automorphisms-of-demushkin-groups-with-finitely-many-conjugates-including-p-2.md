**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $D$ a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $n \ge 3$, with invariant $q$ and canonical character $\chi : D \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. Assume that $D$ belongs to one of the following classes.

- (i) $q \ne 0$ and $q \ne 2$. Since $q \ne 1$, as $D^{\mathrm{ab}}/pD^{\mathrm{ab}} = D/\Phi(D)$ has dimension $n$, for odd $p$ this means $q \ne 0$, and for $p = 2$ it means that $q$ is nonzero and divisible by $4$.
- (ii) $p = 2$, $n$ is even and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = U_2^{[f]}$, the closed subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ generated by $-1 + 2^f$, for some finite $f \ge 2$. Here $q = 2$. As $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ is a pro-$2$ group, $A = \mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ is a nontrivial procyclic pro-$2$ group, so $(A : A^2) = 2$.

Write $[x, y] = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, as in [the classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), and let $F$ be the free pro-$p$ group on $x_1, \dots, x_n$. By the classification, items 1 and 3, $n = 2g$ is even, so $n \ge 4$, and $D \cong F/(r)$ with
$$r = x_1^m R, \qquad R = [x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4] \cdots [x_{n-1}, x_n],$$
where $m = q$ in class (i) and $m = 2 + 2^f$ in class (ii). We identify $D$ with $F/(r)$. In both classes $m = qu$ with $u \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$: in class (ii), $2 + 2^f = 2(1 + 2^{f-1})$ with $1 + 2^{f-1}$ odd.

Since $r \equiv x_1^m$ modulo the closed commutator subgroup, the image of $(r)$ in $F^{\mathrm{ab}} = \mathbb{Z}_p^n$ is $m\mathbb{Z}_pe_1 = q\mathbb{Z}_pe_1$, where $e_i$ is the class of $x_i$. So $D^{\mathrm{ab}} = \mathbb{Z}_p^n/q\mathbb{Z}_pe_1$, its torsion subgroup is $T = (\mathbb{Z}/q)e_1$, and $V = D^{\mathrm{ab}}/T$ is free over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ with basis $v_2, \dots, v_n$, the classes of $x_2, \dots, x_n$. Put $W = \mathbb{Z}_pv_3 \oplus \cdots \oplus \mathbb{Z}_pv_n$. Automorphisms of $D$ act on $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$, preserve $T$, and so act on $V$, and inner automorphisms act trivially. This gives $\rho : \mathrm{Out}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$; put $\Gamma = \rho(\mathrm{Out}(D))$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $D$ be in class (i) or (ii).

1. *An invariant form.* There is a nonzero $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear map $\varphi : V \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ with $\varphi \circ \gamma = \varphi$ for all $\gamma \in \Gamma$, and $\ker\varphi = W$.
2. *No elements of finite conjugacy class.* If $f \in \Gamma$ commutes with some positive power of each element of $\Gamma$, then $f = 1$. In particular $\Gamma$ has trivial centre, no nontrivial element with finitely many conjugates, and no nontrivial finite normal subgroup. The proof uses only $3(g-1)$ explicit elements of $\Gamma$, listed in Step 1.
3. *Consequence for $\mathrm{Out}(D)$.* If $\theta \in \mathrm{Out}(D)$ has only finitely many conjugates in $\mathrm{Out}(D)$, then $\rho(\theta) = 1$. Every finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ lies in $\ker\rho$.

**Corollary 1.** This is proved here. Let $D$ be in class (i) or (ii). Since $n \ge 3$, $D$ has trivial centre ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 4); identify $D$ with $\mathrm{Inn}(D)$, and let $N$ be a closed normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ with $ND/D$ finite. Then every element of $ND$ acts trivially on $V$.

*Proof.* $ND/D$ is a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D) = \mathrm{Aut}(D)/D$, so part 3 applies to each of its elements. $\square$

**Corollary 2 (Tsujimura's question for $p = 2$).** This is proved here. Let $D$ be a pro-$2$ Demushkin group of rank $n \ge 3$ whose invariant $q$ is nonzero and divisible by $4$. For example, $D = G_K(2)$ for a finite extension $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ containing $i$: it is a Demushkin group of rank $[K : \mathbb{Q}_2] + 2$ whose $q$ is the largest power of $2$ with $\mu_q \subset K$ ([maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md), part 2), so $4 \mid q$ and $q \ne 0$. Since $n \ge 3$, $D$ has trivial centre ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 4), and is identified with $\mathrm{Inn}(D)$ as in [the problem](demushkin-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md). Then case (a) of [the reduction](demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three-are-elastic.md), Corollary 2, does not occur. So every closed normal subgroup $N \ne D$ of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ isomorphic to $D$ meets $D$ in a subgroup of infinite index in both $D$ and $N$ that is not topologically finitely generated.

*Proof.* $D$ is in class (i). In case (a), $ND/D$ is a nontrivial finite normal $2$-subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$. By part 3 it lies in the kernel of $\mathrm{Out}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$. A nonidentity element of $ND/D$ then has finite $2$-power order and acts trivially on $V$, which the theorem of [involutions with $q$ divisible by four](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-q-divisible-by-four.md) excludes. So only case (b) of the reduction remains. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.**

*Step 1: some automorphisms of $D$.* An automorphism $\alpha$ of $F$ with $\alpha(r) = r$ maps the closed normal subgroup $(r)$ onto itself, and so induces an automorphism of $D$. An endomorphism of $F$ is determined by the images of $x_1, \dots, x_n$, which may be chosen freely.

(a) For $2 \le i \le g$, let $\sigma_i$ send $x_{2i-1}$ to $x_{2i}x_{2i-1}$, and $\sigma'_i$ send $x_{2i}$ to $x_{2i-1}x_{2i}$, each fixing the other generators. They are automorphisms, with inverses $x_{2i-1} \mapsto x_{2i}^{-1}x_{2i-1}$ and $x_{2i} \mapsto x_{2i-1}^{-1}x_{2i}$. They fix $r$, because in any group
$$[ba, b] = a^{-1}b^{-1}b^{-1}\,ba\,b = [a, b], \qquad [a, ab] = a^{-1}b^{-1}a^{-1}\,a\,ab = [a, b].$$
Let $E_i, E'_i \in \mathrm{End}(V)$ be given by $E_i(v_{2i-1}) = v_{2i}$ and $E'_i(v_{2i}) = v_{2i-1}$, each killing the other basis vectors. Then $\rho(\sigma_i) = 1 + E_i$ and $\rho(\sigma'_i) = 1 + E'_i$.

(b) *An identity.* In any group, for elements $a, b, c, d$ put $\ell = bc^{-1}d^{-1}c$. Then
$$[a, \ell dc]\,[c, \ell^{-1}a\ell d] = [a, b]\,[c, d]. \tag{1}$$
Indeed, the left side is the first expression below, and cancelling $dc \cdot c^{-1}d^{-1}$ and then $\ell^{-1}a\ell \cdot \ell^{-1}a^{-1}\ell$ gives the second:
$$a^{-1}c^{-1}d^{-1}\ell^{-1}a\ell dc \cdot c^{-1}d^{-1}\ell^{-1}a^{-1}\ell c\ell^{-1}a\ell d = a^{-1}c^{-1}d^{-1}\,(c\ell^{-1})\,a\,(\ell d).$$
Here $c\ell^{-1} = dcb^{-1}$ and $\ell d = bc^{-1}d^{-1}cd$, so this equals $a^{-1}b^{-1}ab \cdot c^{-1}d^{-1}cd$.

Now write $a = x_1$, $b = x_2$, $c = x_{2i-1}$, $d = x_{2i}$ for some $2 \le i \le g$, and $\ell = bc^{-1}d^{-1}c$. Let $\alpha_i$ be the endomorphism of $F$ with
$$\alpha_i(b) = \ell dc, \qquad \alpha_i(d) = \ell^{-1}a\ell d,$$
fixing every other generator. Let $\beta_i$ be the endomorphism with
$$\beta_i(d) = d' = dcb^{-1}a^{-1}bc^{-1}, \qquad \beta_i(b) = Lc^{-1}d'c, \quad L = abc^{-1}d^{-1},$$
fixing every other generator. These are mutually inverse. Indeed, $\beta_i(\ell) = Lc^{-1}d'c \cdot c^{-1}d'^{-1}c = L$, so
$$\beta_i\alpha_i(b) = Ld'c = b, \qquad \beta_i\alpha_i(d) = L^{-1}aLd' = (dcb^{-1}abc^{-1}d^{-1})(dcb^{-1}a^{-1}bc^{-1}) = d.$$
Conversely $\alpha_i(L) = a\,\ell dc\,c^{-1}d^{-1}\ell^{-1}a^{-1}\ell = \ell$ and
$$\alpha_i(d') = \ell^{-1}a\ell dc \cdot c^{-1}d^{-1}\ell^{-1} \cdot a^{-1} \cdot \ell dc \cdot c^{-1} = d,$$
so $\alpha_i\beta_i(d) = d$ and $\alpha_i\beta_i(b) = \ell c^{-1}dc = b$. Both composites fix the other generators. So $\alpha_i$ is an automorphism of $F$. As $\alpha_i(a) = a$ and $\alpha_i(c) = c$, (1) shows that it fixes $[x_1, x_2][x_{2i-1}, x_{2i}]$, and it fixes $x_1$ and every generator other than $x_2, x_{2i}$.

(c) *Moving the handle.* Let $M_i = [x_3, x_4] \cdots [x_{2i-3}, x_{2i-2}]$, with $M_2 = 1$, and $M'_i = [x_{2i+1}, x_{2i+2}] \cdots [x_{n-1}, x_n]$. Let $\kappa_i$ send $x_{2i-1}$ to $M_ix_{2i-1}M_i^{-1}$ and $x_{2i}$ to $M_ix_{2i}M_i^{-1}$, fixing the other generators. As $\kappa_i$ fixes $M_i$, which involves only $x_3, \dots, x_{2i-2}$, it is an automorphism, with inverse given by conjugation by $M_i^{-1}$ on $x_{2i-1}, x_{2i}$. Since $M_i[c, d] = [M_icM_i^{-1}, M_idM_i^{-1}]M_i$,
$$R = [x_1, x_2]\,M_i\,[x_{2i-1}, x_{2i}]\,M'_i = \kappa_i(R_i), \qquad R_i = [x_1, x_2][x_{2i-1}, x_{2i}]\,M_iM'_i.$$
By (b), $\alpha_i$ fixes $R_i$, since it fixes every generator occurring in $M_i$ and $M'_i$. Put $\tau_i = \kappa_i\alpha_i\kappa_i^{-1}$. Then $\tau_i(R) = \kappa_i\alpha_i(R_i) = R$ and $\tau_i(x_1) = x_1$, so $\tau_i(r) = r$, and $\tau_i$ induces an automorphism of $D$.

On $F^{\mathrm{ab}}$, $\kappa_i$ acts trivially, and $\alpha_i$ sends $e_2$ to $e_2 + e_{2i-1}$ and $e_{2i}$ to $e_{2i} + e_1$, fixing the other $e_k$, because $\ell$ has class $e_2 - e_{2i}$. As $e_1$ maps into $T$, $\rho(\tau_i) = 1 + E''_i$, where $E''_i(v_2) = v_{2i-1}$ and $E''_i$ kills $v_3, \dots, v_n$.

Each of $E_i, E'_i, E''_i$ squares to zero, so $(1 + E)^N = 1 + NE$ for $N \ge 1$ and these $3(g-1)$ elements of $\Gamma$ have infinite order.

*Step 2: the invariant form (part 1).* First, $\chi \circ \alpha = \chi$ for every $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$. Write $I_j(\chi)$ for $\mathbb{Z}/p^j$ with $D$ acting through $\chi$; then $\chi$ is the unique character for which every $H^1(D, I_j(\chi)) \to H^1(D, I_1(\chi))$ is surjective ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md)). If $c$ is a $1$-cocycle for the action through $\chi$, then $c \circ \alpha$ is one for the action through $\chi \circ \alpha$, since $c(\alpha(x)\alpha(y)) = c(\alpha(x)) + \chi(\alpha(x))c(\alpha(y))$; this gives isomorphisms $H^1(D, I_j(\chi)) \to H^1(D, I_j(\chi \circ \alpha))$ compatible with reduction. So $\chi \circ \alpha$ has the characterizing property, and $\chi \circ \alpha = \chi$. This is the argument of Step 2 of [the odd case](outer-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-with-finitely-many-conjugates.md), which does not use that $p$ is odd.

Put $U = 1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$ for odd $p$ and $U = 1 + 4\mathbb{Z}_2$ for $p = 2$. *$U$ has no nontrivial element of finite order.* Let $s \ge 1$, with $s \ge 2$ if $p = 2$, and let $x = 1 + p^sy$ with $y \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. For $k$ prime to $p$, $x^k \equiv 1 + kp^sy$ modulo $p^{2s}$, so $x^k = 1 + p^s\cdot(\text{unit})$. And $x^p = 1 + p^{s+1}y + \sum_{j=2}^{p}\binom{p}{j}p^{js}y^j$, where each term of the sum has valuation at least $s + 2$: for $2 \le j \le p - 1$ it is at least $1 + 2s$, and for $j = p$ it is $ps$, which is at least $s + 2$ under the assumption on $s$. So $x^p = 1 + p^{s+1}\cdot(\text{unit})$. By induction, $x^{p^ak}$ with $k$ prime to $p$ equals $1 + p^{s+a}\cdot(\text{unit}) \ne 1$.

Define $\psi : D \to U$ as follows. For odd $p$, $\psi = \chi$: in class (i), $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) \subseteq 1 + q\mathbb{Z}_p \subseteq U$, as $q$ is the largest power of $p$ with this property ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md)). For $p = 2$, every unit of $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is congruent to $\pm 1$ modulo $4$, so $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times = \{\pm 1\} \times U$, and $\psi = \pi \circ \chi$ for the projection $\pi : \mathbb{Z}_2^\times \to U$, $\pi(x) = \pm x$ with the sign making $\pi(x) \equiv 1 \bmod 4$. Then $\psi$ is a continuous homomorphism with $\psi \circ \alpha = \psi$ for all $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, and $\psi \ne 1$:

- in class (i), $\chi \ne 1$, since otherwise $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = 1$ lies in $1 + p^k\mathbb{Z}_p$ for every $k$ and $q = 0$; and in class (i) with $p = 2$, $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) \subseteq 1 + q\mathbb{Z}_2 \subseteq U$, so $\psi = \chi$;
- in class (ii), $-1 + 2^f \in \mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ and $\pi(-1 + 2^f) = 1 - 2^f \ne 1$, as $f$ is finite.

The image $\mathrm{Im}(\psi)$ is an abelian pro-$p$ group, hence a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module, written multiplicatively. As $U$ is abelian and torsion-free, $\psi$ factors through $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and kills $T$, so it induces a continuous, hence $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear, surjection $\bar\psi : V \to \mathrm{Im}(\psi)$. So $\mathrm{Im}(\psi)$ is a nonzero, finitely generated, torsion-free module over the principal ideal domain $\mathbb{Z}_p$, hence free of positive rank. Choose a nonzero $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear $\lambda : \mathrm{Im}(\psi) \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ and put $\varphi = \lambda \circ \bar\psi$. It is nonzero, and $\varphi \circ \rho(\alpha) = \varphi$ for every $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, because $\psi \circ \alpha = \psi$.

Applying this to $\sigma_i$ and $\sigma'_i$ gives $\varphi \circ E_i = \varphi \circ E'_i = 0$, so $\varphi(v_{2i}) = \varphi(v_{2i-1}) = 0$ for $2 \le i \le g$. Hence $W \subseteq \ker\varphi$, and as $\varphi \ne 0$ and $V = \mathbb{Z}_pv_2 \oplus W$, $\varphi(v_2) \ne 0$ and $\ker\varphi = W$.

*Step 3: the common centralizer is scalar.* Let $f \in \mathrm{End}(V)$ commute with $E_i$, $E'_i$ and $E''_i$ for $2 \le i \le g$, and write $v_k^*$ for the coordinate functions of the basis $v_2, \dots, v_n$. Evaluating $fE_i = E_if$ at $v_{2i-1}$ gives $f(v_{2i}) = v_{2i-1}^*(f(v_{2i-1}))\,v_{2i}$, and at $v_k$ with $k \ne 2i - 1$ it gives $v_{2i-1}^*(f(v_k)) = 0$. Likewise $fE'_i = E'_if$ gives $f(v_{2i-1}) = v_{2i}^*(f(v_{2i}))\,v_{2i-1}$, and $v_{2i}^*(f(v_k)) = 0$ for $k \ne 2i$. So $f(v_{2i-1}) = \mu_iv_{2i-1}$ and $f(v_{2i}) = \mu_iv_{2i}$ with the same $\mu_i$, and every coordinate of $f(v_2)$ other than $v_2^*$ vanishes, so $f(v_2) = cv_2$ for some $c \in \mathbb{Z}_p$. Finally, evaluating $fE''_i = E''_if$ at $v_2$ gives $\mu_iv_{2i-1} = f(v_{2i-1}) = E''_i(cv_2) = cv_{2i-1}$, so $\mu_i = c$. Hence $f = c \cdot \mathrm{id}_V$.

*Step 4: proof of parts 2 and 3.* Let $f \in \Gamma$ commute with a positive power of each element of $\Gamma$. Taking the product of the exponents, there is one $N \ge 1$ such that $f$ commutes with $1 + NE_i$, $1 + NE'_i$ and $1 + NE''_i$ for $2 \le i \le g$. As $\mathrm{End}(V)$ is torsion-free, $f$ commutes with $E_i$, $E'_i$ and $E''_i$, so $f = c \cdot \mathrm{id}_V$ by Step 3. By part 1, $c\varphi = \varphi \circ f = \varphi \ne 0$, so $c = 1$ and $f = 1$.

If $f \in \Gamma$ has finitely many conjugates, its centralizer $Z$ has finite index in $\Gamma$. Each $\gamma \in \Gamma$ permutes the finitely many cosets in $\Gamma/Z$ by left multiplication, so some $\gamma^N$ with $N \ge 1$ fixes the coset $Z$, that is, $\gamma^N \in Z$. So the first assertion applies. Central elements and elements of finite normal subgroups have finitely many conjugates. This proves part 2.

For part 3, the conjugates of $\rho(\theta)$ in $\Gamma$ are the images of the conjugates of $\theta$, so there are finitely many, and $\rho(\theta) = 1$ by part 2. Elements of a finite normal subgroup have finitely many conjugates. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Odd $p$.* For odd $p$ the theorem is [the odd case](outer-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-with-finitely-many-conjugates.md), and the proof here is a second proof of it. There, an element of $\Gamma$ acting on $V$ as a transvection $v_2 \mapsto v_2 + w_0$ with $w_0 \in W$, fixing $W$, is obtained only modulo $q$, from the [mod-$q$ lifting theorem](automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-modulo-q.md), which is stated for odd $p$, and a lemma on sums of powers handles the error. Here $\tau_2\tau_3\cdots\tau_g$ acts on $V$ exactly as the transvection $v_2 \mapsto v_2 + v_3 + v_5 + \cdots + v_{n-1}$ fixing $W$, for every $p$ and every exponent $m$, so neither input is needed.
- *Where the hypotheses enter.* The automorphisms of Step 1 fix $x_1$ and $R$, so they exist for every relation $x_1^mR$, including $q = 0$. The hypotheses are used only to obtain the nonzero invariant form of part 1 from $\chi$. For $q = 0$ the theorem is false: for the pro-$p$ surface groups every scalar in $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ lies in $\Gamma$ ([symplectic similitudes lift](symplectic-similitudes-lift-to-pro-p-surface-groups.md)). The other groups with $q = 2$ are not treated here. Those of odd rank, and those of even rank with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\} \times U_2^{(f)}$, have other normal forms ([the classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), items 2 and 3), so Step 1 would need other automorphisms. For those with relation $x_1^2R$ and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = U_2^{[\infty]} = \{\pm 1\}$, Step 1 applies but $\pi \circ \chi$ is trivial, so Step 2 gives no invariant form.
- *What is left for Tsujimura's question.* By Corollary 2 here and Corollary 2 of [automorphisms of order $p$](order-p-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-act-nontrivially-modulo-torsion.md), for rank at least $3$ and $q \notin \{0, 2\}$ a closed normal copy $N \ne D$ of $D$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ can only meet $D$ as in case (b). Case (b) is open. For $q = 2$, case (a) is open too: part 3 applies to class (ii), but no analogue of the theorem on [involutions with $q$ divisible by four](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-q-divisible-by-four.md) is recorded for $q = 2$.
- *Sources.* The statements and proofs are not taken from a source. Identity (1) was found by solving for the images of $x_2$ and $x_4$ under an automorphism fixing $x_1$ and $[x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4]$ with the required action on $V$; it was also checked by machine by free reduction, together with the claims of Step 1 for $g \le 5$. No novelty is claimed.
