---
title: The maximal abelian subfield of a 2-adic field
kind: theorem
summary: 'For $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ of odd degree, or with $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ ramified and $K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$ not real, the largest subfield of $K$ abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ is generated by that intersection and the unramified part of $K$. If the intersection is real, it can be larger.'
tags: [local-fields]
---

**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite of degree $n$ and residue degree $f$, inside a fixed algebraic closure. Let $U_m$ be the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of degree $m$, so that $K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}} = U_f$. Put $K^0 = K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and $K' = K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$. The map sending $a$ to the automorphism $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^a$ identifies $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})/\mathbb{Q}_2)$ [@labute1967, §5, p. 131]. Call a subfield of $\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$ *real* if $-1$ fixes it, that is, if $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^{-1}$ does. Put $U^{(2)} = 1 + 4\mathbb{Z}_2$. It is the subgroup fixing $\sqrt{-1}$, since $a$ fixes $\zeta_4$ exactly when $a \equiv 1 \bmod 4$.

**Statement.**

1. If $n$ is odd, then $K' = \mathbb{Q}_2$, $K^0 = U_f$, and $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified.
2. If $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified and $K'$ is not real, then $K^0 = K' U_f$.
3. If $\sqrt{-1} \notin K$, then $K(\sqrt{-1}) \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}} = K^0(\sqrt{-1})$, and $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is unramified exactly when $K^0(\sqrt{-1})/K^0$ is. So the largest power $q$ of $2$ with $\mu_q \subset K(\sqrt{-1})$ depends only on $K^0$.

In cases 1 and 2, $K^0$ and the pair $(f, K')$ determine each other, since $K^0 \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}} = U_f$ and $K^0 \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty}) = K'$. The examples below show that part 2 needs both of its hypotheses.

Schneider sorts the fields with $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ ramified into three classes: $\mathfrak{L}$ for odd $n$, and, for even $n$, $\mathfrak{M}_\alpha$ when $K'$ is real and $\mathfrak{N}_\alpha$ when it is not, with an integer $\alpha \ge 2$ read off from $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})/K')$ [@schneider2016, p. 15 and Remark 2.2]. Parts 1 and 2 cover $\mathfrak{L}$ and the $\mathfrak{N}_\alpha$. For what this means for absolute Galois groups, see the [classification of $p$-adic fields with isomorphic absolute Galois groups](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) and [Schneider's conjecture](schneider-conjecture-on-2-adic-galois-groups.md).

**Proof.** Let $G = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q}_2)$, let $I = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}})$, and let $r : G \to \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ be restriction to $\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$. Take the prime element $2$ in Milne's description of $\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Then $\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the compositum of $\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$ and $\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$, the group $I$ is the image $\phi(\mathbb{Z}_2^\times)$ of the units under the local Artin map, and $\phi$ restricts to an isomorphism $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times \to \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})/\mathbb{Q}_2)$ [@milnecft, I, 1.10–1.11]. Hence:

- $(\ast)$ $r$ maps $I$ isomorphically onto $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times = \{\pm 1\} \times U^{(2)}$. In particular $I$ is a pro-$2$ group.

Let $H = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}/K^0)$, an open subgroup of $G$.

- (a) $HI = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}/U_f)$. Indeed $HI$ is the subgroup fixing $K^0 \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$, which contains $U_f$ because $U_f \subseteq K$ is abelian, and lies in $K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}} = U_f$.
- (b) $B := r(H) = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})/K')$, because $K' \subseteq K^0 \subseteq K$ gives $K^0 \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty}) = K'$.
- (c) $H \cap I = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}/K^0\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}})$. It is open in $I$, so by $(\ast)$, $C := r(H \cap I)$ is an open subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ contained in $B$.
- (d) If $C = B$, then $K^0 = K'U_f$. Since $K'U_f \subseteq K^0$, it suffices to show that every $g \in G$ fixing $K'U_f$ lies in $H$. By (a), $g = h\iota$ with $h \in H$ and $\iota \in I$, and $r(g) \in B$ by (b). Then $r(\iota) = r(h)^{-1}r(g) \in B = C$, so $r(\iota) = r(\iota')$ for some $\iota' \in H \cap I$. By $(\ast)$, $\iota = \iota' \in H$, so $g \in H$.

*Part 3.* Suppose $\sqrt{-1} \notin K$, and let $M = K(\sqrt{-1}) \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Then $K^0(\sqrt{-1}) \subseteq M$ and $M \cap K = K^0$. As $M/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is Galois, restriction identifies $\mathrm{Gal}(MK/K)$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(M/M \cap K)$. So $[M : K^0] = [MK : K] \le 2 = [K^0(\sqrt{-1}) : K^0]$, and $M = K^0(\sqrt{-1})$. Since $\mu_q \subset K(\sqrt{-1})$ exactly when $\mu_q \subset M$, the number $q$ depends only on $K^0$. If $K^0(\sqrt{-1})/K^0$ is unramified, then so is $K(\sqrt{-1}) = K \cdot K^0(\sqrt{-1})$ over $K$. Conversely, if $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is unramified, it is the unramified quadratic extension $KU_{2f}$ of $K$. Then $U_{2f} \subseteq M = K^0(\sqrt{-1})$, and $U_{2f} \not\subseteq K^0$ by (a), so $K^0(\sqrt{-1}) = K^0U_{2f}$ is unramified over $K^0$.

*Part 1.* $[K^0 : \mathbb{Q}_2]$ divides $n$, so $G/H$ has odd order, and the image of the pro-$2$ group $I$ in it is trivial. So $I \subseteq H$ and $C = r(I) = \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$. Then $B = \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$, so $K' = \mathbb{Q}_2$, and $K^0 = U_f$ by (d). Since $(x + 1)^2 + 1 = x^2 + 2x + 2$ is Eisenstein, $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is ramified, so $U_f(\sqrt{-1})/U_f$ is ramified too. As $\sqrt{-1} \notin K$, part 3 shows that $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified.

*Part 2.* By part 3, $K^0(\sqrt{-1})/K^0$ is ramified. The maximal unramified extension of $K^0$ is $K^0\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$, so $\sqrt{-1} \notin K^0\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$. By (c), this says that $C \not\subseteq U^{(2)}$. As $K'$ is not real, $-1 \notin B$, so the projection $\{\pm 1\} \times U^{(2)} \to U^{(2)}$ is injective on $B$. The logarithm maps $U^{(2)}$ isomorphically onto $4\mathbb{Z}_2$ [@labute1967, Remarks after the Corollary to Theorem 4]. So $B$ is isomorphic to a nonzero closed subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}_2$, hence to $\mathbb{Z}_2$, and its open subgroups are $B \supset B^2 \supset B^4 \supset \cdots$. Every square in $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ is $\equiv 1 \bmod 8$, so $B^2 \subseteq U^{(2)}$. Since $C$ is open in $B$ and $C \not\subseteq U^{(2)}$, we get $C = B$, and $K^0 = K'U_f$ by (d). $\square$

**Examples.** These use the quadratic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ and the quadratic subfields $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})$, $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{2})$, $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-2})$ of $\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$, as worked out in [maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md), Example 2. The unramified quadratic extension is $U_2 = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{5})$, since $(1 + \sqrt{5})/2$ is a root of $x^2 - x - 1$, which is irreducible modulo $2$. A biquadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ none of whose quadratic subfields is $U_2$ is totally ramified, since otherwise it would contain $U_2$.

- *Part 2 needs $K'$ not real.* Let $K = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{10})$ and $L = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-10})$. Both are ramified quadratic extensions, since $\pm 10$ has odd valuation, so $n = 2$ and $f = 1$. Neither is one of the three quadratic subfields of $\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$, so $K' = L' = \mathbb{Q}_2$, which is real. The field $K(\sqrt{-1}) = L(\sqrt{-1})$ has quadratic subfields $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{10})$, $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})$ and $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-10})$. None of them is $U_2$, because $5/10$, $-5$ and $5/(-10)$ are not squares in $\mathbb{Q}_2$, so $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ and $L(\sqrt{-1})/L$ are ramified. None of them is $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{2})$ either, because $2/10$, $-2$ and $2/(-10)$ are not squares, so $\zeta_8 \notin K(\sqrt{-1})$ and $q = 4$ for both fields. Yet $K^0 = K$ and $L^0 = L$ differ, while $K'U_f = \mathbb{Q}_2$.
- *Part 2 needs $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ ramified.* Let $K = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-2}, \sqrt{-5})$, with quadratic subfields $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-2})$, $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-5})$ and $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{10})$. None of them is $U_2$, so $f = 1$. Only $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-2})$ lies in $\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$, so $K' = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-2})$. It is not real, since $-1$ maps $\sqrt{-2} = \zeta_8 + \zeta_8^3$ to $\zeta_8^{-1} + \zeta_8^{-3} = -\sqrt{-2}$. Here $K(\sqrt{-1}) = K(\sqrt{5})$, because $(\sqrt{-1}\sqrt{-5})^2 = 5$, so $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is unramified. And $K^0 = K \ne K'U_f$.
