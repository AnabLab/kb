**Setting.** Let $\mathbb F$ be a finite field with algebraic closure $\bar{\mathbb F}$, and $G_{\mathbb F} = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb F}/\mathbb F)$. Let $C$ and $D$ be smooth, proper, geometrically integral curves over $\mathbb F$, put $K = \mathbb F(D)$, and regard $C$ also as the constant curve $C \times_{\mathbb F} K$. The places of $K$ are the closed points $v \in D^1$, with completions $K_v$ and residue fields $\mathbb F_v \subset \bar{\mathbb F}$, which are fixed by embeddings of a separable closure of $K$ into separable closures of the $K_v$. The *locally constant adelic points* form the closed subset $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F}) = \prod_v C(\mathbb F_v)$ of $C(\mathbb A_K) = \prod_v C(K_v)$, and a point of $C(\mathbb F)$ gives a *constant* adelic point. An adelic point *survives* a torsor $P \to C_K$ under a finite group scheme $G$ over $K$ if its family of evaluations in $\prod_v H^1(K_v, G)$ lies in the diagonal image of $H^1(K, G)$. Write $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ for the locally constant adelic points that survive every torsor under a finite étale $K$-group scheme [@creutzvoloch2024, §§1.1, 2.1 and 2.2, pp. 2–5].

Two larger sets are introduced here: $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F}$ and $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F,\mathrm c}$, the locally constant adelic points that survive every torsor $P \to C$ defined over $\mathbb F$ under a finite étale, respectively finite constant, $\mathbb F$-group scheme. Thus
$$C(\mathbb F) \subseteq C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathrm{\acute et}} \subseteq C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F} \subseteq C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F,\mathrm c}.$$

Let $p_C : \pi_1(C) \to G_{\mathbb F}$ be the projection, with kernel $\pi_1(\bar C) = \pi_1(C \times_{\mathbb F} \bar{\mathbb F})$, and similarly for $D$. Identify $\pi_1(D)$ with the Galois group of the maximal everywhere unramified extension of $K$. Each place $v$ then gives a section $t_v : G_{\mathbb F_v} \to \pi_1(D)$ whose image $T_v$ is a decomposition group above $v$ [@creutzvoloch2024, §3, p. 5]. For a pro-point $\tilde w$ of the universal cover $\tilde C$ over a closed point $w$, its stabilizer $D_{\tilde w} \subseteq \pi_1(C)$ is the decomposition group, and $p_C$ maps it isomorphically onto $G_{\mathbb F_w}$. An $\mathbb F_v$-point $x$ of $C$ has sections $G_{\mathbb F_v} \to \pi_1(C)$, unique up to $\pi_1(\bar C)$-conjugacy, whose images are the groups $D_{\tilde x} \cap p_C^{-1}(G_{\mathbb F_v})$ for the pro-points $\tilde x$ above $x$.

A continuous homomorphism $\phi : \pi_1(D) \to \pi_1(C)$ is *well behaved* if it maps every decomposition group of $\pi_1(D)$ to an open subgroup of a decomposition group of $\pi_1(C)$ [@creutzvoloch2024, Definition 3.1, p. 5]. It is *over $G_{\mathbb F}$* if $p_C \circ \phi = p_D$.

**Theorem.** Assume that $C$ has genus at least $2$.

1. *Interpolation, in a weaker form than the paper's* [@creutzvoloch2024, Proposition 3.3, p. 6]. For every $(x_v) \in C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F,\mathrm c}$, and in particular for every $(x_v) \in C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathrm{\acute et}}$, there are a well-behaved $\phi$ over $G_{\mathbb F}$, sections $s_v$ of the points $x_v$, and elements $\gamma_v \in \pi_1(C)$ with $\phi \circ t_v = \gamma_v s_v \gamma_v^{-1}$ for every $v$.
2. *The adelic point of a homomorphism* [@creutzvoloch2024, Construction 3.5 and Lemma 3.7, p. 7]. Let $\phi$ be well behaved over $G_{\mathbb F}$, and for each $v$ choose a pro-point $\tilde w_v$ with $\phi(T_v) \subseteq D_{\tilde w_v}$. Then $\phi(T_v) = D_{\tilde w_v} \cap p_C^{-1}(G_{\mathbb F_v})$, the image $x_v$ of $\tilde w_v$ in $C \times_{\mathbb F} \mathbb F_v$ is an $\mathbb F_v$-rational point, $\phi \circ t_v$ is a section of $x_v$, and $(x_v) \in C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F}$.
3. *Dichotomy* [@creutzvoloch2024, Corollary 3.10, p. 8]. Let $\phi$ be well behaved over $G_{\mathbb F}$. Either $\phi(\pi_1(D))$ is open in $\pi_1(C)$, or $\phi = s \circ p_D$ for a section $s$ of a point of $C(\mathbb F)$. In the second case every adelic point attached to $\phi$ in part 2 is constant, and in the first case none is.
4. *Descent in terms of homomorphisms.* The following are equivalent: (a) some well-behaved $\phi$ over $G_{\mathbb F}$ has open image; (b) $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F} \neq C(\mathbb F)$; (c) $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F,\mathrm c} \neq C(\mathbb F)$.
5. *Isogeny criterion* [@creutzvoloch2024, Theorem 1.3, p. 3]. If (a) holds, then the Jacobian $J_C$ is an isogeny factor of $J_D$. Hence, if $J_C$ is not an isogeny factor of $J_D$, then every morphism $D \to C$ is constant, $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathrm{\acute et}} = C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F,\mathrm c} = C(\mathbb F)$, the [conjecture on étale descent for constant curves](etale-descent-conjecture-for-constant-curves.md) holds for $C$ and $D$, and every well-behaved homomorphism over $G_{\mathbb F}$ is $s \circ p_D$ for a section $s$ of a point of $C(\mathbb F)$.

The paper states parts 1 and 3 with stronger or differently stated conclusions, discussed below, and states part 5 as the conjecture's validity. Part 4 and the last assertions of part 5 are drawn here from the parts above. Creutz and Voloch expect every open homomorphism $\pi_1(D) \to \pi_1(C)$ to be well behaved, but could not prove it [@creutzvoloch2024, p. 3].

*Sections.* Part 3 proves a claim the paper makes without proof [@creutzvoloch2024, Remark 3.2, p. 6]. If $s : G_{\mathbb F} \to \pi_1(C)$ is a section and $s \circ p_D$ is well behaved, then $s$ is the section of a point of $C(\mathbb F)$. Indeed $s(G_{\mathbb F})$ meets the infinite group $\pi_1(\bar C)$ trivially, so it is not open, and $s \circ p_D = s' \circ p_D$ with $s'$ a section of a rational point forces $s = s'$. The paper notes, citing Stix, that uncountably many sections are not conjugate to sections of points [@creutzvoloch2024, Remark 3.2, p. 6], so over a finite field the analogue of the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) fails, but it holds for the sections $s$ with $s \circ p_D$ well behaved.

**Proof.** This is written out here. Parts 1 to 3 follow the paper's arguments, with the changes explained in the next section. Three facts are used.

- (i) *Large genus.* If $X$ is a smooth, proper, geometrically integral curve over $\mathbb F$ of genus greater than that of $D$, then $X(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F} = X(\mathbb F)$. Torsors pulled back from étale isogenies $J' \to J_X$ along an embedding $X \to J_X$ are torsors under finite étale $\mathbb F$-group schemes [@creutzvoloch2022, §2.4, p. 4]. So $X(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F}$ lies in the set $X(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\text{ét-isog}}$ of locally constant points surviving them, which equals $X(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathrm{isog}}$ [@creutzvoloch2022, Proposition 4.1, p. 6]. That set lies in $X(\mathbb A_K)^{\mathrm{isog}} = X(\mathbb A_K)^{\mathrm{Br}}$ [@creutzvoloch2022, Proposition 2.2, p. 4], which is $X(\mathbb F)$ [@creutzvoloch2022, Theorem 1.5, p. 3].
- (ii) *Density.* For every finite quotient of $\pi_1(D)$, every element is a Frobenius element of a closed point, by the [Chebotarev density theorem](chebotarev-density-for-arithmetic-schemes.md), which needs no geometric connectedness hypothesis. So the conjugates of the groups $T_v$ have dense union in $\pi_1(D)$.
- (iii) *Jordan.* A finite group $Q$ is not the union of the conjugates of a proper subgroup $P$, since that union has at most $[Q:P](|P| - 1) + 1 < |Q|$ elements.

*Part 1.* Let $\rho_G : \pi_1(C) \to G$ be a finite quotient. It defines a torsor over $C$ under the constant group $G$, whose evaluation at $x_v$ is the class of $\rho_G \circ s_v$ in $H^1(\mathbb F_v, G) \subseteq H^1(K_v, G)$, by inflation. Survival gives $s \in H^1(K, G)$ restricting to these classes. Each restriction kills inertia, so $s$ factors through $\pi_1(D)$, and $s \circ t_v$ is $G$-conjugate to $\rho_G \circ s_v$ for every $v$. So the set $S_G$ of homomorphisms $s' : \pi_1(D) \to G$ with $s' \circ t_v$ conjugate to $\rho_G \circ s_v$ under $G$ for every $v$ is not empty. It is finite, by the argument in the paper, which uses the Borel–Serre theorem [@creutzvoloch2024, proof of Proposition 3.3, p. 6]. These sets form an inverse system, and an element of the limit is a homomorphism $\phi : \pi_1(D) \to \pi_1(C)$ such that $\rho_G \circ \phi \circ t_v$ is $G$-conjugate to $\rho_G \circ s_v$ for all $G$ and $v$. For fixed $v$, the sets of conjugating elements of $G$ are finite, nonempty and compatible, so their limit gives $\gamma_v$ with $\phi \circ t_v = \gamma_v s_v \gamma_v^{-1}$. As $G_{\mathbb F}$ is abelian, $p_C \circ \phi$ agrees with $p_D$ on every conjugate of every $T_v$, hence everywhere by (ii). Finally, if $\tilde x_v$ is a pro-point with $s_v(G_{\mathbb F_v}) = D_{\tilde x_v} \cap p_C^{-1}(G_{\mathbb F_v})$, then $\phi(T_v) = \gamma_v s_v(G_{\mathbb F_v}) \gamma_v^{-1}$ is open in $D_{\gamma_v \tilde x_v}$, and conjugates of $T_v$ go to conjugates of it, so $\phi$ is well behaved.

*Part 2.* Let $w$ be the closed point below $\tilde w_v$. The map $p_C$ sends $\phi(T_v)$ onto $G_{\mathbb F_v}$, because $\phi$ is over $G_{\mathbb F}$, and it is injective on $D_{\tilde w_v}$, with image $G_{\mathbb F_w}$. So $\mathbb F_w \subseteq \mathbb F_v$, and $D_{\tilde w_v} \cap p_C^{-1}(G_{\mathbb F_v})$ maps isomorphically onto $G_{\mathbb F_v}$ and contains $\phi(T_v)$, so it equals $\phi(T_v)$. Hence $\tilde w_v$ lies above an $\mathbb F_v$-rational point $x_v$, and $\phi \circ t_v$ is the section of $x_v$ with that image. Let $P \to C$ be a torsor under a finite étale $\mathbb F$-group scheme $G$, with class $\alpha \in H^1(\pi_1(C), G(\bar{\mathbb F}))$, where $\pi_1(C)$ acts through $G_{\mathbb F}$. Its evaluation at $x_v$ is $(\phi \circ t_v)^* \alpha = t_v^*(\phi^* \alpha)$. Here $\phi^*\alpha \in H^1(\pi_1(D), G(\bar{\mathbb F}))$ makes sense because $\phi$ is over $G_{\mathbb F}$. Inflated to $H^1(K, G)$, it restricts at each $v$ to the evaluation at $x_v$. This is the paper's proof of Lemma 3.7 [@creutzvoloch2024, proof of Lemma 3.7, pp. 7–8].

*Part 3.* Let $H = \phi(\pi_1(D))$, suppose that $H$ is not open, and choose the $\tilde w_v$ as in part 2. For an open subgroup $U \supseteq H$, let $C_U \to C$ be the étale cover with $\pi_1(C_U) = U$. It is geometrically connected because $p_C(U) = G_{\mathbb F}$, and its genus $g_U$ satisfies $g_U - 1 = [\pi_1(C) : U](g(C) - 1)$. Since $H$ is closed of infinite index, the set $\mathcal U$ of open $U \supseteq H$ with $g_U > g(D)$ is cofinal among the open subgroups containing $H$, so $\bigcap_{U \in \mathcal U} U = H$. For $U \in \mathcal U$, $\phi$ is a well-behaved homomorphism to $U = \pi_1(C_U)$ over $G_{\mathbb F}$, with the same pro-points $\tilde w_v$. By part 2 and (i), the adelic point of $C_U$ it gives is constant, equal to some $y_U \in C_U(\mathbb F)$. So every $\tilde w_v$ lies above $y_U$.

Fix $\tilde w = \tilde w_{v_0}$. Since $y_U$ has residue field $\mathbb F$, $p_C(D_{\tilde w} \cap U) = G_{\mathbb F}$, and as $p_C$ is injective on $D_{\tilde w}$, we get $D_{\tilde w} \subseteq U$. Hence $D_{\tilde w} \subseteq H$. The group $U$ acts transitively on the pro-points above $y_U$, so $\{u \in U : u\tilde w = \tilde w_v\}$ is a nonempty closed set, which shrinks with $U$. By compactness there is $u_v \in H$ with $\tilde w_v = u_v \tilde w$. For $g \in \pi_1(D)$ this gives $\phi(g T_v g^{-1}) \subseteq h D_{\tilde w} h^{-1}$ with $h = \phi(g) u_v \in H$, so by (ii) $H$ lies in the closure of $\bigcup_{h \in H} h D_{\tilde w} h^{-1}$. If $D_{\tilde w} \neq H$, choose an open normal subgroup $N$ of $H$ with $D_{\tilde w} N \neq H$. By (iii), $\bigcup_{h \in H} h D_{\tilde w} N h^{-1}$ is a proper closed subset of $H$ containing every $h D_{\tilde w} h^{-1}$, a contradiction. So $H = D_{\tilde w}$. Since $p_C$ maps $D_{\tilde w}$ isomorphically onto $G_{\mathbb F}$, $\phi = (p_C|_{D_{\tilde w}})^{-1} \circ p_D$, which is $s \circ p_D$ for the section $s$ of the point $x \in C(\mathbb F)$ below $\tilde w$. Moreover $u_v \in D_{\tilde w}$ fixes $\tilde w$, so every $\tilde w_v$ equals $\tilde w$, and the adelic point is constant. The choice of the $\tilde w_v$ was arbitrary.

Conversely, suppose some adelic point attached to $\phi$ is constant, equal to $x \in C(\mathbb F)$. Then each $D_{\tilde w_v}$ is conjugate to $D_{\tilde x}$ for a fixed pro-point $\tilde x$ above $x$. So by (ii), $H$ lies in $\Sigma = \bigcup_{g \in \pi_1(C)} g D_{\tilde x} g^{-1}$, which is closed as the image of the compact space $\pi_1(C) \times D_{\tilde x}$. But $\Sigma \cap \pi_1(\bar C) = 1$, as $p_C$ is injective on each conjugate of $D_{\tilde x}$, while $\pi_1(\bar C)$ is infinite because it has $T_\ell J_C \neq 0$ as a quotient. So $H$ is not open.

*Part 4.* If $\phi$ is as in (a), its adelic point from part 2 lies in $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F}$ and is not constant, by part 3, which gives (b). Next, (b) implies (c) by inclusion. Assume (c), take $(x_v) \in C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F,\mathrm c} \setminus C(\mathbb F)$, and let $\phi$, $s_v$, $\gamma_v$ be as in part 1. Choose pro-points $\tilde x_v$ with $s_v(G_{\mathbb F_v}) = D_{\tilde x_v} \cap p_C^{-1}(G_{\mathbb F_v})$, and put $\tilde w_v = \gamma_v \tilde x_v$, so that $\phi(T_v) \subseteq D_{\tilde w_v}$. The image of $\tilde w_v$ in $C \times_{\mathbb F} \mathbb F_v$ is the conjugate of $x_v$ by $p_C(\gamma_v)$. If $\phi$ did not have open image, part 3 would make this image a single point $x \in C(\mathbb F)$ for every $v$, which is fixed by $G_{\mathbb F}$, so $x_v = x$ for every $v$, a contradiction. So (a) holds.

*Part 5.* Let $\phi$ be as in (a), with image $H$. If $g(D) < g(C)$, its adelic point is constant by part 2 and (i), contradicting part 3. So $g(D) \ge g(C) \ge 2$. Since $\phi$ is over $G_{\mathbb F}$, $\phi(\pi_1(\bar D)) = H \cap \pi_1(\bar C)$, which is open in $\pi_1(\bar C)$. Fix a prime $\ell$ different from the characteristic. Passing to maximal abelian pro-$\ell$ quotients, which are $T_\ell J_D$ and $T_\ell J_C$ as $G_{\mathbb F}$-modules for curves of genus at least $2$ (see [Tate's theorems](tate-isogeny-theorem-over-finite-fields.md)), gives a $G_{\mathbb F}$-equivariant map $T_\ell J_D \to T_\ell J_C$ with open image. Equivariance holds because $\phi$ maps lifts of Frobenius to lifts of Frobenius. So $V_\ell J_D \to V_\ell J_C$ is surjective. By Tate's theorem $\mathrm{Hom}(J_D, J_C) \otimes \mathbb Q_\ell \cong \mathrm{Hom}_{G_{\mathbb F}}(V_\ell J_D, V_\ell J_C)$. Surjectivity is the nonvanishing of some maximal minor, a polynomial in the coordinates for a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\mathrm{Hom}(J_D, J_C)$. It is not identically zero, so it is nonzero at an integral point. So some $f : J_D \to J_C$ has $V_\ell(f)$ surjective, hence is surjective, and $J_C$ is an isogeny factor of $J_D$ by Poincaré reducibility. The paper reaches a surjection of Tate modules through its Proposition 3.9 and a corollary in its earlier paper, and also concludes with Tate's theorem [@creutzvoloch2024, §4.1, p. 8].

If $J_C$ is not an isogeny factor of $J_D$, then (a) fails, so by part 4 $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F,\mathrm c} = C(\mathbb F)$, and hence $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathrm{\acute et}} = C(\mathbb F)$. A nonconstant morphism $D \to C$ would induce a surjection $J_D \to J_C$, so $C(K) = \mathrm{Mor}_{\mathbb F}(D, C)$ consists of the constant maps $C(\mathbb F)$. This set is finite and closed, and its reduction is $C(\mathbb F) = C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathrm{\acute et}}$, which is the conjecture. Every well-behaved $\phi$ over $G_{\mathbb F}$ has image that is not open, so part 3 applies. $\square$

**What the paper's proofs need.** These points were found in checking the paper against the proof above.

- *Homomorphisms over $G_{\mathbb F}$.* The paper's Definition 3.1 does not require this, but its Proposition 3.3 produces such homomorphisms and its proof of Lemma 3.7 uses the property. Without it, the injectivity in its Theorem 3.8 fails. Let $x \in C(\mathbb F)$ have section $s_x$, and let $m_u$ be multiplication by a unit $u \neq 1$ of $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ on $G_{\mathbb F} \cong \widehat{\mathbb Z}$. The maps $s_x \circ p_D$ and $s_x \circ m_u \circ p_D$ are both well behaved and both give the constant adelic point $x$. They are not conjugate, since $p_C \circ \phi$ is unchanged by conjugation.
- *Proposition 3.3.* The paper asserts $\gamma_v \in \pi_1(\bar C)$, which would make the adelic point of $\phi$ in part 2 exactly $(x_v)$ rather than a family of Galois conjugates of the $x_v$. Its proof requires the conjugation in $S_G$ to be by $G_v = \rho_G(\pi_1(C \times_{\mathbb F} \mathbb F_v))$. It gets this from the claim that $H^1(\mathbb F_v, G_v) \to H^1(\mathbb F_v, G)$ is injective because $G$ is constant [@creutzvoloch2024, proof of Proposition 3.3, p. 6]. That claim fails for finite groups in general. For $G = S_3$ and $G_v = A_3$, the three classes in $H^1(\mathbb F_v, A_3) = A_3$ map to two classes in $H^1(\mathbb F_v, S_3)$, since the two $3$-cycles are conjugate in $S_3$. Moreover the proof uses only torsors under constant groups defined over $\mathbb F$. Replacing $x_v$ by a $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb F_v/\mathbb F)$-conjugate replaces $\rho_G \circ s_v$ by a $G$-conjugate, so these torsors cannot tell conjugate points apart. Parts 1 to 5 use only the weaker conclusion, with $\gamma_v \in \pi_1(C)$, which the paper's limit argument gives. Whether the stronger conclusion holds is not settled here.
- *Lemma 3.7.* Its proof treats torsors under group schemes over $\mathbb F$, which gives membership in $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathbb F}$, as in part 2, rather than in $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathrm{\acute et}}$, which is defined with group schemes over $K$. The proofs above need only part 2 as stated.
- *Theorem 3.8 and Proposition 3.9.* The paper asserts a bijection between $C(\mathbb A_{K,\mathbb F})^{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ and well-behaved homomorphisms up to $\pi_1(\bar C)$-conjugacy, and a version with closed points and $\pi_1(C)$-conjugacy. It asserts without argument that the map from homomorphisms to adelic points is injective [@creutzvoloch2024, Theorem 3.8 and its proof, p. 8]. Its Proposition 3.9 asserts, for a nonconstant point, that the corresponding homomorphism has open image, which part 4 proves, and that the induced map $D(\bar{\mathbb F}) \to C(\bar{\mathbb F})$ is surjective [@creutzvoloch2024, Proposition 3.9, p. 8]. The injectivity and the surjectivity are not checked here, and neither is used above.

**Not checked.** The paper's Theorem 1.5, conditional on a conjecture of Sutherland and Voloch, is described in the [conjecture's entry](etale-descent-conjecture-for-constant-curves.md) and is not checked here.
