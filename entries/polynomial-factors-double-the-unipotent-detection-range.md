**Theorem (proved here).** Let $\mathbb F$ be a finite field of characteristic $p$ and cardinality $Q$. Let $G=G_1*\cdots*G_s$ be a finite free pro-$p$ product whose factors are any of:

- free pro-$p$ groups of finite rank;
- free abelian pro-$p$ groups of finite rank;
- Demushkin groups of finite rank at least three;
- rank-two Demushkin groups with classification invariant $q\ne2$;
- for $p=2$, copies of $\mathbb Z/2$.

For every $1\le n\le2Q+2$,
$$
G_{(n)}=\bigcap_{\rho:G\to U_n(\mathbb F)}\ker\rho,
\tag{1}
$$
where $G_{(n)}$ is the $p$-Zassenhaus filtration and homomorphisms are continuous. Thus the range over $\mathbb F_p$ is $n\le2p+2$: for $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$, this includes $n=6$ at $p=2$ and $n=8$ at $p=3$.

**Proof.** Trivial factors can be omitted. Use the notation of [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md). For a factor $H$, put
$$
A(H)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}I_H^k/I_H^{k+1},\qquad
K_k(H)=\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)\subseteq A(H)\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}\mathbb F,
$$
where $I_H$ is the augmentation ideal of $\mathbb F_p[H]$. The space $K_k(H)$ is the common kernel of the corner functionals induced by all $H\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$. Write $A_i=A(G_i)\otimes\mathbb F$, $K_{i,k}=K_k(G_i)$, $A=A(G)\otimes\mathbb F$, and let $J\subseteq A$ be the ideal generated by all $K_{i,k}$. The invisible-ideal theorem gives
$$
J=\mathrm{Inv}(G,\mathbb F).
\tag{2}
$$
Each factor has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_p$ for every $n$, hence over $\mathbb F$. For rank-two Demushkin groups this is also [@minactan2015kernel, Proposition 5.4, p. 14]. The other cases and the factor computations used below are recorded in Corollary 2 and its proof in the linked entry.

*1. Invisible polynomials.* The only factors with a possibly nonzero invisible part are the free abelian factors and rank-two Demushkin factors with $q\ne2$. For these,
$$
A_i=\mathbb F[X_1,\ldots,X_a],\qquad
\Delta(X_b)=X_b\otimes1+1\otimes X_b,
\tag{3}
$$
with $a=2$ in the latter case. For every $k\ge1$, their invisible part consists exactly of the homogeneous degree-$k$ polynomials vanishing at every point of $\mathbb F^a$.

Here is the additional verification of this last assertion in all degrees. For a representation into $U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$, let $v_j\in\mathbb F^a$ collect the $(j,j+1)$ entries of the chosen generators. In an abelian factor, commutation gives
$$
v_j(b)v_{j+1}(c)-v_j(c)v_{j+1}(b)=0
\qquad\text{for every }b,c.
\tag{4}
$$
For a nonabelian rank-two factor, the defining relation has commutator as its degree-two part, so (4) holds as well. Indeed, its power term has degree at least three, as checked in the proof of case (e) of Corollary 2 of the invisible-ideal theorem. Thus consecutive $v_j$ are linearly dependent. If one is zero, their tensor is zero; otherwise they are all proportional and $v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_k$ is a scalar multiple of $v^{\otimes k}$ for one $v\in\mathbb F^a$.

Every $v^{\otimes k}$ occurs. For an abelian factor, send the $b$th generator to $1+v(b)N$, where $N$ is the standard nilpotent Jordan block of size $k+1$; these matrices commute. For a rank-two factor, the constant-superdiagonal construction in the proof of Corollary 2(e) of the invisible-ideal theorem realizes every $v$ for every $k$. Lemma 3 there identifies the corner functional of $v^{\otimes k}$ with evaluation of the polynomial at $v$. This proves the assertion.

Put $d=Q+1$. The homogeneous polynomial lemma in [the large-field theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-over-large-finite-fields-for-free-products.md) now gives
$$
K_{i,k}=0\qquad(k<d)
\tag{5}
$$
for every factor. For the polynomial factors, consider
$$
\delta_{i,k}=\Delta_{1,k-1}:A_{i,k}\longrightarrow A_{i,1}\otimes A_{i,k-1},
\qquad
f\longmapsto\sum_bX_b\otimes\frac{\partial f}{\partial X_b}.
\tag{6}
$$
Its restriction to $K_{i,k}$ is injective whenever
$$
d\le k\le2d-2.
\tag{7}
$$
To prove this even when $p\mid k$, suppose $f\in K_{i,k}$ has every partial derivative zero. Every exponent in each monomial of $f$ is divisible by $p$. Since $\mathbb F$ is perfect, $f=h^p$ for a homogeneous polynomial $h$ of degree $k/p$, unless $f=0$ already. The vanishing of $f$ on $\mathbb F^a$ implies the vanishing of $h$. But
$$
\deg h=\frac{k}{p}\le\frac{2Q}{p}\le Q,
$$
so the same polynomial lemma makes $h=0$. This proves injectivity. For the other factors it is automatic since $K_{i,k}=0$ in every degree.

*2. Runs in the free-product algebra.* Use the normal form established in the proof of [the previous coproduct bound](primitive-obstructions-after-the-first-invisible-degree.md):
$$
A=\mathbb F\oplus\bigoplus_{r\ge1,\ i_j\ne i_{j+1}}
A_{i_1,+}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{i_r,+}.
\tag{8}
$$
The ordered factor indices and their positive degrees specify direct summands. A homogeneous tensor factor in (8) will be called a *run*, with its index as colour and its degree as length. The input behind (8) is the algebra coproduct assertion in [@minacrogelstadtan2015dimensions, proof of Lemma 2.2, p. 4], together with the comparison of abstract and completed augmentation gradings on the same page.

Within a normal-form summand, $J$ is the sum of the tensor subspaces having some run in $K_{i,k}$. Indeed each $K_i=\bigoplus_kK_{i,k}$ is an ideal in $A_i$; multiplying such a tensor by another run either concatenates it or multiplies adjacent runs of the same colour, preserving membership in that factor ideal. Conversely, every tensor with a run in $K_i$ belongs to the generated ideal $J$.

Fix $m<2d$. By (5), any nonzero summand of $J_m$ therefore has exactly one run of length at least $d$, and its component belongs to the tensor subspace with that run in $K_{i,k}$. All other runs together have length $m-k<d$.

*3. Remove pure components of group initial forms.* Let $g\in G_{(m)}$ have invisible initial form $z\in A_m$, so $z\in J_m$ by (2). Jennings' embedding identifies this initial form with $(g-1)+I_G^{m+1}$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 3.4, p. 9]. It is primitive for the graded group-algebra coproduct: the extra term $(g-1)\otimes(g-1)$ has total filtration degree at least $2m>m$.

Retract $G$ onto each factor. The induced algebra map sends $J$ into that factor's invisible ideal and sends $z$ to a group initial form. All-degree detection in the factor makes this image zero. In (8) that image is exactly the pure, single-run component of colour $i$. Consequently every pure component of $z$ vanishes.

*4. Isolate the shortest remaining invisible run.* Suppose $z\ne0$. Choose the least length $k$ of the unique long run among its nonzero mixed components. Choose one such component $z_c$, with long run of colour $i$ and length $k$. Then
$$
d\le k\le m-1\le2d-2.
$$
Let $c^-$ be obtained from its ordered run data $c$ by replacing this length $k$ by $k-1$. The colour order is unchanged, since $k-1>0$. Project $\Delta_{1,m-1}(z)$ onto
$$
A_{i,1}\otimes V_{c^-},
\tag{9}
$$
where $V_{c^-}$ is the normal-form summand with data $c^-$.

Only $z_c$ contributes to (9). To check this, in a coproduct component of left degree one, exactly one run supplies that degree. It loses one degree on the right; if it was a singleton, its removal can merge adjacent runs. Every run in $c^-$ has length at most $k-1$: the other runs have total length $m-k<d\le k$. Every nonzero source component of $z$ has a long run of length at least $k$, by minimality. Unless this long run supplies the left degree, it survives on the right with length at least $k$, possibly increased by a merger, which cannot produce $c^-$. It must therefore have colour $i$ and length exactly $k$.

Its shortened run has colour $i$ and length $k-1$. There is at most one such run in $c^-$: two runs of this colour would need a separating run and hence right degree at least
$$
2(k-1)+1\ge2d-1>m-1.
$$
Thus the source data are precisely $c$, and no run is removed or merged in this contribution. The projection (9) is consequently the map applying $\delta_{i,k}$ to the distinguished run of $z_c$, sending every exterior run to the right, and reordering the tensor factors to put its degree-one output on the left.

Primitivity of $z$ makes (9) zero. By (7), this tensor product of $\delta_{i,k}|_{K_{i,k}}$ with identity maps is injective. Hence $z_c=0$, contradicting its choice. Thus $z=0$.

We have proved that no nonzero degree-$m$ group initial form is invisible for any $m<2d$. Lemma 1 of the invisible-ideal theorem gives (1) through matrix size $2d=2Q+2$. $\square$

**Scope and checks.** This improves the bound $Q+3$ with the same factor hypotheses. It applies at the first characteristic-divisible long-run degrees: for example $Q=2$, $k=4$ is handled by taking a square root of an invisible polynomial, rather than dividing by $k$. The proof stops before group degree $2d$, where two invisible runs can coexist and the uniqueness argument in Step 4 no longer applies. Rank-two dyadic factors with $q=2$ do not have the polynomial algebra (3); the [bound allowing those factors](dyadic-rank-two-factors-in-the-coproduct-unipotent-bound.md) remains $n\le Q+3$. No all-degree assertion or claim of novelty beyond the knowledgebase is made.
