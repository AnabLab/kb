**Setting.** A *$p$-adic field* here is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ for some prime $p$. Let $X_K$ be a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over a $p$-adic field $K$, with compactification $\bar X_K$, and let $\Pi_X = \pi_1(X_K)$, with geometric subgroup $\Delta_X$ and quotient $G_K$. A closed point $x$ of $\bar X_K$ determines a decomposition group $D_x \subseteq \Pi_X$ up to conjugacy. It maps onto an open subgroup of $G_K$, and $I_x = D_x \cap \Delta_X$ is isomorphic to $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)$ if $x$ is a cusp and is trivial otherwise [@mochizuki2005galoissections, §1, pp. 5–6]. Hyperbolic curves $X_K$ and $Y_L$ are *isogenous* if some hyperbolic curve admits finite étale morphisms to both [@mochizuki2005galoissections, §0, p. 4].

Let $Y_L$ be a hyperbolic curve over a $p'$-adic field $L$, and $\alpha : \Pi_X \to \Pi_Y$ an isomorphism of profinite groups. Then $\alpha$ maps $\Delta_X$ onto $\Delta_Y$ and induces an isomorphism $G_K \to G_L$ [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Proposition 1.1 (ii), p. 5]. It maps the decomposition groups of cusps to those of cusps, and their inertia groups to inertia groups [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Theorem 1.3 (iii), p. 6]. No decomposition group of a closed point of $X_K$ lies in the decomposition group of a cusp [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Theorem 1.3 (iv), p. 6].

**Theorem (Mochizuki, 2005).** Suppose that $X_K$ and $Y_L$ are both defined over number fields, and both isogenous to hyperbolic curves of genus $0$. Then every isomorphism of profinite groups $\alpha : \Pi_X \to \Pi_Y$ preserves the decomposition groups of closed points [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Corollary 3.2, p. 14]. Since the hypotheses are symmetric, $\alpha^{-1}$ preserves them too, so $\alpha$ induces a bijection between the two sets of decomposition groups. The case $Y_L = X_K$ of genus $0$ is Theorem A of the paper [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Introduction, Theorem A, p. 1].

Mochizuki later called these curves *of strictly Belyi type* [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, Definition 2.3 (i), pp. 46–47, and Remark 25, p. 47]. They are affine. See [the absolute Grothendieck conjecture over number fields](absolute-grothendieck-conjecture-for-curves-over-number-fields.md) for examples, such as once-punctured elliptic curves over number fields.

**Extension to quasi-Belyi type (Mochizuki, 2007).** Let $U$ be a hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field, defined over a number field [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, Definition 2.3, pp. 46–47].

- $U$ is *of Belyi type* if there is a chain $U = U_1, U_2, \dots, U_m$ of hyperbolic orbicurves ending in a tripod, a curve of type $(0,3)$, in which each $U_{j+1}$ is a finite étale cover of $U_j$, a finite étale quotient of $U_j$, a hyperbolic partial compactification of $U_j$, or a partial coarsification of $U_j$.
- $U$ is *of quasi-Belyi type* if some connected finite étale cover of $U$ admits a dominant morphism to a tripod.

Strictly Belyi type implies Belyi type, which implies quasi-Belyi type [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, Remark 24, p. 47]. Mochizuki notes that every hyperbolic curve of genus $0$ or $1$ defined over a number field is of Belyi type [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, Remark 31, p. 54]. Now let $V$ be a hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field and $\beta : \pi_1(U) \to \pi_1(V)$ an isomorphism of profinite groups [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, Proposition 2.4, p. 48].

1. If $U$ is of quasi-Belyi type, then $\beta$ maps every decomposition group of a closed point of $U$ isomorphically onto a decomposition group of a closed point of $V$. No hypothesis on $V$ is needed.
2. If both are of quasi-Belyi type, then $\beta$ is point-theoretic.
3. If $U$ is of Belyi type, then so is $V$.

If both curves are of quasi-Belyi type, or one of them is of Belyi type, then every isomorphism $\beta$ comes from a unique isomorphism of schemes $U \to V$, compatible with the profinite étale coverings that the two groups define [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, Corollary 2.3, p. 53]. In Mochizuki's terminology, every hyperbolic curve of Belyi type over a $p$-adic field is *absolute* [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, Corollary 2.4, p. 54]. He calls these curves a new countable class of absolute curves [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, Introduction, p. 2].

**Method.** There are three steps.

1. *Dominant localizations.* The objects of the category $\mathrm{DLoc}_K(X_K)$ are the open immersions $Y \hookrightarrow Z$ of hyperbolic curves over finite extensions of $K$ such that $Y$ is a finite étale cover of $X_K$ and $Z$ lies between $Y$ and its compactification. A morphism to another object $Y' \hookrightarrow Z'$ is a dominant $K$-morphism $Z \to Z'$, and the definition does not ask it to be compatible with the maps to $X_K$ [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Definition 2.1 and p. 7]. Its group-theoretic counterpart has as objects the quotients of open subgroups $H \subseteq \Pi_X$ by the closed normal subgroups generated by some inertia groups of cusps, subject to a hyperbolicity condition [@mochizuki2005galoissections, p. 8]. The étale fundamental group gives an equivalence between the two categories. Every isomorphism $\alpha$ as above induces, functorially, an equivalence between the categories for $X_K$ and for $Y_L$ [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Theorem 2.3, pp. 8–9]. The proof uses the Hom form of [Mochizuki's theorem](mochizuki-hom-theorem-for-hyperbolic-curves.md) and the preservation of cuspidal decomposition groups [@mochizuki2005galoissections, proof of Theorem 2.3, p. 9].
2. *Belyi maps.* A closed point $x$ is *of DLoc-type* if an open subgroup of $D_x$ is the image, under a morphism $Z \to X_K$ of $\mathrm{DLoc}_K(X_K)$, of the decomposition group of a cusp of $Z$ [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Definition 2.4, p. 9]. Every $\alpha$ preserves the decomposition groups of DLoc-type [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Corollary 2.5, p. 9]. Now let $X_K$ have genus $0$ with all its cusps $K$-rational, and let $x$ be an *algebraic* closed point, one that is defined over a number field in a suitable sense [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Definition 2.7, p. 10]. Then $\bar X_K \cong \mathbb{P}^1_K$, and removing three of the cusps gives a tripod $T$ containing $X_K$. After enlarging $K$, [Belyi's theorem](belyi-theorem.md) gives a morphism $\bar X_K \to \bar X_K$ that maps $x$ and every cusp of $X_K$ to cusps of $X_K$ and is unramified over $T$. The preimage $Z$ of $X_K$ is then an open subscheme of $X_K$ not containing $x$, and it is finite étale over $X_K$ because $X_K \subseteq T$. So the open immersion $Z \hookrightarrow X_K$ is a morphism of $\mathrm{DLoc}_K(X_K)$, and $x$, a cusp of $Z$, is of DLoc-type [@mochizuki2005galoissections, proof of Corollary 2.8, p. 11]. Hence, if $X_K$ and $Y_L$ are isogenous to hyperbolic curves of genus $0$, every $\alpha$ preserves the decomposition groups of algebraic closed points, and $X_K$ is defined over a number field if and only if $Y_L$ is [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Corollary 2.8, p. 11]. The point is that $\pi_1(Z)$ is an open subgroup of $\Pi_X$, so the surjection $\pi_1(Z) \to \Pi_X$ given by the open immersion is recorded by data inside $\Pi_X$: an open subgroup, and its quotient by some inertia groups of cusps.
3. *$p$-adic approximation.* Since $\Delta_X$ is topologically finitely generated, it has characteristic open subgroups $\Delta_X[j]$, for $j \ge 1$, decreasing with trivial intersection. For a section $\sigma : G_K \to \Pi_X$, the open subgroups $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma) \cdot \Delta_X[j]$ define finite étale covers $X_K[j, \sigma] \to X_K$ by hyperbolic curves over $K$ [@mochizuki2005galoissections, §3, pp. 12–13]. *Lemma 3.1.* Suppose $X_K = X_F \otimes_F K$ for a hyperbolic curve $X_F$ over a number field $F \subseteq K$, and that $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma)$ lies in no decomposition group of a cusp. Then the following are equivalent [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Lemma 3.1, p. 13] [@mochizuki2014galoissectionscomments, item (1)]:
   - (i) $\sigma$ comes from a point $x \in X_K(K)$, that is, $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma) = D_x$;
   - (ii) $X_K[j, \sigma](K) \ne \varnothing$ for every $j$;
   - (iii) for every $j$, $X_K[j,\sigma]$ has a $K$-rational point that is algebraic;
   - (iv) for every $j$, $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma) \cdot \Delta_X[j]$ contains a decomposition group of an algebraic closed point of $X_K$ that maps onto $G_K$.

   The step from (ii) to (i) is a compactness argument, and the step from (ii) to (iii) approximates a $K$-point by points over finite extensions of $F$ inside $K$, using Krasner's lemma [@mochizuki2005galoissections, proof of Lemma 3.1, pp. 13–14]. The theorem then follows from Corollary 2.8, Theorem 1.3 (iii) and (iv), and the equivalence of (i) and (iv) [@mochizuki2005galoissections, proof of Corollary 3.2, p. 14]. Spelled out, as checked here: reduce to a $K$-rational point $x$ that is not a cusp. Then $\alpha(D_x)$ is the image of a section $\sigma'$ for $Y$, and by Theorem 1.3 (iii) and (iv) it lies in no decomposition group of a cusp. The preimage under $\alpha$ of each subgroup $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma') \cdot \Delta_Y[j]$ is an open subgroup of $\Pi_X$ containing $D_x$. It corresponds to a finite étale cover $X' \to X_K$ with a $K$-rational point over $x$. The algebraic points are dense in $X_K(K)$, as in the step from (ii) to (iii), and finite étale maps are local homeomorphisms on $K$-points. So algebraic points of $X_K(K)$ near $x$ lift to $X'(K)$, so this subgroup contains the decomposition groups of such points, which map onto $G_K$. Corollary 2.8 carries them to decomposition groups of algebraic points of $Y$, so $\sigma'$ satisfies (iv) and comes from a rational point.

**The compactness step needs no number field.** This is proved here. Let $X_K$ be any hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field $K$, and $\sigma$ a section whose image lies in no decomposition group of a cusp. Write $\bar X_j$ for the compactification of $X_j = X_K[j, \sigma]$. If $\bar X_j(K) \ne \varnothing$ for every $j$, then $\sigma$ comes from a point of $X_K(K)$. So conditions (i) and (ii) of Lemma 3.1 are equivalent for every hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field, even with $\bar X_j$ in place of $X_j$ in (ii), and only the step to (iii) uses $F$. This fits the one-sided hypothesis in part 1 of the extension above. The proof of that part applies Lemma 3.1 with algebraic points replaced by points of DLoc-type, which are $p$-adically dense in $U(k_U)$ when $U$ is of quasi-Belyi type [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, proof of Proposition 2.4, pp. 48–49]. The conclusion concerns decomposition groups of $V$, so $V$ takes the place of $X_K$ in Lemma 3.1, and by the above the compactness step needs nothing of $V$.

*Proof.* Put $\Pi_j = \mathrm{Im}(\sigma) \cdot \Delta_X[j]$, and write $\mathrm{pr} : \Pi_X \to G_K$.

1. $\bigcap_j \Pi_j = \mathrm{Im}(\sigma)$. If $g \in \Pi_j$, then $g = \sigma(\mathrm{pr}(g)) \delta$ with $\delta = \sigma(\mathrm{pr}(g))^{-1} g$, and $\delta \in \Delta_X[j]$. This $\delta$ does not depend on $j$, so if $g$ lies in every $\Pi_j$, then $\delta \in \bigcap_j \Delta_X[j] = 1$.
2. Each $\bar X_j(K)$ is compact, since $\bar X_j$ is projective over $K$ and $K$ is locally compact, and the maps $\bar X_{j+1}(K) \to \bar X_j(K)$ are continuous. An inverse limit of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces is nonempty, so there are $y_j \in \bar X_j(K)$ with $y_{j+1} \mapsto y_j$ for all $j$. Let $y \in \bar X_K(K)$ be their common image.
3. Let $S$ be the set of points over $y$ of the normalization of $\bar X_K$ in the function field of the universal profinite étale cover of $X_K$. It is a nonempty profinite set on which $\Pi_X$ acts, and the stabilizers $D_{\tilde y}$ of its points $\tilde y$ are the decomposition groups of $y$. A standard fact is used: if $P$ is the point of $\bar X_j$ below $\tilde y$, then $\mathrm{pr}(D_{\tilde y} \cap \Pi_j)$ is the absolute Galois group of the residue field of $P$. The set $S_j$ of $\tilde y \in S$ lying over $y_j$ is nonempty and closed, and $S_{j+1} \subseteq S_j$. So some $\tilde y$ lies in every $S_j$, and since each $y_j$ is $K$-rational, $D_{\tilde y} \cap \Pi_j$ maps onto $G_K$ for every $j$.
4. For $g \in G_K$, the sets $\{d \in D_{\tilde y} \cap \Pi_j : \mathrm{pr}(d) = g\}$ are nonempty, compact and decreasing in $j$. So they have a common element, which lies in $D_{\tilde y} \cap \mathrm{Im}(\sigma)$ by step 1. Thus $D_{\tilde y} \cap \mathrm{Im}(\sigma)$ maps onto $G_K$. As $\mathrm{pr}$ is injective on $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma)$, this gives $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma) \subseteq D_{\tilde y}$.
5. If $y$ were a cusp, step 4 would put $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma)$ in a decomposition group of a cusp, contrary to the hypothesis. So $y \in X_K(K)$, and $D_{\tilde y}$ maps isomorphically onto $G_K$. Since $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma)$ also maps isomorphically onto $G_K$, $\mathrm{Im}(\sigma) = D_{\tilde y}$, and $\sigma$ comes from $y$. $\square$

**Once-punctured elliptic curves.** If $X_K$ and $Y_L$ are once-punctured elliptic curves over $p$-adic fields, every isomorphism $\alpha$ preserves the decomposition groups of the points coming from torsion points, compatibly with the isomorphism of Tate modules $\Delta_X^{\mathrm{ab}} \to \Delta_Y^{\mathrm{ab}}$ induced by $\alpha$ [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Corollary 2.6, p. 10]. In place of a Belyi map, the proof uses multiplication by $n$. It makes $E \setminus E[n]$ a finite étale cover of $X_K = E \setminus \{O\}$ and an open subscheme of it, which exhibits the $n$-torsion points as points of DLoc-type [@mochizuki2005galoissections, proof of Corollary 2.6, p. 10]. Here the curves need not be defined over a number field, so this is not a special case of the theorem [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Remark 3.2.1, p. 14].

**Cusps and units.** For a $K$-rational cusp $x$, the splittings of $D_x \to G_K$ form a torsor under $H^1(G_K, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)) \cong (K^\times)^\wedge$. A stable model of $X_K$ reduces it to an $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$-torsor, the *canonical integral structure* at $x$ [@mochizuki2005galoissections, §4 and Definition 4.1, pp. 14–16]. Let $X_K$ be isogenous to a hyperbolic curve of genus $0$, with no hypothesis about number fields. Then $X_K$ is *unitwise absolute*: for $\alpha$ as above, the isomorphism $H^1(\Pi_X, \mu_{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}(\bar K)) \to H^1(\Pi_Y, \mu_{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}(\bar L))$ induced by $\alpha$ maps the Kummer classes of the units of $X_K$ onto those of the units of $Y_L$. If moreover $X_K$ has stable reduction, then $\alpha$ respects the canonical integral structures at all cusps, in the sense of Definition 4.1 (iv) there [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Definitions 4.1 (iv) and 4.8, pp. 15–16 and 21, and Corollary 4.11, p. 23]. Let $X_K$ be a once-punctured elliptic curve with stable reduction over a field $K$ of residue characteristic different from $2$ that contains a primitive $12$th root of unity. Then the cusp carries a $\mu_{12}(K)$-torsor structure, compatible with the canonical integral structure, that every automorphism of $\Pi_X$ preserves [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Corollary 4.12, p. 23]. The hypothesis on roots of unity was added in the author's comments [@mochizuki2014galoissectionscomments, item (2)]. Mochizuki relates the number $12$ to the line bundle on the moduli stack of elliptic curves given by the cotangent space at the origin of the universal curve, which has order $12$ in the Picard group [@mochizuki2005galoissections, Remark 4.12.1, p. 24].

**Later work.**

- *Algorithms.* In the second paper of his *Topics* series, Mochizuki recasts the method as group-theoretic algorithms, the elliptic and Belyi cuspidalizations. They reconstruct the decomposition groups of certain closed points, and also the fundamental groups of the open subschemes obtained by removing them [@mochizuki2013, §3, pp. 254–255]. For curves of strictly Belyi type over $p$-adic fields, combining these algorithms with Lemma 3.1 gives an algorithm for the decomposition groups of all closed points [@mochizuki2013, Remark 3.7.2, p. 266]. Belyi cuspidalization is the first step of the reconstruction described in [the absolute Grothendieck conjecture over number fields](absolute-grothendieck-conjecture-for-curves-over-number-fields.md).
- *Point-theoreticity.* An isomorphism of geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental groups of hyperbolic curves over $p$-adic fields, for $\Sigma$ of size at least $2$ containing $p$, that matches the decomposition groups of Galois-dense sets of points on the two sides comes from an isomorphism of schemes [@mochizuki2013, Corollary 2.9, p. 242]. Mochizuki reads this as saying that the section conjecture over $p$-adic fields implies the absolute isomorphism version, and adds that a relatively weak version of the section conjecture suffices, "cf. the point of view of" the 2005 paper [@mochizuki2013, Remark 2.9.2, p. 247]. At the time of writing he knew of no result like the theorem above for $\Sigma$ other than the set of all primes [@mochizuki2013, §3, pp. 254–255].
- *All curves.* Mochizuki and Tsujimura prove, in a preprint, that for every hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field the decomposition groups of closed points are preserved, for geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental groups as above. See [reconstructing points from tempered fundamental groups](points-of-p-adic-curves-from-tempered-fundamental-groups.md). This gives the [absolute Grothendieck conjecture over $p$-adic fields](absolute-grothendieck-conjecture-for-p-adic-curves.md) for all hyperbolic curves. Both papers summarized in this entry are published.
