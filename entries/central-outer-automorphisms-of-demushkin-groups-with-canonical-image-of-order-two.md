**Setting.** Let $D$ be a pro-$2$ [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $n \ge 3$ whose canonical character has image $\{\pm 1\}$. As in [finite normal subgroups and rival copies for these groups](rival-copies-of-demushkin-groups-with-canonical-image-of-order-two.md), $D$ is centre-free ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 4), and we identify $D$ with $\mathrm{Inn}(D)$ inside $A = \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ by $d \mapsto c_d$, $c_d(x) = dxd^{-1}$. With $(a, b) = a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ and $F$ free pro-$2$ on $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), items 2 and 3 with $f = \infty$, gives $D = F/(r)$ with
$$r = x_1^2C, \quad C = (x_2, x_3)(x_4, x_5)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n) \quad (n \text{ odd}),$$
$$r = x_1^2(x_1, x_2)C, \quad C = (x_3, x_4)(x_5, x_6)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n) \quad (n \text{ even}).$$
The torsion of $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is generated by the class of $x_1$, and $V_D = D^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathrm{torsion}$ has basis $v_2, \dots, v_n$, the images of $x_2, \dots, x_n$ (same entry, setting). For $\alpha \in A$ let $g_\alpha \in \mathrm{GL}(V_D)$ be its action, and let $A_1$ be the kernel of $\alpha \mapsto g_\alpha$. It contains $D$.

Let $\Lambda = \Lambda^2 V_D$, free over $\mathbb{Z}_2$ with basis $v_k \wedge v_l$ for $2 \le k < l \le n$, and put $V_{\mathbb{Q}} = V_D \otimes \mathbb{Q}_2$ and $\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}} = \Lambda \otimes \mathbb{Q}_2$. Let $\omega \in \Lambda$ be the sum of $v_k \wedge v_l$ over the commutators $(x_k, x_l)$ occurring in $C$; it is nonzero, as $C$ is nonempty when $n \ge 3$ is odd or $n \ge 4$ is even. For $w \in V_{\mathbb{Q}}$ let $\mathrm{ad}(w) : V_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}$ be $u \mapsto w \wedge u$, and write $\mathrm{ad}(V_D)$ and $\mathrm{ad}(V_{\mathbb{Q}})$ for the sets of these maps with $w$ in $V_D$ or $V_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Let $D_2 \supseteq D_3$ be the closed subgroups generated by the commutators $(a, b)$, and by the commutators $(a, b)$ with $a \in D_2$, $b \in D$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *A torsion-free quotient of class $2$.* Let $H = V_D \times \tfrac12\Lambda$ with product $(v, w)(v', w') = (v + v', w + w' + \tfrac12 v \wedge v')$. There is a continuous homomorphism $\rho : D \to H$ with $\rho(x_k) = (v_k, 0)$ for $k \ge 2$ and $\rho(x_1) = (0, -\tfrac12\omega)$. Its kernel $K$ contains $D_3$, and $K/D_3$ is the set of elements of finite order of $D/D_3$. In particular $K$ is a characteristic subgroup of $D$.
2. *The invariant.* Let $Q = \rho(D)$, let $\pi : Q \to V_D$ be the first coordinate, and let $Z_Q = \ker\pi$. Every $\alpha \in A$ induces an automorphism $\bar\alpha$ of $Q$ with $\bar\alpha\rho = \rho\alpha$, and $\pi\bar\alpha = g_\alpha\pi$. For $\alpha \in A_1$ there is a unique $\mathbb{Z}_2$-linear map $\tau(\alpha) : V_D \to \tfrac12\Lambda$ with $\bar\alpha(h)h^{-1} = (0, \tau(\alpha)(\pi h))$ for all $h \in Q$. Moreover, $\tau : A_1 \to \mathrm{Hom}(V_D, \tfrac12\Lambda)$ is a homomorphism, $\tau(c_d) = \mathrm{ad}(\bar d)$ for $d \in D$ with image $\bar d$ in $V_D$, and $\tau(\gamma\alpha\gamma^{-1}) = \Lambda^2(g_\gamma) \circ \tau(\alpha) \circ g_\gamma^{-1}$ for $\gamma \in A$ and $\alpha \in A_1$.
3. *Central elements.* Let $\varphi \in \mathrm{Out}(D)$ be central. Then $\varphi$ acts on $V_D$ as multiplication by some $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$, and $(\lambda - 1)\tau(\alpha) \in \mathrm{ad}(V_D)$ for every $\alpha \in A_1$.
4. *Rank at least $4$.* If $n \ge 4$, then some $\alpha \in A_1$ has $\tau(\alpha) \notin \mathrm{ad}(V_{\mathbb{Q}})$. Consequently every central element of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ acts trivially on $V_D$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here. Let $n \ge 4$. Then the centre of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ has no element $\ne 1$ of finite $2$-power order, $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ has no nontrivial finite normal $2$-subgroup, and $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ has no admissible class in the sense of [the rival-copies theorem](rival-copies-of-demushkin-groups-with-canonical-image-of-order-two.md), Corollary. Consequently, if $N \ne D$ is a closed normal subgroup of $A$ isomorphic to $D$, then $N \cap D$ has infinite index in both $D$ and $N$, and is not topologically finitely generated.

*Proof.* A central element of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ of finite $2$-power order acts trivially on $V_D$ by part 4. So it lies in the kernel of $\mathrm{Out}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V_D)$, which has no element $\ne 1$ of finite $2$-power order by part 3 of [the involution theorem](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-finite-canonical-image.md). By part 2 of the rival-copies theorem, a nontrivial finite normal $2$-subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ has order $2$ and a central generator, which was just excluded. An admissible class is by definition central of order $2$, so it is excluded too. Finally, by [elasticity](demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three-are-elastic.md), Corollary 2, a closed normal copy $N \ne D$ either gives a nontrivial finite normal $2$-subgroup $ND/D$ of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$, which does not exist, or meets $D$ as stated. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.**

*The group $H$.* The product is associative: both bracketings of three factors give second coordinate $w + w' + w'' + \tfrac12(v \wedge v' + v \wedge v'' + v' \wedge v'')$. The identity is $(0, 0)$ and the inverse of $(v, w)$ is $(-v, -w)$. For $h = (v, w)$ and $h' = (v', w')$ one checks
$$hh'h^{-1} = h'\cdot(0, v \wedge v'), \qquad (h, h') = (0, v \wedge v'), \tag{1}$$
so $\{0\} \times \tfrac12\Lambda$ is central and $H$ is nilpotent of class at most $2$. Also $(v, w)^m = (mv, mw)$ for integers $m$, so $H$ is torsion-free, and by induction
$$(u_1, 0)(u_2, 0)\cdots(u_s, 0) = \Big(\sum_i u_i, \ \tfrac12\sum_{i < j} u_i \wedge u_j\Big). \tag{2}$$
$H$ is compact, being homeomorphic to a product of copies of $\mathbb{Z}_2$. For $k \ge 1$ the sets $U_k = 2^kV_D \times 2^{k-1}\Lambda$ are open subgroups of $2$-power index, normal by (1), with trivial intersection. So $H$ is a pro-$2$ group.

*The homomorphism.* The universal property of $F$ gives a continuous homomorphism $F \to H$ with the stated values. By (1), $(x_k, x_l) \mapsto (0, v_k \wedge v_l)$ for $k, l \ge 2$, so $C \mapsto (0, \omega)$, and $(x_1, x_2) \mapsto 1$ because $\rho(x_1)$ is central. Since $\rho(x_1)^2 = (0, -\omega)$, in both cases $r \mapsto 1$, and $\rho$ is defined on $D$.

*The kernel.* As $H$ is torsion-free of class at most $2$, $\rho$ kills $D_3$ and every element whose image in $D/D_3$ has finite order. Conversely let $d \in K$. In $D/D_3$ commutators are central, and $(a, b)$ is multiplicative in each variable, also for exponents in $\mathbb{Z}_2$ by continuity. Collecting terms therefore gives $a_k, b_{kl} \in \mathbb{Z}_2$ with
$$d \equiv x_2^{a_2}\cdots x_n^{a_n}\,x_1^{a_1}\prod_{1 \le k < l \le n}(x_k, x_l)^{b_{kl}} \pmod{D_3}.$$
By (1) and (2), with $(x_1, x_l) \mapsto 1$,
$$\rho(d) = \Big(\sum_{k \ge 2} a_kv_k, \ \tfrac12\sum_{2 \le k < l} a_ka_l\,v_k \wedge v_l - \tfrac12 a_1\omega + \sum_{2 \le k < l} b_{kl}\,v_k \wedge v_l\Big).$$
So $\rho(d) = 1$ forces $a_k = 0$ for $k \ge 2$, and then $\sum_{2 \le k < l} b_{kl}\,v_k \wedge v_l = \tfrac12 a_1\omega$. Comparing coefficients, $b_{kl} = \tfrac12 a_1$ if $(x_k, x_l)$ occurs in $C$ and $b_{kl} = 0$ for the other pairs with $k \ge 2$. As $C$ is nonempty, $t = \tfrac12 a_1$ lies in $\mathbb{Z}_2$. Since $C$ is central modulo $D_3$,
$$x_1^{2t}\prod_{(x_k, x_l) \text{ in } C}(x_k, x_l)^t \equiv (x_1^2C)^t \pmod{D_3},$$
which is $r^t = 1$ for odd $n$, and $r^t(x_1, x_2)^{-t} = (x_1, x_2)^{-t}$ for even $n$. Hence $d \equiv \prod_{l = 2}^n (x_1, x_l)^{b'_l}$ modulo $D_3$ for some $b'_l \in \mathbb{Z}_2$. In $D$, $x_1^2$ equals $C^{-1}$ or $C^{-1}(x_1, x_2)^{-1}$, so it lies in $D_2$, and $(x_1, x_l)^2 \equiv (x_1^2, x_l) \equiv 1$ modulo $D_3$. So $d^2 \in D_3$. Thus $K/D_3$ is exactly the set of elements of finite order of $D/D_3$. As $D_3$ is characteristic and automorphisms preserve orders, $K$ is characteristic. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $\alpha \in A$. By part 1, $\alpha(K) = K$, so $\alpha$ induces an automorphism of $D/K$. Since $\rho$ induces an isomorphism of $D/K$ onto $Q$, a continuous bijection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff one, this gives $\bar\alpha$ with $\bar\alpha\rho = \rho\alpha$. The composite $\pi\rho : D \to V_D$ is the canonical map, since both send $x_k$ to $v_k$ for $k \ge 2$ and $x_1$ to $0$. So $\pi\bar\alpha = g_\alpha\pi$, $\pi$ is onto, and $\bar\alpha(Z_Q) = Z_Q$.

*Action on $Z_Q$.* For $2 \le k < l$, $(0, v_k \wedge v_l) = \rho((x_k, x_l)) \in Q$, so $\{0\} \times \Lambda \subseteq Z_Q$, and by (1) $\bar\alpha$ sends it to the commutator of $\bar\alpha\rho(x_k)$ and $\bar\alpha\rho(x_l)$, which is $(0, g_\alpha v_k \wedge g_\alpha v_l)$. So $\bar\alpha$ acts on $\{0\} \times \Lambda$ as $\Lambda^2(g_\alpha)$. If $(0, w) \in Z_Q$, then $\bar\alpha(0, w) = (0, w')$ with $(0, 2w') = \bar\alpha(0, 2w) = (0, \Lambda^2(g_\alpha)(2w))$, as $2w \in \Lambda$; so $w' = \Lambda^2(g_\alpha)w$. Thus
$$\bar\alpha(0, w) = \big(0, \Lambda^2(g_\alpha)w\big) \qquad \big((0, w) \in Z_Q\big). \tag{3}$$

*Definition of $\tau$.* Let $\alpha \in A_1$ and $\delta(h) = \bar\alpha(h)h^{-1}$. Then $\pi\delta(h) = 0$, so $\delta(h) \in Z_Q$, which is central in $Q$. Hence $\delta(hh') = \bar\alpha(h)\delta(h')h^{-1} = \delta(h)\delta(h')$, and $\delta$ is a continuous homomorphism. It is trivial on $Z_Q$ by (3) with $g_\alpha = 1$. So it factors through $Q/Z_Q \cong V_D$, which defines $\tau(\alpha)$, unique because $\pi$ is onto. A continuous additive map between finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_2$-modules is $\mathbb{Z}_2$-linear.

*Properties.* For $\alpha, \beta \in A_1$ and $h \in Q$, using that $\bar\alpha$ fixes $Z_Q$ pointwise,
$$\bar\alpha\bar\beta(h)h^{-1} = \bar\alpha\big(\bar\beta(h)h^{-1}\big)\,\bar\alpha(h)h^{-1} = \bar\beta(h)h^{-1}\cdot\bar\alpha(h)h^{-1},$$
so $\tau(\alpha\beta) = \tau(\alpha) + \tau(\beta)$. For $d \in D$, $\bar c_d(h) = \rho(d)h\rho(d)^{-1}$, and (1) gives $\bar c_d(h)h^{-1} = (0, \bar d \wedge \pi h)$, so $\tau(c_d) = \mathrm{ad}(\bar d)$. For $\gamma \in A$, $\gamma\alpha\gamma^{-1} \in A_1$, and with $h' = \bar\gamma^{-1}(h)$,
$$\bar\gamma\bar\alpha\bar\gamma^{-1}(h)h^{-1} = \bar\gamma\big(\bar\alpha(h')h'^{-1}\big) = \big(0, \Lambda^2(g_\gamma)\,\tau(\alpha)(g_\gamma^{-1}\pi h)\big)$$
by (3), as $\pi h' = g_\gamma^{-1}\pi h$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** The image of $\varphi$ in the image $\Gamma$ of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ in $\mathrm{GL}(V_D \otimes \mathbb{Q}_2)$ is central, so it is multiplication by some $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ by part 1 of [the rival-copies theorem](rival-copies-of-demushkin-groups-with-canonical-image-of-order-two.md). Let $y \in A$ represent $\varphi$, and let $\alpha \in A_1$. As $\varphi$ is central, $y\alpha y^{-1}\alpha^{-1} = c_d$ for some $d \in D$. Both $y\alpha y^{-1}$ and $\alpha^{-1}$ lie in $A_1$, and by part 2
$$\tau(y\alpha y^{-1}) = \Lambda^2(\lambda) \circ \tau(\alpha) \circ \lambda^{-1} = \lambda^2\lambda^{-1}\tau(\alpha) = \lambda\tau(\alpha).$$
So $\mathrm{ad}(\bar d) = \tau(c_d) = \lambda\tau(\alpha) - \tau(\alpha)$. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** We give $\alpha$ for each parity.

*Even $n \ge 4$.* Let $T$ be the substitution $x_2 \mapsto x_1x_2$, fixing the other generators. It is an automorphism of $F$, with inverse $x_2 \mapsto x_1^{-1}x_2$, and it fixes $r$, because $(x_1, x_1x_2) = (x_1, x_2)$. So it induces an automorphism of $D$. On $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ it adds the class of $x_1$, which is torsion, to the class of $x_2$, so $T \in A_1$. As $\rho(T(x_2))\rho(x_2)^{-1} = \rho(x_1) = (0, -\tfrac12\omega)$ and $T$ fixes $x_k$ for $k \ge 3$,
$$\tau(T)(v_2) = -\tfrac12\omega \ne 0, \qquad \tau(T)(v_k) = 0 \quad (k \ge 3).$$
If $\tau(T) = \mathrm{ad}(w)$ with $w \in V_{\mathbb{Q}}$, then $w \wedge v_3 = w \wedge v_4 = 0$, so $w = 0$, contradicting $\tau(T)(v_2) \ne 0$.

*Odd $n \ge 5$.* Write $n = 2m + 1$ with $m \ge 2$, $a_i = x_{2i}$ and $b_i = x_{2i+1}$, so $C = c_1R$ with $c_1 = (a_1, b_1)$ and $R = (a_2, b_2)\cdots(a_m, b_m)$. Let $\alpha$ be the substitution
$$x_1 \mapsto a_1^{-1}x_1a_1, \qquad a_1 \mapsto a_1, \qquad b_1 \mapsto b_1C^{-1}, \qquad x \mapsto CxC^{-1} \quad (x = a_2, b_2, \dots, a_m, b_m).$$
Here $\alpha(c_1) = a_1^{-1}Cb_1^{-1}a_1b_1C^{-1}$ and $\alpha(R) = CRC^{-1}$. Since $b_1^{-1}a_1b_1R = a_1c_1R = a_1C$,
$$\alpha(C) = a_1^{-1}C\,(b_1^{-1}a_1b_1R)\,C^{-1} = a_1^{-1}Ca_1, \qquad \alpha(r) = a_1^{-1}x_1^2a_1 \cdot a_1^{-1}Ca_1 = a_1^{-1}ra_1.$$
Let $E = a_1Ca_1^{-1}$ and let $\beta$ be the substitution $x_1 \mapsto a_1x_1a_1^{-1}$, $a_1 \mapsto a_1$, $b_1 \mapsto b_1E$, $x \mapsto E^{-1}xE$ for the same $x$. Then $\alpha(E) = C$, so $\alpha\beta$ fixes every generator. Also $\beta(c_1) = a_1^{-1}E^{-1}b_1^{-1}a_1b_1E = a_1^{-1}E^{-1}a_1c_1E$ and $\beta(R) = E^{-1}RE$, so $\beta(C) = a_1^{-1}E^{-1}a_1CE = E$, because $E^{-1}a_1 = a_1C^{-1}$. So $\beta\alpha$ fixes every generator, $\alpha$ is an automorphism of $F$, and as $\alpha(r)$ is conjugate to $r$, it induces an automorphism of $D$. Every generator other than $b_1$ is sent to a conjugate of itself, and $b_1$ is sent to $b_1C^{-1}$, where $C$ is a product of commutators. So $\alpha$ acts trivially on $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and $\alpha \in A_1$. As $\rho(C) = (0, \omega)$ is central, $\rho(\alpha(b_1))\rho(b_1)^{-1} = (0, -\omega)$ and $\rho(\alpha(x)) = \rho(x)$ for the other $x \ne x_1$. So
$$\tau(\alpha)(v_3) = -\omega, \qquad \tau(\alpha)(v_k) = 0 \quad (k \ne 3).$$
If $\tau(\alpha) = \mathrm{ad}(w)$ with $w \in V_{\mathbb{Q}}$, then $w \wedge v_2 = w \wedge v_4 = 0$, so $w = 0$, contradicting $\tau(\alpha)(v_3) \ne 0$. Here $v_4$ exists because $m \ge 2$.

*Conclusion.* Let $\varphi$ be central in $\mathrm{Out}(D)$, acting as $\lambda$. By part 3, $(\lambda - 1)\tau(\alpha) \in \mathrm{ad}(V_D)$ for the $\alpha$ just constructed. If $\lambda \ne 1$, dividing by $\lambda - 1$ gives $\tau(\alpha) \in \mathrm{ad}(V_{\mathbb{Q}})$, which is false. So $\lambda = 1$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Rank $3$.* For $n = 3$, $V_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has dimension $2$ and $\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has dimension $1$. The map $w \mapsto \mathrm{ad}(w)$ is injective, since $w \wedge v_2 = w \wedge v_3 = 0$ forces $w = 0$, so it is an isomorphism onto $\mathrm{Hom}(V_{\mathbb{Q}}, \Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}})$, which also has dimension $2$. Part 3 then says nothing, and this method cannot exclude a central class acting as $-1$. Rank $3$ is settled by a different argument in [the rank-three theorem](rival-copies-of-the-rank-three-pro-2-demushkin-group-with-canonical-image-of-order-two.md).
- *The automorphisms in $D$.* In $D$ the relation gives $C = x_1^{-2}$ for odd $n$, so there $\alpha$ is $x_1 \mapsto x_2^{-1}x_1x_2$, $x_3 \mapsto x_3x_1^2$, $x_k \mapsto x_1^{-2}x_kx_1^2$ for $k \ge 4$, fixing $x_2$. Both $T$ and $\alpha$ act trivially on $V_D$, and by part 2 no nonzero power of either is inner.
- *Scope.* By part 4, when $n \ge 4$ no automorphism acting on $V_D$ as $-1$, such as the lift constructed in part 1 of the rival-copies theorem, has a central outer class. Together with that theorem's Corollary, this excludes case (a) in rank at least $4$. In rank $3$ the only candidate for an admissible class is the class of an explicit involution acting as $-1$, which has involutions in both cosets of $\ker\chi$. So there is no admissible class, and case (a) does not occur there either ([the rank-three theorem](rival-copies-of-the-rank-three-pro-2-demushkin-group-with-canonical-image-of-order-two.md), parts 1–3). So case (a) does not occur for any pro-$2$ Demushkin group of rank at least $3$ with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\}$. Case (b) is not addressed.
