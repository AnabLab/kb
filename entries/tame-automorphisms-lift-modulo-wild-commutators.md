**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $k$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $n$ that contains a primitive $p$-th root of unity. For $p = 2$ this holds for every $k$. Let $q$ be the order of the residue field of $k$, and let $k^{ur} \subseteq k_{tr}$ be the maximal unramified and the maximal tamely ramified extensions of $k$ in $\bar k$. Put $P = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/k_{tr})$, $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(k_{tr}/k)$ and $I = \mathrm{Gal}(k_{tr}/k^{ur})$. Let $[P,P]$ be the closed commutator subgroup of $P$. Its fixed field is the maximal abelian extension $k_{tr}^{ab}$ of $k_{tr}$, so $E = G_k/[P,P] = \mathrm{Gal}(k_{tr}^{ab}/k)$. Let $\bar\pi : E \to \Gamma$ be restriction, with kernel $M = P^{ab}$. Conjugation in $E$ makes $M$ a compact module over $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]]$. For $p = 2$ and $k = U_f$, the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of degree $f$, these are the $T$, $P$, $\Gamma_f$ and $I$ of [the Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md).

**Theorem.** Every continuous automorphism $\beta$ of $\Gamma$ lifts to $E$: there is a continuous automorphism $\tilde\beta$ of $E$ with $\bar\pi \circ \tilde\beta = \beta \circ \bar\pi$. In particular, for every $f \ge 1$ the Frobenius shift $\alpha$ of the problem entry lifts to $G_{U_f}/[P,P] = \mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}/U_f)$.

**Corollary.** Let $K, L \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2$ be finite tamely ramified extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ with the same ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$. Then $\mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}/K) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}/L)$. Here $\mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}/K) = G_K/[P,P]$, and $P$ is the largest closed normal pro-$2$ subgroup of $G_K$ (lemma in the problem entry), so this quotient is determined by the group $G_K$. For example, the non-isomorphic degree-$6$ fields $U_2(\sqrt[3]{2})$ and $U_2(\sqrt[3]{2\omega})$ of [tamely ramified 2-adic fields](tamely-ramified-2-adic-fields.md) have isomorphic quotients of this kind. A proof that their absolute Galois groups are not isomorphic would have to use more of $G_K$ than $G_K/[P,P]$.

*Proof of the corollary.* With $\sigma_f, \tau$ as in the problem entry, part 1 of its reduction gives $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K) = H_c$ and $\mathrm{Gal}(T/L) = H_{c'}$ for some $c, c'$, where $H_c$ is the closed subgroup generated by $\sigma_f\tau^c$ and $\tau^e$. The automorphism $\alpha_{c'-c}$ of $\Gamma_f$ maps $\sigma_f\tau^c$ to $\sigma_f\tau^{c'}$ and fixes $\tau^e$, so it maps $H_c$ onto $H_{c'}$. By the theorem it has a lift $\tilde\beta$, and then $\tilde\beta$ maps $\bar\pi^{-1}(H_c) = \mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}/K)$ onto $\bar\pi^{-1}(H_{c'}) = \mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}/L)$. $\square$

The theorem, the corollary and the remarks below are worked out here. The sources opened for this entry do not state them. For $k = U_f$ the theorem contains the main theorem of [the first Frattini layer entry](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-first-frattini-layer.md). Indeed $\Phi(P)/[P,P] = 2M$, and a lift $\tilde\beta$ maps $M$ onto $M$ and so preserves $2M$, so it induces a lift on $E/2M = G_{U_f}/\Phi(P)$. That entry's explicit decomposition of $T^\times/T^{\times 2}$ is not needed here.

**Proof.** Three facts are taken from Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg.

- (N1) $P$ is a free pro-$p$ group [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Proposition 7.5.1, p. 409].
- (N2) $\Gamma$ is a split extension of $\mathrm{Gal}(k^{ur}/k) \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ by $I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and $\Gamma$ is generated by two elements $\sigma, \tau$ with the single relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Proposition 7.5.2 and Theorem 7.5.3, p. 410]. Here $\tau$ generates $I$ and $\sigma$ lifts the Frobenius. So every element of $\Gamma$ is $\sigma^y\tau^x$ with $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $x \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and $\sigma^y\tau\sigma^{-y} = \tau^{q^y}$, where $q^y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ is defined by continuity.
- (N3) There is an exact sequence of compact $\Lambda$-modules $0 \to \Lambda \to \Lambda^{n+1} \to M \to 0$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Theorem 7.4.5 (i), p. 408]. There $\Gamma$ is written $\mathcal{G}_k$ and $M$ is written $V_k^{ab}$. The sentence before the theorem reads $\mathcal{G}_k = T_k/V_k$, but it calls $\mathcal{G}_k$ the Galois group of $k_{tr}|k$, the proof applies Theorem 7.4.2 to the extension $k_{tr}|k$, and §5 defines $\mathcal{G}_k = G(k_{tr}|k)$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 409–410]. So $\mathcal{G}_k = \Gamma$.

*Step 1: every automorphism of $\Gamma$ induces the identity on $\Gamma/I$.* Let $\beta$ be a continuous automorphism of $\Gamma$. Write the abelian group $\Gamma/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ additively, and let $b$ and $c$ be the images of $\beta(\tau)$ and $\beta(\sigma)$. Applying $\beta$ to $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$ and passing to $\Gamma/I$ gives $b = qb$, so $b = 0$, as $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is torsion-free. Hence $\beta(I) \subseteq I$, likewise $\beta^{-1}(I) \subseteq I$, so $\beta(I) = I$ and $\beta(\tau) = \tau^u$ with $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$. Write $\beta(\sigma) = \sigma^v\tau^h$. Since $\beta$ induces an automorphism of $\Gamma/I$, which is topologically generated by the image of $\sigma$, $v \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$. As $I$ is abelian, $\beta(\sigma)\beta(\tau)\beta(\sigma)^{-1} = \tau^{uq^v}$, while $\beta(\tau^q) = \tau^{uq}$. So $q^{v-1} = 1$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. For $m \ge 1$ the integer $q^m - 1$ is prime to $p$, and $q$ has order $m$ modulo $q^m - 1$, so $v - 1 \in m\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. Hence $v = 1$, and $\beta(\gamma) \in \gamma I$ for every $\gamma \in \Gamma$.

*Step 2: reduction to modules.* Let $N$ be the kernel of $\Gamma \to \Gamma/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{Z}_p$. Both $I$ and $N/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ have order prime to $p$, so $N$ does, and $\mathrm{cd}_p N = 0$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Corollary 3.3.7, p. 174]. The group $\Gamma/N \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ is free pro-$p$, so $\mathrm{cd}_p(\Gamma/N) \le 1$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Proposition 3.5.17, p. 199], and $\mathrm{cd}_p\Gamma \le 1$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Proposition 3.3.8, p. 175]. By (N1), $M$ is pro-$p$. So the extension $1 \to M \to E \to \Gamma \to 1$ splits [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Theorem 3.5.6, (iv) implies (ii), p. 192], applied to the class of finite $p$-groups: there is a continuous homomorphism $s : \Gamma \to E$ with $\bar\pi \circ s = \mathrm{id}$. Then $(m, \gamma) \mapsto m\,s(\gamma)$ is a homeomorphism $M \times \Gamma \to E$, and $\gamma \cdot m = s(\gamma)ms(\gamma)^{-1}$ is the module structure of $M$.

For a compact $\Lambda$-module $X$, let $\beta^*X$ be $X$ with $\gamma$ acting as $\beta(\gamma)$ does on $X$. Let $\varphi : M \to \beta^*M$ be an isomorphism of $\Lambda$-modules, that is, a topological automorphism of $M$ with $\varphi(\gamma \cdot m) = \beta(\gamma) \cdot \varphi(m)$. Then $\tilde\beta(m\,s(\gamma)) = \varphi(m)\,s(\beta(\gamma))$ satisfies
$$\tilde\beta\big(m\,s(\gamma)\,m'\,s(\gamma')\big) = \tilde\beta\big(m(\gamma \cdot m')\,s(\gamma\gamma')\big) = \varphi(m)\big(\beta(\gamma) \cdot \varphi(m')\big)\,s(\beta(\gamma))\,s(\beta(\gamma')) = \tilde\beta(m\,s(\gamma))\,\tilde\beta(m'\,s(\gamma')).$$
It is continuous and bijective, so it is an automorphism of $E$, and $\bar\pi \circ \tilde\beta = \beta \circ \bar\pi$. So it suffices to show $M \cong \beta^*M$.

*Step 3: the part on which $\beta$ acts trivially.* For an open normal subgroup $U$ of $\Gamma$, the image $NU/U$ is a normal subgroup of $\Gamma/U$ of order prime to $p$, so $e_U = (\#NU/U)^{-1}\sum_{x \in NU/U} x$ is a central idempotent of $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma/U]$. These are compatible under the projections, so they define a central idempotent $e$ of $\Lambda = \varprojlim_U \mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma/U]$. Put $\varepsilon = 1 - e$. Every compact $\Lambda$-module $X$ is the direct sum of the submodules $eX$ and $\varepsilon X$. Since $xe = e$ for $x \in N$, and $ey = y$ for every $N$-fixed $y \in X$, the summand $eX$ is the module $X^N$ of $N$-fixed points, and $\varepsilon X$ has no nonzero $N$-fixed point. The ring automorphism of $\Lambda$ induced by $\beta$ maps $e$ to $e$, because $\beta(N) = N$ by step 1. Step 1 also gives $\beta(\gamma)\gamma^{-1} \in N$ for every $\gamma$, so $\gamma$ and $\beta(\gamma)$ act alike on $eM = M^N$, and $\beta^*(eM) = eM$. Hence $\beta^*M = eM \oplus \beta^*(\varepsilon M)$, and it suffices to show $\beta^*(\varepsilon M) \cong \varepsilon M$.

*Step 4: $\varepsilon M$ is projective.* It suffices that $\mathrm{Ext}^1_\Lambda(\varepsilon M, S) = 0$ for every simple $\Lambda$-module $S$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Proposition 5.2.11, p. 279]. Such an $S$ is finite with $pS = 0$, and $S = eS$ or $S = \varepsilon S$. If $S = eS$, then $\Gamma$ acts on $S$ through the pro-$p$ group $\Gamma/N$, which fixes a nonzero vector, so $S = \mathbb{F}_p$ with trivial action. Multiplying (N3) by $\varepsilon$ gives an exact sequence $0 \to \varepsilon\Lambda \to (\varepsilon\Lambda)^{n+1} \to \varepsilon M \to 0$, and evaluation at $\varepsilon$ identifies $\mathrm{Hom}_\Lambda(\varepsilon\Lambda, S)$ with $\varepsilon S$. As $\varepsilon\Lambda$ is projective, this gives an exact sequence
$$0 \to \mathrm{Hom}_\Lambda(\varepsilon M, S) \to (\varepsilon S)^{n+1} \to \varepsilon S \to \mathrm{Ext}^1_\Lambda(\varepsilon M, S) \to 0.$$
If $S = \mathbb{F}_p$, then $\varepsilon S = 0$ and the Ext group vanishes. Otherwise $\varepsilon S = S$ and $S^N = 0$, and with $d = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} S$ the Ext group vanishes once $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}\mathrm{Hom}_\Lambda(\varepsilon M, S) = nd$. Every map $eM \to S$ has $N$-fixed image, hence is $0$, so $\mathrm{Hom}_\Lambda(\varepsilon M, S) = \mathrm{Hom}_\Lambda(M, S) = H^1(P, S)^\Gamma$, the $\Gamma$-invariant continuous homomorphisms $P \to S$.

- Since $\mathrm{cd}_p N = 0$ and $S^N = 0$, the exact sequences of [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Proposition 1.6.7, p. 67] for $N \subseteq \Gamma$ give $H^i(\Gamma, S) = 0$ for all $i \ge 0$. The same proposition for $P \subseteq G_k$ then gives $H^1(P, S)^\Gamma \cong H^1(G_k, S)$.
- $H^0(G_k, S) = S^\Gamma = 0$. By Tate duality, $H^2(G_k, S)$ is dual to $H^0(G_k, \mathrm{Hom}(S, \mu_p))$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Theorem 7.2.6, p. 380]. Since $\mu_p \subset k$, the group $G_k$ acts trivially on $\mu_p$, so this is $\mathrm{Hom}_\Gamma(S, \mathbb{F}_p)$. It is $0$: a nonzero $\Gamma$-map $S \to \mathbb{F}_p$ is onto, its kernel is a proper submodule and so is $0$, and then $S \cong \mathbb{F}_p$, contradicting $S^N = 0$.
- By Tate's Euler characteristic formula, $\#H^0(G_k, S)\,\#H^2(G_k, S)/\#H^1(G_k, S) = \|p^d\|_k = p^{-nd}$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Theorem 7.3.1, p. 392]. So $\dim H^1(G_k, S) = nd$.

So $\varepsilon M$ is projective.

*Step 5: cancellation.* As $\varepsilon M$ is projective, the sequence $0 \to \varepsilon\Lambda \to (\varepsilon\Lambda)^{n+1} \to \varepsilon M \to 0$ splits, so $\varepsilon M \oplus \varepsilon\Lambda \cong (\varepsilon\Lambda)^{n+1}$. The map $x \mapsto \beta(x)$ is an isomorphism $\varepsilon\Lambda \to \beta^*(\varepsilon\Lambda)$, since $\beta(\gamma x) = \beta(\gamma)\beta(x)$ and $\beta(\varepsilon) = \varepsilon$. Applying $\beta^*$ to the splitting therefore gives
$$\beta^*(\varepsilon M) \oplus \varepsilon\Lambda \cong (\varepsilon\Lambda)^{n+1} \cong \varepsilon M \oplus \varepsilon\Lambda.$$
For an open normal subgroup $U$ of $\Gamma$ and $m \ge 1$, let $F(X) = X/(p^mX + I(U)X)$, where $I(U)$ is the closed ideal of $\Lambda$ generated by the $x - 1$ with $x \in U$. Then $F(\beta^*(\varepsilon M)) \oplus F(\varepsilon\Lambda) \cong F(\varepsilon M) \oplus F(\varepsilon\Lambda)$ as finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma/U]$-modules, so $F(\beta^*(\varepsilon M)) \cong F(\varepsilon M)$ by Krull–Schmidt cancellation [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Theorem 5.6.10 (i), p. 327]. The isomorphisms between these finite modules form nonempty finite sets, compatible as $U$ shrinks and $m$ grows, and a compatible family is an isomorphism $\beta^*(\varepsilon M) \cong \varepsilon M$, because both modules are finitely generated. This is the compactness argument that ends the proof of [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Proposition 5.6.11, p. 328]. With step 3, $M \cong \beta^*M$, and step 2 gives $\tilde\beta$. $\square$

**Where $\mu_p \subset k$ enters.** It is used only in step 4, to make $H^2(G_k, S)$ vanish for every simple $S \ne \mathbb{F}_p$, and it cannot be dropped. Define $P$, $\Gamma$, $N$, $E$ and $M$ as above for $k = \mathbb{Q}_3$, although $\mu_3 \not\subset \mathbb{Q}_3$, and take the automorphism $\alpha$ of $\Gamma$ from the first of the two checks in the problem entry, with $e = 2$ and $f = 1$.

- *The proof fails.* The generator $\tau$ acts on $\mu_3 \subset \mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{-3}) \subseteq k_{tr}$ by inversion, so $\mu_3$ is a simple module with $\mu_3^N = 0$. But $H^2(G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}, \mu_3)$ is dual to $H^0(G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}, \mathbb{Z}/3) = \mathbb{F}_3$ by Tate duality, and the count in step 4 gives $\dim \mathrm{Ext}^1_\Lambda(\varepsilon M, \mu_3) = 1$, so $\varepsilon M$ is not projective.
- *The conclusion fails.* By the problem entry, the subgroups $H_0, H_1$ fix $K_0, K_1$, which are $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{3})$ and $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{-3})$ in some order, and $\alpha(H_0) = H_1$. A lift of $\alpha$ to $E$ would map $G_{K_0}/[P,P]$ onto $G_{K_1}/[P,P]$. As $[P,P] \subseteq [G_K, G_K]$, it would induce an isomorphism $G_{K_0}^{ab} \cong G_{K_1}^{ab}$, and so of the maximal pro-$3$ quotients. By the [multiplicative-group calculation](multiplicative-group-of-a-p-adic-field.md), the torsion of $G_{K_i}^{ab}(3)$ is exactly $\mu_{3^\infty}(K_i)$. Here $\mathbb Q_3(\mu_3)=\mathbb Q_3(\sqrt{-3})$; it differs from $\mathbb Q_3(\sqrt3)$ since $-1$ is not a square in $\mathbb Q_3$ (reduce a putative square modulo $3$). Neither quadratic field contains a primitive ninth root of unity, since $[\mathbb Q_3(\mu_9):\mathbb Q_3]=6$ [@fesenkovostokov2002, Chapter IV, Example (1.3), pp. 113–114]. Thus the torsion orders are $1$ and $3$, so no lift exists.

**What this leaves open.** The [problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md) asks for a lift to $G_{U_f}$ itself, which also involves $[P,P]$. Beyond $E$ the method stops. The next quotients of $G_{U_f}$ are extensions of $E$ by abelian pro-$2$ groups. The group $E$ contains $M$, which is torsion-free of rank at least $2$, so $\mathrm{cd}_2 E \ge 2$ and step 2's splitting argument is not available. A lift then has to match extension classes as well as modules. The next case would be $G_{U_f}/[[P,P],[P,P]]$, which has not been tried.
