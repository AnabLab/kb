**Setting.** Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be finitely generated extensions of algebraically closed fields, of any characteristics, and write $F^i$ for the perfect closure. For $t_1,\dots,t_m\in K$, the *relation variety* $X_{\mathbf t}\subset(\mathbb P^1_k)^m$ is the Zariski closure of the point $(t_1,\dots,t_m)$. It is irreducible, and $X_{\mathbf t}\cap\mathbb A^m$ is cut out by the polynomial relations among the $t_i$. The same applies to tuples in $L^i$ over $l$.

Let $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{rac}}(K)$ be the set of $t\in K\setminus k$ with $k(t)$ relatively algebraically closed in $K$. Let $\mathcal T_0(K)$ be the set of quotients $a/b$, where $q$ is a closed point of some model of $K/k$ with regular local ring and $a,b\in\mathfrak m_q$ have linearly independent images in $\mathfrak m_q/\mathfrak m_q^2$.

Two maps *agree mod finite* if they agree outside a finite set. Composition respects this, so the permutations of $\mathbb P^1(l)$ modulo finite agreement form a group. Let $\mathcal H_l$ be the group of permutations of $\mathbb P^1(l)$ generated by $\mathrm{PGL}_2(l)$ and, if $\mathrm{char}\,l=p>0$, by $\mathrm{Frob}(x)=x^p$. A bijection *lies in* $\mathcal H_l$ if it agrees mod finite with a member of $\mathcal H_l$. Write $T_a(x)=x+a$ and $m_c(x)=cx$.

**Statement.** Assume $\mathrm{trdeg}(K/k)\ge2$, and let $\mathcal T$ be a set with $\mathcal T_0(K)\subseteq\mathcal T\subseteq\mathcal T_{\mathrm{rac}}(K)$. For each $t\in\mathcal T$, let $t'\in L$ be an element with $l(t')$ relatively algebraically closed in $L$, and let $B_t:\mathbb P^1(k)\to\mathbb P^1(l)$ be a bijection with $B_t(\infty)=\infty$. Suppose that $L=l(t':t\in\mathcal T)$, and that the following condition holds.

> **(SV)** For every tuple $\mathbf t=(t_1,\dots,t_m)$ in $\mathcal T$, let $Y_{\mathbf t}$ be the relation variety of $(t_1',\dots,t_m')$. There are dense open sets $U\subset X_{\mathbf t}$ and $U'\subset Y_{\mathbf t}$ with
> $$\Bigl(\prod_iB_{t_i}\Bigr)(U(k))\subset Y_{\mathbf t}(l),\qquad\Bigl(\prod_iB_{t_i}^{-1}\Bigr)(U'(l))\subset X_{\mathbf t}(k).$$

Then:

1. There is a field isomorphism $\sigma:k\to l$ and, for each $t\in\mathcal T$, a unique $H_t\in\mathcal H_l$ with $B_t=H_t\circ\sigma$ mod finite, where $\sigma(\infty)=\infty$.
2. There is a field isomorphism $\alpha:K^i\to L^i$ with $\alpha|_k=\sigma$ and $\alpha(t)=H_t^{-1}(t')$ for every $t\in\mathcal T$. In particular $\alpha(k(t)^i)=l(t')^i$.
3. (SV) holds if there is a bijection $v\mapsto v'$ from the prime divisors of $K/k$ to those of $L/l$ such that, for all $t\in\mathcal T$, $a\in k$ and $b\in l$,
$$v(t-a)=1\Rightarrow v'(t'-B_t(a))>0,\qquad v'(t'-b)=1\Rightarrow v(t-B_t^{-1}(b))>0.\tag{D}$$

This is §7 of the October 2026 mod-$\ell$ manuscript [@openai2026modl, Lemmas 7.1–7.3, Proposition 7.4 and Theorem 7.5, pp. 26–32]. There, $\mathcal T$ is the set of good functions; here the Galois input is isolated as (D). The proof follows the manuscript and was rebuilt to check each step.

**Proof.** *0. The set $\mathcal T$.* Let $t=a/b\in\mathcal T_0(K)$ at $q$, and $e=\mathrm{trdeg}(K/k)$. As $\mathcal O_q$ is regular, its associated graded ring is a polynomial ring in $e$ variables over $k$. So the $\mathfrak m_q$-adic order $v_q$ is a valuation, trivial on $k$. Its residue field is $k(\mathbb P^{e-1})$, the degree-zero fractions of initial forms. It is the valuation of the exceptional divisor of the blowup at $q$, hence a prime divisor.

- (i) $\mathcal T_0\subseteq\mathcal T_{\mathrm{rac}}$. The initial forms $\bar a,\bar b$ are independent linear forms, so $v_q(t)=0$ and the residue $\bar t=\bar a/\bar b$ is an affine coordinate of the purely transcendental field $k(\mathbb P^{e-1})$. Thus $\bar t$ is transcendental, and $k(\bar t)$ is relatively algebraically closed in it. A nontrivial valuation of $k(t)$ trivial on $k$ gives $t$ or $1/t$ a residue in $k$, so $v_q$ is trivial on $k(t)$. Let $E$ be the relative algebraic closure of $k(t)$ in $K$. Its value group is torsion over that of $k(t)$, hence zero. So reduction embeds $E$ into $k(\mathbb P^{e-1})$, extending $k(t)\cong k(\bar t)$, and the image is algebraic over $k(\bar t)$. Hence $E=k(t)$ [@openai2026modl, proof of Lemma 6.4, pp. 25–26].
- (ii) $\mathcal T_0$ generates $K$. Given $f\in K^\times$, choose a regular closed point $q$ with $f(q)\ne0$, and $a,b$ as above, using $e\ge2$. Since $fa\equiv f(q)a$ mod $\mathfrak m_q^2$, both $t=a/b$ and $ft=fa/b$ lie in $\mathcal T_0$, and $f=(ft)/t$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 6.4, p. 26].
- (iii) Partners. Given $t_1,\dots,t_m\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{rac}}$, choose $q$ regular with every $t_i$ regular there, and put $s=a/b\in\mathcal T_0$. For $c\in k^\times$, $t_i+cs=(t_ib+ca)/b$, and $t_ib+ca\equiv t_i(q)b+ca$ mod $\mathfrak m_q^2$ is independent of $b$ there. So $t_i+cs\in\mathcal T_0$. Next, $v_q(t_i-t_i(q))>0$, so $v_q$ restricts to $k(t_i)$ with residue field $k$. If $s$ were algebraic over $k(t_i)$, it would lie in $k(t_i)$, and its residue $\bar s$ would lie in $k$, contradicting (i). So $s$ is algebraically independent of each $t_i$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 6.5, p. 26].

*1. Perfect rational functions.* An element $h$ of $l(X_1,\dots,X_r)^i$ has some power $h^{p^N}$ in $l(X_1,\dots,X_r)$, so it has values at general points. Claim: if $h$ has one variable and is injective on a cofinite subset of $\mathbb P^1(l)$, it agrees there with a unique member of $\mathcal H_l$. Members of $\mathcal H_l$ are uniquely $M\circ\mathrm{Frob}^n$ with $M\in\mathrm{PGL}_2(l)$, $n\in\mathbb Z$, and distinct members differ at infinitely many points.

Indeed $\mathrm{Frob}\circ M=M^{(p)}\circ\mathrm{Frob}$, where $M^{(p)}$ raises entries to the $p$th power, so these maps form the group. In characteristic $0$, a rational function of degree $d$ has $d$ preimages over a general point, so $d=1$. In characteristic $p$, write $h^{p^N}=q^{p^a}$ with $q\in l(X)$ and $a$ maximal. Then $q\notin l(X^p)$ is separable, and $h=\mathrm{Frob}^{a-N}\circ q$ on points. Since $\mathrm{Frob}$ is bijective, $q$ is injective on a cofinite set, so $q$ has degree $1$. If $M\mathrm{Frob}^n=M'\mathrm{Frob}^{n'}$ mod finite with $n>n'$, then $X^{p^{n-n'}}$ agrees with a Möbius map at infinitely many points, which is impossible. Two perfect rational functions agreeing at infinitely many points are equal [@openai2026modl, Lemma 7.2, p. 28].

*2. Independence transfers.* If $t,s\in\mathcal T$ are algebraically independent, so are $t',s'$. Here $X_{(t,s)}=(\mathbb P^1)^2$, and for all but finitely many $a\in k$ the open $U$ of (SV) meets $\{a\}\times\mathbb P^1$ in a cofinite set. So the closed set $Y_{(t,s)}$ contains $\{B_t(a)\}\times\mathbb P^1$ for infinitely many $a$, and equals $(\mathbb P^1)^2$.

*3. Transported addition.* Let $t,s\in\mathcal T$ be algebraically independent with $z_c=t+cs\in\mathcal T$ for all $c\in k^\times$. Fix $c$, put $z=z_c$, and let $Y$ be the affine relation variety of $(t',s',z')$, with projection $\pi:Y\to\mathbb A^2$. By step 2, $\pi$ is dominant. The $B$'s restrict to bijections $k\to l$, and $X_{(t,s,z)}\cap\mathbb A^3$ is the plane $w=x+cy$. So (SV) gives a dense open $U'\subset Y$ on which
$$w=B_z\bigl(B_t^{-1}(x)+cB_s^{-1}(y)\bigr).\tag{$*$}$$
If $\dim Y=3$, then $Y=\mathbb A^3$ and $U'$ meets a general fibre in a cofinite set, contradicting $(*)$. So $\dim Y=2$. The closure of $\pi(Y\setminus U')$ is a proper closed subset, and $\pi(Y)$ contains a dense open. So a general fibre is nonempty, lies in $U'$, and is one point.

Let $g(W^{p^e})$, with $g$ separable of degree $d$, be the minimal polynomial of $z'$ over $l(t',s')$. Clearing denominators gives a polynomial $h(x,y,W)$, primitive over $l[x,y]$ and hence irreducible. Then $Y=V(h)$, as both are irreducible surfaces. Where the leading coefficient and the discriminant of $g$ do not vanish, $h(x_0,y_0,W)$ has exactly $d$ distinct roots. Hence $d=1$ and $z'^{p^e}=R(t',s')$ with $R\in l(X,Y)$. The perfect rational function $R_c=R^{p^{-e}}$ then satisfies
$$R_c(x,y)=B_z\bigl(B_t^{-1}(x)+cB_s^{-1}(y)\bigr)\quad\text{for general }(x,y)\in l^2.$$

Fix a general $y_0$ and put $b=B_s^{-1}(y_0)$. Then $x\mapsto R_c(x,y_0)$ agrees mod finite with the bijection $D_b=B_zT_{cb}B_t^{-1}$, so $D_b\in\mathcal H_l$ by step 1, for $b$ in a cofinite $S\subset k$. Since $S\cap(S+d)\ne\varnothing$ for every $d$, the products $D_b^{-1}D_{b'}=B_tT_{c(b'-b)}B_t^{-1}$ put every $B_tT_aB_t^{-1}$ in $\mathcal H_l$. Then $B_zB_t^{-1}=D_b\,B_tT_{-cb}B_t^{-1}\in\mathcal H_l$. Fixing a general $x_0$ instead, with $a=B_t^{-1}(x_0)$, gives $B_zT_am_cB_s^{-1}\in\mathcal H_l$. Multiplying on the left by $B_tT_{-a}B_t^{-1}\cdot B_tB_z^{-1}$ gives $B_tm_cB_s^{-1}\in\mathcal H_l$. With $c=1$ this gives $B_sB_t^{-1}\in\mathcal H_l$, and then $B_tm_cB_t^{-1}\in\mathcal H_l$ for every $c$. Sending $g$ to the member of $\mathcal H_l$ that agrees mod finite with $B_tgB_t^{-1}$ is therefore a homomorphism $\psi_t:\mathrm{Aff}(k)\to\mathcal H_l$. It is injective, since distinct affine maps agree at most at one point of $k$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 7.3 and its proof, pp. 28–29].

*4. Constants.* Fix $t\in\mathcal T$, and choose $s$ by 0(iii), so that step 3 applies.

- The image of $\psi_t$ lies in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(l)$. In characteristic $p$, the exponent $n$ of $M\circ\mathrm{Frob}^n$ is a homomorphism $\mathcal H_l\to\mathbb Z$. It kills $k^\times$, which is divisible, and $(k,+)$, which is divisible or $p$-torsion.
- Translations give a field embedding. Let $U=\psi_t(k)$, an infinite abelian group normalized by $\psi_t(\mathrm{Aff}(k))$. Elements of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(l)$ are semisimple or unipotent. Suppose $u\in U$ is semisimple and nontrivial, with fixed points $P\ne Q$. Since $U$ commutes with $u$, it preserves $\{P,Q\}$, and its subgroup fixing both points has index at most two. This subgroup is infinite and lies in the torus fixing $P$ and $Q$, so it contains some $g$ with $g^2\ne1$. The centralizer of $g$ is that torus, so $U$ lies in it. Anything normalizing $U$ maps the fixed points of $g$ to those of an element of $U\setminus\{1\}$. So it preserves $\{P,Q\}$ and acts on $U$ by identity or inversion. But $\psi_t(m_c)$ acts as multiplication by $c$, since $m_cT_bm_c^{-1}=T_{cb}$, and $c\ne\pm1$ exists. So $U\setminus\{1\}$ is unipotent. After conjugating by some $M$, one element is $x\mapsto x+1$; its centralizer is the translations, so $MUM^{-1}$ consists of translations. Put $B=MB_t$. This gives an injective additive $\chi:k\to l$ with $BT_bB^{-1}=T_{\chi(b)}$ mod finite, and we rescale so that $\chi(1)=1$. Normalizers of an infinite group of translations fix $\infty$. So $M\psi_t(m_c)M^{-1}$ is affine of slope $\lambda_c$, and conjugation gives $\chi(cb)=\lambda_c\chi(b)$, so $\lambda_c=\chi(c)$. Thus $\chi$ is a field embedding, and $\mathrm{char}\,k=\mathrm{char}\,l$.
- $\chi$ is onto. Let $z=t+s$. Step 3 gives $M_s=B_sB_t^{-1}M^{-1}$ and $M_z=B_zB_t^{-1}M^{-1}$ in $\mathcal H_l$. Put $F(X,Y)=M_z^{-1}\bigl(R_1(M^{-1}X,M_sY)\bigr)$. For cofinitely many $b\in k$, and then cofinitely many $X=B(a)$, we get
$$F(X,B(b))=M_z^{-1}B_z(a+b)=B(a+b)=X+\chi(b).$$
For such $b$ this is an identity of perfect rational functions of $X$. Choose $X_0$ with $F(X_0,Y)$ defined for general $Y$, and put $h(Y)=F(X_0,Y)-X_0$. For cofinitely many $b$ the denominator does not vanish at $(X_0,B(b))$, and evaluating gives $\chi(b)=h(B(b))$. So $h$ is injective on a cofinite set. By step 1, $\chi(k)$ contains a cofinite subset of $l$. If $a\notin\chi(k)$, then $a+\chi(k)$ is infinite and disjoint from $\chi(k)$. So $\chi(k)=l$. Put $\sigma=\chi$. If $h$ agrees with $H\in\mathcal H_l$ mod finite, then $B_t=M^{-1}H^{-1}\sigma$ mod finite.
- The same $\sigma$ serves every $r\in\mathcal T$. By 0(iii) there is an $s_0\in\mathcal T$, algebraically independent of $t$ and $r$, with $t+cs_0,r+cs_0\in\mathcal T$. Step 3 puts $B_{s_0}B_t^{-1}$ and $B_{s_0}B_r^{-1}$ in $\mathcal H_l$, so $B_r\in\mathcal H_l\sigma$. Uniqueness of $H_r$ follows from step 1 [@openai2026modl, Proposition 7.4, pp. 30–31].

*5. The isomorphism.* Put $u_t=H_t^{-1}(t')\in L^i$. Writing $H_t=M_t\circ\mathrm{Frob}^{n_t}$, this means $u_t=(M_t^{-1}t')^{p^{-n_t}}$, so $l(u_t)^i=l(t')^i$. Fix a tuple $\mathbf t$, and let $Z$ be the relation variety of $(u_{t_1},\dots,u_{t_m})$. The coordinatewise map $\mathcal H=\prod_iH_{t_i}^{-1}$ is a homeomorphism of $(\mathbb P^1(l))^m$ for the Zariski topology. Möbius maps are automorphisms, and $\mathrm{Frob}$ in one coordinate is the map on points of a finite surjective morphism, hence a closed continuous bijection.

First, $\mathcal H(Y_{\mathbf t}(l))=Z(l)$. Take an affine model of $L(u_{t_1},\dots,u_{t_m})$ on which every $t_i'$ and $u_{t_i}$ is regular. At each of its points $Q$, $(u_{t_i}(Q))_i=\mathcal H((t_i'(Q))_i)$, because each pair is related by a Möbius map and a Frobenius power over $l$. The model maps dominantly onto both relation varieties, so these value sets are dense in $Y_{\mathbf t}(l)$ and $Z(l)$, and $\mathcal H$ carries the closure of one onto the closure of the other.

Next, remove from the $U$ of (SV) the preimages of the finitely many exceptional points of each coordinate. Each coordinate projection is dominant, so a dense open $U_0$ remains. On $U_0(k)$, $\mathcal H\circ\prod_iB_{t_i}=\sigma$, so $\sigma(U_0(k))\subset Z(l)$, and taking closures gives $X_{\mathbf t}^\sigma(l)\subset Z(l)$. Here $X^\sigma_{\mathbf t}$ is the base change along $\sigma$. Symmetrically, on a dense open $U_0'\subset U'$, $\sigma^{-1}\circ\mathcal H=\prod_iB_{t_i}^{-1}$. So $\mathcal H(U_0'(l))$ is dense in $Z(l)$ and lies in $X^\sigma_{\mathbf t}(l)$. Hence $X^\sigma_{\mathbf t}=Z$. On $\mathbb A^m$ this says that for every $P\in k[T_1,\dots,T_m]$,
$$P(t_1,\dots,t_m)=0\iff P^\sigma(u_{t_1},\dots,u_{t_m})=0.$$
By 0(ii), $K=k(\mathcal T)$. So $a\mapsto\sigma(a)$ and $t\mapsto u_t$ define an injective ring map $k[\mathcal T]\to L^i$. It extends to $K$, and then uniquely to $\alpha:K^i\to L^i$. The image is perfect and contains $l$ and every $l(t')^i$, hence $L$, hence $L^i$ [@openai2026modl, Theorem 7.5, pp. 31–32].

*6. Divisor matching gives (SV).* Assume (D), and fix a tuple $\mathbf t$. In characteristic $p$, $t_i\notin K^p$, since a $p$th root would be algebraic over $k(t_i)$ without lying in it. As $k$ is perfect, $kK^p=K^p$, so $dt_i\ne0$ in $\Omega_{K/k}$ [@stacks2026, Definition 15.47.1 and Lemma 15.47.2, Tags 07P1 and 07P2]. In characteristic $0$, $t_i$ is part of a separating transcendence basis. Choose a smooth affine model $V$ on which every $t_i$ is regular and every $dt_i$ is nonzero in every fibre of $\Omega_V$.

At a closed point $P$, put $a_i=t_i(P)$. Since $d(\mathfrak m_P^2)\subset\mathfrak m_P\Omega$, $t_i-a_i\notin\mathfrak m_P^2$, so the prime divisor $v=v_P$ has $v(t_i-a_i)=1$ for each $i$. By (D), $v'(t_i'-B_{t_i}(a_i))>0$. Reducing any relation $G(t_1',\dots,t_m')=0$ modulo the maximal ideal of $v'$ shows $(B_{t_i}(a_i))_i\in Y_{\mathbf t}(l)$. The image of $V$ in $X_{\mathbf t}$ contains a dense open $U$, and every point of $U(k)$ comes from a closed point. This gives the first inclusion. The second is symmetric, using that each $l(t_i')$ is relatively algebraically closed [@openai2026modl, Lemma 7.1, p. 27]. $\square$

**Checks and limits.**

- *The finite exceptions are unavoidable.* Changing any $B_t$ at finitely many points preserves (SV): shrink $U$ and $U'$ by the corresponding coordinate fibres.
- *Transcendence degree one fails.* For $K=k(x)$, take $\mathcal T=\{M(x):M\in\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)\}$, $t'=t$, and $B_{M(x)}=M\beta M^{-1}$ for any bijection $\beta$ of $\mathbb P^1(k)$. Every relation variety is the curve $\{(M_1(a),\dots,M_m(a))\}$, which these maps preserve. So simultaneous values carry no information, apart from the normalization at $\infty$. In the theorem, $\mathrm{trdeg}\ge2$ enters through 0(ii) and 0(iii).
- *Changes from the manuscript.* Step 2 derives independence from (SV), where the manuscript uses the curve-subfield correspondence (p. 29). Step 3 obtains $B_zB_t^{-1}\in\mathcal H_l$ directly. Neither change is needed to close a gap.

**Scope.** In the manuscript, $\mathcal T$ is the set of good functions. A good function generates a relatively algebraically closed rational subfield in which every point line of the mod-$\ell$ character group is a nonzero image of the inertia of a prime divisor [@openai2026modl, Definition 6.2, p. 25]. Its Lemma 6.4 also claims that every element of $\mathcal T_0$ is good. Its Propositions 6.1 and 6.3 claim the divisor bijection and point bijections that its Lemma 7.1 turns into (SV) through (D). These claims, the rest of §§2–6 and §8 are not checked here. Section 8 shows that $\alpha$ induces the given map on $K^\times/\ell$ up to a scalar, and pins down the Frobenius ambiguity.

The statement involves no coefficients. Its pro-$\ell$ application is now proved in [simultaneous values from pro-$\ell$ rational quotients](simultaneous-values-from-pro-l-rational-quotients.md): the matched divisorial pairs and commuting rational quotient maps of part 3 of [divisoriality from completed curve subfields](divisoriality-from-completed-curve-subfields.md) give (D), and hence this theorem recovers $k\cong l$ and $K^i\cong L^i$. [Global scalar rigidity](global-scalar-rigidity-for-pro-l-rational-quotients.md) supplies compatibility with the given isomorphism. Steps 0–7 of [the pro-$\ell$ reconstruction theorem](pro-l-abelian-by-central-reconstruction-of-function-fields.md) supply the earlier correspondences and compose this route, without Pop's reconstruction theorems. Those earlier steps are not re-proved in this entry.

**Status.** This checks one component of the [October 2026 manuscript](manuscripts-claiming-bogomolov-pop.md), not its main theorem. No gap was found in this component. The manuscript is not peer reviewed, and an agent's check does not replace that review.
