**Setting.** Let $\Sigma$ be a nonempty set of primes, and let $c(\Sigma)$ be the class of finite groups whose orders are divisible only by primes in $\Sigma$. It is a full class, that is, closed under subgroups, quotients and extensions [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.5.2), p. 189]. Pro-$\Sigma$ groups are the pro-$c(\Sigma)$ groups. Let $F$ be a free pro-$\Sigma$ group of finite rank $n \ge 2$: every map from a basis of $F$ to a pro-$\Sigma$ group $G$ extends uniquely to a continuous homomorphism $F \to G$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.5.14), p. 198]. Since $F$ is topologically finitely generated, $A = \mathrm{Aut}(F)$ and $\mathrm{Out}(F)$ are profinite, with the topology defined by the characteristic open subgroups of $F$ [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Notations and conventions, p. 3]. For a prime $p$ and a profinite group $B$, write $H^i(B) = H^i(B, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ with trivial action, so $H^1(B) = \mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Z}/p)$.

The inputs are these.

1. An open subgroup $U$ of $F$ is a free pro-$\Sigma$ group with $\mathrm{rk}(U) - 1 = (n - 1)(F : U)$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (4.2.2), p. 254].
2. $F$ is projective, and $\mathrm{cd}_p F \le 1$ for every prime $p$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.5.15) and (3.5.16), p. 199]. A closed subgroup $B$ satisfies $\mathrm{cd}_p B \le \mathrm{cd}_p F$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.3.5), p. 174]. So $H^2(B) = 0$ for every closed subgroup $B$ of $F$ and every prime $p$.
3. Let $B$ be free pro-$\Sigma$ of finite rank $r$ and $p \in \Sigma$. Every abelian pro-$p$ group is pro-$\Sigma$, so by the universal property $\mathrm{Hom}(B, E) \cong E^r$ for each of them. Hence $\dim H^1(B) = r$, and the maximal abelian pro-$p$ quotient of $B$ is $\mathbb{Z}_p^r$. In particular the rank is an isomorphism invariant.
4. $\mathrm{Out}(F)$ is slim: every open subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(F)$ has trivial centralizer in $\mathrm{Out}(F)$ [@minamidetsujimura2024, Definition 1.1 (ii), p. 5, and Lemma 3.4, p. 20]. Minamide and Tsujimura state this for the maximal pro-$\Sigma$ quotient of a free profinite group of rank $n$, which is a free pro-$\Sigma$ group of rank $n$, for every nonempty $\Sigma$ and every $n \ge 2$. Their proof applies the Grothendieck conjecture over number fields to punctured projective lines and punctured elliptic curves, whose automorphism groups give subgroups of $\mathrm{Out}(F)$ with small centralizers [@minamidetsujimura2024, proof of Lemma 3.4, pp. 20–22].

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Elasticity.* Let $U \subseteq F$ be open and $H \ne 1$ a topologically finitely generated closed normal subgroup of $U$. Then $H$ is open in $F$.
2. *Centre.* $F$ has trivial centre, so conjugation identifies $F$ with the closed normal subgroup $\mathrm{Inn}(F)$ of $A$, with $A/F = \mathrm{Out}(F)$.
3. *Rival copies.* Let $N \ne F$ be a closed normal subgroup of $A$ isomorphic to $F$, and put $M = N \cap F$. Then $M \ne 1$ and $[N, F] \subseteq M$. Moreover $M$ has infinite index in both $N$ and $F$, and is not topologically finitely generated. In particular $N$ neither contains $F$ nor lies in $F$.

For $\Sigma = \{p\}$, part 3 is weaker than [Tsujimura's theorem](free-pro-p-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md), which shows that no such $N$ exists. For larger $\Sigma$ it is the analogue of excluding case (a) in [the Demushkin reduction](demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three-are-elastic.md), Corollary 2. What remains of [the question](free-pro-sigma-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md) is the analogue of case (b). For such an $N$, the subgroup $NF/F \cong N/M$ is an infinite closed normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(F)$, and every element of $N$ acts on $F$ by an automorphism that induces the identity on $F/M$.

No novelty is claimed for parts 1 and 2. Statements of this kind are classical for free profinite groups, but no source stating them was opened, so they are proved here from the inputs above. Minamide and Tsujimura also cite a proof of part 2 for free profinite groups [@minamidetsujimura2024, Introduction, Fact 1, p. 2].

**Proof of part 1.**

*Step 1: a special case.* Let $L$ be a topologically finitely generated closed normal subgroup of $F$, and suppose that $H^1(L) \ne 0$ for a prime $p \in \Sigma$, which we fix. We show that $L$ is open. Suppose not. Then $L$ has infinite index, since closed subgroups of finite index are open. Let $L$ be topologically generated by $r$ elements, so that $\dim H^1(L) \le r$, as a homomorphism to $\mathbb{Z}/p$ is determined by its values on generators.

(a) *An element of infinite order modulo $L$.* The open subgroups $LU'$, for $U'$ open and normal in $F$, have intersection $L$, because $L$ is closed. Their indices are unbounded: otherwise choose $U_0$ with $(F : LU_0)$ largest. For open normal $U' \subseteq U_0$, the inclusion $LU' \subseteq LU_0$ forces $LU' = LU_0$, and these $U'$ are cofinal, so $L = LU_0$ would be open. Choose such a $V = LU'$ with $s = (n - 1)(F : V) + 1 > r$. By input 1, $V$ is free pro-$\Sigma$ of rank $s$, and by input 3 its maximal abelian pro-$p$ quotient is $\pi : V \to \mathbb{Z}_p^s$. The image $\pi(L)$ is the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-span of the images of $r$ generators of $L$, so it has rank at most $r < s$. Choose $v \in V$ such that $\pi(v)$ has infinite order in $\mathbb{Z}_p^s/\pi(L)$.

The closed subgroup $C'$ generated by $v$ is procyclic, so it is the product $C \times C''$ of its $p$-Sylow subgroup $C$ and a subgroup $C''$ of order prime to $p$. Write $v = c_0c''$ with $c_0 \in C$ and $c'' \in C''$. Then $c_0$ topologically generates $C$, and $\pi(c'') = 0$, because the image of $C''$ in the pro-$p$ group $\mathbb{Z}_p^s$ is trivial. So $\pi(c_0) = \pi(v)$, and $C$ is an infinite procyclic pro-$p$ group, that is, $C \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$. If $C \cap L \ne 1$, then $C \cap L$ is open in $C$, so it contains $c_0^{p^k}$ for some $k$, and $p^k\pi(v) = \pi(c_0^{p^k})$ lies in $\pi(L)$, which is false. So $C \cap L = 1$.

(b) *A semidirect product.* As $L$ is normal in $F$, $W = LC$ is a closed subgroup, and $W = L \rtimes C$ with $W/L \cong C$. The group $C$ acts on the finite set $H^1(L)$ by $(x \cdot \psi)(h) = \psi(x^{-1}hx)$. The kernel of this action is a closed subgroup of finite index in $C$, hence open, so it is $C^{p^k}$ for some $k$. Replacing $C$ by $C^{p^k}$, which is still isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and meets $L$ trivially, we may assume that $C$ acts trivially on $H^1(L)$. Let $c$ be a topological generator of $C$.

(c) *A contradiction.* Let $t : W \to W/L \cong C \to \mathbb{Z}/p$ be the homomorphism with $t(L) = 0$ and $t(c) = 1$. Choose $\varphi \in H^1(L)$ nonzero, and define $\tilde\varphi : W \to \mathbb{Z}/p$ by $\tilde\varphi(hx) = \varphi(h)$ for $h \in L$, $x \in C$. Multiplication $L \times C \to W$ is a continuous bijection of compact Hausdorff spaces, hence a homeomorphism, so $\tilde\varphi$ is continuous. It is a homomorphism, because $(hx)(h'x') = h(xh'x^{-1})\,xx'$ and $\varphi(xh'x^{-1}) = \varphi(h')$, as $C$ fixes $\varphi$.

By input 2, $H^2(W) = 0$, so the cup product $t \cup \tilde\varphi$, the class of the cocycle $(x, y) \mapsto t(x)\tilde\varphi(y)$, is a coboundary: there is a continuous $\gamma : W \to \mathbb{Z}/p$ with
$$t(x)\tilde\varphi(y) = \gamma(x) + \gamma(y) - \gamma(xy) \qquad \text{for all } x, y \in W.$$
For $x, y \in L$ the left side vanishes, so $\psi = \gamma|_L$ lies in $H^1(L)$. Let $h \in L$. The pair $(c, h)$ gives $\varphi(h) = \gamma(c) + \gamma(h) - \gamma(ch)$. The pair $(chc^{-1}, c)$, with $chc^{-1} \in L$ and $(chc^{-1})c = ch$, gives $0 = \gamma(chc^{-1}) + \gamma(c) - \gamma(ch)$. Subtracting, $\varphi(h) = \psi(h) - \psi(chc^{-1})$. But $\psi(chc^{-1}) = (c^{-1} \cdot \psi)(h) = \psi(h)$, as $C$ acts trivially on $H^1(L)$. So $\varphi = 0$, a contradiction.

*Step 2: the general case.* Let $U$ and $H$ be as in part 1. By input 1, $U$ is free pro-$\Sigma$ of rank $(n-1)(F:U) + 1 \ge 2$, and $H$ is open in $F$ if it is open in $U$, so we may replace $F$ by $U$ and assume that $H$ is normal in $F$.

As $H \ne 1$, it has an open normal subgroup $L_0 \ne H$. The finite group $Q = H/L_0$ is a $c(\Sigma)$-group, since closed subgroups and quotients of pro-$\Sigma$ groups are pro-$\Sigma$. Choose a prime $p \in \Sigma$ dividing $|Q|$, a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P_0 \ne 1$ of $Q$, and let $H_1$ be its preimage in $H$. Then $H_1$ is open in $H$, and $H_1 \to P_0 \to P_0/\Phi(P_0)$ is a surjection onto a nonzero $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space, so $H^1(H_1) \ne 0$.

Since $H_1$ is open in $H$, some open normal subgroup $U_1$ of $F$ has $H \cap U_1 \subseteq H_1$. Put $V_1 = H_1U_1$, an open subgroup of $F$. If $h_1u \in H$ with $h_1 \in H_1$ and $u \in U_1$, then $u \in H \cap U_1 \subseteq H_1$, so $H \cap V_1 = H_1$. Hence $H_1$ is normal in $V_1$, because $H$ is normal in $F$. It is topologically finitely generated: if a finite set $X$ topologically generates $H$, the abstract subgroup $\Gamma$ generated by $X$ is dense in $H$, so $\Gamma H_1 = H$ and $\Gamma \cap H_1$ has finite index in $\Gamma$; by Schreier's lemma it is finitely generated, and it is dense in $H_1$, since $H_1$ is open in $H$. By input 1, $V_1$ is free pro-$\Sigma$ of rank at least $2$, so Step 1, applied to $V_1$ and $H_1$, shows that $H_1$ is open in $V_1$, hence in $F$. So $H \supseteq H_1$ is open. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Suppose $z \ne 1$ lies in the centre of $F$. The closed subgroup $Z$ generated by $z$ is procyclic, normal and nontrivial, so it is open by part 1. By input 1 it is free pro-$\Sigma$ of rank $(n-1)(F:Z) + 1 \ge 2$, so $\dim H^1(Z) \ge 2$ for $p \in \Sigma$ by input 3. But $Z$ is generated by one element, so $\dim H^1(Z) \le 1$. Hence the centre is trivial. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Both $N$ and $F$ are normal in $A$, so $[N, F] \subseteq M$. If $M = 1$, every $\alpha \in N$ commutes with every inner automorphism, so $\mathrm{inn}(\alpha(g)) = \alpha \circ \mathrm{inn}(g) \circ \alpha^{-1} = \mathrm{inn}(g)$, and $\alpha(g) = g$ for all $g \in F$ by part 2. Then $N = 1$, which is impossible. So $M \ne 1$. Also $M$ is normal in $A$, hence in $N$ and in $F$. Fix an isomorphism $\theta : N \to F$. Parts 1 and 2 hold for $N$, transported by $\theta$.

*If $M$ is open in $N$ or in $F$, then it is open in both.* If $M$ is open in $F$, it is free pro-$\Sigma$ of finite rank by input 1, so topologically finitely generated, and part 1 for $N$ makes it open in $N$. If $M$ is open in $N$, then $\theta(M)$ is open in $F$, so $M$ is topologically finitely generated, and part 1 for $F$ makes it open in $F$.

*$M$ is not open.* Suppose it is open in both. Then $M$ and $\theta(M)$ are free pro-$\Sigma$ of ranks $(n-1)(F : M) + 1$ and $(n-1)(N : M) + 1$ by input 1. These ranks agree by input 3, as $M \cong \theta(M)$, and $n \ge 2$, so $(F : M) = (N : M)$. The product $NF$ is a closed normal subgroup of $A$, and the natural map $N/M \to NF/F$ is a continuous bijection of compact groups, hence an isomorphism. So $X = NF/F$ is a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(F)$ of order $(N : M)$. A slim profinite group has no nontrivial finite normal subgroup [@minamidetsujimura2024, Remark 1.1.1 (i), p. 5]. Indeed, conjugation gives a continuous homomorphism from $\mathrm{Out}(F)$ to the finite group $\mathrm{Aut}(X)$, whose kernel $Z$ is the centralizer of $X$ and is open; every element of $X$ commutes with $Z$, so $X$ lies in the centralizer of $Z$, which is trivial by input 4. Hence $(F : M) = (N : M) = 1$ and $N = M = F$, contrary to assumption.

*Conclusion.* So $M$ has infinite index in both $N$ and $F$, since closed subgroups of finite index are open. If $M$ were topologically finitely generated, part 1 would make the nontrivial closed normal subgroup $M$ of $F$ open. Finally, if $N \subseteq F$ then $M = N$ has index $1$ in $N$, and if $F \subseteq N$ then $M = F$ has index $1$ in $F$, so both are excluded. $\square$

**Scope.** Parts 1 and 2 use only inputs 1–3. Part 3 also uses input 4, which rests on Minamide and Tsujimura's anabelian argument and was not reproved here. The statement of part 3 was not found in the sources opened: Tsujimura, in July 2026, reports no partial answers to his question on free pro-$\Sigma$ groups [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Introduction, p. 2].
