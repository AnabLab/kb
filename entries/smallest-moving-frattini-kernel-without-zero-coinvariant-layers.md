**Setting.** Write $Q_8=\{\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm ij\}$, and let $c$ be the automorphism of order three with $c(i)=j$ and $c(j)=ij$, so that $c$ cycles $i,j,ij$. Put $V=Q_8/\{\pm1\}\cong\mathbb F_2^2$, on which $c$ acts without nonzero fixed vectors. Let
$$
P=Q_8\wr C_2=(Q_8\times Q_8)\rtimes\langle s\rangle,\qquad s(a,b)s^{-1}=(b,a),
$$
a group of order $128$. Let $T=\langle t\rangle\cong C_3$ act by $t(a,b)=(c(a),c(b))$ and $t(s)=s$. This action respects the relation defining $s$, so $H=P\rtimes T$ is a group of order $384$.

As in the [layered criterion](layered-zero-coinvariant-frattini-kernels-give-dyadic-count-invariance.md), the *moving kernel* $M$ of a target $P\rtimes T$ with $T$ of odd order is the normal closure of all $f^{-1}t(f)$ with $f\in\Phi(P)$ and $t\in T$. A normal subgroup $R\subseteq\Phi(P)$ of $H$ is *layered* if it has a series of normal subgroups of $H$ with elementary-abelian factors having zero conjugation coinvariants.

**Theorem (proved here).**

1. For the target above, $\Phi(P)=M=\{(a,b):a\equiv b\bmod\{\pm1\}\}$. This group has order $16$ and is isomorphic to $Q_8\times C_2$.
2. No nontrivial elementary-abelian normal subgroup of $H$ contained in $M$ has zero coinvariants. Hence $M$ is not layered. Since $\Phi(P)=M$, it is the only normal $R\subseteq\Phi(P)$ with $T$ acting trivially on $\Phi(P)/R$, so the layered criterion applies to no choice of $R$.
3. Let any finite group $T'$ of odd order act on any finite $2$-group $P'$, with moving kernel $M'$. If $M'$ is not layered, then $M'$ is nonabelian, $|M'|\ge16$, $|P'|\ge128$ and $|T'|\ge3$. In particular $|P'\rtimes T'|\ge384$, with equality in the example above.

**Proof of 1.** The abelianization of $P$ is generated by the images of $Q_8\times1$ and $s$. Since $(a,1)$ and $s(a,1)s^{-1}=(1,a)$ have the same image, the map $(a,b)s^e\mapsto(\bar a+\bar b,e)\in V\times\mathbb F_2$ identifies $P/[P,P]$ with $\mathbb F_2^3$. This quotient is elementary abelian, so
$$
\Phi(P)=P^2[P,P]=[P,P]=\{(a,b):\bar a=\bar b\}=\Delta(Q_8)\times\langle(1,-1)\rangle,
$$
where $\Delta$ is the diagonal. The two factors commute and meet trivially, so $\Phi(P)\cong Q_8\times C_2$, of order $16$.

For $a\in Q_8$, the differences $(a,a)^{-1}t(a,a)=(a^{-1}c(a),a^{-1}c(a))$ include $(-ij,-ij)$, $(-i,-i)$ and $(-j,-j)$, from $a=i,j,ij$. They generate $\Delta(Q_8)$. Conjugating $(i,i)$ by $(1,j)\in P$ gives $(i,-i)$, so the normal closure also contains $(1,-1)$. Thus $M\supseteq\Phi(P)$, and $M\subseteq\Phi(P)$ holds by definition. $\square$

**Proof of 2.** An element $(a,b)\in M$ has $b=\pm a$, and its square is $(a^2,a^2)$. So the elements of order at most two in $M$ are the four elements of $Z=\{\pm1\}\times\{\pm1\}$, and every elementary-abelian subgroup of $M$ lies in $Z$. The group $T$ fixes $Z$ pointwise, $Q_8\times Q_8$ centralizes it, and $s$ swaps $(-1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$. So the nontrivial normal subgroups of $H$ inside $Z$ are $\langle(-1,-1)\rangle$ and $Z$. Conjugation moves elements of either one only within the coset of $\langle(-1,-1)\rangle$. Hence both have coinvariants of order two.

If $1=R_0\subseteq R_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq R_n=M$ were a layering series, the first nontrivial term $R_i$ would be a nontrivial elementary-abelian normal subgroup of $H$ inside $M$ with zero coinvariants, which is impossible. $\square$

**Proof of 3.** Put $H'=P'\rtimes T'$ and $F'=\Phi(P')$.

*(a) Abelian kernels are layered.* By [the layered criterion](layered-zero-coinvariant-frattini-kernels-give-dyadic-count-invariance.md), equation (4) and the first item of its list of earlier cases, $[M',H']=M'$, and an abelian $M'$ is layered by its $2$-power filtration. So $M'$ is nonabelian.

*(b) $T'$ acts nontrivially on $M'$.* Otherwise, for each $f\in F'$ the map $c_f(t)=f^{-1}t(f)$ satisfies $c_f(tu)=c_f(t)\,t(c_f(u))=c_f(t)c_f(u)$. It is a homomorphism from an odd-order group to a $2$-group, hence trivial, so $M'=1$, which is abelian.

*(c) $|M'|\ge16$.* A nonabelian $2$-group of order at most $8$ is $D_8$ or $Q_8$. An odd-order automorphism of $D_8=\langle r,s_0:r^4=s_0^2=1,\ s_0rs_0^{-1}=r^{-1}\rangle$ fixes both elements of order four, $r$ and $r^{-1}$, since its orbits have odd size. If it sends $s_0$ to $s_0r^m$, it sends $s_0r^\ell$ to $s_0r^{m+\ell}$, so it acts on the four reflections by a translation of $\mathbb Z/4$ of odd order, which is trivial. So $D_8$ has no nontrivial odd-order automorphism, contradicting (b).

Suppose $M'\cong Q_8$. Let $X$ be the image of $P'$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(M')$ under conjugation, a $2$-group. The image of $M'$ is $\operatorname{Inn}(Q_8)\cong C_2^2$. It lies in $\Phi(X)$, because $M'\subseteq F'$ and the image of a Frattini subgroup lies in the Frattini subgroup of the quotient. An automorphism of $Q_8$ acting trivially on $Q_8/\{\pm1\}$ sends $i\mapsto\pm i$ and $j\mapsto\pm j$, and all four sign choices are inner. So $X/\operatorname{Inn}(Q_8)$ embeds in a $2$-subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)$, which has order at most two. If $|X|=4$, then $\Phi(X)=\Phi(C_2^2)=1$. If $|X|=8$, then $X$ is not cyclic, as it contains $C_2^2$, so $|\Phi(X)|\le2$. Either way $\Phi(X)\not\supseteq\operatorname{Inn}(Q_8)$, a contradiction.

*(d) $|P'|\ge128$.* If $P'$ were cyclic, $F'$ and hence $M'$ would be abelian. So $|P'/F'|\ge4$, and $|P'|\ge4|M'|\ge64$. Suppose $|P'|=64$. Then $|M'|=16$, $M'=F'$ and $P'/F'\cong\mathbb F_2^2$.

Let $P_3=[M',P']M'^2$, a normal subgroup of $H'$. Then $P'/P_3$ has class at most two, since $[P',P']\subseteq M'$ and $[M',P']\subseteq P_3$. Squaring induces a map $\mathsf s:P'/M'\to M'/P_3$, because $(xm)^2\equiv x^2m^2\equiv x^2$ modulo $P_3$ for $m\in M'$. In class two, $(xy)^2=x^2y^2[y,x]$, and commutators induce an alternating bilinear map $\bigwedge^2(P'/M')\to M'/P_3$. As $M'=P'^2[P',P']$, the space $M'/P_3$ is spanned by the values of $\mathsf s$ and of this commutator map.

The group $P'$ acts trivially on $M'/P_3$, so its coinvariants under $H'$ are its $T'$-coinvariants. They vanish, being a quotient of $M'/[M',H']=1$. By averaging over the odd-order group $T'$, also $(M'/P_3)^{T'}=0$. The commutator map is $T'$-equivariant, and $\bigwedge^2(P'/M')$ is one-dimensional with trivial action, so its image consists of fixed vectors and is zero. Hence $[P',P']\subseteq P_3$. Then $\mathsf s$ is additive, so it is a linear surjection from the plane $P'/M'$ onto $M'/P_3$.

Now $M'/P_3\ne0$: it is the space of $P'$-coinvariants of the nonzero space $M'/\Phi(M')$, and a finite $2$-group acting on a nonzero $\mathbb F_2$-space has nonzero coinvariants. A nonzero module without fixed vectors has dimension at least two, so $\mathsf s$ is an isomorphism, $T'$ has no nonzero fixed vector on $P'/M'$, and $|P_3|=4$.

If $T'$ acts nontrivially on $P_3$, then $P_3\cong C_2^2$, because $\operatorname{Aut}(C_4)$ has order two. An element of order three in $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)$ fixes no nonzero vector, so $P_3$ has zero coinvariants under $T'$ and hence under $H'$. Then $1\subseteq P_3\subseteq M'$ is a layering series, contrary to hypothesis.

So $T'$ fixes $P_3$ pointwise. The quotient $P'/C_{P'}(P_3)$ embeds in a $2$-subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(P_3)$, which has order at most two whether $P_3$ is $C_4$ or $C_2^2$. The subgroup $C_{P'}(P_3)$ is $T'$-stable, and $T'$ acts trivially on the quotient, a group of order at most two. So $x^{-1}t(x)\in C_{P'}(P_3)$ for all $x\in P'$ and $t\in T'$. Their images in $P'/M'$, written additively, are the vectors $t\bar x-\bar x$. They span all of $P'/M'=P'/F'$, because the quotient by their span is the coinvariant space, which is isomorphic by averaging to the fixed space $(P'/M')^{T'}=0$. Hence $C_{P'}(P_3)F'=P'$, and so $C_{P'}(P_3)=P'$: the subgroup $P_3$ is central in $P'$.

Choose generators $x,y$ of $P'$ and put $\gamma=[x,y]$, which lies in $P_3$ and is therefore central. Then $[x^2,y]=x\gamma x^{-1}\gamma=\gamma^2$ and $[x^2,y^2]=\gamma^4=1$, since $P_3$ has order four. As $\mathsf s$ is an isomorphism, $x^2$ and $y^2$ span $M'/P_3$, so $M'=\langle x^2,y^2\rangle P_3$ is generated by two commuting elements and a central subgroup. So $M'$ is abelian, contradicting (a). Hence $|P'|\ge128$.

*(e)* Finally $T'\ne1$, since otherwise $M'=1$. The example attains $|M|=16$, $|P|=128$ and $|T|=3$. $\square$

**Remarks.** The example is not excluded by the [minimal obstruction theorem](minimal-dyadic-count-obstructions-have-central-frattini-involutions.md): the involution $(-1,-1)$ is central in $H$ and lies in $\Phi(P)\subseteq\Phi(H)$. Its moving kernel is outside every earlier removal theorem. It is nonabelian, it is not fixed pointwise by $T$, and $C_M(T)=Z\ne1$. The tame marked counts of this target are nonetheless invariant, by [a direct calculation](dyadic-counts-for-the-quaternion-wreath-target.md). So layering is not necessary for invariance. Part 3 does not classify all targets without a layered moving kernel, and it does not show that the example of order $384$ is the unique smallest one.
