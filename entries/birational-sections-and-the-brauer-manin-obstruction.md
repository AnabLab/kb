**Setting.** Let $k$ be a number field and $X$ a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over $k$. A *birational section* is a section of the restriction map $G_{k(X)} \to G_k$, whose kernel is $G_{\bar k(X)}$. As in the [birational section conjecture](birational-section-conjecture.md), a birational section is geometric if its image lies in the decomposition group $D_a \subseteq G_{k(X)}$ of a point $a$, which is then $k$-rational. For a place $v$ of $k$, let $k_v^h$ be the henselization of $k$ at $v$. For an archimedean $v$ this means the algebraic closure of $k$ in $k_v$ [@hararistix2012, footnote 2, p. 2].

**Theorem (Harari–Stix).**

1. If $X$ has a birational section, then $X$ has an adelic point orthogonal to $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ under the Brauer–Manin pairing. If moreover the Jacobian of $X$ has finitely many rational points and a finite Tate–Shafarevich group, then $X(k) \neq \emptyset$ [@hararistix2012, Theorem 3.2, p. 13].
2. If $X$ has a birational section, then $X$ has an adelic point that survives every torsor under a linear $k$-group scheme [@hararistix2012, Theorem 4.2, p. 18]. Its proof gives an adelic point that survives every torsor under a finite $k$-group scheme [@hararistix2012, proof of Theorem 4.2, p. 18].
3. Let $p$ be a prime with $\mu_p \subset k$. Push the sequence $1 \to G_{\bar k(X)} \to G_{k(X)} \to G_k \to 1$ out along the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $G_{\bar k(X)}$. If the resulting sequence splits, then $X$ has an adelic point that survives every torsor under a finite $k$-group scheme whose geometric monodromy is a $p$-group [@hararistix2012, Theorem 4.3, p. 18].
4. Suppose that there is a nonconstant $k$-morphism $X \to A$ to an abelian variety $A$ with $A(k)$ and the Tate–Shafarevich group of $A$ both finite. Then every birational section of $X$ is geometric. So the birational section conjecture holds for $X$ [@hararistix2012, Theorem 3.3, p. 15].

**Proof of part 1.** This summarizes Harari and Stix's proof [@hararistix2012, proof of Theorem 3.2, p. 14].

- *Local points.* A birational section $s$ induces a birational section $s_v^h$ of $X$ over $k_v^h$. This works because $k_v^h$ is algebraic over $k$: inside a separable closure of $k(X)$, the group $G_{k_v^h(X)}$ is the preimage in $G_{k(X)}$ of the decomposition group $G_{k_v^h} \subseteq G_k$, so $s$ restricts to a section over $k_v^h$. Harari and Stix point out that the same would not be clear over $k_v$, since having a birational section is not stable under extension of scalars [@hararistix2012, footnote 4, p. 14]. For a finite place $v$ the field $k_v^h$ is $p$-adically closed, and for a real place it is real closed. Complex places need no argument. By Koenigsmann's argument, part 1 of [Koenigsmann's theorem on birational sections over local fields](birational-sections-over-local-fields.md), $X(k_v^h) \neq \emptyset$, and so $X(k_v) \neq \emptyset$.
- *A limit over covers.* Apply this to every connected branched cover $X' \to X$ with $s(G_k) \subseteq G_{k(X')}$. Each such $X'$ is geometrically connected over $k$. The sets $X'(k_v)$ form a projective system of nonempty compact spaces, so its limit is nonempty. A compatible family gives a point $P_v \in X(k_v)$ whose section, followed by $G_{k_v(X)} \to G_{k(X)}$, is the restriction of $s$ to $G_{k_v}$.
- *Brauer–Manin.* Compose $s$ with $G_{k(X)} \to \pi_1(X)$. This gives a section of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$ whose restriction to each $k_v$ is the section of $P_v$. By the abelian Brauer–Manin test in [descent obstructions for Galois sections](descent-obstructions-for-galois-sections.md), the adelic point $(P_v)$ is orthogonal to $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ [@hararistix2012, Corollary 3.1, p. 13].
- *Rational points.* When the Jacobian has finite Mordell–Weil and Tate–Shafarevich groups, a result of Stoll, also observed earlier by Scharaschkin and Skorobogatov, shows that an adelic point orthogonal to $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ forces $X(k) \neq \emptyset$.

For part 2, the same argument with the interpolation theorem [@hararistix2012, Theorem 2.1, p. 11] in place of the abelian test gives an adelic point that survives every torsor under a finite group scheme. For curves this implies survival of every torsor under a linear group scheme [@hararistix2012, Theorem 4.1, p. 16], using a result of Demarche [@hararistix2012, proof of Theorem 4.1, p. 17]. For part 3, the local points come instead from Pop's theorem on the birational $p$-adic section conjecture, applied to the local pro-$p$ sections [@hararistix2012, proof of Theorem 4.3, p. 18].

**Proof of part 4.** This summarizes Harari and Stix's proof [@hararistix2012, proof of Theorem 3.3, p. 16]. Let $s$ be a birational section, and $X' \to X$ a finite branched cover with $s(G_k) \subseteq G_{k(X')}$. Part 1 applied to $X'$ gives an adelic point of $X'$ orthogonal to $\mathrm{Br}(X')$. The composite $X' \to X \to A$ is nonconstant, so a theorem of Stoll gives $X'(k) \neq \emptyset$. The covers $X'$ of genus at least $2$ are cofinal, and their sets of rational points are finite by Faltings's theorem, so the limit of the sets $X'(k)$ is nonempty. Its image $a \in X(k)$ satisfies $s(G_k) \subseteq D_a$. The [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) entry writes out the same limit argument in detail.

Harari and Stix call part 4 a corollary of Stoll's results. They point out that Stoll's proof does not explain why a birational section over $k$ gives one over $k_v$ [@hararistix2012, footnote 5, p. 15]. The proof of part 1 avoids this step by working over the henselizations [@hararistix2012, footnote 4, p. 14].

**Examples.**

- *Modular curves.* Harari and Stix note that for $p = 11$ and every prime $p \geq 17$, the Jacobian $J_0(p)$ of the modular curve $X_0(p)$ has a quotient $\tilde J$, Mazur's Eisenstein quotient, with $\tilde J(\mathbb{Q})$ and the Tate–Shafarevich group of $\tilde J$ finite [@hararistix2012, Remark (1) after Theorem 3.3, p. 16]. So part 4 applies over $\mathbb{Q}$ to every curve with a nonconstant $\mathbb{Q}$-morphism to $\tilde J$. Their remark phrases the hypothesis as a nonconstant map to $J_0(p)$. What part 4 needs is that the composite map to $\tilde J$ is nonconstant. As examples they name smooth curves cut out of $J_0(p)$ by repeated hypersurface sections of large degree, which exist by Bertini's theorem.
- *Other number fields.* By a result of Mazur and Rubin, every number field $k$ has infinitely many elliptic curves $E$ with $E(k) = 0$. Since the Tate–Shafarevich group is conjecturally always finite, curves with a nonconstant map to such an $E$ should give examples over every number field [@hararistix2012, Remark (2) after Theorem 3.3, p. 16]. This depends on the finiteness conjecture.
- *The Reichardt–Lind curve.* Let $X$ be the projective curve $2T^2 = A^2 - 17B^2$, $AB = C^2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, a nontrivial torsor under its Jacobian. Stix notes that $X$ has index different from $1$, and that the Tate–Shafarevich group of its Jacobian is known to be finite. So by Esnault and Wittenberg's theorem, $X$ has no abelian birational section, and hence no birational section [@stix2011bm, §6.1, p. 23]. See [descent obstructions for Galois sections](descent-obstructions-for-galois-sections.md) for what is known about sections of its étale fundamental group.

**Comparison.** Esnault and Wittenberg show that a section of the geometrically abelian quotient of the birational sequence gives a divisor of degree $1$, if the Tate–Shafarevich group of the Jacobian is finite [@hararistix2012, Remark after Theorem 3.2, p. 15]. See [abelian birational sections](abelian-birational-sections.md). Part 1 assumes a full birational section, and its first conclusion needs no finiteness assumption. When the Tate–Shafarevich group of the Jacobian is finite, part 1 recovers the divisor of degree $1$. An adelic point orthogonal to $\mathrm{Br}(X)$ is in particular orthogonal to the locally constant Brauer classes, so Stix's homomorphism $\iota_X$ on those classes vanishes [@stix2011bm, §3.4, p. 10]. By Eriksson and Scharaschkin's criterion, $X$ then has index $1$ [@stix2011bm, Corollary 21, p. 13].
