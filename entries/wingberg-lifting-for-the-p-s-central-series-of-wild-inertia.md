**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite, $G = G_k$, $P = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/k^{\mathrm{tr}})$ the [wild inertia group](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md), $\Gamma = G/P = \mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{tr}}/k)$ and $\varphi : G \to \Gamma$ the projection. Let $p^s$, with $s \ge 1$, be the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $k^{\mathrm{tr}}$, and let $\alpha$ and $\mathcal U$ be as in the Setting of [the one-step lifting criterion](one-step-lifting-criterion-for-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md): $\mathcal U$ is a set of open normal subgroups of $\Gamma$ contained in $\ker\alpha$, cofinal among all such subgroups. For $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ put $G_{\mathcal H} = \varphi^{-1}(\mathcal H)$.

For a profinite group $Z$ put $Z^{p,1,s} = Z$, and let $Z^{p,i+1,s}$ be the closure of $(Z^{p,i,s})^{p^s}[Z^{p,i,s}, Z]$. This is the descending $p^s$-central series in Nishio's notation [@nishio2026finite, §0, p. 4]. Wingberg uses the same series indexed from $0$: his $Z^i$ is $Z^{p,i+1,s}$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, p. 100]. So the groups $X^i_{\mathcal H}$ of the one-step criterion are the $G_{\mathcal H}^{p,i+1,s}$. Two facts are used throughout.

- (F1) A continuous surjection $f : Z \to Z'$ of profinite groups maps $Z^{p,i,s}$ onto $Z'^{p,i,s}$. This follows by induction on $i$, because a continuous map from a compact space maps the closure of a set onto the closure of its image. In particular every automorphism of $Z$ preserves each $Z^{p,i,s}$.
- (F2) If $Z_1$ is a closed subgroup of $Z$, then $Z_1^{p,i,s} \subseteq Z^{p,i,s}$ for all $i$, by induction on $i$.

For $s = 1$ this is the $p$-central series, and $P^{p,2,1}$ is the Frattini subgroup $\Phi(P)$, the closure of $P^p[P, P]$. For every $s$, $P^{p,2,s} \subseteq \Phi(P)$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $\bar\varphi : G/P^{p,3,s} \to \Gamma$ be induced by $\varphi$, and let $\psi$ be an automorphism of $G/P^{p,3,s}$ with $\bar\varphi \circ \psi = \bar\varphi$. Then there is an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\varphi \circ \theta = \varphi$ that induces the same automorphism of $G/P^{p,2,s}$ as $\psi$.

The automorphism $\psi$ preserves $\ker\bar\varphi = P/P^{p,3,s}$, so by (F1) it preserves the second term of the series of this group, which is $P^{p,2,s}/P^{p,3,s}$, again by (F1). So $\psi$ does induce an automorphism of $G/P^{p,2,s}$. As $P^{p,2,s} \subseteq \Phi(P)$, $\theta$ and $\psi$ also agree modulo $\Phi(P)$.

**Sources.** Nishio states the theorem as Proposition 2.6: for odd $p$, every automorphism of $G/P^{p,2,s}$ that is induced by an automorphism of $G/P^{p,3,s}$ inducing the identity on $G/P$ is induced by an automorphism of $G$ inducing the identity on $G/P$ [@nishio2026finite, Proposition 2.6, p. 11]. There $P = P(G_k)$ is the wild inertia group [@nishio2026finite, §2, p. 8], and $s = s(G_k)$ is the $s$ above [@nishio2026finite, Definition 2.2 (ii) and Lemma 2.3 (ii), p. 9]. His proof refers to Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's (7.5.15) and to Wingberg's Satz 2. Wingberg's Satz 2 is stated with the $p^s$-central series: for two profinite groups with pro-$p$ kernels $V, W$ over the tame group, satisfying condition I of a Demuškin formation at every tame level with the same $n$ and $s$, an isomorphism from $Y/W^2$ to $X/V^2$ over the tame group lifts to an isomorphism from $Y$ to $X$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Satz 2, pp. 100–101]. His $V^2$ is $V^{p,3,s}$ in the notation here. As printed, Satz 2 claims an exact lift, and [uniqueness of Demuškin formations](uniqueness-of-demuskin-formations.md) records Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's correction: the lift agrees with the given map only one step lower. Wingberg's proof of Satz 2 passes to the tame levels $\mathcal H$ and then argues as for his Satz 3 [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, proof of Satz 2, p. 112]. The proof below does the same through the one-step criterion, and adds the lemma that returns from the tame levels to $P$.

**Lemma.** This is proved here. $\bigcap_{\mathcal H \in \mathcal U}\big(P \cap G_{\mathcal H}^{p,2,s}\big) = P^{p,2,s}$.

*Proof.* Since $P \subseteq G_{\mathcal H}$, (F2) gives $P^{p,2,s} \subseteq G_{\mathcal H}^{p,2,s}$. Conversely, let $v \in P$ with $v \notin P^{p,2,s}$. We find $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ and a continuous homomorphism $\chi : G_{\mathcal H} \to \mathbb{Z}/p^s$ with $\chi(v) \ne 0$. Such a $\chi$ kills $G_{\mathcal H}^{p^s}[G_{\mathcal H}, G_{\mathcal H}]$, and so its closure $G_{\mathcal H}^{p,2,s}$, since its kernel is closed. Then $v \notin G_{\mathcal H}^{p,2,s}$.

*(a) A stable open subgroup.* Put $\bar A = P/P^{p,2,s}$. It is a profinite abelian group of exponent dividing $p^s$, as $P^{p,2,s}$ contains $[P, P]$ and $P^{p^s}$, and $G$ acts on it continuously by conjugation. Choose an open subgroup $B_0 \subseteq \bar A$ not containing the class $\bar v$ of $v$. The action map $G \times \bar A \to \bar A$ is continuous and maps $G \times \{0\}$ into $B_0$. As $G$ is compact and the open subgroups of $\bar A$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of $0$, there is an open subgroup $B_1 \subseteq \bar A$ with $gB_1 \subseteq B_0$ for all $g \in G$. The subgroup $B$ generated by all $gB_1$, $g \in G$, lies in $B_0$, contains $B_1$ and is $G$-stable. So $B$ is open, $G$-stable, and $\bar v \notin B$.

*(b) A tame level.* The group $G$ acts on the finite group $\bar A/B$, and the kernel $G_0$ of this action is an open normal subgroup of $G$ containing $P$. As $\mathcal U$ is cofinal among the open normal subgroups of $\Gamma$ inside $\ker\alpha$, there is $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ with $\mathcal H \subseteq \varphi(G_0) \cap \ker\alpha$. Then $G_{\mathcal H} \subseteq G_0$ acts trivially on $\bar A/B$.

*(c) A central extension.* Let $W \subseteq P$ be the inverse image of $B$. It is open in $P$ and normal in $G$. Put $E = G_{\mathcal H}/W$ and $C = P/W \cong \bar A/B$. Then $C$ is a finite abelian $p$-group of exponent dividing $p^s$, it is central in $E$ by (b), and $E/C \cong G_{\mathcal H}/P \cong \mathcal H$.

*(d) $C$ is a direct factor.* We show that there is a continuous homomorphism $r : E \to C$ that is the identity on $C$. Let $I_\Gamma \subseteq \Gamma$ be the image of the inertia group. It is normal, $I_\Gamma \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ and $\Gamma/I_\Gamma \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ [@nishio2026finite, Lemma 2.1 (ii) and (iii), p. 8].

- Let $E_1 \subseteq E$ be the inverse image of $\mathcal H \cap I_\Gamma$, a normal subgroup of $E$. As $E_1/C \cong \mathcal H \cap I_\Gamma$ is a closed subgroup of the procyclic group $I_\Gamma$, $E_1$ is topologically generated by $C$ and one element. As $C$ is central, $E_1$ is abelian. So $E_1$ is the direct product of its Sylow pro-$\ell$ subgroups. Its Sylow pro-$p$ subgroup maps into that of $E_1/C$, which is trivial, so it is $C$. Hence $E_1 = C \times J$, where $J$, the product of the Sylow pro-$\ell$ subgroups for $\ell \ne p$, is characteristic in $E_1$ and hence normal in $E$.
- In $E/J$ the image of $C$ is a central subgroup isomorphic to $C$, and the quotient by it is $E/E_1 \cong \mathcal H/(\mathcal H \cap I_\Gamma) \cong \mathcal H I_\Gamma/I_\Gamma$. This is an open subgroup of $\Gamma/I_\Gamma \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, so it is $n\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ for some $n \ge 1$, which is isomorphic to $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ by $x \mapsto nx$, as $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is torsion-free. Let $c_0 \in E/E_1$ correspond to $1$ under such an isomorphism, and lift it to $c \in E/J$. The continuous homomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to E/J$ with $1 \mapsto c$ gives a continuous section $E/E_1 \to E/J$. Its image $S$ is a closed subgroup meeting the image of $C$ trivially, and together they generate $E/J$. As $C$ is central, multiplication is an isomorphism $C \times S \to E/J$ of compact groups.

Let $r$ be the composite of $E \to E/J$ with the projection $E/J \cong C \times S \to C$. It is the identity on $C$, as $C \cap J = 1$.

*(e) The character.* The finite abelian group $C$ has exponent dividing $p^s$, so its homomorphisms to $\mathbb{Z}/p^s$ separate points. Choose one, $\chi_0$, with $\chi_0(vW) \ne 0$, and let $\chi$ be the composite $G_{\mathcal H} \to E \xrightarrow{r} C \xrightarrow{\chi_0} \mathbb{Z}/p^s$. Then $\chi(v) = \chi_0(vW) \ne 0$, as $vW \in C$. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.**

*Step 1: tame levels.* Let $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$. By (F2), $P^{p,3,s} \subseteq G_{\mathcal H}^{p,3,s}$. Since $\psi$ and $\psi^{-1}$ commute with $\bar\varphi$, $\psi$ maps $\bar\varphi^{-1}(\mathcal H) = G_{\mathcal H}/P^{p,3,s}$ onto itself. By (F1), the third term of the series of this group is $G_{\mathcal H}^{p,3,s}/P^{p,3,s}$, and $\psi$ preserves it. So $\psi$ induces an automorphism $\psi_{\mathcal H}$ of $G/G_{\mathcal H}^{p,3,s} = G/X^2_{\mathcal H}$ commuting with the projection to $\Gamma/\mathcal H$, an element of the set $A_{\mathcal H, 2}$ of the one-step criterion. For $\mathcal H' \subseteq \mathcal H$ in $\mathcal U$, $\psi_{\mathcal H'}$ induces $\psi_{\mathcal H}$, as both are induced by $\psi$.

*Step 2: the one-step criterion.* Case 1 of [the one-step lifting criterion](one-step-lifting-criterion-for-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), with $i = 1$ and the compatible family $(\psi_{\mathcal H})$, gives an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\varphi \circ \theta = \varphi$ that agrees with every $\psi_{\mathcal H}$ modulo $X^1_{\mathcal H} = G_{\mathcal H}^{p,2,s}$. Since $P^{p,3,s} \subseteq G_{\mathcal H}^{p,2,s}$, this says that $\theta$ and $\psi$ induce the same automorphism of $G/G_{\mathcal H}^{p,2,s}$.

*Step 3: back to $P$.* Let $g \in G$, and choose $g' \in G$ with $g'P^{p,3,s} = \psi(gP^{p,3,s})$. Put $e = \theta(g)g'^{-1}$. Then $\varphi(e) = \varphi(g)\varphi(g)^{-1} = 1$, because $\varphi \circ \theta = \varphi$ and $\bar\varphi \circ \psi = \bar\varphi$. So $e \in P$. By Step 2, $e \in G_{\mathcal H}^{p,2,s}$ for every $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$. By the Lemma, $e \in P^{p,2,s}$. So $\theta(g) \equiv g'$ modulo $P^{p,2,s}$ for every $g$, which is the claim. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What is not claimed.* Nothing is claimed about $\theta$ modulo $P^{p,3,s}$: as in the one-step criterion, one step of precision is lost. For $s \ge 2$ the $p^s$-central series differs from the $p$-central series, and nothing is claimed here about lifting automorphisms of quotients by terms of the $p$-central series.
- *Where the Lemma is needed.* The one-step criterion controls $\theta$ only modulo the subgroups $X^1_{\mathcal H}$, which are not contained in $P$. Their traces on $P$ contain the commutators $[x, v]$ with $x \in G_{\mathcal H}$ and $v \in P$, and the Lemma says these traces shrink to $P^{p,2,s}$. Part (d) of its proof shows that the obstruction to extending an $\mathcal H$-invariant character of $P$ to $G_{\mathcal H}$ vanishes. The proof uses only that the tame inertia part of $\mathcal H$ has order prime to $p$ and that its quotient is procyclic.
- *Use.* [Units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), part 2, and [the mixing automorphisms](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), parts 2 and 3, construct automorphisms of $G_K/P_K^{p,3,s}$ over the tame quotient and lift them with this theorem.
- *The case $p = 2$.* Not treated, as in the one-step criterion.
