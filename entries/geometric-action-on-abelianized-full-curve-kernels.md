**Setting.** Let $C,D$ be smooth, proper, geometrically integral curves over a finite field $k$ of characteristic $p$, with $g(C)\ge2$. Write $\Pi_X=\pi_1(X)$ and $\Delta_X=\pi_1(X_{\bar k})$. Suppose that $\phi:\Pi_D\to\Pi_C$ is continuous, has open image $H$, and induces the identity on $G_k$. Let $Y\to C$ be its finite etale image cover. The [finite-character theorem](finite-character-kernels-of-open-curve-group-maps.md) gives
$$
q:A=\Delta_D\twoheadrightarrow B=\Delta_Y,\qquad
N=\ker q=\ker\phi,\qquad \delta=g(D)-g(Y)\ge0.
$$
For $\ell\ne p$, let $T_\ell$ be the maximal abelian pro-$\ell$ quotient of $N$, with its compact topology, and set
$$
W_{\ell,a}=T_\ell/\ell^a T_\ell
=N/\overline{[N,N]N^{\ell^a}}\qquad(a\ge1).
$$
Here $N^{\ell^a}$ denotes the subgroup generated by all $\ell^a$th powers. Conjugation by $A$ induces continuous $B$-actions on these quotients, since conjugation by $N$ is trivial on them. All cohomology below is continuous.

**Theorem (proved here).**

1. For every open subgroup $V\subseteq B$ and every $a\ge1$,
   $$W_{\ell,a}^{V}=0,\qquad T_\ell^{V}=0.\tag{1}$$
   Thus no nonzero element of these compact modules has finite $B$-orbit, and none of them contains a nonzero finite $B$-stable subgroup.
2. Write $W=W_{\ell,1}$. For every open $V\subseteq B$ of index $m$, put $A_V=q^{-1}(V)$ and
   $$
   \epsilon_V=\dim_{\mathbb F_\ell}\ker\bigl(H^2(V,\mathbb F_\ell)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{inf}}H^2(A_V,\mathbb F_\ell)\bigr)\in\{0,1\}.
   $$
   Its compact coinvariant quotient is finite, with
   $$
   \dim_{\mathbb F_\ell}W_V=2m\delta+\epsilon_V,\qquad
   W_V=W/\overline{\langle vw-w:v\in V,\ w\in W\rangle}.\tag{2}
   $$

No well-behavedness hypothesis on $\phi$ is needed. These are abelian quotients of the full profinite kernel $N$; the source and target groups $A,B$ are not replaced by their maximal pro-$\ell$ quotients.

**Curve-cohomology input.** If $P$ is the geometric fundamental group of a smooth proper connected curve of genus at least one over $\bar k$, then, for a finite continuous $\mathbb Z/\ell^a$-module $L$,
$$
H^2(P,L)\cong L_P,\qquad H^i(P,L)=0\quad(i>2).\tag{3}
$$
Indeed, group cohomology agrees with the curve's etale cohomology, whose top group is the coefficient coinvariant module with a Tate twist; Poincare duality gives the vanishing above degree two [@stacks2026, Section 64.29, Lemma 64.29.1, Proposition 64.29.3 and Remark 64.29.4, tag 03VJ]. Choose a primitive $\ell^a$th root in $\bar k$ to suppress the twist; geometric monodromy acts trivially on it. Every discrete continuous $\mathbb Z/\ell^a$-module is a filtered union of finite stable submodules: the orbit of each element is finite, and the span of finitely many such orbits is finite. Both coinvariants and continuous cohomology commute with this union, the latter by [@gilleszamuely2006, Lemma 4.3.3, pp. 91–92]. Thus (3) holds for arbitrary discrete continuous $\mathbb Z/\ell^a$-modules as well, with algebraic coinvariants.

**Proof of (1).** Fix $V$ and $a$, and write $R_a=\mathbb Z/\ell^a$ and
$$M_a=H^1(N,R_a)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(N,R_a).$$
This is a discrete continuous $V$-module. The [projective-kernel theorem](projective-kernels-of-open-curve-group-maps.md) gives $\operatorname{cd}(N)\le1$. The cohomology exact sequence for such a kernel, applied to $1\to N\to A_V\to V\to1$, contains
$$
H^3(A_V,R_a)\longrightarrow H^2(V,M_a)\longrightarrow H^4(V,R_a)
$$
[@gilleszamuely2006, Construction 6.8.1 and Proposition 6.8.2, pp. 172–173]. Both $A_V$ and $V$ are geometric fundamental groups of proper finite etale covers of curves of genus at least two. Consequently (3) kills the outer terms. It also identifies the middle term with $(M_a)_V$, so
$$ (M_a)_V=0.\tag{4}$$
Continuous character duality identifies $M_a$ with the dual of $W_{\ell,a}$ [@stacks2026, Section 64.28, Trivial duality, tag 03VI]. More explicitly, use the pairing into $\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell$ given by embedding $R_a$ as its subgroup killed by $\ell^a$. A continuous character factors through $W_{\ell,a}$, and character duality recovers this compact quotient. The dual of the discrete coinvariant module $(M_a)_V$ is $W_{\ell,a}^{V}$: a functional annihilates every $vf-f$ exactly when it is fixed by $V$. Thus (4) proves the first vanishing in (1).

If $t\in T_\ell^{V}$, its image in every $W_{\ell,a}$ vanishes, so $t\in\bigcap_a\ell^aT_\ell=0$. To see the last equality, any nonzero element of a compact abelian pro-$\ell$ group is detected in a finite quotient, and that quotient has exponent dividing some $\ell^a$. Finally a finite orbit has a closed finite-index stabilizer, hence an open stabilizer, contradicting (1) unless the element is zero. A finite stable subgroup consists of finite orbits. This proves part 1.

**Proof of (2).** The five-term sequence is
$$
0\longrightarrow H^1(V,\mathbb F_\ell)\longrightarrow H^1(A_V,\mathbb F_\ell)
\longrightarrow H^1(N,\mathbb F_\ell)^V
\longrightarrow H^2(V,\mathbb F_\ell)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{inf}}H^2(A_V,\mathbb F_\ell)
$$
[@gilleszamuely2006, Proposition 3.3.14, p. 65, and Corollary 4.3.5, p. 93]. Both degree-two spaces are one-dimensional by (3) with constant coefficients. The [finite-character theorem](finite-character-kernels-of-open-curve-group-maps.md), equation (1), makes the image of the degree-one restriction map have dimension $2m\delta$. Exactness therefore gives
$$\dim H^1(N,\mathbb F_\ell)^V=2m\delta+\epsilon_V.$$
This discrete invariant space is the continuous dual of the compact coinvariant quotient $W_V$. A compact elementary abelian group with finite-dimensional character space is finite of that same dimension. This proves (2). $\square$

**A ramified-cover check (proved here).** Let $f:D\to C$ be a ramified separable double cover in odd characteristic, with $g(C)\ge2$. Then $f_*:\Pi_D\to\Pi_C$ is surjective: its quadratic function-field extension cannot lie in any unramified extension, so it is linearly disjoint from every connected finite etale cover of $C$, whose pullback therefore stays connected. Here $Y=C$. Under the comparison in (3), inflation on degree-two constant cohomology is $f^*$. Kummer theory identifies $H^2(X_{\bar k},\mu_\ell)$ with $\operatorname{Pic}(X_{\bar k})/\ell\cong\mathbb Z/\ell$ by degree [@milne2013lec, Propositions 14.1–14.2 and the proof of Proposition 14.2, pp. 89–90]. Pullback of a divisor by $f$ doubles its degree, including ramification multiplicities. Consequently $\epsilon_B=1$ if $\ell=2$, and $\epsilon_B=0$ otherwise. Formula (2) gives $\dim W_B=2\delta+1$ at $\ell=2$ and $2\delta$ at odd $\ell\ne p$. Thus the correction term cannot be omitted even for maps induced by morphisms of proper curves.

**Scope and checks.** If $N=1$, all modules vanish and inflation is an isomorphism, so $\delta=\epsilon_V=0$. If $N\ne1$, the finite-character theorem gives $\delta>0$: equation (2) then supplies nonzero finite coinvariant quotients even though (1) forbids nonzero finite stable subgroups. In particular the quotient $W\twoheadrightarrow W_V$ has no $V$-equivariant section. This distinguishes compact invariants from discrete character invariants, which are dual to compact coinvariants.

The argument uses geometric conjugation; it asserts no analogous vanishing for a Frobenius subgroup alone. It also makes no assertion at $\ell=p$, where (3) is unavailable. The [proper-source point-surjectivity question](properness-and-point-surjectivity-of-fundamental-group-maps.md) remains open: the theorem constrains its possible kernels but does not produce a punctured-target lift.
