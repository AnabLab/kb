**Setting.** Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic other than $2$. Curves over $K$ are smooth and connected. As in [unramified correspondences preserve A-qLT-type](unramified-correspondences-preserve-a-qlt-type.md), a curve $U$ *lies over* a curve $V$, written $U \Rightarrow V$, if some connected finite étale cover of $U$ admits a dominant morphism to $V$. A *tripod* is $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$. For a nonconstant morphism $f : X \to \mathbb{P}^1$ from a smooth proper curve and a point $x \in X$, $e_x(f)$ is the ramification index of $f$ at $x$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Criterion.* Let $X$ be a smooth proper connected curve over $K$, let $S \subset X(K)$ be finite, and put $U = X \setminus S$. Suppose there are a nonconstant morphism $f : X \to \mathbb{P}^1$ and four distinct points $b_1, b_2, b_3, b_4 \in \mathbb{P}^1(K)$ such that $f^{-1}(b_4) \subseteq S$ and, for each $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, either $f^{-1}(b_i) \subseteq S$ or $e_x(f)$ is even for every $x \in f^{-1}(b_i)$. Then $U \Rightarrow \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$.
2. *Cases.* The hypothesis of part 1 holds in each of the following cases.
   - (a) There are a nonconstant morphism $\varphi : X \to E$ to a curve $E$ of genus $1$ and a point $P \in E$ with $\varphi^{-1}(P) \subseteq S$.
   - (b) $X$ has genus $1$ and $S$ is nonempty. This is case (a) with $\varphi$ the identity.
   - (c) $X$ is hyperelliptic of genus at least $2$, and $S$ contains a Weierstrass point, or both points of a fibre of the double cover $X \to \mathbb{P}^1$.

   If $X$ has genus $0$ and $S$ has at least three points, then $U \Rightarrow \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ as well.
3. *A-qLT-type.* Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, let $U$ be a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $k$, and let $\bar k$ be an algebraic closure of $k$. If $U_{\bar k}$ lies over the tripod over $\bar k$, for instance if the compactification of $U_{\bar k}$ and its set of cusps satisfy the hypothesis of part 1 or fall under one of the cases of part 2, then $\pi_1(U) \to G_k$ is of A-qLT-type. In particular, every hyperbolic curve of genus $0$ or $1$ over $k$ is of A-qLT-type, whatever its moduli, and so is every hyperelliptic curve over $k$ as in part 2 (c).

For such $U$, part 2 of the [Lubin–Tate criterion](lubin-tate-criterion-for-semi-absolute-anabelian-geometry.md) applies with $U$ as the target: every semi-absolute homomorphism $\pi_1(X_1) \to \pi_1(U)$, for a hyperbolic curve $X_1$ over a finite extension of some $\mathbb{Q}_{p_1}$, arises from a unique dominant morphism of schemes $X_1 \to U$ over a morphism of the base fields [@mochizuki2012, Corollary 3.8, condition (g), p. 42].

**Proof of part 1.** Apply an automorphism of $\mathbb{P}^1$ to $f$, which changes neither fibres nor ramification indices, so that $b_4 = \infty$, $b_1 = 0$, $b_2 = 1$ and $b_3 = \lambda$ with $\lambda \in K \setminus \{0, 1\}$. Let $L = K(X)$. Since $f$ is nonconstant, it is transcendental over $K$.

*Step 1: a cover.* In an algebraic closure of $L$ choose $s_0, s_1, s_\lambda$ with
$$s_0^2 = f, \qquad s_1^2 = f - 1, \qquad s_\lambda^2 = f - \lambda,$$
put $M = L(s_0, s_1, s_\lambda)$, and let $\pi : Y \to X$ be the normalization of $X$ in $M$. Since $K$ is algebraically closed, $Y$ is a smooth proper connected curve and $\pi$ is finite.

*Step 2: $\pi$ is étale over $U$.* Let $x \in U$. Since $f^{-1}(\infty) \subseteq S$, $f(x) \ne \infty$. If $f(x) = 0$, then $f^{-1}(0) \not\subseteq S$, so by hypothesis $e_x(f)$ is even, and $v_x(f) = e_x(f)$. If $f(x) \ne 0$, then $v_x(f) = 0$. So $v_x(f)$ is even, and in the same way $v_x(f - 1)$ and $v_x(f - \lambda)$ are even. Build $M$ in three steps, adjoining $s_0$, then $s_1$, then $s_\lambda$. At each step one adjoins a square root of an element $g$ whose valuation at every place $w$ above $x$ is even, since it is $e(w/x)$ times an even number. Such a step is unramified at $w$. Indeed, write $g = \varpi^{2m} u$ with $\varpi$ a uniformizer at $w$ and $u$ a unit at $w$, so that adjoining $\sqrt g$ is the same as adjoining $\sqrt u$. If $u$ is a square nothing changes. Otherwise $T^2 - u$ has derivative $2T$, which is a unit at its roots because $2$ and $u$ are units, so the extension of local rings is étale. Hence every point of $Y$ over $x$ has ramification index $1$. As $K$ is algebraically closed, $\pi$ is étale over $U$. So $Y_U = \pi^{-1}(U)$ is a finite étale cover of $U$, and it is connected, being a nonempty open subset of the irreducible curve $Y$.

*Step 3: units on $Y_U$.* The function $f$ is regular on $U$, because $f^{-1}(\infty) \subseteq S$. So $s_0^2$, $s_1^2$ and $s_\lambda^2$ are regular on $Y_U$, and since $Y_U$ is normal, $s_0$, $s_1$ and $s_\lambda$ are regular on $Y_U$. Moreover
$$(s_0 - s_1)(s_0 + s_1) = 1, \qquad (s_0 - s_\lambda)(s_0 + s_\lambda) = \lambda, \qquad (s_1 - s_\lambda)(s_1 + s_\lambda) = \lambda - 1.$$
The right sides are nonzero constants, so $s_0 - s_1$, $s_0 - s_\lambda$ and $s_1 - s_\lambda$ are invertible on $Y_U$. Put
$$F = \frac{s_0 - s_1}{s_0 - s_\lambda}, \qquad 1 - F = \frac{s_1 - s_\lambda}{s_0 - s_\lambda}.$$
Both are invertible on $Y_U$, so $F$ defines a morphism from $Y_U$ to $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$.

*Step 4: $F$ is not constant.* Suppose $F = c$ with $c \in K$. Then $c \ne 0$, since $F$ is invertible. From $s_0 - s_1 = c(s_0 - s_\lambda)$ we get $(1 - c) s_0 = s_1 - c s_\lambda$. Squaring gives
$$(1 - c)^2 f = (f - 1) + c^2 (f - \lambda) - 2c\, s_1 s_\lambda,$$
so $2c\, s_1 s_\lambda = 2c f - (1 + c^2 \lambda)$, and $s_1 s_\lambda = f - \beta$ with $\beta = (1 + c^2 \lambda)/(2c)$. Squaring again gives $(f - 1)(f - \lambda) = (f - \beta)^2$. As $f$ is transcendental over $K$, this is an identity of polynomials in $f$. Comparing coefficients gives $1 + \lambda = 2\beta$ and $\lambda = \beta^2$, so $(1 + \lambda)^2 = 4\lambda$, that is, $(1 - \lambda)^2 = 0$. This contradicts $\lambda \ne 1$.

*Step 5.* A nonconstant morphism from the irreducible curve $Y_U$ to the irreducible curve $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ is dominant. So the connected finite étale cover $Y_U \to U$ and the morphism $F$ show that $U \Rightarrow \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.**

- *A double cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ by a genus-$1$ curve.* Let $E$ have genus $1$ and $P \in E$. By Riemann–Roch, $L(2P)$ has dimension $2$ and $L(P)$ consists of the constants, so some $x \in L(2P)$ has a pole of order exactly $2$ at $P$ and no other pole. Then $x : E \to \mathbb{P}^1$ has degree $2$ and $x^{-1}(\infty) = \{P\}$, with $e_P(x) = 2$. Since the characteristic is not $2$, the Riemann–Hurwitz formula gives $0 = 2 \cdot (-2) + \sum_y (e_y(x) - 1)$, so there are exactly four ramification points, each of index $2$. A fibre of a degree-$2$ map containing a ramification point consists of that point alone. So there are three distinct points $c_1, c_2, c_3 \in \mathbb{P}^1(K) \setminus \{\infty\}$ whose fibres are single points $T_1, T_2, T_3$, with $e_{T_i}(x) = 2$.
- *Case (a).* With $x$ as above for $E$ and $P$, put $f = x \circ \varphi$, $b_4 = \infty$ and $b_i = c_i$ for $i \le 3$. Then $f^{-1}(\infty) = \varphi^{-1}(P) \subseteq S$. Every $y \in f^{-1}(c_i)$ has $\varphi(y) = T_i$, so $e_y(f) = e_y(\varphi) \cdot e_{T_i}(x) = 2 e_y(\varphi)$ is even. The map $f$ is nonconstant because $\varphi$ and $x$ are.
- *Case (c).* Let $f : X \to \mathbb{P}^1$ be the double cover. By Riemann–Hurwitz, $2g - 2 = 2 \cdot (-2) + \sum_y (e_y(f) - 1)$, so there are $2g + 2 \ge 6$ ramification points, each of index $2$, each alone in its fibre. They are the Weierstrass points. If $S$ contains a Weierstrass point $W$, take $b_4 = f(W)$ and three other branch values as $b_1, b_2, b_3$. If $S$ contains a fibre $f^{-1}(b)$, take $b_4 = b$ and three branch values other than $b$.
- *Genus $0$.* Choose a coordinate on $X \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ in which $0$, $1$ and $\infty$ lie in $S$. Then $U$ is a dense open subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$, and the inclusion is dominant, with $U$ itself as the cover. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** The tripod $P_k = \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ is of A-qLT-type by Tamagawa's criterion $(\ast^{\mathrm{A\text{-}qLT}})$, applied with $Y = X = P_k$ and the identity morphism [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1, p. 42]. Mochizuki's appendix completes the proof of that criterion [@mochizuki2015, Appendix, (CM5), p. 1153]. By hypothesis $U_{\bar k} \Rightarrow (P_k)_{\bar k}$, so $U$ is of A-qLT-type by part 2 of [unramified correspondences preserve A-qLT-type](unramified-correspondences-preserve-a-qlt-type.md). For a hyperbolic curve $U$ over $k$, the curve $U_{\bar k}$ is $X \setminus S$ with $X$ smooth proper of the same genus and $S$ the set of cusps. So the cases of part 2 give the hypothesis, and hyperbolicity excludes genus $0$ with fewer than three cusps and genus $1$ with no cusp. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What is new.* Tamagawa's criterion asks for a finite étale cover with a dominant morphism to a tripod (see [the problem](a-qlt-problem-for-p-adic-hyperbolic-curves.md)). Part 1 gives an explicit sufficient condition for this. Mochizuki derives the A-qLT property from complex multiplication through Belyi maps, and notes that the problem is of particular interest for classes of curves where Belyi techniques cannot be applied, such as proper ones [@mochizuki2015, p. 1154]. Part 1 needs no number field: it uses only square roots of $f$, $f - 1$ and $f - \lambda$, whose squares differ pairwise by nonzero constants. So it covers curves with transcendental moduli in every genus, for instance every hyperelliptic curve with one Weierstrass point removed.
- *Proper curves.* Part 1 says nothing about proper curves, because $f^{-1}(b_4)$ is nonempty and must lie in $S$. This is necessary for the conclusion, since no finite étale cover of a proper curve maps dominantly to a tripod (see [the problem](a-qlt-problem-for-p-adic-hyperbolic-curves.md)). So the case of $y^2 = x^6 - 1$, to which [unramified correspondences](unramified-correspondences-preserve-a-qlt-type.md) reduce all proper hyperelliptic curves, stays open. That curve minus one of its Weierstrass points is covered by part 2 (c).
- *Curves not covered.* For a hyperelliptic curve $X$ of genus at least $2$ with a single cusp $Q$ that is not a Weierstrass point, the double cover gives no admissible $f$, because the other point of the fibre through $Q$ is not a cusp. Other maps sometimes give one, but in characteristic $0$ only for finitely many $Q$ on a fixed $X$ (see [finitely many single cusps satisfy the four-fibre hypothesis](finitely-many-single-cusps-satisfy-the-four-fibre-hypothesis.md)). Whether such curves lie over a tripod is not settled here.
- *Comparison with Bogomolov and Tschinkel.* Their constructions combine fibre products in which "ramification cancels ramification" with multiplication maps on elliptic curves [@bogomolovtschinkel2002unramified, §2, pp. 4–5]. In their Section 4 the curves are over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@bogomolovtschinkel2002unramified, Remark 4.2, p. 9], so of characteristic zero. There they show that every proper curve over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ with a map to $\mathbb{P}^1$ having even local ramification indices over at least five points has an unramified cover dominating the genus-$2$ curve $\sqrt[6]{z(1-z)}$ [@bogomolovtschinkel2002unramified, Notations 4.4, Example 4.5 and Proposition 4.6, p. 11]. Part 1 is a statement of the same kind for open curves, in every characteristic other than $2$. Four values suffice when one of the fibres consists of cusps, and the target is a tripod.
