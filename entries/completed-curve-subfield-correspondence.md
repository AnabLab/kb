**Setting.** The notation follows the [uniform support bound](uniform-support-bound-for-kummer-pencils.md). Fix a prime $\ell$. For a function field $F/\kappa$, finitely generated over an algebraically closed field $\kappa$, put $W_F = \mathrm{Hom}(F^\times, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ and $\widehat F = \varprojlim_e F^\times/F^{\times \ell^e}$, written additively, with $[f]$ the class of $f$. Then $\widehat F = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(W_F, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$, $\widehat F/\ell^e \widehat F = F^\times/F^{\times \ell^e}$, $\widehat F$ is torsion-free, and evaluation on $W_F$ detects every class modulo $\ell^e$ [@openai2026bp, Lemma 2.1, p. 4]. For a valuation $v$ put $I_v = U_v^\perp \subseteq D_v = (U^1_v)^\perp$ in $W_F$. For a quasi-prime $r$-divisor $v$, restriction to units gives $D_v/I_v \cong W_{Fv}$, and the class of a $v$-unit induces the class of its residue [@openai2026bp, Lemma 3.2, p. 8]; this is checked in the support-bound entry. See [quasi prime divisors](quasi-prime-divisor.md).

A *curve subfield* of $F/\kappa$ is a subfield $E \supseteq \kappa$ that is relatively algebraically closed in $F$, with $\mathrm{trdeg}(E/\kappa) = 1$ [@openai2026bp, §6, p. 17]. For $t \in E \setminus \kappa$, $E$ is finite over $\kappa(t)$: complete $t$ to a transcendence basis $t, x_2, \dots, x_s$ of $F/\kappa$. Then $E/\kappa(t)$ is algebraic and $\kappa(t, x_2, \dots, x_s)/\kappa(t)$ is purely transcendental, so the two are linearly disjoint, and $[E : \kappa(t)] \le [F : \kappa(t, x_2, \dots, x_s)]$. Write $C_E$ for the smooth projective curve with function field $E$. A $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module has *infinite rank* if it contains infinitely many $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-linearly independent elements.

**Matching local data.** Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be function fields of the same transcendence degree $d \ge 2$, with $\mathrm{char}\, k, \mathrm{char}\, l \ne \ell$. Let $\varphi : W_L \to W_K$ be a continuous $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module isomorphism, with evaluation dual $\Theta : \widehat K \to \widehat L$, $\Theta(h)(\sigma) = h(\varphi\sigma)$ [@openai2026bp, (1), p. 4]. $\Theta$ is an isomorphism, and its inverse is the dual of $\varphi^{-1}$. Say that $\varphi$ *matches local data* if:

- (L1) for $r = 1$ and for $r = d-1$, $\varphi$ maps the set of pairs $(I_w, D_w)$, for $w$ a quasi-prime $r$-divisor of $L/l$, onto the set of pairs $(I_v, D_v)$, for $v$ a quasi-prime $r$-divisor of $K/k$;
- (L2) if $w, v$ are quasi-prime $(d-1)$-divisors with $\varphi(I_w) = I_v$ and $\varphi(D_w) = D_v$, then $lw$ is algebraic over a finite field exactly when $kv$ is, and in that case the isomorphism $W_{Lw} = D_w/I_w \to D_v/I_v = W_{Kv}$ induced by $\varphi$ maps the point inertia lines of $C_{Lw}$ onto those of $C_{Kv}$.

The conditions are symmetric, so $\varphi^{-1}$ also matches local data. Both $\varphi$ and $\varphi^{-1}$ then match residual curves in the sense of the support-bound entry, so its part 2 applies to $\Theta$ and to $\Theta^{-1}$.

The manuscript claims that every bracket-compatible isomorphism, the datum of the [Bogomolov–Pop conjecture](bogomolov-pop-conjecture.md), matches local data. (L1) is part of its Proposition 3.5, and (L2) is its Proposition 3.7 [@openai2026bp, Propositions 3.5 and 3.7, pp. 10–12]. **These propositions are not checked here.**

**Theorem** [@openai2026bp, Theorem 6.1, p. 18]. Suppose that $\varphi$ matches local data. For every curve subfield $E$ of $K/k$ there is a unique curve subfield $P$ of $L/l$ with
$$\Theta(\widehat E) = \widehat P,$$
where $\widehat E \subseteq \widehat K$ and $\widehat P \subseteq \widehat L$ by Lemma A. The assignment $E \mapsto P$ is a bijection from the curve subfields of $K/k$ onto those of $L/l$.

**Lemmas.** In Lemmas A, E and F, $\kappa$ is algebraically closed of characteristic different from $\ell$.

*Lemma A (subfields and finite extensions)* [@openai2026bp, Lemma 2.2, pp. 4–5]. (1) If $E \subseteq F$ are function fields over $\kappa$ and $E$ is relatively algebraically closed in $F$, then $\widehat E \to \widehat F$ is injective, and its image is a direct summand, so it is closed and $\ell$-saturated: $\ell h \in \widehat E$ implies $h \in \widehat E$. (2) If $F'/F$ is finite, possibly inseparable, then $\widehat F \to \widehat{F'}$ is injective, and inclusion followed by the norm is multiplication by $[F' : F]$ on $\widehat F$.

*Proof.* (1) Let $V$ be a normal projective variety over $E$ with function field $F$. Its global functions form a finite field extension of $E$ inside $F$, which is $E$. A function with zero divisor on the normal $V$ is a global unit, so $F^\times/E^\times$ embeds in the free group of Weil divisors and is free. So $0 \to E^\times/\kappa^\times \to F^\times/\kappa^\times \to F^\times/E^\times \to 0$ splits. As $\kappa^\times$ is divisible, completing gives $\widehat F = \widehat E \oplus Q$, with $Q$ the completion of a free abelian group, which is torsion-free. (2) The norm $F'^\times \to F^\times$ restricts to $x \mapsto x^{[F':F]}$ on $F^\times$, so the composite is multiplication by $[F' : F]$, which is injective on the torsion-free $\widehat F$. $\square$

*Lemma B (divisor-free Kummer classes).* Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_l$ be a normal integral projective model of $L/l$, and let $G_X$ be the constant of part 1 of the support-bound entry. The classes in $L^\times/L^{\times \ell}$ whose order along every prime divisor of $X$ is divisible by $\ell$ form a subspace of dimension at most $2G_X$ over $\mathbb{F}_\ell$.

*Proof.* Let $g_1, \dots, g_r$ have independent classes in this subspace. Part 1 of the support-bound entry with $q = \ell$ [@openai2026bp, Lemma 4.1, p. 12] gives a curve $C$ of genus at most $G_X$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from $\ell$, and residues $\bar g_j$ in its function field. By (b) the classes of the $\bar g_j$ modulo $\ell$-th powers are independent. By (c), since $s_\ell(g_j) = 0$, each $\bar g_j$ has order divisible by $\ell$ at every point of $C$. The classes with this property form a group isomorphic to $\mathrm{Pic}(C)[\ell]$, of dimension $2g(C)$ [@openai2026bp, Lemma 2.4, p. 5] [@stacks2026, Lemma 53.17.1, Tag 0C1Z]. So $r \le 2G_X$. $\square$

This replaces the manuscript's appeal to Deligne's finiteness theorem for $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(U, \mu_\ell)$ [@openai2026bp, proof of Lemma 6.2, p. 18], which was not opened.

*Lemma C (divisors detected by a pencil)* [@openai2026bp, Lemma 6.2, p. 18]. Suppose that $\varphi$ matches local data, and let $X$ be as in Lemma B. Fix $t \in K \setminus k$, let $E$ be the relative algebraic closure of $k(t)$ in $K$, and put $h_a = \Theta[t - a]$ and $\mathcal{S} = \bigcup_{a \in k} \mathrm{Supp}_X(h_a)$. Then $\mathcal{S}$ is infinite, its members have degree at most $B_t$, and for every $z \in E^\times$, every $j \ge 1$ and every $g \in L^\times$ representing $\Theta[z]$ modulo $\ell^j$, all but finitely many $D \in \mathcal{S}$ satisfy
$$\mathrm{ord}_D(g) = 0, \qquad g|_D \in l(D)^{\times \ell^j}.$$

*Proof.* The degree bound is part 2 of the support-bound entry.

*$\mathcal{S}$ is infinite.* The classes $[t - a]$, $a \in k$, are independent in $k(t)^\times/k(t)^{\times \ell}$, since $\mathrm{ord}_{t = a}(t - b)$ is $1$ for $b = a$ and $0$ otherwise. If $f_1, \dots, f_m \in k(t)^\times$ have independent classes and become $\ell$-th powers in $K$, their $\ell$-th roots lie in $E$. By [Kummer theory](kummer-theory.md) they generate an extension of degree $\ell^m$ of $k(t)$ inside $E$, so $m$ is bounded in terms of $[E : k(t)]$. Hence the $[t - a]$ span an infinite-dimensional subspace of $K^\times/K^{\times\ell} = \widehat K/\ell$, and so do the $h_a$ in $\widehat L/\ell = L^\times/L^{\times \ell}$. If $\mathcal{S}$ were finite, divisor evaluation would map this span into the finite-dimensional space $\bigoplus_{D \in \mathcal{S}} \mathbb{F}_\ell$, and its kernel would contradict Lemma B.

*The tests.* Let $D \in \mathcal{S}$, with divisorial valuation $w_D$. It is a quasi-prime divisor of $L/l$, since its value group is $\mathbb{Z}$, it is trivial on $l$, its residue field $l(D)$ has transcendence degree $d - 1$, and its only proper coarsening is trivial. As $L^\times/U_{w_D} \cong \mathbb{Z}$, $I_{w_D} = \mathbb{Z}_\ell \cdot \mathrm{ord}_D$. By (L1) for $r = 1$ there is a quasi-prime divisor $v$ of $K/k$ with $\varphi(I_{w_D}) = I_v$ and $\varphi(D_{w_D}) = D_v$. As $K^\times/(k^\times U_v) \cong vK/vk \cong \mathbb{Z}$, $I_v = \mathbb{Z}_\ell \cdot \mathrm{ord}_v$, where $\mathrm{ord}_v$ is the composite $K^\times \to vK/vk \cong \mathbb{Z}$, and $\varphi(\mathrm{ord}_D) = c \cdot \mathrm{ord}_v$ with $c \in \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$. Hence, for $y \in K^\times$,
$$\mathrm{ord}_D(\Theta[y]) = c \cdot \mathrm{ord}_v(y).$$
As $D \in \mathrm{Supp}_X(h_a)$ for some $a$, $\mathrm{ord}_v(t - a) \ne 0$, so $vE/vk$ has rational rank $1$. The inequality $\mathrm{trdeg}(Ev/kv) + \mathrm{rr}(vE/vk) \le \mathrm{trdeg}(E/k) = 1$ [@kuhlmann2004valuegroups, Corollary 2.7, (2.4)] makes $Ev$ algebraic over $kv$. Since $k$ is algebraically closed, so is $kv$: a monic polynomial over $kv$ lifts to a monic one over the valuation ring of $k$, whose roots in $k$ are integral and reduce to roots. So $Ev = kv$.

Now let $z \in E^\times$ and $D \in \mathcal{S}$ with $\mathrm{ord}_D(\Theta[z]) = 0$. Then $v(z) \in vk$. Dividing $z$ by a constant of the same value, and then by a constant with the same residue, which exists as $Ev = kv$, gives a principal unit. So $[z]$ vanishes on $D_v$, and $\Theta[z]$ vanishes on $\varphi^{-1}(D_v) = D_{w_D}$. The $D$ with $\mathrm{ord}_D(\Theta[z]) \ne 0$ form the finite set $\mathrm{Supp}_X(\Theta[z])$ [@openai2026bp, Corollary 5.2, p. 17]. Discard these and the finitely many $D$ in the divisor of $g$. For the remaining $D$, $g$ is a $w_D$-unit, $[g] \equiv \Theta[z]$ vanishes on $D_{w_D}$ modulo $\ell^j$, and so the class of the residue $g|_D$ vanishes modulo $\ell^j$ on $D_{w_D}/I_{w_D} = W_{l(D)}$. As evaluation detects classes modulo $\ell^j$, $g|_D \in l(D)^{\times \ell^j}$. $\square$

*Lemma D (one incidence curve for all tests)* [@openai2026bp, Lemma 6.3, p. 19]. Let $l$ be algebraically closed, $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_l$ normal, integral and projective of dimension $d \ge 2$, and $\mathcal{S}$ an infinite set of prime divisors of $X$ of bounded degree. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be any collection of pairs $(g, q)$, with $g \in l(X)^\times$ and $q \ge 1$ prime to $\mathrm{char}\, l$, such that for each pair all but finitely many $D \in \mathcal{S}$ satisfy $\mathrm{ord}_D(g) = 0$ and $g|_D \in l(D)^{\times q}$. Then there are a finite extension $M/l(X)$ and a subfield $P_0 \subseteq M$, finitely generated of transcendence degree $1$ over $l$, such that $M/P_0$ is regular and
$$g \in (M\overline{P_0})^{\times q} \quad \text{for every } (g, q) \in \mathcal{A},$$
the compositum taken in an algebraic closure of $M$. The fields $M$ and $P_0$ do not depend on $\mathcal{A}$.

*Proof.* *A family through $\mathcal{S}$.* Reduced subschemes of $X$ whose components all have dimension $d - 1$ and whose degrees are bounded form a limited family [@grothendieck1961hilbert, Lemme 2.4, p. 255]. So their Hilbert polynomials form a finite set [@grothendieck1961hilbert, Théorème 2.1(b), p. 254]. For each such polynomial $\Phi$, the closed subschemes of $X$ with Hilbert polynomial $\Phi$, in flat families, are the flat quotients of $\mathcal{O}_X$, so they are parametrized by $\mathrm{Quot}^{\Phi}_{\mathcal{O}_X/X/l}$. This is a projective $l$-scheme carrying a universal closed subscheme of $X$ flat over it [@grothendieck1961hilbert, §3, pp. 258–259, and Théorème 3.2, p. 260]. So the members of $\mathcal{S}$ are distinct closed points of finitely many projective $l$-schemes. Their closure has finitely many irreducible components, the points of $\mathcal{S}$ are dense in each, and as $\mathcal{S}$ is infinite one component $T_0$ has positive dimension. Give $T_0$ its reduced structure. The points of $T_0$ whose fibre is geometrically integral form a constructible set, since the geometrically reduced locus [@stacks2026, Lemma 37.26.5, Tag 0579] and the level sets of the number of geometrically irreducible components [@stacks2026, Lemma 37.27.7, Tag 055B] are constructible. This set contains the dense set $\mathcal{S} \cap T_0$, so it contains a dense open $T \subseteq T_0$, in which $\mathcal{S}$ is still dense. Let $Z \subseteq T \times X$ be the universal family over $T$. It is flat over $T$ with geometrically integral fibres.

*The incidence curve.* Fix an integral locally closed curve $T' \subseteq T$, with generic point $\eta'$, and put $Z' = Z \times_T T'$. Over an integral base, flatness makes the functions on an open subset inject into those on its generic fibre, so $Z$ and $Z'$ are integral because their generic fibres are. The projection $Z' \to X$ is dominant. Otherwise its image would lie in a closed subset of dimension at most $d - 1$, which would contain the infinitely many distinct prime divisors $Z_t$, $t \in T'$ closed. As $\dim Z' = d$, $M = l(Z')$ is finite over $l(X)$. Put $P_0 = l(T') \subseteq M$. Then $M/P_0$ is regular, because the generic fibre $Z'_{\eta'}$ is geometrically integral with function field $M$.

*One test.* Fix $(g, q) \in \mathcal{A}$. Let $U \subseteq X$ be the open set where $g$ is a unit, let $Y \subseteq Z$ be the open set where $Z \to T$ is smooth and which maps into $U$, and let $C_g = \mathrm{Spec}_Y\, \mathcal{O}_Y[u]/(u^q - g)$, a finite étale cover of degree $q$. The fibre $Y_{\eta'}$ is not empty. Indeed, $Z'_{\eta'}$ is geometrically integral, hence smooth on a dense open, and these points lie in the smooth locus of the flat $Z \to T$. The preimage of $U$ also meets $Z'_{\eta'}$, because $Z' \to X$ is dominant and $Z'_{\eta'}$ is dense in $Z'$. Likewise $Y_\eta$ is not empty for the generic point $\eta$ of $T$. As $Y \to T$ is smooth, hence open, its image is a dense open of $T$.

Let $t$ be a closed point of $T$ in this image whose divisor $Z_t \in \mathcal{S}$ passes the test. A $q$-th root of $g|_{Z_t}$ in $l(Z_t)$ is integral over the functions on the smooth, hence normal, $Y_t$, so it is regular there. So $C_g$ splits over $Y_t$ into $q$ copies of the integral $Y_t$, and its fibre over $t$ has $q$ geometrically irreducible components. Such $t$ are dense in $T$: only finitely many members of $\mathcal{S}$ fail the test, and removing finitely many points from a dense set of an irreducible variety of positive dimension leaves a dense set. The number of geometrically irreducible components of the fibres of $C_g \to T$ is constant on a nonempty open of $T$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 37.27.6, Tag 055A], so it is $q$ at $\eta$. As $Y_\eta$ is smooth and geometrically integral, $C_g$ becomes, over an algebraic closure of $l(T)$, a finite étale cover of degree $q$ of a normal integral scheme, with $q$ irreducible components. Each component is finite étale of positive degree over the connected base, so each has degree $1$ and is a section. Hence $g = r^q$ for some $r$ in $l(Z) \otimes_{l(T)} F$, for a finite extension $F$ of $l(T)$.

*Moving to $T'$.* Let $\widetilde T \to T$ be the normalization of $T$ in $F$, which is finite and surjective, and put $\widetilde Y = Y \times_T \widetilde T$. It is smooth over the normal $\widetilde T$, hence normal [@stacks2026, Lemma 10.163.9, Tag 033C]. It is flat over the integral $\widetilde T$, with integral generic fibre $Y_\eta \otimes_{l(T)} F$, so it is integral with function field $l(Z) \otimes_{l(T)} F$. As $r^q = g$ is a unit on $\widetilde Y$, $r$ is integral, hence a regular unit on $\widetilde Y$. Choose $\tilde\eta' \in \widetilde T$ over $\eta'$. The fibre $\widetilde Y_{\tilde\eta'} = Y_{\eta'} \otimes_{P_0} \kappa(\tilde\eta')$ is nonempty and integral, with function field $M \otimes_{P_0} \kappa(\tilde\eta') \subseteq M\overline{P_0}$. Restricting $r$ to it gives $g \in (M\overline{P_0})^{\times q}$. The auxiliary $F$ and $\widetilde T$ depend on $(g, q)$, but $T'$, $M$ and $P_0$ were fixed before. $\square$

*Lemma E (Kummer descent)* [@openai2026bp, Lemma 6.4, p. 20]. Let $M/P$ be a regular extension, $q$ prime to the characteristic, and $\mu_q \subseteq P$. If $g \in M^\times$ is a $q$-th power in $M\overline P$, then its class in $M^\times/M^{\times q}$ is the image of a unique class in $P^\times/P^{\times q}$.

*Proof.* A $q$-th root $r$ of $g$ is separable over $M$, and $M\overline P$ is purely inseparable over $MP^{\mathrm{sep}}$, so $r \in MP'$ for a finite Galois extension $P'/P$. By regularity, $M$ and $P'$ are linearly disjoint over $P$, and restriction identifies $\mathrm{Gal}(MP'/M)$ with $G = \mathrm{Gal}(P'/P)$. As $\mu_q \subseteq P$, $\chi(\sigma) = \sigma(r)/r$ is a homomorphism $G \to \mu_q$. By [Hilbert 90](hilbert-theorem-90.md), $\chi(\sigma) = \sigma(b)/b$ for some $b \in P'^\times$. Then $r/b$ is fixed by $G$, so it lies in $M$, and $\sigma(b^q) = b^q$, so $b^q \in P$. Thus $g = b^q (r/b)^q$. Uniqueness holds because a $q$-th root in $M$ of an element of $P$ is algebraic over $P$, hence in $P$. $\square$

*Lemma F (two curve subfields)* [@openai2026bp, Lemma 6.5, p. 20]. Let $N/\kappa$ be a function field and $P \ne Q$ curve subfields. Then $\widehat P \cap \widehat Q \subseteq \widehat N$ embeds in $T_\ell \mathrm{Pic}(C_P) \cong \mathbb{Z}_\ell^{2g(C_P)}$. In particular it does not have infinite rank.

*Proof.* $PQ$ has transcendence degree $2$, since otherwise $Q$ would be algebraic over $P$ and so $Q \subseteq P$, and symmetrically. As $\kappa$ is algebraically closed, $P \otimes_\kappa Q$ is a domain of transcendence degree $2$, so it injects into $PQ$, and $PQ$ is the function field of $C_P \times C_Q$. For a point $x \in C_P(\kappa)$, the prime divisor $\{x\} \times C_Q$ gives a valuation of $PQ$ that is $\mathrm{ord}_x$ on $P^\times$ and $0$ on $Q^\times$. Extend it by a Gauss valuation over a transcendence basis of $N/PQ$, and prolong it to the finite extension $N$. The normalized valuation $w : N^\times \to \mathbb{Z}$ is trivial on $\kappa$, so it extends continuously to $\widehat N \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell$. It restricts to $e \cdot \mathrm{ord}_x$ on $\widehat P$, for some integer $e \ge 1$, and to $0$ on $\widehat Q$. So every $h \in \widehat P \cap \widehat Q$ has $e \cdot \mathrm{ord}_x(h) = 0$, hence $\mathrm{ord}_x(h) = 0$, for every $x$. The elements of $\widehat P$ with all point orders zero form $T_\ell \mathrm{Pic}(C_P)$, of rank $2g(C_P)$ [@openai2026bp, Lemma 2.4, p. 5] [@stacks2026, Lemma 53.17.1, Tag 0C1Z]. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** This is the manuscript's proof [@openai2026bp, proof of Theorem 6.1, p. 21], checked here. Fix a normal integral projective model $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_l$ of $L/l$.

*Step 1: containment over a finite extension.* Let $E$ be a curve subfield of $K/k$ and $t \in E \setminus k$, so that $E$ is the relative algebraic closure of $k(t)$ in $K$. For each $z \in E^\times$ and $j \ge 1$ choose one $g_{z,j} \in L^\times$ representing $\Theta[z]$ modulo $\ell^j$. By Lemma C, the set $\mathcal{S}$ and the pairs $(g_{z,j}, \ell^j)$ satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma D, which gives $M \supseteq L$ and $P_0 \subseteq M$. Put $H = \Theta(\widehat E) \subseteq \widehat L$. Let $h \in H$ and $j \ge 1$. As $\widehat E/\ell^j = E^\times/E^{\times \ell^j}$, $h \equiv \Theta[z] \bmod \ell^j$ for some $z \in E^\times$, so the image of $h$ in $M^\times/M^{\times \ell^j}$ is the class of $g_{z,j}$. By Lemmas D and E, using $\mu_{\ell^j} \subseteq l \subseteq P_0$, this is the image of a unique $p_j \in P_0^\times/P_0^{\times \ell^j}$. Uniqueness makes the $p_j$ compatible, so $(p_j) \in \widehat{P_0}$, and it maps to the image of $h$ in $\widehat M$. So the image of $H$ in $\widehat M$ lies in $\widehat{P_0}$.

*Step 2: a Galois-stable curve subfield.* Choose a finite normal extension $N/L$ containing $M$, possibly inseparable, and let $P_N$ be the relative algebraic closure of $P_0$ in $N$, a curve subfield of $N/l$. By Lemma A, $\widehat L \subseteq \widehat M \subseteq \widehat N$, and $H \subseteq \widehat{P_0} \subseteq \widehat{P_N}$ there. $H$ has infinite rank. Indeed, by the proof of Lemma C, $\widehat E/\ell = E^\times/E^{\times \ell}$ is infinite-dimensional. Lifts of independent classes modulo $\ell$ are $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-independent in the torsion-free $\widehat E$: from a nonzero relation, divide by the largest power of $\ell$ dividing all coefficients and reduce modulo $\ell$. And $\Theta$ and $\widehat L \to \widehat N$ are injective. Every $\sigma \in G = \mathrm{Aut}_L(N)$ acts on $\widehat N$ fixing $\widehat L$, so $H \subseteq \widehat{P_N} \cap \widehat{\sigma P_N}$. By Lemma F, $\sigma P_N = P_N$.

*Step 3: descent to $L$.* Put $P_L = P_N \cap L$. The fixed field $P_N^G$ has transcendence degree $1$, as $P_N$ is algebraic over it, and it lies in $N^G$. If $\mathrm{char}\, l = 0$ then $N^G = L$. If $\mathrm{char}\, l = p > 0$ then $N^G/L$ is finite and purely inseparable, so some $p^a$-th power maps $P_N^G$ into $P_L$. In both cases $\mathrm{trdeg}(P_L/l) = 1$. An element of $L$ algebraic over $P_L$ is algebraic over $P_N$, so lies in $P_N \cap L$. Hence $P_L$ is a curve subfield of $L/l$. For $x \in N^\times$, $N_{N/L}(x) = (\prod_{\sigma \in G} \sigma x)^{s}$, where $s$ is the inseparable degree of $N/L$. So the norm maps $P_N^\times$ into $P_N \cap L = P_L$, and $\widehat{P_N}$ into $\widehat{P_L}$. By Lemma A(2), $[N : L] H \subseteq \widehat{P_L}$. Write $[N : L] = \ell^m u$ with $u$ prime to $\ell$, a unit of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. Then $\ell^m H \subseteq \widehat{P_L}$, and by saturation, Lemma A(1), $H \subseteq \widehat{P_L}$.

*Step 4: equality.* Steps 1–3 apply to $\varphi^{-1}$, which matches local data, with a normal projective model of $K/k$, and to the curve subfield $P_L$. They give a curve subfield $E'$ of $K/k$ with $\Theta^{-1}(\widehat{P_L}) \subseteq \widehat{E'}$. So $\widehat E \subseteq \Theta^{-1}(\widehat{P_L}) \subseteq \widehat{E'}$. As $\widehat E$ has infinite rank, Lemma F gives $E' = E$. So both inclusions are equalities and $\Theta(\widehat E) = \widehat{P_L}$.

*Step 5: uniqueness and bijectivity.* If $\widehat P = \widehat{P'}$ for curve subfields $P, P'$ of $L/l$, then $\widehat P \cap \widehat{P'}$ has infinite rank, so $P = P'$ by Lemma F. So $P$ is unique, and $E \mapsto P$ is injective by the same argument in $K$. Step 4 for $\varphi^{-1}$ shows that every curve subfield of $L/l$ is $\Theta(\widehat E)$ for some $E$. $\square$

**What was checked here.** Every step above was rebuilt from the manuscript, and no gap was found. In particular:

- *Deligne's theorem.* The manuscript bounds the divisor-free classes by Deligne's finiteness theorem. Lemma B bounds them instead by the finite residual tests, which are checked in the support-bound entry.
