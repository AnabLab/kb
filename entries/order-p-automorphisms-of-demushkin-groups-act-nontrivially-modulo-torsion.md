**Setting.** Let $p$ be an odd prime. For a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) $U$ of rank $n(U) \ge 2$, let $V_U$ be $U^{\mathrm{ab}}$ modulo its torsion subgroup, and let $K_U$ be the kernel of $U \to V_U$. Put $\delta(U) = 1$ if $q(U) = 0$ and $\delta(U) = 0$ otherwise. Since $U^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^{n(U)-1} \times \mathbb{Z}_p/q(U)\mathbb{Z}_p$ ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md)), $V_U$ is free over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ of rank $n(U) - 1 + \delta(U)$, and $K_U$ is a closed normal subgroup containing the closed commutator subgroup, equal to it when $q(U) = 0$. Every continuous homomorphism $U \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ vanishes on $K_U$, so $\mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Z}_p) = \mathrm{Hom}(V_U, \mathbb{Z}_p)$. An automorphism of $U$ acts trivially on $V_U$ if and only if it acts trivially on $\mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Z}_p)$, as $V_U$ is free of finite rank.

Every open subgroup $U'$ of $U$ is a Demushkin group with $n(U') - 2 = (U : U')(n(U) - 2)$ ([Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md), Duality). If $U'$ is normal in $U$, then $\delta(U') = \delta(U)$, by part 2 of [the torsion-free extension theorem](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md) applied to $E = U$, which has no element of order $p$ ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 1). That part 2 is used repeatedly below, and says: if a pro-$p$ group $E$ without elements of order $p$ contains a Demushkin group $D'$ of rank at least $2$ as an open normal subgroup, then for $P = E/D'$ acting by conjugation, $\mathrm{Hom}(D', \mathbb{Z}_p)^P$ has rank $n(E) - 1 + \delta(E) = 1 + \delta(D') + (n(D') - 2)/|P|$, with $\delta(E) = \delta(D')$ because $p$ is odd.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p$ be odd and $D$ a Demushkin group of rank $n \ge 3$, with any $q$. If an automorphism $\sigma$ of $D$ satisfies $\sigma^p = 1$ and acts trivially on $V_D$, then $\sigma = 1$.

**Corollary 1.** This is proved here. Let $p$ be odd and $D$ a Demushkin group of rank $n \ge 3$.

1. No automorphism of $D$ of finite order $p^a > 1$ acts trivially on $V_D$.
2. No element of order $p$ of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ acts trivially on $V_D$. So the kernel of $\mathrm{Out}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V_D)$ has no nontrivial element of finite $p$-power order.

*Proof.* (1) Otherwise $\sigma^{p^{a-1}}$ would contradict the theorem. (2) Inner automorphisms act trivially on $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$, so $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ acts on $V_D$. If $\theta \in \mathrm{Out}(D)$ has order $p$ and acts trivially on $V_D$, it is the class of some $\sigma \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ with $\sigma^p = 1$ ([torsion-free extensions](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), Corollary 1). Then $\sigma$ acts on $V_D$ as $\theta$ does, trivially, so $\sigma = 1$ by the theorem and $\theta = 1$, a contradiction. An element of order $p^a > 1$ has a power of order $p$. $\square$

**Corollary 2 (Tsujimura's question).** This is proved here. Let $p$ be odd and $D$ a Demushkin group of rank $n \ge 3$ with $q \ne 0$, such as $G_K(p)$ for a finite extension $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ containing $\zeta_p$ ([outer automorphisms with finitely many conjugates](outer-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-with-finitely-many-conjugates.md), Corollary 2). It has trivial centre ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 4), so $D$ is identified with $\mathrm{Inn}(D)$ as in [the problem](demushkin-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md). Then case (a) of [the reduction](demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three-are-elastic.md), Corollary 2, does not occur. So every closed normal subgroup $N \ne D$ of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ isomorphic to $D$ meets $D$ in a subgroup of infinite index in both $D$ and $N$ that is not topologically finitely generated.

*Proof.* In case (a), $ND$ contains an automorphism of $D$ of order $p$ acting trivially on $V_D$ ([torsion-free extensions](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), Corollary 2). The theorem excludes this, so only case (b) of the reduction remains. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** The proof has four lemmas. Lemma 2 is the main step: a count in the style of Riemann–Hurwitz, in which the only input about groups with torsion is the torsion-free extension theorem applied to their torsion-free open subgroups.

*Lemma 1.* Let $B$ be a Demushkin group of rank at least $2$, let $\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(B)$ act trivially on $V_B$, and let $b \in B$ satisfy $b\,\tau(b)\,\tau^2(b) \cdots \tau^{p-1}(b) = 1$. Then $b \in K_B$.

*Proof.* The image of the product in $V_B$ is $p$ times the image of $b$, as $\tau$ acts trivially on $V_B$. Since $V_B$ is torsion-free, the image of $b$ is $0$. $\square$

*Lemma 2.* Let $B$ be a Demushkin group of rank at least $2$, and let $\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(B)$ satisfy $\tau^p = 1$ and act trivially on $V_B$. Let $U$ be an open subgroup of $B$ containing $K_B$. Then $\tau(U) = U$, and $\tau$ acts trivially on $V_U$.

*Proof.* Put $\delta = \delta(B)$. Let $E = B \rtimes \langle t \rangle$, where $t$ has order $p$ and $tbt^{-1} = \tau(b)$. It is a pro-$p$ group containing $B$ as an open normal subgroup of index $p$.

(a) *The finite quotient.* Since $U \supseteq K_B$ and $B/K_B = V_B$ is abelian, $U$ is normal in $B$ and $B/U$ is abelian. For $b \in B$ we have $\tau(b)b^{-1} \in K_B \subseteq U$, as $\tau$ acts trivially on $V_B$. Hence $\tau(U) \subseteq U$, with equality because $\tau^{p-1}$ maps $U$ into $U$ as well, and $tUt^{-1} = U$. So $U$ is normal in $E$. In $G = E/U$ the image $\bar t$ of $t$ commutes with $B/U$, since $tbt^{-1}b^{-1} = \tau(b)b^{-1} \in U$. As $t \notin B$ and $t^p = 1$, $G$ is the direct product of $B/U$ and $\langle \bar t \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$. Its order $|G| = p\,(B : U)$ is a power of $p$. Every open subgroup of $B$ has rank at least $2$ by the index formula, so $U$ is a Demushkin group of rank at least $2$ with $\delta(U) = \delta$.

(b) *Elements of order $p$.* Let $g \in E$ have order $p$. As $B$ is torsion-free ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 1), $g = bt^j$ with $b \in B$ and $1 \le j \le p - 1$. Then $1 = g^p = b\,\tau^j(b)\,\tau^{2j}(b) \cdots \tau^{(p-1)j}(b)$. Lemma 1 for $\tau^j$ gives $b \in K_B \subseteq U$. So the image of $g$ in $G$ lies in $T = \{\bar t^j : 1 \le j \le p - 1\}$.

(c) *Torsion-free subgroups.* Let $M = \mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Z}_p) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p = \mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Q}_p)$, of dimension $n(U) - 1 + \delta$. Conjugation in $E$ makes $G$ act on it. Let $C \subseteq G$ be a cyclic subgroup with $C \cap T = \emptyset$, and let $W_C$ be its preimage in $E$. It is a closed subgroup containing $U$ as an open normal subgroup with $W_C/U = C$, and by (b) it has no element of order $p$. Part 2 of the torsion-free extension theorem, applied to $W_C$ and $U$, gives
$$\dim M^C = 1 + \delta + \frac{n(U) - 2}{|C|},$$
since invariants of a finite group commute with $\otimes\, \mathbb{Q}_p$. This includes $C = 1$.

(d) *The invariants of $G$.* Part 2 applied to $B$, which has no element of order $p$, and its open normal subgroup $U$ shows that $\mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Z}_p)^{B/U}$ has rank $n(B) - 1 + \delta$, the rank of $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Z}_p)$. Restriction $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Z}_p) \to \mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Z}_p)^{B/U}$ is injective, since a homomorphism vanishing on $U$ factors through a finite group, and it commutes with $\tau$, since $\tau(U) = U$. So it becomes a $\tau$-equivariant isomorphism $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Q}_p) \to M^{B/U}$ after $\otimes\, \mathbb{Q}_p$. As $\tau$ acts trivially on $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{Z}_p)$, $\bar t$ acts trivially on $M^{B/U}$, and $\dim M^G = \dim M^{B/U} = n(B) - 1 + \delta$.

(e) *Characters.* Let $\chi$ be the character of the representation $M$ of the finite group $G$. Two standard facts are used.

- (F1) If $g \in G$ and $k$ is prime to $p$, then $\chi(g^k) = \chi(g)$. Indeed, let $g$ have order $p^a$. As $x^{p^a} - 1$ is the product of the pairwise coprime cyclotomic polynomials $\Phi_{p^b}$, $0 \le b \le a$, $M$ is the direct sum of the subspaces $M_b = \ker \Phi_{p^b}(g)$. Each $\Phi_{p^b}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, by Eisenstein's criterion for $\Phi_{p^b}(x + 1)$, so $M_b$ is a vector space over the field $L_b = \mathbb{Q}_p[x]/(\Phi_{p^b})$ on which $g$ acts as the class $\zeta$ of $x$. Then $\chi(g) = \sum_b \dim_{L_b}(M_b)\, \mathrm{Tr}_{L_b/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\zeta)$, and $g^k$ acts on $M_b$ as $\zeta^k$, a root of $\Phi_{p^b}$ and hence a conjugate of $\zeta$ with the same trace.
- (F2) For every subgroup $C \subseteq G$, $\dim M^C = |C|^{-1} \sum_{c \in C} \chi(c)$.

Define $\psi(g) = \chi(g) - (1 + \delta) - \varepsilon(g)$, where $\varepsilon(1) = n(U) - 2$ and $\varepsilon(g) = 0$ for $g \ne 1$. By (F2), for every subgroup $C \subseteq G$,
$$\frac{1}{|C|} \sum_{c \in C} \psi(c) = \dim M^C - (1 + \delta) - \frac{n(U) - 2}{|C|}. \qquad (\ast)$$

*Claim: $\psi(g) = 0$ for every $g \notin T$.* We induct on the order of $g$. For $g = 1$, $\psi(1) = (n(U) - 1 + \delta) - (1 + \delta) - (n(U) - 2) = 0$. Let $g \ne 1$, $g \notin T$, and $C = \langle g \rangle$. Then $C \cap T = \emptyset$: write $g = (a, \bar t^i)$ with $a \in B/U$; if $g^k = (a^k, \bar t^{ik}) = \bar t^j$ with $j \ne 0$, then $k$ is prime to $p$ and $a^k = 1$, so $a = 1$ as $B/U$ is a $p$-group, and $g = \bar t^i \in T$. By (c) and $(\ast)$, $\sum_{c \in C} \psi(c) = 0$. The elements of $C$ that do not generate $C$ form the subgroup $\langle g^p \rangle$; they have smaller order and are not in $T$, so $\psi$ vanishes on them by induction. The generators are the $g^k$ with $k$ prime to $p$, and $\psi(g^k) = \psi(g)$ by (F1), as $\varepsilon(g^k) = 0 = \varepsilon(g)$. So $\psi(g) = 0$.

*Conclusion.* By the claim and (F1), $\sum_{g \in G} \psi(g) = \sum_{j=1}^{p-1} \psi(\bar t^j) = (p - 1)\psi(\bar t)$. By $(\ast)$ for $C = G$ and by (d),
$$\frac{(p - 1)\psi(\bar t)}{|G|} = (n(B) - 1 + \delta) - (1 + \delta) - \frac{n(U) - 2}{|G|} = (n(B) - 2)\Big(1 - \frac{1}{p}\Big),$$
using $n(U) - 2 = (B : U)(n(B) - 2)$ and $|G| = p\,(B : U)$. Hence $\psi(\bar t) = (B : U)(n(B) - 2) = n(U) - 2$, that is, $\chi(\bar t) = n(U) - 1 + \delta = \dim M$.

Finally, $M = M^{\bar t} \oplus M_1$ with $M_1 = \ker \Phi_p(\bar t)$, a vector space of some dimension $m$ over the field $\mathbb{Q}_p[x]/(\Phi_p)$, on which the trace of the class of $x$ is $-1$. So $\chi(\bar t) = \dim M^{\bar t} - m$, while $\dim M = \dim M^{\bar t} + (p - 1)m$. Equality forces $m = 0$. So $\tau$ acts trivially on $M$, hence on $\mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Z}_p) \subseteq M$, and hence on $V_U$. $\square$

*Lemma 3.* Let $B$ be a Demushkin group of rank at least $3$. There is an open normal subgroup $M \subseteq B$ of index $p$ with $K_B \subseteq M$ such that every continuous homomorphism $B \to \mathbb{Z}/p$ vanishes on $K_M$.

*Proof.* If $q(B) = 0$, take any open normal subgroup $M$ of index $p$. Then $K_B$ and $K_M$ are the closed commutator subgroups, $K_B \subseteq M$ because $B/M$ is abelian, and $K_M \subseteq K_B$, on which every homomorphism to $\mathbb{Z}/p$ vanishes.

Let $q = q(B) \ne 0$. As $p$ is odd, $q \ne 2$, so $n = n(B)$ is even, hence at least $4$, and $B = F/(r)$ for the free pro-$p$ group $F$ on $x_1, \dots, x_n$ and
$$r = x_1^q(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{n-1}, x_n), \qquad (x, y) = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$$
([classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), normal form 1). We write $x_j$ also for its image in $B$. Abelianizing $r$ gives $B^{\mathrm{ab}} = \mathbb{Z}_p^n/q\mathbb{Z}_p e_1$, so the torsion subgroup of $B^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is generated by the class of $x_1$, which has order $q$, and $B^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the direct sum of this cyclic group and the free module on the classes of $x_2, \dots, x_n$.

Let $A = \mathbb{Z}_p[c]/(c^p - 1)$, and let $\Gamma = A \rtimes \langle c \rangle$ with $(a, c^i)(b, c^j) = (a + c^ib, c^{i+j})$, a pro-$p$ group containing $A \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^p$ as an open normal subgroup of index $p$. Put $a_1 = (1 - p) + c + c^2 + \cdots + c^{p-1}$. Its coefficient sum is $0$, so $a_1 = (1 - c^{-1})w$ for some $w \in A$, since $\sum_i b_ic^i = \sum_i b_i(c^i - 1)$ when $\sum_i b_i = 0$, and $c^i - 1 = c^i(1 - c^{-1})(1 + c^{-1} + \cdots + c^{-(i-1)})$. Let $\rho : F \to \Gamma$ be the continuous homomorphism with $\rho(x_1) = (a_1, 1)$, $\rho(x_3) = (0, c)$, $\rho(x_4) = (-qw, 1)$ and $\rho(x_j) = (0, 1)$ for the other $j$. A direct computation gives $\rho((x_3, x_4)) = ((1 - c^{-1})(-qw), 1) = (-qa_1, 1)$. Also $\rho(x_1^q) = (qa_1, 1)$, $\rho((x_1, x_2)) = (0, 1)$ because $\rho(x_1)$ and $\rho(x_2)$ lie in the abelian subgroup $A \times \{1\}$, and the remaining commutators map to $(0, 1)$. So $\rho(r) = (0, 1)$, and $\rho$ induces $\bar\rho : B \to \Gamma$.

Let $M$ be the kernel of the composite $B \to \Gamma \to \langle c \rangle$. This composite sends $x_3$ to $c$ and the other $x_j$ to $1$, so $M$ is open and normal of index $p$. It factors through $B^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and kills the class of $x_1$, so $K_B \subseteq M$. Now $\bar\rho(M) \subseteq A \times \{1\}$, so $\Theta = \varepsilon_0 \circ \bar\rho|_M : M \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ is a continuous homomorphism, where $\varepsilon_0 : A \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ takes the coefficient of $c^0$.

Let $\theta : B \to \mathbb{Z}/p$ be the homomorphism with $\theta(x_1) = 1$ and $\theta(x_j) = 0$ for $j \ge 2$, which exists because $\theta(r) = q \equiv 0$. We show that $\Theta \bmod p$ equals $\theta|_M$ by checking them on the set $S$ of elements $x_3^ix_jx_3^{-i}$ with $0 \le i \le p - 1$ and $j \ne 3$, together with $x_3^p$. This set generates $M$ topologically: the closed subgroup $H$ it generates lies in $M$, contains every $x_j$ with $j \ne 3$, and is normalized by $x_3$, because $x_3 \cdot x_3^{p-1}x_jx_3^{-(p-1)} \cdot x_3^{-1} = x_3^p \cdot x_j \cdot x_3^{-p}$ with $x_3^p \in H$. It is also normalized by $x_3^{-1} = x_3^{p-1} \cdot x_3^{-p}$. So the normalizer of $H$, a closed subgroup, is $B$. Then $B/H$ is generated by the class of $x_3$, whose $p$-th power is trivial, so $(B : H) \le p$ and $H = M$. On $S$: $\bar\rho(x_3^ix_jx_3^{-i}) = (c^ia, 1)$ when $\bar\rho(x_j) = (a, 1)$. For $j = 1$, $\varepsilon_0(c^ia_1)$ is the coefficient of $c^{-i}$ in $a_1$, which is $1 - p$ or $1$, so it is $\equiv 1 = \theta(x_1)$. For $j = 4$, $\varepsilon_0(-qc^iw) \in q\mathbb{Z}_p \subseteq p\mathbb{Z}_p$, matching $\theta(x_4) = 0$. For the other $j \ne 3$ both sides are $0$, and $\bar\rho(x_3^p) = (0, c^p) = (0, 1)$, matching $\theta(x_3^p) = 0$.

So $\theta|_M$ is the reduction of a homomorphism $M \to \mathbb{Z}_p$, and vanishes on $K_M$. Let $f : B \to \mathbb{Z}/p$ be any continuous homomorphism. Then $\beta = f - f(x_1)\theta$ vanishes on $x_1$. Choose $b_j \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ lifting $\beta(x_j)$ for $j \ge 2$. By the description of $B^{\mathrm{ab}}$, there is a homomorphism $B \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ sending $x_1$ to $0$ and $x_j$ to $b_j$, whose reduction is $\beta$. Its restriction to $M$ vanishes on $K_M$, so $\beta$ does too, and hence so does $f$. $\square$

*Lemma 4.* For a Demushkin group $B$ of rank at least $3$, call a subgroup *reachable from* $B$ if it is the last term of a finite chain $B = U_0 \supseteq U_1 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq U_k$ in which each $U_{i+1}$ is an open subgroup of $U_i$ containing $K_{U_i}$. Every open subgroup of $B$ contains a subgroup reachable from $B$.

Each $U_i$ is an open subgroup of $B$, hence a Demushkin group of rank at least $3$ by the index formula, and a subgroup reachable from a subgroup reachable from $B$ is reachable from $B$.

*Proof.* Every open subgroup contains an open normal subgroup, so let $W$ be open and normal in $B$. We induct on $(B : W)$, for all such $B$ at once. If $W = B$, take the chain of length $0$. Otherwise let $M$ be as in Lemma 3. It is reachable from $B$, by the chain $B \supseteq M$. If $W \subseteq M$, then $W$ is open and normal in $M$ with $(M : W) < (B : W)$, and the induction hypothesis for $M$ gives a subgroup reachable from $M$, hence from $B$, inside $W$. If $W \not\subseteq M$, then $MW = B$, as $M$ has prime index. The finite $p$-group $B/W$ is nontrivial, so there is a nonzero continuous homomorphism $f : B \to \mathbb{Z}/p$ vanishing on $W$. Put $M'' = M \cap \ker f$. Since $f(M) = f(MW) = f(B) \ne 0$, $M''$ is open of index $p$ in $M$, and $K_M \subseteq M''$ by Lemma 3. So $M''$ is reachable from $B$. The subgroup $W \cap M = W \cap M''$ is open and normal in $M''$, of index $(M : W \cap M)/p = (B : W)/p$, since $M/(W \cap M) \cong MW/W = B/W$. The induction hypothesis for $M''$ gives a subgroup reachable from $M''$, hence from $B$, inside $W \cap M \subseteq W$. $\square$

*Proof of the theorem.* We show that every subgroup $U$ reachable from $D$ satisfies:

- (i) $\sigma(U) = U$;
- (ii) $\sigma$ acts trivially on $V_U$;
- (iii) $x\sigma(x)^{-1} \in K_U$ for every $x \in D$.

For $U = D$, (i) is clear, (ii) is the hypothesis, and (iii) holds because $\sigma$ acts trivially on $V_D = D/K_D$. Let $U$ be the last term of a chain whose previous term $U_-$ satisfies (i)–(iii). Lemma 2, applied to $B = U_-$ and $\tau = \sigma|_{U_-}$, gives (i) and (ii) for $U$. For (iii), let $x \in D$ and $d = x\sigma(x)^{-1}$. By (iii) for $U_-$, $d \in K_{U_-} \subseteq U$. The product $d\,\sigma(d) \cdots \sigma^{p-1}(d)$ telescopes to $x\sigma^p(x)^{-1} = 1$. Lemma 1, applied to $B = U$ and $\tau = \sigma|_U$, gives $d \in K_U$.

Now let $x \in D$ and let $W$ be an open normal subgroup of $D$. By Lemma 4 some subgroup $U \subseteq W$ is reachable from $D$, so $x\sigma(x)^{-1} \in K_U \subseteq W$ by (iii). The open normal subgroups of $D$ intersect in $1$, so $\sigma(x) = x$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Analogy.* For a closed orientable surface of genus at least $2$, a periodic map of order $p$ acting trivially on $H_1$ would have negative Lefschetz number, which is impossible, as its fixed points are isolated with index $1$. Lemma 2 plays the role of this argument. In the geometric case, the value of $-\psi$ at an element of order $p$ is its number of fixed points on the corresponding cover, and $(\ast)$ for $C = G$ is the Riemann–Hurwitz formula. This is offered only as motivation.
- *The case $q = 0$.* For the pro-$p$ surface groups of genus at least $2$, the theorem and Corollary 1 also follow, for every $p$, from [the lower central series](lower-central-series-of-pro-p-surface-groups.md): by its part 4(a) and 4(d), the kernel $K_1$ of the action on $V_D = D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ has torsion-free quotients $K_i/K_{i+1}$ and $\bigcap_i K_i = 1$, so it is torsion-free, and by 4(c) and 4(d) the same holds for $DK_1/D$ in $\mathrm{Out}(D)$. For $q \ne 0$ no such filtration is available in the knowledgebase.
- *The prime $2$.* The proof uses that $p$ is odd in part 2 of the torsion-free extension theorem, where $\delta(W_C) = \delta(U)$, and in Lemma 3, through the normal form for $q \ne 2$. The case $p = 2$ is not treated.
- *Tsujimura's question.* After Corollary 2, for odd $p$, $q \ne 0$ and rank at least $3$, a closed normal copy $N \ne D$ of $D$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ could only meet $D$ in a closed normal subgroup of infinite index in both that is not topologically finitely generated. Whether this happens is open.
- *Sources.* The statements and proofs are not taken from a source. No novelty is claimed; searches in November 2026 for finite $p$-groups acting on Demushkin groups and for torsion in pro-$p$ Torelli groups found nothing relevant.
