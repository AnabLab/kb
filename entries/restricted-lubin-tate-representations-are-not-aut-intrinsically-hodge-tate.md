**Setting.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $d$ inside $\bar k$, and suppose that $p$ is odd or $\sqrt{-1} \in k$. Let $E \subseteq k$ be a subfield that is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, of degree $e$. Fix a uniformizer $\pi_E$ of $E$, let $\chi_E : G_E^{\mathrm{ab}} \to \mathcal{O}_E^\times$ be the inverse of the reciprocity isomorphism $\mathrm{rec}_E : (E^\times)^\wedge \to G_E^{\mathrm{ab}}$ followed by the projection to $\mathcal{O}_E^\times$ that kills $\pi_E$, and let
$$\rho_E : G_E \to G_E^{\mathrm{ab}} \xrightarrow{\chi_E} \mathcal{O}_E^\times \subset \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(E_+)$$
be the Lubin–Tate representation of $E$ [@kondo2026, Definition 2.3, p. 7]. Since $G_k = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/k)$ is an open subgroup of $G_E = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/E)$, we can restrict: put $\rho = \rho_E|_{G_k}$, a representation of $G_k$ of dimension $e$. A representation of $G_k$ is *Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate* if its composite with every continuous automorphism of $G_k$ is Hodge–Tate [@kondo2026, Introduction, Definition, p. 2]; see [the overview](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md). Let $E_{\mathrm{out}} \subseteq \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ be the set of classes $\alpha$ such that $\rho \circ \tilde\alpha$ is Hodge–Tate for a representative $\tilde\alpha$; inner automorphisms change $\rho$ only up to isomorphism, so this does not depend on $\tilde\alpha$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *The representation.* $\rho$ is Hodge–Tate and irreducible.
2. *A criterion.* For $e' \in k$ let $f_{e'} \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(k, \mathbb{Q}_p)$ be $x \mapsto \mathrm{Tr}_{k/\mathbb{Q}_p}(e'x)$, and let $E^* = \{f_{e'} : e' \in E\}$, a subspace of dimension $e$. If $\rho \circ \theta$ is Hodge–Tate for $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$, then $f \circ \theta_+ \in E^*$ for every $f \in E^*$, where $\theta_+$ is the automorphism of $k_+$ that $\theta$ induces. If moreover $E$ is quadratic, then $\theta_+$ maps the hyperplane
$$H_E = \{x \in k : \mathrm{Tr}_{k/E}(x) \in \mathbb{Q}_p\}$$
onto itself.
3. *Failure for almost every outer automorphism.* If $E \ne \mathbb{Q}_p$ and $E \ne k$, then $E_{\mathrm{out}}$ is contained in a closed subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ of Haar measure zero. In particular, $\rho$ is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate.

**Corollary (dimension two).** Suppose that $k$ contains a quadratic extension $E$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and that $p$ is odd or $\sqrt{-1} \in k$. Then $G_k$ has an irreducible abelian Hodge–Tate representation of dimension $2$ that stays Hodge–Tate only for outer automorphisms in a closed subgroup of Haar measure zero, and so is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate. For $E \ne k$ this is part 3 with $\rho = \rho_E|_{G_k}$. For $E = k$ it is Kondo's theorem for $\rho_k$ [@kondo2026, Theorem 3.5, p. 10]. The hypothesis holds in particular when $p$ is odd and the residue degree of $k$ is even, since then $k$ contains the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and for every $k$ containing $\sqrt{-1}$ when $p = 2$.

Hoshi asked whether $G_k$ has a Hodge–Tate representation of dimension $2$ that is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate [@hoshi2024, Introduction, p. 2]. His answer gives, for each odd $p$, such a representation over some quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@hoshi2024, Theorem B, p. 2, and Corollary 3.4, p. 11], and the knowledgebase has [one over $\mathbb{Q}_p$](two-dimensional-representation-of-g-qp-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md) for $p \ge 5$. The corollary answers it for every field containing a quadratic field. Dimension $2$ is the smallest possible, as every Hodge–Tate character is Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate ([the overview](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md), part 1).

**Notation for the proofs.** These are Kondo's conventions [@kondo2026, pp. 5–6 and 9–10].

- *Basis.* By the presentation of $G_k$ in [@kondo2026, Theorem 1.2, p. 5], there are generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_d$ of $G_k$; for even $d$ the last relation is $\sigma x_0 \sigma^{-1} = (\cdots)\, x_1^{p^s} [x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4] \cdots [x_{d-1}, x_d]$, where $(\cdots)$ involves only $x_0$ and $\tau$. For $1 \le i \le d$ let $y_i \in k_+$ be the image of $x_i$ under the inclusion of the wild inertia group into inertia, the map from inertia to $\mathcal{O}_k^\times$ given by abelianization and inverse reciprocity, and the logarithm. For $d > 1$ these form a basis of $k_+$ [@kondo2026, Lemma 1.3, p. 5]. Write $y_1^*, \dots, y_d^*$ for the dual basis.
- *The homomorphism $\Phi$.* The map $\Phi : \mathrm{Out}(G_k) \to \mathrm{GL}(k_+)$, $\alpha \mapsto \alpha_+$, is a continuous homomorphism, and it is closed [@kondo2026, §0, Groups of MLF-type, p. 4, and Lemma 1.6, p. 6]. So its image $I$ is a compact subgroup. For a subspace $S \subseteq k_+^* = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(k, \mathbb{Q}_p)$ let $\mathrm{St}(S) = \{\beta \in I : S \circ \beta = S\}$, a closed subgroup of $I$. Write $T = f_1 = \mathrm{Tr}_{k/\mathbb{Q}_p}$.
- *Kondo's symplectic block.* Let $d \ge 3$. Put $r = 1$ and $g = (d-1)/2$ if $d$ is odd, and $r = 2$ and $g = (d-2)/2$ if $d$ is even. Let $Y$ be the span of $y_1, \dots, y_r$ and $U$ the span of $y_{r+1}, \dots, y_d$, so $\dim U = 2g$ and $k_+ = Y \oplus U$. Then $I$ contains every $\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{id}_Y, A)$ with $A \in \mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, acting on $U$ through the basis $y_{r+1}, \dots, y_d$ [@kondo2026, Definition 3.2, pp. 9–10, and Lemma 3.3, p. 10]; for $p = 2$ the construction applies because $\sqrt{-1} \in k$ [@kondo2026, Remark 3.1, p. 9]. Let $U^0 \subseteq k_+^*$ be the functionals that vanish on $Y$, and $Y^0$ those that vanish on $U$.
- *The transvection.* Let $d$ be even, and let $\varphi$ fix $\sigma$, $\tau$ and every $x_i$ with $i \ne 2$, and send $x_2$ to $x_2 x_1$. Kondo uses this map as an automorphism of $G_k$: for odd $p$ and Galois $k$ of even degree [@kondo2026, Remark 3.7, p. 11], and for $d = 2$, including $p = 2$ [@kondo2026, proof of Theorem 3.5, p. 11]. In general it is checked here. With $[a, b] = aba^{-1}b^{-1}$, $[x_1, x_2 x_1] = x_1 x_2 x_1^{-1} x_2^{-1} = [x_1, x_2]$, so the relation above is preserved; with the other convention $[a, b] = a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$, sending $x_2$ to $x_1 x_2$ does the same, and everything below uses only the abelianization, where the two agree. The new images of $x_0, \dots, x_d$ lie in the pro-$p$ normal subgroup that $x_0, \dots, x_d$ generate, $\sigma$ and $\tau$ are fixed, and the relations of Kondo's Theorem 1.2 hold for them. So the assignment extends to an endomorphism of the presented group $G_k$. It is surjective, since $x_2 = \varphi(x_2)\varphi(x_1)^{-1}$, hence an automorphism, as $G_k$ is topologically finitely generated. On abelianizations $\varphi$ sends the class of $x_2$ to the sum of the classes of $x_2$ and $x_1$, and fixes the classes of the other $x_i$. So $\Phi(\bar\varphi) = 1 + N$ with $N(y_2) = y_1$ and $N(y_i) = 0$ for $i \ne 2$. Note $N^2 = 0$.

**Proof of part 1.** As $E$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $\rho_E$ is crystalline, hence Hodge–Tate [@kondo2026, Remark 2.4, pp. 7–8]. Restriction to the open subgroup $G_k$ keeps it Hodge–Tate, because $B_{\mathrm{HT}}^{G_k} = k$ (see [Tate–Sen invariants](ax-sen-tate-fixed-field-theorem.md) and the first standard fact in the proof of the Proposition in [the non-Galois entry](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md)).

For irreducibility, let $n = [k : E]$ and $u \in \mathcal{O}_E^\times \subseteq \mathcal{O}_k^\times$. By the norm compatibility of reciprocity (step 3 of the proof in the non-Galois entry), the image of $\mathrm{rec}_k(u)$ in $G_E^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $\mathrm{rec}_E(N_{k/E}\,u) = \mathrm{rec}_E(u^n)$, so $\rho$ maps an element of $G_k$ to multiplication by $u^n$. The $\mathbb{Q}_p$-span $F$ of these $u^n$ is closed under products, hence a subfield of $E$. For $m$ large, the logarithm maps $1 + p^m\mathcal{O}_E$ onto $p^m\mathcal{O}_E$, so the logarithms $n\log u$ of the elements $u^n$ with $u \in 1 + p^m\mathcal{O}_E$ fill an open subgroup of $E$, which spans $E$. They lie in $F$, so $F = E$. A $G_k$-stable subspace of $E_+$ is therefore stable under multiplication by $E$, so it is $0$ or $E_+$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $\tilde k$ be the Galois closure of $k$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ in $\bar k$, $m = [\tilde k : k]$, $U_{\tilde k} = G_{\tilde k}$, and $j : U_{\tilde k}^{\mathrm{ab}} \to G_k^{\mathrm{ab}}$ the map induced by inclusion. Suppose $\rho \circ \theta$ is Hodge–Tate.

1. *Restrict to $\tilde k$.* The restriction of $\rho \circ \theta$ to $U_{\tilde k}$ is Hodge–Tate, and it is $U_{\tilde k} \to U_{\tilde k}^{\mathrm{ab}} \xrightarrow{\psi} \mathcal{O}_E^\times$ acting on $E_+$, with $\psi = \chi_E \circ \mathrm{res}^{\mathrm{ab}} \circ \theta^{\mathrm{ab}} \circ j$, where $\mathrm{res}^{\mathrm{ab}} : G_k^{\mathrm{ab}} \to G_E^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is induced by $G_k \subseteq G_E$. An $E$-basis of $\tilde k$ identifies $\tilde k_+$ with $E_+^{[\tilde k : E]}$, compatibly with multiplication by $E$, so the same $\psi$ followed by $\mathcal{O}_E^\times \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{\tilde k}^\times \subset \mathrm{Aut}(\tilde k_+)$ is a direct sum of copies, and is Hodge–Tate.
2. *Hoshi's lemma.* As $\tilde k$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, Hoshi's lemma with coefficient field $\tilde k$ gives integers $i_\sigma$, for $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(\tilde k/\mathbb{Q}_p)$, and an open subgroup of the inertia group of $\tilde k$ on which $\psi$ agrees with $\prod_\sigma \chi_{\pi,\sigma}^{i_\sigma}$, where $\chi_{\pi,\sigma}(\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(y)) = \sigma(y)$ for units $y$ [@hoshi2024, Definition 1.7 and Lemma 1.8, p. 5]. The image of that open subgroup in $U_{\tilde k}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(U')$ for an open subgroup $U' \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{\tilde k}^\times$ ([local class field theory](local-class-field-theory.md)). So $\psi(\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(y)) = \prod_\sigma \sigma(y)^{i_\sigma}$ for $y \in U'$.
3. *Compute $\psi$.* By norm compatibility, $j(\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(y)) = \mathrm{rec}_k(N_{\tilde k/k}\,y)$ and $\mathrm{res}^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathrm{rec}_k(w)) = \mathrm{rec}_E(N_{k/E}\,w)$. By fact (i) of the non-Galois entry, $\theta^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathrm{rec}_k(w)) = \mathrm{rec}_k(\theta^\times(w))$ for $w \in \mathcal{O}_k^\times$. Hence $\psi(\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(y)) = N_{k/E}\big(\theta^\times(N_{\tilde k/k}\,y)\big)$ for every unit $y$.
4. *Take logarithms.* The logarithm commutes with field embeddings, so it turns norms into traces, and by fact (ii) of the non-Galois entry it turns $\theta^\times$ into $\theta_+$. So $\mathrm{Tr}_{k/E}\big(\theta_+(\mathrm{Tr}_{\tilde k/k}\,z)\big) = \sum_\sigma i_\sigma\,\sigma(z)$ for $z$ in the open subgroup $\log U'$ of $\tilde k$, hence, both sides being $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linear, for all $z \in \tilde k$. For $z = x \in k$ this reads $m\,\mathrm{Tr}_{k/E}(\theta_+ x) = L(x)$ with $L(x) = \sum_\sigma i_\sigma\,\sigma(x)$.
5. *Factor through $\mathrm{Tr}_{k/E}$.* Let $H_E' = \mathrm{Gal}(\tilde k/E)$, a normal subgroup because $E$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. As $L(x) \in E$, $L(x) = |H_E'|^{-1}\sum_{h \in H_E'} h(L(x))$. For each $\sigma$, normality gives $\sum_{h \in H_E'} h\sigma(x) = \sigma\big(\sum_{h \in H_E'} h(x)\big) = \sigma\big(m\,\mathrm{Tr}_{k/E}(x)\big)$, since each $E$-embedding of $k$ into $\tilde k$ extends to exactly $m$ elements of $H_E'$. So $\mathrm{Tr}_{k/E} \circ \theta_+ = w \circ \mathrm{Tr}_{k/E}$ with $w = \sum_{\varepsilon \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q}_p)} q_\varepsilon\,\varepsilon$ and rational $q_\varepsilon$.
6. *Dualize.* For $e' \in E$, $f_{e'}(x) = \mathrm{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}_p}\big(e'\,\mathrm{Tr}_{k/E}(x)\big)$. Since $\mathrm{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}_p}(e'\,\varepsilon(t)) = \mathrm{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\varepsilon^{-1}(e')\,t)$,
$$f_{e'} \circ \theta_+ = f_{e''}, \qquad e'' = \sum_\varepsilon q_\varepsilon\,\varepsilon^{-1}(e') \in E.$$
For quadratic $E = \mathbb{Q}_p(\delta)$ with $\delta^2 \in \mathbb{Q}_p$ and nontrivial automorphism $\iota$, $\iota(\delta) = -\delta$ gives $f_\delta \circ \theta_+ = (q_1 - q_\iota)\, f_\delta$. As $\mathrm{Tr}_{E/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\delta(a + b\delta)) = 2b\delta^2$, the kernel of $f_\delta$ is $H_E$, so $\theta_+$ maps $H_E$ into, hence onto, itself. $\square$

So $\Phi(E_{\mathrm{out}}) \subseteq \mathrm{St}(E^*)$, and for quadratic $E$, $\Phi(E_{\mathrm{out}}) \subseteq \mathrm{St}(\mathbb{Q}_p f_\delta)$.

**Proof of part 3.** Let $E \ne \mathbb{Q}_p, k$. Then $2 \le e \le d/2$, so $d \ge 4$, and Kondo's block is available.

*Step A: the cyclotomic functional is fixed.* Let $\chi$ be the $p$-adic cyclotomic character of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$; its restriction to $G_k$ is that of $k$. The map $h = \log \circ \chi \circ \mathrm{rec}_{\mathbb{Q}_p} : \mathbb{Z}_p^\times \to \mathbb{Q}_p$ is a continuous homomorphism. It is nonzero: $\chi$ maps the inertia group of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ onto $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)), and the image of inertia in $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $\mathrm{rec}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(\mathbb{Z}_p^\times)$. It kills torsion, so it factors through $\log : \mathbb{Z}_p^\times \to \mathbb{Q}_p$, whose image is a rank-one $\mathbb{Z}_p$-lattice; hence $h = c \log$ with $c \ne 0$. By norm compatibility, $\log \chi(\mathrm{rec}_k(u)) = c\log N_{k/\mathbb{Q}_p}(u) = c\,T(\log u)$ for $u \in \mathcal{O}_k^\times$. Every $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ satisfies $\chi \circ \theta = \chi$ ([invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md)), so with fact (i), $\chi(\mathrm{rec}_k(\theta^\times u)) = \chi(\theta^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathrm{rec}_k u)) = \chi(\mathrm{rec}_k u)$. With fact (ii) this gives $T(\theta_+ z) = T(z)$ on $\log \mathcal{O}_k^\times$, which spans $k$. So $T \circ \beta = T$ for every $\beta \in I$, and $\mathrm{St}(\mathbb{Q}_p T) = I$.

*Step B: the symplectic block.* Let $S \subseteq k_+^*$ be a subspace with $\mathrm{St}(S)$ open in $I$. Then $S \subseteq Y^0$ or $S \supseteq U^0$. Indeed, the $A \in \mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ with $\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{id}_Y, A) \in \mathrm{St}(S)$ form an open subgroup $V$. For $A \in V$ and $f \in S$, $f \circ \mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{id}_Y, A) - f = f \circ \mathrm{diag}(0, A - I)$ lies in $S$; it vanishes on $Y$ and equals $f|_U \circ (A - I)$ on $U$. By the Claim in the proof of part 2 of [the non-Galois entry](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), the $A - I$ with $A \in V$ span all of $\mathrm{End}(U)$. So $S$ contains every functional vanishing on $Y$ and equal to $f|_U \circ X$ on $U$, for $f \in S$ and $X \in \mathrm{End}(U)$. If some $f \in S$ has $f|_U \ne 0$, the $f|_U \circ X$ are all of $\mathrm{Hom}(U, \mathbb{Q}_p)$, so $S \supseteq U^0$. Otherwise $S \subseteq Y^0$.

*Step C: the transvection.* Let $d$ be even and $f \ne 0$ with $\mathrm{St}(\mathbb{Q}_p f)$ open in $I$. Then $f(y_1) = 0$. Indeed, $I$ is closed and contains $(1 + N)^n = 1 + nN$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, so it contains $1 + aN$ for all $a \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, and $\mathrm{St}(\mathbb{Q}_p f)$ contains $1 + aN$ for some $a \ne 0$. Then $f + a\,f \circ N = bf$ for some $b$. Here $f \circ N = f(y_1)\,y_2^*$, and $(f \circ N) \circ N = f \circ N^2 = 0$. Composing with $N$ gives $(b - 1)\, f \circ N = 0$. If $f \circ N \ne 0$, then $b = 1$ and $a\, f \circ N = 0$, a contradiction. So $f(y_1) y_2^* = f \circ N = 0$.

*Step D: $T$ is a coordinate.* Apply Step B to $S = \mathbb{Q}_p T$, allowed by Step A. As $\dim U^0 = 2g \ge 2$, $\mathbb{Q}_p T \not\supseteq U^0$, so $T \in Y^0$. If $d$ is even, Step C gives $T(y_1) = 0$ as well. So $T \in \mathbb{Q}_p y_1^*$ for odd $d$, and $T \in \mathbb{Q}_p y_2^*$ for even $d$. In both cases $T \notin U^0$, since $T \ne 0$.

*Step E: the subgroup has infinite index.* Let $\mathrm{St} = \mathrm{St}(E^*)$ if $e \ge 3$, and $\mathrm{St} = \mathrm{St}(\mathbb{Q}_p f_\delta)$ if $E = \mathbb{Q}_p(\delta)$ is quadratic. By part 2, $\Phi(E_{\mathrm{out}}) \subseteq \mathrm{St}$. Suppose $\mathrm{St}$ is open in $I$. Then $\mathrm{St}(E^*)$ is open: for quadratic $E$, $E^*$ is spanned by $T = f_1$ and $f_\delta$, and every element of $\mathrm{St}(\mathbb{Q}_p f_\delta)$ fixes $T$ by Step A, so it preserves $E^*$. Apply Step B to $S = E^*$.

- If $E^* \supseteq U^0$, then $E^* \supseteq U^0 + \mathbb{Q}_p T$, which has dimension $2g + 1 \ge d - 1$ by Step D. But $e \le d/2 < d - 1$, as $d \ge 4$.
- If $E^* \subseteq Y^0$, then $e \le \dim Y^0 = r$. For odd $d$, $r = 1 < e$. For even $d$, this forces $e = 2$, so $E$ is quadratic and $\mathrm{St} = \mathrm{St}(\mathbb{Q}_p f_\delta)$. Then $f_\delta \in Y^0$, Step C gives $f_\delta(y_1) = 0$, so $f_\delta \in \mathbb{Q}_p y_2^*$, and by Step D $f_\delta = c\,T$ for some $c \in \mathbb{Q}_p$. That says $\mathrm{Tr}_{k/\mathbb{Q}_p}((\delta - c)x) = 0$ for all $x \in k$, so $\delta = c$, as the trace form of $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is nondegenerate. But $\delta \notin \mathbb{Q}_p$.

Both cases are impossible, so $\mathrm{St}$ is a closed subgroup of infinite index in $I$.

*Step F: measure zero.* $\Phi^{-1}(\mathrm{St})$ is a closed subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ containing $E_{\mathrm{out}}$. It has infinite index, because $\Phi$ maps $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ onto $I$ and $\Phi^{-1}(\mathrm{St})$ onto $\mathrm{St}$. A closed subgroup of infinite index in a profinite group has Haar measure zero, having infinitely many disjoint cosets of equal measure, as in the non-Galois entry. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Degree two.* When $k = E$ is quadratic, $H_E = \mathbb{Q}_p$, and part 2 says that $\theta_+$ preserves $\mathbb{Q}_p \subset k$. That is the condition Kondo shows to fail for every nonzero power of $\varphi$, citing Hoshi and Nishio for odd $p$ and Nishio for $p = 2$ [@kondo2026, proof of Theorem 3.5, p. 11]; it is the role played by $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}^\times \subset \mathcal{O}_k^\times$ in Hoshi's example [@hoshi2024, Lemma 3.1 (v), pp. 9–10]. In this case $f_\delta$ spans the annihilator of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and Steps A and C alone give part 3 for $E = k$: an open $\mathrm{St}(\mathbb{Q}_p f_\delta)$ would force $f_\delta, T \in \mathbb{Q}_p y_2^*$. For $E = k$ of degree at least $3$, $E^*$ is all of $k_+^*$ and part 2 says nothing; there Kondo's finer criterion, that $\alpha_+$ is an integer combination of elements of $\mathrm{Gal}(k/\mathbb{Q}_p)$ [@kondo2026, Theorem 2.6, p. 8], is what gives the conclusion.
- *Relation to Kondo's representation.* On inertia, $\rho$ is the composite of Kondo's character of $k$ with the norm $\mathcal{O}_k^\times \to \mathcal{O}_E^\times$, by norm compatibility. For Galois $k$, Kondo's theorem says that the classes keeping $\rho_k$ Hodge–Tate form a null set. The classes keeping $\rho$ Hodge–Tate form a priori a larger set, since part 2 constrains $\theta_+$ only through $E^*$, or through $H_E$, rather than through the whole algebra $\mathbb{Q}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(k/\mathbb{Q}_p)]$. So part 3 does not follow formally from Kondo's theorem.
- *Absolutely abelian fields.* Hoshi's proof of his Proposition 3.2 uses that, for $k$ abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of even degree, $G_k$ is characteristic in $G_E$ for a quadratic subfield $E$ of $k$ [@hoshi2024, proof of Proposition 3.2, p. 11]. For that $E$, an automorphism $\alpha$ of $G_E$ for which $\rho_E \circ \alpha$ is not Hodge–Tate, which Hoshi and Kondo provide, restricts to an automorphism of $G_k$ for which $\rho \circ \alpha|_{G_k} = (\rho_E \circ \alpha)|_{G_k}$ is not Hodge–Tate, because Hodge–Tate-ness can be tested on an open subgroup. For such $k$ and that $E$, the last sentence of part 3 is therefore a short consequence of their work. The new cases are the fields that are not abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, where $G_k$ need not be characteristic in $G_E$, and the measure-zero statement.
- *Not covered here.* Subfields $E$ that are not Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$: step 5 of part 2 uses that $\mathrm{Gal}(\tilde k/E)$ is normal. The converse of part 2. The case $p = 2$ with $\sqrt{-1} \notin k$, where Kondo's presentation is not available. And dimension $2$ over fields with no quadratic subfield, such as fields of odd degree.
