**Setting.** Use the Setting and notation of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ has degree $d$ and residue field of order $q$, $G = G_K = F/(\varrho)$ with wild inertia $P_K$, and $\Gamma = G/P_K = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$ is identified with $\mathcal{G}$, generated by $\sigma, \tau$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$. Tame inertia $I$ is the closed subgroup generated by $\tau$, and $x \mapsto \tau^x$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell \to I$. Every element of $\Gamma$ is uniquely $\tau^x\sigma^y$ with $x \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ and $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ (same entry, proof of part 3). Also $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\Gamma]]$, $\omega : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$ is the action on $\mu_p$, $\lambda \mapsto \lambda^*$ is the anti-automorphism with $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, and by part 1 there
$$\bar V = P_K/P_K^{(2)} = \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \Lambda\bar x_d,$$
with $\Lambda\bar x_i \cong \Lambda$ for $i \ge 1$ and $\rho\bar x_0 = \omega(\rho)\bar x_0$. Let $\Lambda_I \subseteq \Lambda$ be the closure of the $\mathbb{F}_p$-span of $I$, a commutative closed subalgebra.

For $d \ge 3$, write $d = 2g + r$ with $r \in \{1, 2\}$, and use the complementary generators $x_0, x_1$ (and $x_2$ if $d$ is even), the surface generators $a_i, b_i$ ($1 \le i \le g$), and Kondo's homomorphism $\Psi : \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi) \to \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ of the Setting of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md). The surface pair $(x_s, x_t)$ of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) is $(a_1, b_1)$. For $\lambda \in \Lambda^\times$ let $\Theta_\lambda$ be the restriction to $\bar V$ of the automorphism $\bar\psi_\lambda$ of $G/P_K^{(2)}$ induced by $\theta_\lambda$ (same entry, parts 2 and 3): it is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i \notin \{s, t\}$, and maps $\bar x_s \mapsto \lambda\bar x_s$ and $\bar x_t \mapsto (\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar x_t$. Put
$$\bar V_c = \bigoplus_{x_j\ \text{complementary},\ j \ge 1}\Lambda\bar x_j,\qquad \bar V_s = \bigoplus_{i=1}^g(\Lambda\bar a_i \oplus \Lambda\bar b_i),$$
so that $\bar V = \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0 \oplus \bar V_c \oplus \bar V_s$, with $\bar V_c = \Lambda\bar x_1$ for odd $d$.

For $m \ge 1$ prime to $p$ let $I_m$ be the closed subgroup generated by $\tau^m$, which is normal in $\Gamma$, and $k_m$ the order of $q$ modulo $m$. Let $\pi_p \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ have component $1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $0$ in every other $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$, and let $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ have order $p^a$, with only finitely many conjugates of its class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. Then $\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$, and the induced automorphism $\bar\theta$ of $\bar V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, with:

1. $\bar\theta(\bar x_0) = \bar x_0$;
2. $\bar\theta(\bar a_i) - \bar a_i$ and $\bar\theta(\bar b_i) - \bar b_i$ lie in $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ for $1 \le i \le g$;
3. $\bar\theta(\bar V_c) \subseteq \bar V_c \oplus \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$.

Let $B$ be the $\Lambda$-linear automorphism of $\bar V_c \cong (\bar V_c \oplus \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0)/\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ induced by $\bar\theta$, the *complementary block*; $B^{p^a} = 1$. For odd $d$, $B(\mu\bar x_1) = \mu\xi\,\bar x_1$ for a unit $\xi \in \Lambda$ with $\xi^{p^a} = 1$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here. Let $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$.

1. For $\theta$ as in the Theorem, $\theta = 1$ if and only if $(B - 1)^{p-3} = 0$. For odd $d$, this means $(\xi - 1)^{p-3} = 0$.
2. *What a counterexample needs.* Every element of order $p$ of a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ is the class of an automorphism $\theta$ of order $p$ as in the Theorem whose complementary block satisfies $(B - 1)^p = 0$ but $(B - 1)^{p-3} \ne 0$. This applies to every $K \ni \zeta_p$, for which $d \ge p - 1 \ge 4$.

**Lemma 1 (central elements).** This is proved here. For $m \ge 1$ prime to $p$, the group $I_m \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ has order prime to $p$. Let $\varepsilon_m \in \Lambda$ be its normalized Haar measure: for an open normal subgroup $U$ of $\Gamma$, the image of $\varepsilon_m$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U]$ is $|W|^{-1}\sum_{w \in W}w$, where $W$ is the image of $I_m$, a finite group of order prime to $p$. These images are compatible, so $\varepsilon_m$ is well defined. (It is the idempotent $h_O$ of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) for $O$ the characters of order dividing $m$.)

- (a) $\varepsilon_m$ is a central idempotent, $w\varepsilon_m = \varepsilon_m$ for $w \in I_m$, and $\varepsilon_m \to 1$ as $m \to \infty$ along divisibility. If $(p - 1) \mid m$, then $\varepsilon_m^* = \varepsilon_m$.
- (b) Put $k = k_m$ and $\gamma_m = \sigma^{k\pi_p} \in \Gamma$. Then $\varepsilon_m\gamma_m$ and $\eta_m = \varepsilon_m(\gamma_m - 1)$ are central, $\eta_m^{p^j} \to 0$ as $j \to \infty$, and $1 + \eta_m = (1 - \varepsilon_m) + \varepsilon_m\gamma_m$ is a unit. If $(p - 1) \mid m$, then $\eta_m^* = \varepsilon_m(\gamma_m^{-1} - 1) = (1 + \eta_m)^{-1} - 1$.
- (c) If $z \in \Lambda$ and $\eta_m^jz = 0$ for some $j \ge 0$, then $\varepsilon_mz = 0$.

*Proof.* (a) At each level, $|W|^{-1}\sum_wW$ is idempotent and absorbs every element of $W$. Conjugation by $\rho \in \Gamma$ maps $I_m$ to itself, as $I_m$ is the unique closed subgroup of index $m$ of $I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ and $I$ is normal; so it permutes $W$, and $\rho\varepsilon_m = \varepsilon_m\rho$. As the span of $\Gamma$ is dense in $\Lambda$, $\varepsilon_m$ is central. Every open normal $U$ meets $I$ in an open subgroup, which contains $I_{m_0}$ for some $m_0$; for $m_0 \mid m$, $W = 1$ and $\varepsilon_m$ maps to $1$. If $(p - 1) \mid m$, then $\omega(\tau^m) = \omega(\tau)^m = 1$, so $w^* = w^{-1}$ for $w \in I_m$, and inversion permutes $W$.

(b) $\varepsilon_m\sigma^k$ commutes with $\sigma$, and $\tau\varepsilon_m\sigma^k\tau^{-1} = \varepsilon_m\tau^{1 - q^k}\sigma^k = \varepsilon_m\sigma^k$, because $m \mid q^k - 1$, so $\tau^{1-q^k} \in I_m$. The elements of $\Gamma$ commuting with $\varepsilon_m\sigma^k$ form a closed subgroup containing $\sigma$ and $\tau$, hence all of $\Gamma$, so $\varepsilon_m\sigma^k$ is central. Choosing integers $n_j \to \pi_p$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, $\varepsilon_m\gamma_m = \lim(\varepsilon_m\sigma^k)^{n_j}$ is central, and so is $\eta_m$. In characteristic $p$, $\eta_m^{p^j} = \varepsilon_m(\gamma_m^{p^j} - 1)$, and $\gamma_m^{p^j} = \sigma^{kp^j\pi_p} \to 1$ as $p^j\pi_p \to 0$. As $\varepsilon_m$ is a central idempotent, $((1 - \varepsilon_m) + \varepsilon_m\gamma_m)((1 - \varepsilon_m) + \varepsilon_m\gamma_m^{-1}) = 1$, so $1 + \eta_m$ is a unit with inverse $1 + \varepsilon_m(\gamma_m^{-1} - 1)$. Finally, $\gamma_m^* = \omega(\sigma)^{k\pi_p}\gamma_m^{-1} = \gamma_m^{-1}$, because $\omega(\sigma)$ has order dividing $p - 1$ while $\pi_p$ is divisible by $p - 1$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$; so if $(p - 1) \mid m$, then $\eta_m^* = (\gamma_m^* - 1)\varepsilon_m^* = \varepsilon_m(\gamma_m^{-1} - 1)$ by (a).

(c) $\eta_mz = (\gamma_m - 1)\varepsilon_mz$. The closed subgroup generated by $\sigma$ is $\{\sigma^y : y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, by the normal form, so the one generated by $\gamma_m$ is $\{\sigma^y : y \in k\pi_p\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$, infinite and pro-$p$. Apply Lemma 2 $j$ times. $\square$

**Lemma 2.** This is proved here. Let $\gamma \in \Gamma$ generate an infinite pro-$p$ closed subgroup. If $z \in \Lambda$ and $(\gamma - 1)z = 0$ or $z(\gamma - 1) = 0$, then $z = 0$.

*Proof.* Suppose $(\gamma - 1)z = 0$; the other case is symmetric. Let $U$ be an open normal subgroup of $\Gamma$, and $n$ the order of the image $\bar\gamma$ of $\gamma$ in $\Gamma/U$, a power of $p$. The orders of the images of $\gamma$ in the finite quotients of $\Gamma$ are unbounded, since otherwise $\gamma^{p^c}$ would lie in every open subgroup for some $c$. So there is an open normal $U' \subseteq U$ in which $\bar\gamma$ has order $n'$ with $n'/n \in p\mathbb{Z}$. Let $C$ be the cyclic group generated by $\bar\gamma$ in $\Gamma/U'$. Then $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U']$ is a free left $\mathbb{F}_p[C]$-module on representatives of the right cosets of $C$, and in $\mathbb{F}_p[C] \cong \mathbb{F}_p[t]/(t^{n'} - 1)$ the elements killed by $t - 1$ are the multiples of $\sum_{i < n'}t^i$. So the image of $z$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U']$ is $(\sum_{i<n'}\bar\gamma^i)z'$ for some $z'$. In $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U]$, $\sum_{i<n'}\bar\gamma^i = (n'/n)\sum_{i<n}\bar\gamma^i = 0$. So $z$ maps to $0$ in every $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U]$, and $z = 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.** This is proved here. Let $z \in \Lambda$ commute with every element of $\Lambda_I$, and suppose $z^p = 0$. Then $z = 0$.

*Proof.* For $m$ prime to $p$ and $n \ge 1$ divisible by $k_m$, let $H_{m,n}$ be the closed subgroup generated by $\tau^m$ and $\sigma^n$. It is normal of index $mn$ ([tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md), proof of part 3 (b)), and equals $\{\tau^x\sigma^y : m \mid x,\ n \mid y\}$, as this set is a closed subgroup ($\tau^x\sigma^y\tau^{x'}\sigma^{y'} = \tau^{x + q^yx'}\sigma^{y + y'}$) containing the generators. So the elements of $\Gamma/H_{m,n}$ are the $\bar\tau^i\bar\sigma^j$ with $0 \le i < m$ and $0 \le j < n$, each once. Every open normal subgroup $U$ contains some $H_{m,n}$: $U \cap I \supseteq I_m$ and $\sigma^n \in U$ for suitable $m$ and $n$, and replacing $n$ by $nk_m$ we may take $n$ divisible by $k_m$. Put $\Lambda_{m,n} = \mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/H_{m,n}]$ and let $A_m \subseteq \Lambda_{m,n}$ be the span of the $\bar\tau^i$, the image of $\Lambda_I$. Then $\Lambda_{m,n} = \bigoplus_{j<n}A_m\bar\sigma^j$, free as a left $A_m$-module, and $\bar\sigma a\bar\sigma^{-1} = \alpha(a)$ for the automorphism $\alpha$ of $A_m$ with $\alpha(\bar\tau) = \bar\tau^q$.

Fix $m$. As $p \nmid m$, $A_m$ is a finite product of finite fields $F_i = e_iA_m$, with primitive idempotents $e_i$ permuted by $\alpha$. Write the image of $z$ as $z_n = \sum_{j<n}c_j\bar\sigma^j$ with $c_j \in A_m$. Comparing $z_na = \sum_jc_j\alpha^j(a)\bar\sigma^j$ with $az_n = \sum_jac_j\bar\sigma^j$ gives $c_j(\alpha^j(a) - a) = 0$ for all $a \in A_m$. Let $k_i\mathbb{Z}$ be the set of $j \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $\alpha^j$ fixes $e_i$ and is the identity on $F_i$; it contains $k_m\mathbb{Z}$. If $j \notin k_i\mathbb{Z}$, then $c_je_i = 0$: if $\alpha^j(e_i) \ne e_i$, multiply $c_j(\alpha^j(e_i) - e_i) = 0$ by $e_i$; otherwise choose $a \in F_i$ with $\alpha^j(a) - a \ne 0$, which is a unit of $F_i$. Hence
$$e_iz_n = \sum_{l < n/k_i}(e_ic_{lk_i})\,\bar\sigma^{lk_i}$$
lies in the set $S_{i,n}$ of such sums with coefficients in $F_i$. As $\alpha^{k_i}$ is the identity on $F_i$ and $\bar\sigma^n = 1$, $S_{i,n}$ is a commutative ring, isomorphic to $F_i[\mathbb{Z}/(n/k_i)]$ with $e_i\bar\sigma^{k_i}$ corresponding to a generator, by the freeness above. For $n \mid n'$, the reduction $\Lambda_{m,n'} \to \Lambda_{m,n}$ fixes $A_m$ and $\bar\sigma$, and maps $S_{i,n'}$ to $S_{i,n}$ as the reduction $F_i[\mathbb{Z}/(n'/k_i)] \to F_i[\mathbb{Z}/(n/k_i)]$. So the $e_iz_n$, for $n$ running over the multiples of $k_m$, form an element $\chi$ of $F_i[[\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}]] = \varprojlim_NF_i[\mathbb{Z}/N]$, and $\chi^p = 0$, because $e_i$ commutes with $z_n$, so $(e_iz_n)^p = e_iz_n^p = 0$.

It remains to see that $\chi = 0$, that is, that its image $\chi_N$ in $F_i[\mathbb{Z}/N]$ vanishes for every $N$ in the cofinal set involved. Write $N = p^cN'$ with $p \nmid N'$. Then $F_i[\mathbb{Z}/N'] = \prod_kF'_k$ is a product of finite fields, and $F_i[\mathbb{Z}/pN] = \prod_kF'_k[t]/(t^{p^{c+1}})$ and $F_i[\mathbb{Z}/N] = \prod_kF'_k[t]/(t^{p^c})$, with $t = g - 1$ for a generator $g$ of the $p$-part, compatibly with reduction. If $f = \sum_lf_lt^l$ satisfies $f^p = \sum_lf_l^pt^{pl} = 0$ in $F'_k[t]/(t^{p^{c+1}})$, then $f_l = 0$ for $l < p^c$, so $f$ maps to $0$ modulo $t^{p^c}$. Hence $\chi_N$, the image of $\chi_{pN}$, is $0$. So $e_iz_n = 0$ for all $i$ and $n$, every $z_n = \sum_ie_iz_n$ is $0$, and as the $H_{m,n}$ are cofinal, $z = 0$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.** This is proved here.

- (a) For every $b \ge 0$, every element of $\Lambda_I$ is the $p^b$-th power of an element of $\Lambda_I$.
- (b) Let $u \in \Lambda^\times$ with $u^{p^j} \to 1$ as $j \to \infty$, let $e'$ be an integer prime to $p$, and let $z \in \Lambda$ commute with $u^{e'}$. Then $z$ commutes with $u$.

*Proof.* (a) With the notation of Lemma 3, $\Lambda_I$ is the inverse limit of its images $A_m$, being closed in $\Lambda = \varprojlim\Lambda_{m,n}$. Each $A_m$ is a finite product of finite fields, on which $a \mapsto a^{p^b}$ is bijective. The $p^b$-th roots of the images of an element are therefore unique and compatible, and form an element of $\Lambda_I$.

(b) An element of $\Lambda$ is a unit exactly when its image in every $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U]$ is, so $\Lambda^\times = \varprojlim\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U]^\times$ is a profinite group. The closure of $\langle u\rangle$ in it is procyclic, and pro-$p$ because $u^{p^j} \to 1$. As $p \nmid e'$, it is topologically generated by $u^{e'}$, so $u$ is a limit of powers of $u^{e'}$, all of which commute with $z$. $\square$

**Proof of the Theorem.**

*Step 0: the tame quotient and $\bar x_0$.* By part 2 of the Theorem of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), $\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$. So $\theta(\rho) = \rho c_\rho$ with $c_\rho \in P_K$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, and $c_\rho$ acts trivially on $\bar V$; hence $\bar\theta(\rho v) = \rho\,\bar\theta(v)$, and $\bar\theta$, being continuous and additive, is $\Lambda$-linear. Also $\bar\theta^{p^a} = 1$. Let $\gamma = \sigma^{\pi_p}$, which generates a closed subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$, with $\omega(\gamma) = 1$. If $v = c\bar x_0 + \sum_{i \ge 1}\lambda_i\bar x_i$ satisfies $\rho v = \omega(\rho)v$ for all $\rho \in \Gamma$, then $(\gamma - 1)\lambda_i = 0$, so $\lambda_i = 0$ by Lemma 2. Thus $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ is the set of such $v$, and $\bar\theta$ preserves it. So $\bar\theta(\bar x_0) = c\bar x_0$ with $c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ and $c^{p^a} = 1$, hence $c = 1$. This is part 1.

For $j \ge 1$ write $\bar\theta(\bar x_j) = \mu_{j0}\bar x_0 + \sum_{i \ge 1}\mu_{ji}\bar x_i$ with $\mu_{j0} \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mu_{ji} \in \Lambda$, uniquely. By $\Lambda$-linearity, $\bar\theta(\lambda\bar x_j) = \omega(\lambda)\mu_{j0}\bar x_0 + \sum_{i \ge 1}\lambda\mu_{ji}\bar x_i$.

*Step 1: commuting with the $\Theta_\nu$.* Let $\mathcal{A}$, the finite set $\mathcal{C}$, and the subgroup $\mathcal{P} = \{\bar\psi_\lambda : \lambda \in \Lambda^\times\}$ of $\mathcal{A}$ be as in Step 1 of the proof of part 1 of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md). The stabilizer $\mathcal{P}_0$ of the class of the automorphism $\theta_2$ of $G/P_K^{(2)}$ induced by $\theta$ has finite index in $\mathcal{P}$, and so has its normal core $\mathcal{P}_1$. Put $e = (\mathcal{P} : \mathcal{P}_1)$ and write $e = p^be'$ with $p \nmid e'$. For $\lambda \in \Lambda^\times$, $\bar\psi_\lambda^e \in \mathcal{P}_1$, and $\bar\psi_\lambda^e = \bar\psi_{\lambda^e}$, as $\lambda \mapsto \bar\psi_\lambda$ is an anti-homomorphism (part 3 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). For $\bar\psi \in \mathcal{P}_0$, $\bar\psi\theta_2\bar\psi^{-1} = \theta_2c_x$ with $c_x$ conjugation by some $x \in G/P_K^{(2)}$. Both sides induce the identity on $\Gamma$, which has trivial centre (same entry, part 3), so $x \in \bar V$, and $c_x$ is trivial on $\bar V$. Hence, with $(\Lambda^\times)^e = \{\lambda^e : \lambda \in \Lambda^\times\}$,
$$\Theta_\nu\bar\theta = \bar\theta\Theta_\nu \qquad (\nu \in (\Lambda^\times)^e). \tag{C}$$
Put $\nu' = (\nu^*)^{-1}$. As $*$ reverses products, $\lambda \mapsto (\lambda^*)^{-1}$ is an automorphism of the group $\Lambda^\times$, so $\nu \mapsto \nu'$ maps $(\Lambda^\times)^e$ onto itself.

*Step 2: the pair $(x_s, x_t) = (a_1, b_1)$, off the diagonal.* Evaluating (C) on $\bar x_j$ and comparing coordinates in the free summands $\Lambda\bar x_i$ gives, for every $\nu \in (\Lambda^\times)^e$:

- (R1) $\mu_{js}(\nu - 1) = 0$ and $\mu_{jt}(\nu' - 1) = 0$ for $j \ge 1$, $j \notin \{s, t\}$, since $\Theta_\nu$ fixes $\bar x_j$;
- (R2) $(\nu - 1)\mu_{si} = 0$ and $(\nu' - 1)\mu_{ti} = 0$ for $i \ge 1$, $i \notin \{s, t\}$;
- (R3) $\nu\mu_{ss} = \mu_{ss}\nu$, $\nu\mu_{st} = \mu_{st}\nu'$, $\nu'\mu_{ts} = \mu_{ts}\nu$ and $\nu'\mu_{tt} = \mu_{tt}\nu'$.

For instance, $\bar\theta\Theta_\nu(\bar x_s) = \bar\theta(\nu\bar x_s)$ has $\bar x_i$-coordinate $\nu\mu_{si}$, while $\Theta_\nu\bar\theta(\bar x_s)$ has $\bar x_i$-coordinates $\mu_{si}$ for $i \notin \{s, t\}$, $\mu_{ss}\nu$ for $i = s$ and $\mu_{st}\nu'$ for $i = t$.

Fix $m$ prime to $p$ with $(p - 1) \mid m$, and $c \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{0, 1\}$. By Lemma 1 (b), $\eta_m$ is central and topologically nilpotent, so $1 + c\eta_m$ is a central unit, and $\nu = (1 + c\eta_m)^e$ lies in $(\Lambda^\times)^e$. We use that, for a central topologically nilpotent $\eta$,
$$(1 + \eta)^e - 1 = (1 + \eta^{p^b})^{e'} - 1 = \eta^{p^b}\big(e' + \eta^{p^b}r\big)$$
with $r$ a polynomial in $\eta^{p^b}$, and $e' + \eta^{p^b}r$ is a unit. With $\eta = c\eta_m$ and $c^{p^b} = c$, this shows that $\nu - 1$ is $\eta_m^{p^b}$ times a central unit. Next $\nu^* = (1 + c\eta_m^*)^e$ with $\eta_m^* = -\eta_m(1 + \eta_m)^{-1}$, so in the same way $\nu^* - 1$ is $\eta_m^{p^b}$ times a central unit, and hence so is $\nu' - 1 = -\nu'(\nu^* - 1)$. Finally $\nu - \nu' = \nu'(\nu^*\nu - 1)$, and $\nu^*\nu = \big((1 + c\eta_m^*)(1 + c\eta_m)\big)^e = (1 + h)^e$ with
$$h = c(\eta_m + \eta_m^*) + c^2\eta_m\eta_m^* = (c - c^2)\,\eta_m^2(1 + \eta_m)^{-1},$$
using $\eta_m + \eta_m^* = \eta_m^2(1 + \eta_m)^{-1}$ and $\eta_m\eta_m^* = -\eta_m^2(1 + \eta_m)^{-1}$. As $c - c^2 \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, $\nu - \nu'$ is $\eta_m^{2p^b}$ times a central unit.

Now (R1) gives $\eta_m^{p^b}\mu_{js} = 0$, so $\varepsilon_m\mu_{js} = 0$ by Lemma 1 (c), and letting $m \to \infty$, $\mu_{js} = \lim\varepsilon_m\mu_{js} = 0$ by Lemma 1 (a). In the same way $\mu_{jt} = 0$ for $j \notin \{s, t\}$, and (R2) gives $\mu_{si} = \mu_{ti} = 0$ for $i \ge 1$, $i \notin \{s, t\}$. As $\nu$ and $\nu'$ are central, (R3) gives $\mu_{st}(\nu - \nu') = 0$ and $\mu_{ts}(\nu' - \nu) = 0$, so $\mu_{st} = \mu_{ts} = 0$ in the same way.

So $\bar\theta(\bar x_s) = \mu_{s0}\bar x_0 + \mu_{ss}\bar x_s$ and $\bar\theta(\bar x_t) = \mu_{t0}\bar x_0 + \mu_{tt}\bar x_t$, and $\bar\theta(\bar x_j)$ has zero $\bar x_s$- and $\bar x_t$-coordinates for $j \notin \{s, t\}$. By induction, using part 1 and $\Lambda$-linearity, $\bar\theta^n(\bar x_s) \in \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0 + \mu_{ss}^n\bar x_s$, so $\mu_{ss}^{p^a} = 1$, and likewise $\mu_{tt}^{p^a} = 1$.

*Step 3: the diagonal.* Let $z \in \Lambda$ and $m$ as in Step 2. The open two-sided ideals $\ker(\Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U])$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of $0$, and $\eta_m^n$ lies in any of them for large $n$; so $(\eta_mz)^n = \eta_m^nz^n \to 0$, and $1 + \eta_mz$ is a unit. So $\nu = (1 + \eta_mz)^e \in (\Lambda^\times)^e$, and by (R3) $\mu_{ss}$ commutes with $\nu = u^{e'}$, where $u = (1 + \eta_mz)^{p^b} = 1 + \eta_m^{p^b}z^{p^b}$, as $\eta_m$ is central. Since $u^{p^j} = 1 + \eta_m^{p^{b+j}}z^{p^{b+j}} \to 1$, Lemma 4 (b) shows that $\mu_{ss}$ commutes with $u$, hence with $\eta_m^{p^b}z^{p^b}$. So $\eta_m^{p^b}(\mu_{ss}z^{p^b} - z^{p^b}\mu_{ss}) = 0$, Lemma 1 (c) gives $\varepsilon_m(\mu_{ss}z^{p^b} - z^{p^b}\mu_{ss}) = 0$, and letting $m \to \infty$, $\mu_{ss}$ commutes with $z^{p^b}$ for every $z \in \Lambda$. By Lemma 4 (a), $\mu_{ss}$ commutes with every element of $\Lambda_I$. Now $(\mu_{ss} - 1)^{p^a} = \mu_{ss}^{p^a} - 1 = 0$. If $\mu_{ss} \ne 1$, let $j \ge 1$ be least with $(\mu_{ss} - 1)^{p^j} = 0$; then $(\mu_{ss} - 1)^{p^{j-1}}$ is nonzero, commutes with $\Lambda_I$ and has $p$-th power $0$, contradicting Lemma 3. So $\mu_{ss} = 1$. By (R3), $\mu_{tt}$ commutes with $\nu'$ for every $\nu \in (\Lambda^\times)^e$, that is, with every element of $(\Lambda^\times)^e$ (Step 1), and the same argument gives $\mu_{tt} = 1$.

*Step 4: the other pairs.* Steps 0 to 3 apply to every automorphism satisfying the hypotheses. For $1 \le i \le g$, let $\varphi_i \in \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ induce on $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ the symplectic automorphism exchanging $[\alpha_1]$ with $[\alpha_i]$ and $[\beta_1]$ with $[\beta_i]$ and fixing the other basis vectors; it exists because the automorphisms induced by $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ include $\mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$ ([even degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md), Setting). Put $\kappa_i = \Psi(\varphi_i)$. It is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and on the complementary generators, so its action $\bar\kappa_i$ on $\bar V$ is $\Lambda$-linear and fixes $\bar x_0$ and the complementary $\bar x_j$. It maps $a_k, b_k$ to $\iota(\varphi_i(\alpha_k)), \iota(\varphi_i(\beta_k))$, and the composite of $\iota : \Pi \to P$ with the projection to the abelian group $\bar V$ factors through $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$, with $[\alpha_k] \mapsto \bar a_k$ and $[\beta_k] \mapsto \bar b_k$. So $\bar\kappa_i$ exchanges $\bar a_1 \leftrightarrow \bar a_i$ and $\bar b_1 \leftrightarrow \bar b_i$ and fixes the other $\bar x_j$. The automorphism $\kappa_i^{-1}\theta\kappa_i$ has order $p^a$, and its outer class, being conjugate to that of $\theta$, has finitely many conjugates. Applying Steps 2 and 3 to it and transporting by $\bar\kappa_i$: $\bar\theta(\bar a_i) - \bar a_i$ and $\bar\theta(\bar b_i) - \bar b_i$ lie in $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$, and for every $j \ge 1$ with $x_j \notin \{a_i, b_i\}$, $\bar\theta(\bar x_j)$ has zero $\bar a_i$- and $\bar b_i$-coordinates.

Doing this for every $i$ gives part 2, and, for complementary $x_j$, part 3. Then $\bar\theta$ induces a $\Lambda$-linear automorphism $B$ of $\bar V_c$ with $B^{p^a} = 1$. For odd $d$, $B(\mu\bar x_1) = \mu\mu_{11}\bar x_1$, and $\xi = \mu_{11}$ satisfies $\xi^{p^a} = 1$. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** (1) If $\theta = 1$, then $B = 1$. Conversely, define $N_0, B' : \bar V \to \bar V$ as follows. For $v \in \bar V_c$ write $\bar\theta(v) - v = N_0(v) + B'(v)$ with $N_0(v) \in \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ and $B'(v) \in \bar V_c$, by part 3; for $v \in \bar V_s$ put $N_0(v) = \bar\theta(v) - v$, which lies in $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ by part 2 and $\Lambda$-linearity, and $B'(v) = 0$; and put $N_0(\bar x_0) = B'(\bar x_0) = 0$. By part 1, $\bar\theta - 1 = N_0 + B'$. Both $N_0$ and $B'$ kill $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$, which contains the image of $N_0$, and $B'$ preserves $\bar V_c$, on which it corresponds to $B - 1$. By induction on $k \ge 1$, $(\bar\theta - 1)^k = N_0B'^{k-1} + B'^k$: applying $N_0 + B'$ to $N_0B'^{k-1}$ gives $0$, and to $B'^k$ gives $N_0B'^k + B'^{k+1}$. So if $(B - 1)^{p-3} = 0$, then $(\bar\theta - 1)^{p-2} = 0$, and $\theta = 1$ by part 1 of [detection on the Frattini quotient](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-on-the-frattini-quotient-of-wild-inertia.md). For odd $d$, $(B - 1)^k(\mu\bar x_1) = \mu(\xi - 1)^k\bar x_1$, so $(B - 1)^k = 0$ exactly when $(\xi - 1)^k = 0$.

(2) An element $f$ of order $p$ of a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has finitely many conjugates. By part 3 (a) of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), it is the class of an automorphism $\theta$ of order $p$, which is not the identity as $f \ne 1$. By part 1, $(B - 1)^{p-3} \ne 0$, while $(B - 1)^p = B^p - 1 = 0$. If $\zeta_p \in K$, then $p - 1$ divides the ramification index of $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$, so $d \ge p - 1 \ge 4$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What remains.* For $p \ge 5$ and $d \ge 3$, the question of [Tsujimura's question for local Galois groups](p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md) on finite normal subgroups of order divisible by $p$ comes down to the complementary block $B$, an element of $\mathrm{GL}_r(\Lambda)$ of $p$-power order. Kondo's automorphisms and the $\theta_\lambda$ fix the complementary generators modulo $P_K^{(2)}$, so they give no information on $B$. Automorphisms of $G_K$ that move a complementary generator into the surface part modulo $P_K^{(2)}$, along an infinite family closed under composition, would constrain $B$ by the argument of Step 2; none is used here.
- *Why the central elements $\eta_m$.* Finite outer conjugacy only gives (C) for $\nu$ in a subgroup of finite index of $\Lambda^\times$, which contains the $e$-th powers for an $e$ that is not controlled. A unit whose order divides $e$ gives nothing, such as $1 + (c - 1)h_O$ for a central idempotent $h_O$ as in [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) when $p - 1$ divides $e$. The $\eta_m$ come from the pro-$p$ part of the Frobenius, and $(1 + c\eta_m)^e \ne 1$ for every $e \ge 1$: by Step 2, $(1 + c\eta_m)^e - 1$ is $\eta_m^{p^b}$ times a unit, and $\eta_m^{p^b} \ne 0$ by Lemma 1 (c), as $\varepsilon_m \ne 0$. For the same reason, an automorphism of $G_K$ whose action on $G_K/P_K^{(2)}$ has finite order may give no information through finite outer conjugacy alone.
- *Relation to the tame levels.* The Theorem works modulo $P_K^{(2)}$ over the whole group algebra $\Lambda$, so it sees all tamely ramified levels at once, including those whose degree over $K$ is divisible by $p$, which part 1 of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md) does not treat. It is a statement modulo $p$; it is combined with integral information only through [detection on the Frattini quotient](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-on-the-frattini-quotient-of-wild-inertia.md).
- *Not covered.* For $p = 3$, [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) does not give the identity on $\Gamma$, and the Corollary would need $(\bar\theta - 1)^{1} = 0$, which part 2 of the Theorem does not give, as the coordinates $\mu_{j0}$ are not controlled. For $d \le 2$ the $\theta_\lambda$ are not available.
