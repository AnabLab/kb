**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd and $G=G_{\mathbb Q_p}$. Use the Setting of [nonabelian finite subgroups](nonabelian-finite-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md): $V\subset G$ is wild inertia, $V^2=V^p[V,V]$, $\mathcal G=G/V=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q_p^{\mathrm{tr}}/\mathbb Q_p)$ has generators $\sigma,\tau$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^p$, the character $\omega$ giving the action on $\mu_p$ has $\omega(\sigma)=1$ and $\omega(\tau)$ of order $p-1$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\mathcal G]]$ carries the anti-involution $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, and $\delta_0=\delta-\delta^*$, so $\delta_0^*=-\delta_0$. Levels $K'$, the groups $\Gamma'=\mathrm{Gal}(K'/\mathbb Q_p)$ and the elements $\lambda_{K'}\in\mathbb F_p[\Gamma']$ are as there. By part 1 there, $\mathcal U$ is the group of $v\in\Lambda^\times$ with $v^*\delta_0v=\delta_0$ and $\lambda_{K'}v=\lambda_{K'}$ in $\mathbb F_p[\Gamma']$ for every level $K'$. For $v\in\mathcal U$ there is an automorphism $\theta_v$ of $G$ inducing the identity on $\mathcal G$, and the automorphism $\Phi(v)$ of $G/V^2$ induced by $\theta_v$ depends only on $v$, with $\Phi(v)\Phi(v')=\Phi(v'v)$.

Write $\bar V=V/V^2$ additively, a compact $\Lambda$-module. By part 1 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), which has no condition on the degree, $\bar V=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0\oplus\Lambda\bar x_1$ with $\Lambda\bar x_1\cong\Lambda$ and $\rho\bar x_0=\omega(\rho)\bar x_0$. Let $\Theta_v$ be the restriction of $\Phi(v)$ to $\bar V$. By part 1 of the nonabelian entry it is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_0$, and maps $\mu\bar x_1\mapsto\mu v\bar x_1$. By part 3 of the tame-group-algebra theorem and its proof, which concern $\mathcal G$ alone, $\mathcal G$ has trivial centre.

*Inertia.* Let $I\subseteq\mathcal G$ be the closed subgroup generated by $\tau$, so $x\mapsto\tau^x$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb Z}'=\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell\to I$; let $I_m$ be the closed subgroup generated by $\tau^m$ and $\Lambda_I$ the closure of the span of $I$ in $\Lambda$. Let $X$ be the group of continuous characters $\xi:I\to\bar{\mathbb F}_p^\times$, with Frobenius orbits $O(\xi)=\{\xi^{p^k}:k\ge0\}$ and the involution $\iota(O)=\{\omega\xi^{-1}:\xi\in O\}$ on orbits, as in step (a) of the proof of [finite abelian subgroups](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md). Distinct orbits are disjoint. For $v\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}'^\times$ put $\xi^v(t)=\xi(t^v)$ and $O^v=\{\xi^v:\xi\in O\}$, again an orbit. We use the idempotents $T_O\in\Lambda$ of that step, defined at each level by $T_O=\sum_{\xi\in O}t_\xi$, and the following.

- (T1) The $T_O$ are central idempotents, $T_OT_{O'}=0$ for $O\ne O'$, and $T_O\ne0$ (properties 1, 2 and 5 there).
- (T2) $T_O^*=T_{\iota(O)}$ (property 3), and $\lambda_{K'}$ is the image of $T_{\{\omega\}}$ at each level (property 4).
- (T3) Let $x\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$ and $c\in\mathbb F_p$. If $\xi(\tau)^x=c$ for every $\xi\in O$, then $T_O\tau^x=cT_O$. Indeed $\bar\tau t_\xi=\xi(\tau)t_\xi$ at each level, so the image of $T_O\tau^x$ is $\sum_{\xi\in O}\xi(\tau)^xt_\xi$.
- (T4) If $O$ has $f$ elements, then $T_O\sigma^f$ is central in $T_O\Lambda$, by the first bullet of step (c) of the proof of the nonabelian entry, which uses only $\xi^{p^f}=\xi$ for $\xi\in O$.
- (T5) Comparing formulas level by level, $T_O$ is the idempotent $h_O$ of the section *Idempotents* of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md). That section uses only the group $\mathcal G$ and $\omega$, so it applies here, with $q=p$. By its property (iv), if $\eta\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has tame twist $u$ and induces $\beta$ on $\mathcal G$, then $\beta(T_O)=T_{O^{u^{-1}}}$.
- (T6) For $m$ prime to $p$ and $n$ divisible by the order $k_m$ of $p$ modulo $m$, let $\Lambda_{m,n}=\mathbb F_p[\mathcal G/H_{m,n}]=\bigoplus_{j<n}A_m\bar\sigma^j$ be as in the proof of Lemma 3 of [the surface-generator entry](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md), free as a left module over the image $A_m\cong\mathbb F_p[I/I_m]$ of $\Lambda_I$. If $p-1$ and the orders of all $\xi\in O$ divide $m$, the image of $T_O$ in $A_m$ is nonzero: by (T5) it is $h_{O,(m)}$, whose value at $\xi\in O$ is $1$.

*Tame twists.* As in the Setting of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md), every $\eta\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ induces $\beta=\beta_{u,a}$ on $\mathcal G$, with $\beta(\tau)=\tau^u$ and $\beta(\sigma)=\sigma\tau^a$ for unique $u\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}'^\times$, the tame twist, and $a\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$, and $\beta_{u,a}\beta_{u',a'}=\beta_{uu',a+ua'}$. As in the Setting of tame twists, $\eta$ induces an additive automorphism $\bar\eta$ of $\bar V$ with $\bar\eta(\lambda w)=\beta(\lambda)\bar\eta(w)$, where $\beta$ also denotes the continuous ring automorphism of $\Lambda$ extending it. By Step 0 (ii) of the proof of [finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md), $\omega\circ\beta=\omega$ and $\beta(\lambda^*)=\beta(\lambda)^*$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, and let $\theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(G_{\mathbb Q_p})$ have only finitely many conjugates of its class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb Q_p})$. Then its tame twist is $p^\nu$ for some $\nu\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, and there is $g\in G_{\mathbb Q_p}$ such that $\mathrm{inn}(g)\circ\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathcal G=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q_p^{\mathrm{tr}}/\mathbb Q_p)$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, and let $x\in\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb Q_p})$ have finitely many conjugates.

- (a) $x$ has a representative inducing the identity on $\mathcal G$, and two such representatives differ by $\mathrm{inn}(g)$ with $g\in V$.
- (b) If $x$ has finite order $r$ prime to $p$, then $x$ has a representative of order dividing $r$ that induces the identity on $\mathcal G$.

**Lemma A (unitary block units; proved here).** Let $O$ be an orbit with $O\ne\iota(O)$ and $O\notin\{\{1\},\{\omega\}\}$, and put $e=T_O$. Then $e^*=T_{\iota(O)}$ and $ee^*=0$. For a unit $g$ of the ring $e\Lambda$, with identity $e$, that commutes with $e\delta_0$, put
$$v_g=(1-e-e^*)+g+(g^*)^{-1},$$
where $(g^*)^{-1}$ is the inverse of $g^*$ in $e^*\Lambda$. Then $v_g\in\mathcal U$, and $v_gv_{g'}=v_{gg'}$.

*Proof.* By (T1) and (T2), $e^*=T_{\iota(O)}$, and $1-e-e^*$, $e$, $e^*$ are orthogonal central idempotents with sum $1$, so $\Lambda=(1-e-e^*)\Lambda\times e\Lambda\times e^*\Lambda$ as rings. The anti-automorphism $*$ maps $e\Lambda$ onto $e^*\Lambda$, so $g^*$ is a unit of $e^*\Lambda$, and $v_g$ is a unit of $\Lambda$, with inverse $v_{g^{-1}}$. Since $(gg')^*=g'^*g^*$, $v_gv_{g'}=v_{gg'}$. Next, $v_g^*=(1-e-e^*)+g^*+g^{-1}$, with $g^{-1}$ the inverse in $e\Lambda$, so
$$v_g^*\delta_0v_g=(1-e-e^*)\delta_0+g^{-1}(e\delta_0)g+g^*(e^*\delta_0)(g^*)^{-1}.$$
The middle term is $e\delta_0$ by hypothesis. Applying $*$ to $g\,e\delta_0=e\delta_0\,g$ and using $(e\delta_0)^*=-e^*\delta_0$ shows that $g^*$ commutes with $e^*\delta_0$, so the last term is $e^*\delta_0$, and the sum is $\delta_0$. Finally, at a level $K'$, $\lambda_{K'}$ is the image of $T_{\{\omega\}}$, which is orthogonal to the images of $e$ and $e^*$, as $O\ne\{\omega\}$ and $\iota(O)\ne\{\omega\}$. The images of $g$ and $(g^*)^{-1}$ lie in those of $e\Lambda$ and $e^*\Lambda$. So $\lambda_{K'}v_g=\lambda_{K'}(1-e-e^*)=\lambda_{K'}$. $\square$

**Lemma B (auxiliary primes; proved here).** For every finite set $S$ of primes there are an odd prime $r\notin S$ and a prime $\ell\notin S$ with $\ell\nmid p(p-1)$ such that $p$ has order exactly $r$ modulo $\ell$. Then $-1\notin\langle p\bmod\ell\rangle$, and every $\psi\in X$ of order $\ell$ has an orbit $O(\psi)$ with $r$ elements.

*Proof.* Let $r$ be an odd prime and $\Phi_r(p)=1+p+\cdots+p^{r-1}$. If a prime $\ell$ divides $\Phi_r(p)$, it divides $p^r-1$, so the order of $p$ modulo $\ell$ is $1$ or $r$; if it is $1$, then $\Phi_r(p)\equiv r\pmod\ell$, so $\ell=r$. Also $\Phi_r(p)>r$. If $r\mid\Phi_r(p)$, then $p\equiv1\pmod r$, as the order of $p$ modulo $r$ divides both $r$ and $r-1$; writing $p=1+tr$, $p^i\equiv1+itr\pmod{r^2}$, so $\Phi_r(p)\equiv r+tr\cdot r(r-1)/2\equiv r\pmod{r^2}$, as $r$ is odd. So $\Phi_r(p)$ is not a power of $r$, and it has a prime factor $\ell_r\ne r$, of which $p$ has order exactly $r$. Then $\ell_r\nmid p$, as $\Phi_r(p)\equiv1\pmod p$, and $\ell_r\nmid p-1$, as the order is not $1$. Different $r$ give different $\ell_r$, so for all but finitely many odd primes $r$, both $r$ and $\ell_r$ lie outside $S$. The group $\langle p\bmod\ell\rangle$ has odd order $r$, so it does not contain $-1$, which has order $2$. Finally $\xi\mapsto\xi^p$ acts on the characters of order $\ell$ as multiplication of exponents by $p$ modulo $\ell$, so orbits have $r$ elements. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.**

*Step 1: commuting with unitary units.* Let $\theta$ induce $\beta=\beta_{u,a}$, $\theta_2$ on $G/V^2$ and $\bar\theta$ on $\bar V$. We run the argument of Step 1 of the proof of part 1 of tame twists, with $\Phi(\mathcal U)$ in place of the group of the $\bar\psi_\lambda$. The classes modulo $\mathrm{Inn}(G/V^2)$ of the $\eta_2\theta_2\eta_2^{-1}$, $\eta\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)$, form a finite set, the image of the finitely many conjugates of the class of $\theta$. Each $\Phi(v)$ is induced by $\theta_v\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)$, so $\Phi(\mathcal U)$ acts on this set by conjugation. As $\Phi$ is an anti-homomorphism, the $v\in\mathcal U$ with $\Phi(v)$ fixing the class of $\theta_2$ form a subgroup $L_0$ of finite index. For $v\in L_0$, $\Phi(v)\theta_2\Phi(v)^{-1}=\theta_2c_y$ for conjugation $c_y$ by some $y\in G/V^2$. Both sides induce $\beta$ on $\mathcal G$, as $\Phi(v)$ induces the identity there, so the image of $y$ in $\mathcal G$ is central, hence trivial. So $y\in\bar V$, and $c_y$ is trivial on $\bar V$. Hence $\Theta_v\bar\theta=\bar\theta\Theta_v$ for $v\in L_0$. Let $N$ be the index in $\mathcal U$ of the normal core of $L_0$. Then $v^N\in L_0$ for every $v\in\mathcal U$, and $\Theta_{v^N}=\Phi(v^N)|_{\bar V}$, so
$$\Theta_{v^N}\bar\theta=\bar\theta\,\Theta_{v^N}\qquad(v\in\mathcal U).\tag{C}$$

By Step 0 of the proof of the surface-generator entry, applied with $\gamma=\sigma^{\pi_p}$, where $\pi_p\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ has component $1$ in $\mathbb Z_p$ and $0$ elsewhere, so that $\omega(\gamma)=1$, together with Lemma 2 there, $\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$ is the set of $w\in\bar V$ with $\rho w=\omega(\rho)w$ for all $\rho\in\mathcal G$. For such $w$, $\rho\bar\theta(w)=\bar\theta(\beta^{-1}(\rho)w)=\omega(\rho)\bar\theta(w)$, as $\omega\circ\beta=\omega$. So $\bar\theta(\mathbb F_p\bar x_0)=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$. Write $\bar\theta(\bar x_1)=c_1\bar x_0+\mu_1\bar x_1$ with $c_1\in\mathbb F_p$ and $\mu_1\in\Lambda$. As $\bar\theta$ is surjective, $\bar x_1=\bar\theta(c\bar x_0+\lambda\bar x_1)$ for some $c,\lambda$, and comparing $\bar x_1$-coordinates gives $\beta(\lambda)\mu_1=1$. The argument at the end of Step 2 of the proof of finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient, in every $\mathbb F_p[\mathcal G/U]$, gives $\mu_1\beta(\lambda)=1$, so $\mu_1\in\Lambda^\times$. Evaluate (C) at $\bar x_1$: the left side is $c_1\bar x_0+\mu_1v^N\bar x_1$, and the right side is $\bar\theta(v^N\bar x_1)=\beta(v^N)\bar\theta(\bar x_1)=\omega(v^N)c_1\bar x_0+\beta(v^N)\mu_1\bar x_1$. Comparing $\bar x_1$-coordinates,
$$\mu_1v^N\mu_1^{-1}=\beta(v)^N\qquad(v\in\mathcal U).\tag{R}$$

*Step 2: orbits are preserved up to $\iota$.* Let $O$ be as in Lemma A and $O'=O^{u^{-1}}$. We show $O'\in\{O,\iota(O)\}$. Put $e=T_O$ and $f=|O|$. By (T4), $g=e\sigma^f$ is a central unit of $e\Lambda$, with inverse $e\sigma^{-f}$, so it commutes with $e\delta_0$, and $v_g\in\mathcal U$ by Lemma A, with $v_g^N=v_{g^N}$. Suppose $O'\notin\{O,\iota(O)\}$. By (T5) and (T2), $\beta(e)=T_{O'}$ and $\beta(e^*)=\beta(e)^*=T_{\iota(O')}$. As $O'\ne O$ and $\iota(O')\ne O$, (T1) gives $eT_{O'}=eT_{\iota(O')}=0$. Now
$$\beta(v_g^N)=(1-T_{O'}-T_{\iota(O')})+\beta(g^N)+\beta\big((g^*)^{-N}\big),$$
with $\beta(g^N)\in T_{O'}\Lambda$ and $\beta((g^*)^{-N})\in T_{\iota(O')}\Lambda$, so $e\beta(v_g^N)=e$. Also $ev_g^N=g^N$. Multiplying (R) for $v=v_g$ by the central idempotent $e$ gives $\mu_1g^N\mu_1^{-1}=e$, so $g^N=e$, that is, $e\sigma^{fN}=e$.

This is false. Choose $m$ prime to $p$ and divisible by $p-1$ and by the orders of all $\xi\in O$, and $n$ divisible by $k_m$ with $n\nmid fN$. In $\Lambda_{m,n}$, the image $\bar e$ of $e$ is a nonzero element of $A_m$ by (T6), and the image of $e\sigma^{fN}$ is $\bar e\bar\sigma^j$ with $0<j<n$ and $j\equiv fN\pmod n$. As $\Lambda_{m,n}=\bigoplus_{j<n}A_m\bar\sigma^j$ is free over $A_m$, $\bar e\bar\sigma^j\ne\bar e$. So $O'\in\{O,\iota(O)\}$.

*Step 3: the tame twist.* Let $m\ge1$ be prime to $p$. Apply Lemma B with $S$ the set of primes dividing $m$, giving $r$ and $\ell$. Choose $\xi_0\in X$ of order $m$, which exists as $I/I_m$ is cyclic of order $m$, and $\psi\in X$ of order $\ell$, and put $\xi=\xi_0\psi$. Put $M=m(p-1)$, which is prime to $\ell$; raising to the power $M$ kills $\xi_0$ and $\omega|_I$, and is injective on characters of order $\ell$.

- (i) *$O(\psi)$ and $O(\xi)$ satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma A.* Let $\varphi\in\{\psi,\xi\}$. If $\varphi^{p^i}=\omega\varphi^{-p^j}$, raising to the power $M$ gives $\psi^{Mp^i}=\psi^{-Mp^j}$, so $\psi^{p^i+p^j}=1$ and $p^i\equiv-p^j\pmod\ell$, which puts $-1$ in $\langle p\bmod\ell\rangle$, contrary to Lemma B. So $O(\varphi)\cap\iota(O(\varphi))=\emptyset$. Also $\varphi^M=\psi^M\ne1$, while $1^M=\omega^M=1$, so $O(\varphi)\notin\{\{1\},\{\omega\}\}$.
- (ii) By Step 2 for $O(\psi)$, $\psi^{u^{-1}}\in O(\psi)\cup\iota(O(\psi))$. An element $\omega\psi^{-p^j}$ of $\iota(O(\psi))$ has $\ell$-th power $\omega^\ell\ne1$, as $\omega|_I$ has order $p-1\ge2$, which does not divide $\ell$. As $(\psi^{u^{-1}})^\ell=1$, $\psi^{u^{-1}}=\psi^{p^k}$ for some $k$.
- (iii) By Step 2 for $O(\xi)$, $\xi^{u^{-1}}\in O(\xi)\cup\iota(O(\xi))$. If $\xi^{u^{-1}}=\omega\xi^{-p^j}$, raising to the power $M$ gives $\psi^{u^{-1}}=\psi^{-p^j}$, and with (ii), $\psi^{p^k+p^j}=1$, which is excluded as in (i). So $\xi^{u^{-1}}=\xi^{p^i}$ for some $i$. Raising to the power $\ell$, which is prime to $m$, gives $\xi_0^{u^{-1}}=\xi_0^{p^i}$, and as $\xi_0(\tau)$ has order $m$, $u^{-1}\equiv p^i\pmod m$.

So $u\bmod m\in\langle p\bmod m\rangle$ for every $m$ prime to $p$. As in Step 2 of the proof of part 1 of tame twists, the closure $\overline{\langle p\rangle}$ in $\widehat{\mathbb Z}'^\times$ is the intersection of the inverse images of the subgroups $\langle p\bmod m\rangle$, so $u\in\overline{\langle p\rangle}$. By the Setting of tame twists, $\overline{\langle p\rangle}=\{p^\nu:\nu\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}\}$, which proves the first assertion.

*Step 4: the Frobenius shift.* Write $u=p^\nu$ and choose $g_1\in G$ mapping to $\sigma^{-\nu}$. By the Setting of finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient (*Inner automorphisms*), $\mathrm{inn}(g_1)$ induces conjugation by $\sigma^{-\nu}$, whose tame twist is $p^{-\nu}$. So $\theta_1=\mathrm{inn}(g_1)\circ\theta$ has tame twist $1$ by the composition rule, and induces $\beta_{1,a}$ for some $a\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$. It represents the class of $\theta$, so Step 1 applies to it; we keep the notation $N$, $\mu_1$ and (R), now with $\beta=\beta_{1,a}$. We show $a\in(p-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$.

Apply Lemma B with $S$ the set of primes dividing $p-1$, giving an odd prime $r\nmid p-1$ and $\ell$. Choose $\psi\in X$ of order $\ell$ and put $\xi=\omega|_I\cdot\psi$. As $\omega^p=\omega$, $O=O(\xi)=\{\omega\psi^{p^i}:0\le i<r\}$ has $r$ elements. It satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma A, by the argument of Step 3 (i) with $\xi_0=\omega|_I$ and $m=p-1$. Put $e=T_O$. As the tame twist is $1$, $\beta(e)=e$ by (T5).

- (a) *The ring $R$.* By (T4), $e\sigma^r$ is central in $e\Lambda$. Let $R$ be the closure of the span of the $e\sigma^{rk}$, $k\in\mathbb Z$. It is a closed commutative subring of $e\Lambda$ with identity $e$, contained in the centre of $e\Lambda$, which is closed.
- (b) *$\beta$ on $R$.* By the formula in Step 0 (iii) of the proof of finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient, with $q=p$, $\beta(\sigma^r)=(\sigma\tau^a)^r=\sigma^r\tau^{as_r}$ with $s_r=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}p^{-i}\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$. As $p\equiv1\pmod{p-1}$, $s_r\equiv r\pmod{p-1}$. As $(1-p^{-1})s_r=1-p^{-r}\equiv0\pmod\ell$ and $p\not\equiv1\pmod\ell$, $s_r\equiv0\pmod\ell$. So for every $\omega\psi^{p^i}\in O$, $(\omega\psi^{p^i})(\tau)^{as_r}=\omega(\tau)^{ar}$, and (T3) gives $e\tau^{as_r}=ce$ with $c=\omega(\tau)^{ar}\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Hence $\beta(e\sigma^r)=e\sigma^r\tau^{as_r}=(e\sigma^r)(e\tau^{as_r})=c\,e\sigma^r$, so $\beta(e\sigma^{rk})=c^ke\sigma^{rk}$ for $k\in\mathbb Z$, and $\beta(R)\subseteq R$ by continuity.
- (c) *A $\beta$-fixed topologically nilpotent element.* Let $\gamma_0=\sigma^{r\pi_p}$. As in Lemma 1 (c) of the surface-generator entry, it generates the closed subgroup $\{\sigma^y:y\in r\pi_p\widehat{\mathbb Z}\}\cong\mathbb Z_p$, which is infinite and pro-$p$, so Lemma 2 there applies to it. Choose integers $n_j\to\pi_p$ in $\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Then $e\gamma_0=\lim(e\sigma^r)^{n_j}\in R$, and $\beta(e\gamma_0)=\lim c^{n_j}(e\sigma^r)^{n_j}=e\gamma_0$, because $c^{p-1}=1$ and $n_j\equiv\pi_p\equiv0\pmod{p-1}$ for large $j$. Put $\eta=e\gamma_0-e=(\gamma_0-1)e\in R$. Then $\beta(\eta)=\eta$, and $\eta^{p^j}=(\gamma_0^{p^j}-1)e\to0$, as $\gamma_0^{p^j}\to1$.
- (d) *$\beta$ is the identity on $R$.* Let $z\in R$. The open two-sided ideals $\ker(\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p[\mathcal G/U])$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of $0$, and $\eta^n$ lies in any of them for large $n$. As $\eta$ and $z$ commute, $(\eta z)^n=\eta^nz^n\to0$, so $1+\eta z$ is a unit of $\Lambda$ with inverse $s=\sum_{k\ge0}(-\eta z)^k$, and $g=e+\eta z$ is a unit of $R$ with inverse $es$. It is central in $e\Lambda$, so $v_g\in\mathcal U$ by Lemma A. Multiply (R) for $v=v_g$ by $e$. On the left, $e\mu_1v_g^N\mu_1^{-1}=\mu_1g^N\mu_1^{-1}=(e\mu_1)g^N(e\mu_1^{-1})=g^N$, as $g^N$ is central in $e\Lambda$. On the right, $e\beta(v_g^N)=\beta(ev_g^N)=\beta(g^N)$, as $\beta(e)=e$. So $(e+\eta z)^N=(e+\eta\beta(z))^N$, with $\beta(z)\in R$ by (b) and $\beta(\eta)=\eta$. Adding the orthogonal idempotent $1-e$ gives $(1+\eta z)^N=(1+\eta\beta(z))^N$ in $\Lambda^\times$. Write $N=p^bN'$ with $p\nmid N'$. As $\eta$, $z$ and $\beta(z)$ commute and $\Lambda$ has characteristic $p$, $w=(1+\eta z)^{p^b}=1+\eta^{p^b}z^{p^b}$ and $w'=(1+\eta\beta(z))^{p^b}=1+\eta^{p^b}\beta(z)^{p^b}$. Their $p^j$-th powers tend to $1$ by (c), and $w^{N'}=w'^{N'}$, so $w=w'$ by the Lemma of finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient. Thus $\eta^{p^b}y=0$ for $y=z^{p^b}-\beta(z)^{p^b}\in e\Lambda$, that is, $(\gamma_0-1)^{p^b}y=0$, and Lemma 2 of the surface-generator entry, applied $p^b$ times, gives $y=0$. So $(z-\beta(z))^{p^b}=0$. The element $z-\beta(z)$ is central in $e\Lambda$, so it commutes with every $\lambda\in\Lambda_I$: $(z-\beta(z))\lambda=(z-\beta(z))e\lambda=e\lambda(z-\beta(z))=\lambda(z-\beta(z))$. Lemma 3 of the surface-generator entry, applied in turn to $(z-\beta(z))^{p^{b-1}},\dots,z-\beta(z)$, gives $z=\beta(z)$.
- (e) *Conclusion.* For $z=e\sigma^r$, (b) and (d) give $c\,e\sigma^r=e\sigma^r$, so $(c-1)e=0$ after multiplying by $e\sigma^{-r}$, and $c=1$ as $e\ne0$. So $\omega(\tau)^{ar}=1$. As $\omega(\tau)$ has order $p-1$, $p-1$ divides $ar$, and as $r$ is prime to $p-1$, $a\in(p-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$.

By the Setting of finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient (*Inner automorphisms*, with $q=p$), $\beta_{1,a}$ is conjugation by $\tau^t$ for some $t\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$. For $g_2\in G$ mapping to $\tau^{-t}$, $\mathrm{inn}(g_2g_1)\circ\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathcal G$. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** (a) The theorem gives the representative. If $\alpha$ and $\mathrm{inn}(g)\circ\alpha$ both induce the identity on $\mathcal G$, then conjugation by the image of $g$ in $\mathcal G$ is the identity, so that image is central, hence trivial, and $g\in V$.

(b) Let $\alpha$ be as in (a). As $x^r=1$, $\alpha^r=\mathrm{inn}(g)$ for some $g\in G$, and $g\in V$ by the argument of (a). We follow the proof of the corollary of finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient: $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ is profinite, the closure of $\langle\alpha^r\rangle$ lies in the pro-$p$ group $\mathrm{Inn}(V)$, and for $\pi'\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ with component $0$ in $\mathbb Z_p$ and $1$ in every other $\mathbb Z_\ell$, $\alpha'=\alpha^{\pi'}$ satisfies $(\alpha')^r=(\alpha^r)^{\pi'}=1$. As $p\nmid r$, $\pi'\equiv1\pmod r$, so $\alpha'$ represents $x^{\pi'}=x$. It lies in the closure of $\langle\alpha\rangle$, so it induces the identity on $\mathcal G$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* In degree at least $3$, the proof of finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient uses an automorphism for every unit of $\Lambda$, acting on a surface pair. $G_{\mathbb Q_p}$ has no surface pair, and the automorphisms available act on $\bar x_1$ through the unitary group $\mathcal U$ of the form $\delta_0$, about which little is known. Lemma A needs only $\delta_0^*=-\delta_0$: on a block $T_O$ with $O\ne\iota(O)$, every unit commuting with $T_O\delta_0$, paired with its adjoint inverse on $T_{\iota(O)}$, is unitary. Central units of the blocks therefore suffice. They show that the tame twist permutes the orbits up to $\iota$ (Step 2). Characters of an order $\ell$ with $-1\notin\langle p\bmod\ell\rangle$ remove this ambiguity (Step 3). The Frobenius shift is read on the block of $\omega\psi$, whose centre contains $T_O\sigma^r$ with $r$ prime to $p-1$. The pro-$p$ element $\eta$ removes the uncontrolled exponent $N$, as $\eta_m$ does in degree at least $3$.
- *What remains for $\mathbb Q_p$.* The later layers of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535), from wild Frattini to the higher Johnson maps, use Kondo's automorphisms or units on a surface pair, so none of them is known for $\mathbb Q_p$. Corollary (b) reduces finite-order classes of order prime to $p$ with finitely many conjugates to automorphisms of finite order trivial on $\mathcal G$; unlike degree at least $3$, it is not shown here that these are trivial. Automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb Q_p}$ of finite order prime to $p$ trivial on $\mathcal G$ do exist ([finite abelian subgroups](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md)), so the question is whether one of them can have finitely many outer conjugates.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$ and $K=\mathbb Q_p$ only. Degree $2$ and $p=2$ are not covered: the automorphisms $\theta_v$ are recorded here only for $\mathbb Q_p$, and in degree $2$ the generator $x_1$ occurs in both $x_1^{p^s}$ and the commutator. Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
