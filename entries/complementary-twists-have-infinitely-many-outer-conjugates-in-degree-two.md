**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$. Use the Setting and notation of [the degree-two abelianized wild inertia theorem](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia-in-degree-two.md), called the abelianized entry below, which takes them from [the degree-two Frattini theorem](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-wild-frattini-in-degree-two.md), called the Frattini entry. So $G=G_K=F/(\varrho)$ with $F=P\rtimes\mathcal G$, wild inertia $P_K$ is the image of $P$, $\Gamma=G/P_K$ is identified with $\mathcal G$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$ with augmentation $\mathrm{aug}$, $\omega:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ is the action on $\mu_p$, $\lambda\mapsto\lambda^*$ is the continuous anti-automorphism with $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, and
$$\varrho=R_0\,w,\qquad R_0=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa,\qquad w=x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2].$$
Also $H=P_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, with $\xi_i$ the class of $x_i$, $\mathcal L_2=(P_K)_2/(P_K)_3$, $V=H/pH=L\oplus\Lambda\bar x_1\oplus\Lambda\bar x_2$ with $L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$, and $\mathcal L_2(V)=\mathcal L_2/p$. An automorphism is tame-trivial if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$. For an automorphism $\theta$ acting trivially on $H$, $J_1(\theta):H\to\mathcal L_2$ is its degree-one Johnson map, with the rules recalled in the Setting of the Frattini entry: $J_1(\theta\theta')=J_1(\theta)+J_1(\theta')$, $J_1(\beta\theta\beta^{-1})=\beta_2J_1(\theta)\beta_H^{-1}$, and $J_1(c_g)=\mathrm{ad}(g(P_K)_2)$ for $g\in P_K$, where $\mathrm{ad}(h)=[h,\cdot\,]$. Let $j:H\to\mathcal L_2$ be the continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant homomorphism of Step 1 of the Frattini entry, with $j(\xi_0)=j(\xi_1)=0$ and $j(\xi_2)=[\xi_1,\xi_2]$. Its reduction $\bar\jmath:V\to\mathcal L_2(V)$ is $\Lambda$-linear and kills $L+\Lambda\bar x_1$.

*Scaling units.* Put $\Lambda^\times_1=\{\mu\in\Lambda^\times:\omega(\mu)=1\}$, a subgroup of $\Lambda^\times$, and $\Gamma_1=\{\rho\in\Gamma:\omega(\rho)=1\}$, the kernel of the character $\omega|_\Gamma$. Distinct elements of $\Gamma$ stay distinct in $\Lambda$, so $\Gamma_1$ is a subgroup of $\Lambda^\times_1$. For each $\mu\in\Lambda^\times_1$ fix an automorphism $\theta_\mu$ of $G$ as in the Lemma of [the degree-two tame-quotient theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-on-the-tame-quotient-in-degree-two.md). It is tame-trivial, as $\theta_\mu(\rho)\in\rho P_K^{(2)}$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$, and its action $\bar\psi_\mu$ on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_0$, and maps $\bar x_1\mapsto\mu\bar x_1$ and $\bar x_2\mapsto(\mu^*)^{-1}\bar x_2$. Moreover $\bar\psi_\lambda\bar\psi_\mu=\bar\psi_{\mu\lambda}$.

**Complementary twists.** For $t\in\mathbb Z_p$, let $T_t$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$, fixes $x_0$, and maps $x_i\mapsto w^tx_iw^{-t}$ for $i=1,2$. For $t=1$ this is the complementary twist $T$ of the Frattini entry.

**Lemma 1 (proved here).**

- (a) $T_t$ induces an automorphism of $G$, again written $T_t$, and $T_tT_{t'}=T_{t+t'}$.
- (b) $T_t$ is tame-trivial and acts trivially on $H$.
- (c) $J_1(T_t)=tp^sj$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$. Let $\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ be tame-trivial and act trivially on $H=P_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, with
$$J_1(\alpha)=a\,j+\mathrm{ad}(h_0)\qquad\text{for some }a\in\mathbb Z_p\setminus\{0\}\text{ and }h_0\in H.$$
If $\mu,\mu'\in\Lambda^\times_1$ and $\theta_\mu\alpha\theta_\mu^{-1}$ and $\theta_{\mu'}\alpha\theta_{\mu'}^{-1}$ have the same class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$, then $\mu\mu'^{-1}\in\Gamma_1$. Consequently the class of $\alpha$ has at least as many conjugates in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ as there are right cosets of $\Gamma_1$ in $\Lambda^\times_1$, and this number is infinite.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$ and $t\in\mathbb Z_p\setminus\{0\}$. Let $D$ be a tame-trivial automorphism of $G_K$ acting trivially on $P_K/(P_K)_3$; examples are $D=1$ and conjugation by an element of $(P_K)_2$. Then the class of $T_tD$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has infinitely many conjugates. In particular $T_t$ is not inner, and $t\mapsto[T_t]$ is an injective homomorphism $\mathbb Z_p\to\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ whose nonzero values are classes of infinite order, trivial on $\Gamma$ and on $P_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, each with infinitely many conjugates.

**Proof of Lemma 1.** (a) As in the Frattini entry, $F$ is free among semidirect products of $\mathcal G$ with pro-$p$ groups, and $w^tx_iw^{-t}$ lies in $P$, so $T_t$ is a well-defined continuous endomorphism fixing $\mathcal G$. On the closed subgroup generated by $x_1,x_2$, $T_t$ and conjugation by $w^t$ are continuous homomorphisms that agree on $x_1,x_2$, so they agree; as $w$ lies in this subgroup, $T_t(w)=w^tww^{-t}=w$, and hence $T_t(w^{t'})=w^{t'}$ for $t'\in\mathbb Z_p$, by continuity. As $R_0$ is built from $\mathcal G$ and $x_0$, $T_t(R_0)=R_0$, so $T_t(\varrho)=\varrho$. For $i=1,2$,
$$T_tT_{t'}(x_i)=T_t(w^{t'}x_iw^{-t'})=w^{t'}w^tx_iw^{-t}w^{-t'}=T_{t+t'}(x_i),$$
and both sides fix $\mathcal G$ and $x_0$, so $T_tT_{t'}=T_{t+t'}$ on $F$. As $T_0=1$, $T_t$ is an automorphism of $F$ with inverse $T_{-t}$. It maps the closed normal subgroup $(\varrho)$ onto itself, so it induces an automorphism of $G$, and the composition rule passes to $G$.

(b) $T_t$ fixes $\mathcal G$, so it is tame-trivial and its action on $H$ commutes with $\Gamma$. It fixes $\xi_0$, and $T_t(x_i)x_i^{-1}=[w^t,x_i]\in(P_K)_2$ for $i=1,2$. As $\xi_0,\xi_1,\xi_2$ topologically generate $H$ as a $\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$-module (Setting of the abelianized entry), $T_t$ acts trivially on $H$.

(c) In $P_K/(P_K)_3$, which has class at most $2$, the commutator induces a biadditive map $H\times H\to\mathcal L_2$, and $[y^t,x]\equiv[y,x]^t$ modulo $(P_K)_3$ for $t\in\mathbb Z_p$, by continuity. For $i=1,2$, $J_1(T_t)(\xi_i)$ is the class of $[w^t,x_i]$, which is $t$ times the class of $[w,x_i]$. As $w=x_1^{p^s}z$ with $z=[x_1,x_2]\in(P_K)_2$ and $[z,x_i]\in(P_K)_3$, the class of $[w,x_i]$ is that of $[x_1^{p^s},x_i]$, which is $p^s[\xi_1,\xi_i]$. So $J_1(T_t)(\xi_1)=0$ and $J_1(T_t)(\xi_2)=tp^s[\xi_1,\xi_2]$, while $J_1(T_t)(\xi_0)=0$ as $T_t$ fixes $x_0$. Since $T_t$ fixes $\mathcal G$, $J_1(T_t)$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant, by the conjugation rule. So $J_1(T_t)$ and $tp^sj$ are continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant homomorphisms that agree on $\xi_0,\xi_1,\xi_2$, and they are equal. $\square$

**Proof of the Theorem.**

*Step 0: removing $h_0$.* Choose $g_0\in P_K$ with class $h_0$ in $H$, and put $\alpha'=\alpha c_{g_0}^{-1}$. It is tame-trivial, acts trivially on $H$, and $J_1(\alpha')=J_1(\alpha)-\mathrm{ad}(h_0)=a\,j$. Also $\theta_\mu\alpha'\theta_\mu^{-1}=\theta_\mu\alpha\theta_\mu^{-1}c_{\theta_\mu(g_0)}^{-1}$, which has the same class as $\theta_\mu\alpha\theta_\mu^{-1}$, and likewise for $\mu'$. So we may assume $h_0=0$.

*Step 1: the Johnson identity.* Suppose that $\theta_\mu\alpha\theta_\mu^{-1}=\theta_{\mu'}\alpha\theta_{\mu'}^{-1}c_{g'}$ with $g'\in G$. Put $\psi=\theta_{\mu'}^{-1}\theta_\mu$ and $\nu=\mu\mu'^{-1}\in\Lambda^\times_1$. Conjugating by $\theta_{\mu'}^{-1}$ gives $\psi\alpha\psi^{-1}=\alpha c_g$ with $g=\theta_{\mu'}^{-1}(g')$. The automorphism $c_g=\alpha^{-1}\psi\alpha\psi^{-1}$ is tame-trivial, so the image of $g$ in $\Gamma$ is central, hence trivial (part 3 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md)), and $g\in P_K$. Both $\alpha$ and $c_g$ act trivially on $H$, and the rules for $J_1$ give
$$a\,\psi_2\,j\,\psi_H^{-1}=J_1(\psi\alpha\psi^{-1})=J_1(\alpha c_g)=a\,j+\mathrm{ad}(\bar g),\qquad\bar g=g(P_K)_2.$$
Write $a=p^ku$ with $k\ge0$ and $u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$. Then $[\bar g,H]\subseteq p^k\mathcal L_2$, and the saturation argument of Step 1 of the Frattini entry, which uses only that $\mathcal L_2$ is torsion-free and that $\mathrm{ad}$ is injective modulo $p$, gives $\bar g=p^kg_k$ with $g_k\in H$ (for $k=0$ take $g_k=\bar g$). As $\mathrm{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ is torsion-free, $\psi_2j\psi_H^{-1}-j=\mathrm{ad}(u^{-1}g_k)$. Reduce modulo $p$. The action $\psi_H$ reduces to the action $\bar\psi$ of $\psi$ on $V$, and $\psi_2$ to $\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)$ (Setting of the Frattini entry). With $h\in V$ the reduction of $u^{-1}g_k$,
$$\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)\,\bar\jmath\,\bar\psi^{-1}-\bar\jmath=\mathrm{ad}(h)\quad\text{on }V.\tag{1}$$
By the composition rule, $\bar\psi=\bar\psi_{\mu'}^{-1}\bar\psi_\mu=\bar\psi_{\mu'^{-1}}\bar\psi_\mu=\bar\psi_{\nu}$, as $\bar\psi_{\mu'^{-1}}\bar\psi_{\mu'}=\bar\psi_1=1$. So $\bar\psi$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_0$, and maps
$$\bar x_1\mapsto\nu\bar x_1,\qquad\bar x_2\mapsto\nu'\bar x_2,\qquad\nu'=(\nu^*)^{-1}.$$

*Step 2: the inner term vanishes.* Let $v\in\Lambda\bar x_1$. Then $\bar\psi^{-1}v\in\Lambda\bar x_1$, and $\bar\jmath$ kills both, so (1) gives $[h,v]=0$. Taking $v=\bar x_1$ and $v=\tau\bar x_1$, the argument of Step 2 of the abelianized entry applies word for word: for every open normal subgroup $U$ of $\Gamma$ with $\tau\notin U$, the image of $h$ in the finite quotient $W_U$ defined there commutes in $W_U\otimes W_U$ with the distinct basis vectors $e_1$ and $\bar\tau e_1$, so it is $0$; these $U$ are cofinal, so $h=0$. Composing (1) with $\bar\psi$ on the right,
$$\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)\,\bar\jmath=\bar\jmath\,\bar\psi.\tag{2}$$

*Step 3: comparison at $\bar x_2$.* Evaluate (2) at $\bar x_2$. As $\bar\jmath(\bar x_2)=[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]$ and $\mathcal L_2(\bar\psi)[v,v']=[\bar\psi v,\bar\psi v']$,
$$[\nu\bar x_1,\nu'\bar x_2]=\bar\jmath(\nu'\bar x_2).$$
Let $U$ be as in Step 2, with $\Gamma_U=\Gamma/U$, $\lambda\mapsto\bar\lambda$ the map $\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p[\Gamma_U]$, and $\pi_U:V\to W_U$ the surjection of the abelianized entry, mapping $\lambda_1\bar x_1+\lambda_2\bar x_2+b\bar x_0\mapsto\bar\lambda_1e_1+\bar\lambda_2e_2+bX_0$, where $W_U$ has basis $X_0$ and $te_1,te_2$ for $t\in\Gamma_U$. Write $\bar\nu=\sum_tn_tt$ and $\bar\nu'=\sum_tn'_tt$. By Step 2 of the Frattini entry, $\mathcal L_2(\pi_U)\bar\jmath(\lambda\bar x_2)=\sum_t\lambda_t[te_1,te_2]$ when $\bar\lambda=\sum_t\lambda_tt$. Applying $\mathcal L_2(\pi_U)$, with $[y,y']=y\otimes y'-y'\otimes y$,
$$\sum_{t,t'\in\Gamma_U}n_tn'_{t'}\,[te_1,t'e_2]=\sum_{t\in\Gamma_U}n'_t\,[te_1,te_2].$$
The basis tensor $te_1\otimes t'e_2$ occurs on the left only in $[te_1,t'e_2]$, with coefficient $n_tn'_{t'}$, and on the right only when $t'=t$, with coefficient $n'_t$. So
$$n_t\,n'_{t'}=\delta_{tt'}\,n'_t\qquad(t,t'\in\Gamma_U).$$
Sum over $t'$. For $\rho\in\Gamma$, $\mathrm{aug}(\rho^*)=\omega(\rho)$, so $\mathrm{aug}(\lambda^*)=\omega(\lambda)$ for all $\lambda\in\Lambda$ by linearity and continuity. Hence $\mathrm{aug}(\nu^*)=\omega(\nu)=1$ and $\sum_{t'}n'_{t'}=\mathrm{aug}(\nu')=1$, as $\mathrm{aug}$ is a ring homomorphism. This gives $n_t=n'_t$ for every $t$, so $\bar\nu=\bar\nu'$, and the identity becomes $n_tn_{t'}=\delta_{tt'}n_t$. So $n_tn_{t'}=0$ for $t\ne t'$ and $n_t^2=n_t$. As $\bar\nu$ is a unit, it is nonzero, so $\bar\nu=t_U$ for a unique $t_U\in\Gamma_U$. As in Step 2 of the Frattini entry, for $U'\subseteq U$ in this family $t_{U'}$ maps to $t_U$, because distinct elements of $\Gamma_U$ are linearly independent in $\mathbb F_p[\Gamma_U]$; so the $t_U$ define $\rho\in\Gamma=\varprojlim\Gamma/U$ with $\bar\rho=\bar\nu$ for cofinally many $U$, and $\nu=\rho$. As $\omega(\rho)=\omega(\nu)=1$, $\mu\mu'^{-1}=\nu\in\Gamma_1$.

*Step 4: counting.* As $\Gamma_1$ is a subgroup of $\Lambda^\times_1$, the relation $\mu\mu'^{-1}\in\Gamma_1$ says that $\mu$ and $\mu'$ lie in the same right coset $\Gamma_1\mu=\Gamma_1\mu'$. By Step 3, elements of different right cosets give different classes $[\theta_\mu\alpha\theta_\mu^{-1}]$, and these are conjugates of the class of $\alpha$. Let $\lambda=1+c(\gamma-1)$ be the unit of Step 4 of the abelianized entry, with $c\in\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0,1\}$ and $\gamma=\sigma^{\pi_p}$. There it is shown that $\lambda\in\Lambda^\times_1$ and $(\lambda^n)^*\lambda^n\ne1$ for every $n\ge1$. Every $\rho\in\Gamma_1$ has $\rho^*\rho=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}\rho=1$, so $\lambda^n\notin\Gamma_1$ for $n\ge1$. If $\Gamma_1\lambda^i=\Gamma_1\lambda^{i'}$ with $0\le i<i'$, then $\lambda^{i'-i}=\lambda^{i'}(\lambda^{i})^{-1}\in\Gamma_1$, which is impossible. So the cosets $\Gamma_1\lambda^i$, $i\ge0$, are pairwise distinct, and the class of $\alpha$ has infinitely many conjugates. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** As $D$ acts trivially on $P_K/(P_K)_3$, it acts trivially on $H$ and $J_1(D)=0$. Conjugation by $g\in(P_K)_2$ is such a $D$, since $gxg^{-1}x^{-1}\in[(P_K)_2,P_K]=(P_K)_3$ for $x\in P_K$ and $c_g$ is tame-trivial. By Lemma 1, $\alpha=T_tD$ is tame-trivial, acts trivially on $H$, and $J_1(\alpha)=tp^sj$. The Theorem with $a=tp^s\ne0$ and $h_0=0$ shows that its class has infinitely many conjugates. An inner automorphism has a single outer conjugate, so $T_t$ is not inner for $t\ne0$. By Lemma 1 (a), $t\mapsto[T_t]$ is a homomorphism, and its kernel is $0$ as $T_t$ is not inner for $t\ne0$, so it is injective and $[T_t]$ has infinite order for $t\ne0$. Each $T_t$ fixes $\mathcal G$ and acts trivially on $H$ by Lemma 1 (b). $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* This answers [research issue 634](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/634) along the route proposed there. In even degree at least $4$, [the higher-degree theorem](complementary-twists-have-infinitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) separates conjugates with the additive family of mixing automorphisms of a surface pair and a contraction of $\mathcal L_2(V)$. In degree $2$ there is no surface pair. The scaling units act diagonally on $\bar x_1,\bar x_2$, so the Johnson identity still vanishes on the whole summand $\Lambda\bar x_1$, which kills the inner term. At $\bar x_2$ it says that $\nu\otimes(\nu^*)^{-1}$ equals $\Delta((\nu^*)^{-1})$, where $\Delta(\rho)=\rho\otimes\rho$ on tame group elements, in every finite quotient. Augmentation on the second factor gives $\nu=(\nu^*)^{-1}$, as the issue proposed, and then $\nu$ is a group element of $\Gamma_1$. No positive power of the unipotent unit of the abelianized entry lies in $\Gamma_1$. Whether $\theta_\rho$ with $\rho\in\Gamma_1$ actually fixes the class of $\alpha$ is not decided here.
- *Another representative.* In $G$ the relation $\varrho=1$ gives $w=R_0^{-1}$. As $T_t$ fixes $w$, the automorphism $T_tc_{w^{-t}}$ maps $y\mapsto w^{-t}T_t(y)w^t$. It fixes $x_1$ and $x_2$, and conjugates $x_0$ and every element of $\mathcal G$ by $R_0^t$. It has the same outer class as $T_t$, so for $t\ne0$ it too is not inner and has infinitely many outer conjugates.
- *What is not covered.* The Theorem concerns only automorphisms trivial on $\Gamma$ and $H$ whose degree-one Johnson map is a nonzero multiple of $j$ up to an inner term. For a tame-trivial $\alpha$ with finitely many outer conjugates, which acts trivially on $H$ by the abelianized entry, it is not decided here whether the cocycle $\Gamma\to H$ of $\alpha$ is a coboundary, what $J_1(\alpha)$ can be, or whether $\alpha$ is inner. Also not covered: $K=\mathbb Q_p$, where there is no second complementary generator, and $p=2$.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$ and every quadratic $K/\mathbb Q_p$, ramified or unramified, with every $s$, including $K=\mathbb Q_3(\mu_3)$.
- *Sources.* Everything is proved here from the linked entries on `main`, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
