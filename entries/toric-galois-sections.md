**Virtually toric gerbes.** Let $k$ be a field. An affine group scheme of finite type over $k$ is *virtually toric* (vt) if it is an extension of a finite étale group scheme by a connected group scheme of multiplicative type. An affine gerbe over $k$ is vt if the automorphism groups of its geometric points are vt, and pro-vt groups and gerbes are projective limits of vt ones [@bresciani2024toric, §3.4, p. 11].

**Definition.** A fibered category $X$ over $k$ is *firm* if it is concentrated, geometrically reduced and $H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = k$. For instance, a proper, geometrically connected, geometrically reduced scheme is firm [@bresciani2024toric, Definition 3.2, p. 10]. The *toric fundamental gerbe* of a firm $X$ is a pro-vt gerbe $\Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ with a morphism $X \to \Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ that is universal among morphisms from $X$ to vt gerbes. In Borne and Vistoli's terminology, it is the fundamental gerbe for the class of vt groups [@bresciani2024toric, Definition 3.4, p. 11].

Write $\Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k} = \varprojlim_i \Gamma_i$ as a cofiltered limit of affine algebraic gerbes. The space of *toric Galois sections* is the set of quasi-sections
$$\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k} = \Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}(k)_{\mathrm{q}} = \varprojlim_i \Gamma_i(k)/\!\sim,$$
the projective limit of the sets of isomorphism classes of $k$-points [@bresciani2024toric, Definition 2.4, p. 6, and Definition 3.7, p. 11]. The *toric Kummer map* $X(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ evaluates $X \to \Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ on $k$-points [@bresciani2024toric, §1.2, p. 2].

**The étale case.** Using finite étale groups instead gives the étale fundamental gerbe $\Pi^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$. For a quasi-compact, quasi-separated scheme $X$, the isomorphism classes of $k$-points of $\Pi^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$, equivalently its quasi-sections, are the usual Galois sections of $1 \to \pi_1(X_{\bar k}) \to \pi_1(X) \to G_k \to 1$ up to conjugation. Evaluating $X \to \Pi^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ on $k$-points gives the usual map from points to sections [@bresciani2024toric, §3.1, p. 9, and §3.2, p. 9]. Finite étale groups are vt, so there is a natural morphism $\Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k} \to \Pi^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$, which induces a map $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k} \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 3.11, p. 12].

**Why quasi-sections.** For profinite gerbes, quasi-sections and isomorphism classes of sections coincide [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 2.5, p. 7]. For pro-algebraic gerbes they need not. For the $\ell$-adic solenoid $\mathbb{S}_\ell = \varprojlim(\cdots \xrightarrow{\ell} \mathbb{G}_m \xrightarrow{\ell} \mathbb{G}_m)$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the classifying gerbe has a single quasi-section but non-trivial $H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p, \mathbb{S}_\ell)$ [@bresciani2024toric, Example 3.10, p. 12]. Bresciani chooses quasi-sections because, for curves of genus at least $1$ in characteristic $0$, they map injectively to étale sections, while the fibres of the corresponding map on isomorphism classes can be huge [@bresciani2024toric, Remark 3.9, p. 12].

**Tannakian description.** Let $X$ be smooth, projective and geometrically connected over $k$, of dimension $n \ge 1$, with an ample divisor $H$. For a line bundle $L$ on a finite étale cover $f : Y \to X$, put $d_H(L) = \deg_k(c_1(L) \cdot (f^*H)^{n-1})$. A vector bundle $V$ on $X$ is a *pure cactus of slope* $q \in \mathbb{Q}$ if, for some connected finite étale cover $f : Y \to X$, $f^*V$ is a direct sum of line bundles $L_i$ with $d_H(L_i) = \deg(Y/X) \cdot q$. A *cactus* is a vector bundle with a finite grading $V = \bigoplus_q V_q$ by pure cactuses of slope $q$, and morphisms of cactuses preserve the grading [@bresciani2024toric, Definition 12.1, p. 39]. For curves, $d_H$ is the ordinary degree and $H$ is not needed [@bresciani2024toric, §12, p. 39]. Cactuses form a Tannakian category [@bresciani2024toric, Proposition 12.3, p. 40], and $\Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ is its gerbe of fibre functors [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 12.5, p. 41]. A toric Galois section is then a *quasi-fibre functor*: a compatible choice, for each finitely generated Tannakian subcategory, of an isomorphism class of fibre functors on it [@bresciani2024toric, Definition 12.6, p. 41]. Bresciani calls this section a sketch, and the rest of his paper does not depend on it [@bresciani2024toric, §12, p. 39].

**Structure in characteristic $0$.** Let $X$ be a firm scheme over a field $k$ of characteristic $0$.

- The morphism $\Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k} \to \Pi^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ is a relative abelian gerbe banded by a relative connected pro-multiplicative group. So the toric fundamental group is an extension of the étale one by a connected pro-multiplicative group $T$ [@bresciani2024toric, Proposition 5.7, p. 18].
- If $\pi_1(X_{\bar k})$ is trivial, then $\Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ is abelian, banded by a connected pro-multiplicative group $T$ with $\underline{\mathrm{Pic}}_X(k) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(T, \mathbb{G}_m)$ [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 5.6, p. 18].
- $X$ has *virtually divisible line bundles* if, for every finite étale cover $Y \to X$, line bundle $L$ on $Y$ and integer $n \ge 1$, some finite étale cover $Z \to Y$ carries a line bundle $M$ with $L|_Z \cong M^{\otimes n}$ [@bresciani2024toric, Definition 5.1, p. 16]. The group $T$ is torsion free exactly when $X$ has this property [@bresciani2024toric, Proposition 5.7, p. 18]. Smooth projective curves of genus at least $1$ have it [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 5.2, p. 17], and so do torsors under abelian varieties [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 5.3, p. 17].

**Comparison with étale sections.** An *étale neighbourhood* of $s \in \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ is a geometrically connected finite étale cover $Y \to X$ with a section $r \in \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{Y/k}$ mapping to $s$ [@bresciani2024toric, §7, p. 23]. The *relative Brauer group* of a firm $X$ is $\mathrm{Br}(X/k) = \ker(\mathrm{Br}(k) \to H^2_{\mathrm{fppf}}(X, \mathbb{G}_m))$ [@bresciani2024toric, §3.4, p. 11].

1. For any firm $X$ over any field $k$, if $\mathrm{Br}(X/k) \ne 0$ then $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k} = \varnothing$ [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 3.8, p. 11].
2. If $k$ has characteristic $0$ and the firm scheme $X$ has virtually divisible line bundles, then $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k} \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ is injective [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 5.10, p. 20]. Its image consists of the étale sections $s$ such that $\mathrm{Br}(Y_{k'}/k') = 0$ for every étale neighbourhood $Y \to X$ of $s$ and every finite extension $k'/k$ [@bresciani2024toric, Proposition 7.5, p. 24].

So toric sections are the étale sections that never meet a Brauer obstruction, on any étale neighbourhood or after any finite extension of $k$.

**Example: Brauer–Severi varieties.** Let $P$ be a Brauer–Severi variety of positive dimension over $k$ with $P(k) = \varnothing$. Then $\Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{P/k}$ is of finite type, abelian and banded by $\mathbb{G}_m$, and it has no $k$-point [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 6.2, p. 22], so $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{P/k} = \varnothing$ [@bresciani2024toric, Corollary 6.3 and its proof, p. 22]. On the other hand, $P_{\bar k}$ is a projective space, which is simply connected, so $\pi_1(P) \to G_k$ is an isomorphism and $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{P/k}$ has exactly one element. Here the toric sections see the Brauer obstruction to a rational point, while the étale section does not.

**Base change.** Formation of $\Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ commutes with algebraic separable extensions of $k$ [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 3.5, p. 11], but not with arbitrary ones. Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$, and let $\tilde E = \varprojlim E$ be its universal cover, the limit of copies of $E$ along multiplication maps. Over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, the band of the toric fundamental gerbe of $\tilde E$ has character group $\mathbb{Q} \oplus E(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})/E[\infty]$, which is countable. Over $\mathbb{C}$ it has character group $\mathbb{Q} \oplus E(\mathbb{C})/E[\infty]$, which is not. So the gerbe over $\mathbb{C}$ is not the base change of the gerbe over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@bresciani2024toric, Example 3.6, p. 11]. The étale fundamental gerbe, by contrast, commutes with arbitrary extensions in characteristic $0$ [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 3.1, p. 9, and p. 11].

**The toric section conjecture.** For $k$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ and $X$ a smooth projective curve of genus at least $2$ over $k$, Bresciani conjectures that the toric Kummer map $X(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ is bijective. By item 2, Grothendieck's [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) implies it [@bresciani2024toric, Conjecture 1.2 and the paragraph after it, p. 2]. The converse holds up to a Brauer condition. For a field $k_0$ of characteristic $0$, the following are equivalent [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 7.6, p. 25]:

- for every smooth projective curve $X$ of genus at least $2$ over a finite extension $k$ of $k_0$, the map $X(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ is bijective;
- for every such $X$ and $k$, the map $X(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ is bijective, and $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k} = \varnothing$ whenever $\mathrm{Br}(X/k) \ne 0$.

Over $p$-adic fields the toric conjecture is a theorem; see [the toric section theorem over $p$-adic fields](toric-section-conjecture-over-p-adic-fields.md).
