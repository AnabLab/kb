**Setting.** Let $k$ be a field finitely generated over $\mathbb{F}_p$, with algebraic closure $\bar k$. For a $k$-scheme $X$ and $m \ge 0$, the *Frobenius twist* $X(m)$ is the base change of $X$ along the $p^m$-th power map of $k$, and the relative, or geometric, Frobenius is a $k$-morphism $X \to X(m)$ [@stix2002affine, §3, p. 5], [@tsujimura2024positive, Definition 4.1, p. 24]. Frobenius twists do not change the étale or tame fundamental groups [@tsujimura2024positive, Introduction, pp. 2–3]. A curve $X$ over $k$ is *isotrivial* if $X_{\bar k}$ is defined over a finite field [@stix2002affine, §3, p. 5]. Curves are [hyperbolic](hyperbolic-curve.md) in the usual sense [@stix2002thesis, Definition 5.0.1, p. 71].

**Why the usual form fails.** For a hyperbolic curve $X$ over $k$, there are $m \neq m'$ with $X(m) \cong X(m')$ over $k$ exactly when $X$ is isotrivial [@stix2002thesis, Corollary B.2.4, p. 106]. So the twists of a nonisotrivial curve are pairwise non-isomorphic over $k$, although they all have the same fundamental group over $G_k$, and the usual isomorphism form of the conjecture cannot hold [@stix2002thesis, Introduction, p. vii]. Stix's example is that the twist of $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \lambda, \infty\}$ is $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \lambda^p, \infty\}$ [@stix2002affine, §3, p. 5]. He therefore formally inverts the geometric Frobenius maps, and writes $\mathrm{Isom}_{k, F^{-1}}$ for isomorphisms in the resulting category. He describes this category through the perfect closures of the function fields with their places at infinity [@stix2002affine, §3, p. 5].

**Theorem (Stix).** Let $X, X'$ be hyperbolic curves over $k$, at least one of them not isotrivial. Then $\pi_1$ induces a bijection of finite sets
$$\mathrm{Isom}_{k, F^{-1}}(X, X') \to \mathrm{Isom}_{G_k}\bigl(\pi_1(X_{\bar k}), \pi_1(X'_{\bar k})\bigr)$$
[@stix2002thesis, Theorem 5.1.1, p. 71]. The right side consists of the isomorphisms of the outer Galois representations $G_k \to \mathrm{Out}(\pi_1(X_{\bar k}))$ and $G_k \to \mathrm{Out}(\pi_1(X'_{\bar k}))$ [@stix2002affine, §3, p. 4]. In particular, the two representations are isomorphic exactly when $X(m) \cong X'(m')$ over $k$ for some $m, m' \ge 0$ [@stix2002thesis, Corollary 5.1.2, p. 71].

- *Affine curves.* For affine hyperbolic curves, at least one not isotrivial, the same holds with tame fundamental groups [@stix2002affine, Theorem 3.2, p. 5]. That theorem allows any field finitely generated over its prime field, and Stix presents it as generalizing Tamagawa's theorem, which treats finite fields and fields of characteristic $0$ [@stix2002affine, §1, p. 1]. The thesis proves the proper case, and deduces the affine case for the full fundamental group from the tame one [@stix2002thesis, Theorem 5.1.3 and the Remark before it, p. 72].
- *Isotrivial curves.* If both affine curves are isotrivial, the map for tame groups is injective with dense image [@stix2002affine, Theorem 3.3, p. 5]. Stix explains that the geometric side then carries an action of $\mathbb{Z}$, generated by a power of Frobenius, and the group side one of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, the Galois group of a finite field. For affine curves, Tamagawa's theorem essentially gives a bijection on orbits. He remarks that for affine hyperbolic curves the criterion with twists of Corollary 5.1.2 also remains unchanged [@stix2002thesis, Remark (1) after Corollary 5.1.2, p. 72]. The remark says nothing about isotrivial proper curves, and this entry does not claim the criterion for them.

**Proof outline (Stix).**

1. *Rigidity of Frobenius.* Let $X, Y$ be of finite type over $\mathbb{Z}$, with $X$ irreducible and reduced, and let $f, g : X \to Y$ agree on underlying topological spaces. Then $f = g$, or $X$ lies over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $f$, $g$ differ by a power of Frobenius. The proof counts points over finite fields with the Hasse–Weil bound [@stix2002affine, Proposition 2.3 and its proof, pp. 2–3].
2. *Specialization.* Spread $X, X'$ out over a base $S$ of finite type over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with function field $k$, with level-$n$ structures for some $n \ge 3$, so that they are classified by maps $\xi_X, \xi_{X'}$ to a fine moduli scheme. At each closed point, [Tamagawa's theorem](tamagawa-grothendieck-conjecture-for-affine-curves.md) over the finite residue field recovers the fibre, but only up to a Frobenius twist that may depend on the point. So $\xi_X$ and $\xi_{X'}$ agree topologically, and step 1 gives a single twist [@stix2002affine, Proposition 5.1 and proof of Theorem 3.2, p. 9], [@stix2002thesis, §7.3.3, p. 96].
3. *Nonisotriviality.* For proper hyperbolic curves, at least one not isotrivial, and $n \ge 3$ invertible in $k$, an isomorphism with Frobenius inverted is determined by its action on $\pi_1(X_{\bar k})^{\mathrm{ab}}/n$. The proof uses Serre's rigidity theorem and a Lefschetz fixed-point argument [@stix2002affine, Proposition 4.6, p. 8], [@stix2002thesis, Proposition 7.3.3, p. 95]. Stix calls a variety over an algebraically closed field *$F$-cohomologically rigid* if its automorphisms, with Frobenius inverted, act faithfully on cohomology with coefficients $\mathbb{Z}/n$ for all large $n$ prime to $p$. A smooth projective hyperbolic curve is $F$-cohomologically rigid exactly when it is not isotrivial [@stix2002thesis, Definition B.3.3 and Corollary B.3.5, p. 108].
4. *Proper curves.* Over finite fields the conjecture was then known only for affine curves. Stix degenerates Galois covers of the generic fibre to stable curves with non-smooth reduction. With a van Kampen theorem in logarithmic geometry, he recovers affine opens of the special fibre with their tame fundamental groups [@stix2002thesis, Introduction, p. viii; proof of Theorem 7.1.8, p. 90]. Bad reduction is supplied by Tamagawa's theorem that the specialization map of a family whose classifying map to the coarse moduli space is finite fails to be an isomorphism at all but finitely many closed points [@stix2002thesis, Theorem 7.1.1, p. 88]. Mochizuki later settled the [proper case over finite fields](mochizuki-grothendieck-conjecture-proper-curves-finite-fields.md).

**Tsujimura's versions (preprint).** Let $\Sigma$ be a set of primes whose complement is finite and contains $p$. For a variety $S$ over $k$, let $\Delta_S$ be the maximal pro-$\Sigma$ quotient of $\pi_1(S_{\bar k})$, and $\Pi_S$ the quotient of $\pi_1(S)$ by the kernel of $\pi_1(S_{\bar k}) \to \Delta_S$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Introduction, p. 3].

- *Cyclotomes.* Let $Y$ be a hyperbolic curve whose smooth compactification $\bar Y$ has genus at least $2$. Then $H^2(\Delta_{\bar Y}, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma)$ is isomorphic to $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma(-1)$ as a $G_k$-module [@tsujimura2024positive, Lemma 2.2 (i), p. 15]. Let $Z \to Y$ be a finite étale Galois covering, tamely ramified along the cusps, of geometric degree $d$. Under these identifications, the pullback $H^2(\Delta_{\bar Y}, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma) \to H^2(\Delta_{\bar Z}, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma)$ is multiplication by $d$, so it is not an isomorphism in general. Dividing by $d$ first gives a natural isomorphism $H^2(\Delta_{\bar Y}, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma) \to \tfrac{1}{d} \cdot H^2(\Delta_{\bar Y}, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma) \to H^2(\Delta_{\bar Z}, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma)$ of $G_k$-modules [@tsujimura2024positive, Lemma 2.2 (ii), pp. 15–16]. For hyperbolic $X$, the cyclotome $\Lambda_X$ is the $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma$-dual of the direct limit of the groups $H^2(\Delta_{\bar Y}, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma)$, over the geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ finite étale Galois coverings $Y \to X$ with $\bar Y$ of genus at least $2$, with these degree-normalized isomorphisms as transition maps. Since every transition map is an isomorphism, $\Lambda_X$ is naturally isomorphic to $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma(1)$ as a $G_k$-module [@tsujimura2024positive, Definition 2.3, p. 16]. It can be reconstructed from $\Pi_X$ with $\Delta_X$ when some $\ell$-adic cyclotomic character of $k$ with $\ell \in \Sigma$ is open [@tsujimura2024positive, Lemma 2.4 (ii), p. 16], and Tsujimura uses this for finitely generated $k$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Remark 2.13.1 and Corollary 2.14, p. 21]. So an isomorphism $\sigma : \Pi_{X_1} \to \Pi_{X_2}$ over $G_k$ induces an automorphism of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma(1)$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Definition 2.5 (ii), p. 17]. The relative Frobenius $X \to X(m)$ induces multiplication by $p^m$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Lemma 4.2, p. 24].
- *Relative form with cyclotomes.* For hyperbolic curves $X_1, X_2$ over $k$, finite or not, the natural map
  $$\mathrm{Isom}_k(X_1, X_2) \to \mathrm{Isom}^\Lambda_{G_k}(\Pi_{X_1}, \Pi_{X_2})/\mathrm{Inn}(\Delta_{X_2})$$
  is bijective. Here $\mathrm{Isom}^\Lambda_{G_k}$ is the set of isomorphisms over the identity of $G_k$ that induce the identity of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma(1)$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Definition 2.5 (ii), p. 17; Theorem 2.9, p. 19]. No isotriviality hypothesis is needed. Over finite fields the usual relative form fails because their absolute Galois groups are abelian, and the cyclotome condition repairs it [@tsujimura2024positive, Theorem 2.6 and Remark 2.6.2, pp. 17–18].
- *Nonisotriviality forces powers of $p$.* Let $k$ be transcendental over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $X_1$ not isotrivial. Then $X_2$ is not isotrivial, and every isomorphism $\Pi_{X_1} \to \Pi_{X_2}$ over $G_k$ acts on cyclotomes as multiplication by $p^m$ for some integer $m$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Proposition 4.4, p. 25]. Specializing to closed points shows that this unit comes from an automorphism $\bar\sigma$ of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ that relates the classifying maps to the coarse moduli scheme. An automorphism of an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$ whose graph in $\mathbb{A}^1 \times \mathbb{A}^1$ is not Zariski-dense is an integral power of Frobenius. Applied to the image of the classifying map, which has positive dimension because $X_1$ is not isotrivial, this forces $\bar\sigma$ to be a power of Frobenius. Tsujimura attributes the observation on graphs to Hoshi, Mochizuki and Tsujimura [@tsujimura2024positive, Introduction, p. 4; Lemma 4.3 and proof of Proposition 4.4, pp. 25–26].

**Theorem A (Tsujimura, preprint).** Let $k$ be finitely generated and transcendental over $\mathbb{F}_p$, $X_1$ a nonisotrivial hyperbolic curve over $k$ and $X_2$ a hyperbolic curve over $k$. Let $\sigma : \Pi_{X_1} \to \Pi_{X_2}$ be an isomorphism over the identity of $G_k$, taken up to inner automorphisms by $\Delta_{X_2}$. Then after replacing $X_1$ or $X_2$ by a suitable Frobenius twist, $\sigma$ arises from an isomorphism $X_1 \to X_2$ over $k$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Introduction, Theorem A, p. 3]. The precise form concerns quotients of the tame fundamental groups, including the tame groups themselves, and follows from the last two items [@tsujimura2024positive, Theorem 4.5 and its proof, pp. 26–27]. Tsujimura notes that Stix's argument, combined with Saïdi–Tamagawa's theorem, also proves Theorem A [@tsujimura2024positive, Introduction, p. 4].

**Theorem B (Tsujimura, preprint).** Let $k_1, k_2$ be the perfections of fields finitely generated and transcendental over $\mathbb{F}_p$, $X_1$ a nonisotrivial hyperbolic curve over $k_1$ and $X_2$ a hyperbolic curve over $k_2$. Then the natural map
$$\mathrm{Isom}(X_1, X_2) \to \mathrm{OutIsom}(\Pi_{X_1}, \Pi_{X_2})$$
is bijective. Here $\mathrm{Isom}$ is the set of isomorphisms of schemes, with no base field fixed, and $\mathrm{OutIsom}$ the set of isomorphisms of profinite groups modulo inner automorphisms [@tsujimura2024positive, Introduction, Theorem B, pp. 3–4]. The same holds for the tame fundamental groups [@tsujimura2024positive, Theorem 4.10, p. 28]. He regards it as an analogue, over base fields of higher dimension, of [Saïdi–Tamagawa's theorem](prime-to-p-anabelian-curves-over-finite-fields.md) over finite fields [@tsujimura2024positive, Introduction, p. 4].

*Proof outline.* For $k$ the perfection of a finitely generated field, the quotient $\Pi_X \to G_k$ can be reconstructed from the group $\Pi_X$. When $k$ is infinite, its kernel is the maximal topologically finitely generated closed normal subgroup, because the kernel is topologically finitely generated and $G_k$ has no nontrivial such subgroups [@tsujimura2024positive, Proposition 4.7, p. 27]. [Pop's theorem](pop-mochizuki-finitely-generated-fields.md), sharpened so that field isomorphisms of the perfections up to Frobenius correspond bijectively to outer isomorphisms of Galois groups [@tsujimura2024positive, Theorem 4.9, p. 28], reduces the claim to the case $k_1 = k_2$ with $\sigma$ inducing the identity outer automorphism of $G_k$. The precise form of Theorem A then gives the isomorphism. Uniqueness holds because an automorphism of $X$ over a power of Frobenius of $k$ that induces the identity outer automorphism of $\Pi_X$ is the identity [@tsujimura2024positive, Lemma 4.6 and proof of Theorem 4.10, pp. 27–28].

*Nonisotriviality cannot be dropped.* Let $X$ over $k = k_1$ descend to a hyperbolic curve over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and let $\sigma \in G_k$ map to an element of $G_{\mathbb{F}_p}$ that is nontrivial and not an integral power of Frobenius. The outer representation of $X$ factors through the abelian group $G_{\mathbb{F}_p}$, so there is an automorphism of $\Pi_X$ inducing the identity outer automorphism of $\Delta_X$ and conjugation by $\sigma$ on $G_k$. Its class in $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi_X)$ comes from no automorphism of $X$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Remark 4.10.1, p. 29].

**Why perfections.** This is checked here.

1. *Over a perfect field the twists disappear.* Let $k$ be perfect. Then the projection $X(m) \to X$ is the base change, along $X \to \mathrm{Spec}\, k$, of the morphism $\mathrm{Spec}\, k \to \mathrm{Spec}\, k$ given by the $p^m$-th power map. That map is a field automorphism, so the projection is an isomorphism of schemes, though not over $k$. So in Theorem B, where $\mathrm{Isom}$ fixes no base field, every twist of $X_2$ is isomorphic to $X_2$, and no twist is needed.
2. *Passing to the perfection does not change the group.* Let $k$ be any field of characteristic $p$, $X$ a hyperbolic curve over $k$, and $k^{\mathrm{pf}}$ the perfection. The field $\bar k$ is also an algebraic closure of $k^{\mathrm{pf}}$, and $X_{k^{\mathrm{pf}}} \otimes_{k^{\mathrm{pf}}} \bar k = X \otimes_k \bar k$. The exact sequences $1 \to \pi_1(X_{\bar k}) \to \pi_1(X_{k^{\mathrm{pf}}}) \to G_{k^{\mathrm{pf}}} \to 1$ and $1 \to \pi_1(X_{\bar k}) \to \pi_1(X) \to G_k \to 1$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Definition 2.1, p. 15] are compatible with the maps induced by $X_{k^{\mathrm{pf}}} \to X$. The map on geometric groups is the identity, and restriction $G_{k^{\mathrm{pf}}} \to G_k$ is an isomorphism because $k^{\mathrm{pf}}/k$ is purely inseparable [@tsujimura2024positive, proof of Proposition 4.7, p. 27]. By the five lemma, $\pi_1(X_{k^{\mathrm{pf}}}) \to \pi_1(X)$ is an isomorphism, and since the kernel of $\pi_1(X_{\bar k}) \to \Delta_X$ is the same for both, so is $\Pi_{X_{k^{\mathrm{pf}}}} \to \Pi_X$. Moreover $X$ and $X_{k^{\mathrm{pf}}}$ have the same geometric curve, so one is isotrivial exactly when the other is. Hence Theorem B also describes $\mathrm{OutIsom}(\Pi_{X_1}, \Pi_{X_2})$ for hyperbolic curves over fields finitely generated and transcendental over $\mathbb{F}_p$ with $X_1$ not isotrivial: it is in bijection with the isomorphisms of schemes between their base changes to the perfections.

**Isotriviality is group-theoretic.** Let $k \subseteq K$ be a finitely generated field extension, $X$ a hyperbolic curve over $K$, and $\Sigma$ a nonempty set of primes invertible in $k$. Call $X$ *isotrivial relative to $k$* if $X_{\bar K}$ descends to a variety over $\bar k$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Definition 1.2, p. 7]. Then $X$ is isotrivial relative to $k$ exactly when the pro-$\Sigma$ outer representation $G_K \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_X)$ is trivial on $G_{K \bar k}$ [@tsujimura2024positive, Introduction, Theorem D, p. 5; Theorem 1.14, p. 14]. For proper $X$ of genus at least $1$, the action on $\Delta_X^{\mathrm{ab}}$ suffices, but properness cannot be dropped from that version [@tsujimura2024positive, Introduction, Theorem C, pp. 4–5; Theorem 1.9, p. 10]. The condition $\Sigma \neq \emptyset$ is needed: for $\Sigma = \emptyset$ the group $\Delta_X$ is trivial, so both representations are trivial even for a curve that is not isotrivial relative to $\mathbb{F}_p$, such as $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_p(t)} \setminus \{0, 1, t, \infty\}$. That curve is not isotrivial because an isomorphism over $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p(t)}$ with $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \mu, \infty\}$ for some $\mu \in \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ would be a Möbius transformation carrying $\{0, 1, t, \infty\}$ to $\{0, 1, \mu, \infty\}$, so $t$ would be one of the cross-ratios $\mu$, $1/\mu$, $1 - \mu$, $1/(1-\mu)$, $\mu/(\mu - 1)$, $(\mu - 1)/\mu$, all algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p$. With $k = \mathbb{F}_p$, this reads the hypothesis of Theorem A off the outer Galois representation.
