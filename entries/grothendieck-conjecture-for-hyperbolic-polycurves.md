**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime. A field is *sub-$p$-adic* if it is isomorphic to a subfield of a finitely generated extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Definition 3.1]. Number fields and fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ are examples (see [Mochizuki's Hom theorem](mochizuki-hom-theorem-for-hyperbolic-curves.md)). For a variety $X$ over a field $K$ of characteristic $0$, write $\Pi_X$ for its étale fundamental group and $\Delta_{X/K}$ for the kernel of $\Pi_X \to G_K$. For [hyperbolic polycurves](hyperbolic-polycurve.md) $X, Y$ over $K$, let $\mathrm{Isom}_{G_K}(\Pi_X, \Pi_Y)$ be the set of isomorphisms of profinite groups over $G_K$. A $K$-isomorphism $X \to Y$ induces an element of it, well defined up to $\mathrm{Inn}(\Delta_{Y/K})$.

**Statement.** Let $K$ be sub-$p$-adic and let $X, Y$ be hyperbolic polycurves over $K$.

1. *Injectivity.* For every integral variety $Z$ over $K$, the map $\mathrm{Hom}_K(Z, X) \to \mathrm{Hom}_{G_K}(\Pi_Z, \Pi_X)/\mathrm{Inn}(\Delta_{X/K})$ is injective [@hoshi2014polycurves, Proposition 3.2 (ii)].
2. *Dimension at most $4$ (Hoshi).* If $X$ or $Y$ has dimension at most $4$, then the natural map
$$\mathrm{Isom}_K(X, Y) \to \mathrm{Isom}_{G_K}(\Pi_X, \Pi_Y)/\mathrm{Inn}(\Delta_{Y/K})$$
is bijective [@hoshi2014polycurves, Theorem B and Corollary 3.18]. Corollary 3.18 also covers hyperbolic orbi-polycurves, which are algebraic stacks satisfying conditions that include having a finite étale Galois covering by a hyperbolic polycurve over a finite extension of $K$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Definition 3.17].
3. *Every dimension (Carlson).* The same map is bijective for hyperbolic polycurves of any dimension [@carlson2026, Theorem A and Theorem 3.11]. This is in a preprint, arXiv version 1 of 27 April 2026, which is not known to have been peer reviewed.
4. *Finiteness (Hoshi).* In every dimension, $\mathrm{Isom}_{G_K}(\Pi_X, \Pi_Y)/\mathrm{Inn}(\Delta_{Y/K})$ is finite [@hoshi2014polycurves, Theorem C and Theorem 4.4]. If $X, Y$ are defined over number fields $k_X, k_Y$, then $\mathrm{Isom}(\Pi_X, \Pi_Y)/\mathrm{Inn}(\Pi_Y)$, with no compatibility with Galois groups imposed, is finite [@hoshi2014polycurves, Corollary 4.6].
5. *Automorphisms (Hoshi).* Let $X_i \to X_{i-1}$ have type $(g_i, r_i)$ in a sequence of parametrizing morphisms of $X$, of length $n$. If $2g_{i+1} + \max\{r_{i+1} - 1, 0\} < 2g_i + \max\{r_i - 1, 0\}$ for $1 \le i \le n - 1$, then $\mathrm{Aut}_K(X) \to \mathrm{Aut}_{G_K}(\Pi_X)/\mathrm{Inn}(\Delta_{X/K})$ is bijective, in any dimension [@hoshi2014polycurves, Theorem 4.3]. For example, this applies to $C_1 \times_K C_2$ for proper curves of genus $3$ and $2$, parametrized through $C_1$, as checked here.

In dimension $2$, part 2 is Mochizuki's theorem on hyperbolically fibred surfaces [@mochizuki1999, Theorem D, p. 5, and Theorem a2.4] [@carlson2026, Introduction, p. 3]. His proof applies the Hom version of his theorem for curves to the surjection from $\Pi_X$ onto the fundamental group of the base curve of $Y$, which is not an isomorphism [@mochizuki1999, Appendix, p. 97].

**Homomorphisms.** Hoshi's main theorem is a Hom version under conditions [@hoshi2014polycurves, Theorem A]. Let $X$ have dimension $n$, let $Y$ be a normal variety over $K$, and let $\phi : \Pi_Y \to \Pi_X$ be an open homomorphism over $G_K$. Then $\phi$ arises from a unique dominant morphism $Y \to X$ in each of these cases:

- $n = 1$, which is essentially Mochizuki's Theorem A [@hoshi2014polycurves, Remark A.1 (ii)];
- $n = 2$ and $\ker \phi$ is topologically finitely generated;
- $n = 3$, $\ker \phi$ is finite, $Y$ is of LFG-type and $\dim Y \ge 3$;
- $n = 4$, $\phi$ is injective, $Y$ is a hyperbolic polycurve and $\dim Y \ge 4$.

In every dimension, Hoshi characterizes the image of dominant morphisms. Call $\phi$ *nondegenerate* if it is open and, for every open $U \subseteq Y$ and every smooth, surjective, geometrically connected morphism $U \to Z$ onto a normal variety with $\dim Z < \dim X$, the composite $\Pi_U \to \Pi_Y \to \Pi_X$ does not factor through $\Pi_U \to \Pi_Z$. Call it *poly-nondegenerate* if, for some sequence of parametrizing morphisms of $X$, every composite $\Pi_Y \to \Pi_X \to \Pi_{X_i}$ is nondegenerate [@hoshi2014polycurves, Definition 3.6]. Then dominant morphisms $Y \to X$ correspond bijectively to poly-nondegenerate homomorphisms modulo $\mathrm{Inn}(\Delta_{X/K})$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Theorem 3.7]. This condition is not purely group-theoretic, since it quantifies over morphisms of varieties.

Openness alone does not suffice once the target has dimension at least $2$. Let $Y$ be a proper hyperbolic polycurve of dimension at least $2$ and $H \subset Y$ a smooth hyperplane section. By the Lefschetz theorem, $\Pi_H \to \Pi_Y$ is surjective, but there is no dominant morphism $H \to Y$. If $\dim Y \ge 3$, then $\Pi_H \to \Pi_Y$ is an isomorphism. On $Y \times_K H$ the maps induced by the first projection and by the second projection followed by $H \subset Y$ then differ by an automorphism of $\Pi_{Y \times_K H}$, yet only the first comes from a dominant morphism. So no group-theoretic property of open homomorphisms from smooth projective varieties singles out the dominant morphisms when $\dim Y \ge 3$ [@carlson2026, Example 3.13]. Nagamachi makes the first point too [@nagamachi2024, Introduction, p. 93].

Carlson's replacement is cohomological. An open homomorphism $\phi : \Pi_Z \to \Pi_Y$ over $G_K$ is *cohomologically injective* if $H^*(\Delta_{Y/K}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell) \to H^*(\Delta_{Z/K}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ is injective for every prime $\ell$, and *stably* so if $\phi^{-1}(N) \to N$ is cohomologically injective for every open subgroup $N$ of $\Pi_Y$ [@carlson2026, Definition B and Definition 3.2]. If $Z$ is smooth, proper and a $K(\pi, 1)$, and $Y$ is a proper hyperbolic polycurve, then dominant morphisms $Z \to Y$ correspond bijectively to stably cohomologically injective homomorphisms modulo $\mathrm{Inn}(\Delta_{Y/K})$. If $\dim Y \le 2$, this holds for every smooth proper $Z$ [@carlson2026, Theorem C and Theorem 3.9 (1)]. With étale homotopy types in place of fundamental groups, it holds for every smooth proper $Z$ [@carlson2026, Theorem D and Theorem 3.9 (2)]. Over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ there is a version without properness, using a Hodge–Tate condition [@carlson2026, Theorem 3.9 (3)].

For a target of dimension $2$, Nagamachi proves a conditional Hom version. Suppose that a form of the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md), which for affine curves sets aside the sections at cusps [@nagamachi2024, Conjecture 2.1, p. 96], holds for every hyperbolic curve over a field finitely generated over $K$ of transcendence degree $1$. Then every open outer homomorphism $\Pi_Y \to \Pi_X$ over $G_K$, for $X$ of dimension $2$ and $Y$ normal, comes from a nonconstant morphism $Y \to X$ [@nagamachi2024, Theorem 3.4, p. 99].

**Absolute versions.** Let $X$ and $Y$ be defined over fields $k_X, k_Y$ of characteristic $0$, and let $\phi : \Pi_Y \to \Pi_X$ be open, with no compatibility with Galois groups assumed, in one of the four cases listed above. If $k_X, k_Y$ are finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, Hoshi shows that $\phi$ lies over an open homomorphism $G_{k_Y} \to G_{k_X}$, and that it arises from a dominant morphism if this map of Galois groups is injective [@hoshi2014polycurves, Corollary 3.20 (i), (ii)]. If they are finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, he obtains the same conclusion under extra conditions, for instance when $\phi$ lies over an open map of Galois groups and $X$ maps dominantly to a hyperbolic curve whose fundamental group is of A-qLT-type [@hoshi2014polycurves, Corollary 3.20 (iii)]. With Pop's theorem on finitely generated fields, the relative statement in every dimension gives a bijection
$$\mathrm{Isom}(X, Y) \to \mathrm{Isom}(\Pi_X, \Pi_Y)/\mathrm{Inn}(\Pi_Y)$$
between isomorphisms of schemes and outer isomorphisms of profinite groups, with no compatibility with Galois groups imposed, for hyperbolic polycurves over possibly different fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, by the argument of Schmidt and Stix [@carlson2026, Remark 3.12]. The quotient is by conjugation by all of $\Pi_Y$, not only by $\Delta_{Y/k_Y}$ as in the relative statements, because without fixed base points a scheme isomorphism determines a group isomorphism only up to the action of the whole target fundamental group [@schmidtstix2016, proof of Theorem 7.1]. It inherits the preprint status of part 3.

**How the proofs go.**

*Hoshi.* The proof climbs a sequence of parametrizing morphisms. The case of curves comes from Mochizuki's Theorem A [@hoshi2014polycurves, Theorem 3.3]. The geometric fundamental group $\Pi_C$ of a hyperbolic curve over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ is elastic, so a homomorphism from a fundamental group to $\Pi_C$ whose image is normal in an open subgroup is nontrivial exactly when it is open. Hoshi presents this as a group-theoretic analogue of the fact that a morphism to a curve is dominant exactly when its image is not a point [@hoshi2014polycurves, Introduction, p. 156]. Say that $(\dagger_n)$ holds if, for every hyperbolic polycurve $X$ of dimension $n$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, the group $\Pi_X$ has no chain $\{1\} = H_0 \subseteq H_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq H_{n+1} = \Pi_X$ of $n + 2$ closed subgroups, each topologically finitely generated, normal in the next, and with infinite successive quotients [@hoshi2014polycurves, Definition 3.9]. Elasticity gives $(\dagger_1)$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Lemma 3.10]. If $(\dagger_i)$ holds for $i \le n - 1$, then an open injection $\Pi_Y \to \Pi_X$, where $X$ is a hyperbolic polycurve of relative dimension $n$ over a base $S$ and $Y$ is a hyperbolic polycurve with $\dim Y \ge \dim X$, comes from a quasi-finite dominant morphism, provided that its composite to $\Pi_S$ comes from a morphism $Y \to S$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Theorem 3.11]. With $(\dagger_1)$ alone this covers two steps of the tower [@hoshi2014polycurves, Corollary 3.12]. A separate argument, essentially Mochizuki's for surfaces, handles the first two steps when the kernel is topologically finitely generated [@hoshi2014polycurves, Theorem 3.14 and Remark 3.14.1]. Together they reach dimension $4$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, proofs of Corollaries 3.16 and 3.18]. Hoshi expected $(\dagger_n)$ to hold for every $n$, which would remove the bound, but did not know how to prove it for any $n > 1$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Remark 3.18.1].

*Carlson.* Carlson's proof does not use $(\dagger_n)$. It proves non-degeneracy directly by $p$-adic Hodge theory [@carlson2026, Introduction, p. 3].

1. *Log-canonical forms.* If every curve in a sequence of parametrizing morphisms of a hyperbolic polycurve has genus at least $2$, every compactification of the sequence carries a nonzero canonical form. The proof is an induction using Fujita's semipositivity and Riemann–Roch on the base curve [@carlson2026, Proposition 2.16]. Hence every hyperbolic polycurve has a finite étale covering whose finite étale coverings all have a nonzero log-canonical form [@carlson2026, Corollary 2.20]. A hyperbolic polycurve itself need not have one [@carlson2026, Remark 2.21].
2. *Hodge–Tate weights.* Let $K$ be finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $n = \dim Y$. Let $V$ be a smooth variety over $K$ with a smooth proper compactification $\bar V$ whose boundary $D$ is a reduced simple normal crossings divisor. The [logarithmic Hodge–Tate comparison](logarithmic-hodge-tate-comparison.md) identifies $(H^n(V_{\bar K}, \mathbb{Q}_p) \otimes \mathbb{C}_p(n))^{G_K}$, naturally in $V$, with the space $H^0(\bar V,\Omega^n_{\bar V/K}(\log D))$ of global logarithmic $n$-forms. For $V = Y$, where $n$ is the top degree, this is $H^0(\bar Y,\Omega^n_{\bar Y/K}(D))$, which is $H^0(Y,\Omega^n_{Y/K})$ if $Y$ is proper. Carlson writes $\Omega^n(D)$ in every dimension [@carlson2026, §3.1, paragraph preceding Definition 3.7, p. 13], which is right in top degree but not valid in general below it. His argument also applies the formula to $Z$ and its open subsets, which may have larger dimension, and it goes through with logarithmic forms, as checked in that entry. If $\Pi_Z \to \Pi_Y$ factored, on an open $U \subseteq Z$, through $\Pi_W$ for a smooth $W$ with $\dim W < n$, the induced map on these spaces would factor through the corresponding space for $W$, which is zero. Injectivity in cohomology together with a nonzero log-canonical form rules this out. So stably *étale* cohomologically injective maps are nondegenerate, where the étale variant uses the étale cohomology of $Z_{\bar K}$ and agrees with the group version when $Z$ is a $K(\pi, 1)$ [@carlson2026, Definition 3.2, Remark 3.4, and Proposition 3.15 (1) with its proof].
3. *Sub-$p$-adic fields.* A specialization argument for étale homotopy types extends this to every sub-$p$-adic field [@carlson2026, Lemma 2.10, Proposition 3.15 (2) and Corollary 3.16].
4. *Conclusion.* Applied to each $Y_i$ in a sequence of parametrizing morphisms of the target $Y$, this shows that stably cohomologically injective maps are poly-nondegenerate, so they come from dominant morphisms by Hoshi's Theorem 3.7 [@carlson2026, Corollary 3.16 and Theorem 3.20]. An isomorphism of fundamental groups is stably cohomologically injective, so it and its inverse come from dominant morphisms, which are mutually inverse by injectivity [@carlson2026, proof of Theorem 3.11]. Here the $K(\pi, 1)$ property of hyperbolic polycurves and their coverings identifies group cohomology with étale cohomology.

Mochizuki's proof for curves also starts from differentials recovered by Hodge–Tate comparison. Carlson uses forms of top degree on the target instead, which is where the hyperbolicity of every step of the tower enters.

**Strongly hyperbolic Artin neighbourhoods.** Over fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, Schmidt and Stix proved the isomorphism version for strongly hyperbolic Artin neighbourhoods, a class of hyperbolic polycurves of any dimension (see the definition entry), first for étale homotopy types and then for fundamental groups [@schmidtstix2016, Theorem 1.5 and Corollary 1.6]. For smooth geometrically connected varieties that embed into a product of hyperbolic curves, they get, for étale homotopy types, a split injection with a functorial retraction [@schmidtstix2016, Theorem 1.2]. Their method reduces to finite fields, following Tamagawa, which is why they need fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ [@schmidtstix2016, Remarks 1.4 (i)]. As every point of a smooth variety has a basis of such neighbourhoods, every point of a smooth geometrically connected variety over a field finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ has a basis of Zariski neighbourhoods that are anabelian for isomorphisms, as Grothendieck predicted in his letter to Faltings [@schmidtstix2016, Corollary 1.7 and Lemma 6.3]. Since these neighbourhoods are hyperbolic polycurves, combining part 3 with the same Lemma 6.3, which holds over every infinite perfect field, gives the same conclusion over every sub-$p$-adic field, as noted here. It inherits the preprint status of part 3.

**Pro-$p$ versions fail.** For curves, Mochizuki's theorem holds with geometrically pro-$p$ fundamental groups. Sawada proved a pro-$p$ analogue of Hoshi's Theorem A under an extra condition on the fundamental groups [@nagamachi2024, §4, p. 100]. In general, though, it fails. For $p$ odd and $K$ finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, Nagamachi takes a proper hyperbolic curve $X_1$ with a geometrically connected étale double covering $X_1' \to X_1$ over $K$, and a hyperbolic curve $X_2$ with an involution $\iota$ that fixes a $K$-point and acts on the abelianized pro-$p$ geometric fundamental group without nonzero coinvariants. For example, $X_2$ can be hyperelliptic with two $K$-rational Weierstrass points [@nagamachi2024, Lemma 4.3]. Let $Z$ be the quotient of $X_2 \times_K X_1'$ by the diagonal involution. Then $Z \to X_1$ is a hyperbolic curve, $Z$ is a hyperbolic polycurve of dimension $2$, its geometrically pro-$p$ fundamental group is isomorphic over $G_K$ to that of the curve $X_1$, and the automorphism of $Z$ induced by $\iota \times \mathrm{id}$ is nontrivial but induces the trivial outer automorphism of it [@nagamachi2024, Example 4.4, pp. 102–103]. So the pro-$p$ isomorphism version fails, and the pro-$p$ geometric fundamental group does not even determine the dimension. A second example gives non-isomorphic polycurves with isomorphic geometrically pro-$p$ fundamental groups [@nagamachi2024, Example 4.5, pp. 103–104].

Such curves exist, as checked here. Let $H$ be the genus-$2$ curve $y^2 = x(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)(x-4)$ over $K$, with hyperelliptic involution $\iota$, rational Weierstrass points $P_a = (a, 0)$ for $0 \le a \le 4$, and point at infinity $\infty$. Then $\mathrm{div}(x(x - 1)) = 2(P_0 + P_1 - 2\infty)$. The divisor $P_0 + P_1 - 2\infty$ is not principal over $\bar K$, since a divisor $P + Q \sim 2\infty$ is a fibre $\{P, \iota P\}$ of $x$, while $\iota P_0 = P_0 \ne P_1$. So adjoining $\sqrt{x(x - 1)}$ gives an étale double covering of $H$, unramified because every valuation of $x(x - 1)$ is even, and geometrically connected because $x(x - 1)$ is not a square in $\bar K(H)$. Hence $X_1 = X_2 = H$, with the fixed point $P_0$, satisfies Nagamachi's hypotheses.

**Status.** The isomorphism version over sub-$p$-adic fields is proved in dimension at most $4$, and claimed in every dimension by a preprint. A characterization of dominant morphisms by group-theoretic data is known for proper targets from smooth proper $K(\pi, 1)$ sources, and is impossible in the naive form above. Whether $(\dagger_n)$ holds for $n > 1$ is open as far as the sources here show, though it is no longer needed for the isomorphism version if Carlson's proof stands.
