**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite of degree $n$, with $\mu_p\subset K$, including every dyadic field. Put
$$
E=G_K/[P,P],\qquad M=P^{ab},\qquad
\Gamma=G_K/P,\qquad \Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]],
$$
where $P$ is wild inertia and commutator subgroups are closed. Use the setting and Theorem 1 of [the group lifting theorem](tame-automorphisms-lift-as-a-group-modulo-wild-commutators.md): $M$ is characteristic in $E$, a splitting identifies $E=M\rtimes\Gamma$, and
$$
\Gamma=\langle\sigma,\tau\mid\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^q\rangle,
\qquad I=\langle\tau\rangle\cong A:=\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell.
$$
Here $q$ is the residue cardinality. Every $\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$ has the form $\tau\mapsto\tau^u$, $\sigma\mapsto\sigma\tau^a$ with $u\in A^\times$, $a\in A$. If $N=\ker(\Gamma\to\mathbb Z_p)$ and $e\in\Lambda$ is its averaging idempotent, then
$$
(1-e)M\cong\big((1-e)\Lambda\big)^n. \tag{1}
$$

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(E)$ induce $\beta$ on $\Gamma$, with inertia exponent $u$.

1. If $u$ is outside the closed subgroup $\overline{\langle p\rangle}\subset A^\times$, then the class of $\alpha$ has infinitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(E)$.
2. If $\alpha$ has finite order and its class has finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(E)$, then $\beta=1$.

Thus every finite-order lift to $E$ of a nonidentity finite-order tame automorphism has infinitely many outer conjugates, including all the nontrivial finite-order examples in part 4 of the group lifting theorem.

**Proof.**

*1. Central idempotents detect an exponent outside $\overline{\langle p\rangle}$.* Since the reductions modulo integers $m$ prime to $p$ form the topology of $A^\times$, there is such an $m$ with
$$
u\bmod m\notin\langle p\bmod m\rangle.
$$
Choose a character $\chi:I\to\mu_m$ of order $m$, and let $O=\{\chi,\chi^p,\chi^{p^2},\ldots\}$ be its finite orbit. Its values lie in the integers $\mathcal O$ of a finite unramified extension of $\mathbb Q_p$: lift the $m$th roots from a finite residue field by Hensel's lemma, using $p\nmid m$. The Galois group of this extension is generated by the lift of $x\mapsto x^p$ on the roots.

Here is an explicit idempotent $h_O\in\Lambda$. In a finite quotient $\Gamma/U$, write $I_U$ for the image of $I$. For a character $\psi$ factoring through $I_U$, put
$$
h_{\psi,U}=\frac{1}{|I_U|}\sum_{t\in I_U}\psi(t)^{-1}t
\quad\text{in }\mathcal O[\Gamma/U];
$$
put it equal to zero otherwise. These idempotents are compatible as $U$ shrinks. Indeed, grouping a finer sum over the kernel gives the coarser sum if the character descends and zero otherwise. Character orthogonality follows from summing a nontrivial geometric progression. Thus the sums $\sum_{\psi\in O}h_{\psi,U}$ define $h_O$. They are Galois invariant, so have coefficients in $\mathbb Z_p$, and are invariant under conjugation by $\sigma$, since $q$ is a power of $p$. Hence $h_O$ is central in $\Lambda$. It is nonzero: use the finite tame quotient with inertia $C_m$ and Frobenius of order $\operatorname{ord}_m(q)$.

The ring automorphism $\beta_\Lambda$ sends $h_O$ to $h_{O'}$, where $O'=\{\psi\circ\beta^{-1}:\psi\in O\}$. The choice of $m$ makes $O$ and $O'$ disjoint. Consequently, writing $h=h_O$ and $h'=\beta_\Lambda(h)$, we have
$$
h^2=h,\qquad (h')^2=h',\qquad hh'=0,\qquad h\ne0.
$$
All these characters are nontrivial on $I\subset N$, so $eh=eh'=0$. By (1), $hM\cong(h\Lambda)^n$ is nonzero and has no $\mathbb Z_p$-torsion.

*2. Infinitely many conjugates remain distinct modulo inner automorphisms.* For $z\in1+p^2\mathbb Z_p$ let $\rho_z$ act on $M$ as multiplication by
$$
b_z=1+(z-1)h
$$
and fix the chosen section of $\Gamma$ in $E$. This defines an automorphism of $E$: $b_z$ is central, and its inverse is $1+(z^{-1}-1)h$. The restriction $\varphi=\alpha|_M$ is $\beta_\Lambda$-semilinear, irrespective of whether $\alpha$ preserves the section. Therefore
$$
(\rho_z\alpha\rho_z^{-1})|_M
=\big(1+(z-1)h+(z^{-1}-1)h'\big)\varphi. \tag{2}
$$

We first check $Z(\Gamma)=1$. If $\sigma^y\tau^x$ is central, commuting with $\tau$ gives $q^y=1$ in $A^\times$. For every integer $r\ge2$, the order of $q$ modulo $q^r-1$ is exactly $r$, so $y\in r\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ for all $r$, hence $y=0$. Commuting with $\sigma$ then gives $(q-1)x=0$ in the torsion-free group $A$, so $x=0$.

Suppose two conjugates in (2), indexed by $z,w$, differ by conjugation by $x\in E$. Both induce $\beta$ on $\Gamma$, so the image of $x$ in $\Gamma$ is central and thus trivial. Hence $x\in M$, whose inner automorphism fixes the abelian group $M$ pointwise. Their restrictions in (2) are equal. Cancel $\varphi$ and restrict to $hM$ to get $(z-w)hM=0$. The nonzero torsion-free module $hM$ gives $z=w$. The infinite set $1+p^2\mathbb Z_p$ therefore supplies pairwise distinct conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(E)$, proving part 1.

*3. Finite order.* The continuous map
$$
\widehat{\mathbb Z}\longrightarrow A^\times,\qquad y\longmapsto p^y
$$
is injective by the same order calculation modulo $p^r-1$, $r\ge2$. Its compact image is $\overline{\langle p\rangle}$, which is therefore torsion-free. If $\alpha$ has order $s$ and has finitely many outer conjugates, part 1 gives $u\in\overline{\langle p\rangle}$; also $u^s=1$, hence $u=1$. Now $\beta(\tau)=\tau$ and $\beta(\sigma)=\sigma\tau^a$, so $\beta^s(\sigma)=\sigma\tau^{sa}$. As $\beta^s=1$ and $A$ is torsion-free, $a=0$. This proves part 2 and the stated consequence. $\square$

**Scope.** The conjugating automorphisms $\rho_z$ are constructed on $E$ using its abelian wild kernel. No lift of them to $G_K$ is established. A class with finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G_K)$ has finitely many conjugates under the image of that group in $\operatorname{Out}(E)$, which can be smaller than $\operatorname{Out}(E)$. Thus this theorem tests the explicit quotient examples but does not exclude the order-$p$ classes sought in the full-Galois-group problem. The finite-order hypothesis concerns the automorphism $\alpha$ itself; finite order of its outer class alone is not used as a substitute.

**Checks.** For the dyadic inversion $u=-1$, one may take $m=7$: the orbit of a primitive character under squaring has exponents $\{1,2,4\}$, whereas inversion gives $\{3,5,6\}$. Conversely, conjugation by the section of $\sigma$ has $u=q=p^f\in\overline{\langle p\rangle}$ and trivial outer class. Thus a nonidentity inertia exponent alone would not suffice in part 1.
