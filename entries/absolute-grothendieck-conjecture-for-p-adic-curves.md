**Question.** Let $k, k'$ be finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and let $X, X'$ be [hyperbolic curves](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $k, k'$. Is the natural map
$$\mathrm{Isom}(X, X') \to \mathrm{OutIsom}(\pi_1(X), \pi_1(X'))$$
bijective? Here $\pi_1$ is the étale fundamental group, $\mathrm{Isom}$ is the set of isomorphisms of schemes, with no base field fixed, and $\mathrm{OutIsom}$ is the set of isomorphisms of profinite groups modulo composition with inner automorphisms of the target. This is the question as Mochizuki and Tsujimura state it [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, pp. 2 and 5]. In words: does the profinite group $\pi_1(X)$ alone, with no map to $G_k$ given, determine $X$ as a scheme, functorially?

*Variants.* One may replace $\pi_1$ by the geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental group $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X$, the quotient of $\pi_1(X)$ by the kernel of the maximal pro-$\Sigma$ quotient of $\pi_1(X \otimes_k \bar k)$, for a set of primes $\Sigma$ containing $p$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, p. 2]. The case $\Sigma = \{p\}$ is the geometrically pro-$p$ version. One may also ask the question over more general base fields.

**History.** When $k$ is a number field or a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $G_k$ is held fixed, the curve is recovered functorially from $\pi_1(X)$, by results of Tamagawa and Mochizuki [@mochizuki2004absolute, Introduction, p. 1] (see [Mochizuki's Hom theorem](mochizuki-hom-theorem-for-hyperbolic-curves.md)). Mochizuki then asked: "To what extent are the 'absolute analogues' of this result valid — i.e., what if one does not hold $G_K$ fixed?" [@mochizuki2004absolute, Introduction, p. 1]. Over number fields the Neukirch–Uchida theorem gives the absolute analogue [@mochizuki2004absolute, Introduction, pp. 1–2] (see [the absolute Grothendieck conjecture over number fields](absolute-grothendieck-conjecture-for-curves-over-number-fields.md)). Over $p$-adic fields the analogue of the Neukirch–Uchida theorem fails, and Mochizuki wrote that it was then his opinion that recovering $X$ from $\pi_1(X)$ in general was unlikely [@mochizuki2004absolute, Introduction, p. 2]. Mochizuki and Tsujimura call the question one of the major open questions in anabelian geometry. They explain that it resisted the earlier methods because $G_k$ has outer automorphisms that do not come from field isomorphisms [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, pp. 5–6]. For such automorphisms see the [naive local Neukirch–Uchida theorem](naive-local-neukirch-uchida.md), which fails.

**Partial results.** All are due to Mochizuki. The first recovers part of the curve for every $X$, and the others cover special cases.

- *Logarithmic special fibres.* Every isomorphism $\pi_1(X) \to \pi_1(X')$ of hyperbolic curves over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ preserves stable reduction, and when it holds, induces an isomorphism of the logarithmic special fibres of the stable models, functorially in finite étale coverings [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 2.1, p. 22, and Theorem 2.7, p. 35]. See [logarithmic special fibres](reconstruction-of-log-special-fibres-of-p-adic-curves.md), which also explains how the [resolving theorem](absolute-grothendieck-theorem-for-p-adic-curves.md) answers two questions Mochizuki raised there.
- *Belyi type.* Every isomorphism $\pi_1(X) \to \pi_1(X')$ comes from a unique isomorphism of schemes if $X$ or $X'$ is of Belyi type, or if both are of quasi-Belyi type [@mochizuki2007cuspidal, Corollary 2.3, p. 53]. Such curves are affine and defined over number fields. The proof goes through the preservation of decomposition groups of closed points, which Belyi maps give for algebraic points and $p$-adic approximation for the others. See [decomposition groups of curves of Belyi type](decomposition-groups-of-p-adic-curves-of-belyi-type.md).
- *Point-theoreticity.* With $\Sigma$ a set of primes with at least two elements that contains $p$, an isomorphism of geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental groups of hyperbolic curves over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ that induces a bijection between the sets of decomposition groups of closed points comes from a unique isomorphism of schemes. This is [@mochizuki2013, Corollary 2.9], in the form stated by Mochizuki and Tsujimura, who call it the strongest result before theirs [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, p. 6]. Mochizuki reads it as saying that the section conjecture over $p$-adic fields implies the absolute isomorphism version [@mochizuki2013, Remark 2.9.2]. For pro-curves obtained by removing a suitably dense set of closed points from a proper curve, he removes the hypothesis on decomposition groups [@mochizuki2013, Corollary 2.10].
- *Belyi maps.* Mochizuki counts [@mochizuki2012, Corollary 3.8 (g)], with Tamagawa's criterion [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1], among the absolute isomorphism versions over $p$-adic fields that rely on Belyi maps [@mochizuki2013, Remark 2.10.1]. It covers curves with a finite étale cover that maps dominantly to the projective line minus three points. See [Lubin–Tate characters and semi-absolute geometricity](lubin-tate-criterion-for-semi-absolute-anabelian-geometry.md).
- *Strictly Belyi type.* A hyperbolic orbicurve is of strictly Belyi type if it is defined over a number field and isogenous to a hyperbolic curve of genus $0$ [@mochizuki2013, Definition 3.5]. For such $X$ over a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, a functorial group-theoretic algorithm reconstructs the function field of $X$ from the profinite group $\pi_1(X)$ [@mochizuki2015, Corollary 1.10 (iii)]. Mochizuki and Tsujimura credit the Belyi cuspidalization behind this, through Theorem 1.9 of the same paper, with an absolute version for such curves [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, p. 6].

**Claimed answer.** Mochizuki and Tsujimura's preprint claims a positive answer, for $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}$ whenever $\Sigma$ has at least two elements and contains the residue characteristics, and so for $\pi_1$ itself [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Theorem 3.12 and Remark 3.12.1, p. 91]. The [resolving theorem](absolute-grothendieck-theorem-for-p-adic-curves.md) records the statement, its consequences for the base field, the method and the status of the source. This entry retains the question.

**Still open.** The authors list two questions as open [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Remark 3.12.1, pp. 91–92]: the geometrically pro-$p$ version, where Higashiyama has partial results, and base fields more general than finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, such as complete discretely valued fields of characteristic $0$ whose residue fields are algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p$.
