**Statement.** Let $k, k'$ be finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathbb{Q}_{p'}$, and let $X, X'$ be [hyperbolic curves](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $k, k'$. Let $\Sigma$ be a set of primes with at least two elements that contains $p$ and $p'$. Write $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X$ for the geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental group, the quotient of $\pi_1(X)$ by the kernel of the maximal pro-$\Sigma$ quotient of $\pi_1(X \otimes_k \bar k)$. When $\Sigma$ is the set of all primes, $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X = \pi_1(X)$. Then the natural map
$$\mathrm{Isom}(X, X') \to \mathrm{OutIsom}(\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X, \Pi^{(\Sigma)}_{X'})$$
is bijective. Here $\mathrm{Isom}$ is the set of isomorphisms of schemes, with no base field fixed, and $\mathrm{OutIsom}$ is the set of isomorphisms of profinite groups modulo composition with inner automorphisms of the target [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Theorem 3.12, and Introduction, Theorem D]. Every isomorphism of schemes $X \to X'$ lies over an isomorphism between $k$ and $k'$, as one sees by considering the subgroups of the unit groups of $X$ and $X'$ whose union with $0$ is closed under addition [@mochizukitsujimura2024, proof of Theorem 3.12].

The same holds for the configuration spaces of $X$ and $X'$, with their profinite fundamental groups [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Theorem 3.13]. The proof combines Theorem 3.12 with the relative theorem of [Mochizuki](mochizuki-hom-theorem-for-hyperbolic-curves.md) [@mochizukitsujimura2024, proof of Theorem 3.13].

**Absolute and relative.** The relative version fixes the base field and asks about isomorphisms compatible with the projections to $G_k$. [Mochizuki's Hom theorem](mochizuki-hom-theorem-for-hyperbolic-curves.md) settles it over all subfields of finitely generated extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. The absolute version starts from the abstract profinite group. The authors call it one of the major open questions in anabelian geometry, and explain that it resisted the earlier methods because $G_k$ has outer automorphisms that do not come from field isomorphisms [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, pp. 5–6]. For such automorphisms see the [naive local Neukirch–Uchida theorem](naive-local-neukirch-uchida.md), which fails.

**Consequences for the base field.** These are checked here.

1. *$\pi_1(X)$ determines $k$.* If $X/k$ and $X'/k'$ are hyperbolic curves over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathbb{Q}_{p'}$ with $\pi_1(X) \cong \pi_1(X')$, then $k \cong k'$. Indeed, take $\Sigma$ to be all primes. Then $\mathrm{OutIsom}$ is nonempty, so by the theorem there is an isomorphism of schemes $X \to X'$, and it lies over an isomorphism between $k$ and $k'$. For example, for odd $p$ the fields $K = \mathbb{Q}_p(\sqrt[p]{p})$ and $L = \mathbb{Q}_p(\sqrt[p]{p + p^2})$ are not isomorphic but have $G_K \cong G_L$ (see the examples in [the classification of $p$-adic Galois groups](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). So $\pi_1(X) \not\cong \pi_1(Y)$ for every hyperbolic curve $X$ over $K$ and every hyperbolic curve $Y$ over $L$.
2. *Non-geometric automorphisms of $G_k$ do not lift.* Let $X$ be a hyperbolic curve over $k$, with $\Delta_X$ the kernel of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$, and let $\psi$ be an automorphism of $\pi_1(X)$. Then $\psi(\Delta_X) = \Delta_X$ [@mochizuki2012, Corollary 2.8 (ii)], so $\psi$ induces an automorphism $\alpha$ of $G_k$. By the theorem, $\psi$ agrees up to an inner automorphism with the map induced by an automorphism $f$ of the scheme $X$, and $f$ lies over an automorphism $\sigma$ of $k$. The square formed by $f$, $\sigma$ and the structure morphisms commutes, so the map that $f$ induces on $\pi_1(X)$ induces on $G_k$ the map given by an extension of $\sigma$ to $\bar k$. Inner automorphisms of $\pi_1(X)$ induce inner automorphisms of $G_k$. Hence the class of $\alpha$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ lies in the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$. So no outer automorphism of $G_k$ outside that image, such as those of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ for odd $p$ found by Jannsen and Wingberg (see [generators and relations](local-galois-group-presentations.md)), lifts to an automorphism of $\pi_1(X)$. Compare [the field-theoretic subgroup of outer automorphisms](field-theoretic-subgroup-of-outer-automorphisms.md).

**Earlier results.** All are due to Mochizuki. The first recovers part of the curve for every $X$, and the others cover special cases.

- *Logarithmic special fibres.* Every isomorphism $\pi_1(X) \to \pi_1(X')$ of hyperbolic curves over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ preserves stable reduction, and when it holds, induces an isomorphism of the logarithmic special fibres of the stable models, functorially in finite étale coverings [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 2.1, p. 22, and Theorem 2.7, p. 35]. See [logarithmic special fibres](reconstruction-of-log-special-fibres-of-p-adic-curves.md), which also explains how the theorem above answers two questions Mochizuki raised there.
- *Point-theoreticity.* With $\Sigma$ as above, an isomorphism of geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental groups of hyperbolic curves over $p$-adic fields that induces a bijection between the sets of decomposition groups of closed points comes from a unique isomorphism of schemes. This is [@mochizuki2013, Corollary 2.9], in the form stated by Mochizuki and Tsujimura, who call it the strongest result before theirs [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, p. 6]. Mochizuki reads it as saying that the section conjecture over $p$-adic fields implies the absolute isomorphism version [@mochizuki2013, Remark 2.9.2]. For pro-curves obtained by removing a suitably dense set of closed points from a proper curve, he removes the hypothesis on decomposition groups [@mochizuki2013, Corollary 2.10].
- *Belyi maps.* Mochizuki counts [@mochizuki2012, Corollary 3.8 (g)], with Tamagawa's criterion [@mochizuki2012, Remark 3.8.1], among the absolute isomorphism versions over $p$-adic fields that rely on Belyi maps [@mochizuki2013, Remark 2.10.1]. It covers curves with a finite étale cover that maps dominantly to the projective line minus three points. See [Lubin–Tate characters and semi-absolute geometricity](lubin-tate-criterion-for-semi-absolute-anabelian-geometry.md).
- *Strictly Belyi type.* A hyperbolic orbicurve is of strictly Belyi type if it is defined over a number field and isogenous to a hyperbolic curve of genus $0$ [@mochizuki2013, Definition 3.5]. For such $X$ over a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, a functorial group-theoretic algorithm reconstructs the function field of $X$ from the profinite group $\pi_1(X)$ [@mochizuki2015, Corollary 1.10 (iii)]. Mochizuki and Tsujimura credit the Belyi cuspidalization behind this, through Theorem 1.9 of the same paper, with an absolute version for such curves [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, p. 6].

**Method.** The main input is [resolution of nonsingularities](resolution-of-nonsingularities.md) for arbitrary hyperbolic curves over $p$-adic fields, generalizing results of Tamagawa and of Lepage, the latter for Mumford curves [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, pp. 3–4, Theorem A]. With combinatorial anabelian geometry, the authors reconstruct from $\Pi^{(\Sigma)}_X$ the set of decomposition groups of closed points (see [the reconstruction of points from tempered fundamental groups](points-of-p-adic-curves-from-tempered-fundamental-groups.md)), and then apply the point-theoreticity result above [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, pp. 6–7, and proof of Theorem 3.12].

**Open questions.** The authors list two [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Remark 3.12.1]: the geometrically pro-$p$ version, where Higashiyama has partial results, and base fields more general than finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, such as complete discretely valued fields of characteristic $0$ whose residue fields are algebraic over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

**Status of the source.** The paper was issued as RIMS Preprint 1974 in June 2023, and a revised version dated 30 March 2024 is on Mochizuki's web page. This entry cites the revised version. No journal version was found in November 2026, but that search was not exhaustive.
