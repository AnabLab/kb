**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite, $C = \widehat{\bar K}$, $I_K$ the inertia group and $\chi$ the $p$-adic cyclotomic character. Representations are continuous, on finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_p$-vector spaces. As in [potentially ordinary representations](potentially-ordinary-representations-are-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), $\mathbb{Q}_p(1)$ has Hodge–Tate weight $1$: $h$ is a weight of $V$ when $D^h(V) = (C \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V(-h))^{G_K} \ne 0$. For de Rham $V$ put $t_V = D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)/\mathrm{Fil}^0 D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V)$.

A *single-weight filtration* of $V$ is a datum $(K', F_\bullet, h_\bullet, J)$: a finite extension $K'/K$ in $\bar K$, a filtration $0 = F_0 \subsetneq F_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq F_m = V$ by $G_{K'}$-stable subspaces, integers $h_1, \dots, h_m$, and an open subgroup $J \subseteq I_{K'}$ that acts on each $F_i/F_{i-1}$ by $\chi^{h_i}$. The $h_i$ may repeat; when they are pairwise distinct, this is the datum that makes $V$ *potentially ordinary*. By [Sen's inertia criterion](sen-inertia-criterion.md), a filtration by $G_{K'}$-stable subspaces is part of a single-weight filtration exactly when each graded piece is Hodge–Tate with a single weight, as a representation of $G_{K'}$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Extensions in the wrong order split.* Let $F/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite, and let $A$, $B$ be representations of $G_F$ on which $I_F$ acts by the scalars $\chi^a$ and $\chi^b$, with $a < b$. Then every de Rham extension $0 \to A \to E \to B \to 0$ of representations of $G_F$ splits.
2. *De Rham criterion.* Let $V$ have a single-weight filtration. Then $V$ is de Rham if and only if it has a single-weight filtration with $h_1 > h_2 > \cdots > h_m$. In particular a de Rham representation with a single-weight filtration is potentially ordinary.
3. *Transport.* Let $L/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite, $\alpha : G_L \to G_K$ a continuous isomorphism, and $V$ a representation of $G_K$ with a single-weight filtration. Then $V \circ \alpha$ is de Rham if and only if $V$ is, and potentially semistable if and only if $V$ is. If $V$ is de Rham, $V \circ \alpha$ is Hodge–Tate with the same weights and multiplicities.

So in a de Rham representation the larger weights sit at the bottom of the filtration, after reordering.

**Consequences.**

- *Recurring weights.* The entry on potentially ordinary representations leaves open the Hodge–Tate representations all of whose single-weight filtrations repeat a weight, the smallest shape having weights $0, 1, 0$. By part 2 none of them is de Rham, since a de Rham representation with a single-weight filtration has one with distinct weights. For instance, a de Rham representation with a single-weight filtration of shape $0, 1, 0$ also has one of shape $1, 0$, with a line of weight $1$ at the bottom. So every de Rham representation with a single-weight filtration is potentially ordinary, hence [Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md) by part 3 of that entry, and the open question there concerns only representations that are Hodge–Tate but not de Rham.
- *The basic example.* For $a = 0$ and $b = 1$, part 1 says that a non-split extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p(1)$ by $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is not de Rham, although it is Hodge–Tate. Brinon and Conrad derive this from potential semistability [@brinonconrad2009, Example 6.3.5, p. 78]. Extensions in the other order, of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ by $\mathbb{Q}_p(1)$, are all de Rham, by part 2.
- *Ordinary representations.* Perrin-Riou's ordinary representations, as Berger recalls them, have a decreasing filtration $\mathrm{Fil}^i V$ by $G_K$-stable subspaces with $I_K$ acting on $\mathrm{Fil}^i V/\mathrm{Fil}^{i+1} V$ by $\chi^i$ [@berger2002differential, proof of Corollary 6.3, p. 63]. These are single-weight filtrations over $K$ with $J = I_K$ and decreasing weights. Part 2 says that every de Rham representation with a single-weight filtration becomes of this kind over a finite extension of $K$. Semistability and crystallinity over $K$ itself are treated in [ordinary representations under Galois isomorphisms](galois-isomorphisms-preserve-crystalline-ordinary-representations.md).

**Facts used.**

- (a) Subrepresentations, quotients, tensor products and duals of de Rham representations are de Rham [@brinonconrad2009, Theorem 5.2.1, p. 68, and §6.1, p. 73]. Being de Rham is unchanged by restriction to $G_{K'}$ for a finite extension $K'/K$ [@brinonconrad2009, Proposition 6.3.8, p. 80].
- (b) A representation on which an open subgroup of inertia acts trivially is de Rham with $0$ as its only weight [@brinonconrad2009, paragraph after Example 6.3.9, p. 82]. Twisting by $\mathbb{Q}_p(h)$ preserves de Rham-ness [@brinonconrad2009, Corollary 6.3.4, p. 78] and shifts weights by $h$. So the pieces $F_i/F_{i-1}$ of a single-weight filtration are de Rham over $K'$, with the single weight $h_i$.
- (c) For de Rham $V$, $\mathrm{gr}^i D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V) \cong (C(i) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V)^{G_K} = D^{-i}(V)$ [@brinonconrad2009, Proposition 6.3.2, p. 76]. Brinon and Conrad call $i$ the weight there, the opposite sign. Hence if all weights of $V$ are $\le 0$ then $t_V = 0$, and if all are $\ge 1$ then $\mathrm{Fil}^0 D_{\mathrm{dR}}(V) = 0$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p} t_V = [K:\mathbb{Q}_p] \dim V$. In a short exact sequence of de Rham representations the multiplicities of weights add, because $D_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is exact as a functor to filtered vector spaces [@brinonconrad2009, Proposition 6.3.3, p. 77].
- (d) *Extension classes.* Given an exact sequence $0 \to A \to E \to B \to 0$, let $W = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(B, A)$ and let $E_W \subseteq \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(B, E)$ be the maps whose composite with $E \to B$ is a scalar. Then $0 \to W \to E_W \to \mathbb{Q}_p \to 0$ is exact; let $e \in H^1(K, W)$ be its class. The original sequence splits if and only if $e = 0$, since a $G_K$-invariant element of $E_W$ over $1$ is an equivariant section $B \to E$. If $E$ is de Rham, so is $E_W \subseteq B^\vee \otimes E$, by (a). Conversely, if $E_W$ and $B$ are de Rham, so is $E$, as the image of the evaluation map $E_W \otimes B \to E$. This map is onto, because $W \otimes B \to A$ is onto and $E_W$ contains a lift of $\mathrm{id}_B$. When $A$ and $B$ are de Rham, so is $W$, and Bloch and Kato show that $E_W$ is de Rham exactly when $e \in H^1_g(K, W)$ [@blochkato1990tamagawa, remark after (3.7.4), p. 354]. So $E$ is de Rham exactly when $e \in H^1_g(K, W)$. The same holds with "crystalline" and $H^1_f$ in place of "de Rham" and $H^1_g$, with the same references.

**Proof of part 1.** Put $W = \mathrm{Hom}(B, A) = B^\vee \otimes A$ and $X = W^\vee(1)$, of dimension $n$. Then $I_F$ acts on $W$ by $\chi^r$ with $r = a - b \le -1$, and on $X$ by $\chi^s$ with $s = 1 - r \ge 2$. By (b), $W$ and $X$ are de Rham, with the single weights $r$ and $s$. Neither has nonzero $G_F$-invariants: a vector fixed by $I_F$ would force $\chi^r$ or $\chi^s$ to be trivial on $I_F$, but $\chi$ has infinite image on $I_F$ [@brinonconrad2009, Example 1.1.5, p. 5]. By (c), $t_W = 0$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p} t_X = [F:\mathbb{Q}_p]\, n$.

- *$\dim H^1(F, X) = [F:\mathbb{Q}_p]\, n$.* The cup-product pairing $H^1(F, W) \times H^1(F, X) \to H^2(F, \mathbb{Q}_p(1)) \cong \mathbb{Q}_p$ is perfect, and $H^1_f(F, W)$ and $H^1_f(F, X)$ are exact annihilators of each other [@blochkato1990tamagawa, Proposition 3.8, p. 354]. So $\dim H^1(F, X) = \dim H^1(F, W) = \dim H^1_f(F, W) + \dim H^1_f(F, X)$. By $\dim H^1_f(F, V) = \dim t_V + \dim H^0(F, V)$ [@blochkato1990tamagawa, Corollary 3.8.4, p. 355], this is $0 + [F:\mathbb{Q}_p]\, n$.
- *$D_{\mathrm{cris}}(X)^{\varphi = 1} = 0$.* Let $F' = \widehat{F^{\mathrm{un}}}$, whose absolute Galois group is $I_F$. The natural map $F'_0 \otimes_{F_0} D_{\mathrm{cris}, F}(X) \to D_{\mathrm{cris}, F'}(X)$ is a Frobenius-compatible isomorphism [@brinonconrad2009, Proposition 9.3.1, p. 146], so it suffices to show $D_{\mathrm{cris}, F'}(X)^{\varphi = 1} = 0$. As a representation of $I_F$, $X$ is a sum of $n$ copies of $\mathbb{Q}_p(s)$. Over $F'$, $D_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathbb{Q}_p(s))$ is the line $F'_0\, t^{-s}$, with $\varphi(c\, t^{-s}) = p^{-s} \sigma(c)\, t^{-s}$, where $\sigma$ is the Frobenius of $F'_0 = W(\bar k)[1/p]$ [@brinonconrad2009, §9.1, p. 131, and Example 9.1.12, p. 135, for $r = -s$]. A $\varphi$-fixed element would need coordinates $c$ with $\sigma(c) = p^s c$. Since $\sigma$ preserves the $p$-adic valuation and $s \ne 0$, every $c$ is $0$.
- *Conclusion.* Bloch and Kato's exact sequence $0 \to H^0(F, X) \to D_{\mathrm{cris}}(X)^{\varphi=1} \to t_X \to H^1_e(F, X) \to 0$ [@blochkato1990tamagawa, Corollary 3.8.4, p. 355], recorded in [Bloch–Kato local conditions](bloch-kato-local-conditions.md), gives $\dim H^1_e(F, X) = [F:\mathbb{Q}_p]\, n$, so $H^1_e(F, X) = H^1(F, X)$. As $H^1_g(F, W)$ and $H^1_e(F, X)$ are exact annihilators [@blochkato1990tamagawa, Proposition 3.8, p. 354], $H^1_g(F, W) = 0$. By (d), the class of a de Rham extension lies in $H^1_g(F, W) = 0$, so the extension splits. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.**

*If.* Let $(K', F_\bullet, h_\bullet, J)$ have $h_1 > \cdots > h_m$. By (b), $F_1$ is de Rham over $K'$ with the single weight $h_1$. Suppose $F_i$ is de Rham over $K'$ with weights $h_1, \dots, h_i$. These all exceed $h_{i+1}$, the only weight of the de Rham representation $F_{i+1}/F_i$. An extension of a de Rham representation by one whose weights are all strictly larger is de Rham [@brinonconrad2009, Example 6.3.6, pp. 78–79], so $F_{i+1}$ is de Rham, with weights $h_1, \dots, h_{i+1}$ by (c). The example's proof uses the convention of this entry, in which $\mathbb{Q}_p(r)$ with $r > 0$ has the larger weight. Berger proves the same statement in cohomological form [@berger2002differential, Lemma 6.5, p. 63]. So $V$ is de Rham over $K'$, and hence over $K$ by (a).

*Only if.* Let $V$ be de Rham with a single-weight filtration $(K', F_\bullet, h_\bullet, J)$. There is a finite extension $K_1/K'$ with $I_{K_1} \subseteq J$: $J$ contains $I_{K'} \cap N$ for some open normal subgroup $N$ of $G_{K'}$, and the fixed field $K_1$ of $N$ has $I_{K_1} = I_{K'} \cap N$. So $(K_1, F_\bullet, h_\bullet, I_{K_1})$ is a single-weight filtration, and $V$ and its subquotients are de Rham over $K_1$ by (a). Apply the following moves as long as one applies. Each yields a single-weight filtration $(K_j, F_\bullet, h_\bullet, I_{K_j})$, on whose pieces all of $I_{K_j}$ acts by the given powers of $\chi$.

- *Merge.* If $h_i = h_{i+1} = h$, then $Q = F_{i+1}/F_{i-1}$ is de Rham with $h$ as its only weight, by (c). By [Sen's inertia criterion](sen-inertia-criterion.md), an open subgroup $J' \subseteq I_{K_j}$ acts on $Q$ by $\chi^h$. Delete $F_i$ from the flag, and replace $K_j$ by a finite extension $K_{j+1}$ with $I_{K_{j+1}} \subseteq J'$, found as above. This lowers $m$.
- *Swap.* If $h_i < h_{i+1}$, part 1 over $F = K_j$ splits the de Rham extension $0 \to F_i/F_{i-1} \to Q \to F_{i+1}/F_i \to 0$, where $Q = F_{i+1}/F_{i-1}$. So $Q = F_i/F_{i-1} \oplus B'$ with $B' \cong F_{i+1}/F_i$ stable under $G_{K_j}$. Replace $F_i$ by the preimage $F'_i$ of $B'$ in $F_{i+1}$. Then $F'_i/F_{i-1} \cong F_{i+1}/F_i$ and $F_{i+1}/F'_i \cong F_i/F_{i-1}$, so $h_i$ and $h_{i+1}$ trade places. This keeps $m$ and lowers by one the number of pairs $i < j$ with $h_i < h_j$.

The pair ($m$, number of such pairs) decreases lexicographically, so the process stops, and then $h_1 > \cdots > h_m$. Distinct weights make $V$ potentially ordinary. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** The proof of part 3 of the [potentially ordinary entry](potentially-ordinary-representations-are-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md) does not use that the $h_i$ are distinct. Let $L'$ be the finite extension of $L$ with $G_{L'} = \alpha^{-1}(G_{K'})$ and $\alpha' : G_{L'} \to G_{K'}$ the restriction. Then $(L', F_\bullet, h_\bullet, \alpha'^{-1}(J))$ is a single-weight filtration of $V \circ \alpha$, with the same integers in the same order. Now let $V$ be de Rham. Part 2 gives it a single-weight filtration with strictly decreasing weights; the transported filtration of $V \circ \alpha$ has the same weights, so $V \circ \alpha$ is de Rham by part 2. The same argument for $\alpha^{-1}$, applied to $V \circ \alpha$, gives the converse. Potential semistability is equivalent to de Rham-ness by the [p-adic monodromy theorem](p-adic-monodromy-theorem.md). Finally, $V$ and $V \circ \alpha$ are then potentially ordinary with the same distinct weights $h_i$ and pieces of the same dimensions, so both are Hodge–Tate with weights $h_i$ of multiplicity $\dim F_i/F_{i-1}$, by part 2 of the potentially ordinary entry. $\square$
