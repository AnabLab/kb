**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $\mathbb F$ a finite field of characteristic $p$; write $W^{\mathbb F}=W\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}\mathbb F$ for an $\mathbb F_p$-space $W$. For a finitely generated pro-$p$ group $H$ let $I_H$ be the augmentation ideal of the group algebra $\mathbb F_p[H]$ of the underlying abstract group, and
$$A(H)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}A_k(H),\qquad A_k(H)=I_H^k/I_H^{k+1}.$$
Each $A_k(H)$ is finite-dimensional [@minacrogelstadtan2015dimensions, §2, p. 4]. The $p$-Zassenhaus filtration $H_{(k)}$ is as in [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md). By Jennings' theorem, the assignment $hH_{(k+1)}\mapsto(h-1)+I_H^{k+1}$ is a well-defined monomorphism of restricted Lie algebras from $\bigoplus_kH_{(k)}/H_{(k+1)}$ to $A(H)$, and $A(H)$ is the restricted universal envelope of its source [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 3.4, p. 9]. So $h-1\in I_H^k$ for $h\in H_{(k)}$, and $h-1\notin I_H^{k+1}$ if moreover $h\notin H_{(k+1)}$. Write $\mathrm{in}_k(h)=(h-1)+I_H^{k+1}$ for $h\in H_{(k)}$, and $L_k(H)=\mathrm{in}_k(H_{(k)})\subseteq A_k(H)$, an $\mathbb F_p$-subspace.

Let $U_n(\mathbb F)$ be the upper unitriangular $n\times n$ matrices, and $\mathfrak n^{[k]}$ the matrices supported on entries $(i,j)$ with $j-i\ge k$. As in [the large-field theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-over-large-finite-fields-for-free-products.md), $H$ has the *kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$* if $H_{(n)}$ is the intersection of the kernels of all continuous homomorphisms $H\to U_n(\mathbb F)$; over $\mathbb F_p$ this is the property of the conjecture.

*Corner functionals.* A continuous homomorphism $\rho:H\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$ extends linearly to an algebra homomorphism $\tilde\rho:\mathbb F_p[H]\to M_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$. As $I_H$ is spanned by the $h-1$, $\tilde\rho(I_H)\subseteq\mathfrak n^{[1]}$, so $\tilde\rho(I_H^j)\subseteq\mathfrak n^{[j]}$ and $\tilde\rho(I_H^{k+1})=0$. Define
$$\kappa_\rho:A_k(H)^{\mathbb F}\to\mathbb F,\qquad \kappa_\rho\bigl(u+I_H^{k+1}\bigr)=\tilde\rho(u)_{1,k+1},$$
extended $\mathbb F$-linearly. For $h\in H_{(k)}$, $\rho(h)-1=\tilde\rho(h-1)\in\mathfrak n^{[k]}=\mathbb F E_{1,k+1}$, so
$$\rho(h)=1+\kappa_\rho(\mathrm{in}_k(h))\,E_{1,k+1}.\tag{1}$$

**Definition.** The *invisible part* of $H$ over $\mathbb F$ in degree $k\ge1$ is
$$\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)=\bigcap_{\rho:H\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)}\ker\kappa_\rho\ \subseteq A_k(H)^{\mathbb F},$$
over continuous homomorphisms, and $\mathrm{Inv}(H,\mathbb F)=\bigoplus_{k\ge1}\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)$. Say that $H$ *detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F$* if every $h\in H_{(m)}\setminus H_{(m+1)}$ has $\rho(h)\ne1$ for some continuous $\rho:H\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $G=G_1*\cdots*G_s$ be a free pro-$p$ product of finitely many finitely generated pro-$p$ groups, let $\iota_i:A(G_i)^{\mathbb F}\to A(G)^{\mathbb F}$ be induced by the inclusions, and let $J\subseteq A(G)^{\mathbb F}$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the subspaces $\iota_i(\mathrm{Inv}(G_i,\mathbb F))$.

1. $\mathrm{Inv}(G,\mathbb F)=J$.
2. For $m\ge1$, $G$ detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F$ if and only if $L_m(G)\cap J_m=0$.
3. If $\mathrm{Inv}_k(G_i,\mathbb F)=0$ for all $i$ and all $k\le m$, then $\mathrm{Inv}_k(G,\mathbb F)=0$ for all $k\le m$, and $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ for every $n\le m+1$.

**Corollary 1 (the first invisible degree).** This is proved here. Let $m\ge1$, and suppose that $\mathrm{Inv}_k(G_i,\mathbb F)=0$ for all $i$ and all $k<m$, and that every $G_i$ detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F$. Then $G$ detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F$.

**Corollary 2.** This is proved here. Consider the following finitely generated pro-$p$ groups.

- (a) A free pro-$p$ group of finite rank: $\mathrm{Inv}(\cdot,\mathbb F)=0$.
- (b) A [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank at least $3$: $\mathrm{Inv}(\cdot,\mathbb F)=0$.
- (c) For $p=2$, the group $\mathbb Z/2$: $\mathrm{Inv}(\cdot,\mathbb F)=0$.
- (d) $\mathbb Z_p^d$ with $d\ge2$: $\mathrm{Inv}_k(\cdot,\mathbb F)=0$ for $k\le|\mathbb F|$.
- (e) A Demushkin group of rank $2$ with $q\ne2$, that is, $\langle x_1,x_2\mid x_1^q(x_1,x_2)\rangle$ with $q=p^g$, $g\ge1$, or $q=0$, which gives $\mathbb Z_p^2$: $\mathrm{Inv}_k(\cdot,\mathbb F)=0$ for $k\le|\mathbb F|$, and $A(\cdot)\cong\mathbb F_p[X_1,X_2]$.

Let $G$ be a free pro-$p$ product of finitely many groups of these types. Then $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ whenever $|\mathbb F|\ge n-2$; over $\mathbb F_p$ this means $n\le p+2$. If no factor has type (d) or (e), it has the property over every $\mathbb F$ for every $n$.

**Examples of invisible elements.** These are proved here.

- (i) *$\mathbb Z_p^2$ over $\mathbb F_p$.* Under $A(\mathbb Z_p^2)\cong\mathbb F_p[X_1,X_2]$, $\mathrm{Inv}_\ell(\mathbb Z_p^2,\mathbb F_p)$ is the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $\ell$ vanishing on $\mathbb F_p^2$. It is $0$ for $\ell\le p$ and contains $X_1^{\ell-p-1}(X_1^pX_2-X_1X_2^p)\ne0$ for $\ell\ge p+1$.
- (ii) *$\mathbb Z_p\times F_r$ over every $\mathbb F$.* Let $F_r$ be free pro-$p$ on $y_1,\ldots,y_r$ with $r\ge2$, and $c$ a generator of $\mathbb Z_p$. With $X=\mathrm{in}_1(c)$ and $Y_i=\mathrm{in}_1(y_i)$, the element $X(Y_1Y_2-Y_2Y_1)\in A_3(\mathbb Z_p\times F_r)$ is nonzero and lies in $\mathrm{Inv}_3(\mathbb Z_p\times F_r,\mathbb F)$ for every finite field $\mathbb F$ of characteristic $p$.

So part 3 cannot be applied to a free product with a factor $\mathbb Z_p\times F_r$, however large $\mathbb F$ is, although $\mathbb Z_p\times F_r$ itself has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_p$ for every $n$ and is a maximal pro-$p$ Galois group, by Corollaries 1 and 3 of [the cyclotomic semidirect product theorem](cyclotomic-semidirect-products-preserve-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md) with $A=\mathbb Z_p$ and trivial orientation.

**Proofs.**

*Lemma 1 (detection).* For $m\ge1$, $H$ detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F$ if and only if $L_m(H)\cap\mathrm{Inv}_m(H,\mathbb F)=0$. If $H$ detects every degree $m\le n-1$, it has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$; conversely, the kernel $(m+1)$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ implies detection of degree $m$.

*Proof.* By (1), $\rho(h)\ne1$ exactly when $\kappa_\rho(\mathrm{in}_m(h))\ne0$, for $h\in H_{(m)}$. By Jennings' theorem $\mathrm{in}_m(h)\ne0$ exactly when $h\notin H_{(m+1)}$. This gives the first statement. For the second, the inclusion of $H_{(n)}$ in the intersection of kernels holds by the Setting of the large-field theorem. If $g\notin H_{(n)}$, let $m$ be largest with $g\in H_{(m)}$, so $m\le n-1$; a detecting $\rho:H\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$, followed by the embedding as the upper left block of $U_n(\mathbb F)$, does not kill $g$. The converse is the definition applied to $h\in H_{(m)}\setminus H_{(m+1)}$. $\square$

*Lemma 2 (an ideal).* $\mathrm{Inv}(H,\mathbb F)$ is a two-sided ideal of $A(H)^{\mathbb F}$.

*Proof.* Let $u\in\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)$, $v\in A_l(H)^{\mathbb F}$, and $\rho:H\to U_{k+l+1}(\mathbb F)$. Choose representatives $\tilde u$ of $u$ and $\tilde v$ of $v$ in $I_H^k\otimes\mathbb F$ and $I_H^l\otimes\mathbb F$. Then $\tilde\rho(\tilde u)\in\mathfrak n^{[k]}$ and $\tilde\rho(\tilde v)\in\mathfrak n^{[l]}$, so in $\kappa_\rho(uv)=\sum_j\tilde\rho(\tilde u)_{1,j}\tilde\rho(\tilde v)_{j,k+l+1}$ only $j=k+1$ contributes. The upper left $(k+1)\times(k+1)$ block of $\rho$ is a continuous homomorphism $\rho':H\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$, and $\tilde\rho(\tilde u)_{1,k+1}=\kappa_{\rho'}(u)=0$. So $\kappa_\rho(uv)=0$. For $vu$ use the lower right block in the same way. $\square$

*Lemma 3 (generators).* Let $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ be a minimal generating set of $H$, $T=\mathbb F_p\langle X_1,\ldots,X_d\rangle$ the free algebra and $\mu:T\to A(H)$ the algebra map with $X_i\mapsto\mathrm{in}_1(x_i)$. Identify $\mathrm{Hom}(H,\mathbb F)$ with $(V^{\mathbb F})^*$, $V=T_1$, by $a(X_i)=a(x_i)$, and pair $T_k^{\mathbb F}$ with $(V^{\mathbb F})^{*\otimes k}$ by $\langle X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_k},a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_k\rangle=\prod_ja_j(x_{i_j})$. Then:

- $\mu$ is onto.
- For $\rho:H\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$ with superdiagonal homomorphisms $a_j=\rho(\cdot)_{j,j+1}$, $\kappa_\rho\circ\mu=\langle\cdot,a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_k\rangle$ on $T_k^{\mathbb F}$.
- Let $R_k$ be the $\mathbb F$-span of these superdiagonal tensors over all $\rho$. Then $\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)=\mu(R_k^\perp)$. If $N_k\subseteq\ker\mu\cap T_k$ is a subspace with $R_k^\perp\subseteq N_k^{\mathbb F}$, then $\ker\mu\cap T_k=N_k$ and $\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)=0$.

*Proof.* The map $h\mapsto(h-1)+I_H^2$ is a homomorphism $H\to A_1(H)$, since $gh-1=(g-1)+(h-1)+(g-1)(h-1)$. It kills $H_{(2)}$ by Jennings' theorem and is onto because $I_H$ is spanned by the $h-1$. As $H/H_{(2)}$ is spanned by the images of the $x_i$, $A_1(H)$ is spanned by the $\mathrm{in}_1(x_i)$, and then $I_H^k$ is spanned by products of $k$ elements $x_i-1$ modulo $I_H^{k+1}$. Minimality makes $a\mapsto(a(x_i))_i$ a bijection $\mathrm{Hom}(H,\mathbb F)\to\mathbb F^d$, because $H_{(2)}=H^p[H,H]$ is the Frattini subgroup. For the second point, the $(1,k+1)$ entry of a product of $k$ matrices in $\mathfrak n^{[1]}$ of size $k+1$ is the product of their entries in places $(1,2),\ldots,(k,k+1)$. For the third, $\mu(t)\in\mathrm{Inv}_k$ exactly when $t\in R_k^\perp$, by the second point. In particular $\ker\mu^{\mathbb F}\cap T_k^{\mathbb F}\subseteq R_k^\perp\subseteq N_k^{\mathbb F}\subseteq\ker\mu^{\mathbb F}$, so all are equal, $\ker\mu\cap T_k=N_k$, and $\mathrm{Inv}_k=\mu(\ker\mu^{\mathbb F})=0$. $\square$

*Lemma 4 (finite quotients).* For $k\le m$ and $P=H/H_{(m+1)}$, the quotient map induces an isomorphism $A_k(H)\to I_P^k/I_P^{k+1}$, where $I_P$ is the augmentation ideal of $\mathbb F_p[P]$.

*Proof.* The map $I_H^k\to I_P^k$ is onto. Its kernel on $\mathbb F_p[H]$ is the ideal generated by the $h-1$ with $h\in H_{(m+1)}$, which lies in $I_H^{m+1}\subseteq I_H^{k+1}$ by Jennings' theorem. So an element of $I_H^k$ mapping into $I_P^{k+1}$ lies in $I_H^{k+1}$. $\square$

Lemma 4 lets one use the finite-quotient constructions of [the free-product theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-free-products-of-demushkin-and-free-groups.md) and [the large-field theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-over-large-finite-fields-for-free-products.md), whose maps $\mu$ take values in $\bigoplus_kI_P^k/I_P^{k+1}$, in degrees $k\le m$.

*Proof of the theorem.* Write $A_i=A(G_i)^{\mathbb F}$ and $A=A(G)^{\mathbb F}$, and fix $m\ge1$. A *colour word* of degree $m$ is a sequence $c=((\kappa_1,\ell_1),\ldots,(\kappa_r,\ell_r))$ with $\kappa_j\in\{1,\ldots,s\}$, $\kappa_j\ne\kappa_{j+1}$, $\ell_j\ge1$ and $\sum_j\ell_j=m$. Put
$$V_c=A_{\kappa_1,\ell_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{\kappa_r,\ell_r},\qquad C_m=\bigoplus_cV_c,$$
where $A_{\kappa,\ell}$ is the degree-$\ell$ part of $A_\kappa$, and let $\Phi:C_m\to A_m$ send $w_1\otimes\cdots\otimes w_r$ to $\iota_{\kappa_1}(w_1)\cdots\iota_{\kappa_r}(w_r)$. The proof of the first point of Lemma 3 uses only that the $x_i$ generate $H$. Applied to the union of generating sets of the $G_i$, it shows that $A_m$ is spanned by products of $m$ elements $\iota_i(\mathrm{in}_1(x))$ with $x\in G_i$; grouping adjacent letters of equal colour shows that $\Phi$ is onto. Put
$$J'_m=\bigoplus_c\sum_{j=1}^rA_{\kappa_1,\ell_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathrm{Inv}_{\ell_j}(G_{\kappa_j},\mathbb F)\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{\kappa_r,\ell_r}\ \subseteq C_m.$$
Clearly $\Phi(J'_m)\subseteq J_m$.

*$J\subseteq\mathrm{Inv}(G,\mathbb F)$.* For $u\in\mathrm{Inv}_k(G_i,\mathbb F)$ and $\rho:G\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$, $\kappa_\rho(\iota_i(u))=\kappa_{\rho|_{G_i}}(u)=0$. So $\iota_i$ maps $\mathrm{Inv}(G_i,\mathbb F)$ into $\mathrm{Inv}(G,\mathbb F)$, which is an ideal by Lemma 2.

*$\mathrm{Inv}_m(G,\mathbb F)\subseteq\Phi(J'_m)$.* Fix a colour word $c$ and, for each $j$, a continuous $\rho_j:G_{\kappa_j}\to U_{\ell_j+1}(\mathbb F)$. Put $t_0=1$ and $t_j=1+\ell_1+\cdots+\ell_j$, and let $B_j=\{t_{j-1},\ldots,t_j\}$, a block of $\ell_j+1$ consecutive indices in $\{1,\ldots,m+1\}$. Blocks $B_j,B_{j'}$ with $\kappa_j=\kappa_{j'}$ and $j<j'$ are disjoint, because $j'\ge j+2$ and $t_{j'-1}\ge t_{j+1}>t_j$. For each colour $i$ let $\rho^{(i)}:G_i\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ act by $\rho_j$ on each block $B_j$ with $\kappa_j=i$ and as the identity on the other indices; this is a continuous homomorphism. By the universal property of the free pro-$p$ product, quoted in the Setting of [the free-product theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-free-products-of-demushkin-and-free-groups.md), there is a continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ restricting to $\rho^{(i)}$ on each $G_i$.

We compute $\kappa_\rho\circ\Phi$ on $V_{c'}$ for a colour word $c'=((\kappa'_j,\ell'_j))_{j\le r'}$. For $w_j\in A_{\kappa'_j,\ell'_j}$ with representatives in $I_{G_{\kappa'_j}}^{\ell'_j}\otimes\mathbb F$, the matrix $\tilde\rho(\iota(w_j))=\tilde\rho^{(\kappa'_j)}(w_j)$ is supported inside the blocks of colour $\kappa'_j$ and on entries at distance at least $\ell'_j$, since $\rho^{(\kappa'_j)}(h)-1$ is supported inside those blocks and lies in $\mathfrak n^{[1]}$. The $(1,m+1)$ entry of the product is a sum over indices $1=t'_0<\cdots<t'_{r'}=m+1$ of products of the entries $(t'_{j-1},t'_j)$. As $t'_j-t'_{j-1}\ge\ell'_j$ and the $\ell'_j$ add up to $m$, only $t'_j=1+\ell'_1+\cdots+\ell'_j$ contributes, and its term is nonzero only if each interval $\{t'_{j-1},\ldots,t'_j\}$ lies in one block of colour $\kappa'_j$. Then no endpoint $t_q$ with $0<q<r$ lies strictly inside an interval, since such an interval would contain $t_q-1\in B_q\setminus B_{q+1}$ and $t_q+1\in B_{q+1}\setminus B_q$; so each $B_q$ is a union of consecutive intervals of $c'$, all of colour $\kappa_q$. Two consecutive intervals of one colour are excluded in a colour word. Hence each block contains exactly one interval, and $c'=c$. Thus $\kappa_\rho\circ\Phi$ vanishes on $V_{c'}$ for $c'\ne c$, and on $V_c$ it is
$$\varphi_{c,(\rho_j)}(w_1\otimes\cdots\otimes w_r)=\prod_{j=1}^r\kappa_{\rho_j}(w_j),$$
the corner entry of block $B_j$ being $\kappa_{\rho_j}(w_j)$.

Since $\mathrm{Inv}_\ell(G_\kappa,\mathbb F)$ is the common kernel of the functionals $\kappa_{\rho'}$, $\rho':G_\kappa\to U_{\ell+1}(\mathbb F)$, on a finite-dimensional space, its annihilator is their span. By Step 5 (iv) of the proof of [the free-product theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-free-products-of-demushkin-and-free-groups.md), the annihilator of $J'_m\cap V_c$ in $V_c^*$ is $\bigotimes_j\mathrm{Inv}_{\ell_j}(G_{\kappa_j},\mathbb F)^\perp$. So the annihilator of $J'_m$ in $C_m^*$ is spanned by the functionals $\varphi_{c,(\rho_j)}$, extended by $0$ on the other summands. Now let $u\in\mathrm{Inv}_m(G,\mathbb F)$ and choose $\tilde u\in C_m$ with $\Phi(\tilde u)=u$. Every $\varphi_{c,(\rho_j)}(\tilde u)=\kappa_\rho(u)=0$, so $\tilde u\in J'_m$ and $u\in\Phi(J'_m)$.

Together, $\Phi(J'_m)\subseteq J_m\subseteq\mathrm{Inv}_m(G,\mathbb F)\subseteq\Phi(J'_m)$, proving part 1. Part 2 follows from part 1 and Lemma 1. In part 3, $J'_k=0$ for $k\le m$, so $\mathrm{Inv}_k(G,\mathbb F)=0$; Lemma 1 then gives detection in every degree $k\le m$ and the kernel $n$-unipotent property for $n\le m+1$. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 1.* In a colour word of degree $m$ with $r\ge2$ every $\ell_j<m$, so the corresponding summands of $J'_m$ vanish, and $J_m=\Phi(J'_m)=\sum_i\iota_i(\mathrm{Inv}_m(G_i,\mathbb F))$. Let $g\in G_{(m)}$ with $\mathrm{in}_m(g)\in J_m$, say $\mathrm{in}_m(g)=\sum_i\iota_i(v_i)$ with $v_i\in\mathrm{Inv}_m(G_i,\mathbb F)$. Let $\pi_i:G\to G_i$ be the retraction that is the identity on $G_i$ and trivial on the other factors. It maps $G_{(m)}$ into $(G_i)_{(m)}$, by induction on $m$ over the definition, and the induced algebra map $A(\pi_i)$ is the identity on $\iota_i(A(G_i))$ and kills $\iota_j(A_k(G_j))$ for $j\ne i$ and $k\ge1$. So $v_i=A(\pi_i)(\mathrm{in}_m(g))=\mathrm{in}_m(\pi_i(g))\in L_m(G_i)$. As $G_i$ detects degree $m$, Lemma 1 gives $v_i=0$. Hence $\mathrm{in}_m(g)=0$, so $L_m(G)\cap J_m=0$, and part 2 applies. $\square$

*Proof of Corollary 2, cases (a) to (d).* For every $H$, $\mathrm{Inv}_1(H,\mathbb F)=0$: the functionals $\kappa_\rho$ with $\rho:H\to U_2(\mathbb F)\cong\mathbb F$ are the homomorphisms $H\to\mathbb F$, which separate $A_1(H)^{\mathbb F}=(H/H_{(2)})\otimes\mathbb F$ by Lemma 3. Also, if $\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F_p)=0$, then the functionals $\kappa_\rho$ with $\rho$ over $\mathbb F_p$ span $A_k(H)^*$, so their $\mathbb F$-linear extensions span the dual of $A_k(H)^{\mathbb F}$, and $\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)=0$.

- (a) In Lemma 3 take $N_k=0$. Every sequence $(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$ is a superdiagonal: send $x_i$ to $1+\sum_ja_j(x_i)E_{j,j+1}$ and extend by freeness. So $R_k$ contains all pure tensors and $R_k^\perp=0$.
- (b) Take $N_k$ the degree-$k$ part of the ideal generated by the tensor $\xi$ of the cup product pairing, with minimal generators and the identification of $\mathrm{Hom}(H,\mathbb F_p)$ with $H^1(H,\mathbb F_p)$ as in the Notation of the proof of [the free-product theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-free-products-of-demushkin-and-free-groups.md), with $s=1$. Step 4 there and Lemma 4 give $N_k\subseteq\ker\mu$. Step 5 (v) there, which is part 1 of [the chain theorem](isotropic-chains-detect-the-quadratic-algebra-of-a-nondegenerate-form.md), says that $N_k^\perp$ is spanned by tensors of chains, and Step 1 there makes every chain a superdiagonal over $\mathbb F_p$. So $R_k^\perp\subseteq N_k$ over $\mathbb F_p$, and $\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F_p)=0$.
- (c) $(x-1)^2=x^2-1=0$ in $\mathbb F_2[\mathbb Z/2]$, so $A_k(\mathbb Z/2)=0$ for $k\ge2$, and $\mathrm{Inv}_1=0$ as above.
- (d) Take $N_k$ the degree-$k$ part of the ideal generated by the $X_iX_j-X_jX_i$. Step 4 of the proof of [the large-field theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-over-large-finite-fields-for-free-products.md), for type (A), and Lemma 4 give $N_k\subseteq\ker\mu$. Step 1 there, for one factor of type (A) and one run, makes every sequence $(\lambda_1v,\ldots,\lambda_kv)$ with $\lambda_j\in\mathbb F$ and $v\in(V^{\mathbb F})^*$ a superdiagonal. The proof of $(*)$ for type (A) there shows that for $k\le|\mathbb F|$ the annihilator of the tensors $v^{\otimes k}$ is $N_k^{\mathbb F}$.

*Proof of case (e).* For $q=0$ this is case (d) with $d=2$, so let $q=p^g\ge3$, and use the relation $x_1^q(x_1,x_2)=1$, $(x,y)=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, from [the classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md). Let $N_k$ be the degree-$k$ part of the ideal generated by $X_1X_2-X_2X_1$.

*$N_k\subseteq\ker\mu$.* We have $x_1x_2-x_2x_1=x_2x_1\bigl((x_1,x_2)-1\bigr)$ and $(x_1,x_2)=x_1^{-q}$. By induction on $j$, $x^{p^j}\in H_{(p^j)}$, since $H_{(p^j)}\supseteq H_{(p^{j-1})}^p$. So $(x_1,x_2)\in H_{(q)}\subseteq H_{(3)}$, and by Jennings' theorem $(x_1,x_2)-1\in I_H^3$. Hence $\mu(X_1X_2-X_2X_1)=(x_1x_2-x_2x_1)+I_H^3=0$.

*Superdiagonals.* Fix $k\ge1$ and $\lambda,\beta\in\mathbb F$. Let $W=\mathbb F[t]/(t^{k+1})$, $f=1+\lambda t$ and $u=1+\beta t$, units of $W$. If $\lambda\ne0$, let $\sigma$ be the $\mathbb F$-algebra endomorphism of $W$ with $\sigma(t)=\lambda^{-1}(f^{1-q}-1)$; if $\lambda=0$, let $\sigma$ be the identity. In both cases $\sigma(f)=f^{1-q}$. Since $(1+\lambda t)^q=1+\lambda^qt^q$ in characteristic $p$,
$$f^{1-q}-1=(1+\lambda t)(1+\lambda^qt^q)^{-1}-1\equiv\lambda t\pmod{t^q},$$
so $\sigma(t)\equiv t\pmod{t^3}$, as $q\ge3$. Thus $\sigma(t)=t\cdot(\text{unit})$, $\sigma$ is an automorphism, and $\sigma(t^i)\equiv t^i\pmod{t^{i+2}}$. Let $A$ be multiplication by $f$ and $B$ the composite of $\sigma$ followed by multiplication by $u$. Since $\sigma\circ(\text{multiplication by }w)\circ\sigma^{-1}$ is multiplication by $\sigma(w)$, and multiplications commute,
$$BAB^{-1}=\text{multiplication by }\sigma(f)=A^{1-q}.$$
In the basis $e_j=t^{k+1-j}$, $j=1,\ldots,k+1$, we have $A e_j=e_j+\lambda e_{j-1}$ and $Be_j\in e_j+\beta e_{j-1}+\mathrm{span}(e_1,\ldots,e_{j-2})$, with $e_0=0$. So $A,B\in U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$, with superdiagonals constant equal to $\lambda$ and $\beta$. Put $\rho(x_1)=A$ and $\rho(x_2)=B^{-1}$. Then $x_1^q(x_1,x_2)\mapsto A^qA^{-1}BAB^{-1}=A^qA^{-1}A^{1-q}=1$, so $\rho$ extends to a continuous homomorphism of the one-relator group into the finite $p$-group $U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$. Its superdiagonal tensor is $v^{\otimes k}$ with $v(x_1)=\lambda$, $v(x_2)=-\beta$, as inverting a unitriangular matrix negates its superdiagonal. Every $v\in(V^{\mathbb F})^*$ arises.

*Conclusion.* As in case (d), for $k\le|\mathbb F|$ the annihilator of the $v^{\otimes k}$ is $N_k^{\mathbb F}$, by the proof of $(*)$ for type (A) and the polynomial lemma in [the large-field theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-over-large-finite-fields-for-free-products.md). Lemma 3 gives $\ker\mu\cap T_k=N_k$ and $\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)=0$. Choosing $\mathbb F$ with $|\mathbb F|\ge k$ for each $k$ shows $A(H)=T/(X_1X_2-X_2X_1)\cong\mathbb F_p[X_1,X_2]$. $\square$

*Proof of the conclusion of Corollary 2.* Every factor $G_i$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_p$, hence over $\mathbb F$, for every $n$: in cases (a) to (c) by Lemma 1, since $\mathrm{Inv}(G_i,\mathbb F_p)=0$; in case (d) by Corollary 1 of [the cyclotomic semidirect product theorem](cyclotomic-semidirect-products-preserve-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md); in case (e) by the Corollary of [the Demushkin theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-demushkin-groups.md). By Lemma 1 every $G_i$ detects every degree over $\mathbb F$. Let $m\le n-1\le|\mathbb F|+1$. If $m\le|\mathbb F|$, all $\mathrm{Inv}_k(G_i,\mathbb F)$ with $k\le m$ vanish and part 3 gives detection of degree $m$. If $m=|\mathbb F|+1$, Corollary 1 gives it. Lemma 1 then gives the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$. Without factors of types (d) and (e), part 3 applies in every degree. $\square$

*Proof of Example (i).* By case (d) and Lemma 3, applied with $|\mathbb F|\ge\ell$, $\mu$ identifies $A_\ell(\mathbb Z_p^2)$ with the degree-$\ell$ part of $\mathbb F_p[X_1,X_2]$, and $\langle t,v^{\otimes\ell}\rangle$ is the value of the image of $t$ at $(v(x_1),v(x_2))$. A continuous $\rho:\mathbb Z_p^2\to U_{\ell+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ is a pair of commuting matrices $C,M$. Let $w_j=(C_{j,j+1},M_{j,j+1})\in\mathbb F_p^2$. The $(j,j+2)$ entry of $CM-MC$ is the determinant of $w_j,w_{j+1}$, so consecutive $w_j$ are dependent. If all $w_j\ne0$, they are all multiples of $w_1$, and the superdiagonal tensor is a multiple of $w^{\otimes\ell}$ for a single $w\in\mathbb F_p^2$; otherwise it is $0$. Every $w^{\otimes\ell}$ occurs, by case (d). So $R_\ell$ is spanned by the $w^{\otimes\ell}$ with $w\in\mathbb F_p^2$, and $\mathrm{Inv}_\ell=\mu(R_\ell^\perp)$ is as stated. It vanishes for $\ell\le p$ by the polynomial lemma, and $X_1^pX_2-X_1X_2^p$ vanishes on $\mathbb F_p^2$ because $a^p=a$ there. $\square$

*Proof of Example (ii).* Let $H=\mathbb Z_p\times F_r$, with minimal generators $c,y_1,\ldots,y_r$, and let $\xi=(c-1)(y_1-1)(y_2-1)-(c-1)(y_2-1)(y_1-1)\in I_H^3$, so that $X(Y_1Y_2-Y_2Y_1)=\xi+I_H^4$.

*It is invisible.* Let $\rho:H\to U_4(\mathbb F)$ be continuous, with superdiagonal entries $\lambda_j$ for $\rho(c)$ and $m_j^{(i)}$ for $\rho(y_i)$. By Lemma 3, $\kappa_\rho(\xi)=\lambda_1\bigl(m_2^{(1)}m_3^{(2)}-m_2^{(2)}m_3^{(1)}\bigr)$. As $\rho(c)$ commutes with each $\rho(y_i)$, the $(j,j+2)$ entries of $\rho(c)\rho(y_i)-\rho(y_i)\rho(c)=0$ give $\lambda_jm_{j+1}^{(i)}=m_j^{(i)}\lambda_{j+1}$ for $j=1,2$. Suppose $\lambda_1\ne0$. If $\lambda_2=0$, then $m_2^{(i)}=0$ for all $i$ and $\kappa_\rho(\xi)=0$. If $\lambda_2\ne0$, then $m_3^{(i)}=m_2^{(i)}\lambda_3/\lambda_2$, and the bracket becomes $(\lambda_3/\lambda_2)(m_2^{(1)}m_2^{(2)}-m_2^{(2)}m_2^{(1)})=0$.

*It is nonzero.* Let $E=E_{1,2}\in M_2(\mathbb F_p)$ and define $\rho_1:\mathbb Z_p\to\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)$ by $c\mapsto1+E$, and $\rho_2:F_r\to U_3(\mathbb F_p)$ by $y_1\mapsto1+E_{1,2}$, $y_2\mapsto1+E_{2,3}$ and $y_i\mapsto1$ for $i\ge3$. Then $\tau(z,y)=\rho_1(z)\otimes\rho_2(y)$ is a homomorphism $H\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb F_p^2\otimes\mathbb F_p^3)$. Give $e_a\otimes e_b$ the weight $a+b\in\{2,3,4,5\}$. Each $\tau(h)-1$ strictly lowers weight, because $\rho_1(z)$ and $\rho_2(y)$ are upper unitriangular, so the linear extension $\tilde\tau$ maps $I_H$ into the strictly weight-lowering operators, and $\tilde\tau(I_H^4)=0$, as a product of four such operators vanishes. But
$$\tilde\tau(\xi)=(E\otimes1)\bigl(1\otimes(E_{1,2}E_{2,3}-E_{2,3}E_{1,2})\bigr)=E\otimes E_{1,3}\ne0.$$
So $\xi\notin I_H^4$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What remains open.* Part 2 makes the question for a free product purely algebraic: whether a nonzero element of the restricted Lie algebra lies in the ideal generated by the invisible parts of the factors. Each $\iota_i$ is injective, with left inverse induced by the retraction onto $G_i$. So Example (i) shows that this ideal is nonzero for $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$ over $\mathbb F_p$ in every degree at least $p+1$, and Example (ii) shows that it is nonzero for $(\mathbb Z_p\times F_r)*\mathbb Z_p$ over every $\mathbb F$ in degree $3$. Whether the restricted Lie algebra meets it in either case is not decided here. Such a product is a maximal pro-$p$ Galois group, by the realizability statement quoted in the proof of Corollary 3 of [the cyclotomic semidirect product theorem](cyclotomic-semidirect-products-preserve-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md), so a nonzero element in that intersection would refute the [conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md).
- *Relation to earlier proofs.* The proofs of [the free-product theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-for-free-products-of-demushkin-and-free-groups.md) and [the large-field theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-over-large-finite-fields-for-free-products.md) are part 3 with the factor computations of cases (a) to (d). The argument of [the low-degree theorem](kernel-unipotent-property-in-low-degrees-for-free-products.md), which handles pure colour components by factor projections, is Corollary 1. Corollary 2 over $\mathbb F_p$ recovers its range $n\le p+2$ and adds factors $\mathbb Z_p^d$ with $d\ge3$; it does not cover rank-two Demushkin groups with $q=2$, which that theorem includes.
- *Invisibility depends on the matrix size.* Example (ii) shows an element of $A_3$ that no representation in $U_4(\mathbb F)$ sees but a unipotent representation of dimension $6$ does. The ideal in part 1 concerns representations of size exactly $k+1$ in degree $k$.

**Sources and status.** The theorem, the corollaries and the examples are proved here. The external inputs are Jennings' theorem for finitely generated pro-$p$ groups [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 3.4, p. 9], finite-dimensionality of the graded pieces [@minacrogelstadtan2015dimensions, §2, p. 4], and the cited steps of the linked entries. The proof does not use that $\Phi$ is injective. By a theorem of Lichtman quoted by Mináč, Rogelstad and Tân, the graded completed group algebra of a free product is the coproduct of those of the factors [@minacrogelstadtan2015dimensions, proof of Lemma 2.2, p. 4], which would make $\Phi$ an isomorphism; Lichtman's paper was not opened. A search through November 2026 found no statement of the theorem, but it was not exhaustive.
