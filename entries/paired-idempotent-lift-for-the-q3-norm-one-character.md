**Setting.** Use the presentation and notation of [the modulo-nine entry](norm-one-units-of-q9-modulo-nine-under-automorphisms-of-g-q3.md): $G=G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}=F/N$, $F=R\rtimes\Gamma$, with $R$ free pro-$3$ as a $\Gamma$-operator group on $x_0,x_1$, and $N$ normally generated by
$$r=A(x_0)x_1^3[x_1,y_1],\qquad A(x_0)=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}\langle x_0,\tau\rangle^g.$$
The word $y_1$ involves only $x_1,\sigma,\tau$, lies in $R$, and has Frattini class $\delta\bar x_1$ for some $\delta\in\Lambda=\mathbb F_3[[\Gamma]]$. This follows from part 1 (b),(c) of [mixing complementary and surface generators](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md); that argument applies in degree one and does not use the presence of surface generators.

Put $R_1=R$ and $R_{j+1}=\overline{R_j^3[R_j,R]}$, and write $V_j$ for its image in wild inertia $V=R/N$. Let $\omega:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_3$ be the mod-$3$ cyclotomic character, with $\omega(\sigma)=1$, and let $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$. The [norm-one-unit construction](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md), part 2 (c), supplies central orthogonal idempotents
$$P=T_1S_-,\qquad Q=T_\omega S_-,\qquad P^*=Q,\quad Q^*=P.$$
For the unramified quadratic character $\chi$ there, $\chi(P)=1$ and $\chi(Q)=0$. Moreover $\omega(P)=\omega(Q)=0$: $\omega(S_-)=0$, since $\omega(\sigma_2)=1$ whereas $S_-$ projects to the sign character of $\sigma_2$. Thus
$$\nu=P-Q\quad\text{is central},\qquad \nu^*=-\nu,\qquad \omega(\nu)=0,\qquad\chi(\nu)=1. \tag{1}$$
Let $u=\omega(\sigma)e_\omega-\sigma=e_\omega-\sigma$, where $e_\omega$ is the tame-inertia averaging idempotent. The Lemma and Computation in [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) give
$$\ker\omega=u\Lambda=\Lambda u. \tag{2}$$
For a compact $\Lambda$-module $M$ and a continuous equivariant biadditive map $c:R/R_2\times R/R_2\to M$, they also give
$$c(a v,b w)\equiv c(v,a^*b w)\pmod{uM}. \tag{3}$$
Their proofs of (2), (3), and the Computation work for every degree, independently of the surface-pair hypothesis in their main theorem.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. There is an automorphism $\bar\psi$ of $G/V_4$ fixing $\Gamma$ pointwise whose induced element of $A_{\mathcal H_0,2}$ lies in $S_4$, in the notation of the modulo-nine entry. It is obtained by sending $x_1$ to $x_1y^3$, with $\bar y=\nu\bar x_1$, and $x_0$ to $x_0z$ for a suitable $z\in R_3$.
2. Some continuous automorphism $\theta$ of $G$, inducing the identity on its tame quotient, satisfies $b(\theta)\equiv4\pmod9$. Consequently $G_{\mathbb Q_3}$ has an irreducible two-dimensional Hodge–Tate representation which is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate.

**Collection facts.** Write $L_j=R_j/R_{j+1}$ additively. We need only the first three layers. They are compact elementary abelian $3$-groups with $\Gamma$-action. Commutator collection gives
$$[R_2,R_2]\subseteq R_4,\qquad
[a^3,b]\equiv[a,b]^3\equiv[a,b^3]\pmod{R_4}\quad(a,b\in R). \tag{4}$$
Here is a justification at the required precision. Modulo $R_4$, $R_3$ is central of exponent $3$, and a commutator of length four is trivial. In collecting $[a^3,b]$, the term $[a,b]$ occurs three times, and the correction of length three has exponent $\binom32=3$, so it vanishes. The same argument in the second variable proves the other congruence. To check the first inclusion, $R_2$ is generated by cubes and commutators. For $t\in R_2$, collection of $[a^3,t]$ gives $[a,t]^3$ modulo $R_4$; this vanishes because $[a,t]\in R_3$. Also $[[a,b],t]\in R_4$ by the three-subgroup identity, since both $[a,t]$ and $[b,t]$ lie in $R_3$. Continuity gives the inclusion for the closed subgroups.

Cubing defines continuous $\Lambda$-linear maps
$$\pi_1:L_1\to L_2,\qquad\pi_2:L_2\to L_3.$$
For $\pi_1$, collect $(ab)^3$ modulo $R_3$: its commutator correction has exponent $3$, and its length-three terms lie in $R_3$. Changing a representative by $R_2$ changes its cube only by $R_3$. For $\pi_2$, use $[R_2,R_2]\subseteq R_4$ and $R_3^3\subseteq R_4$; both additivity and independence of representatives follow. Cubing commutes with conjugation, so both maps are $\Lambda$-linear. Similarly
$$\beta:L_1\times L_1\to L_3,\qquad
\beta(\bar a,\bar b)=[a,b]^3R_4$$
is continuous, equivariant and biadditive: it is $\pi_2$ composed with the commutator pairing into $L_2$. By (4), if $t\in R_2$ has class $\pi_1(w)$, then
$$[a,t]R_4=\beta(\bar a,w). \tag{5}$$
Indeed choose a representative $b$ of $w$; then $t\equiv b^3\pmod{R_3}$ and apply (4).

**Proof of part 1.** Choose $y\in R$ with class $\nu\bar x_1$, and let $\psi_0:F\to F$ fix $\Gamma,x_0$ and send $x_1$ to $x_1y^3$. Freeness gives this endomorphism. It is the identity on $F/R_2$.

*The variation of $y_1$.* On $R/R_3$, the difference
$$d(w)=\psi_0(w)w^{-1}\pmod{R_3}$$
takes values in the central elementary abelian group $L_2$. It is a continuous homomorphism on $R$, vanishes on $R_2$, and is $\Gamma$-equivariant because $\psi_0$ fixes $\Gamma$. Hence it is $\Lambda$-linear on $L_1$. As
$$d(x_0)=0,\qquad d(x_1)=\pi_1(\nu\bar x_1),$$
we have
$$d(y_1)=\delta\pi_1(\nu\bar x_1)=\pi_1(\delta\nu\bar x_1). \tag{6}$$
This uses only the Frattini class of $y_1$, not an uncomputed higher term of its word.

*The relator defect.* Put $x=\bar x_1$. Since $y^3\in R_2$, collection modulo $R_4$ gives
$$(x_1y^3)^3\equiv x_1^3y^9\pmod{R_4}. \tag{7}$$
In fact $[y^3,x_1]\in R_3$ is central of exponent $3$ in that quotient, and its collecting exponent is $3$. Write $\psi_0(y_1)=y_1t$ with $t\in R_2$; by (6), $t$ has class $\pi_1(\delta\nu x)$ in $L_2$. The central changes of the commutator are therefore
$$[x_1y^3,y_1t][x_1,y_1]^{-1}R_4
=\beta(\nu x,\delta x)+\beta(x,\delta\nu x). \tag{8}$$
To obtain (8), collect the left side into $[y^3,y_1]$ and $[x_1,t]$: these lie in $R_3$, and all further factors lie in $[R_2,R_2]$ or $[R_3,R]$, hence in $R_4$. Apply (4), (5), and $\bar y_1=\delta x$.

The factor $A(x_0)$ is fixed. Equations (7), (8) show $\psi_0(r)r^{-1}\in R_3$ with class
$$D=\pi_2\pi_1(\nu x)+\beta(\nu x,\delta x)+\beta(x,\delta\nu x)\quad\text{in }L_3. \tag{9}$$
Conjugating by $A(x_0)\in R$ does not affect that class. The first summand is $\nu\pi_2\pi_1(x)\in uL_3$ by (1), (2). Applying (3) to $\beta$, the other two sum, modulo $uL_3$, to
$$\beta\big(x,(\nu^*\delta+\delta\nu)x\big)=0,$$
because $\nu$ is central and $\nu^*=-\nu$. Thus $D\in uL_3$.

*Absorb the defect.* Choose $a\in L_3$ with $ua=-D$, and choose $z\in R_3$ representing $a$. Let $\psi:F\to F$ agree with $\psi_0$ except that $\psi(x_0)=x_0z$. Apply the Computation of the tame-group-algebra entry with its $N=R_4$, $Q=R/R_4$, and central elementary abelian subgroup $L_3$. It says that changing $x_0$ to $x_0z$ changes $A(x_0)$ by $ua$. The other factors of $r$ do not involve $x_0$, so (9) gives
$$\psi(r)r^{-1}\equiv ua+D=0\pmod{R_4}.$$
Therefore $\psi$ preserves $NR_4$ and induces an endomorphism of $F/NR_4=G/V_4$.

This endomorphism is onto: $\psi$ fixes $\Gamma$, its image on $R$ is closed and $\Gamma$-stable, and it is the identity on $R/R_2$. The Frattini property of $R_2$ gives $\psi(R)=R$. The quotient $G/V_4$ is topologically generated by $\sigma,\tau,x_0,x_1$, so a surjective endomorphism is injective, by the finite-index-subgroup argument in the proof of part 2 of the tame-group-algebra entry. Denote the resulting automorphism by $\bar\psi$.

*The congruence.* Let $f:G_K\to\mathbb Z_3$ be the anticyclotomic quotient, with $K/\mathbb Q_3$ unramified quadratic. The companion construction gives $f(x_0)\in3\mathbb Z_3$, $f(x_1)\in\mathbb Z_3^\times$, $f|_{\Gamma\cap G_K}=0$, and
$$f(y)\equiv\chi(\nu)f(x_1)=f(x_1)\pmod3.$$
Since $z$ maps into $V_3$, $f(z)\in9\mathbb Z_3$. Consequently
$$f(x_0z)\equiv4f(x_0),\qquad
f(x_1y^3)\equiv4f(x_1)\pmod9.$$
Both $f\circ\bar\psi$ and $4f$ on $V/V_4$ are homomorphisms modulo $9$, equivariant for $\chi$. They agree on $x_0,x_1$, hence on their $\Gamma$-conjugates and on all of $V/V_4$. They also agree on $\mathcal H_0\subseteq\Gamma\cap G_K$, since both vanish there and $\bar\psi$ fixes $\Gamma$. Thus the induced element at $(\mathcal H_0,2)$ lies in $S_4$.

**Proof of part 2.** For every $\mathcal H\in\mathcal U$, $V\subseteq X_{\mathcal H}$ implies $V_4\subseteq X^3_{\mathcal H}$. Since $\bar\psi$ fixes $\Gamma$, it preserves $X_{\mathcal H}/V_4$ and its third $3$-central term $X^3_{\mathcal H}/V_4$. It therefore induces an element of $A_{\mathcal H,3}$. For every $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal H_0$, its reduction at $(\mathcal H_0,2)$ is the element of $S_4$ just constructed. Part 3 of the modulo-nine entry applies, with $\lambda=4\ne\pm1$ in $(\mathbb Z/9)^\times$, and gives both conclusions. $\square$

**Scope.** The argument changes the earlier substitution: its direction is $P-Q$, rather than $P$, and $x_0$ is corrected in $R_3$. It proves the sufficient target of the one-step criterion at every tame level. It does not assert that the particular $P$-substitution lifts unchanged, or that $\theta$ agrees with $\bar\psi$ modulo $V_4$.
