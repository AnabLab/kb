**Setting.** Fix a prime $p$, a finite extension $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$, an algebraic closure $\bar k$ and its completion $\Omega$. Analytic spaces are Berkovich spaces over $\Omega$, and $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ means $(X_\Omega)^{\mathrm{an}}$. Let $X/\bar k$ be a smooth, proper, connected curve of genus at least $2$. Choose distinct points $b_1, \dots, b_d \in X(\bar k)$, with $d \ge 1$, and rational local parameters $t_i$ at them. Suppose that there are pairwise disjoint open disks $B_i \subset X^{\mathrm{an}}$ on which $t_i$ identifies $B_i$ with $\{|T| < R_i\}$, and radii $r_i \in |\Omega^\times|$ with $0 < r_i < R_i$. Put
$$V_i = \{x \in B_i : |t_i(x)| < r_i\}, \qquad U = X^{\mathrm{an}} \setminus \bigcup_{i=1}^d V_i,$$
and suppose that $U$ is nonempty and contains an $\Omega$-rational point. Then $U$ is a compact connected analytic domain [@openai2026exteriorsheet, §1, p. 1].

**Theorem.** There are a smooth proper connected curve $Y/\bar k$ and a finite étale surjective morphism $g : Y \to X$ such that:

1. a connected component of $g^{-1}(U)$ maps isomorphically onto $U$;
2. for every $i$, every connected component of $g^{-1}(V_i)$ maps to $V_i$ with degree divisible by $p$.

Inverse images and degrees refer to the induced finite étale analytic maps, and the disks are the given $V_i$, not smaller ones [@openai2026exteriorsheet, Theorem 1.1, pp. 1–2].

Two remarks, checked here, help to read the statement. The degrees of the components of $g^{-1}(V_1)$ add up to $\deg g$, so $p$ divides $\deg g$, and the sheet in 1 is not the whole of $g^{-1}(U)$. Condition 2 is about components, not points: every $\Omega$-rational point of $V_i$ has residue field $\Omega$, which is algebraically closed, so each of its preimages has local degree $1$.

**Context.** [Resolution of nonsingularities](resolution-of-nonsingularities.md) gives, for a closed point of the special fibre of a model, a finite étale cover whose stable model has a component of positive genus above that point. The theorem takes this as its geometric input and imposes two conditions on a single cover: a sheet over the whole compact domain $U$, and a factor $p$ in the degree of every component above each deleted disk [@openai2026exteriorsheet, §1, p. 2]. The manuscript states no application. Its hosting repository's [manuscript map](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/CONTENTS.md) lists it in one family with the manuscript proving the [$p$-adic section theorem](p-adic-section-theorem.md), but that manuscript does not cite it [@openai2026padicsections, References, pp. 22–24]. The [completion theorem](completion-of-the-universal-etale-function-field.md) used there builds covers with prescribed behaviour over entire inverse images of collars by related means.

**Outline of the proof.**

1. *Counting components* [@openai2026exteriorsheet, Lemma 2.1, p. 3]. Let $f : C \to X$ be a connected finite étale Galois cover with group $G$, and $D$ either $U$ or some $V_i$. Every connected component of $f^{-1}(D)$ is finite étale and surjective over $D$, $G$ permutes these components transitively, and a component with stabilizer $B$ has degree $|B|$ over $D$. The proof uses only that $D$ is connected and locally path connected, and step 5 uses it in that generality.
2. *A skeletal path above each disk* [@openai2026exteriorsheet, proof of Proposition 2.2, p. 3]. Over a finite extension of $k$, choose a split semistable model with, for each $i$, two distinct smooth closed points of the special fibre whose tubes lie in $V_i$. To get them, add a type-2 point of rational radius inside $V_i$ to the vertex set, and take two smooth points of its component in residue directions pointing away from the exterior [@bakerpaynerabinoff2013, Proposition 3.13 (3), p. 10, and Theorem 4.11, p. 13]. Resolution of nonsingularities at each of these points gives a connected finite étale Galois cover whose stable model maps to this model, with a component of genus at least $1$ mapping to the point. The generic points of these components give type-2 points $x_{i1}, x_{i2}$ in the two tubes, hence in $V_i$. Let $Z_0 \to X$ be a connected finite étale Galois cover dominating all these covers. Every point of $Z_0^{\mathrm{an}}$ above $x_{ij}$ has positive genus. One of them lies above a point of positive genus, so its residue field contains a function field of positive genus and is not rational, by Lüroth's theorem, and the Galois group permutes them transitively. So they belong to the stable vertex set of $Z_0$ [@bakerpaynerabinoff2013, Remark 4.17, p. 15, and Theorem 4.22 (2), p. 16]. Step 1 puts lifts of $x_{i1}$ and $x_{i2}$ in one component of the inverse image of $V_i$; join them by a path there. Each connected component of the complement of the stable skeleton $\Gamma_0$ is an open disk whose only limit point in $Z_0^{\mathrm{an}}$ is one point of $\Gamma_0$ [@bakerpaynerabinoff2013, Lemma 3.4 (3), p. 8]. So a path between points of $\Gamma_0$ that enters such a disk enters and leaves it through that point, and the retraction of the path to $\Gamma_0$ lies inside the path. This gives a simple edge path $P_i$ in $\Gamma_0$, above $V_i$, between distinct vertices of positive genus.
3. *Cycles from a residue torsor* [@openai2026exteriorsheet, proof of Proposition 2.2, pp. 3–4]. Fix a prime $\ell \ne p$. Let $C$ be the geometric special fibre of the stable model of $Z_0$. The normalization sequence makes $H^1(C, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ surject onto the product of the groups $H^1$ of the normalized components, and a smooth component $D$ of positive genus has $H^1(D, \mu_\ell) = \mathrm{Jac}(D)[\ell] \ne 0$. So some étale $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$-torsor on $C$ is connected over the normalized component at each endpoint of each $P_i$. Over a finite extension it lifts to a finite étale cover of the stable model, whose generic fibre $Z_1$ is geometrically connected. Its model is again stable. On normalized components the map is finite étale, so by Riemann–Hurwitz a component of genus $0$ maps with degree $1$ to a component of genus $0$ and keeps at least three node branches, and a component of genus $1$ lies over one of genus $1$ and keeps at least one. If $P_i$ has $m$ edges, its inverse image in the skeleton of $Z_1$ has $m\ell$ edges, since each node has $\ell$ lifts, and at most $(m-1)\ell + 2$ vertices, since each endpoint has one vertex above it. With $c \ge 1$ connected components, its first Betti number is at least $m\ell - (m-1)\ell - 2 + 1 = \ell - 1 > 0$, so it contains a circle, and this circle lies above $V_i$. Let $Z \to X$ be given by a connected component of the fibre product over $Z_0$ of all $\mathrm{Gal}(Z_0/X)$-conjugates of $Z_1$. It is Galois over $X$. Above a circle with $q$ edges and $q$ vertices, a finite étale cover of models of degree $e$ has $eq$ edges and at most $eq$ vertices, so circles persist, and the stable skeleton of $Z$ contains an embedded circle $C_i$ above each $V_i$ [@openai2026exteriorsheet, Proposition 2.2, p. 3].
4. *A degree-$p$ cover split over the exterior* [@openai2026exteriorsheet, Lemma 3.1 and its proof, p. 5]. Let $\tau : Z^{\mathrm{an}} \to \Gamma$ be the retraction to the stable skeleton and $U_Z$ the inverse image of $U$. Choose a point $\eta_i$ at an irrational position inside an edge of $C_i$. It has type $3$, and $\tau^{-1}(\eta_i) = \{\eta_i\}$, because the limit point of an open disk in the complement of $\Gamma$ has type $2$ [@bakerpaynerabinoff2013, Remark 4.13, pp. 13–14]. As $\eta_i$ lies above $V_i$, it is not in $U_Z$, so $\tau(U_Z)$ avoids $\eta_i$. Cut $\Gamma$ at $\eta_i$, take $p$ copies labelled by $\mathbb{F}_p$, and glue one side of the cut in copy $a$ to the other side in copy $a + 1$. This cyclic graph cover of degree $p$ is trivial away from $\eta_i$ and connected over $C_i$. Its pullback along $\tau$ is a finite topological covering of $Z^{\mathrm{an}}$ that splits over $U_Z$ and is connected over the component of the inverse image of $V_i$ that contains $C_i$. Topological coverings of the underlying space of an analytic space are the same as topological covering spaces in de Jong's sense, which are étale [@dejong1995etale, Definition 2.1, p. 91, and Lemma 2.6, p. 93]. The manuscript then algebraizes the cover by Poineau's proper GAGA and descends it from $\Omega$ to $\bar k$ by the invariance of finite étale covers of proper schemes under extension of algebraically closed fields, citing SGA 1, Exposé X, Corollary 1.8. This gives a cyclic finite étale cover of $Z$ of degree $p$ with the same two properties.
5. *The stabilizer quotient* [@openai2026exteriorsheet, §4, pp. 5–6]. Let $T$ be a connected component of the fibre product over $Z$ of the covers from step 4, for all $i$, and of all their $\mathrm{Gal}(Z/X)$-conjugates. Then $T \to X$ is Galois. Put $G = \mathrm{Gal}(T/X)$ and $P = \mathrm{Gal}(T/Z)$, which is elementary abelian of exponent $p$. Every element of $\mathrm{Gal}(Z/X)$ preserves $U_Z$, so all the conjugate covers split over $U_Z$, and so does $T$. Let $H \subseteq G$ be the stabilizer of a connected component of the inverse image of $U$ in $T$. That component maps isomorphically onto a component of $U_Z$, so $H \cap P = 1$. For a component $A$ of $T$ above the component $W$ of $Z$ chosen in step 4, with stabilizer $B$, the degree of $A$ over $W$ is $|B \cap P|$, and it is divisible by $p$, because $A$ maps onto the connected degree-$p$ cover of $W$. All components of $T$ above $V_i$ are $G$-conjugate and $P$ is normal, so $B \cap P \ne 1$ for each of their stabilizers $B$. Put $Y = T/H$. A component of the inverse image in $T$ of $U$ or $V_i$, with stabilizer $B$, maps onto a component of the inverse image in $Y$ of degree $[B : B \cap H]$. For the chosen exterior component $B = H$, which proves 1. Over $V_i$, $N = B \cap P$ is a nontrivial normal $p$-subgroup of $B$ with $N \cap H = 1$, so $B \cap H$ embeds in $B/N$, and $[B : B \cap H] = |N| \cdot [B/N : \mathrm{im}(B \cap H)]$ is divisible by $p$, which proves 2.

**What was checked here.** Each step of the manuscript was rebuilt, and no gap was found. The checks include the following.

- Lemma 2.1: the fibre of $f$ over an $\Omega$-rational point is a free transitive $G$-set, which every component meets, and two points of it in one component differ by an element of that component's stabilizer.
- Proposition 2.2: the Lüroth argument, which needs only that the residue field of the point upstairs contains one of positive genus; the claim that the retraction of the path lies in the path; the Riemann–Hurwitz identity $2g' - 2 = \deg \cdot (2g - 2)$ behind stability of the lifted model; that a $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$-torsor with nonzero class is connected because $\ell$ is prime; the edge and vertex counts, including after the Galois refinement.
- Lemma 3.1: that $\eta_i$ has a singleton fibre under $\tau$, as above; that the graph cover is connected, since $\Gamma \setminus \{\eta_i\}$ is connected when $\eta_i$ lies on a circle; that every component of the pulled-back cover over the component containing $C_i$ meets the connected part above $C_i$.
- Section 4: $H \cap P = 1$, where an element of $H \cap P$ acts trivially on a component that contains a classical point, because $U$ contains an $\Omega$-rational point, and deck transformations of the connected Galois cover $T \to Z$ act freely on fibres over such points; the transitivity giving $B \cap P \ne 1$ for every component over $V_i$; the degree $[B : B \cap H]$, since $B \cap H$ acts freely on the component $A$.
- Hypotheses: genus at least $2$ enters through stable models, skeleta and the Riemann–Hurwitz stability argument.

The following inputs are used as cited by the manuscript and were not opened here: resolution of nonsingularities in the positive-genus form attributed to Mochizuki and Tsujimura, whose preprint is discussed in [its entry](resolution-of-nonsingularities.md); the descent of the chosen semistable model to a finite extension of $k$, which the manuscript asserts; the lifting of finite étale covers from the special fibre (SGA 1, Exposé IX, Theorem 1.10, as cited); Poineau's proper GAGA; Temkin's criterion that étaleness can be checked after analytification; SGA 1, Exposé X, Corollary 1.8; and Milne for $\mathrm{Jac}(D)[\ell] \ne 0$. The results of de Jong and of Baker, Payne and Rabinoff cited above were opened at the given locations.
