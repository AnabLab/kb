**Setting.** A finite group is *supersolvable* if it has a chain $1 = H_0 \subseteq H_1 \subseteq \dots \subseteq H_n = H$ of normal subgroups of $H$ with cyclic quotients $H_{i+1}/H_i$. Subgroups, quotients and finite direct products of supersolvable groups are supersolvable: intersect the chain with a subgroup, take its image in a quotient, or concatenate the chains of two factors. For a number field $K$ inside a fixed $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, let $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ be the compositum of the finite Galois extensions $M/K$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)$ supersolvable, and $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}} = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ss}}/K)$, the maximal prosupersolvable quotient of $G_K$. By these closure properties, a finite Galois extension $M/K$ lies in $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ exactly when $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)$ is supersolvable. A field isomorphism $\sigma : K_1^{\mathrm{ss}} \to K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}$ with $\sigma(K_1) = K_2$ *induces* the isomorphism $g \mapsto \sigma g \sigma^{-1}$ from $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$ to $G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{ss}}$.

For a prime $\ell$:

- $E_\ell(K)$ is the maximal abelian extension of $K$ of exponent dividing $\ell - 1$. It contains $\mu_\ell$, and $E_2(K) = K$.
- $\Omega_\ell(K)$ is the maximal pro-$\ell$ extension of $E_\ell(K)$. It is Galois over $K$; put $\Gamma_\ell(K) = \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K)/K)$. So $\Omega_2(K) = K(2)$ is the maximal pro-$2$ extension of $K$.
- $\mathcal{X}_\ell$ is the class of finite groups $Q$ with a normal Sylow $\ell$-subgroup $P$ such that $Q/P$ is abelian of exponent dividing $\ell - 1$.

Let $\Omega_{\mathrm{odd}}(K)$ be the compositum of the $\Omega_\ell(K)$ over odd primes $\ell$, and let $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)$ be the maximal abelian pro-$2$ extension of $K$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $K$ be a number field.

1. A finite Galois extension $M/K$ lies in $\Omega_\ell(K)$ if and only if $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K) \in \mathcal{X}_\ell$. Every $\Omega_\ell(K)$ lies in $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$, and $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ is the compositum of all the $\Omega_\ell(K)$.
2. Let $\Omega/K$ be a Galois extension with $\mu_\ell \subset \Omega$ in which every element of $\Omega^\times$ is an $\ell$-th power. Then $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/K)$ has trivial centre. In particular every $\Gamma_\ell(K)$ has trivial centre, and so does $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$.
3. Let $K_1, K_2$ be number fields. Each isomorphism $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}} \to G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{ss}}$ is induced by at most one field isomorphism $K_1^{\mathrm{ss}} \to K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}$.
4. $\Omega_2(K) \cap \Omega_{\mathrm{odd}}(K) = K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)$. For $g \in \Gamma_2(K)$ there is a unique automorphism $\alpha_g$ of $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ with $\alpha_g(x)|_{\Omega_2(K)} = g \, x|_{\Omega_2(K)} \, g^{-1}$ and $\alpha_g(x)|_{\Omega_{\mathrm{odd}}(K)} = x|_{\Omega_{\mathrm{odd}}(K)}$. The map $g \mapsto \alpha_g$ induces an injective homomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)/K) \to \mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}})$. If $g$ is nontrivial on $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)$, no automorphism in the class of $\alpha_g$ is induced by a field automorphism of $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$.

Since $K(\mu_{2^\infty}) \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)$, the group $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}})$ is infinite. So for the second question of [the prosupersolvable problem](prosupersolvable-neukirch-uchida-problem.md), uniqueness holds and existence fails, already for $K_1 = K_2$. The failure comes from part 4: $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ is a fibre product of two centre-free groups over an abelian group, so conjugating one factor gives automorphisms that are not inner.

**Proof.** Kummer theory, the approximation theorem and the Galois theory of composita are used without a source. [Chebotarev's theorem](chebotarev-density-theorem.md) is cited from [@milnecft, Chapter VIII, Theorem 7.4, p. 259]. With $C = \{1\}$ it gives infinitely many primes of $K$ that split completely in a given finite Galois extension. Applied to $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_n)/\mathbb{Q}$, it gives Dirichlet's theorem that there are infinitely many primes $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod n$.

*Step 1: groups in $\mathcal{X}_\ell$ are supersolvable.* Let $Q \in \mathcal{X}_\ell$ with Sylow subgroup $P$; induct on $|P|$. If $P = 1$, $Q$ is abelian, and a chain of subgroups with steps of prime index works. Otherwise $W = \{x \in Z(P) : x^\ell = 1\}$ is a nontrivial normal subgroup of $Q$ and an $\mathbb{F}_\ell$-vector space, on which $Q$ acts through $Q/P$. Each element of $Q/P$ acts by an operator $T$ with $T^e = 1$ for some $e \mid \ell - 1$. As $X^e - 1$ has $e$ distinct roots in $\mathbb{F}_\ell$, these commuting operators are simultaneously diagonalizable, so $W$ has a normal subgroup $W_1$ of $Q$ of order $\ell$. Now $Q/W_1 \in \mathcal{X}_\ell$ has a smaller Sylow subgroup, so by induction it has a chain as in the definition. Its preimage, with $1 \subseteq W_1$ added, is one for $Q$.

*Step 2: three facts about a supersolvable group $H$.*

- *A chain with factors of prime order.* Each subgroup of a cyclic $H_{i+1}/H_i$ is characteristic in it, hence normal in $H/H_i$. So the chain refines to one of normal subgroups with factors of prime order.
- *$H'$ is nilpotent.* With such a chain, $H$ acts on each $H_{i+1}/H_i$ through its abelian automorphism group, so $[H', H_{i+1}] \subseteq H_i$. Then the subgroups $D_i = H' \cap H_i$ satisfy $[H', D_{i+1}] \subseteq D_i$, so they form a central series of $H'$.
- *Minimal normal subgroups have prime order.* If $Z$ is minimal normal and $i$ is least with $Z \cap H_i \ne 1$, then $Z \cap H_i = Z$ and $Z \cap H_{i-1} = 1$, so $Z$ embeds in $H_i/H_{i-1}$.

*Step 3: a supersolvable group with a unique minimal normal subgroup $Z$, of order $\ell$, lies in $\mathcal{X}_\ell$.* Call the group $H$.

- *No normal $\ell'$-subgroups.* A nontrivial normal subgroup of order prime to $\ell$ would contain a minimal normal subgroup, which can only be $Z$.
- *$H/P$ is abelian of order prime to $\ell$, where $P$ is the largest normal $\ell$-subgroup.* The Sylow subgroups of the nilpotent group $H'$ are characteristic in $H'$, hence normal in $H$. Those for primes other than $\ell$ are trivial, so $H' \subseteq P$. The preimage of the Sylow $\ell$-subgroup of the abelian group $H/P$ is a normal $\ell$-subgroup, so it equals $P$. Hence $P$ is a normal Sylow $\ell$-subgroup, containing every element of $\ell$-power order.
- *$H/P$ has exponent dividing $\ell - 1$.* Intersecting a chain from Step 2 with $P$ gives normal subgroups $1 = P_0 \subset \dots \subset P_m = P$ of $H$ with factors of order $\ell$. Write $h \in H$ as $h = uv$, with $u, v$ commuting powers of $h$, $u$ of $\ell$-power order and $v$ of order prime to $\ell$. Then $u \in P$. Conjugation by $v$ acts on each $P_{i+1}/P_i \cong \mathbb{Z}/\ell$ by an automorphism of order dividing $\ell - 1$, so $b = v^{\ell - 1}$ acts trivially on each factor. Let $k$ be the order of $b$, prime to $\ell$.
  - *$b$ centralizes $P$.* Suppose $b$ centralizes $P_i$, and let $x \in P_{i+1}$. Then $b x b^{-1} = x y$ with $y \in P_i$, so $b^j x b^{-j} = x y^j$ for all $j$. Taking $j = k$ gives $y^k = 1$, so $y = 1$.
  - *$b$ is central.* Let $C$ be the centralizer of $P$, a normal subgroup. Then $[H, C] \subseteq C \cap H' \subseteq C \cap P = Z(P)$. For $h' \in H$ write $h' b h'^{-1} = z b$ with $z \in Z(P)$. As $b$ commutes with $z$, $1 = h' b^k h'^{-1} = z^k b^k = z^k$, so $z = 1$.
  - *So $b = 1$*, because $\langle b \rangle$ is a normal subgroup of order prime to $\ell$. Hence $h^{\ell - 1} = u^{\ell - 1} \in P$.

*Step 4: every supersolvable $H$ is a subdirect product of quotients in the classes $\mathcal{X}_\ell$.* For $h \ne 1$, let $N_h$ be maximal among the normal subgroups not containing $h$. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of $H/N_h$ contains $h N_h$, so $H/N_h$ has a unique minimal normal subgroup. It has prime order $\ell_h$ by Step 2, so $H/N_h \in \mathcal{X}_{\ell_h}$ by Step 3. And $\bigcap_h N_h = 1$.

*Step 5: part 1.* Let $M/K$ be finite Galois with group $Q$.

- *If $M \subseteq \Omega_\ell(K)$, then $Q \in \mathcal{X}_\ell$.* $P = \mathrm{Gal}(M/M \cap E_\ell(K))$ is normal and isomorphic to $\mathrm{Gal}(M E_\ell(K)/E_\ell(K))$, a finite quotient of the pro-$\ell$ group $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K)/E_\ell(K))$. And $Q/P \cong \mathrm{Gal}(M \cap E_\ell(K)/K)$ is abelian of exponent dividing $\ell - 1$, so of order prime to $\ell$. Hence $P$ is a normal Sylow $\ell$-subgroup.
- *If $Q \in \mathcal{X}_\ell$, then $M \subseteq \Omega_\ell(K)$.* The fixed field $F$ of the Sylow subgroup $P$ lies in $E_\ell(K)$, and $\mathrm{Gal}(M E_\ell(K)/E_\ell(K))$ embeds in $\mathrm{Gal}(M/F) = P$.

By Step 1, $\Omega_\ell(K) \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ss}}$. If $M \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ss}}$, Step 4 applied to $Q$ shows that $M$ is the compositum of the fixed fields $M^{N_h}$. Each lies in $\Omega_{\ell_h}(K)$ by the second bullet.

*Step 6: part 2.* Let $z$ be central in $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/K)$, and let $\omega$ be the mod-$\ell$ cyclotomic character, with values in $(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^\times$. Let $M \subseteq \Omega$ be finite Galois over $K$ with $\mu_\ell \subset M$. Such fields are cofinal in $\Omega$.

- *The Kummer extension of $M$ lies in $\Omega$.* Every $a \in M^\times$ has an $\ell$-th root in $\Omega$, so the field $\widetilde M$ obtained by adjoining all of them to $M$ lies in $\Omega$ and is Galois over $K$.
- *$z$ acts on $M^\times/M^{\times\ell}$ by $\omega(z)$.* Kummer theory gives a perfect pairing $\langle s, a \rangle = s(\alpha)/\alpha$, with $\alpha^\ell = a$, between $\mathrm{Gal}(\widetilde M/M)$ and $M^\times/M^{\times\ell}$. It satisfies $\langle \gamma s \gamma^{-1}, \gamma(a) \rangle = \gamma(\langle s, a \rangle) = \langle s, a \rangle^{\omega(\gamma)}$ for $\gamma \in \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/K)$. As $z s z^{-1} = s$, this gives $\langle s, z(a) \rangle = \langle s, a^{\omega(z)} \rangle$ for all $s$, so $z(a) \equiv a^{\omega(z)} \pmod{M^{\times\ell}}$ for all $a \in M^\times$.
- *$\omega(z) = 1$.* Take a prime $\mathfrak{q}$ of $K$ unramified in $M$ and $a \in K^\times$ with $v_{\mathfrak{q}}(a) = 1$. Then $z(a) = a$, so $a^{\omega(z) - 1} \in M^{\times\ell}$, where $\omega(z)$ is represented by an integer in $[1, \ell - 1]$. At a prime of $M$ over $\mathfrak{q}$ this has valuation $\omega(z) - 1$, which must be divisible by $\ell$, so $\omega(z) = 1$. Hence $z(a)/a \in M^{\times\ell}$ for all $a \in M^\times$.
- *$z$ is trivial on $M$.* Otherwise, take a prime of $K$ that splits completely in $M$. $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)$ permutes the primes of $M$ over it simply transitively, so one of them, $\mathfrak{Q}$, satisfies $z^{-1}\mathfrak{Q} \ne \mathfrak{Q}$. Choose $a \in M^\times$ with $v_{\mathfrak{Q}}(a) = 1$ and $v_{z^{-1}\mathfrak{Q}}(a) = 0$. Then $v_{\mathfrak{Q}}(z(a)) = v_{z^{-1}\mathfrak{Q}}(a) = 0$, so $v_{\mathfrak{Q}}(z(a)/a) = -1$. This contradicts $z(a)/a \in M^{\times\ell}$.

So $z = 1$. The field $\Omega_\ell(K)$ satisfies the hypotheses: if $b \in \Omega_\ell(K)^\times$ were not an $\ell$-th power, the Galois closure of $\Omega_\ell(K)(b^{1/\ell})$ over $E_\ell(K)$ would be a pro-$\ell$ extension of $E_\ell(K)$ strictly containing $\Omega_\ell(K)$. A central element of $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ restricts to a central element of each $\Gamma_\ell(K)$, because restriction is surjective. So it is trivial on every $\Omega_\ell(K)$, hence on $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ by part 1.

*Step 7: an automorphism $t$ of $K$ with $t(a)/a \in K^{\times 2}$ for all $a \in K^\times$ is the identity.* Suppose $t \ne 1$, with fixed field $B$. Then $K/B$ is Galois with group $\langle t \rangle$. Take a prime of $B$ that splits completely in $K$, and a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $K$ over it. Then $t^{-1}\mathfrak{p} \ne \mathfrak{p}$. Choose $a$ with $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a) = 1$ and $v_{t^{-1}\mathfrak{p}}(a) = 0$. Then $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(t(a)/a) = -1$ is odd.

*Step 8: part 3.* Let $\sigma, \sigma'$ both induce the isomorphism, and put $\tau = \sigma^{-1}\sigma'$. It is an automorphism of $K_1^{\mathrm{ss}}$ that commutes with every element of $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$. So it maps the fixed field of each closed subgroup $U$ to the fixed field of $\tau U \tau^{-1} = U$. In particular $\tau(K_1) = K_1$, and $\tau$ preserves each quadratic extension $K_1(\sqrt a) \subseteq K_1^{\mathrm{ss}}$. So $t = \tau|_{K_1}$ satisfies $K_1(\sqrt{t(a)}) = K_1(\sqrt a)$, that is, $t(a)/a \in K_1^{\times 2}$. By Step 7, $t = 1$. So $\tau \in G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$ is central, and $\tau = 1$ by part 2.

*Step 9: part 4.* Write $\Omega_2, \Omega_{\mathrm{odd}}, \Gamma_2$ and $I = \Omega_2 \cap \Omega_{\mathrm{odd}}$, and $G_{\mathrm{odd}} = \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{\mathrm{odd}}/K)$.

- *$I \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)$.* Let $M \subseteq I$ be finite Galois over $K$. Then $M$ lies in a compositum $M'$ of finite Galois extensions $M_j \subseteq \Omega_{\ell_j}(K)$ of $K$, for distinct odd primes $\ell_j$. So $H = \mathrm{Gal}(M'/K)$ embeds in $\prod_j Q_j$, where $Q_j = \mathrm{Gal}(M_j/K) \in \mathcal{X}_{\ell_j}$ by part 1 has Sylow subgroup $P_j$. Then $R = H \cap \prod_j P_j$ has odd order, and $H/R$ embeds in the abelian group $\prod_j Q_j/P_j$. Since $M \subseteq K(2)$, $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)$ is a $2$-group, so the image of $R$ in it is trivial. Hence $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)$ is a quotient of $H/R$, which is abelian.
- *$K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2) \subseteq I$.* A finite subextension of $K^{\mathrm{ab}}(2)/K$ is a compositum of cyclic extensions of degree $2^k$. Each lies in $E_\ell(K)$ for a prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod{2^k}$, and these exist by Dirichlet's theorem.
- *A fibre product.* By part 1, $K^{\mathrm{ss}} = \Omega_2 \Omega_{\mathrm{odd}}$. So restriction identifies $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ with $\{(x, y) \in \Gamma_2 \times G_{\mathrm{odd}} : x|_I = y|_I\}$.
- *The automorphisms $\alpha_g$.* For $g \in \Gamma_2$ and $(x, y)$ in this group, $g x g^{-1}|_I = x|_I$, because $\mathrm{Gal}(I/K)$ is abelian. So $\alpha_g(x, y) = (g x g^{-1}, y)$ is an automorphism with inverse $\alpha_{g^{-1}}$, and it is unique, since an element is determined by its two restrictions. Clearly $\alpha_g \alpha_{g'} = \alpha_{g g'}$.
- *Trivial centralizer.* $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ projects onto $\Gamma_2$ and onto $G_{\mathrm{odd}}$. Both have trivial centre: $\Gamma_2$ by part 2, and $G_{\mathrm{odd}}$ by the argument at the end of Step 6, since $\Omega_{\mathrm{odd}}$ is the compositum of the $\Omega_\ell(K)$ for odd $\ell$. So only $(1, 1)$ in $\Gamma_2 \times G_{\mathrm{odd}}$ commutes with all of $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$.
- *$\alpha_g$ is inner if and only if $g|_I = 1$.* If $g|_I = 1$, then $(g, 1) \in G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ and $\alpha_g$ is conjugation by it. If $\alpha_g$ is conjugation by $h = (h_1, h_2) \in G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$, then $(h_1^{-1} g, h_2^{-1})$ commutes with $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$. So $h_1 = g$ and $h_2 = 1$, and $g|_I = h_1|_I = h_2|_I = 1$. Hence $g \mapsto \alpha_g$ induces an injective homomorphism $\mathrm{Gal}(I/K) \to \mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{ss}})$.
- *Field automorphisms.* Suppose $\alpha_g \circ c_x$ is induced by a field automorphism $\sigma$ of $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$, where $x \in G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ and $c_x$ is conjugation by $x$. Then $\alpha_g$ is induced by $\sigma' = \sigma x^{-1}$, which normalizes $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$, so $\sigma'(K) = K$. As $\mathrm{Gal}(I/K)$ is abelian, $\alpha_g$ acts trivially on it. Hence it acts trivially on its quotient $\mathrm{Gal}(F/K)$, where $F = K(\sqrt a : a \in K^\times) \subseteq I$. So $\sigma'|_F$ commutes with $\mathrm{Gal}(F/K)$ and preserves each $K(\sqrt a)$, as in Step 8. By Step 7, $\sigma'$ fixes $K$. So $\sigma' \in G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$, $\alpha_g$ is inner, and $g|_I = 1$. $\square$

**Scope.** The theorem says nothing about whether $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ determines $K$ up to isomorphism, the first question of the problem.
