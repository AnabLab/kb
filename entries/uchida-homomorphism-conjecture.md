**Theorem.** Let $k_1, k_2$ be number fields, let $\Omega_i/k_i$ be solvably closed Galois extensions, possibly infinite, and put $G_i = \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_i/k_i)$. Let $\sigma : G_1 \to G_2$ be a continuous homomorphism such that $\sigma(G_1)$ is open in $G_2$. Then there is a unique injection of fields $\tau : \Omega_2 \to \Omega_1$ such that $\tau \cdot \sigma(g) = g \tau$ for all $g \in G_1$.

This is Uchida's conjecture [@uchida1981, Conjecture, p. 595], and it is proved in [@openai2026, Theorem 1.1, p. 1]. Uniqueness is due to Uchida [@uchida1981, Proposition 2, p. 600]. Uchida notes that the conclusion means that $\tau(\Omega_2)$ is $G_1$-invariant, that $\tau(k_2) \subseteq k_1$, and that $k_1 \cdot \tau(\Omega_2)$ is the Galois extension of $k_1$ cut out by the kernel of $\sigma$, whose Galois group is then isomorphic to an open subgroup of $G_2$ [@uchida1981, p. 595]. No hypothesis on the kernel is needed.

For example, with $\Omega_i$ algebraic closures of $k_i$: every continuous homomorphism with open image between the absolute Galois groups of two number fields comes from a unique embedding of the algebraic closures, which maps $k_2$ into $k_1$.

**Status.** The proof is in a manuscript credited to OpenAI and dated 5 October 2026, which is not peer reviewed [@openai2026, p. 1]. Each step of its §§2–6 was checked here, as described below, and no gap was found. The [repository](https://github.com/openai/math) that hosts it says that some of its unformalized results could have issues, and its formalization catalogue listed nothing for this manuscript in November 2026.

Earlier sources treat the statement as a conjecture: [@coratosaidi2023, Introduction], for absolute Galois groups, and [@hoshi2026, Introduction]. The work in [@maosaidi2025open, §1] is conditional in the same way. A literature search in November 2026 found no refereed proof, but that search was not exhaustive.

**Inputs.** The manuscript states its external inputs explicitly [@openai2026, §2.2, pp. 4–5]. The rest of the proof uses class field theory, Kummer theory and Chebotarev's theorem. Write $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{sol}}$ for the maximal prosolvable extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, which lies in every solvably closed field [@openai2026, Lemma 2.1, p. 3], and put $G = G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, $P = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{sol}}/\mathbb{Q})$ and $P_M = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{sol}}/M)$ for $M \subseteq \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{sol}}$.

1. [Uchida's isomorphism theorem](uchida-solvably-closed-theorem.md), with its uniqueness. It implies that an element of $P$ centralizing an open subgroup is trivial [@openai2026, Lemma 2.3, p. 4].
2. *Uchida's local correspondence.* Let $M$ be a number field, $N \subseteq \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{sol}}$ a number field, $\varphi : G_M \to P_N$ an open homomorphism and $\ell$ an odd prime. Call a prime $v \nmid \ell$ of $M$ active if $\varphi$ is nontrivial on a pro-$\ell$ Sylow subgroup of the inertia group $I_v$. Then $v$ determines a unique prime $w \nmid \ell$ of $N$ with $\varphi(D_v) \subseteq D_w$ for suitable prolongations, $\varphi$ is injective on that Sylow subgroup and maps it into the inertia group at $w$, and all but finitely many primes of $N$ arise from active primes [@openai2026, Theorem 2.4, p. 4]. This was checked against Uchida, who assumes throughout that $\sigma(G_1)$ is open [@uchida1981, p. 595]. His case ii), which for odd $\ell$ is exactly the active case because case iii) needs $\ell = 2$, gives the prime $w$, its uniqueness, $w \nmid \ell$ and the inertia statement [@uchida1981, §1, pp. 595–596]. His Proposition 1 gives the coverage of almost all primes of $N$ for fixed $\ell$ [@uchida1981, Proposition 1, p. 597].
3. *Hoshi's criterion.* With $\chi_i : G_i \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$ the cyclotomic characters, an open $\sigma$ comes from a field homomorphism $\Omega_2 \to \Omega_1$ as in the theorem if and only if $\chi_2 \circ \sigma = \chi_1$ [@hoshi2025cyclotomic, Theorem 3.4, p. 12].

**Outline of the proof.** For a number field $M$ and a prime $\ell$, let $X_\ell(M) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_M, \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$. Its elements are unramified at every finite prime $v \nmid \ell$, so their values $x(\mathrm{Fr}_v)$ at Frobenius elements are defined.

1. *Reduction* [@openai2026, proof of Theorem 1.1, p. 22]. Embed $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$ in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. The composite $G_{k_1} \to G_1 \to G_2 \to P$ of restriction, $\sigma$ and restriction is open. If it is conjugate in $P$ to restriction, then $\chi_2 \circ \sigma = \chi_1$, because all roots of unity lie in $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{sol}}$, and Hoshi's criterion gives $\tau$. So it suffices to show that every open homomorphism $\varphi : G_F \to P$, for a number field $F$, is conjugate in $P$ to restriction [@openai2026, Theorem 6.2, p. 21].
2. *Frobenius detection* [@openai2026, Proposition 3.2, pp. 8–10]. If $x \in X_\ell(M)$ vanishes at the Frobenius elements of infinitely many primes $v \nmid \ell$ of absolute residue degree one, then $x = 0$. Class field theory identifies $X_\ell(M)$ with the linear forms $\lambda$ on $M \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ that kill the $\ell$-adic logarithms of the global units, and $\lambda(\log b) = -h\, x(\mathrm{Fr}_v)$ when $(b) = v^h$. Take $m > n(n-1)$ such primes, where $n = [M:\mathbb{Q}]$, over distinct rational primes unramified in a Galois closure of $M$. Then the conjugates of the corresponding elements $b$ have pairwise disjoint supports. A rank lemma then says that their logarithm vectors span: if $u_1, \dots, u_m \in (E^\times)^n$ have coordinates that are algebraic integers and $\ell$-adic units, and for every nonzero $a \in \mathbb{Z}^m$ the coordinates of $\prod_i u_i^{a_i}$ are multiplicatively independent modulo roots of unity, and $m > n(n-1)$, then the vectors $\log u_i$ span $K^n$ over the $\ell$-adic field $K \supseteq E$ [@openai2026, Lemma 3.1, p. 6]. The manuscript calls this a special case of the $\ell$-adic rank theorem of Waldschmidt and Masser, and proves it with a zero estimate for translates in $\mathbb{G}_m^n$ and Laurent's interpolation determinants. No Leopoldt-type hypothesis is used. Two variants follow: for $M/\mathbb{Q}$ Galois and infinitely many zero primes with a fixed Frobenius $\delta$, the average of $x$ over $\langle\delta\rangle$ vanishes; and if the corestriction of $x$ to $\mathbb{Q}$ is nonzero, $x$ vanishes at only finitely many Frobenius elements.
3. *Characters on which a Galois group acts faithfully* [@openai2026, Proposition 3.3, p. 10]. For $L/\mathbb{Q}$ finite Galois and totally imaginary, with group $R$ and a complex conjugation $c$, the equivariant Dirichlet unit theorem gives $R$-equivariant embeddings $\mathrm{Ind}_{\langle c\rangle}^R(\mathrm{sign}) \hookrightarrow X_\ell(L) \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Q}_\ell[R]$. So $R$ acts faithfully on $X_\ell(L)$.
4. *Extension to a normalizer* [@openai2026, Lemma 4.1, p. 11]. Choose an open normal subgroup $U$ of $G$ inside $G_F$, and put $N = \ker(\varphi|_U)$ and $T = N_G(N) = G_k$. Conjugation by $T$ acts on $U/N \cong \varphi(U)$, and by Uchida's isomorphism theorem each such automorphism is conjugation by a unique element of $P$. This extends $\varphi|_U$ uniquely to an open homomorphism $\beta : T \to P$. Each conjugate $N_i \ne N$ of $N$ has nontrivial image $H_i = \beta(N_i)$.
5. *Surjectivity and cyclotomic logarithms* [@openai2026, Lemma 4.2, Proposition 4.3 and Theorem 4.4, pp. 11–16]. Given an odd $\ell$ and a finite quotient of $P$, enlarge it to a suitable quotient $R_1 = \mathrm{Gal}(L_1/\mathbb{Q})$ with $\mathbb{Q}(i) \subseteq L_1$. Then adjoin to $L_1$ the square roots of all conjugates of suitable elements, to get $R = \mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})$ with kernel $\mathbb{F}_2[R_1]^m$ over $R_1$. For large $m$ the images of the $H_i$ and the conjugacy class of $c$ are so large that, for every cyclic $C = \langle\gamma\rangle \subseteq R$ and every proper $D < C$, some $x \in X_\ell(L)$ is fixed by $D$, killed by averaging over $C$, and outside $\sum_i X_\ell(L)^{H_i}$. Uchida's correspondence and Chebotarev's theorem give infinitely many active primes $v$ of $k$ whose partners in $L$ have Frobenius $\gamma$. If the image of a Frobenius lift at $v$ generated only some $D < C$, then the first variant in step 2, applied to the pullback of $x$, would put $x$ in $\sum_i X_\ell(L)^{H_i}$. So every element of every finite quotient of $P$ lies in the image of $\beta$, and $\beta$ is surjective. The tame relation $(Nv)^{b} = (Nw)^{a}$ in $\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ at an active pair, where $b$ and $a$ are the residue exponents of an element of $D_v$ and of its image [@openai2026, Lemma 2.6, p. 5], then shows the following. If $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod{[k:\mathbb{Q}]!}$ and $\gamma$ generates $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_\ell)/\mathbb{Q})$, the primes $v$ have residue degree one, and step 2 gives $\log \chi_\ell \circ \beta = \log \chi_{k,\ell}$ on $G_k$.
6. *Matching residue characteristics* [@openai2026, §5, pp. 16–20]. Put $B = \mathbb{Q}(i)$ and $G_K = \beta^{-1}(P_B)$, so that $[K:k] = 2$. For all but finitely many primes $w$ of $B$ above split rational primes $q$, some prime $v$ of $K$ above the same $q$ has $\beta(D_v) \subseteq D_w$ and is active for some $\ell$ as in step 5 [@openai2026, Proposition 5.5, p. 19]. Three facts give this.
   - Almost every such $w$ has only finitely many partners, over all such $\ell$. A character of $G_B$ on which $\mathrm{Gal}(B/\mathbb{Q})$ acts by its sign, minus a multiple of $\log \chi_r$ chosen to vanish at $w$, pulls back to a character of $G_K$ with nonzero corestriction to $\mathbb{Q}$ that vanishes at every partner of $w$; the second variant in step 2 applies [@openai2026, Lemma 5.1, p. 16].
   - For each such $\ell$, at most $2[K:\mathbb{Q}]$ primes of $B$ not above $\ell$ have no partner at $\ell$. Their Kummer classes, pulled back to the cyclic extension $K'/K$ cut out by the finite-order character $(\chi_\ell \circ \beta)\chi_{K,\ell}^{-1}$, are independent, unramified away from $\ell$, and lie in one eigenspace of the $S$-units of $K'$, for $S$ the archimedean and $\ell$-adic places. That eigenspace has dimension at most the number of archimedean and $\ell$-adic places of $K$ [@openai2026, Proposition 5.3, p. 18].
   - If there were infinitely many exceptions $w$, take more than $2[K:\mathbb{Q}] + 2$ of them, $w_1, w_2, \dots$, above distinct primes $q_j$, each with finitely many partners. Chebotarev's theorem in Kummer extensions gives such an $\ell$ for which no norm of a partner of $w_j$ lies in $\langle q_j \bmod \ell\rangle$ [@openai2026, Lemma 5.4, p. 19]. By the tame relation every $w_j$ then has no partner at $\ell$, contrary to the bound.
7. *Identification* [@openai2026, Proposition 6.1 and Theorem 6.2, pp. 20–21]. Let $L \supseteq \mathbb{Q}(i)$ be finite Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$, let $F'/\mathbb{Q}$ be finite Galois with $F' \supseteq K, L$ and $\beta(G_{F'}) \subseteq P_L$, and fix $r$ as in step 5. For almost every $q$ split completely in $F'$, step 6 gives a prime $v'$ of $F'$ above $q$ with $\beta(D_{v'})$ inside the decomposition group of a prime $u$ of $L$ above $q$. By step 5 the image of a Frobenius lift at $v'$ has residue exponent at $u$ with $r$-adic component $1$. So the pullbacks of each $x \in X_r(L)$ along $\beta$ and along a suitable conjugate $\mathrm{Ad}(a) \circ \mathrm{res}$, with $a$ fixed for infinitely many $q$, agree at infinitely many primes of degree one, hence agree by step 2. Step 3 then gives $\beta \equiv \mathrm{Ad}(a) \circ \mathrm{res} \pmod{P_L}$ on $T$, and compactness of $P$ gives one $a$ for all $L$. Finally, for $g \in G_F$ the element $\mathrm{res}(g)^{-1}\varphi(g)$ centralizes the open subgroup $\varphi(U)$, so it is trivial.

**What was checked here.** Each step of §§2–6 of the manuscript was checked, with no gap found, including the following.

- Lemma 3.1: the Zariski density of the cyclic groups generated by the points, the descending sets of common zeros of translates with the bound on their irreducible components, the upper bound for the interpolation determinant and the comparison of exponents, which needs $n/r \ge n/(n-1) > 1 + n/m$.
- Proposition 3.2: the identities from class field theory, the disjoint supports of the conjugates, and the restriction and corestriction arguments.
- Lemma 4.2: the rank $mn(1 - 1/d)$ of $g - 1$ on $\mathbb{F}_2[R_1]^m$ for $g$ of order $d$, and the bound on invariants of $\mathrm{Ind}_{\langle c\rangle}^R(\mathrm{sign})$ from its character.
- Proposition 4.3: the changes of prolongation, the use of uniqueness of the target prime at the level of $L$, the residue degree formula (4.7), and the $U$-equivariant projection.
- Proposition 5.3: that the pulled-back classes are unramified at primes not above $\ell$, because the partner of an active prime is not among the missed primes, and the multiplicity bound for the eigenspace.
- Lemma 5.4: the independence of the radicals, which uses that the ramification index of a listed prime in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m\prod_j s_j})$ divides $[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m):\mathbb{Q}]$, which is smaller than each prime $s_j$.

Not opened: Bass's paper, cited for the equivariant Dirichlet unit theorem, which is a standard result, and the papers of Waldschmidt, Masser, Laurent and Dasgupta, which are cited only as background for Lemma 3.1, whose proof is in the manuscript.

**Consequences.** Hoshi showed that the existence statement of the theorem is equivalent to each of the following. In both, $F \subseteq K \subseteq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, the extension $F/\mathbb{Q}$ is finite, $K/F$ is Galois, and $\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)$ is isomorphic to an open subgroup of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{sol}}$, the [maximal prosolvable quotient](m-step-solvable-quotient.md) of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@hoshi2026, Theorem B]. So both hold, given the theorem.

- For all primes $l$ and all but finitely many primes $p$, every $l$-th root of $p$ in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ lies in $K$.
- If $F/\mathbb{Q}$ is moreover Galois, then $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is Galois.

**Earlier partial results.** Before this proof, the conjecture was known for $k_1 = \mathbb{Q}$, for injective homomorphisms, under a condition on decomposition groups, and under compatibility with the cyclotomic characters. See [open homomorphisms](open-homomorphisms-number-fields.md).
