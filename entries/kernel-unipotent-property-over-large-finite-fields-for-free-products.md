**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $q$ a power of $p$, and write $\mathbb F=\mathbb F_q$. For a pro-$p$ group $G$ with $p$-Zassenhaus filtration $G_{(n)}$, as in [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md), say that $G$ has the *kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_q$* if
$$G_{(n)}=\bigcap_{\rho:G\to U_n(\mathbb F_q)}\ker\rho,$$
over all continuous homomorphisms to the finite group $U_n(\mathbb F_q)$ of upper unitriangular matrices. For $q=p$ this is Mináč and Tân's kernel $n$-unipotent property [@minactan2015kernel, Definition 1.2, p. 2]; the variant over $\mathbb F_q$ is named here. As $U_n(\mathbb F_p)\subseteq U_n(\mathbb F_q)$, the property over $\mathbb F_p$ implies the property over every $\mathbb F_q$.

The inclusion $\subseteq$ holds for every pro-$p$ group and every $q$. For $k\ge1$ let $U^{[k]}\subseteq U_n(\mathbb F)$ be the subgroup of matrices $I+N$ with $N_{ij}=0$ whenever $j-i<k$. If $A=I+N\in U^{[i]}$ and $B=I+N'\in U^{[j]}$, then $ABA^{-1}B^{-1}-I=(NN'-N'N)A^{-1}B^{-1}$ lies in $U^{[i+j]}-I$, and $A^p=I+N^p\in U^{[pi]}$ by the binomial theorem in characteristic $p$. So for every continuous $\rho:G\to U_n(\mathbb F)$, induction on $k$ from the definition of $G_{(k)}$ gives $\rho(G_{(k)})\subseteq U^{[k]}$, and $U^{[n]}=1$. Mináč, Tân and Trà give the same argument for their generalized unitriangular groups [@minactantra2025, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4, pp. 4–5].

**Basic groups.** Call a pro-$p$ group *basic* if it has one of the types (F), (D), (Z) of [free products of Demushkin and free groups](kernel-unipotent-property-for-free-products-of-demushkin-and-free-groups.md), namely a free pro-$p$ group of finite rank, a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank at least $3$, or, for $p=2$, the group $\mathbb Z/2$, or the following type:

- (A) the free abelian pro-$p$ group $\mathbb Z_p^d$ of rank $d\ge2$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $G=G_1*\cdots*G_s$ be a free pro-$p$ product of finitely many basic groups, $s\ge1$.

1. If $m\ge1$, $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$ and $q\ge m$, there is a continuous homomorphism $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_q)$ with $\rho(g)\ne1$.
2. For every $n\ge2$ and every power $q$ of $p$ with $q\ge n-1$, $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_q$.

If no factor has type (A), both parts hold for every $q$, by the [theorem for free and Demushkin factors](kernel-unipotent-property-for-free-products-of-demushkin-and-free-groups.md) and $U_n(\mathbb F_p)\subseteq U_n(\mathbb F_q)$. A single group $\mathbb Z_p^d$ has the property over $\mathbb F_p$ for every $n$, by the [cyclotomic semidirect product theorem](cyclotomic-semidirect-products-preserve-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md) applied with $A=\mathbb Z_p^d$ and the trivial group. So what the theorem adds concerns free products with a factor of type (A) and at least one other factor, such as $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$.

**Corollaries.**

1. *Over $\mathbb F_p$ in low degree.* If $n\le p+1$, part 2 applies with $q=p$: every such $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property. For $p=2$ this gives only $n\le3$.
2. *Fields.* Let $G_0$ be a free pro-$p$ product of finitely many oriented groups, each a finitely generated free pro-$p$ group with any orientation, $\mathbb Z_p^d$ with the trivial orientation, $G_L(p)$ with its cyclotomic character for a finite extension $L/\mathbb Q_p$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity, or, for $p=2$, $\mathbb Z/2$ with the orientation of image $\{\pm1\}$. Then $G_0\cong G_K(p)$ for some field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity, and $G_0$ satisfies part 2 and Corollary 1. So the [kernel-unipotent conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md) holds for $n\le p+1$ for every field $K$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity with $G_K(p)\cong G_0$.

*Proof of Corollary 2.* Each $G_L(p)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $[L:\mathbb Q_p]+2\ge3$ ([maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md)), so $G_0$ is a free product of basic groups. For realizability, Quadrelli's building blocks include the finitely generated free groups with any orientation, the groups of $p$-adic fields and the oriented $\mathbb Z/2$, his operations are the cyclotomic semidirect product $\mathbb Z_p\rtimes_\theta G_0$ and the free pro-$p$ product, and he states that every oriented group so constructed occurs as a maximal pro-$p$ Galois group with its cyclotomic character, because both operations preserve realizability [@quadrelli2024, §3.2, Definition 3.4, Conjecture 3.6 and the paragraph after it, pp. 12–13]. The group $\mathbb Z_p^d$ with trivial orientation arises from $\mathbb Z_p$ with trivial orientation by $d-1$ cyclotomic semidirect products with $\mathbb Z_p$, all with trivial action, and $G_0$ is obtained from the blocks by free products. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.**

*Notation.* Use the notation of the proof of the [theorem for free and Demushkin factors](kernel-unipotent-property-for-free-products-of-demushkin-and-free-groups.md): minimal generators $x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,d_i}$ of $G_i$, the spaces $V_i$, $V=\bigoplus_iV_i$, $U_i=V_i^*$, $U=V^*$, the free algebra $T=\bigoplus_mT_m$, the pairing of $T_m$ with $U^{\otimes m}$, and the identification of $U$ with $H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)$. For a factor of type (A), take for the $x_{i,k}$ a $\mathbb Z_p$-basis; every choice of values on it extends to a continuous homomorphism $\mathbb Z_p^{d_i}\to\mathbb F_p$, so the identification holds as before. For factors of types (F), (D) and (Z) let $\xi_i\in V_i\otimes V_i$ and $\omega_i$ be as there. For a factor of type (A) put
$$\xi_{i,k,l}=X_{i,k}X_{i,l}-X_{i,l}X_{i,k}\in V_i\otimes V_i\qquad(1\le k<l\le d_i).$$
Let $(\xi)$ be the two-sided ideal of $T$ generated by all these elements, and $(\xi)_m=(\xi)\cap T_m$.

For an $\mathbb F_p$-space $W$ write $W^{\mathbb F}=W\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}\mathbb F$. Then $U^{\mathbb F}$ is the space of continuous homomorphisms $G\to\mathbb F$, and the pairing extends $\mathbb F$-bilinearly to $T_m^{\mathbb F}\times(U^{\mathbb F})^{\otimes m}$, tensor powers over $\mathbb F$. For a subspace $N$ of a finite-dimensional space $W$ with dual $W^*$, $(N^{\mathbb F})^\perp=(N^\perp)^{\mathbb F}$ in $(W^*)^{\mathbb F}$ and $N^{\mathbb F}\cap W=N$, as one sees in a basis of $W$ extending one of $N$.

*Step 1: run sequences give representations.* Fix $m\ge1$ and a colour sequence $c=(c_1,\ldots,c_m)\in\{1,\ldots,s\}^m$, with maximal intervals $J_1,\ldots,J_r$ on which $c$ is constant, of colours $\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_r$ and lengths $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r$. A *run sequence* for $c$ is a sequence $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ with $a_j\in U_{c_j}^{\mathbb F}$ such that, for each $q'$, the part $(a_j)_{j\in J_{q'}}$

- is arbitrary if $\kappa_{q'}$ has type (F);
- lies in $U_{\kappa_{q'}}$, with $\omega_{\kappa_{q'}}(a_j,a_{j+1})=0$ whenever $j,j+1\in J_{q'}$, if $\kappa_{q'}$ has type (D);
- has length $\ell_{q'}=1$ if $\kappa_{q'}$ has type (Z);
- has the form $a_j=\lambda_jv$ with $\lambda_j\in\mathbb F$ and one $v\in U_{\kappa_{q'}}^{\mathbb F}$, if $\kappa_{q'}$ has type (A).

So if some run of a colour of type (Z) has length at least $2$, there is no run sequence for $c$.

*Claim: for every run sequence there is a continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ with $\rho(g)_{j,j+1}=a_j(g)$ for all $g$ and $j$.* For each $i$ we build $\rho_i:G_i\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ with $\rho_i(h)_{j,j+1}=a_j(h)$ for $h\in G_i$. Note that $a_j$ vanishes on $G_i$ unless $c_j=i$, and that if $j$ and $j+1$ both have colour $i$, they lie in the same run.

- Type (F). Set $\rho_i(x_{i,k})=1+\sum_ja_j(x_{i,k})E_{j,j+1}$, with $E_{j,l}$ the matrix units, and extend by freeness.
- Type (Z), $p=2$. Put $x=x_{i,1}$ and $N=\sum_ja_j(x)E_{j,j+1}$. No two adjacent positions both have colour $i$, so $a_j(x)a_{j+1}(x)=0$ for all $j$ and $N^2=0$. Hence $(1+N)^2=1$; set $\rho_i(x)=1+N$.
- Type (D). The sequence of restrictions $(a_1|_{G_i},\ldots,a_m|_{G_i})$ lies in $U_i=H^1(G_i,\mathbb F_p)$, and its adjacent $\omega_i$-values, which are the adjacent cup products in $H^2(G_i,\mathbb F_p)$, vanish: inside a run of colour $i$ by assumption, and otherwise because one of the two entries is $0$. Step 1 of the proof of the [theorem for free and Demushkin factors](kernel-unipotent-property-for-free-products-of-demushkin-and-free-groups.md), type (D), gives $\rho_i$ with values in $U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)\subseteq U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$.
- Type (A). For each run $J$ of colour $i$ write $a_j=\lambda_jv_J$ for $j\in J$, and put $N_J=\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_jE_{j,j+1}$. For distinct runs $J\ne J'$ of colour $i$, $N_JN_{J'}=0$: the product $E_{j,j+1}E_{j',j'+1}$ is nonzero only if $j'=j+1$, and then $j$ and $j+1$ both have colour $i$, so they lie in one run. Set $\rho_i(x_{i,k})=I+\sum_Jv_J(x_{i,k})N_J$. Then
$$\rho_i(x_{i,k})\rho_i(x_{i,l})=I+\sum_J\bigl(v_J(x_{i,k})+v_J(x_{i,l})\bigr)N_J+\sum_Jv_J(x_{i,k})v_J(x_{i,l})N_J^2,$$
which is symmetric in $k$ and $l$, so these matrices commute. Commuting elements of the finite $p$-group $U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ are the images of a basis under a unique continuous homomorphism $\rho_i:\mathbb Z_p^{d_i}\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$. The $(j,j+1)$ entry of $\rho_i(x_{i,k})$ is $v_J(x_{i,k})\lambda_j=a_j(x_{i,k})$ for $j\in J$, and $0$ at positions not of colour $i$.

For unitriangular matrices $(MM')_{j,j+1}=M_{j,j+1}+M'_{j,j+1}$, so each $h\mapsto\rho_i(h)_{j,j+1}$ is a homomorphism $G_i\to\mathbb F$; it agrees with $a_j$ on the generators, hence on $G_i$. By the universal property of the free pro-$p$ product there is a continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ restricting to each $\rho_i$, and $g\mapsto\rho(g)_{j,j+1}$ agrees with $a_j$ on every $G_i$, so equals $a_j$.

*Step 2: a finite quotient.* Exactly as in Step 2 of the proof for free and Demushkin factors, which uses only that the $x_{i,k}$ generate $G$: $P=G/G_{(m+1)}$ is a finite $p$-group with $P_{(k)}=G_{(k)}/G_{(m+1)}$ for $k\le m+1$; with $I$ the augmentation ideal of $\mathbb F_p[P]$, Jennings' theorem gives $y-1\in I^k\setminus I^{k+1}$ for $y\in P_{(k)}\setminus P_{(k+1)}$; and the algebra homomorphism $\mu:T\to\bigoplus_kI^k/I^{k+1}$, $X_{i,k}\mapsto(\bar x_{i,k}-1)+I^2$, is onto. Let $K=\ker\mu$ and $K_k=K\cap T_k$.

*Step 3: corner entries over $\mathbb F$.* Let $k\le m$ and let $\rho:G\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$ be continuous, with superdiagonal entries $a_1,\ldots,a_k\in U^{\mathbb F}$. By the Setting, $\rho$ kills $G_{(k+1)}\supseteq G_{(m+1)}$, so it factors through $P$; let $\tilde\rho:\mathbb F_p[P]\to M_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$ be the $\mathbb F_p$-linear extension, an algebra homomorphism. It maps $I$ into the strictly upper triangular matrices, so $\tilde\rho(I^{k+1})=0$, and the $(1,k+1)$ entry of $\tilde\rho\bigl((\bar x_{i_1,l_1}-1)\cdots(\bar x_{i_k,l_k}-1)\bigr)$ is $\prod_ja_j(x_{i_j,l_j})=\langle X_{i_1,l_1}\cdots X_{i_k,l_k},a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_k\rangle$, as in Step 3 of the proof for free and Demushkin factors. Hence $t\mapsto\langle t,a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_k\rangle$ factors through $\mu$ on $T_k$, so it vanishes on $K_k$, and its $\mathbb F$-linear extension vanishes on $K_k^{\mathbb F}$.

*Step 4: $(\xi)_k\subseteq K_k$ for $k\le m$.* As $K$ is an ideal, it suffices that $\mu$ kills each generator of $(\xi)$ when $m\ge2$. For types (F), (Z) and (D) this is Step 4 of the proof for free and Demushkin factors. For type (A), $\mu(\xi_{i,k,l})$ is the class of $(\bar x_{i,k}-1)(\bar x_{i,l}-1)-(\bar x_{i,l}-1)(\bar x_{i,k}-1)=\bar x_{i,k}\bar x_{i,l}-\bar x_{i,l}\bar x_{i,k}$, which is $0$ because $x_{i,k}$ and $x_{i,l}$ commute.

*Step 5: run tensors span the dual of $(T/(\xi))_m^{\mathbb F}$ if $q\ge m$.* Let $R_m\subseteq(U^{\mathbb F})^{\otimes m}$ be the $\mathbb F$-span of the tensors $a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m$ of run sequences, over all colour sequences. We show $R_m^\perp=(\xi)_m^{\mathbb F}$ in $T_m^{\mathbb F}$.

For $\supseteq$: Steps 1 and 3, with $k=m$, give $R_m\subseteq(K_m^{\mathbb F})^\perp$, and Step 4 gives $(\xi)_m^{\mathbb F}\subseteq K_m^{\mathbb F}$.

For $\subseteq$, use the colour decomposition of Step 5 (ii)–(iv) of the proof for free and Demushkin factors, over $\mathbb F$: $T_m^{\mathbb F}=\bigoplus_cV_c^{\mathbb F}$, components $h_c$ and $t_c$, and $(\xi)_m^{\mathbb F}=\bigoplus_cE_c^{\mathbb F}$ with
$$E_c=\sum_{q'=1}^rW_1\otimes\cdots\otimes N_{q'}\otimes\cdots\otimes W_r,\qquad W_{q'}=V_{\kappa_{q'}}^{\otimes\ell_{q'}},$$
where $N_{q'}$ is the degree-$\ell_{q'}$ part of the two-sided ideal of $T(V_{\kappa_{q'}})$ generated by the generators of $(\xi)$ that lie in $V_{\kappa_{q'}}\otimes V_{\kappa_{q'}}$. The argument of (ii) and (iii) there applies unchanged to a factor of type (A) with its several generators, each of which lies in $V_i\otimes V_i$. By (iv) and finite-dimensional duality, $E_c^{\mathbb F}$ is the annihilator in $V_c^{\mathbb F}$ of $(N_1^{\mathbb F})^\perp\otimes\cdots\otimes(N_r^{\mathbb F})^\perp$.

For a run of colour $\kappa$ and length $\ell$, let $R(\kappa,\ell)\subseteq(U_\kappa^{\mathbb F})^{\otimes\ell}$ be the $\mathbb F$-span of the tensors of the sequences allowed for that run in Step 1, so $R(\kappa,\ell)=0$ for type (Z) and $\ell\ge2$. Every run sequence for $c$ has its tensor in $U_c^{\mathbb F}$, and concatenation shows that the run tensors for $c$ span $R(\kappa_1,\ell_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes R(\kappa_r,\ell_r)$. We show below that
$$R(\kappa_{q'},\ell_{q'})=(N_{q'}^{\mathbb F})^\perp\qquad\text{for every run, when }q\ge m.\tag{$*$}$$
Granting this, let $h\in R_m^\perp$. For each $c$ and each run tensor $t$ for $c$, $\langle h_c,t\rangle=\langle h,t_c\rangle=\langle h,t\rangle=0$, so $h_c$ annihilates $(N_1^{\mathbb F})^\perp\otimes\cdots\otimes(N_r^{\mathbb F})^\perp$ and lies in $E_c^{\mathbb F}$. Hence $h=\sum_ch_c\in(\xi)_m^{\mathbb F}$.

Proof of $(*)$, by type of $\kappa=\kappa_{q'}$, with $\ell=\ell_{q'}\le m$ and $N=N_{q'}$:

- (F): $N=0$, and pure tensors span $(U_\kappa^{\mathbb F})^{\otimes\ell}$.
- (D): $R(\kappa,\ell)=C_\ell^{\mathbb F}$, where $C_\ell\subseteq U_\kappa^{\otimes\ell}$ is the $\mathbb F_p$-span of the tensors of chains for $\omega_\kappa$. By part 1 of [the chain theorem](isotropic-chains-detect-the-quadratic-algebra-of-a-nondegenerate-form.md), $C_\ell=N^\perp$, so $R(\kappa,\ell)=(N^\perp)^{\mathbb F}=(N^{\mathbb F})^\perp$.
- (Z): if $\ell=1$ then $N=0$ and $R(\kappa,1)=U_\kappa^{\mathbb F}$. If $\ell\ge2$, then $T_\ell(V_\kappa)$ is spanned by $X_{\kappa,1}^\ell=X_{\kappa,1}^{\ell-2}\xi_\kappa\in N$, so $(N^{\mathbb F})^\perp=0=R(\kappa,\ell)$.
- (A): write $d=d_\kappa$ and $X_k=X_{\kappa,k}$. Let $S_\ell$ be the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $\ell$ over $\mathbb F_p$ in commuting variables $X_1,\ldots,X_d$, and $\pi:T_\ell(V_\kappa)\to S_\ell$ the map that lets the letters commute. The quotient of $T(V_\kappa)$ by the ideal generated by the $X_kX_l-X_lX_k$ is generated by commuting elements, so it is commutative, and the universal property of the polynomial ring gives an inverse to the induced map onto $\mathbb F_p[X_1,\ldots,X_d]$. So $N=\ker\pi$, and $N^{\mathbb F}$ is the kernel of the $\mathbb F$-linear extension of $\pi$. For $v\in U_\kappa^{\mathbb F}$ and $t\in T_\ell^{\mathbb F}$, checking on words gives $\langle t,v^{\otimes\ell}\rangle=\pi(t)\bigl(v(X_1),\ldots,v(X_d)\bigr)$. The tensor of $(\lambda_jv)_j$ is $(\prod_j\lambda_j)v^{\otimes\ell}$, so $R(\kappa,\ell)$ is spanned by the $v^{\otimes\ell}$, and as $v\mapsto(v(X_k))_k$ is a bijection $U_\kappa^{\mathbb F}\to\mathbb F^d$, $R(\kappa,\ell)^\perp$ consists of the $t$ for which $\pi(t)$ vanishes on $\mathbb F^d$. Since $\ell\le m\le q$, the lemma below shows that these are the $t$ with $\pi(t)=0$. So $R(\kappa,\ell)^\perp=N^{\mathbb F}$, and $R(\kappa,\ell)=(N^{\mathbb F})^\perp$.

*Lemma.* If $1\le\ell\le q$, every homogeneous polynomial $f$ of degree $\ell$ over $\mathbb F_q$ in $d$ variables that vanishes on $\mathbb F_q^d$ is $0$.

*Proof.* A polynomial in which every variable has exponent at most $q-1$ and which vanishes on $\mathbb F_q^d$ is $0$, by induction on $d$: its coefficients as a polynomial in the last variable, evaluated at any point of $\mathbb F_q^{d-1}$, give a one-variable polynomial of degree at most $q-1$ with $q$ roots. If $\ell<q$ this applies to $f$. If $\ell=q$, the only monomials of degree $q$ with an exponent at least $q$ are the $X_k^q$; let $c_k$ be the coefficient of $X_k^q$ in $f$. As $a^q=a$ on $\mathbb F_q$, $f'=f-\sum_kc_k(X_k^q-X_k)$ vanishes on $\mathbb F_q^d$ and has all exponents at most $q-1$, so $f'=0$. Its part of degree $1$ is $\sum_kc_kX_k$, which is distinct from its part of degree $q\ge2$, so all $c_k=0$ and $f=f'=0$. $\square$

*Step 6: conclusion.* Let $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$ and $q\ge m$. By Step 2, $\bar g-1\in I^m\setminus I^{m+1}$; choose $t\in T_m$ with $\mu(t)=(\bar g-1)+I^{m+1}$, so $t\notin K_m$, and hence $t\notin K_m^{\mathbb F}$, as $K_m^{\mathbb F}\cap T_m=K_m$. By Steps 4, 1, 3 and 5, $(\xi)_m^{\mathbb F}\subseteq K_m^{\mathbb F}\subseteq R_m^\perp=(\xi)_m^{\mathbb F}$, so $K_m^{\mathbb F}=R_m^\perp$ and some run sequence $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ has $\langle t,a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m\rangle\ne0$. For the representation $\rho$ of Step 1, Step 3 shows that the $(1,m+1)$ entry of $\rho(g)-I=\tilde\rho(\bar g-1)$ is this nonzero number. So $\rho(g)\ne1$, proving part 1.

For part 2, let $g\notin G_{(n)}$ and let $m$ be largest with $g\in G_{(m)}$, so $1\le m\le n-1\le q$. Compose the representation of part 1 with the embedding of $U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_q)$ in $U_n(\mathbb F_q)$ as the upper left block. With the inclusion $\subseteq$ from the Setting, this proves part 2. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Where $\mathbb F_p$ is too small.* The bound $q\ge m$ is used only in $(*)$ for type (A). Over $\mathbb F_p$, with $d\ge2$ and $\ell\ge p+1$, the polynomial $X_1^{\ell-p-1}(X_1^{p}X_2-X_1X_2^{p})$ is nonzero in $S_\ell$ and vanishes on $\mathbb F_p^d$, so the tensors $v^{\otimes\ell}$ with $v$ rational do not span $N^\perp$, and Step 5 fails. For $d=2$ this is the rank-two failure recorded in [the chain theorem](isotropic-chains-detect-the-quadratic-algebra-of-a-nondegenerate-form.md), as $\mathbb Z_p^2$ is a Demushkin group of rank $2$ with alternating cup product. It does not decide the property itself: a single $\mathbb Z_p^d$ has it over $\mathbb F_p$, as noted after the theorem. Whether $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$, for instance, has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_p$ for $n\ge p+2$ is not decided here.
- *The property over $\mathbb F_q$ is restrictive.* This is proved here. Let $N\ge4$, let $S$ be the free pro-$p$ group on $x_1,\ldots,x_N$, and let $G=S/R$, where $R$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by the third term $S_3$ of the lower central series and by the elements $[x_1,x_2][x_i,x_j]^{-1}$ for $1\le i<j\le N$. Then $G$ is torsion-free and $c=[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]$ is not in $G_{(3)}$ [@minactan2015kernel, Appendix, Proposition A.1 (a) and Remark A.2 (3), p. 21]. But for every $q$ every continuous $\rho:G\to U_3(\mathbb F_q)$ kills $c$, so $G$ fails the kernel $3$-unipotent property over every $\mathbb F_q$. Indeed, let $v_i=(\alpha_i,\beta_i)\in\mathbb F^2$ be the superdiagonal of $\rho(\bar x_i)$. In $U_3(\mathbb F)$ the commutator of two matrices with superdiagonals $(\alpha,\beta)$ and $(\alpha',\beta')$ is $I+(\alpha\beta'-\alpha'\beta)E_{1,3}$, for either order convention, so $\rho(c)=\rho([\bar x_i,\bar x_j])$ forces $\det(v_i,v_j)=\delta$ for all $i<j$, with $\rho(c)=I+\delta E_{1,3}$. If $\delta\ne0$, then $v_1,v_2$ is a basis; writing $v_3=sv_1+tv_2$, the conditions $\det(v_1,v_3)=\det(v_2,v_3)=\delta$ give $t=1$ and $s=-1$, and the same computation gives $v_4=v_2-v_1=v_3$, so $\det(v_3,v_4)=0\ne\delta$. Hence $\delta=0$ and $\rho(c)=1$. The appendix by Efrat, Mináč and Tân shows that these groups fail the property over $\mathbb F_p$ once $N>p^3$, by counting [@minactan2015kernel, Appendix, Proposition A.1 (b) and Remark A.2 (2), p. 21]; the argument above needs only $N\ge4$ and works over every $\mathbb F_q$. By the case $n=3$ recorded in [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md), such $G$ is not $G_F(p)$ for any field $F$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity.

Mináč, Tân and Trà describe $G_{(n+1)}$ through representations into generalized unitriangular groups whose corner entry lies in $\mathbb F_p$, when $G=S/R$ with $S$ free pro-$p$ and $R\subseteq S_{(n)}$ [@minactantra2025, Theorem 1.2, p. 2]. For $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$ the defining relation is a commutator, which lies in $S_{(2)}$ but not in $S_{(3)}$, so that theorem describes only $G_{(2)}$ and $G_{(3)}$ there. A search through November 2026 found no statement of the theorem above, but it was not exhaustive.
