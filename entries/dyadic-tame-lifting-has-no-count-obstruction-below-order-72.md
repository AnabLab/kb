**Statement (proved here).** Let $k/\mathbb Q_2$ be finite, let $G=G_k$, and let $\pi:G\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma$ be its tame quotient. For every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$ and finite epimorphisms $q:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow D$ and $r:H\twoheadrightarrow D$, the [marked surjection counts](finite-quotient-criterion-for-frobenius-shift-lifting.md) satisfy
$$
e_r(q\pi)=e_r(q\alpha\pi)\quad\text{if }|H|<72,
\qquad
e_r(\eta)=\#\{f:G\twoheadrightarrow H:rf=\eta\}.
\tag{1}
$$
Only continuous maps are counted, with no identification under conjugacy.

**Proof.** Write $P$ for wild inertia, $A=[P,P]$, and $N=\ker(G\to G_k(2))$. We use the [bound below order 48](dyadic-tame-lifting-has-no-count-obstruction-below-order-48.md), including its reduction for abelian wild images. Every tame automorphism fixes the unramified quotient pointwise.

*1. A normal odd-order subgroup suffices.* Suppose $O\triangleleft H$ has odd order and $H/O$ is a $2$-group. Every homomorphism $f:G\to H$ has $f(N)\subseteq O$, since its composite with $H\to H/O$ kills $N$. Also $f(A)$ is a $2$-group. Thus
$$f(A\cap N)\subseteq f(A)\cap O=1.$$
Every such map factors through $G/(A\cap N)$. The [joint lifting theorem](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-joint-wild-abelian-and-pro-p-quotient.md) lifts $\alpha$ to this quotient, since $\mu_2\subset k$. Precomposition gives the required bijection for every marking, preserving surjectivity. This includes all $2$-group targets.

*2. An order-three action on a nonabelian group of order sixteen.* We prove the group-theoretic assertion needed below: if $S$ is a nonabelian group of order $16$ and $a\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ has order three, then, compatibly with the action,
$$
S\rtimes\langle a\rangle\cong B\times C_2
\quad\text{or}\quad
(B\times C_4)/\langle(-1,z^2)\rangle,
\qquad B=Q_8\rtimes C_3.
\tag{2}
$$
Here $z$ generates $C_4$, and $C_3$ cycles the three quaternion directions.

Put $\Phi=S^2[S,S]$ and $V=S/\Phi$. Lifts of a basis of $V$ generate $S$: a proper subgroup lies in a maximal subgroup, which has index two and contains $\Phi$. If $a$ acted trivially on $V$, each $\Phi$-coset would be stable under $a$. Its power-of-two cardinality is not divisible by three, so it contains an $a$-fixed element. Choosing such lifts of a basis would make $a$ fix generators of $S$, a contradiction. Thus $a$ acts nontrivially on $V$.

Write $t=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}V$. The cases $t=1$ and $t=4$ make $S$ cyclic and elementary abelian respectively. Hence $t=2$ or $3$.

Suppose $t=2$, so $|\Phi|=4$ and the action on $V$ is irreducible. We first show that $a$ fixes $\Phi$ pointwise. This is automatic if $\Phi$ is cyclic. Otherwise $\Phi\cong C_2^2$. If $a$ acted nontrivially on $\Phi$, it would give a three-cycle in $\operatorname{Aut}(\Phi)\cong S_3$. The conjugation image of $S$ in this automorphism group is a $2$-subgroup normalized by that three-cycle; it must be trivial, since no subgroup of order two in $S_3$ is normalized by a three-cycle. Then $\Phi$ is central. For generators $x,y$ of $S$, the derived group is generated by $[x,y]$, and $[x,y]^2=[x^2,y]=1$. Since $S$ is nonabelian, this gives an $a$-stable subgroup $[S,S]$ of order two in $\Phi$, contradicting the three-cycle action. Thus $a$ fixes $\Phi$ in both cases.

Now the conjugation map $S\to\operatorname{Aut}(\Phi)$ is $a$-invariant, with image of order at most two. A nontrivial image would induce an invariant quotient $V\to C_2$, contradicting irreducibility. Hence $\Phi$ is central again, and the same commutator calculation gives $[S,S]\cong C_2$. The abelianization has order eight and generator rank two, so it is $C_4\times C_2$. Its squaring map induces a nonzero $a$-equivariant map
$$V\longrightarrow 2(S^{\mathrm{ab}})\cong C_2,$$
another contradiction. Therefore $t=2$ is impossible.

For $t=3$, $\Phi=\langle w\rangle$ has order two and is central, and $[S,S]=\Phi$. The square map and commutator give an invariant quadratic form and its nonzero alternating polarization
$$
Q:V\to\mathbb F_2,\quad x^2=w^{Q(\bar x)},
\qquad b(\bar x,\bar y)=[x,y]\in\langle w\rangle\cong\mathbb F_2.
$$
Averaging over $\langle a\rangle$ decomposes $V=U\oplus W$, where $U$ is the one-dimensional fixed space and $W$ is the irreducible plane. Invariance gives $b(U,W)=0$. Also $b(U,U)=0$, so $b|_W$ is nonzero. The three nonzero vectors of $W$ have the same $Q$-value; polarization forces that value to be one. Thus the inverse image of $W$ in $S$ is $Q_8$. A lift $c$ of the nonzero vector in $U$ is central, and $c^2=1$ or $w$. These cases give $S=Q_8\times C_2$ or $Q_8\circ C_4$. Moreover $a(c)=cw^j$ implies $a^3(c)=cw^{3j}$, so $j=0$. The quaternion subgroup is $a$-stable and its three directions are cycled. Its order-three actions are conjugate, by the order-eight calculations in the smaller-order bound. This proves (2).

*3. Every tame marking of the central product.* Set
$$H_*= (B\times\langle z\rangle)/\langle(-1,z^2)\rangle,\qquad |z|=4.$$
The embedded $B$ has index two; $z$ is central and $z^2=-1$. A finite tame group has cyclic Sylow $2$-subgroups. Therefore $r(Q_8)$ is cyclic. As in the smaller-order bound, the irreducible cubic action on $Q_8/\{\pm1\}$ forces $r(Q_8)=1$. Since
$$H_*/Q_8\cong C_3\times C_2,$$
the possibilities for $D$ are $1,C_2,C_3,C_6$.

For $D=1,C_2$, $q\alpha=q$, because a $2$-group marking is unramified. For $D=C_3$, the [central-product count](central-product-tetrahedral-embedding-counts-over-dyadic-fields.md) is independent of the cubic epimorphism, so it proves (1).

For $D=C_6$, identify the marking with the quotient $H_*\to C_3\times C_2$, and write its pullback as $(\epsilon,\delta)$. The quadratic character $\delta$ is unramified and nonzero, and it lifts to an unramified character $\lambda:G\to\langle z\rangle\cong C_4$. Its reduction modulo $\langle z^2\rangle$ is $\delta$. Central multiplication gives inverse maps
$$
a\longmapsto a\lambda,\qquad f\longmapsto f\lambda^{-1}
\tag{3}
$$
between epimorphisms $a:G\twoheadrightarrow B$ above $\epsilon$ and epimorphisms $f:G\twoheadrightarrow H_*$ above $(\epsilon,\delta)$.

Here surjectivity needs checking. An image in $B$ mapping onto $A_4$ is all of $B$, since a proper such image would split $Q_8\to C_2^2$. An image $T\subseteq H_*$ mapping onto $A_4$ satisfies $H_*=T\langle z\rangle$. Centrality makes $T$ normal with cyclic $2$-group quotient. Every proper such $T$ lies in an index-two subgroup; the unique index-two subgroup of $H_*$ is $B$, since $H_*^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong C_3\times C_2$. The nonzero quadratic coordinate excludes this. Thus both maps in (3) preserve the stated surjectivity conditions. The resulting count is exactly the [binary tetrahedral count](binary-tetrahedral-embedding-counts-over-dyadic-fields.md), independent of $\epsilon$. Precomposition by $\alpha$ leaves $\delta$ unchanged, proving (1) for the final marking.

*4. Exhausting order forty-eight.* It suffices to consider a target $H$ of order $48$ for which some epimorphism from $G$ has nonabelian wild image $R$. Otherwise the wild-abelian reduction proves (1). The normal subgroup $R$ has order eight or sixteen.

If a Sylow $2$-subgroup $S$ of $H$ is normal, an element of order three gives $H=S\rtimes C_3$. If $S$ is abelian, the wild-abelian reduction applies. If the action is trivial, step 1 applies with a normal $C_3$. In the remaining case step 2 gives exactly $B\times C_2$ or $H_*$. The [direct-product theorem](binary-tetrahedral-direct-products-give-no-dyadic-count-obstruction.md) and step 3 handle every tame marking of these targets.

Suppose instead that a Sylow $2$-subgroup is not normal. Then $|R|=8$, so $R\cong D_8$ or $Q_8$, and $H/R$ has order six. It must be $S_3$: if it were cyclic, the inverse image of its unique subgroup of order two would be a normal Sylow subgroup of $H$. Let $E$ be the inverse image of $A_3$; it is normal of order $24$. Cauchy's theorem gives $E=R\rtimes C_3$. If this cubic action is trivial, $E=R\times C_3$ has a unique subgroup $O$ of order three. It is characteristic in $E$, hence normal in $H$, and step 1 applies. If the action is nontrivial, the order-eight calculations force $R=Q_8$, with the three quaternion directions cycled. Thus $H/R=S_3$ and $E$ acts transitively on those directions. These are precisely the hypotheses of the [symmetric-marking theorem](symmetric-tame-markings-give-no-dyadic-count-obstruction.md). This exhausts all cases of order $48$ without using a table of groups of that order.

*5. The orders between forty-eight and seventy-two.* If every epimorphism to $H$ has abelian wild image, use the wild-abelian reduction. Otherwise a nonabelian normal $2$-subgroup $R$ of order at least eight exists. Apart from $2$-group targets, which step 1 handles, divisibility and $48\le|H|<72$ leave only $|H|=48$ or $56$. The former is settled. In the latter case $|R|=8$ and Cauchy's theorem splits $H=R\rtimes C_7$. The automorphism groups of $D_8$ and $Q_8$ have orders eight and twenty-four respectively, so the action is trivial. Hence $H=R\times C_7$, and step 1 applies. Together with the bound below order $48$, this proves (1). $\square$

**Checks and scope.** The nonnormal Sylow case includes both split and nonsplit extensions of $S_3$ by $Q_8$; their presentations are not needed. In the central-product case, fixing only a cubic marking gives $7104$ lifts over the unramified quadratic dyadic field, whereas fixing the cubic and unramified quadratic coordinates gives $576$. Distinguishing these two marking types is essential. All arguments preserve the actual marked maps and include zero counts.

The bound concerns the order of the finite target, not an index in a canonical quotient diagram. It gives no assertion for order $72$ and constructs no automorphism of $G_k$. The full Frobenius-shift and dyadic classification problems remain open.
