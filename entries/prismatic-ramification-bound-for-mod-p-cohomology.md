**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime, let $K/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, normalize $v_K(K^\times)=\mathbb Z$, and put $e=v_K(p)$. Let $X$ be a smooth proper $p$-adic formal scheme over $\mathcal O_K$, and $i\ge1$. Write
$$T=H^i_{\mathrm{\acute et}}(X_{\mathbb C_K},\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z),$$
and let $L/K$ be the finite Galois extension cut out by $T$.

**Theorem (Čoupek).** Put
$$
\alpha=\left\lfloor\log_p\left(\max\left\{\frac{ip}{p-1},\frac{(i-1)e}{p-1}\right\}\right)\right\rfloor+1,
\qquad
\beta=\frac{iep/(p-1)-1}{p^\alpha}.
$$
Then $G_K^u$ acts trivially on $T$ in Serre [upper numbering](ramification-filtration.md) whenever
$$u>e\alpha+\max\left\{\beta,\frac{e}{p-1}\right\},$$
and, extending $v_K$ to $L$,
$$v_K(\mathfrak D_{L/K})<1+e\alpha+\beta$$
[@coupek2026crystalline, Theorem 1.1, p. 142; Theorem 5.15, pp. 189–190]. For odd $p$ this is also [@coupek2022crystalline, Theorem 1.1, p. 2; Theorem 5.15, pp. 33–34]; that arXiv version assumes $p>2$ [@coupek2022crystalline, Abstract and §1, p. 1], and the published version drops the assumption. The source uses $G_K^{(\mu)}=G_K^{\mu-1}$ [@coupek2026crystalline, §1, p. 141; Remark 5.1, p. 181]. The different bound is proved by transitivity of differents through $K(\pi^{1/p^\alpha})$ [@coupek2026crystalline, proof of Theorem 5.15, p. 189]. The printed proof of the upper cutoff has a gap, described below; the proof given here closes it.

**The case $p=2$.** The source fixes a uniformizer $\pi$ and works with $K_\infty=K(\pi^{1/p^\infty})$ and $K_{p^\infty}=K(\mu_{p^\infty})$. For odd $p$ it quotes Liu for the fact that $\operatorname{Gal}(K_\infty K_{p^\infty}/K)$ is generated by the subgroups fixing $K_{p^\infty}$ and $K_\infty$. For $p=2$ it chooses $\pi$ with $K_\infty\cap K_{2^\infty}=K$, citing Wang [@coupek2026crystalline, §3.1 and Lemma 3.1, p. 161]. The [dyadic disjointness theorem](uniformizer-radical-towers-disjoint-from-the-dyadic-cyclotomic-tower.md) proves this lemma, and shows that at $p=2$ the generation holds exactly for such $\pi$; it fails for $\pi=2$ over $\mathbb Q_2$. The source also states that the arguments it takes from Caruso–Liu and Hattori, which are written for $p>2$, apply to $p=2$ with identical proofs [@coupek2026crystalline, §5.1, p. 183]. The statement of the theorem does not involve $\pi$. Of the dyadic inputs, this entry checks the choice of $\pi$ and steps 2 and 3 of the proof below, which hold for every $p$ and every uniformizer. Step 1 rests on the prismatic comparison and the conditions $(\mathrm{Cr}_s)$ of the source, and is taken from the source at $p=2$ as at odd $p$.

**Proof of the upper cutoff.** Use the numbering $G^{(\mu)}=G^{\mu-1}$ of [@coupek2026crystalline, §5.1, p. 181], which is also that of [@carusoliu2011ramification, §4.2, p. 17]. For a finite Galois extension $M/F$, let $\mu_{M/F}$ be the infimum of the $\mu$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(M/F)^{(\mu)}=1$. Put $s=\alpha$ and $a=iep/(p-1)$. Let $\pi$ be the uniformizer fixed by the source, choose roots $\pi_j$ of $X^{p^j}-\pi$ with $\pi_{j+1}^p=\pi_j$, and set $K_j=K(\pi_j)$, $N_s=K_s(\zeta_{p^s})$, $L_s=LK_s$ and $\mu_j=1+ej+e/(p-1)$. Then $K_j/K$ is totally ramified of degree $p^j$, $N_s$ is the Galois closure of $K_s/K$, and $L_s/K_s$ is Galois.

1. *Over $K_s$.* By definition $\alpha$ exceeds the threshold $M_0$ of [@coupek2026crystalline, Theorem 5.13, p. 187]. So Fontaine's property $(P_m^{L_s/K_s})$ holds for $m=a/p^s$ [@coupek2026crystalline, Proposition 5.14, p. 188]. Proposition 5.3(1) of the source then gives $\mu_{L_s/K_s}\le e_{K_s/K}\,m=a$ [@coupek2026crystalline, Proposition 5.3, p. 182]. Hence $G_{K_s}^{(\mu)}$ fixes $L_s$ for every $\mu>a$.
2. *Restriction to $G_{K_s}$.* For $j\ge1$, the [radical splitting-field theorem](ramification-of-uniformizer-radical-splitting-fields.md), which holds for every $p$, gives $G_K^{(\nu)}\subseteq G_{K_j}$ for $\nu>\mu_j$; for $j=s$ the source states the same through $\mu_{N_s/K}=\mu_s$ [@coupek2026crystalline, proof of Theorem 5.15, p. 189]. Caruso and Liu extend $\varphi_{F_1/F_0}$ and its inverse $\psi_{F_1/F_0}$ to finite extensions that are not Galois, and prove that $G_{F_1}^{(\mu)}=G_{F_1}\cap G_{F_0}^{(\varphi_{F_1/F_0}(\mu))}$ for all $\mu\ge0$ [@carusoliu2011ramification, Lemma 4.3.1, p. 19]. Its proof uses only the corresponding facts for Galois extensions and transitivity, so it applies at $p=2$. Now take $\nu>\max\{\mu_s,\ \varphi_{K_s/K}(a)\}$. Then $G_K^{(\nu)}=G_{K_s}\cap G_K^{(\nu)}=G_{K_s}^{(\psi_{K_s/K}(\nu))}$ with $\psi_{K_s/K}(\nu)>a$. By step 1 this group fixes $L_s$, and hence $L$. This is the argument behind [@carusoliu2011ramification, (4.3.1), p. 19].
3. *The function $\varphi_{K_s/K}$.* By definition $\psi_{K_s/K}(\mu)=\int_0^\mu c(t)\,dt$ with $c(t)=[G_K^{(1)}:G_{K_s}^{(1)}G_K^{(t)}]$ [@carusoliu2011ramification, p. 19], where $G_F^{(1)}$ is the inertia group of $F$. The index $c(t)$ is nondecreasing in $t$, so $\varphi_{K_s/K}$ is concave. Let $1\le j\le s$ and $t>\mu_j$. By step 2, $G_K^{(t)}$ lies in $G_{K_j}\cap G_K^{(1)}=G_{K_j}^{(1)}$, which also contains $G_{K_s}^{(1)}$ and has index $e_{K_j/K}=p^j$ in $G_K^{(1)}$. So $c(t)\ge p^j$, with equality for $j=s$, where the product is $G_{K_s}^{(1)}$. Hence $\varphi_{K_s/K}$ has slope $1/p^s$ beyond $\lambda_s=\psi_{K_s/K}(\mu_s)$. Integrating $c\ge1$ on $(0,\mu_1]$ and $c\ge p^j$ on $(\mu_j,\mu_{j+1}]$ for $1\le j<s$,
$$\lambda_s\ge\mu_1+\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}p^j(\mu_{j+1}-\mu_j)=1+\frac{ep^s}{p-1}.$$
By concavity, for all $\lambda\ge0$,
$$\varphi_{K_s/K}(\lambda)\le\mu_s+\frac{\lambda-\lambda_s}{p^s}\le1+es+\frac{\lambda-1}{p^s}.$$
For odd $p$, Caruso and Liu use that the subextensions of $K_s/K$ are the $K_j$ to find the exact shape of $\varphi_{K_s/K}$, with $\lambda_s=1+ep^s/(p-1)$ [@carusoliu2011ramification, pp. 19–20]; only the inequality is needed here. In particular $\varphi_{K_s/K}(a)\le 1+e\alpha+(a-1)/p^\alpha=1+e\alpha+\beta$.

So $G_K^{(\nu)}$ acts trivially on $T$ for $\nu>1+e\alpha+\max\{\beta,e/(p-1)\}$. With $u=\nu-1$, this is the stated cutoff.

**The gap in the printed proof.** That proof bounds the last lower break $\lambda_{N_s/K}$ from below by the displacement $v(\sigma\pi_s-\pi_s)$ of the single element $\pi_s$ [@coupek2026crystalline, proof of Theorem 5.15, p. 190], as does the arXiv version [@coupek2022crystalline, p. 34]. Lower ramification groups are defined by the displacements of all integers, so the displacement of one element only bounds the break of $\sigma$ from above. The inequality fails already for $p=3$, $K=\mathbb Q_3$, $i=1$, where $\alpha=1$. Take $s=1$, $\pi=3$ and $r=3^{1/3}$, so that $N_1=\mathbb Q_3(r,\zeta_3)$ is totally ramified of degree $6$. Then $v_{N_1}(\zeta_3-1)=3$ and $v_{N_1}(r)=2$, so $w=(\zeta_3-1)/r$ is a uniformizer and $\mathcal O_{N_1}=\mathbb Z_3[w]$. An element $\sigma$ of order $3$ fixes $\zeta_3$ and sends $r$ to $\zeta_3^{\pm1}r$, so $\sigma w=\zeta_3^{\mp1}w$ and $v_{N_1}(\sigma w-w)=4$. Elements of order $2$ lie in no lower group of index above $1$. So $\lambda_{N_1/K}=4$ in the source's numbering, whereas the printed inequality asserts $\lambda_{N_1/K}\ge 6(1/2+1/3)=5$. In general the last lower break is $e_{N_s/K}(e/(p-1)+1/p^s)-v_{N_s}(\mathfrak D_{N_s/K_s})$, so the printed inequality holds exactly when $K(\zeta_{p^s})/K$ is unramified; for $s=1$ it equals $1+pe/\gcd(p-1,e)$ ([last lower break](last-lower-break-of-the-uniformizer-radical-splitting-field.md)). At $p=2$ the inequality fails within the theorem's range already for $K=\mathbb Q_2$ and $i=1$: then $\alpha=2$ and $\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_4)/\mathbb Q_2$ is ramified, so it fails for every choice of $\pi$.

**Scope.** No restriction on $e$ or $i$ [@coupek2026crystalline, §1, p. 142], or torsion-freeness of integral cohomology, is imposed. A [semistable lattice quotient](ramification-bounds-for-torsion-semistable-representations.md) with weights in $[0,i]$ is not assumed [@coupek2026crystalline, §1, p. 143; Remark 5.16(1), p. 191]. The source allows any perfect residue field [@coupek2026crystalline, §1, p. 141]; this entry keeps $K$ finite over $\mathbb Q_p$. For $i=1$ and the algebraic cohomology of a smooth proper $\mathcal O_K$-scheme, the [degree-one theorem](ramification-bound-for-degree-one-cohomology-with-good-reduction.md) gives the sharper cutoff $u>ep/(p-1)-1$, which is $u>2e-1$ at $p=2$.
