**Setting.** Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p \ge 0$. A pointed stable curve $X^\bullet = (X, D_X)$ of type $(g_X, n_X)$ over $k$ is a connected proper curve $X$ of arithmetic genus $g_X$, with at worst nodes as singularities, and a set $D_X$ of $n_X$ smooth points, such that on the normalization every component of genus $0$ has at least three points over nodes or marked points and every component of genus $1$ at least one. This is the standard definition, not cited. The dual semi-graph $\Gamma_{X^\bullet}$ has a vertex for each irreducible component, a closed edge for each node and an open edge for each marked point, and $r_X = \dim_{\mathbb C} H^1(\Gamma_{X^\bullet}, \mathbb C)$ [@yang2018admissible, §2, p. 654]. For a vertex $v$, let $\widetilde X^\bullet_v$ be the normalization $\widetilde X_v$ of its component, marked by the points over nodes and marked points, of type $(g_v, n_v)$ [@yang2018admissible, Remark 1.2.1, p. 652].

A *Galois admissible covering* of $X^\bullet$ is the quotient $Y^\bullet \to Y^\bullet/G = X^\bullet$ of a pointed stable curve by a finite group of automorphisms that is étale over the smooth unmarked locus and sends nodes to nodes. At a marked point it has the form $a \mapsto b^m$, and at a node $u \mapsto s^n$, $v \mapsto t^n$ on the two branches, with $m, n$ prime to $p$ and the decomposition group acting on $s$ and $t$ by mutually inverse roots of unity. The admissible coverings, those dominated by Galois ones, form a Galois category, whose fundamental group $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$ is the *admissible fundamental group* [@yang2018admissible, Definition 2.2, pp. 654–655]. It surjects onto the étale fundamental group of $X$, and onto the profinite completion $\Pi^{\mathrm{top}}_{X^\bullet}$ of the topological fundamental group of $\Gamma_{X^\bullet}$ [@yang2018admissible, §2, pp. 654–655]. If $X^\bullet$ is smooth, $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$ is the tame fundamental group of $X - D_X$ [@yang2018admissible, Remark 2.2.2, p. 656]. In general it is the geometric log étale fundamental group of the stable log curve that $X^\bullet$ defines [@yang2018admissible, Remark 2.2.1, p. 656].

If $p = 0$, then $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$ is the profinite completion of $\langle a_1, \dots, a_g, b_1, \dots, b_g, c_1, \dots, c_n \mid [a_1,b_1] \cdots [a_g,b_g]\, c_1 \cdots c_n = 1 \rangle$ with $(g,n) = (g_X, n_X)$. So it depends only on the type and does not determine $\Gamma_{X^\bullet}$ [@yang2018admissible, §1, p. 650]. In Yang's usage, an invariant *can be reconstructed group-theoretically* from $\Pi$ if isomorphic groups give equal invariants, and an additional structure on $\Pi$ can be if every isomorphism of groups preserves it [@yang2018admissible, §1, pp. 651–652].

**Theorem (Yang).** Let $X^\bullet$ be a pointed stable curve over an algebraically closed field $k$. The characteristic $p$ of $k$ can be reconstructed group-theoretically from $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$. If $p > 0$, so can the isomorphism class of the semi-graph of anabelioids of PSC-type $\mathcal G_{X^\bullet}$ arising from $X^\bullet$ [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 1.2, p. 652, and Theorem 6.9, p. 671].

Roughly, $\mathcal G_{X^\bullet}$ is $\Gamma_{X^\bullet}$ with a Galois category at each vertex and each edge and gluing isomorphisms, in Mochizuki's sense [@yang2018admissible, §1, p. 650]. So for $p > 0$ the group $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$ determines the following [@yang2018admissible, Remark 1.2.1, p. 652]:

- the type $(g_X, n_X)$ and the dual semi-graph $\Gamma_{X^\bullet}$;
- the conjugacy classes of the inertia groups of the marked points and of the nodes;
- for each vertex $v$, the type $(g_v, n_v)$ and the conjugacy class of the admissible fundamental group of $\widetilde X^\bullet_v$ in $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$.

Hence in characteristic $p > 0$, two pointed stable curves of the same type with nonisomorphic dual semi-graphs have nonisomorphic admissible fundamental groups, while in characteristic $0$ their groups are isomorphic.

There is also a solvable version. If $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$ is replaced by its maximal prosolvable quotient, and $\mathcal G_{X^\bullet}$ by the semi-graph of anabelioids of prosolvable PSC-type $\mathcal G^{\mathrm{sol}}_{X^\bullet}$, whose vertex groups are the corresponding prosolvable groups, then the proof gives the same conclusion [@yang2018admissible, Remark 1.2.2, p. 652]. That remark does not say that the full vertex groups are recovered from the prosolvable quotient.

Yang calls this a version of the combinatorial Grothendieck conjecture in characteristic $p$. That conjecture concerns pro-$\Sigma$ semi-graphs of anabelioids, with $\Sigma$ not containing the characteristics, and assumes in addition outer Galois actions on the two fundamental groups compatible with the given isomorphism. Mochizuki proved it for actions of IPSC-type, and Hoshi and Mochizuki for certain actions of NN-type [@yang2018admissible, Remark 6.9.1, pp. 671–672]. In characteristic $p$ no action is needed. The extra information comes from $p$-ranks of coverings. Mochizuki's criterion with vertex and edge filtrations on abelianizations of covers is recorded as [the combinatorial Grothendieck theorem](combinatorial-grothendieck-conjecture.md).

**How the semi-graph is read off.**

1. *The characteristic and the $p$-rank.* The prime $p$ is the unique prime $\ell$ at which $\dim_{\mathbb F_\ell}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}_{X^\bullet} \otimes \mathbb F_\ell)$ differs from its value at every other prime [@yang2018admissible, Proposition 6.1 and proof, p. 664]. The dimension at $p$ is the $p$-rank $\sigma(X^\bullet) = \sum_v \sigma(\widetilde X_v) + r_X$ [@yang2018admissible, Definition 2.3 and Remark 2.3.1, p. 656].
2. *Averages of $p$-ranks.* For a profinite group $\Pi$, let $\Pi(N)$ be the closure of $[\Pi,\Pi]\Pi^N$, and put $\gamma^{\mathrm{av}}_p(N) = \dim_{\mathbb F_p}(\Pi(N)^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb F_p)/[\Pi : \Pi(N)]$. Let $\Gamma^{\mathrm{cpt}}$ be $\Gamma_{X^\bullet}$ itself if there are no open edges, and otherwise $\Gamma_{X^\bullet}$ with one new vertex at which all open edges end [@yang2018admissible, Definition 2.1 (c), p. 653]. Let $b(v)$ be the number of edges at $v$, a loop counted twice. Suppose that removing any one vertex from $\Gamma^{\mathrm{cpt}}$ leaves it empty or connected. Then, by a theorem of Tamagawa,
   $$\lim_{t \to \infty} \gamma^{\mathrm{av}}_p(p^t - 1)(\Pi_{X^\bullet}) = g_X - r_X - \#\{v : b(v) \le 1\}$$
   [@yang2018admissible, Definition 2.1, pp. 653–654, Definition 2.4, pp. 656–657, and Proposition 2.5, p. 657]. The group determines an open characteristic subgroup whose covering satisfies this hypothesis and has no vertex with $b(v) \le 1$, and whose covering also satisfies Condition 4.2, used in step 4 [@yang2018admissible, Condition 6.2 and Lemma 6.3, p. 665]. Under these conditions Yang recovers $g_X$, $n_X$, $r_X$ and the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $\Pi^{\mathrm{top}}_{X^\bullet}$. One step: if $\Pi_{X^\bullet}/H$ is a $p$-group, every component of the covering $X_H$ has trivial decomposition group exactly when $g_{X_H} - r_{X_H} = [\Pi_{X^\bullet} : H](g_X - r_X)$ [@yang2018admissible, Lemma 6.4 and proof, pp. 666–667].
3. *Components of positive $p$-rank.* Suppose some component has normalization of positive $p$-rank. Let $M = \operatorname{Hom}(\Pi_{X^\bullet}, \mathbb F_p) = H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(X, \mathbb F_p)$, containing $M^{\mathrm{top}} = H^1(\Gamma_{X^\bullet}, \mathbb F_p)$. For $\alpha \in M - M^{\mathrm{top}}$, let $X_\alpha \to X$ be the étale $\mathbb Z/p$-covering. Let $V$ be the set of such $\alpha$ for which $X_\alpha$ has the largest possible number $p(\#\mathrm{Irr}(X) - 1) + 1$ of irreducible components. For these, there is exactly one component $X^\alpha_v$ such that the pullback of $X_\alpha$ to its normalization $\widetilde X_v$ is nontrivial. Equivalently, $X^\alpha_v$ is the only component with a single irreducible component of $X_\alpha$ over it. Yang writes "a nontrivial étale covering over $X_v$" [@yang2018admissible, §3, p. 658], and his proof of Lemma 3.3 works with components upstairs that have nontrivial decomposition group. Over a singular component the phrase must be read after pullback to the normalization, as the check below explains. Write $\alpha \sim \beta$ if every $\lambda\alpha + \mu\beta$ with $\lambda, \mu \in \mathbb F_p^\times$ that lies outside $M^{\mathrm{top}}$ lies in $V$. Then $\sim$ is an equivalence relation, and $\alpha \mapsto X^\alpha_v$ identifies $V/\sim$ with the set of components whose normalization has positive $p$-rank [@yang2018admissible, §3, p. 658, and Lemma 3.3 and proof, pp. 658–659]. Under the conditions of step 2 the group determines all of this [@yang2018admissible, Corollary 6.5, p. 667].
4. *Vertices, then edges.* Every pointed stable curve has a connected Galois admissible covering whose components are all smooth, of genus at least $2$ and of positive $p$-rank [@yang2018admissible, Proposition 3.2, p. 657]. Taking components of positive $p$-rank of coverings modulo the Galois action, and passing to the limit, gives the vertices and the maps on vertices induced by coverings [@yang2018admissible, Proposition 6.6 and proof, pp. 667–669]. Counting nodes of coverings by the Euler–Poincaré formula recovers the étale fundamental group, and then Hoshi and Mochizuki's pro-$\ell$ combinatorial anabelian geometry, for $\ell \ne p$, gives the pro-$\ell$ semi-graph of anabelioids [@yang2018admissible, Proposition 5.3, p. 663, and Lemma 6.7 and proof, p. 669]. Edges are matched after passing to the covering of Lemma 6.3, which satisfies Condition 4.2: it is untangled and sturdy, two distinct components that meet do so in at least $3$ points, and a component that contains a marked point contains at least $3$ [@yang2018admissible, Condition 4.2, p. 660]. For such a curve and a prime $\ell \ne 2, p$, each marked point $q$ has a Galois admissible covering of degree $\ell$ that is étale over $q$ and totally ramified over the other marked points, and likewise for nodes [@yang2018admissible, Propositions 4.3 and 4.4, p. 660]. These coverings match the edges [@yang2018admissible, Lemma 6.8 and proof, pp. 669–670]. Condition 6.2 alone would not give them. On a smooth curve with exactly two marked points, suitable generators $c_1, c_2$ of the inertia groups satisfy $c_1 c_2 = \prod [a_i, b_i]$, so an abelian covering ramified over one of them is ramified over the other.

Yang does not discuss whether the reconstruction is effective. Proposition 2.5 is a limit statement. The proof of Lemma 6.3 uses a prime $\ell \gg 0$ and the finiteness of the set of pro-$\ell$ semi-graphs of anabelioids with a given fundamental group, without listing them [@yang2018admissible, proof of Lemma 6.3, pp. 665–666].

**Checks, done here.**

- *Smooth curves.* If $X^\bullet$ is smooth, then $\Gamma_{X^\bullet}$ has one vertex $v$, with $b(v) = n_X$, and $r_X = 0$. If $n_X = 0$, then $\Gamma^{\mathrm{cpt}} = \Gamma_{X^\bullet}$ is a single vertex. Otherwise $\Gamma^{\mathrm{cpt}}$ has two vertices joined by $n_X$ edges. Either way, removing a vertex leaves nothing or a single vertex. So the limit in step 2 is $g_X - 1$ if $n_X \le 1$ and $g_X$ if $n_X \ge 2$. This is Tamagawa's limit $g'$ for tame fundamental groups ([Tamagawa's theorems](tame-fundamental-groups-in-positive-characteristic.md)). The two averages agree, since $[\Pi : \Pi(N)] = \#(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb Z/N)$.
- *The criterion in step 2.* Let $\Pi_{X^\bullet}/H$ be a $p$-group of order $d$. The inertia groups of an admissible covering at nodes and marked points have order prime to $p$, by the local forms above, so $X_H \to X$ is finite étale. Normalization commutes with étale base change. So over $\widetilde X_v$ there are $d/|D_v|$ components of the normalization of $X_H$, each étale of degree $|D_v|$ over $\widetilde X_v$, where $D_v$ is the decomposition group of one of them. By Riemann–Hurwitz each has genus $|D_v|(g(\widetilde X_v) - 1) + 1$. A connected nodal curve has $g - r = \sum_v g(\widetilde X_v)$, by the arithmetic genus formula. Hence
  $$g_{X_H} - r_{X_H} = \sum_v \Big( d\,\big(g(\widetilde X_v) - 1\big) + \frac{d}{|D_v|} \Big) = d\,(g_X - r_X) - d \sum_v \Big(1 - \frac{1}{|D_v|}\Big).$$
  So $g_{X_H} - r_{X_H} \le d(g_X - r_X)$, with equality exactly when every $D_v$ is trivial.
- *The count in step 3.* Let $\alpha \ne 0$, and let $m$ be the number of vertices $v$ such that the pullback $\alpha|_{\widetilde X_v} \in H^1(\widetilde X_v, \mathbb F_p)$ is nonzero. Normalization commutes with étale base change, so the irreducible components of $X_\alpha$ over $X_v$ correspond to the connected components of the $\mathbb Z/p$-torsor $X_\alpha \times_X \widetilde X_v$ over the smooth connected curve $\widetilde X_v$. If $\alpha|_{\widetilde X_v} \ne 0$, this torsor is connected, since a nontrivial $\mathbb Z/p$-torsor over a connected base is connected. Otherwise it is $p$ copies of $\widetilde X_v$. So $\#\mathrm{Irr}(X_\alpha) = p(\#\mathrm{Irr}(X) - m) + m$. Counting components where the covering is nontrivial over the singular component itself gives the wrong number. Let $A$ be an irreducible nodal rational curve and $B$ an ordinary elliptic curve, meeting at one point, a stable curve of type $(2,0)$. Take $\alpha$ with nonzero monodromy around the node of $A$ and nonzero restriction to $B$. Then $X_\alpha$ is nontrivial over both $A$ and $B$, but it has $p$ irreducible components over $A$, a cycle of rational curves, and $1$ over $B$. The total is $p + 1 = p(2 - 1) + 1$, with $m = 1$ as defined here. Each node has $p$ preimages, and $X_\alpha$ is connected because $\alpha \ne 0$, so the Euler–Poincaré formula gives $r_{X_\alpha} - p\, r_X = (p - 1)(m - 1)$. The sequence $0 \to \mathbb F_p \to \nu_* \mathbb F_p \to \bigoplus_{\text{nodes}} \mathbb F_p \to 0$, for the normalization $\nu$, shows the following. $H^1(X, \mathbb F_p)$ maps onto $\bigoplus_v H^1(\widetilde X_v, \mathbb F_p)$, with kernel $H^1(\Gamma_{X^\bullet}, \mathbb F_p)$. So $m \ge 1$ exactly when $\alpha \notin M^{\mathrm{top}}$, and $m = 1$ occurs whenever some component has positive $p$-rank. The maximum $p(\#\mathrm{Irr}(X) - 1) + 1$ is attained exactly when $m = 1$, that is, when $r_{X_\alpha} = p\, r_X$, as Yang states [@yang2018admissible, §3, p. 658].

**Applications.**

- *Components of genus $0$.* Suppose every $\widetilde X_v$ has genus $0$. Then $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$ determines whether $X^\bullet$ can be defined over $\bar{\mathbb F}_p$. If moreover $k = \bar{\mathbb F}_p$ and $X$ is irreducible, then $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$ determines $X - D_X$ up to isomorphism of schemes [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 1.3 (a), p. 653]. Now let $k_1 = k_2 = \bar{\mathbb F}_p$, let every normalized component of $X_1^\bullet$ have genus $0$, and let $\Pi_{X_1^\bullet} \cong \Pi_{X_2^\bullet}$, inducing $\gamma : \Gamma_{X_1^\bullet} \cong \Gamma_{X_2^\bullet}$. Then the components at $v$ and $\gamma(v)$ are isomorphic as schemes [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 7.6 (ii-b), p. 675]. The proofs combine the theorem with Tamagawa's genus-$0$ theorem [@yang2018admissible, proof of Theorem 7.6, p. 675].
- *Finiteness.* If $k = \bar{\mathbb F}_p$, only finitely many $k$-isomorphism classes of pointed stable curves over $k$ have admissible fundamental group isomorphic to $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$ [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 1.3 (b), p. 653].
- *Specialization.* Let $\mathscr X^\bullet$ be a pointed stable curve over an $\mathbb F_p$-scheme $S$, and $s$ a specialization of a point $\eta$ of $S$. Suppose the geometric fibre over $\eta$ cannot be defined over an algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_p$, and the one over $s$ can. Then the specialization map of admissible fundamental groups is not an isomorphism [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 7.9, p. 676]. This extends Theorem 7.8, the case of smooth curves, which Yang attributes to Raynaud and to Pop and Saïdi under conditions, and to Tamagawa in general [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 7.8 and Remark 7.8.1, p. 676]. His proof cites the theorem above for the specialization statement, and the theorem together with Theorem 7.8 for the second part of Theorem 7.9, a finiteness statement [@yang2018admissible, proof of Theorem 7.9, p. 676]. See [variation of fundamental groups in characteristic $p$](variation-of-fundamental-groups-of-curves-in-positive-characteristic.md).
- *Essential dimension.* Let $p > 0$, and let $\mathrm{ed}(X^\bullet)$ be the least transcendence degree over $\bar{\mathbb F}_p$ of an algebraically closed subfield $k_1 \subseteq k$ such that $X^\bullet \cong X_1^\bullet \times_{k_1} k$ over $k$ for some curve $X_1^\bullet$ over $k_1$ [@yang2018admissible, Definition 7.1, p. 672]. The definition is printed with a smooth $X_1^\bullet$. For singular $X^\bullet$, as in Conjecture 7.5, it has to be read with pointed stable curves. Tamagawa conjectured that for smooth $X^\bullet$, $\mathrm{ed}(X^\bullet)$ can be reconstructed group-theoretically from $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$. Yang conjectures the same for all pointed stable curves [@yang2018admissible, Conjectures 7.2 and 7.5, pp. 673–674]. His Theorem 7.6 (i-a) states that Tamagawa's conjecture implies his. The proof rests on the formula $\mathrm{ed}(X^\bullet) = \max_v \mathrm{ed}(X^\bullet_v)$, with $X^\bullet_v$ the irreducible component at $v$ [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 7.6 (i-a) and proof, pp. 674–675]. That formula is false, as the check below shows, so the implication is not recorded here as proved. The counterexample refutes only the formula, not the implication between the conjectures.

*Check, done here: the maximum formula fails.* Let $t, u$ be algebraically independent over $\bar{\mathbb F}_p$, and let $k$ be an algebraic closure of $\bar{\mathbb F}_p(t,u)$. Glue two copies of $\mathbb P^1_k$ at their points $\infty$, and mark $0, 1, t$ on the first and $0, 1, u$ on the second. This is a pointed stable curve $X^\bullet$ of type $(0,6)$, and both components are smooth.

- Each component with its three marks and the node has $\mathrm{ed} = 1$. Read with only its three marks, each has $\mathrm{ed} = 0$, since any three points of $\mathbb P^1$ can be moved to $0, 1, \infty$. So on either reading the maximum is at most $1$.
- $\mathrm{ed}(X^\bullet) = 2$. It is at most $2$, since $\operatorname{trdeg}_{\bar{\mathbb F}_p} k = 2$. Conversely, let $X^\bullet \cong X_1^\bullet \times_{k_1} k$ with $k_1$ algebraically closed. The components of $X_1$ are smooth of genus $0$ over $k_1$, so they are copies of $\mathbb P^1_{k_1}$ with their node and marks at $k_1$-points. So the first component of $X^\bullet$ is carried by an automorphism of $\mathbb P^1_k$ onto $\mathbb P^1_k$ with the node at a $k_1$-point and the three marks at $k_1$-points. Cross-ratios are invariant under automorphisms of $\mathbb P^1$, and in any order the cross-ratio of $(0, 1, t, \infty)$ is one of $t$, $1-t$, $1/t$, $1/(1-t)$, $t/(t-1)$ and $(t-1)/t$. So one of these lies in $k_1$, and hence $t \in k_1$. Likewise $u \in k_1$, so $\operatorname{trdeg} k_1 \ge 2$. The same argument, with the component defined over the algebraic closure of $\bar{\mathbb F}_p(t)$ in $k$, gives the value $1$ in the first bullet.

The inequality $\mathrm{ed}(X^\bullet) \ge \max_v \mathrm{ed}(\widetilde X^\bullet_v)$ does hold. If $X^\bullet$ descends to an algebraically closed $k_1$, so do its components, their normalizations and the points over nodes and marks, because normalization commutes with extension of a perfect base field. Equality holds when the right side is $0$. If every $\widetilde X^\bullet_v$ descends to $\bar{\mathbb F}_p$, its marked points are $\bar{\mathbb F}_p$-points. Gluing the descended components at the same pairs of points gives a curve over $\bar{\mathbb F}_p$ whose base change is $X^\bullet$, since gluing along finite sets of points commutes with flat base change (standard). This case is all that the genus-$0$ detection statement needs [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 1.3 (a), p. 653, and Theorem 7.6 (i-b), p. 674]. Theorem 6.9 recovers each $\Pi_{\widetilde X^\bullet_v}$ up to conjugacy, and Tamagawa's genus-$0$ theorem detects from it whether $\widetilde X^\bullet_v$ descends to $\bar{\mathbb F}_p$ [@yang2018admissible, Theorem 7.3, p. 673].

*Check, done here: the [weak Isom-version conjecture](weak-isom-conjecture-in-positive-characteristic.md) implies Tamagawa's conjecture on $\mathrm{ed}$.* Let $X^\bullet$ be smooth of type $(g,n)$, with moduli point $q$ in $M_{g,n,\mathbb F_p}$ and closure $V_q$. We show that $\mathrm{ed}(X^\bullet) = \operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb F_p} k(q) = \dim V_q$.

- If $X^\bullet \cong X_1^\bullet \times_{k_1} k$, the classifying morphism of $X^\bullet$ factors through $\operatorname{Spec} k_1$, so $k(q)$ embeds in $k_1$ and $\operatorname{trdeg} k_1 \ge \operatorname{trdeg} k(q)$.
- Conversely, let $k_1$ be the algebraic closure in $k$ of the image of $k(q)$. Over an algebraically closed field, isomorphism classes of curves correspond bijectively to points of the coarse moduli space (standard). So the $k_1$-point given by $q$ comes from a curve $X_1^\bullet$ over $k_1$, and $X_1^\bullet \times_{k_1} k \cong X^\bullet$, since both give the same $k$-point.
- The equality $\operatorname{trdeg} k(q) = \dim V_q$ holds for any point of a scheme of finite type over a field (standard).

The tame fundamental group determines $p$ and $(g,n)$ (Tamagawa). So if it also determines $V_q$, as the weak Isom-version conjecture predicts, it determines $\mathrm{ed}(X^\bullet)$.

**Type $(1,1)$.** Yang recalls Sarashina's theorem that étale fundamental groups distinguish smooth curves of type $(1,1)$ over algebraic closures of finite fields of characteristic $\ne 2$, up to isomorphism of schemes. He writes that by Tamagawa's theory this also holds for admissible fundamental groups, citing Tamagawa's 2015 lecture slides [@yang2018admissible, Remark 7.4.1, p. 674]. From this he deduces that among pointed stable curves of type $(1,1)$ over algebraic closures of finite fields, $\Pi_{X^\bullet}$ determines $X - D_X$ up to isomorphism of schemes, with $p \ne 2$ assumed when a component has type $(1,1)$ [@yang2018admissible, Remark 7.6.2, pp. 675–676]. The slides list the cited theorem as "in progress". They state it only for $p \ne 2$ and for curves that, together with their $2$-torsion points, are defined over $\mathbb F_p$ [@tamagawa2015slides, slide 46]. So neither statement is recorded here as proved.
