**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $G = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, $T$ the maximal tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, $V = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p/T)$ the wild inertia group and $\mathcal{G} = G/V = \mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_p)$. Let $V^1 = V$ and $V^{i+1} = (V^i)^p[V^i, V]$ be the descending $p$-central series [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 421], so $V^2 = V^p[V,V]$ is the Frattini subgroup of $V$. Every automorphism of $G$ preserves $V$, the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), and hence each $V^i$. Put $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathcal{G}]]$; conjugation makes $V/V^2$ a $\Lambda$-module.

**Theorem.** This is proved here, from a construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg and a theorem of Nishio.

1. *An involution.* There is an automorphism $\gamma$ of $G$ with $\gamma^2 = 1 \ne \gamma$ that induces the identity on $G/V$. It is not inner, so its class in $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ has order $2$.
2. *Tame fields.* Let $K \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ be a finite tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. This includes every $K$ with $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] \le 2$, since $p$ is odd. Then $\gamma(G_K) = G_K$, and $\gamma_K = \gamma|_{G_K}$ is an automorphism of $G_K$ of order $2$ that is not inner. So $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has an element of order $2$, and the subgroup it generates is not quasi-geometric.
3. *Not an absolute Galois group.* For $K$ as in part 2, the centralizer of $\gamma_K$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ has more than two elements. Hence $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ is not isomorphic, even as an abstract group, to the absolute Galois group of any field.

**Consequences.** See [finite subgroups of outer automorphism groups](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) for the definitions and for Nishio's results used here.

- *Nishio's Problem 1 for odd $p$.* Nishio asks whether every finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ is quasi-geometric [@nishio2026finite, Introduction, p. 2], and shows that it is not when $p$ is odd and $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 3$ [@nishio2026finite, Corollary 3.4, p. 15]. Part 2 covers every $k$ with $p$ odd and $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \le 2$. So for every finite $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ with $p$ odd, $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ has a finite subgroup that is not quasi-geometric. For $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$, where Problem 1 asks exactly whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ is torsion-free (consequence 2 of that entry), part 1 says that it is not.
- *Field automorphisms and ramification.* By consequence 1 of that entry, the class of $\gamma_K$ lies in no $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$-conjugate of $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$. By consequence 4, $\gamma_K$ does not preserve the filtration of $G_K$ by its higher ramification groups in upper numbering of index $> 0$.
- *Degree.* Consequence 5 of that entry shows that $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ is not an absolute Galois group when $p$ is odd and $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 5$, using a subgroup $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$. Part 3 reaches every tamely ramified $k$, in every degree, through a centralizer instead. Applied to Nishio's involutions through a fixed-coset form of the lemma below, the same argument covers every $k$ with $p$ odd, including the wildly ramified ones of degree $3$ or $4$: see [centralizers of involutions](centralizers-of-involutions-in-p-adic-galois-automorphism-groups.md).

**Proof of part 1.**

*(a) The construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg.* Let $\alpha : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ give the action on $\mu_p$. Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg use generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\mathcal{G}$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^p$, $\alpha(\sigma) = 1$ and $\alpha(\tau)$ a primitive $(p-1)$-th root of unity [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422]. They define
$$E = \lim_K \frac{1}{e}\sum_{i=0}^{e-1} \alpha(\tau)^{-i}\tau^{2i} \in \Lambda,$$
where $K$ runs through the finite tamely ramified Galois extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $e$ is the ramification index of $K$, and the sum is computed in $\mathbb{F}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}_p)]$. (It makes sense when $p - 1$ divides $e$, and these levels are cofinal.) They show that $E$ is a central idempotent, so that $\varepsilon = 1 - 2E$ is a central unit with $\varepsilon^2 = 1$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 421–422]. They fix a splitting $G = V \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, let $F = P_2 \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ with $P_2$ the free pro-$p$ $\mathcal{G}$-operator group on $x_0, x_1$, and take a surjection $F \to G$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and maps $P_2$ onto $V$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422]. Let $\psi : F \to F$ fix $\sigma, \tau, x_0$ and send $x_1$ to an element $x_1^\varepsilon$ of $P_2$ whose image in the Frattini quotient of $P_2$ is $\varepsilon \bar x_1$. They show that $\psi$ induces an automorphism $\psi_3$ of $G/V^3$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, and they state that the image $\bar x_1 \in V/V^2$ of $x_1$ generates a free $\Lambda$-module [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423]. Write $\bar x_0$ for the image of $x_0$.

*(b) An involution of $G/V^2$.* Let $\psi_2$ be the automorphism of $G/V^2 = (V/V^2) \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ induced by $\psi_3$. It is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, so its restriction to $V/V^2$ commutes with the action of $\mathcal{G}$ and is $\Lambda$-linear. It fixes $\bar x_0$ and sends $\bar x_1$ to $\varepsilon\bar x_1$. Since $P_2$ maps onto $V$, $V/V^2 = \Lambda\bar x_0 + \Lambda\bar x_1$. By linearity and centrality of $\varepsilon$, $\psi_2^2(\bar x_1) = \varepsilon\,\psi_2(\bar x_1) = \varepsilon^2\bar x_1 = \bar x_1$, so $\psi_2^2$ is the identity on $V/V^2$ and on $\mathcal{G}$, hence on $G/V^2$. Moreover:

- $\psi_2 \ne \mathrm{id}$. Indeed $\psi_2(\bar x_1) - \bar x_1 = -2E\bar x_1$, which is nonzero because $\bar x_1$ generates a free $\Lambda$-module and $E \ne 0$. To see $E \ne 0$, take a level $K$ whose ramification index $e$ is an even multiple of $p - 1$, for instance the splitting field of $x^{2(p-1)} - p$, where $e = 2(p-1)$. The image $\bar\tau$ of $\tau$ has order $e$. In the component of $E$ at $K$, the coefficient of $1$ collects $i = 0$ and $i = e/2$, and equals $\frac1e(1 + \alpha(\tau)^{-e/2}) = \frac2e$, because $\alpha(\tau)^{(p-1)/2} = -1$ and $e/(p-1)$ is even. This is nonzero, as $p$ is odd and prime to $e$.
- $\psi_2$ fixes $u = (1 - E)\bar x_1$, since $(1 - E)\varepsilon = 1 - 3E + 2E^2 = 1 - E$. And $u \ne 0$: under the augmentation $\Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$, which sends every element of $\mathcal{G}$ to $1$, the component of $E$ at each level maps to $\frac1e\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\alpha(\tau)^{-i} = 0$, because $\alpha(\tau) \ne 1$ and $\alpha(\tau)^e = 1$. So $1 - E \ne 0$, and $u \ne 0$ by freeness. This is used in part 3.

*(c) Lifting.* By Wingberg's lifting theorem in the corrected form of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, an automorphism of $G/V^3$ inducing the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ lifts to an automorphism of $G$ that agrees with it modulo $V^2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15), p. 421]. So there is $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ inducing $\psi_2$ on $G/V^2$. Let $M \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ be the subgroup of automorphisms inducing the identity on $G/V^2$. Apply Nishio's theorem with $(J, P, i, s) = (G, V, 2, 1)$; here $P^{p,2,1} = V^p[V,V] = V^2$, and $G$ is topologically finitely generated. It says that $M$ is a pro-$p$ group [@nishio2026finite, Theorem 1.2, p. 6]. It is closed in the profinite group $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ [@nishio2026finite, Lemma 1.1, p. 5], and normal, being the kernel of the action on $G/V^2$. As $\psi_2^2 = \mathrm{id}$, $\varphi^2 \in M$.

*(d) An element of order $2$.* Let $h : \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ be the continuous homomorphism with $h(1) = \varphi$. Since $M$ is closed and $\varphi^2 \in M$, $h(2\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}) \subseteq M$. Write $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \prod_\ell \mathbb{Z}_\ell$, so that $2\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = 2\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \prod_{\ell \ne 2}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. For $\ell \ne p$, the image under $h$ of the pro-$\ell$ group $2\mathbb{Z}_2$ (for $\ell = 2$) or $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ (for $\ell$ odd) is pro-$\ell$ and lies in the pro-$p$ group $M$, so it is trivial. Let $e_2 \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ have component $1$ at $2$ and $0$ elsewhere, and put $\gamma = h(e_2)$. Then $\gamma^2 = h(2e_2) = 1$, since $2e_2 \in 2\mathbb{Z}_2$. And $1 - e_2 \in 2\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, as its component at $2$ is $0$, so $h(1 - e_2) \in M$ and $\gamma = \varphi\, h(1 - e_2)^{-1} \in \varphi M$. Hence $\gamma \notin M$, so $\gamma \ne 1$, and $\gamma$ induces $\psi_2$ on $G/V^2$, in particular the identity on $G/V$.

*(e) Not inner.* Groups of MLF-type are torsion-free [@nishio2026finite, Lemma 2.1 (iv), p. 8] and slim [@nishio2026finite, proof of Theorem 3.3, p. 14], hence centre-free. If $\gamma = \mathrm{inn}_g$, then $\mathrm{inn}_{g^2} = \gamma^2 = 1$, so $g^2 = 1$, so $g = 1$ and $\gamma = 1$, a contradiction. So the class of $\gamma$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ has order exactly $2$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** As $K$ is tamely ramified, $K \subseteq T$ and $V \subseteq G_K$. For $g \in G_K$, $\gamma(g) \in gV \subseteq G_K$, because $\gamma$ is the identity on $G/V$. As $\gamma = \gamma^{-1}$, $\gamma(G_K) = G_K$, and $\gamma_K^2 = 1$.

- $\gamma_K \ne 1$. Suppose $\gamma_K = 1$, and let $g \in G$. The subgroup $H = G_K \cap g^{-1}G_Kg$ is open, and for $h \in H$ we have $ghg^{-1} \in G_K$, so $ghg^{-1} = \gamma(ghg^{-1}) = \gamma(g)\,h\,\gamma(g)^{-1}$. So $g^{-1}\gamma(g)$ centralizes $H$, and it is $1$ because $G$ is slim. Then $\gamma = 1$, a contradiction.
- $\gamma_K$ is not inner, by the argument of part 1 (e) applied to $G_K$, which is also of MLF-type.

So the class of $\gamma_K$ has order $2$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. The inverse image in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ of the subgroup it generates contains the involution $\gamma_K$, so it is not torsion-free, and hence not of MLF-type. This is Nishio's Theorem 3.3 applied to $\{1, \gamma\} \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ and the open subgroup $G_K$ [@nishio2026finite, Theorem 3.3 and its proof, p. 14]. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.**

*Lemma.* Let $W$ be a pro-$p$ group with $p$ odd, $c$ a continuous automorphism of $W$ with $c^2 = 1$, and $W' \subseteq W$ a closed normal subgroup with $c(W') = W'$. If $w_0 \in W$ satisfies $c(w_0) \in w_0W'$, then $c$ fixes some element of $w_0W'$.

*Proof.* First let $W$ be finite. The coset $w_0W'$ is stable under $c$, since $c(w_0W') = c(w_0)W' = w_0W'$. Its cardinality $|W'|$ is odd, and $c$ permutes it with orbits of size $1$ or $2$, so some orbit has size $1$. In general, the open normal subgroups $U$ of $W$ with $c(U) = U$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$, since $U \cap c(U)$ is one whenever $U$ is open and normal. For each such $U$, the finite case in $W/U$ gives some $x \in w_0W'$ with $x^{-1}c(x) \in U$. The sets $X_U = \{x \in w_0W' : x^{-1}c(x) \in U\}$ are therefore nonempty and closed in the compact set $w_0W'$, and $X_{U \cap U'} \subseteq X_U \cap X_{U'}$. So they have a common point $x$, and $x^{-1}c(x) \in \bigcap_U U = 1$. $\square$

*A fixed point in $V$.* Apply the lemma to $W = V$, $c = \gamma|_V$ and $W' = V^2$, with $w_0 \in V$ a lift of the element $u = (1 - E)\bar x_1$ of part 1 (b). Since $\gamma$ induces $\psi_2$ on $V/V^2$ and $\psi_2(u) = u$, $\gamma(w_0) \in w_0V^2$. So $\gamma$ fixes some $v \in w_0V^2$, and $v \ne 1$ because $u \ne 0$.

*The centralizer.* Let $K$ be as in part 2, so $v \in V \subseteq G_K$, and let $\mathrm{inn}_v \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ be conjugation by $v$. Then $\gamma_K \circ \mathrm{inn}_v \circ \gamma_K^{-1} = \mathrm{inn}_{\gamma(v)} = \mathrm{inn}_v$. Also $\mathrm{inn}_v \ne 1$, as $G_K$ is centre-free, and $\mathrm{inn}_v \ne \gamma_K$, as $\gamma_K$ is not inner. So $1, \gamma_K, \mathrm{inn}_v$ are three distinct elements of the centralizer of the involution $\gamma_K$.

*Involutions in absolute Galois groups.* Let $F$ be a field and $s \in G_F$ an element of order $2$, with fixed field $R$ in the separable closure $F^{\mathrm{sep}}$. By the [Artin–Schreier theorem](artin-schreier-theorem.md), applied to $R$, whose separable closure $F^{\mathrm{sep}}$ has degree $2$ over it, the nonzero squares of $R$ are the positive elements of an ordering of $R$. Let $t \in G_F$ commute with $s$. Then $t(R) = R$, and $t|_R$ is an automorphism of $R$ fixing $F$. It maps squares to squares, so it preserves the ordering. For $x \in R$, the roots in $R$ of the minimal polynomial of $x$ over $F$ form a finite totally ordered set, which $t$ permutes preserving the order. So $t$ fixes each of them, in particular $x$. Hence $t|_R = \mathrm{id}$ and $t \in \mathrm{Gal}(F^{\mathrm{sep}}/R) = \{1, s\}$. So in every absolute Galois group the centralizer of an element of order $2$ has exactly two elements. This property is preserved by isomorphisms of abstract groups, and $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ does not have it. $\square$

**Scope and remarks.**

- The involution $\gamma$ is not canonical. It differs from the automorphism $\varphi$ that Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg use to show that $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ has outer automorphisms by an element of the pro-$p$ group $M$, and nothing here says whether $\varphi$ itself has finite order.
- Nishio's finite subgroups for $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 3$ come from permuting and inverting pairs of generators in the commutator part of the Jannsen–Wingberg relation [@nishio2026finite, proof of Theorem 2.7, pp. 11–13]. For $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \le 2$ there are no such pairs, and the central unit $\varepsilon$ takes their place here. Like Nishio's subgroups, $\gamma$ acts trivially on the tame quotient.
- Larger finite subgroups occur too: for every tamely ramified $K$, every finite abelian group of exponent dividing $p - 1$ embeds in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$, through automorphisms that are again trivial on the tame quotient ([finite abelian subgroups](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). Whether other finite groups occur in $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ is not settled.
- The case $p = 2$ is not covered: the construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg assumes $p$ odd, and Problem 1 remains open for $p = 2$ as far as the sources opened here show.

**Taken from the sources without re-verification.** The statements in part 1 (a) that $\psi$ induces an automorphism of $G/V^3$, which Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg derive from Jannsen and Wingberg's description of the Demuškin relations of the groups $G_K(p)$ modulo their third $p$-central terms, and that $\bar x_1$ generates a free $\Lambda$-module, which they credit to Jannsen and Wingberg, §2 [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423]. Also the lifting theorem (7.5.15), which they attribute to Wingberg's proof [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, remark after (7.5.15), p. 421], and Nishio's Theorem 1.2.
