**Setting.** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}} \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$, let $X_n$ be its $n$-th configuration space, and write $\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}} = \Pi_X$ and $\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}}_n = \Pi_{X_n}$ for their étale fundamental groups [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Introduction, p. 2, and §3, p. 31]. Hoshi, Mochizuki and Tsujimura regard the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group as a closed subgroup $GT \subseteq \mathrm{Out}(\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}})$. Mochizuki defines it as the image in $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}})$ of a group $\mathrm{Out}^{\mathrm{FC}}(\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}}_2)^{\mathrm{S}}$ of outer automorphisms of $\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}}_2$, and identifies it with the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group using the theorem of Harbater and Schneps [@mochizuki2010combinatorial, Definition 1.11 (i) and Remark 1.11.1]. The absolute Galois group $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ embeds in $GT$ through its outer action on $\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}}$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 3.3 (vi)]. The Harbater–Schneps isomorphism matches this outer action with Ihara's homomorphism $\mathrm{Ih}$, as recorded in the [definition entry](grothendieck-teichmuller-group.md), so the image of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ in $GT$ corresponds to $\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}}) \subseteq \widehat{GT}$. This entry does not trace the identification further.

For a subfield $K \subseteq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ write $G_K = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/K) \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}} \subseteq GT$. For a closed subgroup $H$ of a profinite group $G$, the commensurator $C_G(H)$ is the set of $g \in G$ such that $H \cap gHg^{-1}$ has finite index in both $H$ and $gHg^{-1}$, and $N_G(H) \subseteq C_G(H)$ is the normalizer [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Notations and Conventions, pp. 13–14]. For a profinite group $\Pi$ with trivial center and a homomorphism $N \to \mathrm{Out}(\Pi)$, write $\Pi \rtimes^{\mathrm{out}} N$ for the pull-back of $1 \to \Pi \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Pi) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Pi) \to 1$ to $N$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Notations and Conventions, p. 14].

**Arithmetic Belyi diagrams.** Let $J \subseteq GT$ be closed and $N \subseteq J$ open and normal. An *arithmetic Belyi diagram* over $N$ is a pair of homomorphisms $\Pi_U \rtimes^{\mathrm{out}} N \to \Pi_X \rtimes^{\mathrm{out}} N$, one horizontal and one vertical, a group-theoretic version of an open immersion $U \hookrightarrow X$ of a finite étale covering $U \to X$ into $X$. The notion is Tsujimura's [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 3.3, pp. 33–34]. One diagram *dominates* another if, over a smaller open subgroup $M$, there is a surjection between the groups $\Pi_U \rtimes^{\mathrm{out}} M$ that extends to the second configuration spaces with kernel generated by cuspidal inertia, and is compatible with the horizontal arrows. Its restriction to the groups $\Pi_U$ is a *geometric domination* [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 3.3 (i)].

The subgroup $J$ has the *COF-property* if any two arithmetic Belyi diagrams over open normal subgroups of $J$ are dominated by a third one. It has the *RGC-property* if there is at most one geometric domination between any two of them, and the *BC-property* if it has both. A closed subgroup with the BC-property is written $BGT$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 3.3 (ii), (iii), (v)]. The authors read these conditions together as compatibility with Zariski localization on the projective line minus three points [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Introduction, p. 3]. A cuspidal separatedness property, the CS-property, implies the RGC-property [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Corollary 3.7].

**The field $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT}$ (Theorem A).** For $BGT$ with the BC-property, the arithmetic Belyi diagrams $\mathbb{B}$ over open normal subgroups form a directed preordered set $I_{BGT}$, and the unique geometric dominations give injections between the sets $\mathrm{Cusp}(\mathbb{B})$ of conjugacy classes of cuspidal inertia subgroups of the groups $\Pi_U$. Put
$$\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT} = \varinjlim_{\mathbb{B} \in I_{BGT}} \mathrm{Cusp}(\mathbb{B}) \setminus \{\infty\},$$
where $0, 1, \infty$ are the classes coming from the cusps of $X$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 4.1, pp. 54–55]. The commensurator $C_{GT}(BGT)$ acts continuously on this discrete set [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Proposition 4.3]. For $J = G_K$ with $K$ a number field, the arithmetic Belyi diagrams have a scheme-theoretic interpretation [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, proof of Corollary 6.5, p. 109]. The horizontal arrow comes from an open immersion $U \subseteq X$, so $\mathrm{Cusp}(\mathbb{B})$ is a finite subset of $\mathbb{P}^1(\bar{\mathbb{Q}})$, and the vertical arrow is a finite étale covering of $X$, that is, a Belyi map. Using Mochizuki's noncritical Belyi maps, any finite set of algebraic points can be removed in this way [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, proof of Theorem 4.4 (iii), pp. 62–64].

Composing the horizontal arrow with the automorphisms $t \mapsto t^{-1}$ and $t \mapsto 1-t$ of $X$, or recompactifying at the cusps other than $0, x, \infty$ and dividing by $x$, gives bijections $\{t^{-1}\}$, $\{1-t\}$ and $\{t/x\}$, for $x \ne 0, 1$. Let $-1_{BGT}$ be the cusp other than $0, 1, \infty$ of the double covering of $X$ ramified over $0$ and $\infty$. The operations are
$$x \boxtimes y = \{t/\{t^{-1}\}(x)\}(y), \qquad x \boxplus y = x \boxtimes \{1-t\}\bigl(-1_{BGT} \boxtimes (\{t^{-1}\}(x) \boxtimes y)\bigr),$$
together with $0 \boxtimes y = 0$, $1 \boxtimes y = y$ and $0 \boxplus y = y$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, proof of Theorem 4.4, pp. 58–59]. In the scheme-theoretic case $\{t/x\}(y) = y/x$, $\{t^{-1}\}(x) = 1/x$ and $\{1-t\}(z) = 1 - z$. So $x \boxtimes y = xy$ and $x \boxplus y = x(1 + y/x) = x + y$, as checked here.

*Theorem A* [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Theorem 4.4].

1. These operations, with the involutions $\{t^{-1}\}$ and $\{1-t\}$ of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT} \cup \{\infty\}$, are equivariant for $C_{GT}(BGT)$.
2. Say that $BGT$ has the *ArBC-property* if $\boxplus$ and $\boxtimes$ make $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT}$ a field isomorphic to $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. Then $C_{GT}(BGT)$ acts on this field, which gives a natural outer homomorphism $C_{GT}(BGT) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$.
3. The ArBC-property holds if $BGT$ has a *conducting field* $K$ with the *ZISC-property*. Here a field $K$ of characteristic $0$ is conducting if the image of $G_K \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ lies in a $GT$-conjugate of $BGT$. It has the ZISC-property if, for every hyperbolic curve $Y$ of genus $0$ over a finite extension $L$ of $K$, distinct points of $Y(L)$ have distinct conjugacy classes of decomposition groups in $\Pi_Y$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 3.3 (vi), pp. 37–38].

All of these constructions can be phrased in terms of the topological group $\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}}_2$ alone [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Corollary 4.5 and Remark 4.5.1]. For $F$ finite over $\mathbb{Q}$ or over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the subgroup $G_F \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}} \subseteq GT$ has the BC- and ArBC-properties, and $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{G_F} \cong \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ compatibly with $G_F \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 4.4.1]. Whether $GT$ itself has the BC-property is not known to the authors [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 4.4.2].

**Algebraic ampleness and maximality (Theorems B and C).** A subgroup $BGT$ with the ArBC-property has the *QAA-property* if it satisfies conditions (i)–(iii) below, and the *AA-property* (algebraically ample) if it satisfies (i)–(iv) [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 5.12, pp. 87–90]. Let $\Pi$ be the inverse limit of the groups $\Pi_U$ along geometric dominations, and $\Pi_{X_{0\infty}} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ the quotient of $\Pi_X$ by the cuspidal inertia at $1$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, §5, p. 69, and Definition 5.1]. In outline:

- (i) $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT}$ is an infinite extension of the subfield generated by the kernel of a Kummer map from $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT}^\times$ to a limit of groups $H^1(N, \Pi_{X_{0\infty}})$.
- (ii) For open normal subgroups of $\Pi$, certain groups of invariants in $H^1$ vanish, and the group-theoretic Kummer classes are determined by their values at cusps.
- (iii) For each open normal subgroup $\Pi^\dagger \subseteq \Pi$ there is a set $K_{\Pi^\dagger}$ of functions on the cusps of $\Pi^\dagger$ with values in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT} \cup \{\infty\}$, compatible with pull-back to smaller $\Pi^\ddagger$. An element is determined by its values away from any finite set of cusps. For any $f, g \in K_{\Pi^\dagger}$ there is a finite set $T$ of cusps outside which both take values in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT}$, and $K_{\Pi^\dagger}$ carries a unique field structure whose sum and product agree with the pointwise ones outside such a $T$. Each $K_{\Pi^\dagger}$ is a finite Galois extension of $K_\Pi = \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT}(t)$ with group $\Pi/\Pi^\dagger$, and its nonzero elements give exactly the Kummer classes [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 5.12 (iii) (a)–(f), and Remark 5.9.2]. The value $\infty$ is needed: in the scheme-theoretic case $t$ is the standard coordinate, with a pole at the cusp $\infty$, and $1/t \in K_\Pi$ has a pole at $0$.
- (iv) The resulting identification of $\Pi_X$ with the fundamental group of $\mathbb{P}^1_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT}} \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ comes from an outer isomorphism of second configuration space groups.

*Theorem B.* If $BGT$ has the QAA-property, the homomorphism $C_{GT}(BGT) \to \mathrm{Aut}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT})$ is injective [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Theorem 5.15 (iii)]. With the AA-property, it is also compatible with the inclusions of $C_{GT}(BGT)$ into $GT$ and of $\mathrm{Aut}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT})$ into the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group of $\mathbb{P}^1_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{BGT}} \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, which condition (iv) identifies with $GT$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Theorem 5.15 (iv)]. Under the QAA-property the family of fields in (iii) is unique, and the proof of this uses an elementary fact: if the graph of a field automorphism $\alpha$ of an algebraically closed field is not Zariski dense in the plane, then $\alpha$ is a power of Frobenius, or the identity in characteristic $0$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Theorem 1.2, Corollary 1.3, Theorem 5.14 and its proof]. Without the QAA-property, the authors do not know whether $C_{GT}(BGT) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is injective [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Introduction, p. 4].

*Theorem C* [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Theorem 5.17].

- (ii) The $GT$-conjugates of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ are exactly the maximal elements, for inclusion, of the set of closed subgroups of $GT$ with the AA-property.
- (i) Let $\mathrm{Out}^{|C|}(\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}})$ be the group of outer automorphisms fixing each conjugacy class of cuspidal inertia subgroups. The $\mathrm{Out}^{|C|}(\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}})$-conjugates of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ are exactly the maximal elements among the $\mathrm{Out}^{|C|}(\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}})$-conjugates of closed subgroups of $GT$ with the QAA-property.

Since the properties are defined in terms of $\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}}_2$, this constructs the conjugacy class of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ from the abstract topological group $\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}}_2$. *Proof.* $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has the AA-property [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 5.12.1]. By Theorem 5.15 (iv), every $GT$-conjugate of a closed subgroup with the AA-property lies in a $GT$-conjugate of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, and Theorem 5.15 (ii) gives the analogue for (i). In a profinite group, $H \subseteq gHg^{-1}$ forces $H = gHg^{-1}$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Lemma 5.16 and proof of Theorem 5.17]. That lemma is checked here: in every finite quotient $G/U$ the images of $H$ and $gHg^{-1}$ have the same order, so $HU = gHg^{-1}U$ for all open normal $U$, and a closed subgroup is the intersection of these products. The authors stress that this approach to $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is in no sense unique [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Introduction, p. 6].

**Fields whose Galois groups are algebraically ample (Theorem F).** A field $K \subseteq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ is *AVKF* if, for every finite extension $L/K$ and abelian variety $A/L$, the group $A(L)$ has no nonzero divisible elements [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 6.1 (iii)]. It is *TKND* if $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ is an infinite extension of $K_{\mathrm{div}} = \bigcup_{L} \mathbb{Q}(L^{\times\infty})$, where $L$ runs over the finite extensions of $K$ and $L^{\times\infty} = \bigcap_{m \ge 1} (L^\times)^m$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 6.6]. Kummer-faithful fields are TKND-AVKF [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 6.6.2], so number fields and all sub-$p$-adic subfields of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ are [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Introduction, p. 11]. The field $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and its finite extensions are TKND-AVKF [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Proposition 6.3 (i) and Remark 6.6.3]. They contain all roots of unity [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 6.6.4], which are divisible elements of their multiplicative groups, so they are not Kummer-faithful. More examples are in the entry on [their Grothendieck conjecture over these fields](grothendieck-conjecture-for-configuration-spaces-over-tknd-avkf-fields.md).

*Theorem F* [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Theorem 6.8]. If $K \subseteq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ is TKND-AVKF, then $G_K \subseteq GT$ has the AA-property, and the natural homomorphism $C_{GT}(G_K) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is injective and compatible with the inclusions of both groups into $GT$. Hence $C_{GT}(G_K) \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Abstract].

For $K = \mathbb{Q}$ this gives $C_{GT}(G_{\mathbb{Q}}) = G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. The authors also derive $C_{GT}(G_{\mathbb{Q}}) = G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, $C_{GT}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}) = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, for a decomposition group $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, and the triviality of the local centralizer of $GT$, from their Corollary 6.9 and the commensurable terminality of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ in $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. They call this a new proof of these equalities, and it does not use the Grothendieck conjecture for hyperbolic curves over number fields [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 6.9.1].

**Reformulations of the Grothendieck–Teichmüller conjecture.** These are checked here. Identify $GT$ with $\widehat{GT}$ and $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with $\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})$, as in the setting, and let $\widehat{GT}^1$ be the kernel of $\lambda : \widehat{GT} \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$. The following are equivalent.

- (a) $\mathrm{Ih}$ is surjective.
- (b) $\widehat{GT}$ has the AA-property.
- (c) $\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})$ has finite index in $\widehat{GT}$.
- (d) $\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}})$ is normal in $\widehat{GT}$.
- (e) $\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}})$ has finite index in $\widehat{GT}^1$.

*Proof.* (a) implies (b) because $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has the AA-property, and (a) implies (c), (d) and (e) because $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ is normal in $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and maps onto $\widehat{GT}^1$ when $\mathrm{Ih}$ is surjective, by test 1 of the [conjecture entry](grothendieck-teichmuller-conjecture.md). If (b) holds, $\widehat{GT}$ is a maximal element of the set in Theorem C (ii), so $\widehat{GT} = g\,G_{\mathbb{Q}}\,g^{-1}$ for some $g$, and then $G_{\mathbb{Q}} = g^{-1}\widehat{GT}g = \widehat{GT}$. For the rest, note that if $H$ has finite index in a group $G'$, then so does $gHg^{-1}$ for each $g \in G'$, and hence $G' \subseteq C_{G'}(H)$. Under (c) this gives $\widehat{GT} = C_{\widehat{GT}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}}) = G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ by Theorem F for $K = \mathbb{Q}$. The field $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is TKND-AVKF, so Theorem F gives $C_{\widehat{GT}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}) \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Under (d), $\widehat{GT} = N_{\widehat{GT}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}) \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Under (e), $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}} \subseteq \widehat{GT}^1$ because $\lambda \circ \mathrm{Ih}$ is the cyclotomic character $\chi$, so $\widehat{GT}^1 \subseteq C_{\widehat{GT}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}) \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. An element of $\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})$ with $\lambda = 1$ is $\mathrm{Ih}(\sigma)$ with $\chi(\sigma) = 1$, so $\widehat{GT}^1 = \mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}})$, and test 1 gives (a).

The same argument shows, without assuming the conjecture, that $N_{\widehat{GT}}(\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}})) = C_{\widehat{GT}}(\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}})) = \mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})$. Also, (a) implies that $\widehat{GT}$ has the BC-property, by Remark 4.4.1 with $F = \mathbb{Q}$, and the authors do not know whether it does. None of these conditions is known to hold or to fail.
