**Printed statement.** Let $K$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb Q$. Voevodsky's Theorem 3.1 says that if $X,Y$ are finite-type $K$-schemes and $X$ is "reduced and nonsingular in codimension 1", then $\operatorname{Mor}_K(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Mor}^0_K(X_{\mathrm{\acute et}},Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}})$ is bijective [@voevodsky1991etale, Theorem 3.1, p. 519]. The Russian original has the same hypothesis, so this is not a translation artefact [@voevodsky1990etale, Theorem 3.1, p. 1163]. Corollary 3.1 is stated only for normal $X,Y$. Remark 3.1 gives the cusp as a counterexample when no condition is put on the singularities, and says the proof works whenever $\operatorname{Pic}(\bar X)$ has no subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb G_a$ [@voevodsky1991etale, Corollary 3.1 and Remark 3.1, p. 519]. The notation is that of [the normal case](voevodsky-etale-topos-reconstruction.md).

**Status.** The gap is known. Berens gives the example below, notes that it is R1 but neither S2 nor seminormal, and says that Voevodsky's lemma on Picard groups needs S2 [@berens2025, Remark 1.3, pp. 3–4]. He restates the theorem for normal $X$ [@berens2025, Theorem 1.1 and footnote 2, p. 2]. Carlson–Haine–Wolf also state it for normal $X$, and note that the argument needs $\operatorname{Pic}$ to have no infinitely divisible torsion. They know this for seminormal S2 schemes, and say they know neither a proof nor a counterexample without S2 [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Introduction and footnote 1, pp. 2–3]. Berens asserts, without proof, that the inverse equivalence below comes from no morphism, and that some étale open of $X$ has such torsion in its Picard group [@berens2025, Remark 1.3, p. 4]. Both are proved here, the second on an explicit Zariski open.

**The example.** Let $B=K[x,y]$, $\mathfrak m=(x,y)$, $A=K+\mathfrak m^2\subset B$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $Y=\mathbb A^2_K=\operatorname{Spec}B$. Let $\nu\colon Y\to X$ come from the inclusion, and let $o\in X$ be the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m\cap A=\mathfrak m^2$.

1. *$X$ is reduced and R1 but not normal.* $A$ is generated by the monomials of degrees 2 and 3, and $B=A+Ax+Ay$ is finite over $A$. For $h\in\{x^2,y^2\}$ we have $A_h=B_h$, since $x=x^3/x^2$ and $y=x^2y/x^2$, and symmetrically. A prime of $A$ containing $x^2$ and $y^2$ contains $(x^ay^b)^2=(x^2)^a(y^2)^b$ for $a+b\ge2$, so it contains $\mathfrak m^2$ and equals $o$. Hence $\nu$ is an isomorphism over $X\smallsetminus\{o\}$, and $X$ is regular away from the codimension-2 point $o$. The domain $A$ is not normal, because $x=x^3/x^2$ is a root of $T^2-x^2$ and $x\notin A$.
2. *$\nu$ is a universal homeomorphism.* It is finite, and its fibre over $o$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(B/\mathfrak m^2)$, a single point with residue field $K=\kappa(o)$. So $\nu$ is integral, surjective and radicial [@stacks2026, Definition 29.10.1, Tag 01S3]. Hence it is universally injective [@stacks2026, Lemma 29.10.2, Tag 01S4], and so a universal homeomorphism [@stacks2026, Lemma 29.46.5, Tag 04DF].
3. *An admissible morphism $\psi\colon X_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\to Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$.*
   - Base change $V\mapsto V_Y=V\times_XY$ is an equivalence from étale $X$-schemes to étale $Y$-schemes [@stacks2026, Theorem 59.45.2, Tag 04DZ]. Each $V_Y\to V$ is a homeomorphism, being a base change of $\nu$.
   - It restricts to Voevodsky's sites, whose objects are separated étale schemes of finite type. Quasi-compactness of $V$ and $V_Y$ is equivalent. The diagonal of an étale morphism is an open immersion [@stacks2026, Lemma 29.36.13, Tag 02GE], so separatedness means its image is closed, and that is unchanged by the homeomorphic base change.
   - Base change preserves fibre products, and it preserves and reflects coverings, because the images of $V_i\to V$ and $(V_i)_Y\to V_Y$ correspond. So a quasi-inverse $\psi^{-1}$ defines a morphism of sites [@voevodsky1991etale, Definition 1.2, p. 511]. It lies over $(\operatorname{Spec}K)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ through $\psi^{-1}(E\times_KY)\cong\psi^{-1}\bigl((E\times_KX)_Y\bigr)\cong E\times_KX$ for étale $K$-schemes $E$ [@voevodsky1991etale, Definition 1.3, p. 513].
   - If $U\subseteq Y$ is open and $V=\psi^{-1}(U)$, then $V_Y\cong U$ and $V_Y\to V$ is surjective, so $V$ has image $\nu(U)$ in $X$. By the uniqueness in [@voevodsky1991etale, Proposition 1.1, p. 512], $T(\psi)=|\nu|^{-1}$. This is a homeomorphism, so $\psi$ is admissible [@voevodsky1991etale, definition of admissibility, p. 513].
4. *No morphism realizes $\psi$.* Suppose a $K$-morphism $f\colon X\to Y$ has $f_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\cong\psi$.
   - For open $U\subseteq Y$, the étale $X$-schemes $U\times_YX$ and $\psi^{-1}(U)$ are isomorphic, so they have the same image: $|f|^{-1}(U)=\nu(U)$. A point of a scheme is determined by the open sets containing it, so $|f|=|\nu|^{-1}$.
   - So $e=f\circ\nu$ is a $K$-endomorphism of $Y$ fixing every point. At a $K$-point $P$, the residue map $\kappa(P)=K\to K$ is $K$-linear, hence the identity, so $(e^*h)(P)=h(P)$ for all $h\in B$. Thus $e^*x-x$ and $e^*y-y$ vanish on $K^2$. Since $K$ is infinite, they are zero, and $e=\mathrm{id}_Y$.
   - But $e^*=\nu^*\circ f^*$, and $\nu^*$ is the inclusion $A\subset B$. So $b=f^*(b)\in A$ for every $b\in B$, which contradicts $x\notin A$.

So $\operatorname{Mor}_K(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Mor}^0_K(X_{\mathrm{\acute et}},Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}})$ is not surjective. Moreover, $X$ and $Y$ are both reduced and R1, and $\psi$ is an equivalence of étale sites over $(\operatorname{Spec}K)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$, yet $X\not\cong Y$ because only $Y$ is normal. So Corollary 3.1 does not extend to R1 schemes either.

**Where the proof breaks.** Voevodsky reduces to affine, geometrically connected, reduced $X$ of finite type with $X(K)\ne\varnothing$ [@voevodsky1991etale, Proposition 3.1 and the convention after (3.1), p. 520]. His Proposition 3.5 then claims that if such an $X$ is R1, then $\widehat T(\operatorname{Pic}\bar X)^{\Gamma_K}=0$, where $\widehat T(\operatorname{Pic}\bar X)=\varprojlim_n\operatorname{Pic}_n(\bar X)$ is built from the $n$-torsion subgroups. The proof asserts that $\operatorname{Pic}(\bar X)^{\Gamma_K}$ is finitely generated [@voevodsky1991etale, Proposition 3.5, p. 523].

*Counterexample.* Let $g=1-3x^2+2x^3=(x-1)^2(1+2x)\in A$ and $V=D(g)=\operatorname{Spec}A_g$. This $V$ is affine, reduced, R1 and geometrically connected, and it contains the $K$-point $o$, since $g(0,0)=1$. Over $\bar K$, put $R=\bar A_g$, $S=\bar K[x,y]_g$ and $I=\bar{\mathfrak m}^2S$.

- *A Milnor square.* $I\subseteq R$ because $\bar{\mathfrak m}^2\subseteq\bar A$. Since $g\equiv1\pmod{\bar{\mathfrak m}^2}$, each $a/g^k$ with $a\in\bar A=\bar K+\bar{\mathfrak m}^2$ is congruent to the constant $a(0,0)$ modulo $I$, so $R=\bar K+I$. Hence $R/I=\bar K$ and $S/I=\bar K\oplus\bar Kx\oplus\bar Ky$, and $R\to S$ with $I$ is a Milnor square [@weibel2013kbook, Chapter I, 2.6, p. 11]. The units–Pic sequence [@weibel2013kbook, Chapter I, Theorem 3.10, p. 22] embeds the cokernel of $S^\times\times(R/I)^\times\to(S/I)^\times$ in $\operatorname{Pic}(R)=\operatorname{Pic}(\bar V)$.
- *The units.* We have $(S/I)^\times=\bar K^\times\times(1+\bar Kx+\bar Ky)$, and $1+ax+by\mapsto(a,b)$ identifies the second factor with $\bar K^2$, because products of two elements of $\bar{\mathfrak m}$ vanish in $S/I$. Since $\bar K[x,y]$ is a UFD, a unit $r/g^k$ of $S$ has $r$ dividing a power of $g$. So $S^\times$ is generated by $\bar K^\times$, $x-1=-(1-x)$ and $1+2x$, and the image of $S^\times\times(R/I)^\times$ is $\bar K^\times\times\mathbb Z(1,0)$.
- *The conclusion.* So $\operatorname{Pic}(\bar V)$ contains $\bar K^2/\mathbb Z(1,0)\cong\bar K/\mathbb Z\oplus\bar K$, compatibly with the action of $\Gamma_K$ on coefficients. The subgroup $\mathbb Q(1,0)/\mathbb Z(1,0)\cong\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ is fixed pointwise. Inverse limits are left exact, so $\widehat T(\operatorname{Pic}\bar V)$ contains $\varprojlim_n\frac1n\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ with trivial action. Hence $\widehat T(\operatorname{Pic}\bar V)^{\Gamma_K}\ne0$, and $\operatorname{Pic}(\bar V)^{\Gamma_K}$ contains $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$, so it is not finitely generated. The same computation over $K$ shows that $\operatorname{Pic}(V)$ itself contains $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$.

The open set is needed. On $X$ itself the units of $B$ are constants, and Berens computes that $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ is isomorphic to the additive group $K^2$, which is torsion-free (his $k$ is our $K$) [@berens2025, Remark 1.3, p. 4]. On $V$, the linear terms of the new units $x-1$ and $1+2x$ produce the torsion.

**Context.** $X$ is not seminormal: $x^2,x^3\in A$ but $x\notin A$. So the example is consistent with the [seminormal reconstruction theorems](etale-topos-reconstruction-over-sub-p-adic-fields.md). For seminormal schemes, with or without S2, the torsion found here cannot occur: their [Picard groups are free plus finite](picard-groups-of-seminormal-schemes.md), which answers the question that Carlson–Haine–Wolf leave open.
