**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite, inside a fixed algebraic closure, with ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$, and put $q = 2^f$. Let $U_f$ be the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of degree $f$, so that $U_f = K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$, and let $\mu_{q-1} \subset U_f$ be the group of $(q-1)$-th roots of unity. Since the residue characteristic is $2$, $K$ is tamely ramified exactly when $e$ is odd. As in [the maximal abelian subfield of a 2-adic field](maximal-abelian-subfields-of-2-adic-fields.md), put $K^0 = K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and $K' = K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$. Finally, put $d = \gcd(e, q - 1)$.

**Statement.** Let $K$ be tamely ramified.

1. $K^0 = U_f$, $K' = \mathbb{Q}_2$, and $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified. Every $K$ of odd degree is tamely ramified.
2. $K = U_f(\varpi)$ for a root $\varpi$ of $x^e - 2\zeta$, with $\zeta \in \mu_{q-1}$. Conversely, for every $\zeta \in \mu_{q-1}$ and every root $\varpi$ of $x^e - 2\zeta$, the field $U_f(\varpi)$ has ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$.
3. Let $L = U_f(\varpi')$ with $\varpi'^e = 2\zeta'$ and $\zeta' \in \mu_{q-1}$. Then $K \cong L$ as fields exactly when $\zeta' \in \zeta^{2^k}\mu_{q-1}^e$ for some $k \ge 0$. So the isomorphism classes of fields with ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$ correspond to the orbits of $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^2$ on $\mu_{q-1}/\mu_{q-1}^e$, a cyclic group of order $d$. There is exactly one class if $d = 1$, and there are at least two if $d > 1$.
4. The finite $L/\mathbb{Q}_2$ with $[L:\mathbb{Q}_2] = [K:\mathbb{Q}_2]$ and $L^0 = K^0$ are exactly the tamely ramified fields with ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$. If $d = 1$, every such $L$ is isomorphic to $K$, and $G_L \cong G_K$.

By part 4, part 2 of the [classification of $p$-adic fields with isomorphic absolute Galois groups](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) holds for every tamely ramified $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ with $\gcd(e, 2^f - 1) = 1$. This includes every $K$ of odd degree less than $21$, as shown below. These fields all have $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ ramified, which is the case that entry leaves open.

**Proof.**

*Part 1.* If $[K:\mathbb{Q}_2]$ is odd, then so is $e$, which divides it. Now let $e$ be odd. Let $T$ be the maximal tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$. Its Galois group is topologically generated by a generator $\tau$ of the inertia group $\mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}})$ and a lift $\sigma$ of Frobenius, with the single relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^2$ [@luo2024, Proposition 2.15]. The field $K^0$ is finite and abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, with ramification index dividing $e$, so it is tamely ramified and $K^0 \subseteq T$. The images $\bar\sigma, \bar\tau$ of $\sigma, \tau$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(K^0/\mathbb{Q}_2)$ commute, so $\bar\tau = \bar\tau^2$, and $\bar\tau = 1$. Since $\bar\tau$ generates the inertia group of $K^0/\mathbb{Q}_2$, the field $K^0$ is unramified over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, so $K^0 \subseteq K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}} = U_f$. Conversely $U_f \subseteq K^0$, because $U_f/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is cyclic.

Next, $K' \subseteq K^0 = U_f$, and $K'$ lies in $\mathbb{Q}_2(\zeta_{2^m})$ for some $m \ge 2$. That field is totally ramified over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, because $\zeta_{2^m} - 1$ is a root of the polynomial $(x + 1)^{2^{m-1}} + 1$, which is Eisenstein since the binomial coefficients $\binom{2^{m-1}}{i}$ with $0 < i < 2^{m-1}$ are even. So $K'/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is both unramified and totally ramified, and $K' = \mathbb{Q}_2$.

Finally, $(x + 1)^2 + 1$ is Eisenstein, so $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})$ has ramification index $2$ over $\mathbb{Q}_2$. Hence the ramification index of $K(\sqrt{-1})$ over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ is even, while that of $K$ is the odd number $e$, so $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified.

*Part 2.* The residue field of both $K$ and $U_f$ is $\mathbb{F}_q$. By Hensel's lemma, $x^{q-1} - 1$ splits in $U_f$, so $\mu_{q-1}$ maps isomorphically onto $\mathbb{F}_q^\times$, and $\mathcal{O}_K^\times = \mu_{q-1} \times (1 + \mathfrak{m}_K)$. The group $1 + \mathfrak{m}_K$ is pro-$2$, since its quotients $(1 + \mathfrak{m}_K)/(1 + \mathfrak{m}_K^i)$ have order $q^{i-1}$. As $e$ is odd, $x \mapsto x^e$ is therefore bijective on $1 + \mathfrak{m}_K$. Take a uniformizer $\varpi_0$ of $K$. Since $v_K(2) = e$, we can write $\varpi_0^e = 2\zeta w$ with $\zeta \in \mu_{q-1}$ and $w \in 1 + \mathfrak{m}_K$. Write $w = w_1^e$ with $w_1 \in 1 + \mathfrak{m}_K$, and put $\varpi = \varpi_0/w_1$, so that $\varpi^e = 2\zeta$. The polynomial $x^e - 2\zeta$ is Eisenstein over $U_f$, so $[U_f(\varpi) : U_f] = e = [K : U_f]$, and $K = U_f(\varpi)$. Conversely, for any $\zeta \in \mu_{q-1}$, a root $\varpi$ of the Eisenstein polynomial $x^e - 2\zeta$ generates a totally ramified extension of $U_f$ of degree $e$, and this has ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$ over $\mathbb{Q}_2$.

*Part 3.* The Frobenius automorphism of $U_f$ acts on $\mu_{q-1}$ by $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^2$, since it induces $x \mapsto x^2$ on $\mathbb{F}_q$ and reduction is injective on $\mu_{q-1}$.

Suppose first that $\zeta' = \zeta^{2^k}\eta^e$ with $\eta \in \mu_{q-1}$. Choose an automorphism $\phi$ of the algebraic closure that restricts to the $k$-th power of Frobenius on $U_f$. Then $\phi(K) = U_f(\phi(\varpi))$ and $(\eta\phi(\varpi))^e = 2\zeta'$, so $\phi(K) = U_f(\eta\phi(\varpi))$. Thus $\phi(K)$ and $L$ are both generated over $U_f$ by a root of the irreducible polynomial $x^e - 2\zeta'$, so they are isomorphic over $U_f$, and $K \cong \phi(K) \cong L$.

Conversely, let $\iota : K \to L$ be a field isomorphism. It is a $\mathbb{Q}_2$-algebra isomorphism [@hyeon2025, §1]. So $\iota(U_f)$ is a conjugate of $U_f$, which is $U_f$ itself because $U_f/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is Galois, and $\iota$ restricts to a power of Frobenius on $U_f$. Hence $\iota(\zeta) = \zeta^{2^k}$ for some $k \ge 0$, and $\iota(\varpi)$ is a uniformizer of $L$ with $\iota(\varpi)^e = 2\zeta^{2^k}$. Write $\iota(\varpi)/\varpi' = \eta u$ with $\eta \in \mu_{q-1}$ and $u \in 1 + \mathfrak{m}_L$. Taking $e$-th powers, $u^e = \zeta^{2^k}\zeta'^{-1}\eta^{-e}$, which lies in $\mu_{q-1} \cap (1 + \mathfrak{m}_L) = \{1\}$. So $\zeta' = \zeta^{2^k}\eta^{-e} \in \zeta^{2^k}\mu_{q-1}^e$.

By part 2, every field with ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$ is isomorphic to one of the fields $U_f(\varpi)$. With the two directions above, this gives the correspondence with orbits. The quotient of the cyclic group $\mu_{q-1}$ of order $q - 1$ by its subgroup of $e$-th powers has order $\gcd(e, q - 1) = d$. If $d > 1$, the trivial class is an orbit by itself, and there is at least one other orbit. (Counting elements of each order $k$ dividing $d$, the number of orbits is $\sum_{k \mid d} \varphi(k)/\mathrm{ord}_k(2)$, where $\mathrm{ord}_k(2)$ is the order of $2$ modulo $k$.)

*Part 4.* Let $[L:\mathbb{Q}_2] = [K:\mathbb{Q}_2]$ and $L^0 = K^0 = U_f$. Then $L \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$ contains $U_f \subseteq L$, and it lies in $L^0 = U_f$ because it is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_2$. So $L$ has residue degree $f$, hence ramification index $e$, and it is tamely ramified. Conversely, every tamely ramified $L$ with ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$ has degree $ef$ and $L^0 = U_f$, by part 1. If $d = 1$, part 3 gives an isomorphism $K \to L$. It is a $\mathbb{Q}_2$-algebra isomorphism [@hyeon2025, §1], so it extends to an automorphism $\phi$ of the algebraic closure, and $G_L = \phi G_K \phi^{-1}$. $\square$

**Odd degrees.** Let $K$ have odd degree $n$, so that $e$ and $f$ are odd, and suppose that a prime $r$ divides $d = \gcd(e, 2^f - 1)$. Let $t$ be the order of $2$ modulo $r$. Then $t > 1$ and $t$ divides the odd number $f$, so $t \ge 3$. Also $t$ divides the even number $r - 1$, so $2t$ divides $r - 1$, and $r \ge 2t + 1 \ge 7$. Since $r$ divides $e$ and $t$ divides $f$, we get $n = ef \ge rt \ge 21$. So part 4 proves part 2 of the classification for every $K$ of odd degree less than $21$. Degree $21$ is the first exception, with $e = 7$, $f = 3$ and $d = 7$.

**Examples where $d > 1$.**

- *Degree $6$.* Take $e = 3$ and $f = 2$, so $q = 4$ and $d = 3$. Here $U_2 = \mathbb{Q}_2(\omega)$ for a primitive cube root of unity $\omega$, since $x^2 + x + 1$ is irreducible modulo $2$. As $\mu_3^3 = 1$ and squaring swaps $\omega$ and $\omega^2$, part 3 gives exactly two isomorphism classes, represented by $K = U_2(\sqrt[3]{2})$, the splitting field of $x^3 - 2$, and by $L = U_2(\beta)$ with $\beta^3 = 2\omega$. Both have degree $6$ and $K^0 = L^0 = U_2$. Part 2 of the classification predicts $G_K \cong G_L$.
- *Degree $21$.* Take $e = 7$ and $f = 3$, so $q = 8$ and $\mu_{q-1} = \mu_7$, with $\mu_7^7 = 1$. Squaring has the orbits $\{1\}$, $\{\zeta_7, \zeta_7^2, \zeta_7^4\}$ and $\{\zeta_7^3, \zeta_7^6, \zeta_7^5\}$ on $\mu_7$. So there are three isomorphism classes, represented by $U_3(\sqrt[7]{2})$, $U_3(\sqrt[7]{2\zeta_7})$ and $U_3(\sqrt[7]{2\zeta_7^3})$. All have degree $21$ and maximal abelian subfield $U_3$. Part 2 of the classification predicts that their absolute Galois groups are isomorphic.

**What remains.** For tamely ramified $K$ with $d > 1$, the sources opened here do not settle whether $G_K \cong G_L$ for the non-isomorphic $L$ of part 4. Diekert's presentation does not apply, since $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified. [Schneider's conjecture](schneider-conjecture-on-2-adic-galois-groups.md) does not cover these fields either: it needs even degree and $K'$ not real, while here $K' = \mathbb{Q}_2$, which is real. Since $K \subseteq T$, every such $G_K$ is an open subgroup of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ containing $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2/T)$, so the question concerns open subgroups of the single group $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$. A presentation of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ by Roe and Turturean is recorded in the classification entry.
