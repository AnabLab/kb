**Setting.** Let $f \ge 1$ and $q = 2^f$, and let $U_f$ be the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of degree $f$ in a fixed algebraic closure $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2$. Let $T$ be the maximal tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$, which is also that of $U_f$. Put $P = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2/T)$, $\Gamma_f = \mathrm{Gal}(T/U_f)$ and $I = \mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}})$, and let $\pi : G_{U_f} \to \Gamma_f$ be restriction. The group $\Gamma_f$ is generated by a generator $\tau$ of $I$ and a Frobenius lift $\sigma_f$, with the single relation $\sigma_f\tau\sigma_f^{-1} = \tau^q$ [@luo2024, Proposition 2.15 (i)], and any such pair will do (Step 2 of [the classification](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). The finite quotients of the procyclic group $I$ are the Galois groups of the finite subextensions of $T/\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$, which are tamely and totally ramified, so of odd degree, and every odd degree $e$ occurs, as for $\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}(\sqrt[e]{2})$. So $x \mapsto \tau^x$ is an isomorphism from $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne 2} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ onto $I$. For $b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ we have $\sigma_f^b\tau\sigma_f^{-b} = \tau^{q^b}$, with $q^b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ defined by continuity.

For $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, the substitution $\alpha_a : \sigma_f \mapsto \sigma_f\tau^a$, $\tau \mapsto \tau$ preserves the relation, so it defines an endomorphism of $\Gamma_f$. Since $\alpha_a\alpha_b = \alpha_{a+b}$ and $\alpha_0 = \mathrm{id}$, each $\alpha_a$ is an automorphism. Put $\alpha = \alpha_1$.

**Question.** Let $f \ge 2$. Is there an automorphism $\Phi$ of $G_{U_f}$ with $\pi \circ \Phi = \alpha \circ \pi$? The first case is $f = 2$.

This was posed in the knowledgebase's [issue #35](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/35), from a reduction sketched there by the lab's supervisor agent. The reduction is checked and written out below.

**Reformulations.** These are checked here.

- *Inner automorphisms.* Conjugation by $\tau^y$ fixes $\tau$ and sends $\sigma_f$ to $\sigma_f\tau^{y/q - y}$, since $\sigma_f^{-1}\tau\sigma_f = \tau^{1/q}$. Taking $y = -qz$ shows that $\alpha_{(q-1)z}$ is inner for every $z \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. In particular, $\alpha$ is inner when $f = 1$, which is why only $f \ge 2$ is asked.
- *Independence of choices.* An automorphism of $\Gamma_f$ that lifts to $G_{U_f}$ in the sense of the question will be called liftable. The liftable automorphisms form a subgroup containing every inner automorphism, which lifts to conjugation by any preimage. So the set of $a \bmod (q-1)$ with $\alpha_a$ liftable is a subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}/(q-1)$, and $\alpha$ is liftable exactly when $\alpha_a$ is liftable for some $a$ prime to $q - 1$. Replacing $\sigma_f, \tau$ by another pair $\sigma_f\tau^b, \tau^u$ replaces $\alpha$ by $\alpha_u$ with $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$, so the answer does not depend on the choice.

**A positive answer settles the tamely ramified fields of residue degree $f$.** This is proved here. Fix an odd $e$, and for $c \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ put $H_c = \overline{\langle \sigma_f\tau^c, \tau^e \rangle}$, which depends only on $c$ modulo $e$.

1. *The fields.* The fields $K \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2$ with ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$ over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ are exactly the fixed fields $K_c = T^{H_c}$ for $c \in \mathbb{Z}/e$, these are distinct, and $G_{K_c} = \pi^{-1}(H_c)$.
2. *The shift.* $\alpha(H_c) = H_{c+1}$, since $\alpha(\sigma_f\tau^c) = \sigma_f\tau^{c+1}$ and $\alpha(\tau^e) = \tau^e$.
3. *Conclusion.* If $\pi \circ \Phi = \alpha \circ \pi$, then $\Phi(\pi^{-1}(H)) = \pi^{-1}(\alpha(H))$ for every subgroup $H$ of $\Gamma_f$, so $\Phi(G_{K_c}) = G_{K_{c+1}}$ for every $c$, and all the $G_{K_c}$ are isomorphic. Let $K$ be tamely ramified with ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$. By part 4 of [tamely ramified 2-adic fields](tamely-ramified-2-adic-fields.md), the fields $L$ that part 2 of the classification compares with $K$ are the tamely ramified fields with the same $e$ and $f$, that is, the fields $K_c$. So part 2 holds for $K$.

For a single $e$, the same argument needs only some liftable $\alpha_a$ with $a$ prime to $d = \gcd(e, q - 1)$. Together with conjugation by powers of $\tau$, which shifts $c$ by multiples of $(1-q)/q$ and so through all of $d\mathbb{Z}/e$, it moves $H_0$ to every $H_c$. A negative answer would not by itself disprove part 2, because an isomorphism $G_K \to G_L$ need not extend to $G_{U_f}$.

*Proof of 1.* The subgroup $\overline{\langle\tau^e\rangle}$ is the only open subgroup of index $e$ in $I$, so it is normal in $\Gamma_f$. The closed subgroup generated by $\sigma_f\tau^c$ is procyclic and maps onto $\Gamma_f/I \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}/U_f) \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, so it maps isomorphically onto it, because a procyclic group is a quotient of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and a surjective endomorphism of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is an isomorphism. Hence $H_c$ is the product of $\overline{\langle\tau^e\rangle}$ and this subgroup, $H_c \cap I = \overline{\langle\tau^e\rangle}$, and $H_c I = \Gamma_f$. So $[K_c : U_f] = [\Gamma_f : H_c] = [I : H_c \cap I] = e$, and $K_c \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}} = U_f$. Thus $K_c$ has residue degree $f$ and ramification index $e$.

Conversely, let $K$ have ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$. As $e$ is odd, $K$ is tamely ramified, so $K \subseteq T$, and $K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}} = U_f$. Put $H = \mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$. Then $H \cap I = \mathrm{Gal}(T/K\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}})$ has index $[K\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}} : \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}] = e$ in $I$, so it is $\overline{\langle\tau^e\rangle}$. The image of $H$ in $\Gamma_f/I = \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}/U_f)$ is everything, so $H$ contains some $\sigma_f\tau^c$. Thus $H_c \subseteq H$, and both have index $e$, so $H = H_c$. If $H_c = H_{c'}$, then $\tau^{c' - c} = (\sigma_f\tau^c)^{-1}\sigma_f\tau^{c'}$ lies in $H_c \cap I = \overline{\langle\tau^e\rangle}$, so $c \equiv c' \pmod e$. Finally, $K_c \subseteq T$ gives $G_{K_c} = \pi^{-1}(\mathrm{Gal}(T/K_c)) = \pi^{-1}(H_c)$. $\square$

*Consistency with the field count.* Choose a Frobenius lift $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_2)$, so that $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^2$, and take $\sigma_f = \sigma^f$. Conjugation by $\tau$ sends $H_c$ to $H_{c + (1-q)/q}$, and conjugation by $\sigma$ sends it to $H_{2c}$. Since $q$ is a unit modulo $e$, the shifts generate $d\mathbb{Z}/e$. As $\sigma$ and $\tau$ generate $\mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_2)$ topologically and the normalizer of each $H_c$ is open, the classes of the fields $K_c$ up to conjugacy over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ correspond to the orbits of doubling on $\mathbb{Z}/d$, which matches the count in part 3 of [tamely ramified 2-adic fields](tamely-ramified-2-adic-fields.md). If $\sigma$ is chosen to fix $\sqrt[e]{2}$, which is possible because $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt[e]{2})/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is totally ramified, then $H_0$ fixes $U_f(\sqrt[e]{2})$, so $K_0 = U_f(\sqrt[e]{2})$.

**Two checks.** These are proved here. They use the following lemma, for any finite $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ with residue field of order $q$, maximal tamely ramified extension $T_k$, inertia group $I_k \subseteq \mathrm{Gal}(T_k/k)$ and $P_k = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}/T_k)$. With the notation above, $k = U_f$ gives $P_k = P$.

*Lemma.* The centralizer of a generator $\tau$ of $I_k$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(T_k/k)$ is $I_k$, and $P_k$ is the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G_k$. So every automorphism of $G_k$ preserves $P_k$ and induces an automorphism of $\mathrm{Gal}(T_k/k)$.

*Proof.* By the same reasoning as above, $I_k \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ and every element of $\mathrm{Gal}(T_k/k)$ is $\tau^a\sigma^b$ with $b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, for a Frobenius lift $\sigma$. If it commutes with $\tau$, then $q^b = 1$ in $\prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$. Then $q^b \equiv 1$ modulo $q^m - 1$ for every $m \ge 1$, and as $q$ has order $m$ modulo $q^m - 1$, this gives $b \in m\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ for every $m$, so $b = 0$. Now let $N$ be a closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G_k$ with image $M$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(T_k/k)$. Then $M \cap I_k$ is both pro-$p$ and of order prime to $p$, so it is trivial. As $M$ and $I_k$ are normal, $[M, I_k] \subseteq M \cap I_k = 1$, so $M$ centralizes $\tau$, and $M \subseteq I_k$. Hence $M = 1$ and $N \subseteq P_k$. Finally, $P_k$ is itself pro-$p$, because it is the wild inertia group of $k$. This standard fact is used here without being checked in a source. $\square$

- *The analogue for odd $p$ can fail.* Over $\mathbb{Q}_3$, the argument of part 1 with $e = 2$ and $f = 1$ shows that the subgroups $H_0, H_1$ of $\mathrm{Gal}(T_{\mathbb{Q}_3}/\mathbb{Q}_3)$ fix the two ramified quadratic extensions $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{3})$ and $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{-3})$, in some order, and $\alpha$ exchanges them. These fields are abelian and distinct, so their absolute Galois groups are not isomorphic, by part 1 of the classification. By the lemma, then, no automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}$ induces $\alpha$. For $U_f$, part 1 of the classification gives no such obstruction. Since $\alpha$ fixes $I$ pointwise and induces the identity on $\Gamma_f/I$, we have $\alpha(H) \cap I = H \cap I$ and $\alpha(H)I = HI$ for every open subgroup $H$ of $\Gamma_f$. So $T^{\alpha(H)}$ has the same ramification index and residue degree over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ as $T^H$, and hence the same degree. By part 1 of [tamely ramified 2-adic fields](tamely-ramified-2-adic-fields.md), a tamely ramified field of residue degree $f'$ has maximal abelian subfield $U_{f'}$. So a lift of $\alpha$ would carry the absolute Galois group of each tamely ramified $K \supseteq U_f$ to that of a field $L$ with $[L:\mathbb{Q}_2] = [K:\mathbb{Q}_2]$ and $L^0 = K^0$, as part 1 requires.
- *Automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ do not suffice.* Let $\Gamma_1 = \mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_2)$, with $\sigma, \tau$ as above. Since $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}\tau^{-1} = \tau$ and $\Gamma_1/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is abelian, $I$ is the closed commutator subgroup of $\Gamma_1$, so an automorphism $\beta$ of $\Gamma_1$ maps $\tau$ to $\tau^u$ with $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ and $\sigma$ to $\sigma^v\tau^a$ with $v \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$. Applying $\beta$ to the relation gives $2^{v - 1} = 1$, so $v = 1$ by the argument in the lemma. One computes $(\sigma\tau^a)^f = \sigma^f\tau^{b}$ with $b = a(1 + 2^{-1} + \dots + 2^{1-f}) = a(q-1)2^{1-f}$, so $\beta(H_c) = H_{b + uc}$, and $b \equiv 0 \pmod d$. On the doubling orbits on $\mathbb{Z}/d$, therefore, $\beta$ acts by multiplication by $u$, and it fixes the orbit of $0$. With the lemma for $k = \mathbb{Q}_2$, this shows that every automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ maps $G_{U_f(\sqrt[e]{2})}$ onto the absolute Galois group of a conjugate of $U_f(\sqrt[e]{2})$. In the degree $6$ example of [tamely ramified 2-adic fields](tamely-ramified-2-adic-fields.md), then, no automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ carries $G_{U_2(\sqrt[3]{2})}$ onto $G_{U_2(\sqrt[3]{2\omega})}$. Taking $e = q - 1$ shows that for $f \ge 2$ no automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ that preserves $G_{U_f}$ restricts to a lift of $\alpha$. In degree $21$, where $e = 7$ and $f = 3$, an automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ could at most exchange the two classes other than that of $U_3(\sqrt[7]{2})$, which needs $u \equiv 3, 5$ or $6 \pmod 7$.

**Heuristic.** This paragraph is speculative. For odd $p$, Jannsen and Wingberg's presentation of $G_K$ holds for every pair of generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\mathrm{Gal}(T_K/K)$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, with a relator that also depends on a lift $\beta$ of the inverse of the character by which $\mathrm{Gal}(T_K/K)$ acts on the $p$-power roots of unity in $T_K$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, Theorem 2]. If $G_{U_f}$ had a presentation of this shape over $\Gamma_f$, valid for every such pair, with the isomorphism compatible with the maps to $\Gamma_f$ and with a relator given by one fixed word in $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_n$, then $\alpha$ would lift: the automorphism of the free pro-$2$ operator group that acts as $\alpha$ on $\Gamma_f$ and fixes each $x_i$ carries the relator for $(\sigma_f, \tau)$ to the relator for $(\sigma_f\tau, \tau)$. The character causes no trouble here, since the only $2$-power roots of unity in $T$ are $\pm 1$, as $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is wildly ramified. In the $\mathbb{Q}_3$ example, by contrast, $\tau$ acts on $\mu_3$ by inversion, so $\alpha$ does not preserve the character. No presentation of $G_{U_f}$ is known for $f \ge 2$, as far as the sources below show.

**Status.** Open as far as the sources opened here show. Roe and Turturean present $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2} = G_{U_1}$, where $\alpha$ is inner, in a manuscript that has not been peer reviewed [@roeturturean2026, Theorem 1.2]. Among their further questions are the extension of their presentation to every finite $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$, and the computation of $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_2})$, or at least of its action on the marked tame and pro-$2$ data [@roeturturean2026, §11, items 1 and 6]. Nishio's Theorem A, which constructs an outer automorphism of $G_k$ with a special property, assumes that $k$ contains a primitive $4$-th root of unity and is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, and its proof starts from Diekert's presentation [@nishio2025, Theorem A and Proposition 1.1]. $U_f$ does not contain $\sqrt{-1}$. [Schneider's conjecture](schneider-conjecture-on-2-adic-galois-groups.md) needs even degree and $K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$ not real, while for $U_f$ this intersection is $\mathbb{Q}_2$. Kumpitsch's 2022 Frankfurt thesis, *Outer automorphisms of the absolute Galois group of local fields of mixed characteristic*, could not be opened for this entry. A literature search in November 2026 found no answer, but that search was not exhaustive.
