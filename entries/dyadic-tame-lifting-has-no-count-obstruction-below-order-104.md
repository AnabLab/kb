**Statement (proved here).** Let $k/\mathbb Q_2$ be finite, let $G=G_k$, and let $\pi:G\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma$ be its tame quotient. For every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$ and finite epimorphisms $q:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow D$, $r:H\twoheadrightarrow D$, the [marked surjection counts](finite-quotient-criterion-for-frobenius-shift-lifting.md) satisfy
$$
e_r(q\pi)=e_r(q\alpha\pi)\quad\text{if }|H|<104,
\qquad e_r(\eta)=\#\{f:G\twoheadrightarrow H:rf=\eta\}.
\tag{1}
$$
All maps are continuous, and conjugate maps are counted separately.

**Proof.** The [bound below order 96](dyadic-tame-lifting-has-no-count-obstruction-below-order-96.md) is already known. If every epimorphism to $H$ has abelian wild image, the [wild-abelian reduction](dyadic-tame-lifting-has-no-count-obstruction-below-order-48.md), Step 1, proves (1). Otherwise $H$ has a nonabelian normal $2$-subgroup, so its order is divisible by eight. The only multiple of eight in $[96,104)$ is $96$; assume henceforth $|H|=96$.

*1. A normal Sylow subgroup.* Suppose the Sylow $2$-subgroup $S$ is normal. An element of order three gives $H=S\rtimes C_3$. If the cubic action is trivial, the direct-product reduction proves (1). Otherwise the [small cubic-action theorem](cubic-actions-on-small-2-groups-have-central-frattini-subgroups.md) gives $\Phi(S)\subseteq Z(S)$, since $|S|=32$. The [central Frattini theorem at arbitrary exponent](dyadic-central-frattini-counts-at-arbitrary-exponent.md) proves (1) for every marking. It allows both fixed quotient vectors and nontrivial action on the Frattini subgroup.

*2. A nonnormal Sylow subgroup.* Fix a tame marking $q,r$. Write $I$ for tame inertia. Every tame automorphism fixes $\Gamma/I$ pointwise, as in the [symmetric-marking calculation](symmetric-tame-markings-give-no-dyadic-count-obstruction.md). If $q(I)=1$, then $q\alpha=q$ and there is nothing to prove.

Otherwise $q(I)=C_3$, since it is cyclic of odd order dividing $96$. The quotient $D/C_3$ is a cyclic $2$-group. If $C_3$ were central in $D$, then $D$ would be abelian: it is generated by that central subgroup and one lift of a generator of its cyclic quotient. Such a finite abelian group has a quotient of order three. Composing with $r$ would give an epimorphism $H\to C_3$, whose kernel has order $32$, making its Sylow subgroup normal. This contradicts the present case.

Thus a generator of $D/C_3$ acts by inversion on $C_3$. Choose tame generators $\tau,\sigma$, with $\tau$ generating $I$ and $\sigma$ a Frobenius lift, and put $u=q(\tau)$, $v=q(\sigma)$. Then $u$ has order three and
$$
vuv^{-1}=u^{-1}.
$$
The tame automorphism formulas from the symmetric-marking calculation give
$$
q\alpha(\tau)=u^b,\qquad q\alpha(\sigma)=vu^h,
\qquad b\equiv1\text{ or }-1\pmod3.
$$
These pairs are conjugate in $D$. First conjugate by $1$ or $v$ to give the required image of $u$, leaving $v$ fixed. Then conjugate by a power $u^j$: it fixes the new image of $u$ and sends $v$ to $vu^{-2j}$. Multiplication by $-2$ is bijective modulo three, so any $h$ is obtained. Hence $q\alpha=c_zq$ for some $z\in D$.

Lift $z$ to $w\in H$. The bijection $f\mapsto c_wf$, with inverse $c_{w^{-1}}$, preserves continuity and surjectivity and changes the marking from $q\pi$ to $q\alpha\pi$. This proves (1) for every marking in the nonnormal case, and completes the proof. $\square$

**Checks and scope.** The second case does not assume that the Frobenius image is an involution. For instance it includes $C_3\rtimes C_4$ with inversion action, where it has order four. In the first case the Frattini subgroup may have exponent four, as in the order-96 example in the arbitrary-exponent theorem; restricting to elementary-abelian Frattini subgroups would miss it. The conjugacy argument and the cited count theorem also preserve zero counts.

This bounds the order of a finite target, not the index of a canonical quotient. It asserts no obstruction at order $104$, constructs no automorphism of $G_k$, and leaves the full Frobenius-shift and dyadic classification problems open.
