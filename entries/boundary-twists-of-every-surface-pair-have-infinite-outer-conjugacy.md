**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, and $P$ its wild inertia, which is free pro-$p$ and characteristic ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). Put $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$ with augmentation $\epsilon$, and $V=P/P^{(2)}$. Use the presentation $G=F/(\varrho)$ of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md): $\mathcal G$, generated by $\sigma,\tau$, maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma$, the wild generators are $x_0,\dots,x_d$, and
$$\varrho=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa\,x_1^{p^s}\,C,$$
where $C=[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d]$ for even $d$, and $C=[x_1,y_1][x_2,x_3]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d]$ for odd $d$, with $y_1$ in the closed subgroup generated by $x_1$ and $\mathcal G$ (Setting of [the shear theorem](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-wild-frattini.md)). By parts 1 and 3 of the tame-group-algebra theorem, $V=L\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^d\Lambda\bar x_j$ with $L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$ and each $\Lambda\bar x_j$ free of rank one, and $Z(\Gamma)=1$. An automorphism of $G$ is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$; it then acts $\Lambda$-linearly on $V$. Write $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$.

Let $r=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor$. The surface pairs, numbered in the order of their commutators in $\varrho$, are $(a_i,b_i)=(x_{2i},x_{2i+1})$ for odd $d$ and $(x_{2i+1},x_{2i+2})$ for even $d$, for $1\le i\le r$. Put $w_i=[a_i,b_i]$. Then
$$\varrho=R\,w_1w_2\cdots w_r,$$
where $R$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by $\mathcal G$, $x_0$, $x_1$ and, for even $d$, $x_2$. So no $a_i$ or $b_i$ occurs in $R$, and $x_1$ lies in no pair. The first pair $(a_1,b_1)$ is the pair of the tame-group-algebra theorem.

*Twists.* For an interval $I=\{i,i+1,\dots,j\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}$ put $W_I=w_iw_{i+1}\cdots w_j$. For $t\in\mathbb Z_p$, let $T_{I,t}$ fix $\mathcal G$ and every $x_l$ outside the pairs indexed by $I$, and send $a_m\mapsto W_I^ta_mW_I^{-t}$ and $b_m\mapsto W_I^tb_mW_I^{-t}$ for $m\in I$. Write $T^{(i)}_t=T_{\{i\},t}$ for the *boundary twist* of the $i$-th pair; $T^{(1)}_t$ is the twist $T_t$ of [the first-pair theorem](first-surface-pair-boundary-twists-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md). For $\mathbf t=(t_1,\dots,t_r)\in\mathbb Z_p^r$ put $T_{\mathbf t}=T^{(1)}_{t_1}\cdots T^{(r)}_{t_r}$.

*Lower central input.* As in [the boundary-twist theorem](boundary-twists-exclude-finite-outer-conjugacy-of-wild-scalars.md), let $P_j$ be the closed lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$ and $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$. Its lower central input gives the following. The $\mathcal L_j$ are torsion-free. $\mathcal L_j/p$ is the completed degree-$j$ free Lie space $\mathcal L_j(V)$ on $V=H/p$, functorially: an automorphism acting on $V$ by $X$ acts on $\mathcal L_3/p$ by $\mathcal L_3(X)$, with $\mathcal L_3(X)[[x,y],z]=[[Xx,Xy],Xz]$. The bracket map $H\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ is injective. The bracket map $\mathcal L_2\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_3)$ has saturated image: if $[h,H]\subseteq p^k\mathcal L_3$, then $h\in p^k\mathcal L_2$. A degree-two element $\bar h$ of $\mathcal L_2(V)$ with $[\bar h,\bar x_1]=0$ is zero (step 3 of the first-pair theorem).

For an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ acting trivially on $P/P_3$, step 1 of the boundary-twist theorem defines the continuous homomorphism $J(\theta):H\to\mathcal L_3$, $xP_2\mapsto\theta(x)x^{-1}P_4$. It satisfies $J(\theta\theta')=J(\theta)+J(\theta')$ and $J(\beta\theta\beta^{-1})=\beta_3J(\theta)\beta_H^{-1}$, where $\beta_H,\beta_3$ are the actions of $\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ on $H,\mathcal L_3$. Directly from the definition, $J(c_g)=\operatorname{ad}(gP_3)$ for $g\in P_2$, where $\operatorname{ad}(h)=[h,\cdot\,]$. If $\theta$ fixes $\mathcal G$ pointwise, then $J(\theta)$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant, since $\theta(\rho x\rho^{-1})(\rho x\rho^{-1})^{-1}=\rho\,\theta(x)x^{-1}\rho^{-1}$.

*A coefficient map.* For $1\le i\le r$, let $\pi_{a_i},\pi_{b_i}:V\to\Lambda$ be the coefficients of $\bar a_i,\bar b_i$; they are continuous and $\Lambda$-linear. View $\mathcal L_3(V)$ inside the completed tensor space $\varprojlim_U(V/U)^{\otimes3}$, with $[[x,y],z]=xyz-yxz-zxy+zyx$, and let
$$\kappa_i=\pi_{a_i}\otimes\pi_{a_i}\otimes\pi_{b_i}:\mathcal L_3(V)\to\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^3]].$$
It is defined level by level on finite quotients and is continuous and linear; it records the tensor pattern $a_ia_ib_i$ of step 3 of the first-pair theorem. Put $\Delta_3(\rho)=\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\rho$, extended continuously to $\Lambda$. Then:

- ($\kappa$1) $\kappa_i(\mu\cdot\xi)=\Delta_3(\mu)\,\kappa_i(\xi)$ for $\mu\in\Lambda$, because $\pi_{a_i}(\rho x)=\rho\,\pi_{a_i}(x)$ and $\pi_{b_i}(\rho x)=\rho\,\pi_{b_i}(x)$ for $\rho\in\Gamma$, and by linearity and continuity.
- ($\kappa$2) Let $X$ be a $\Lambda$-linear automorphism of $V$ preserving $L$ and every $\Lambda\bar x_j$, with $X\bar a_i=u\bar a_i$ and $X\bar b_i=v\bar b_i$. Then $\pi_{a_i}(Xx)=\pi_{a_i}(x)u$ and $\pi_{b_i}(Xx)=\pi_{b_i}(x)v$, so $\kappa_i(\mathcal L_3(X)\xi)=\kappa_i(\xi)\,(u\otimes u\otimes v)$.
- ($\kappa$3) $\kappa_i([[\bar a_i,\bar b_i],\bar a_i])=-1$, since the expansion is $2\bar a_i\bar b_i\bar a_i-\bar b_i\bar a_i\bar a_i-\bar a_i\bar a_i\bar b_i$. And $\kappa_i$ kills $[[x,y],z]$ whenever one of $x,y,z$ lies in a summand other than $\Lambda\bar a_i$ and $\Lambda\bar b_i$, since $\pi_{a_i}$ and $\pi_{b_i}$ kill it.

**Lemma 1 (adjacent swaps; proved here).** Let $1\le i<r$, and write $(a,b)=(a_i,b_i)$, $(c,e)=(a_{i+1},b_{i+1})$, $w=w_i$ and $w'=w_{i+1}$. Let $\varphi_i$ fix $\mathcal G$ and every other $x_l$, and set
$$\varphi_i(a)=c,\qquad\varphi_i(b)=e,\qquad\varphi_i(c)=w'^{-1}aw',\qquad\varphi_i(e)=w'^{-1}bw'.$$

- (a) $\varphi_i$ induces a tame-trivial automorphism of $G$. On $V$ it exchanges $\mu\bar a\leftrightarrow\mu\bar c$ and $\mu\bar b\leftrightarrow\mu\bar e$ for $\mu\in\Lambda$, and fixes $L$ and the other $\Lambda\bar x_j$.
- (b) $\varphi_i^{-1}T^{(i)}_t\varphi_i=T^{(i+1)}_t$ and $\varphi_i^{-1}T^{(i+1)}_t\varphi_i=T^{(i)}_t$, while $\varphi_i$ commutes with $T^{(l)}_t$ for $l\notin\{i,i+1\}$.

Put $\Psi_1=1$ and $\Psi_i=\varphi_{i-1}^{-1}\cdots\varphi_1^{-1}$. Fix the automorphism $\beta$ of step 1 of the first-pair theorem: it is tame-trivial and acts on $V$ $\Lambda$-linearly, fixing $L$ and every $\Lambda\bar x_j$ except $\bar a_1\mapsto\lambda\bar a_1$ and $\bar b_1\mapsto(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar b_1$, where $\lambda=1+(\gamma-1)^2$. Put $\beta_i=\Psi_i\beta\Psi_i^{-1}$. Then:

- (c) $T^{(i)}_t=\Psi_iT^{(1)}_t\Psi_i^{-1}$, and $\beta_i$ is tame-trivial and acts on $V$ $\Lambda$-linearly by $\bar a_i\mapsto\lambda\bar a_i$ and $\bar b_i\mapsto(\lambda^*)^{-1}\bar b_i$, fixing $L$ and every other $\Lambda\bar x_j$.

**Lemma 2 (twists; proved here).**

- (a) $T_{I,t}$ induces a tame-trivial automorphism of $G$ that acts trivially on $P/P_3$, and $T_{I,t}T_{I,t'}=T_{I,t+t'}$.
- (b) *Commuting twists.* Let $X,X'$ be sets of wild generators and $U,U'$ elements of the closed subgroups they generate. Let $\theta$ fix $\mathcal G$ and the generators outside $X$ and conjugate each element of $X$ by $U$, with $\theta(U)=U$; define $\theta'$ from $X',U'$ in the same way, with $\theta'(U')=U'$. If $X\cap X'=\emptyset$, or if $X'\subseteq X$ and $\theta'(U)=U$, then $\theta\theta'=\theta'\theta$. In particular the $T^{(i)}_t$ commute with each other, and $\mathbf t\mapsto T_{\mathbf t}$ is a homomorphism $\mathbb Z_p^r\to\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.
- (c) $J(T_{I,t})=tD_I$ for a continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant $D_I:H\to\mathcal L_3$. Its reduction $\delta_I$ modulo $p$ satisfies $\delta_I(\bar x_1)=0$, $\kappa_m(\delta_I(\bar a_m))=-1$ for $m\in I$, and $\delta_I(\bar a_m)=0$ for $m\notin I$.

**Theorem (a criterion; proved here).** Let $\theta$ be a tame-trivial automorphism of $G$ acting trivially on $P/P_3$. Suppose
$$J(\theta)=p^kD+\operatorname{ad}(h_0)$$
with $k\ge0$, $h_0\in\mathcal L_2$ and $D:H\to\mathcal L_3$ continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant, and let $\delta:V\to\mathcal L_3(V)$ be the reduction of $D$ modulo $p$. If $\delta(\bar x_1)=0$ and $\kappa_i(\delta(\bar a_i))\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ for some $1\le i\le r$, then the classes
$$[\beta_i^j\theta\beta_i^{-j}]\in\operatorname{Out}(G),\qquad j=0,1,2,\ldots,$$
are pairwise distinct. In particular the class of $\theta$ has infinitely many conjugates, and $\theta$ is not inner.

**Corollary 1 (products of twists).** Let $\theta$ be a finite product, in any order, of twists $T_{I,t}$, and let $t_I\in\mathbb Z_p$ be the sum of the parameters of its factors with interval $I$. Suppose the $t_I$ are not all zero, put $k=\min_Iv_p(t_I)$, and suppose that $v_p\big(\sum_{I\ni m}t_I\big)=k$ for some pair $m$. Then $\theta$ has infinitely many outer conjugates. This applies to $T_{I,t}$ for every interval $I$ and every $t\ne0$, and to $T_{\mathbf t}$ for every $\mathbf t\ne0$, taking $m$ with $v_p(t_m)$ minimal.

**Corollary 2 (the boundary-twist subgroup).** The map $\mathbf t\mapsto[T_{\mathbf t}]$ is an isomorphism of $\mathbb Z_p^r$ onto a closed subgroup of $\operatorname{Out}(G)$, every nonidentity element of which has infinitely many conjugates. Each $[T^{(i)}_t]$ is conjugate to $[T^{(1)}_t]$.

**Corollary 3 (with complementary twists).** Let $d\ge4$ be even and $1\le k\le d/2$, and let $T_{k,t}$ be the complementary twist of [the complementary-twist theorem](complementary-twists-have-infinitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), which conjugates $x_1,\dots,x_{2k}$ by $U_k^t$ with $U_k=x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2]\cdots[x_{2k-1},x_{2k}]$. Then $(t_0,\mathbf t)\mapsto[T_{k,t_0}T_{\mathbf t}]$ is an isomorphism of $\mathbb Z_p^{d/2}$ onto a closed subgroup of $\operatorname{Out}(G)$, every nonidentity element of which has infinitely many conjugates.

**Proof of Lemma 1.** As $F$ is free among semidirect products of $\mathcal G$ with pro-$p$ groups, $\varphi_i$ and the map $\psi_i$ given by
$$\psi_i(c)=a,\qquad\psi_i(e)=b,\qquad\psi_i(a)=wcw^{-1},\qquad\psi_i(b)=wew^{-1},$$
fixing $\mathcal G$ and the other $x_l$, are endomorphisms of $F$. They satisfy $\varphi_i(w)=w'$, $\varphi_i(w')=w'^{-1}ww'$, $\psi_i(w')=w$ and $\psi_i(w)=ww'w^{-1}$. Hence $\psi_i\varphi_i(c)=\psi_i(w')^{-1}\psi_i(a)\psi_i(w')=w^{-1}(wcw^{-1})w=c$, and similarly $\psi_i\varphi_i(e)=e$, while $\psi_i\varphi_i(a)=\psi_i(c)=a$ and $\psi_i\varphi_i(b)=b$. Likewise $\varphi_i\psi_i(a)=\varphi_i(w)\varphi_i(c)\varphi_i(w)^{-1}=w'(w'^{-1}aw')w'^{-1}=a$, similarly for $b$, and $\varphi_i\psi_i(c)=c$, $\varphi_i\psi_i(e)=e$. So $\varphi_i$ is an automorphism of $F$ with inverse $\psi_i$. It fixes $R$ and the $w_l$ with $l\notin\{i,i+1\}$, and
$$\varphi_i(ww')=w'\cdot w'^{-1}ww'=ww'.$$
So $\varphi_i(\varrho)=\varrho$ and $\psi_i(\varrho)=\varrho$. Hence both map $(\varrho)$ into itself, and $\varphi_i$ induces an automorphism of $G$. It fixes $\mathcal G$, so it is tame-trivial and $\Lambda$-linear on $V$. Its values on the generators give (a), since conjugation acts trivially on $V$.

(b) For $t\in\mathbb Z_p$, continuity gives $\psi_i(w^t)=ww'^tw^{-1}$. We compute $\psi_iT^{(i)}_t\varphi_i$ on generators. It sends $a\mapsto\psi_i(T^{(i)}_t(c))=\psi_i(c)=a$, and likewise $b\mapsto b$. It sends
$$c\mapsto\psi_i\big(w'^{-1}w^taw^{-t}w'\big)=w^{-1}\cdot ww'^tw^{-1}\cdot wcw^{-1}\cdot ww'^{-t}w^{-1}\cdot w=w'^tcw'^{-t},$$
and likewise $e\mapsto w'^tew'^{-t}$. It fixes $\mathcal G$ and the other $x_l$. So $\psi_iT^{(i)}_t\varphi_i=T^{(i+1)}_t$. Next, $\psi_iT^{(i+1)}_t\varphi_i$ sends $a\mapsto\psi_i(w'^tcw'^{-t})=w^taw^{-t}$, likewise $b\mapsto w^tbw^{-t}$, and $c\mapsto\psi_i(w'^{-1}aw')=w^{-1}(wcw^{-1})w=c$, likewise $e\mapsto e$. So it equals $T^{(i)}_t$. For $l\notin\{i,i+1\}$, $T^{(l)}_t$ fixes $a,b,c,e$, while $\varphi_i$ and $\psi_i$ fix $a_l$, $b_l$ and $w_l$. So $\psi_iT^{(l)}_t\varphi_i=T^{(l)}_t$.

(c) $\Psi_{i+1}=\varphi_i^{-1}\Psi_i$, so by induction $\Psi_{i+1}T^{(1)}_t\Psi_{i+1}^{-1}=\varphi_i^{-1}T^{(i)}_t\varphi_i=T^{(i+1)}_t$. By (a), $\varphi_i^{-1}$ acts on $V$ like $\varphi_i$. So, by induction, $\Psi_i$ acts on $V$ by a $\Lambda$-linear map sending $\bar a_1\mapsto\bar a_i$ and $\bar b_1\mapsto\bar b_i$ and permuting the summands $L,\Lambda\bar x_1,\dots,\Lambda\bar x_d$ by sending basis vectors to basis vectors. Conjugating the action of $\beta$ by it gives the action of $\beta_i$. Each $\varphi_j$ fixes $\mathcal G$, so $\beta_i$ is tame-trivial. $\square$

**Proof of Lemma 2.** (a) $T_{I,t}$ is an endomorphism of $F$ fixing $\mathcal G$. For $m\in I$ it sends $w_m\mapsto W_I^tw_mW_I^{-t}$, so $T_{I,t}(W_I)=W_I^tW_IW_I^{-t}=W_I$, and $T_{I,t}(W_I^{t'})=W_I^{t'}$ by continuity. It fixes $R$ and the $w_l$ with $l\notin I$. As $W_I$ is a consecutive segment of $w_1\cdots w_r$, $T_{I,t}(\varrho)=\varrho$. Also $T_{I,t}T_{I,t'}(a_m)=T_{I,t}(W_I^{t'}a_mW_I^{-t'})=W_I^{t'}W_I^ta_mW_I^{-t}W_I^{-t'}=T_{I,t+t'}(a_m)$, likewise for $b_m$, and both sides fix the other generators. As $T_{I,0}=1$, $T_{I,t}$ is an automorphism of $F$ with inverse $T_{I,-t}$, preserving $(\varrho)$. So it induces an automorphism of $G$, which is tame-trivial.

For triviality on $P/P_3$: $T_{I,t}(a_m)a_m^{-1}=[W_I^t,a_m]\in[P_2,P]=P_3$, likewise for $b_m$, and the other $x_l$ are fixed. The elements $y\in P$ with $T_{I,t}(y)y^{-1}\in P_3$ form a closed subgroup $S$, because $T_{I,t}(yy')(yy')^{-1}=T_{I,t}(y)\big(T_{I,t}(y')y'^{-1}\big)T_{I,t}(y)^{-1}\cdot T_{I,t}(y)y^{-1}$ and $P_3$ is normal. It is stable under conjugation by $\mathcal G$, which $T_{I,t}$ fixes. So $S\mathcal G$ is a closed subgroup of $G$ containing all the generators, whence $S\mathcal G=G$. As $S\subseteq P$ and $P\cap\mathcal G=1$, $S=P$.

(b) In the disjoint case, $\theta'$ fixes $X$ pointwise, hence fixes $U$, and $\theta$ fixes $U'$. So for $x\in X$, $\theta'\theta(x)=\theta'(UxU^{-1})=UxU^{-1}=\theta\theta'(x)$, symmetrically for $x\in X'$, and both composites fix everything else. In the nested case, $\theta$ restricts on the closed subgroup generated by $X$ to conjugation by $U$, so $\theta(U')=UU'U^{-1}$. For $x\in X'$,
$$\theta\theta'(x)=\theta(U'xU'^{-1})=UU'U^{-1}\cdot UxU^{-1}\cdot UU'^{-1}U^{-1}=UU'xU'^{-1}U^{-1}=\theta'(UxU^{-1})=\theta'\theta(x),$$
using $\theta'(U)=U$. For $x\in X\setminus X'$, both composites give $UxU^{-1}$, again by $\theta'(U)=U$. Both fix everything else. For $T^{(i)}_t$ and $T^{(l)}_{t'}$ with $i\ne l$, the disjoint case applies with $U=w_i^t$ and $U'=w_l^{t'}$.

(c) For $y\in P_2$ and $x\in P$, the class $[y,x]P_4\in\mathcal L_3$ depends only on $yP_3\in\mathcal L_2$ and $xP_2\in H$, as $[P_3,P]\subseteq P_4$ and $[P_2,P_2]\subseteq P_4$. It is additive in $y$, since $[yy',x]=y[y',x]y^{-1}\cdot[y,x]$ and $[P_2,P_3]\subseteq P_4$. So $[y^t,x]P_4=t\,[yP_3,xP_2]$ for $t\in\mathbb Z_p$, by continuity. As $W_IP_3=\sum_{l\in I}[a_l,b_l]$ in $\mathcal L_2$, for $m\in I$
$$J(T_{I,t})(a_mP_2)=t\sum_{l\in I}[[a_l,b_l],a_m],\qquad J(T_{I,t})(b_mP_2)=t\sum_{l\in I}[[a_l,b_l],b_m],$$
and $J(T_{I,t})$ vanishes at the other $x_lP_2$. The map $J(T_{I,t})$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant, as $T_{I,t}$ fixes $\mathcal G$. By the argument in (a), $H$ is topologically generated by the $\mathcal G$-conjugates of the $x_lP_2$. So $J(T_{I,t})=tD_I$ with $D_I=J(T_{I,1})$, both sides being continuous, $\Gamma$-equivariant and equal on the $x_lP_2$. Reducing modulo $p$, $\delta_I(\bar x_1)=0$, $\delta_I(\bar a_m)=0$ for $m\notin I$, and for $m\in I$
$$\delta_I(\bar a_m)=[[\bar a_m,\bar b_m],\bar a_m]+\sum_{l\in I,\,l\ne m}[[\bar a_l,\bar b_l],\bar a_m].$$
By ($\kappa$3), $\kappa_m$ maps the first term to $-1$ and kills the others. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** Choose $g_0\in P_2$ with $g_0P_3=h_0$. The automorphism $c_{g_0}$ is tame-trivial, acts trivially on $P/P_3$, and $J(c_{g_0})=\operatorname{ad}(h_0)$. So $\theta c_{g_0}^{-1}$ satisfies the hypotheses with $h_0=0$. Since $\beta_i^jc_{g_0}\beta_i^{-j}=c_{\beta_i^j(g_0)}$, it gives the same classes as $\theta$. We may therefore assume $h_0=0$.

Suppose two of the classes agree. Then for some $n>0$ and $g\in G$,
$$\beta_i^n\theta\beta_i^{-n}\theta^{-1}=c_g.$$
The left side is tame-trivial, so $gP\in Z(\Gamma)=1$ and $g\in P$. It acts trivially on $P/P_3$, as $P_3$ is characteristic, so $[g,P]\subseteq P_3$, and injectivity of $H\to\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ gives $g\in P_2$. Let $C_H$ and $C_3$ be the actions of $\beta_i^n$ on $H$ and $\mathcal L_3$. The rules for $J$ give
$$p^k\big(C_3DC_H^{-1}-D\big)=\operatorname{ad}(h),\qquad h=gP_3\in\mathcal L_2.$$
So $[h,H]\subseteq p^k\mathcal L_3$, and saturation gives $h=p^kh'$ with $h'\in\mathcal L_2$. As $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_3)$ is torsion-free,
$$C_3DC_H^{-1}-D=\operatorname{ad}(h').\tag{1}$$
By Lemma 1 (c), $\beta_i^n$ acts on $V$ by the $\Lambda$-linear map $C$ that preserves $L$ and every $\Lambda\bar x_j$, fixes all of them except $\Lambda\bar a_i$ and $\Lambda\bar b_i$, and satisfies
$$C\bar a_i=u\bar a_i,\qquad C\bar b_i=v\bar b_i,\qquad u=\lambda^n,\quad v=(u^*)^{-1},\quad\epsilon(v)=1,$$
as in step 1 of the first-pair theorem. $C_H$ reduces to $C$, and by functoriality $C_3$ reduces to $\mathcal L_3(C)$. The map $\delta$ is continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant, hence $\Lambda$-linear. Modulo $p$, (1) becomes
$$\mathcal L_3(C)\,\delta\,C^{-1}-\delta=\operatorname{ad}(\bar h')\qquad\text{on }V.\tag{2}$$
Evaluate (2) at $\bar x_1$. Since $C^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1$ and $\delta(\bar x_1)=0$, the left side vanishes, so $[\bar h',\bar x_1]=0$ and $\bar h'=0$. Thus $\mathcal L_3(C)\delta=\delta C$. Evaluate this at $\bar a_i$ and apply $\kappa_i$, with $c=\kappa_i(\delta(\bar a_i))\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. By ($\kappa$2), the left side gives $c\,(u\otimes u\otimes v)$. The right side is $\delta(u\bar a_i)=u\cdot\delta(\bar a_i)$, which by ($\kappa$1) gives $c\,\Delta_3(u)$. Dividing by $c$,
$$u\otimes u\otimes v=\Delta_3(u).$$
Applying $\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon$ and using $\epsilon(v)=1$ gives $u\otimes u=\Delta_2(u)$. This is identity (3) of the first-pair theorem for the same $u=\lambda^n$, which its step 4 shows to be false for $n>0$, by comparing the coefficient of $X^NY^N$ in the unramified pro-$p$ quotient. This contradiction proves that the classes are pairwise distinct. An inner automorphism has a single outer conjugate. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 1.** Each factor is tame-trivial and acts trivially on $P/P_3$ by Lemma 2 (a), so $\theta$ does too. By additivity of $J$ and Lemma 2 (c), $J(\theta)=\sum_It_ID_I=p^kD$ with $D=\sum_I(t_I/p^k)D_I$, a finite sum with coefficients in $\mathbb Z_p$. So $D$ is continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant. Its reduction $\delta=\sum_I\overline{t_I/p^k}\,\delta_I$ satisfies $\delta(\bar x_1)=0$ and, by Lemma 2 (c),
$$\kappa_m(\delta(\bar a_m))=-\sum_{I\ni m}\overline{t_I/p^k},$$
which is nonzero by hypothesis. The theorem applies with $i=m$ and $h_0=0$. For $\theta=T_{I,t}$ with $t\ne0$, the only nonzero $t_I$ is $t$, and any $m\in I$ works. For $T_{\mathbf t}$, only singletons occur, $\sum_{I\ni m}t_I=t_m$, and $m$ is chosen with $v_p(t_m)=k$. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 2.** By Lemma 2 (b) the map is a homomorphism. By Corollary 1, $[T_{\mathbf t}]$ has infinitely many conjugates for $\mathbf t\ne0$, so it is not the identity, and the map is injective. $G$ is topologically finitely generated. Modulo each open characteristic subgroup $N$, the images under $T_{\mathbf t}$ of the finitely many generators $\sigma,\tau,x_0,\dots,x_d$ are locally constant in $\mathbf t$, since $w_i^{t_i}a_iw_i^{-t_i}$ and $w_i^{t_i}b_iw_i^{-t_i}$ are continuous in $t_i$. They determine the induced automorphism of $G/N$. So the map is continuous into $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, and hence into $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. The group $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ is Hausdorff, because $\operatorname{Inn}(G)$ is the compact image of $G$. So the image of the compact group $\mathbb Z_p^r$ is closed, and the continuous bijection onto it is a homeomorphism. The last assertion is Lemma 1 (c). $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 3.** Each pair $(a_i,b_i)=(x_{2i+1},x_{2i+2})$ lies either inside $\{x_1,\dots,x_{2k}\}$, when $i\le k-1$, or outside it. $T^{(i)}_t$ fixes $U_k^{t_0}$: either $w_i$ is a factor of $U_k$ and the other letters are fixed, or no letter of $U_k$ is moved. Lemma 2 (b), in the nested or the disjoint case, shows that $T_{k,t_0}$ commutes with every $T^{(i)}_t$. With $T_{k,t_0}T_{k,t_0'}=T_{k,t_0+t_0'}$ (Lemma 1 (a) of the complementary-twist theorem), the map is a homomorphism from $\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_p^r$, and $1+r=d/2$. Let $(t_0,\mathbf t)\ne0$. If $t_0\ne0$, the Corollary of the complementary-twist theorem with $B=T_{\mathbf t}$, which is tame-trivial and acts trivially on $P/P_3$, gives infinitely many conjugates. If $t_0=0$, Corollary 1 does. Injectivity, continuity and closedness follow as in Corollary 2. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Origin.* The swap of Lemma 1 and the overall plan are suggested in [research issue 540](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/540). The plan there conjugates by $\beta$ and kills the terms of the other pairs modulo $p$. The theorem here does not need that cancellation: only $\delta(\bar x_1)$ and the pattern $a_ia_ib_i$ of $\delta(\bar a_i)$ enter, and both are blind to the other pairs. Lemma 1 (b) also shows that conjugation by $\varphi_i$ exchanges the parameters $t_i,t_{i+1}$ of $T_{\mathbf t}$, but this is not used.
- *What is not covered.* Corollary 1 needs the valuation condition. For $d\ge5$ take $\theta=T^{(1)}_1T^{(2)}_1T_{\{1,2\},-1}$. Then $\sum_{I\ni1}t_I=\sum_{I\ni2}t_I=0$, and $\kappa_m(\delta(\bar a_m))=0$ for every $m$. Yet $\delta(\bar a_1)=-[[\bar a_2,\bar b_2],\bar a_1]\ne0$, and $\theta$ is not inner. If $\theta=c_g$, then $g\in P_2$ as above, so $\delta=\operatorname{ad}(\bar h)$ with $\bar h=0$ by evaluation at $\bar x_1$, a contradiction. Whether $\theta$ has finitely many outer conjugates is not decided here; other tensor patterns, such as $a_2b_2a_1$, might decide it. For odd $d$, no exact analogue of the complementary twists is given, so Corollary 3 is stated for even $d$ only.
- *Scope.* Whether every tame-trivial automorphism with finitely many outer conjugates is inner remains item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535). Degree at most two and $p=2$ are not covered: the first has no surface pair, and the second is outside the linked inputs. For $d\in\{3,4\}$ there is one pair, and Corollary 2 is the first-pair theorem. Everything here is proved from the linked entries on `main`. No novelty is claimed, and the sources behind those entries were not reopened.
