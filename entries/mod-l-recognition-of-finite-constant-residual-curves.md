**Setting.** Use the notation of [mod-$\ell$ quasi-divisor chains](mod-l-quasi-divisor-chains-from-alternating-characters.md): $\Lambda=\mathbb F_\ell$, $A_F=\mathrm{Hom}(F^\times,\Lambda)$ with pointwise convergence, $I_v\subseteq D_v$ and $\rho_v:D_v\to A_{Fv}$. Let $F/\kappa$ be a function field of transcendence degree $d\ge2$ over an algebraically closed field with $\mathrm{char}\,\kappa\ne\ell$. Put
$$S^{\mathrm{in}}_F=\overline{\bigcup_{w}I_w}\subseteq A_F,$$
over all quasi-prime divisors $w$ of $F/\kappa$. This is the closure of the union, not of the span. For a quasi-prime $(d-1)$-divisor $v$, put $F_0=Fv$, $\kappa_0=\kappa v$ and
$$J_v=\rho_v(S^{\mathrm{in}}_F\cap D_v)\subseteq A_{F_0}.$$
Here $F_0/\kappa_0$ is a function field of one variable over an algebraically closed field, possibly of characteristic $\ell$, and $\rho_v$ is onto with kernel $I_v$. The set $J_v$ is stable under $\Lambda$; let $\mathcal L_v$ be the family of lines contained in it [@openai2026modl, (3.1), p. 11].

A valuation $c$ of a function field $B/b$, with $b$ algebraically closed, is a *constant reduction* if $\mathrm{trdeg}(Bc/bc)=\mathrm{trdeg}(B/b)$. Composing it with a prime divisor of $Bc/bc$ gives a *c.r. quasi-prime divisor*. The trivial $c$ is allowed, so prime divisors are included [@openai2026modl, §3.1, p. 11]. For a point $x$ of the smooth projective curve $C$ of $F_0/\kappa_0$, let $\delta_x=\mathrm{ord}_x\bmod\ell\in A_{F_0}$; its line $\Lambda\delta_x$ is $I_x$, a *point line*.

**The test.** Choose a generator $e_H$ of each $H\in\mathcal L_v$. The test asks:

- (i) for each $f\in F_0^\times$, $e_H(f)=0$ for all but finitely many $H$;
- (ii) the summation map $\prod_{H\in\mathcal L_v}\Lambda\to A_{F_0}$, $(a_H)\mapsto\sum_Ha_He_H$, which (i) makes well defined, has a one-dimensional kernel spanned by a vector with every coordinate nonzero.

Changing generators rescales coordinates by $\Lambda^\times$, which preserves both conditions [@openai2026modl, §3.3, p. 13].

**Theorem.**

1. $J_v\supseteq I_u$ for every c.r. quasi-prime divisor $u$ of $F_0/\kappa_0$. In particular every point line lies in $\mathcal L_v$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 3.2, p. 12].
2. Every element of $S^{\mathrm{in}}_F$ is valuative, and every element of $J_v$ lies in $I_{u'}$ for some valuation $u'$ of $F_0$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 3.3, p. 12].
3. Let $B/b$ be a function field of one variable over an algebraically closed field of any characteristic, with smooth projective curve $C$. The $\delta_x$, $x\in C(b)$, are nonzero and span distinct lines. Each $f\in B^\times$ has $\delta_x(f)\ne0$ for only finitely many $x$. The kernel of $\prod_x\Lambda\to A_B$, $(a_x)\mapsto\sum_xa_x\delta_x$, consists of the constant families. If $\mathrm{char}\,b\ne\ell$, the common kernel of the $\delta_x$ in $V_B$ has dimension $2g(C)$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 3.4, p. 13].
4. $\mathcal L_v$ passes the test if and only if $\kappa_0$ is algebraic over a finite field. In that case $\mathcal L_v$ is exactly the set of point lines [@openai2026modl, Proposition 3.5, p. 14].
5. Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be function fields over algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from $\ell$, of transcendence degree at least $2$. Let $\Phi:A_L\to A_K$ be a continuous linear isomorphism that preserves alternating pairs in both directions, for example the dual of a compatible isomorphism. Then $\Phi(S^{\mathrm{in}}_L)=S^{\mathrm{in}}_K$. For quasi-prime $(d-1)$-divisors $w\mapsto v$ matched as in part 4 of the [chains theorem](mod-l-quasi-divisor-chains-from-alternating-characters.md), the residual isomorphism $\varphi:A_{Lw}\to A_{Kv}$ maps $J_w$ onto $J_v$ and $\mathcal L_w$ onto $\mathcal L_v$. Hence $lw$ is algebraic over a finite field exactly when $kv$ is, and then $\varphi$ maps the point lines of $Lw$ bijectively onto those of $Kv$ [@openai2026modl, §3, pp. 11 and 13–14].

**Pop's density input.** Put $\widehat A_E=\mathrm{Hom}(E^\times,\mathbb Z_\ell)$, and define $\widehat I_a,\widehat D_a,\widehat\rho_v,\widehat S^{\mathrm{in}}_F,\widehat J_v$ by the same formulas [@openai2026modl, §3.1, p. 11]. The manuscript's Theorem 3.1 is:

- (P) Let $v$ be a valuation of $F$, with $Fv/\kappa v$ a function field, such that $vF$ has no nonzero $\ell$-divisible convex subgroup, and some field $\kappa\subseteq\kappa_1\subseteq F$ has $\kappa_1v=\kappa v$ and $\mathrm{trdeg}(F/\kappa_1)=\mathrm{trdeg}(Fv/\kappa_1v)=1$. Then $\widehat J_v\supseteq\widehat I_u$ for every c.r. quasi-prime divisor $u$ of $Fv/\kappa v$.

Pop works with a function field $K|k$, $k$ algebraically closed of characteristic $\ne\ell$ [@pop2015minimized, §1, p. 335]. In §4 he fixes a valuation $w$ with exactly the two properties of (P), and writes $K_0|k_0$ for its residue field. Let $\mathcal T^1(K)$ be the closure of the union of the minimized inertia groups $T^1_{\mathfrak v}$ over all quasi prime divisors $\mathfrak v$ of $K|k$. Let $\mathcal T^1(K_0)$ be the image of $\mathcal T^1(K)\cap Z^1_w$ in $\Pi^1_{K_0}=Z^1_w/T^1_w$ [@pop2015minimized, §4, p. 348]. From his Theorem 1.2 he deduces that the minimized inertia groups of all c.r. quasi prime divisors of $K_0|k_0$ lie in $\mathcal T^1(K_0)$ [@pop2015minimized, Theorem 1.2, p. 335, and §4, item 2), p. 351].

As $\kappa$ contains all $\ell$-power roots of unity, Kummer theory identifies the Galois group $\Pi_F$ of the maximal pro-$\ell$ abelian extension with $\widehat A_F$, with the Krull topology matching pointwise convergence. The fixer of $F[\sqrt[\ell^\infty]M]$ corresponds to $M^\perp$. So $T^1_a$ and $Z^1_a$, the fixers of $F[\sqrt[\ell^\infty]{U_a}]$ and $F[\sqrt[\ell^\infty]{U^1_a}]$, correspond to $\widehat I_a$ and $\widehat D_a$ [@pop2015minimized, §1, p. 334]. Pop records that $\Pi^1_{K_0}=Z^1_w/T^1_w$ is $\ell$-adically dual to $K_0^\times=U_w/U^1_w$, and the residual inertia $T^1_{\mathfrak w}/T^1_w$ to $K_0^\times/U_{w_0}$ for $\mathfrak w=w_0\circ w$, including in residue characteristic $\ell$ [@pop2015minimized, Remark 4.1, p. 349]. For the valuations used here, $\widehat\rho_v:\widehat D_v\to\widehat A_{Fv}$ is onto with kernel $\widehat I_v$, by the split sequence of Step 2 of the chains theorem, since $\mathbb Z_\ell$-characters also kill divisible groups. With Pop's $w$ taken to be $v$, these identifications turn $\mathcal T^1(K_0)$ into $\widehat J_v$, and the residual groups $T^1_{u\circ v}/T^1_v$ into the $\widehat I_u$. So item 2) is (P).

A quasi-prime $(d-1)$-divisor $v$ satisfies the hypotheses of (P). Its value group has no nonzero $\ell$-divisible convex subgroup by part 3 of the chains theorem; Pop also notes this class meets his hypotheses [@pop2015minimized, §4, p. 348]. For $\kappa_1$, choose $x_1,\dots,x_{d-1}$ with values independent modulo $v\kappa$, and put $\kappa_1=\kappa(x_1,\dots,x_{d-1})$. Distinct monomials in the $x_i$ have distinct values modulo $v\kappa$, so a nonzero polynomial has a unique term of least value, and the $x_i$ are algebraically independent. If a quotient $f/g$ of polynomials has value $0$, the least terms of $f$ and $g$ have the same monomial, and the residue of $f/g$ is that of the ratio of their coefficients, which lies in $\kappa v$. So $\kappa_1v=\kappa v$, and both transcendence degrees are $1$ [@openai2026modl, pp. 11–12].

**Proof.**

*Part 1.* Reduction modulo $\ell$ gives continuous maps $r_E:\widehat A_E\to A_E$. They carry $\widehat I_a$ into $I_a$ and $\widehat D_a$ into $D_a$, and commute with restriction to units. By continuity, $r_F(\widehat S^{\mathrm{in}}_F)\subseteq S^{\mathrm{in}}_F$, and $\rho_vr_F=r_{F_0}\widehat\rho_v$ on $\widehat D_v$.

Let $u=q\circ c$ be a c.r. quasi-prime divisor of $F_0/\kappa_0$. By Step 1 of the chains theorem, the relative value group of the constant reduction $c$ is torsion free of rational rank $0$, so $cF_0=c\kappa_0$. As $q$ is trivial on $\kappa_0c$, $u\kappa_0$ maps isomorphically onto $cF_0$, so $uF_0/u\kappa_0\cong qF_0c\cong\mathbb Z$. Characters with either coefficient ring kill the divisible group $u\kappa_0$. So $\widehat I_u\cong\mathbb Z_\ell$ maps onto $I_u\cong\Lambda$. Given $\chi\in I_u$, lift it to $\widehat\chi\in\widehat I_u$. By (P), $\widehat\chi=\widehat\rho_v(\widehat\eta)$ with $\widehat\eta\in\widehat S^{\mathrm{in}}_F\cap\widehat D_v$. Then $r_F(\widehat\eta)\in S^{\mathrm{in}}_F\cap D_v$, and $\rho_v(r_F\widehat\eta)=\chi$. Point valuations are prime divisors, which are c.r. quasi-prime divisors with trivial $c$. This uses only the inclusion between the two closed unions; it does not lift $\Phi$ to $\ell$-adic coefficients [@openai2026modl, Lemma 3.2, p. 12].

*Part 2.* Give the set $\mathcal V(F)$ of valuation rings its patch topology, which is compact [@pop2015minimized, §2, p. 336]. In $A_F\times\mathcal V(F)$, the set of pairs with $\chi(\mathcal O^\times)=0$ is closed. Its complement is the union over $x\in F^\times$ of the open sets where $x,x^{-1}\in\mathcal O$ and $\chi(x)\ne0$. Projection along a compact factor is closed. The image is the set of valuative characters, which contains every $I_w$ and hence $S^{\mathrm{in}}_F$.

Let $\chi\in S^{\mathrm{in}}_F\cap D_v$, so $\chi\in I_u$ for some valuation $u$. If $u$ coarsens $v$, then $\chi\in I_v$ and $\rho_v\chi=0$. If $v$ coarsens $u$, then $U_u$ reduces onto the units of the induced valuation $u/v$ of $F_0$, so $\rho_v\chi\in I_{u/v}$. Otherwise let $a$ be the finest common coarsening. The induced valuations on $Fa$ are nontrivial and independent [@anscombedittmannfehm2020, Remark 2.7, p. 5]. For $y\in U_a$, approximation gives $b\in Fa$ with $(u/a)(b-1)>0$ and $(v/a)(b-\bar y)>(v/a)(\bar y)$ [@anscombedittmannfehm2020, Theorem 1.2, p. 1]. Then $b$ is a $u/a$-unit and $\bar y/b$ is a principal $v/a$-unit. Lifting $b$ to $\beta\in U_a$ gives $\beta\in U_u$ and $y/\beta\in U_v^1$. So $U_a=U_uU^1_v$, the reverse inclusion being clear. Thus $\chi$ kills $U_a$, and $\chi\in I_a\subseteq I_v$, so $\rho_v\chi=0$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 3.3, p. 12].

*Part 3.* For $x\ne y$, approximation gives $f$ with $\mathrm{ord}_x f=1$ and $\mathrm{ord}_yf=0$. So $\delta_x\ne0$ and $\delta_x\notin\Lambda\delta_y$. A function has finitely many zeros and poles. A family $(a_x)$ is in the kernel exactly when the homomorphism $\mathrm{Div}(C)\to\Lambda$ with $x\mapsto a_x$ kills principal divisors, that is, factors through $\mathrm{Pic}(C)$. Multiplication by $\ell$ on the Jacobian is an isogeny, so $\mathrm{Pic}^0(C)=J(b)$ is $\ell$-divisible, also in characteristic $\ell$. So the homomorphism factors through $\deg:\mathrm{Pic}(C)\to\mathbb Z$, and since points have degree $1$, $(a_x)$ is constant. Conversely, constant families kill principal divisors, which have degree $0$. For the last claim, if every order of $f$ is divisible by $\ell$, write $\mathrm{div}f=\ell D$ and send $[f]$ to $[D]\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)[\ell]$. This is well defined on $V_B$ and onto. It is injective because $b^\times$ is $\ell$-divisible. When $\mathrm{char}\,b\ne\ell$, $J[\ell]\cong(\mathbb Z/\ell)^{2g}$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 3.4, p. 13].

*Part 4, finite constants.* Suppose $\kappa_0$ is algebraic over a finite field. Then every valuation of $\kappa_0$ is trivial, as nonzero elements are roots of unity. A nontrivial valuation of $F_0$ trivial on $\kappa_0$ is a point valuation: by properness it has a centre on $C$, a smooth point whose local ring is a discrete valuation ring dominated by, hence equal to, the valuation ring. By part 2, every nonzero element of $J_v$ lies in some $I_x=\Lambda\delta_x$. So every line of $\mathcal L_v$ is a point line, and part 1 gives the converse. By part 3, with $e_H=c_H\delta_x$, condition (i) holds and the kernel is spanned by $(c_H^{-1})_H$, all of whose coordinates are nonzero.

*Part 4, other constants.* Suppose $\kappa_0$ is not algebraic over a finite field. It has a nontrivial valuation $b$: in characteristic $0$ extend a $p$-adic valuation of $\mathbb Q$, and in characteristic $p$ extend the valuation of $\mathbb F_p(\tau)$ at $\tau$, for some transcendental $\tau\in\kappa_0$. Choose $t\in F_0$ with $F_0/\kappa_0(t)$ finite, and prolong the Gauss extension of $b$ on $\kappa_0(t)$ to a valuation $c$ of $F_0$. The Gauss extension has rational residue field of transcendence degree $1$ over $\kappa_0b$ and value group $b\kappa_0$. The prolongation has finite residue extension, so $c$ is a constant reduction. Its value group contains the divisible group $b\kappa_0$ with finite index and is torsion free, so $cF_0=b\kappa_0$, which is divisible. Let $q$ be a point valuation of $F_0c/\kappa_0c$ and $u=q\circ c$. Then $I_u$ is a line by part 1, and it lies in $\mathcal L_v$.

It is not a point line. Let $p$ be a point valuation of $F_0/\kappa_0$. As $u$ is nontrivial on $\kappa_0$ and $p$ is trivial there, $u$ is neither $p$ nor trivial, so $u$ does not coarsen the rank-one $p$. Every nonzero convex subgroup of $uF_0$ contains the rank-one convex subgroup $qF_0c$. So every proper coarsening of $u$ has a value group that is a quotient of $uF_0/qF_0c\cong cF_0$, hence divisible, and $p$ is not one. Thus their finest common coarsening is trivial, and Step 2 of the chains theorem gives $I_p\cap I_u\subseteq D_p\cap D_u=0$.

If (i) holds, then by part 3 the vector with coordinates $c_H^{-1}$ on point lines and $0$ on all other lines, $I_u$ among them, is a nonzero element of the kernel. So the kernel either has dimension at least $2$ or is spanned by a vector with a zero coordinate, and (ii) fails. If (i) fails, the test fails [@openai2026modl, Proposition 3.5, p. 14].

*Part 5.* By part 4 of the chains theorem with $r=1$, $\Phi$ permutes the quasi-prime inertia lines. Being a homeomorphism, it maps the closure of their union onto the corresponding closure. For matched $w\mapsto v$, $\Phi(D_w)=D_v$ and $\varphi\rho_w=\rho_v\Phi$ on $D_w$, so $\varphi(J_w)=J_v$. As $\varphi$ is a linear homeomorphism, it maps $\mathcal L_w$ onto $\mathcal L_v$. Condition (i) says that the generators tend to $0$ along the cofinite filter, which $\varphi$ preserves. A summation is the limit of its finite partial sums, so $\varphi(\sum a_He_H)=\sum a_H\varphi(e_H)$, and the kernels agree. Part 4 then gives the conclusion [@openai2026modl, §3, pp. 11 and 13–14]. $\square$

**Comparison with the $\ell$-adic criterion.** The [$\ell$-adic version](recognition-of-finite-constant-residual-curves.md) applies Pop's Theorem 4.2. Its test asks for diagonal kernel $\mathbb Z_\ell$ and finitely generated cokernel, and its proof refers to Topaz's appendix for the general case [@pop2015minimized, Lemma 4.5, p. 351]. Here only item 2) of Pop's proof enters, through part 1. The test with lines is then proved directly, from part 2 and the Jacobian computation of part 3.

**Scope.** This checks §3 of the [October 2026 mod-$\ell$ manuscript](manuscripts-claiming-bogomolov-pop.md); no gap was found. Pop's density statement and Theorem 1.2, the approximation theorem, the Gauss extension, and $\ell$-divisibility of the Jacobian are cited or standard inputs, not re-proved. The support bounds, curve subfields and true divisors of §§4–6 are not checked here.
