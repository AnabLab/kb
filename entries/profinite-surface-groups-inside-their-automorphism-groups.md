**Question (Tsujimura).** Let $g \ge 2$, and let $\Pi = \widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0}$ be the profinite group on $x_1, y_1, \dots, x_g, y_g$ with the single relation $[x_1, y_1] \cdots [x_g, y_g] = 1$. It is the profinite completion of the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface of genus $g$, and the étale fundamental group of every proper smooth connected curve of genus $g$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ ([geometric fundamental groups of curves](geometric-fundamental-groups-of-curves.md), parts 2 and 4). It is centre-free ([slimness entry](outer-automorphism-groups-of-profinite-surface-groups-are-slim.md), Setting) and topologically finitely generated, so conjugation identifies $\Pi$ with the closed normal subgroup $\mathrm{Inn}(\Pi)$ of the profinite group $\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)$, whose topology is defined by the characteristic open subgroups of $\Pi$ [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Notations and conventions, p. 3].

1. Is $\Pi$ characteristic in $\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)$, that is, preserved by every continuous automorphism of $\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)$?
2. More strongly, is $\Pi$ the only closed normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)$ isomorphic to $\Pi$?

A positive answer to 2 gives one to 1, since a continuous automorphism of $\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)$ carries $\Pi$ to a closed normal subgroup isomorphic to $\Pi$. A positive answer to 1 gives $\mathrm{Out}(\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)) = 1$: the proof of part 2 of [the pro-$p$ theorem](pro-p-surface-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md) uses only that the group is centre-free and characteristic in its automorphism group.

**Attribution.** After proving both forms for free pro-$p$ groups, Tsujimura asks, as his Q.2: "Can we obtain similar results for other types of highly nonabelian profinite groups? [For instance, is a nonabelian Demushkin group or a profinite surface group characteristic in its automorphism group?]" He reports no partial answers [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Introduction, p. 2]. He does not define "profinite surface group". Here it is taken to mean the profinite completion of the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface of genus $g \ge 2$, the case that is not abelian and not free. For a surface with at least one puncture this completion is a free profinite group ([geometric fundamental groups of curves](geometric-fundamental-groups-of-curves.md), consequence 1), which is the subject of his Q.1, and in genus $0$ and $1$ it is abelian.

**Known.**

- *Pro-$p$ analogue.* For the pro-$p$ surface group of genus $g \ge 2$, both forms hold, for every prime $p$ ([pro-$p$ surface groups](pro-p-surface-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md)).
- *Elasticity and slimness.* $\Pi$ is slim and elastic, and every nontrivial closed normal subgroup of $\Pi$ has trivial centralizer. These follow from Minamide, Sawada and Tsujimura's theorem that pro-$\mathcal{C}$ surface groups are strongly sn-internally indecomposable and sn-elastic [@minamidesawadatsujimura2022, Theorem 3.13, p. 21], as explained in the settings of the [slimness entry](outer-automorphism-groups-of-profinite-surface-groups-are-slim.md) and of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-profinite-surface-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md).
- *The outer automorphism group.* For $g \ge 3$, $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi)$ is slim, so it has no nontrivial finite normal subgroup. For $g = 2$, a nontrivial finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi)$ has order $2$, is central, and is generated by the class of a hyperelliptic involution ([slimness entry](outer-automorphism-groups-of-profinite-surface-groups-are-slim.md)).
- *Rival copies.* A closed normal subgroup $N \ne \Pi$ of $\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)$ isomorphic to $\Pi$ neither contains nor lies in $\Pi$. For $g \ge 3$ it meets $\Pi$ in a subgroup of infinite index in both that is not topologically finitely generated. For $g = 2$ it may instead meet $\Pi$ in a subgroup of index $2$ in both, but only if the class of the hyperelliptic involution is central in $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi)$ ([rival copies](rival-copies-of-profinite-surface-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md)). So for $g \ge 3$, form 2 holds exactly when no rival copy of the infinitely generated kind exists.

**Status.** Open for every $g \ge 2$, in either form, as far as the sources opened here show.
