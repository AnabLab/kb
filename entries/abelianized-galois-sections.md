**Setting.** Let $k$ be a perfect field with algebraic closure $\bar k$, let $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/k)$, and let $X$ be a smooth projective geometrically integral variety over $k$, with $\bar X = X \times_k \bar k$. Pushing out the fundamental exact sequence $1 \to \pi_1(\bar X) \to \pi_1(X) \to \Gamma \to 1$ along the abelianization of $\pi_1(\bar X)$ gives the *abelianized sequence*
$$1 \to \pi_1^{\mathrm{ab}}(\bar X) \to \Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}(X) \to \Gamma \to 1,$$
and a section of the first sequence gives one of the second [@harariszamuely2009, (1.1)–(1.2), p. 2]. There is an exact sequence of $\Gamma$-modules $0 \to S \to \pi_1^{\mathrm{ab}}(\bar X) \to T(\mathrm{Alb}_X(\bar k)) \to 0$, where $T$ denotes the full Tate module of the Albanese variety and $S$ is finite, dual to the torsion subgroup of the Néron–Severi group $NS(\bar X)$. Pushing out along the second map gives an extension
$$1 \to T(\mathrm{Alb}_X(\bar k)) \to \Pi \to \Gamma \to 1,$$
which is the abelianized sequence when $NS(\bar X)$ is torsion-free, for example for curves [@harariszamuely2009, (1.3)–(1.4), p. 2]. Let $\mathrm{Alb}^1_X$ be the Albanese torsor, the $k$-torsor under $\mathrm{Alb}_X$ through which every morphism from $X$ to a torsor under an abelian variety factors uniquely [@harariszamuely2009, p. 2]. For a curve, $\mathrm{Alb}_X$ is the Jacobian $\mathrm{Pic}^0_X$ [@harariszamuely2009, proof of Proposition 3.4, p. 9], and $\mathrm{Alb}^1_X$ is $\mathrm{Pic}^1_X$, through the Abel–Jacobi map $X \to \mathrm{Pic}^1_X$. This standard identification is not checked in a source here.

**Theorem** [@harariszamuely2009, Theorem 1.2, pp. 2–3]. The projection $\Pi \to \Gamma$ has a continuous section if and only if the class $[\mathrm{Alb}^1_X]$ lies in the maximal divisible subgroup of $H^1(k, \mathrm{Alb}_X)$.

The maximal divisible subgroup can be smaller than the subgroup of infinitely divisible elements, so the condition is stronger than $[\mathrm{Alb}^1_X]$ being infinitely divisible [@harariszamuely2009, p. 3]. The theorem and its proof carry over to smooth quasi-projective $X$, with the tame fundamental group in place of $\pi_1$ and Serre's generalized Albanese torsor in place of $\mathrm{Alb}^1_X$ [@harariszamuely2009, Remark 2.4, p. 7].

*Proof idea.* The class of the extension in $H^2_{\mathrm{cont}}(\Gamma, T(\mathrm{Alb}_X(\bar k)))$ is the product over all primes $\ell$ of the classes $[\Pi_\ell]$ of its pushouts to the $\ell$-adic Tate modules. Multiplication by $\ell^n$ on $\mathrm{Alb}_X$ gives a coboundary map $\delta_\ell$ from $H^1(k, \mathrm{Alb}_X)$ to Jannsen's continuous cohomology group $H^2_{\mathrm{cont}}(k, ({}_{\ell^n}\mathrm{Alb}_X))$, and $\delta_\ell([\mathrm{Alb}^1_X]) = [\Pi_\ell]$ [@harariszamuely2009, Propositions 2.1 and 2.2, pp. 4–5]. The kernel of $\delta_\ell$ is the maximal $\ell$-divisible subgroup, because the target has no nonzero $\ell$-divisible subgroup, while the kernel is the image of an $\ell$-divisible group [@harariszamuely2009, proof of Proposition 2.1, pp. 6–7].

**Zero-cycles.** The *period* of $X$ is the order of the cokernel of the degree map $CH_0(\bar X)^\Gamma \to \mathbb{Z}$, and the *index* is the order of the cokernel of $CH_0(X) \to \mathbb{Z}$. The period equals the order of $[\mathrm{Alb}^1_X]$, by a theorem of Eriksson and Scharaschkin. So if $\bar X$ has a Galois-invariant zero-cycle class of degree $1$, for instance if $X(k) \ne \emptyset$, then $\Pi \to \Gamma$ splits [@harariszamuely2009, §3, p. 7]. For a curve, period $1$ means that $\mathrm{Pic}^1_X(k) \ne \emptyset$, that is, that $X$ has a $k$-rational divisor class of degree $1$ [@stix2010, (1.4), p. 2]. For curves over a number field with points everywhere locally, and for curves of odd genus over a $p$-adic field, this is equivalent to $X$ having a zero-cycle of degree $1$ [@harariszamuely2009, §1, p. 3].

Harari and Szamuely ask whether the converse holds, that is, whether $[\mathrm{Alb}^1_X]$ can be a nonzero element of the maximal divisible subgroup [@harariszamuely2009, Question 3.1, p. 7]. Over $p$-adic fields it can, even for curves. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ it can too, for Selmer's genus one curve, which has points everywhere locally (see below). For some Jacobians over $\mathbb{Q}$ it cannot, and this gives the examples below.

**$p$-adic fields.** Let $k$ be finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. For an abelian variety $A$ over $k$, Tate duality and Mattuck's theorem give $H^1(k, A) \cong F \oplus (\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)^r$ with $F$ finite [@harariszamuely2009, Fact 3.2, p. 7]. So a divisible class lies in the second summand and has order a power of $p$ [@harariszamuely2009, proof of Proposition 3.3, p. 8]. In particular, a section of the abelianized sequence of a curve over $k$ forces its period to be a power of $p$, which is the conclusion about the period in [Stix's theorem](section-conjecture-period-index.md) for the full fundamental group.

- *Detection* [@harariszamuely2009, Proposition 3.3, p. 8]. Let $X$ be a smooth proper geometrically connected curve of genus $g \ge 1$ over $k$, with $p \nmid g - 1$, which excludes $g = 1$. Then $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}(X) \to \Gamma$ has a section if and only if $X$ has period $1$. If $g$ is odd, this holds if and only if $X$ has a zero-cycle of degree $1$. The proof uses Lichtenbaum's theorem that the period divides $g - 1$.
- *Failure of detection* [@harariszamuely2009, Proposition 3.4, p. 8]. For every odd prime $p$ there is a curve $Y$ of genus $p + 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ such that $[\mathrm{Alb}^1_Y]$ is a nonzero divisible class. So $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}(Y) \to \Gamma$ has a section, but $Y$ does not have period $1$. In the construction, $X$ is a torsor of order $p$ under the Tate curve with parameter $p$, with divisible class, split by a totally ramified cyclic extension of degree $p$. Then $Y$ is a double cover of $X$ ramified at $2p$ points, chosen after Sharif so that $[\mathrm{Alb}^1_Y]$ has order $p$. Pullback sends $[X]$ to $2[\mathrm{Alb}^1_Y]$ [@harariszamuely2009, proof of Proposition 3.4, pp. 9–10]. For torsors under abelian varieties $A$ with $A(k)$ infinite, such examples are immediate [@harariszamuely2009, p. 8].

*Two consequences for $Y$, checked here.* The period of $Y$ is the order $p$ of $[\mathrm{Alb}^1_Y]$. Since the period divides the index and equals $1$ when there is a rational point [@stix2010, (1.4), p. 2], $Y$ has no $\mathbb{Q}_p$-point and no divisor of degree $1$.

1. *Abelian birational sections are finer.* The surjection $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p(Y)} \to \pi_1(Y)$ maps the geometric subgroup $G_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p(Y)}$ onto $\pi_1(\bar Y)$, a standard fact, so it induces a map over $\Gamma$ from the [abelian birational extension](abelian-birational-sections.md) onto $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}(Y)$, and a section of the first gives one of the second. Over $p$-adic fields the abelian birational extension splits exactly when there is a divisor of degree $1$ [@esnaultwittenberg2010, Remarks 2.3 (ii), p. 5]. So for $Y$ it does not split, although $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}(Y) \to \Gamma$ does.
2. *The section conjecture for $Y$.* If $\pi_1(Y) \to \Gamma$ had a section, Stix's theorem would force the index of $Y$ to equal its period $p$ [@stix2010, Theorem 2 (1), p. 2]. The sources opened here do not compute the index of $Y$, so they do not decide whether $\pi_1(Y) \to \Gamma$ splits. The $p$-adic form of the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) predicts that it does not, since $Y$ has genus at least $4$ and no rational point. The abelianized quotient gives no obstruction here.

**Number fields.** Let $k$ be a number field. If $X$ has points over every completion of $k$, then $[\mathrm{Alb}^1_X]$ lies in the Tate–Shafarevich group of $\mathrm{Alb}_X$. Harari and Szamuely write that, to their knowledge, it is not known whether a nonzero element of the Tate–Shafarevich group of an abelian variety $A$ can be divisible in $H^1(k, A)$, a question studied by Bashmakov [@harariszamuely2009, §4, p. 10]. Çiperiani and Stix later showed that it can. For the Jacobian $E$ of Selmer's curve $3X^3 + 4Y^3 + 5Z^3 = 0$, the intersection of $Ш(E/\mathbb{Q})$ with the maximal divisible subgroup of $H^1(\mathbb{Q}, E)$ is generated by the class of that curve, so the curve has a section but no rational point [@ciperianistix2013, Theorem B, p. 3, and Proposition 23, p. 20]. See [genus-one curves](galois-sections-of-genus-one-curves.md) for this and for their results on elliptic curves of rank $0$ and $1$.

- *Trivial divisible subgroup* [@harariszamuely2009, Proposition 4.4, p. 12]. Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $\mathbb{Q}$, isogenous over $\mathbb{Q}$ to a product of elliptic curves each with finite Tate–Shafarevich group and infinitely many rational points. Then the maximal divisible subgroup of $H^1(\mathbb{Q}, A)$ is trivial. Consequently, if $X$ is a smooth projective curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ with such a Jacobian and no $\mathbb{Q}$-rational divisor class of degree $1$, then $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}(X) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has no section.
- *Key step.* For an abelian variety $A$ over a number field with finite Tate–Shafarevich group, finiteness of the $\ell$-primary part of $H^1(U, \mathcal{A}^*)$, over an open $U \subseteq \mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}_k$ where $A$ and its dual have abelian scheme models and $\ell$ is invertible, is equivalent to the closure of $A(k)$ in $\prod_{v \mid \ell} A(k_v)$ having finite index [@harariszamuely2009, Proposition 4.1, pp. 10–11]. For an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ with infinitely many rational points, the closure has finite index because $E(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ has a finite-index subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ [@harariszamuely2009, Corollary 4.2 and its proof, p. 12].
- *Local but not global sections* [@harariszamuely2009, Proposition 1.3, p. 3]. Let $X$ be a smooth projective curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ whose Jacobian is as above, with points everywhere locally but no $\mathbb{Q}$-rational divisor class of degree $1$. Then $\pi_1(X) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ has sections over every completion of $\mathbb{Q}$, coming from the local points, but no section over $\mathbb{Q}$.
- *Flynn's examples* [@harariszamuely2009, Example 6.3 and its proof, pp. 19–20]. The genus $2$ curve $Y^2 = 2(X^2 + 7)(X^2 + 14)(X^2 - 11)$ satisfies these hypotheses. It has points everywhere locally and no $\mathbb{Q}$-rational divisor class of degree $1$, so no rational point. Its Jacobian is isogenous over $\mathbb{Q}$ to the product of the elliptic curves $Y^2 = 2(X + 7)(X + 14)(X - 11)$ and $Y^2 = 2(1 + 7X)(1 + 14X)(1 - 11X)$. Flynn reports that each has analytic rank $1$, which can be checked with Magma, so each has rank $1$ and finite Tate–Shafarevich group by Kolyvagin's theorem together with modularity [@harariszamuely2009, Theorem 6.1, p. 18]. The curves $Y^2 = 2(X^2 + p)(X^2 + 2p)(X^2 + a)$ with $(p, a)$ one of $(7, -19)$, $(7, -11)$, $(23, 13)$, $(31, -14)$, $(31, -11)$, $(31, 5)$, $(31, 13)$, $(47, 13)$ satisfy the hypotheses for the same reasons [@harariszamuely2009, §6, pp. 20–21].
- *Stoll's example* [@harariszamuely2009, Remark 4.5, pp. 12–13]. The curve $y^2 = 3x^6 + 8x^4 + 2x^2 - 6$ has Jacobian isogenous over $\mathbb{Q}$ to the product of the rank $1$ elliptic curves $y^2 = x^3 - x^2 - 15x - 27$ and $y^2 = x^3 - x^2 - 49x + 157$, which is what Proposition 4.4 needs, and has a zero-cycle of degree $1$ everywhere locally but not globally. So $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ has sections everywhere locally but not globally. This curve has no $\mathbb{Q}_2$-point, so, unlike Flynn's curves, it has no local point to supply a section of the full fundamental group over $\mathbb{Q}_2$.

*Significance for the section conjecture.* Flynn's curves have genus $2$, no rational points and no section over $\mathbb{Q}$, as the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) predicts. Since they have points over every completion, every local fundamental group sequence splits, and no local obstruction explains the absence of a global section. Harari and Szamuely note that earlier examples where the section conjecture holds in this nonobvious way, due to Stix, had no points over some nonarchimedean completion, and that their curves answer the question of whether there are curves of genus at least $2$ with points everywhere locally and no section [@harariszamuely2009, §1, p. 2]. Proposition 4.4 needs Jacobians whose elliptic factors have infinitely many rational points. Compare the genus $1$ Reichardt–Lind curve in [descent obstructions for Galois sections](descent-obstructions-for-galois-sections.md), whose lack of sections is proved by a Brauer–Manin argument instead.

**Elementary obstruction.** Assume that $k$ has characteristic $0$. The elementary obstruction of Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc vanishes if the exact sequence $1 \to \bar k^\times \to \bar k(X)^\times \to \bar k(X)^\times/\bar k^\times \to 1$ has a Galois-equivariant section. If it vanishes, then $\Pi \to \Gamma$ splits [@harariszamuely2009, Corollary 5.5, p. 18], and so does $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}(X) \to \Gamma$ [@harariszamuely2009, Remark 5.6, p. 18]. The converse fails, since there are geometrically rational varieties, whose geometric fundamental group is trivial, with nontrivial elementary obstruction [@harariszamuely2009, p. 18]. Harari and Szamuely say "rational" there in the geometric sense: over a field of characteristic $0$, which is infinite, a $k$-rational variety has a $k$-point on the open set isomorphic to an open subset of affine space, and the elementary obstruction vanishes whenever there is a zero-cycle of degree $1$ [@esnaultwittenberg2010, §3, p. 6]. Harari and Szamuely report that Esnault and Wittenberg obtained a similar result independently [@harariszamuely2009, Remark 5.6, p. 18].
