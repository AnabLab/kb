**Setting.** Let $f \ge 1$, $q = 2^f$, and for $f \mid k$ let $U_k$ be the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of degree $k$ in a fixed $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2$. Let $T$ be the maximal tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$, $P = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2/T)$, $\Gamma_f = \mathrm{Gal}(T/U_f)$ and $I = \mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}})$, as in [the Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md). Let $\Phi(P)$ be the Frattini subgroup of the pro-$2$ group $P$, the intersection of the kernels of all continuous homomorphisms $P \to \mathbb{Z}/2$. Its fixed field is $E = T(\sqrt{x} : x \in T^\times)$, so $M = P/\Phi(P) = \mathrm{Gal}(E/T)$, and put $Q = \mathrm{Gal}(E/U_f) = G_{U_f}/\Phi(P)$, with restriction $\pi_Q : Q \to \Gamma_f$.

**Theorem.** Every automorphism $\beta$ of $\Gamma_f$ with $\beta(I) = I$ lifts to $Q$: there is an automorphism $\tilde\beta$ of $Q$ with $\pi_Q \circ \tilde\beta = \beta \circ \pi_Q$. This includes the Frobenius shift $\alpha$ of the problem entry, for every $f$, since $\alpha$ fixes $I$ pointwise.

**Corollary.** Let $K, L \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2$ be finite tamely ramified extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ with the same ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$. Then $\mathrm{Gal}(E/K) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(E/L)$. Here $\mathrm{Gal}(E/K) = G_K/\Phi(P)$, and $P$ is the largest closed normal pro-$2$ subgroup of $G_K$ (lemma in the problem entry), so this quotient is determined by the group $G_K$. For example, the non-isomorphic degree-$6$ fields $U_2(\sqrt[3]{2})$ and $U_2(\sqrt[3]{2\omega})$ of [tamely ramified 2-adic fields](tamely-ramified-2-adic-fields.md) have isomorphic quotients of this kind, although no automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ carries one absolute Galois group onto the other (problem entry, second check).

The theorem, the corollary and their proofs are worked out here. The sources opened for this entry do not state them. A stronger result, which lifts every automorphism of $\Gamma_f$ to $G_{U_f}/[P,P]$ without the explicit computation below, is proved in [automorphisms of the tame Galois group lift modulo wild commutators](tame-automorphisms-lift-modulo-wild-commutators.md). Its step 1 shows that the condition $\beta(I) = I$ always holds.

**Proof.**

*Step 0: generators.* For odd $b$ choose roots $\varpi_b$ of $x^b - 2$ with $\varpi_{bc}^c = \varpi_b$, choosing them along a cofinal chain of odd numbers ordered by divisibility. Put $F_{k,b} = U_k(\varpi_b)$. Since $x^b - 2$ is Eisenstein, $F_{k,b}/U_k$ is totally ramified of degree $b$, with uniformizer $\varpi_b$ and residue field $\mathbb{F}_{2^k}$, and $F_{k,b}$ is tamely ramified over $\mathbb{Q}_2$.

(a) *$T$ is the union of the $F_{k,b}$.* One inclusion is clear. A finite tamely ramified $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ has odd ramification index $e$, so by part 2 of [tamely ramified 2-adic fields](tamely-ramified-2-adic-fields.md) it is $U_{f_K}(\varpi)$ with $\varpi^e = 2\zeta$ and $\zeta$ a root of unity of odd order. Choose $\eta$ with $\eta^e = \zeta$, again of odd order. Then $(\varpi/\eta)^e = \varpi_e^e$, so $\varpi = \eta\xi\varpi_e$ with $\xi^e = 1$. Roots of unity of odd order lie in $\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$, so $K \subseteq F_{k,e}$ for suitable $k$.

(b) *Inertia.* By (a), $T = \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}(\varpi_b : b \text{ odd})$. For $g \in I$ put $\zeta_b(g) = g(\varpi_b)/\varpi_b \in \mu_b$. As $\mu_b \subset \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$, the map $g \mapsto (\zeta_b(g))_b$ is an injective continuous homomorphism $I \to \varprojlim_b \mu_b \cong \prod_{\ell \ne 2}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. It is onto: $x^b - 2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$ by Eisenstein's criterion, so $I$ permutes its roots $\zeta\varpi_b$ transitively and the image maps onto each $\mu_b$, and the image is closed. So $I$ is procyclic and pro-odd. Fix a generator $\tau$, so every element of $I$ is $\tau^x$ with $x \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne 2}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and write $\zeta_b = \zeta_b(\tau)$.

(c) *Frobenius.* Let $L = \bigcup_b U_f(\varpi_b)$. Each $U_f(\varpi_b)/U_f$ is totally ramified, so $L \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}} = U_f$, and $L\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}} = T$ by (a). Hence restriction $\mathrm{Gal}(T/L) \to \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}/U_f)$ is an isomorphism, and there is $\sigma_f \in \Gamma_f$ that fixes every $\varpi_b$ and acts on $\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$ as the $q$-power Frobenius. It acts on roots of unity of odd order by $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^q$, so $\sigma_f g \sigma_f^{-1}(\varpi_b) = \zeta_b(g)^q\varpi_b$ for $g \in I$, that is, $\sigma_f g \sigma_f^{-1} = g^q$. If $\sigma_f^y \in I$ with $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, then the $fy$-th power of Frobenius is trivial on $\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$, so $y = 0$. Thus $\langle\sigma_f\rangle \cap I = 1$, so $\langle\sigma_f\rangle$ maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma_f/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. Also $\Gamma_f = I\langle\sigma_f\rangle$, and every element of $\Gamma_f$ is $\sigma_f^y\tau^x$.

(d) *The automorphisms.* Let $\beta(I) = I$. Then $\beta(\tau)$ generates $I$, so $\beta(\tau) = \tau^u$ with $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$. Write $\beta(\sigma_f) = \sigma_f^v\tau^h$. As $\beta$ induces an automorphism of $\Gamma_f/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, $v \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$. Applying $\beta$ to $\sigma_f\tau\sigma_f^{-1} = \tau^q$ gives $\tau^{uq^v} = \tau^{uq}$, so $q^{v-1} = 1$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. Reducing modulo $q^m - 1$, where $q$ has order $m$, gives $v - 1 \in m\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ for every $m \ge 1$, so $v = 1$. Hence
$$\beta(\tau) = \tau^u, \qquad \beta(\sigma_f) = \sigma_f\tau^h \qquad (u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times,\ h \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}').$$

*Step 1: Shafarevich exponentials.* Let $\widehat{\mathcal{O}}$ be the ring of integers of the completion of $\mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and $\varphi$ its Frobenius. For $w \in \widehat{\mathcal{O}}$ put
$$E(w, X) = \exp\Big(\sum_{n \ge 0} \varphi^n(w)\frac{X^{2^n}}{2^n}\Big).$$
This is the Artin–Hasse–Shafarevich map $E_X$ of Fesenko–Vostokov applied to $wX$. They prove that $E_X$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module isomorphism from $X\widehat{\mathcal{O}}[[X]]$ onto $1 + X\widehat{\mathcal{O}}[[X]]$ [@fesenkovostokov2002, Ch. VI, (2.1) and Proposition (2.2), pp. 214–215]. So $E(w, X) \in 1 + X\widehat{\mathcal{O}}[[X]]$ and $E(w + w', X) = E(w, X)E(w', X)$. If $w$ lies in the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_{U_k}$, the coefficients are rational polynomials in the $\varphi^n(w)$, so they lie in $\widehat{\mathcal{O}} \cap U_k = \mathcal{O}_{U_k}$. Thus for $x$ in the maximal ideal of a finite extension $F$ of $U_k$, the value $e(w, x) = E(w, x)$ converges in $F$, and:

- (E1) $e(w, x) \in 1 + wx + x^2\mathcal{O}_F$, since $E(w, X) = 1 + wX + \dots$.
- (E2) $e(w + w', x) = e(w, x)e(w', x)$.
- (E3) $e(w, \zeta x) = e(\zeta w, x)$ for $\zeta$ a root of unity of odd order, since $\varphi(\zeta) = \zeta^2$ gives $\varphi^n(w)\zeta^{2^n} = \varphi^n(\zeta w)$.
- (E4) $g(e(w, x)) = e(g(w), g(x))$ for every automorphism $g$ of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2$ over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, and so for elements of $\Gamma_f$ when $x \in T$. Indeed $g$ is continuous and acts on $U_k$ as a power of $\varphi$, which commutes with $\varphi$.

For $c$ in $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_2$ write $[c]$ for its Teichmüller lift, the root of unity of odd order with residue $c$, or $0$ if $c = 0$.

*Step 2: square classes of $T$.* Let $A = T^\times/T^{\times 2}$, a discrete $\Gamma_f$-module. Let $V$ be the set of rationals $v = a/b$ in lowest terms with $a, b$ odd and $0 < v < 2$. For $v = a/b \in V$ and $c \in \bar{\mathbb{F}}_2$, let $\Theta_v(c) \in A$ be the class of $e([c], \varpi_b^a)$. Since $[c] + [c'] - [c + c'] \in 2\mathcal{O}_{U_k}$ for $c, c' \in \mathbb{F}_{2^k}$, (E2) shows that $\Theta_v : \bar{\mathbb{F}}_2 \to A$ is additive.

*Claim.* The map $\mathbb{F}_2 \oplus \bigoplus_{v \in V}\bar{\mathbb{F}}_2 \to A$, $(\varepsilon, (c_v)) \mapsto \varepsilon[2] + \sum_v \Theta_v(c_v)$, is an isomorphism.

Fix $F = F_{k,e}$ with $e$ odd, its valuation $v_F$ with $v_F(\varpi_e) = 1$, so $v_F(2) = e$, and $U^{(i)} = 1 + \varpi_e^i\mathcal{O}_F$. The map $v \mapsto ev$ is a bijection from the $v \in V$ whose denominator divides $e$ onto the odd integers $0 < i < 2e$, and $\varpi_b^a = \varpi_e^{ev}$ for such $v = a/b$. We use one lemma.

*Lemma.* If $y \in F^\times$ and $1 \ne y^2 \in U^{(1)}$, then $v_F(y^2 - 1)$ is even or at least $2e$. *Proof.* Write $y = \eta y_1$ with $\eta$ a root of unity of odd order and $y_1 \in U^{(1)}$. Then $\eta^2 \in U^{(1)}$, so $\eta = 1$. Put $y_1 = 1 + z$ with $j = v_F(z) \ge 1$. Then $y^2 - 1 = z(z + 2)$ has valuation $2j$ if $j < e$, $j + e > 2e$ if $j > e$, and at least $2e$ if $j = e$. $\square$

*Surjectivity.* Every $y \in T^\times$ lies in some $F = F_{k,e}$ and is $\varpi_e^m\eta u$ with $\eta$ of odd order, hence a square, and $u \in U^{(1)}$. If $m$ is odd, then $\varpi_e^m = 2\varpi_e^{m-e}$ with $m - e$ even. So $y \equiv 2^\varepsilon u$. Now reduce $u$ level by level.

- If $u \in U^{(i)}$ with $i < 2e$ odd and $u \equiv 1 + d\varpi_e^i$, then $u\,e([\bar d], \varpi_e^i)^{-1} \in U^{(i+1)}$ by (E1), and $e([\bar d], \varpi_e^i)$ represents $\Theta_{i/e}(\bar d)$.
- If $u \in U^{(2j)}$ with $j < e$ and $u \equiv 1 + d\varpi_e^{2j}$, choose $c'$ with $c'^2 \equiv d$ modulo $\varpi_e$. Since $v_F(2c'\varpi_e^j) \ge e + j > 2j$, we get $u(1 + c'\varpi_e^j)^{-2} \in U^{(2j+1)}$.
- If $u \in U^{(2e)}$, then $u = 1 + 4d$ as $\varpi_e^{2e} = 4$. Let $F' = F_{2k,e}$. The residue $\bar d$ has trace $0$ to $\mathbb{F}_2$ from $\mathbb{F}_{2^{2k}}$. The map $x \mapsto x^2 + x$ of $\mathbb{F}_{2^{2k}}$ is $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear with kernel $\mathbb{F}_2$, and its image lies in the kernel of the trace, which also has index $2$, so $\bar d = \bar c^2 + \bar c$. Lifting $\bar c$ to $c \in \mathcal{O}_{F'}$, $(1 + 2c)^2 = 1 + 4(c^2 + c)$ gives $u(1 + 2c)^{-2} \in 1 + 4\varpi_e\mathcal{O}_{F'}$.
- If $v_F(z) > 2e$, that is, $v_2(z) > 2$ in the valuation with $v_2(2) = 1$, then $1 + z$ is a square, because $\binom{1/2}{n} = (-1)^{n-1}\binom{2n}{n}/((2n-1)4^n)$ has $v_2 \ge -2n$, so the binomial series for $(1 + z)^{1/2}$ converges.

So $u \equiv \prod_{i \text{ odd} < 2e} e([d_i], \varpi_e^i)$ modulo $T^{\times 2}$, and the map is onto.

*Injectivity.* Suppose $\varepsilon[2] + \sum_v \Theta_v(c_v) = 0$ with finitely many $c_v \ne 0$. Choose $F = F_{k,e}$ containing the relevant $\varpi_b$, the Teichmüller lifts $[c_v]$, and a square root $y$ of $2^\varepsilon\prod_v e([c_v], \varpi_b^a)$. Then $v_F(y^2) = \varepsilon e$ is even with $e$ odd, so $\varepsilon = 0$. If some $c_v \ne 0$, let $i_0 = ev$ be the least level with $c_v \ne 0$. By (E1) the product is $1 + [c_v]\varpi_e^{i_0}$ modulo $\varpi_e^{i_0+1}$, with $[c_v]$ a unit, so $v_F(y^2 - 1) = i_0$, which is odd and less than $2e$. This contradicts the lemma, so every $c_v = 0$. $\square$

*Step 3: the action.* The class $[2]$ is fixed by $\Gamma_f$. Put $N_v = \Theta_v(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_2)$. For $v = a/b$, (E4), (E3) and (b), (c) give
$$\sigma_f\Theta_v(c) = \Theta_v(c^q), \qquad \tau\Theta_v(c) = \Theta_v(\bar\zeta_b^{\,a}c),$$
where $\bar\zeta_b$ is the residue of $\zeta_b$: indeed $\sigma_f[c] = [c^q]$ and $\sigma_f(\varpi_b) = \varpi_b$, while $\tau[c] = [c]$ and $\tau(\varpi_b^a) = \zeta_b^a\varpi_b^a$. So
$$A = \mathbb{F}_2[2] \oplus \bigoplus_{v \in V} N_v$$
as $\Gamma_f$-modules, and $N_v \cong \bar{\mathbb{F}}_2$ with $\sigma_f$ acting by $c \mapsto c^q$ and $\tau$ by multiplication by $\bar\zeta_b^{\,a}$.

*Step 4: a $\beta$-semilinear automorphism of $A$.* Let $\beta$ be as in (d). For $v = a/b \in V$, let $v' = a'/b$ be the element of $V$ with $a' \equiv au^{-1}$ modulo $b$. It exists and is unique, since each residue class modulo $b$ contains exactly one odd integer in $(0, 2b)$. So $v \mapsto v'$ is a bijection of $V$. As $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_2$ is algebraically closed, choose $\lambda_v \in \bar{\mathbb{F}}_2^\times$ with
$$\lambda_v^{\,q-1} = \bar\zeta_b^{\,-a'hq}.$$
Define $\psi : A \to A$ by $\psi([2]) = [2]$ and $\psi(\Theta_v(c)) = \Theta_{v'}(\lambda_v c)$. It is an additive bijection. For $x = \Theta_v(c)$,
$$\psi(\tau x) = \Theta_{v'}(\lambda_v\bar\zeta_b^{\,a}c) = \Theta_{v'}(\bar\zeta_b^{\,a'u}\lambda_v c) = \tau^u\psi(x),$$
$$\beta(\sigma_f)\psi(x) = \sigma_f\tau^h\Theta_{v'}(\lambda_v c) = \Theta_{v'}(\bar\zeta_b^{\,a'hq}\lambda_v^{\,q}c^q) = \Theta_{v'}(\lambda_v c^q) = \psi(\sigma_f x).$$
So $\psi \circ \gamma = \beta(\gamma) \circ \psi$ on $A$ for $\gamma = \tau, \sigma_f$. The set of $\gamma \in \Gamma_f$ with this property is a subgroup, and it is closed because $A$ is discrete. So it is all of $\Gamma_f$.

*Step 5: duality, splitting and the lift.* By Kummer theory, $\langle g, x\rangle = g(\sqrt{x})/\sqrt{x}$ identifies $A$ with $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(P, \mu_2) = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(M, \mu_2)$, and $\langle hgh^{-1}, h(x)\rangle = \langle g, x\rangle$ for $h \in G_{U_f}$. By Pontryagin duality, $M = \mathrm{Hom}(A, \mathbb{F}_2)$, the inverse limit of $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{F}_2)$ over the finite $\Gamma_f$-stable subgroups $B \subseteq A$. These exhaust $A$, because each class has an open stabilizer and $A$ has exponent $2$. Conjugation in $Q$ makes $M$ a $\Gamma_f$-module dual to $A$.

*Splitting.* Let $t \in Q$ lift $\tau$, and put $t' = t^{\epsilon}$ with $\epsilon \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ equal to $0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2$ and $1$ in each $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, $\ell \ne 2$. Then $\langle t'\rangle$ is pro-odd, so $\langle t'\rangle \cap M = 1$ and $\pi_Q$ maps $\langle t'\rangle$ isomorphically onto $I$, as $I$ is pro-odd. Let $s \in Q$ lift $\sigma_f$, so that $st's^{-1} = mt'^q$ with $m \in M$. On each $\mathrm{Hom}(B, \mathbb{F}_2)$ the element $t'$ acts through a cyclic group of odd order $n$, also generated by $t'^q$ since $q$ is prime to $n$. The idempotent $\sum_{j<n} t'^j$ gives a decomposition into the invariants and the image of $t'^q - 1$, on which $t'^q - 1$ is bijective. These decompositions are compatible with restriction, so $M = M^{I} \oplus (t'^q - 1)M$. Write $m = m_0 + (t'^q - 1)n_1$ additively. Replacing $s$ by $n_1s$ turns $m$ into $m - (t'^q - 1)n_1 = m_0$, so $st's^{-1} = m_0t'^q$, where $m_0$ commutes with $t'$. Let $\epsilon' = 1 - \epsilon$. Then $(m_0t'^q)^{\epsilon'} = m_0$. But $st's^{-1}$ lies in the pro-odd group $s\langle t'\rangle s^{-1}$, so its $\epsilon'$-th power is $1$, and $m_0 = 1$. Thus $st's^{-1} = t'^q$. So $s$ normalizes $\langle t'\rangle$, and $C = \langle t'\rangle\langle s\rangle$ is a closed subgroup with $\pi_Q(C) = \Gamma_f$. If $\pi_Q(t'^xs^y) = 1$, then $\sigma_f^y \in I$, so $y = 0$ by (c), and then $t'^x = 1$. So $\pi_Q|_C$ is an isomorphism and $Q = M \rtimes C$.

*The lift.* Let $\beta_C = (\pi_Q|_C)^{-1} \circ \beta \circ \pi_Q|_C$ and define $\Psi \in \mathrm{Aut}(M)$ by $\langle\Psi(m), \psi(x)\rangle = \langle m, x\rangle$. For $c \in C$ with image $\gamma$, Step 4 gives $\psi(\gamma^{-1}x) = \beta(\gamma)^{-1}\psi(x)$, hence
$$\langle\Psi(cmc^{-1}), \psi(x)\rangle = \langle m, \gamma^{-1}x\rangle = \langle\Psi(m), \beta(\gamma)^{-1}\psi(x)\rangle = \langle\beta_C(c)\Psi(m)\beta_C(c)^{-1}, \psi(x)\rangle.$$
So $\Psi(cmc^{-1}) = \beta_C(c)\Psi(m)\beta_C(c)^{-1}$, and $\tilde\beta(mc) = \Psi(m)\beta_C(c)$ is a continuous automorphism of $M \rtimes C$ with $\pi_Q \circ \tilde\beta = \beta \circ \pi_Q$. $\square$

*Proof of the corollary.* Here $K, L \supseteq U_f$ and $K, L \subseteq T$. With the pair $\sigma_f, \tau$ fixed above, part 1 of the reduction in the problem entry gives $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K) = H_c$ and $\mathrm{Gal}(T/L) = H_{c'}$, where $H_c$ is the closed subgroup generated by $\sigma_f\tau^c$ and $\tau^e$. By (c), $\Gamma_f = I \rtimes \langle\sigma_f\rangle$ with $\langle\sigma_f\rangle \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, and conjugation by $\sigma_f\tau^a$ acts on the abelian group $I$ as conjugation by $\sigma_f$ does. So $\alpha_a(\tau^x\sigma_f^y) = \tau^x(\sigma_f\tau^a)^y$ defines an endomorphism of $\Gamma_f$, with inverse $\alpha_{-a}$. Now $\alpha_{c'-c}$ fixes $I$ and maps $H_c$ onto $H_{c'}$, so a lift of it to $Q$ maps $\pi_Q^{-1}(H_c) = \mathrm{Gal}(E/K)$ onto $\pi_Q^{-1}(H_{c'}) = \mathrm{Gal}(E/L)$. $\square$

**What this does not settle.** The [problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md) asks for a lift to $G_{U_f}$, which also needs the layers below $\Phi(P)$. A lift to $G_{U_f}$ would, for every open subgroup, respect the cup product on mod-$2$ cohomology. Over a field $F$ this is the Hilbert symbol on $F^\times/F^{\times 2}$, which is not alternating when $\sqrt{-1} \notin F$. The construction above uses only the $\Gamma_f$-module $T^\times/T^{\times 2}$ and does not address these pairings. It is also far from unique: each $\lambda_v$ can be multiplied by any element of $\mathbb{F}_q^\times$. The argument uses that the Kummer pairing takes values in $\mu_2$, on which $\Gamma_f$ acts trivially. For odd $p$ the analogous pairing over the maximal tame extension involves the action of the tame Galois group on $\mu_p$.
