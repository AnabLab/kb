**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the higher cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-higher-cocycle-classes-of-wild-inertia.md), which is that of [the higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md) with its lower central input. So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$, $P_j$ is the closed lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$ with $\xi_i\in H$ the class of $x_i$, $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$, and $V=H/pH=L\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d$ with $L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$, $\rho\bar x_0=\omega(\rho)\bar x_0$ and $V_k=\Lambda\bar x_k\cong\Lambda$. Each $\mathcal L_j$ is torsion-free, and $\mathcal L_j/p$ is the completed free Lie space $\mathcal L_j(V)\subseteq V^{\hat\otimes j}$, with compatible brackets. The element $\gamma\in\mathcal G$ has $\omega(\gamma)=1$, $e=[K(\mu_p):K]$ is the order of $\omega$, and $\omega$ also denotes the continuous ring homomorphism $\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ extending it. The group $\mathcal G=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\subseteq G$ maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma$, and $I\subseteq\mathcal G$ is the closed subgroup generated by $\tau$, which is tame inertia.

*The relator.* As in the Setting of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), $G=F/(\varrho)$ with $F=P_F\rtimes\mathcal G$, $P$ the image of $P_F$, and
$$\varrho=R_0\,x_1^{p^s}\,C,\qquad R_0=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa,$$
where $p^s$ is the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, $C$ is a product of commutators of elements of $P_F$, and $(x_0,\tau)$, $\kappa$, $h$ are as in the Setting of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), which writes $P$ for $P_F$ and $P_K$ for $P$. From there we use $\bar h=\omega(\tau)$, $\bar g=\omega(\sigma)$, the central idempotent $e_h\in\Lambda$ with $\tau e_h=\bar he_h$ and $\omega(e_h)=1$, and $u=\bar ge_h-\sigma\in\ker\omega$ (its Lemma (a) and (b)).

*Twisted coinvariants.* For a compact $\Lambda$-module $W$ let $W\langle\omega\rangle$ be the quotient of $W$ by the closure of the span of the $(\rho-\omega(\rho))w$, $\rho\in\Gamma$, $w\in W$, and $\theta_W:W\to W\langle\omega\rangle$ the quotient map. Then $\theta_W(\rho w)=\omega(\rho)\theta_W(w)$, so $\theta_W(\lambda w)=\omega(\lambda)\theta_W(w)$ for $\lambda\in\Lambda$, both sides being continuous and linear in $\lambda$. In particular $\theta_W(uw)=0$. A $\Gamma$-equivariant isomorphism $W\to W'$ for the twisted action $\rho\star w'=\omega(\rho)^{-1}\rho w'$ on $W'$ induces an isomorphism $W_\Gamma\to W'\langle\omega\rangle$, since $W'\langle\omega\rangle$ is the module of coinvariants of $W'$ for $\star$.

*Words.* Words $w$, the summands $V_w$ of $V^{\hat\otimes m}$ and the word $(1@1)$, with the letter $1$ in slot $1$ and $0$ elsewhere, are as in the higher cocycle theorem. For $Y\in V^{\hat\otimes m}$ let $Y_w$ be its component in $V_w$, so $(V^{\hat\otimes m})_\Gamma=\bigoplus_w(V_w)_\Gamma$ and $V^{\hat\otimes m}\langle\omega\rangle=\bigoplus_wV_w\langle\omega\rangle$.

*Cocycles.* Let $n\ge2$ and let $\mathcal A'_n$ be the set of all $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ inducing the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$, with no condition on outer conjugates. For such $\alpha$ the formula $c_\alpha(\rho)=\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}P_{n+1}$, $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$ over $\rho$, defines a continuous cocycle $\Gamma\to\mathcal L_n$, by the argument in the Setting of the higher cocycle theorem, which does not use finite outer conjugacy; put $u(\alpha)=[c_\alpha]\in H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$. The formula (u1) there, $c_{c_g\alpha}(\rho)=c_\alpha(\rho)+(1-\rho)(gP_{n+1})$ for $g\in P_n$, has the same proof for $\alpha\in\mathcal A'_n$, and $c_g\alpha\in\mathcal A'_n$. Call a continuous cocycle $c:\Gamma\to\mathcal L_n$ *normalized* if $c$ vanishes on $I$ and $c(\sigma)\in\mathcal L_n^I$. By steps (i) to (iii) of the proof of Lemma 2 of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), which apply to every compact abelian pro-$p$ group with a continuous $\Gamma$-action (Lemma 1 (a) of [the theorem modulo the third lower central term](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-the-third-lower-central-term-of-wild-inertia.md)), every continuous cocycle differs from a normalized one by a coboundary.

**Lemma 1 (the relator in $H$; proved here).**

- (a) There is $\varepsilon'$ in the closure $\mathbb Z_p[[I]]$ of the span of $I$ in $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ such that $u_{\mathbb Z}=\kappa\varepsilon'-\sigma$ satisfies $u_{\mathbb Z}\xi_0+p^s\xi_1=0$ in $H$. The image of $u_{\mathbb Z}$ in $\Lambda$ is $u$. So $\sigma\xi_0=\kappa\varepsilon'\xi_0+p^s\xi_1$.
- (b) For $m\ge2$, $u$ acts injectively on $\mathcal L_m(V)$.
- (c) Let $m\ge2$, $z\in\mathcal L_m$ and $t\ge1$. If $pz\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_m+p^t\mathcal L_m$, then $z\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_m+p^{t-1}\mathcal L_m$. If $pz\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_m$, then $z\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_m$.

*Proof.* (a) Write $\mathrm{ab}:P_F\to P_F^{\mathrm{ab}}$, a $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$-linear map for the conjugation actions. The element $(x_0,\tau)$ lies in the closed subgroup $Q_0$ generated by $x_0$ and $\tau$, and in $P_F$, as its image in $\mathcal G$ is $\tau^\pi=1$ (the Computation of the tame-group-algebra theorem). Let $N'$ be the closed subgroup generated by the $\tau^jx_0\tau^{-j}$, $j\in\mathbb Z$. It is normalized by $x_0$ and $\tau^{\pm1}$, hence by $Q_0$, so $Q_0=N'\cdot\overline{\langle\tau\rangle}$; as $N'\subseteq P_F$ and $\overline{\langle\tau\rangle}\cap P_F=1$, $Q_0\cap P_F=N'$. Its image under $\mathrm{ab}$ is the closed subgroup generated by the $\tau^j\mathrm{ab}(x_0)$, which is the compact set $\mathbb Z_p[[I]]\,\mathrm{ab}(x_0)$. So $\mathrm{ab}((x_0,\tau))=\varepsilon'\mathrm{ab}(x_0)$ with $\varepsilon'\in\mathbb Z_p[[I]]$. Also $\mathrm{ab}(x_0^\sigma)=\sigma\,\mathrm{ab}(x_0)$, $\mathrm{ab}(x_1^{p^s})=p^s\mathrm{ab}(x_1)$ and $\mathrm{ab}(C)=0$. So $\mathrm{ab}(\varrho)=u_{\mathbb Z}\mathrm{ab}(x_0)+p^s\mathrm{ab}(x_1)$. The surjection $P_F\to P$ induces a $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$-linear map $P_F^{\mathrm{ab}}\to H$ killing $\mathrm{ab}(\varrho)$ and mapping $\mathrm{ab}(x_i)$ to $\xi_i$, which gives the relation. Reducing $\mathrm{ab}(\varrho)$ modulo $p$ gives the image of $\varrho$ in $\bar P=P_F/P_F^p[P_F,P_F]$, namely $\bar u_{\mathbb Z}\bar x_0$, with $\bar u_{\mathbb Z}$ the image of $u_{\mathbb Z}$ in $\Lambda$. By the proof of part 1 of the tame-group-algebra theorem, this image is $u\bar x_0$ and $\bar P$ is a free $\Lambda$-module on the $\bar x_i$. So $\bar u_{\mathbb Z}=u$.

(b) Let $um=0$ with $m\in\mathcal L_m(V)$. As $e_h$ is central and $u(1-e_h)=-\sigma(1-e_h)$, $0=(1-e_h)um=-\sigma(1-e_h)m$, so $m=e_hm$. Then $\tau m=\bar hm=\omega(\tau)m$, and $0=um=\bar gm-\sigma m$ gives $\sigma m=\omega(\sigma)m$. The set of $\rho\in\Gamma$ with $\rho m=\omega(\rho)m$ is a closed subgroup containing $\sigma$ and $\tau$, hence is $\Gamma$. So $\gamma m=m$, and $m=0$ by Lemma 1 (c) of the higher Johnson theorem, which gives $\mathcal L_m(V)^\gamma=0$.

(c) Write $pz=u_{\mathbb Z}a+p^tb$ with $a,b\in\mathcal L_m$. Modulo $p$, $u\bar a=0$ by (a), so $\bar a=0$ by (b), and $a=pa'$. As $\mathcal L_m$ is torsion-free, $z=u_{\mathbb Z}a'+p^{t-1}b$. For the last assertion take $b=0$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (bracketing with $\bar x_0$; proved here).** Let $n\ge2$ with $p\nmid n$, and $\bar y\in\mathcal L_n(V)$. If $[\bar y,\bar x_0]\in u\,\mathcal L_{n+1}(V)$, then $\bar y\in I_\Gamma\mathcal L_n(V)$.

*Proof.* Put $W=V^{\hat\otimes n+1}$. For every word $z$ of length $n+1$, the composite $\theta_z$ of the projection $W\to V_z$ and $\theta_{V_z}$ kills $u\,\mathcal L_{n+1}(V)\subseteq uW$, by the Setting. So $\theta_z([\bar y,\bar x_0])=0$, where $[\bar y,\bar x_0]=\bar y\otimes\bar x_0-\bar x_0\otimes\bar y$. For a word $w$ of length $n$, the maps $\iota^R_w:V_w\to V_{(w,0)}$, $y\mapsto y\otimes\bar x_0$, and $\iota^L_w:V_w\to V_{(0,w)}$, $y\mapsto\bar x_0\otimes y$, are isomorphisms with $\iota(\rho y)=\omega(\rho)^{-1}\rho\,\iota(y)$, so by the Setting they induce isomorphisms $\hat\iota^R_w:(V_w)_\Gamma\to V_{(w,0)}\langle\omega\rangle$ and $\hat\iota^L_w:(V_w)_\Gamma\to V_{(0,w)}\langle\omega\rangle$. Let $X_w\in(V_w)_\Gamma$ be the class of $\bar y_w$.

Let $w$ be a word of length $n$ with a nonzero letter, and $\ell$ its number of leading zeros; we show $X_w=0$ by induction on $\ell$. Put $z=(w,0)$. The component of $\bar x_0\otimes\bar y$ in $V_z$ is $0$ if $w_1\ne0$, and is $\bar x_0\otimes\bar y_{w'}$ if $w=(0,v)$ and $w'=(v,0)$. So $0=\theta_z([\bar y,\bar x_0])$ reads $\hat\iota^R_w(X_w)=0$ if $\ell=0$, and $\hat\iota^R_w(X_w)=\hat\iota^L_{w'}(X_{w'})$ if $\ell\ge1$. As $w'$ has a nonzero letter and $\ell-1$ leading zeros, $X_{w'}=0$ by induction, and in both cases $X_w=0$. The class of the component in $V_{(0,\dots,0)}$ is $0$ by Lemma 2 (c) of the higher cocycle theorem. So the image of $\bar y$ in $(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma$ is $0$, and by Lemma 1 there (the Lie projection, which needs $p\nmid n$) the class of $\bar y$ in $\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma$ is $0$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the obstruction; proved here).** Let $n\ge2$ with $p\nmid n$. For $1\le t\le s$ say that a normalized cocycle $c:\Gamma\to\mathcal L_n$ satisfies $(\mathrm O_t)$ if
$$[c(\sigma),\sigma\xi_0]\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_{n+1}+p^t\mathcal L_{n+1}.$$

- (i) If $c,c'$ are normalized and $c'-c=\delta k$, $k\in\mathcal L_n$, then $c'$ satisfies $(\mathrm O_t)$ if and only if $c$ does.
- (ii) If $c$ satisfies $(\mathrm O_t)$, then $[c]\in pH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$. If moreover $t\ge2$, then $[c]=p[c_1]$ for a normalized $c_1$ satisfying $(\mathrm O_{t-1})$.

So if $c$ satisfies $(\mathrm O_s)$, then $[c]\in p^sH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$.

*Proof.* (i) As $\delta k$ vanishes on $I$, $k\in\mathcal L_n^I$. For $\rho\in I$, $[k,\rho\xi_0]=\rho[\rho^{-1}k,\xi_0]=\rho[k,\xi_0]$; by continuity and linearity $[k,\lambda\xi_0]=\lambda[k,\xi_0]$ for $\lambda\in\mathbb Z_p[[I]]$. By Lemma 1 (a), $[k,\sigma\xi_0]=\kappa\varepsilon'[k,\xi_0]+p^s[k,\xi_1]$, and $[\sigma k,\sigma\xi_0]=\sigma[k,\xi_0]$. So
$$[\delta k(\sigma),\sigma\xi_0]=\sigma[k,\xi_0]-[k,\sigma\xi_0]=-u_{\mathbb Z}[k,\xi_0]-p^s[k,\xi_1]\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_{n+1}+p^t\mathcal L_{n+1}.$$

(ii) Put $y=c(\sigma)$. Reducing $(\mathrm O_t)$ modulo $p$, with $\sigma\bar x_0=\omega(\sigma)\bar x_0$ and Lemma 1 (a), gives $[\bar y,\bar x_0]\in u\,\mathcal L_{n+1}(V)$. By Lemma 2, $\bar y\in I_\Gamma\mathcal L_n(V)$, which is the image of the compact group $I_\Gamma\mathcal L_n$. So $y\in I_\Gamma\mathcal L_n+p\mathcal L_n$, that is, $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}([c])\in p(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma$, and $[c]\in pH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$ by Lemma 1 (b) of the theorem modulo the third lower central term, as $\mathcal L_n$ is torsion-free. Write $[c]=pv_1$ and let $c_1$ be a normalized cocycle in $v_1$. Then $c-pc_1$ is a coboundary between normalized cocycles, so $pc_1$ satisfies $(\mathrm O_t)$ by (i): $p[c_1(\sigma),\sigma\xi_0]\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_{n+1}+p^t\mathcal L_{n+1}$. If $t\ge2$, Lemma 1 (c) gives $(\mathrm O_{t-1})$ for $c_1$. The last assertion follows by applying (ii) $s$ times. $\square$

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$.

- (a) Let $n\ge2$. Every continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant homomorphism $J:H\to\mathcal L_n$ satisfies $J(\xi_1)\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_n$. So its reduction $\bar J:V\to\mathcal L_n(V)$ has $\bar J(\bar x_1)\in u\,\mathcal L_n(V)$, and $\eta(\bar J(\bar x_1))=0$ for every continuous linear $\eta:\mathcal L_n(V)\to\mathbb F_p$ with $\eta(\rho m)=\omega(\rho)\eta(m)$.
- (b) Let $n\ge2$ with $p\nmid n$, and let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ induce the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$. Then $u(\alpha)\in p^sH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$. Consequently $c_h\circ\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/P_n^{p^s}P_{n+1}$ for some $h\in P_n$, where $P_n^{p^s}P_{n+1}$ is the preimage of $p^s\mathcal L_n$ in $P_n$.

Neither part assumes finite outer conjugacy or $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$.

*Proof.* (a) By continuity and equivariance $J$ is $\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$-linear, so Lemma 1 (a) gives $u_{\mathbb Z}J(\xi_0)+p^sJ(\xi_1)=0$. Thus $p^sJ(\xi_1)\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_n$, and $J(\xi_1)\in u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_n$ by Lemma 1 (c), applied $s$ times. Reducing modulo $p$ gives $\bar J(\bar x_1)\in u\,\mathcal L_n(V)$. For $\eta$ as stated, $\eta(\lambda m)=\omega(\lambda)\eta(m)$ for $\lambda\in\Lambda$ by continuity and linearity, and $\omega(u)=0$.

(b) *Normalizing.* Choose a normalized $c=c_\alpha-\delta k$ and $g\in P_n$ over $k$. By (u1), $\alpha_1=c_g\alpha\in\mathcal A'_n$ has $c_{\alpha_1}=c$, and $u(\alpha_1)=u(\alpha)$.

*The next Johnson map.* As $\alpha_1(x)x^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$ for $x\in P$, Lemma 1 (a) of [the degree-one Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-on-wild-inertia.md) gives a continuous homomorphism $J:H\to\mathcal L_{n+1}$, $xP_2\mapsto\alpha_1(x)x^{-1}P_{n+2}$. Let $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$ lie over $\rho$, write $\alpha_1(\tilde\rho)=j\tilde\rho$ with $j\in P_n$ of class $c(\rho)$, and let $x\in P$ and $y=\tilde\rho x\tilde\rho^{-1}$. Then $\alpha_1(y)=j\cdot\tilde\rho\,\alpha_1(x)x^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}\cdot y\cdot j^{-1}$, so $\alpha_1(y)y^{-1}=j\,w\,j^{-1}\cdot[j,y]$ with $w=\tilde\rho\,\alpha_1(x)x^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$. Modulo $P_{n+2}$ this gives
$$J(\rho h)=\rho J(h)+[c(\rho),\rho h]\qquad(h\in H,\ \rho\in\Gamma),$$
with the bracket $\mathcal L_n\times H\to\mathcal L_{n+1}$ induced by commutators.

*The relator.* For $\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ put $B(\lambda)=J(\lambda\xi_0)-\lambda J(\xi_0)$. It is continuous and $\mathbb Z_p$-linear, and $B(\rho)=[c(\rho),\rho\xi_0]$ for $\rho\in\Gamma$. As $c$ vanishes on $I$, $B$ vanishes on $\mathbb Z_p[[I]]$, and $B(\sigma)=[c(\sigma),\sigma\xi_0]$. Applying $J$ to the relation of Lemma 1 (a),
$$0=u_{\mathbb Z}J(\xi_0)+\kappa B(\varepsilon')-B(\sigma)+p^sJ(\xi_1)=u_{\mathbb Z}J(\xi_0)-[c(\sigma),\sigma\xi_0]+p^sJ(\xi_1).$$
So $c$ satisfies $(\mathrm O_s)$, and $u(\alpha)=[c]\in p^sH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$ by Lemma 3.

*The element $h$.* Write $u(\alpha)=p^s[c']$, so $c_\alpha-p^sc'=\delta k'$ with $k'\in\mathcal L_n$, and choose $h\in P_n$ over $k'$. By (u1), $c_{c_h\alpha}=p^sc'$, so $c_h\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}\in P_n^{p^s}P_{n+1}$ for $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$. Also $c_h\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$ for $x\in P$, as $[h,x]\in P_{n+1}$. Every element of $G$ is $x\tilde\rho$ with $x\in P$, and $c_h\alpha(x\tilde\rho)(x\tilde\rho)^{-1}=c_h\alpha(x)x^{-1}\cdot x\big(c_h\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}\big)x^{-1}$, which lies in the normal subgroup $P_n^{p^s}P_{n+1}$. Finally, the image of $P_n^{p^s}$ in $\mathcal L_n$ is $p^s\mathcal L_n$, being compact, contained in the closed subgroup $p^s\mathcal L_n$ and containing every $p^s$-multiple, so $P_n^{p^s}P_{n+1}$ is the stated preimage. $\square$

**Corollary 1 (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$.

- (i) If $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$ and $e\ge3$, every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha'$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha'$ inducing the identity on $G/P_e^{p^s}P_{e+1}$ and on $P/P_{e+1}$. So every outer class with finitely many conjugates lies in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_e^{p^s}P_{e+1})$.
- (ii) If $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$ and $e=2$, the same holds with $e$ replaced by $2$: every such outer class lies in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_2^{p^s}P_3)$. This covers every $K$ with $p=3$ and $\mu_3\not\subseteq K$.

*Proof.* (i) By the corollary of the higher cocycle theorem, $\alpha=c_{g_0}\circ\alpha_e$ with $\alpha_e$ inducing the identity on $G/P_e$ and on $P/P_{e+1}$. As $e\mid p-1$, $p\nmid e$, so part (b) of the theorem with $n=e$ gives $h\in P_e$ with $c_h\alpha_e$ inducing the identity on $G/P_e^{p^s}P_{e+1}$; it is still trivial on $P/P_{e+1}$. Take $\alpha'=c_h\alpha_e$ and $g=g_0h^{-1}$. The subgroup $P_e^{p^s}P_{e+1}$ is characteristic, so the map on outer automorphism groups is defined. (ii) By Corollary 1 of [the degree-one Johnson theorem in degrees three and four](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-in-degrees-three-and-four.md), which covers every degree at least $3$, $\alpha=c_{g_0}\circ\alpha_0$ with $\alpha_0$ inducing the identity on $G/P_2$ and on $P/P_3$. Apply part (b) with $n=2$ as in (i). If $p=3$, then $e$ divides $2$, so $e=2$ when $\mu_3\not\subseteq K$. $\square$

**Corollary 2 (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $n\ge2$. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many outer conjugates and induce the identity on $G/P_n$. Then $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$ for every $x\in P$. That is, [the higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md) holds without its hypothesis $e\nmid n-1$.

*Proof.* If $e\nmid n-1$ this is the higher Johnson theorem, so let $e\mid n-1$, that is, $\omega^{n-1}=1$. By the saturation argument at the start of its proof, it suffices to show $\bar J=0$ for every $J\in\mathcal S$, the set of its Lemma 5. Fix $J\in\mathcal S$.

*Surface generators.* By its remark *When $e$ divides $n-1$*, steps 1 to 4 of its proof and step 5 up to $\rho c=c$ use only $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. As $\omega^{n-1}=1$, Lemma 1 (c) there gives $c=c_0\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}$ with $c_0\in\mathbb F_p$, so the coordinate $\varphi$ of (4) there is $c_0(1\otimes\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1})$, and Lemma 3 there gives $P=c_0[\cdots[\tilde e,\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]$. Let $D:V\to\mathcal L_n(V)$ be $D(v)=[\cdots[v,\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]$, with $n-1$ copies of $\bar x_0$. By (3) there, $\bar J(\bar x_k)=c_0D(\bar x_k)$ for every surface index $k$. The map $D$ is continuous and $\Lambda$-linear, since $\rho D(v)=\omega(\rho)^{n-1}D(\rho v)=D(\rho v)$, and $\mathcal L_n(B)DB^{-1}=D$ for every $\Lambda$-linear automorphism $B$ of $V$ fixing $\bar x_0$. So $\bar J^\natural=\bar J-c_0D$ is $\Lambda$-linear and vanishes on $S$.

*Complementary generators.* We rerun step 6 there for $\bar J^\natural$. Put $Q^\natural_{c'}=\bar J^\natural(\bar x_{c'})$ for complementary $c'$; it lies in $\mathcal L_n(A)$, by (1) there and as $D(\bar x_{c'})\in\mathcal L_n(A)$. Let $B$ be the action on $V$ of the automorphism from part 2 (even $d$) or part 3 (odd $d$) of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md) used there; it fixes $\bar x_0$. By (i') and (1) there, $\mathcal L_n(B)\bar JB^{-1}$ maps complementary generators into $\mathcal L_n(A)$, hence so does $\mathcal L_n(B)\bar J^\natural B^{-1}=\mathcal L_n(B)\bar JB^{-1}-c_0D$. As $B^{-1}\bar x_{c'}-\bar x_{c'}\in S$ and $\bar J^\natural(S)=0$, this map sends $\bar x_{c'}$ to $\mathcal L_n(B)Q^\natural_{c'}$. So the claim in step 6 there shows that no component of $Q^\natural_{c'}$ has a slot in $V_1$. For odd $d$, $A=L\oplus V_1$, so $Q^\natural_{c'}\in\mathcal L_n(V)\cap L^{\hat\otimes n}=\mathcal L_n(L)=0$ by (M1), as $n\ge2$ and $\dim L=1$. For even $d$, (iii') there for $T_1$ and $\mathcal L_n(\bar T_1)D=D\bar T_1$ give $\mathcal L_n(\bar T_1)\bar J^\natural=\bar J^\natural\bar T_1$, and the rest of step 6 applies verbatim to the $Q^\natural_{c'}$, giving $Q^\natural_{c'}=0$ for $c'=0,1,2$. So $\bar J^\natural=0$ on generators, hence on $V$, and $\bar J=c_0D$.

*The constant.* Let $\eta:V^{\hat\otimes n}\to\mathbb F_p$ be the projection to $V_{(1@1)}$, followed by its coordinate in $\Lambda$ (through $\mu\mapsto\mu\bar x_1\otimes\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}$) and the ring homomorphism $\omega^{2-n}:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$. As $\rho$ acts on this coordinate by $\omega(\rho)^{n-1}$ times left multiplication by $\rho$, $\eta(\rho m)=\omega(\rho)\eta(m)$. The component of $D(\bar x_1)$ in $V_{(1@1)}$ is $\bar x_1\otimes\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}$, by the last assertion of Lemma 3 there, so $\eta(\bar J(\bar x_1))=c_0$. As $J$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant, part (a) of the theorem gives $\eta(\bar J(\bar x_1))=0$. So $c_0=0$ and $\bar J=0$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* The earlier layers of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535) use only conjugation by automorphisms and finite outer conjugacy. Here the input is that the next Johnson map of an automorphism of $G=F/(\varrho)$ must respect the relation $u_{\mathbb Z}\xi_0+p^s\xi_1=0$ of $H$. For an equivariant Johnson map this puts the value at $\xi_1$ into $u_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal L_n$, a condition that conjugation does not see. For a cocycle class it gives $(\mathrm O_s)$. Modulo $p$ this says that $[\bar y,\bar x_0]$ dies in the $\omega$-twisted coinvariants, and bracketing with the eigenvector $\bar x_0$ embeds $\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma$ into them (Lemma 2). The $p$-adic layers can be peeled off only up to $p^s$, because $x_1$ enters the relator only through $x_1^{p^s}$.
- *The coordinates left open earlier.* Part (b) of the higher cocycle theorem leaves the reduction of $u(\alpha_e)$ in $\mathbb F_pE_e$, and part (b) of the theorem modulo the third lower central term leaves the coordinate $X_{01}$ when $e=2$. By part (b) here, these reductions vanish for every automorphism in question, and the classes themselves lie in $p^sH^1$. The remark *When $e$ divides $n-1$* of the higher Johnson theorem leaves the constant $c_0$ and the complementary values open; Corollary 2 shows that they vanish.
- *Where this stops.* Every normalized cocycle with values in $p^s\mathcal L_n$ satisfies $(\mathrm O_s)$, so the relator gives nothing on $u(\alpha)$ beyond divisibility by $p^s$. For $s=1$, which includes every unramified $K$, this is divisibility by $p$ only. So the chain of the earlier layers still stops at level $e$: if $u(\alpha_e)$ vanished, Corollary 2 with $n=e+1$ would give triviality on $P/P_{e+2}$, and the next obstruction would be the cocycle class at level $e+1$, where $e\mid n-1$ and part (a) of the higher cocycle theorem does not apply, though part (b) here still kills its reduction.
- *What remains.* (1) Whether the class $u(\alpha_e)\in p^sH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_e)$ of Corollary 1 vanishes. (2) The cocycle classes at the levels $n$ with $e\mid n-1$, beyond their reductions. (3) Degrees $3$ and $4$ in Corollary 2 and in Corollary 1 (i), and $p\mid n$ in part (b), where the Lie projection fails. (4) The case $\mu_p\subseteq K$ for the corollaries; the theorem itself holds there.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, every $s$, and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$ for the theorem; the corollaries as stated. Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase. This continues item 3 of research issue 535.
