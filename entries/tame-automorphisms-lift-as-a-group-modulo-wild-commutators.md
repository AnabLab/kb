**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of degree $n$ with $\mu_p \subset k$, which always holds for $p = 2$. As in [tame automorphisms lift modulo wild commutators](tame-automorphisms-lift-modulo-wild-commutators.md), let $T = k_{tr}$ be the maximal tamely ramified extension of $k$, and put $P = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/T)$, $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(T/k)$, $I = \mathrm{Gal}(T/k^{ur})$, $M = P^{ab}$ and
$$E = G_k/[P,P] = \mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}/k),$$
with restriction $\pi : E \to \Gamma$. Write $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma]] = \varprojlim_U \mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma/U]$, over the open normal subgroups $U$ of $\Gamma$. Choose generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\Gamma$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, where $\tau$ generates $I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, $\sigma$ lifts Frobenius and $q$ is the order of the residue field. For $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ and $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, the substitution $\beta_{u,a} : \tau \mapsto \tau^u$, $\sigma \mapsto \sigma\tau^a$ preserves the relation, and every automorphism of $\Gamma$ is of this form (step 1 of the cited proof).

Every continuous automorphism $\beta$ of $\Gamma$ permutes the open normal subgroups, so it induces a continuous ring automorphism $\beta_\Lambda$ of $\Lambda$ with $\beta_\Lambda(\gamma) = \beta(\gamma)$ for $\gamma \in \Gamma$. Clearly $(\beta\beta')_\Lambda = \beta_\Lambda\beta'_\Lambda$. The following facts are steps of the proof in the cited entry.

- (F1) $E \to \Gamma$ splits (step 2). Fix a continuous homomorphism $s : \Gamma \to E$ with $\pi s = \mathrm{id}$. Then every element of $E$ is $m\,s(\gamma)$ with unique $m \in M$, $\gamma \in \Gamma$, and $\gamma \cdot m = s(\gamma)ms(\gamma)^{-1}$ makes $M$ a compact $\Lambda$-module. If $\varphi : M \to M$ is a topological automorphism with $\varphi(\gamma \cdot m) = \beta(\gamma) \cdot \varphi(m)$, then $m\,s(\gamma) \mapsto \varphi(m)\,s(\beta(\gamma))$ is a continuous automorphism of $E$ lying over $\beta$ (step 2).
- (F2) Every $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ maps $I$ onto itself and satisfies $\beta(\gamma)\gamma^{-1} \in I$ for all $\gamma$ (step 1).
- (F3) Let $N$ be the kernel of $\Gamma \to \Gamma/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{Z}_p$. Averaging over the images of $N$ defines a central idempotent $e \in \Lambda$; put $\varepsilon = 1 - e$. Then $M = eM \oplus \varepsilon M$ with $eM = M^N$, $\beta_\Lambda(e) = e$ for every $\beta$, and $\gamma$ and $\beta(\gamma)$ act alike on $eM$ (step 3).
- (F4) $\varepsilon M \oplus \varepsilon\Lambda \cong (\varepsilon\Lambda)^{n+1}$ (step 5, which uses the projectivity of $\varepsilon M$ proved in step 4; that is where $\mu_p \subset k$ enters).

Every automorphism of $E$ maps $M$ onto itself, because $M$ is the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $E$ (step 1 of the proof in [the quotient by wild commutators](p-adic-galois-groups-modulo-wild-commutators.md)). So restriction gives a homomorphism $r : \mathrm{Aut}(E) \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ with $\pi\alpha = r(\alpha)\pi$, and the cited lifting theorem says that $r$ is onto.

Let $\Omega = k^{ur}(p)$ be the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $k^{ur}$. As $\mu_p \subset k$, this is the field $\Omega_k$ of [the entry on automorphisms with finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md).

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Assume $\mu_p \subset k$.

1. *Freeness.* $\varepsilon M \cong (\varepsilon\Lambda)^n$ as $\Lambda$-modules.
2. *A section.* Fix $s$ as in (F1) and an isomorphism $\iota : (\varepsilon\Lambda)^n \to \varepsilon M$. For $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ define $\varphi_\beta : M \to M$ by $\varphi_\beta(m) = m$ for $m \in eM$ and $\varphi_\beta(\iota(x_1, \dots, x_n)) = \iota(\beta_\Lambda(x_1), \dots, \beta_\Lambda(x_n))$, and define
$$\tilde\beta(m\,s(\gamma)) = \varphi_\beta(m)\,s(\beta(\gamma)).$$
Then $\tilde\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(E)$ with $r(\tilde\beta) = \beta$, and $\beta \mapsto \tilde\beta$ is a continuous injective homomorphism $\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma) \to \mathrm{Aut}(E)$. Hence $\mathrm{Aut}(E) = \ker r \rtimes \{\tilde\beta : \beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)\}$.
3. *What the lifts fix.* Each $\tilde\beta$ fixes $eM$ pointwise, maps $s(\Gamma)$ onto itself, and induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}((\Omega \cap T^{ab})/k)$.
4. *Finite order.* Every finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ is the isomorphic image under $r$ of a finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(E)$. For example, let $p$ be odd, let $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$ be prime, and let $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ have $\ell$-component a primitive $p$-th root of unity in $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ and all other components $1$. Then $\tilde\beta_{u,0}$ is an automorphism of $E$ of order $p$ that acts on tame inertia by $\tau \mapsto \tau^u$ and induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}((\Omega \cap T^{ab})/k)$. For $p = 2$, $\tilde\beta_{-1,0}$ is an involution of $E$ that inverts tame inertia.

**Proof of part 1.** Step 5 of the cited proof deduces $\beta^*(\varepsilon M) \cong \varepsilon M$ from $\beta^*(\varepsilon M) \oplus \varepsilon\Lambda \cong \varepsilon M \oplus \varepsilon\Lambda$, by Krull–Schmidt cancellation for the finite modules $X/(p^mX + I(U)X)$ and a compactness argument over all $m$ and $U$, which uses only that both modules are finitely generated. Apply the same argument to $\varepsilon M \oplus \varepsilon\Lambda \cong (\varepsilon\Lambda)^n \oplus \varepsilon\Lambda$, which is (F4). The module $\varepsilon M$ is finitely generated, as a quotient of $M$, which is a quotient of $\Lambda^{n+1}$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.**

*$\varphi_\beta$ is a topological automorphism of $M$.* By (F3), $\beta_\Lambda(\varepsilon) = \varepsilon$, so $\beta_\Lambda$ maps $\varepsilon\Lambda$ onto itself, and $\varphi_\beta$ is a well-defined continuous map on $M = eM \oplus \varepsilon M$. The rule $(\beta\beta')_\Lambda = \beta_\Lambda\beta'_\Lambda$ gives $\varphi_\beta\varphi_{\beta'} = \varphi_{\beta\beta'}$ on both summands, and $\varphi_{\mathrm{id}} = \mathrm{id}$. So $\varphi_\beta$ has inverse $\varphi_{\beta^{-1}}$.

*Semilinearity.* We show $\varphi_\beta(\lambda m) = \beta_\Lambda(\lambda)\varphi_\beta(m)$ for $\lambda \in \Lambda$ and $m \in M$. On $\varepsilon M$, for $x \in (\varepsilon\Lambda)^n$,
$$\varphi_\beta(\lambda\,\iota(x)) = \varphi_\beta(\iota(\lambda x)) = \iota(\beta_\Lambda(\lambda)\beta_\Lambda(x)) = \beta_\Lambda(\lambda)\,\varphi_\beta(\iota(x)),$$
since $\iota$ is $\Lambda$-linear and $\beta_\Lambda$ is multiplicative, coordinate by coordinate. On $eM$, for $\gamma \in \Gamma$, $\gamma m$ and $\beta(\gamma)m$ coincide by (F3), so $\varphi_\beta(\gamma m) = \gamma m = \beta(\gamma)\varphi_\beta(m)$. Both sides are continuous and $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear in $\lambda$, and the $\mathbb{Z}_p$-span of $\Gamma$ is dense in $\Lambda$, so the identity holds for all $\lambda$.

*The lifts.* By (F1), $\tilde\beta$ is a continuous automorphism of $E$ with $\pi\tilde\beta = \beta\pi$, so $r(\tilde\beta) = \beta$. For $\beta, \beta' \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$,
$$\tilde\beta\tilde\beta'(m\,s(\gamma)) = \tilde\beta(\varphi_{\beta'}(m)\,s(\beta'(\gamma))) = \varphi_\beta\varphi_{\beta'}(m)\,s(\beta\beta'(\gamma)) = \widetilde{\beta\beta'}(m\,s(\gamma)),$$
and $\widetilde{\mathrm{id}} = \mathrm{id}$. So $\beta \mapsto \tilde\beta$ is a homomorphism, injective because $r(\tilde\beta) = \beta$. As $r$ is onto with this homomorphic section, $\mathrm{Aut}(E)$ is the semidirect product of $\ker r$ and the image of the section.

*Continuity.* $\mathrm{Aut}(E)$ and $\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ carry the topologies defined by characteristic open subgroups, both groups being topologically finitely generated. Let $W \subseteq E$ be open and characteristic. The submodules $p^mM + I(U)M$, where $I(U)$ is the closed ideal of $\Lambda$ generated by the $x - 1$ with $x \in U$, are open and form a basis of neighbourhoods of $0$ in the finitely generated module $M$, because the ideals $p^m\Lambda + I(U)\Lambda$ do so in $\Lambda$. So there are $m$ and an open characteristic $U \subseteq s^{-1}(W)$ with $p^mM + I(U)M \subseteq W$. Suppose that $\beta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma/U$. Then $\beta_\Lambda(\gamma) - \gamma = (\beta(\gamma)\gamma^{-1} - 1)\gamma \in I(U)$ for $\gamma \in \Gamma$, so $\beta_\Lambda(\lambda) - \lambda \in I(U)$ for all $\lambda \in \Lambda$ by continuity, and hence $\varphi_\beta(m') - m' \in I(U)M \subseteq W$ for all $m' \in M$. Also $\tilde\beta(s(\gamma))s(\gamma)^{-1} = s(\beta(\gamma)\gamma^{-1}) \in s(U) \subseteq W$. As $W$ is normal, $\tilde\beta$ induces the identity on $E/W$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** The first two assertions hold by construction. For the third, averaging over the images of $I$, which has order prime to $p$, gives a central idempotent $e_I \in \Lambda$; put $\varepsilon_I = 1 - e_I$. For a compact $\Lambda$-module $X$, as in (F3), $X = e_IX \oplus \varepsilon_IX$ with $e_IX = X^I$, and $\varepsilon_IX$ is the closed subgroup generated by the elements $(x - 1)y$ with $x \in I$, $y \in X$: these lie in $\varepsilon_IX$ because $e_I(x - 1) = 0$, and $\varepsilon_I$ is a limit of averages of elements $1 - x$. For $X = \Lambda$, this identifies $\varepsilon_I\Lambda$ with the kernel of $\Lambda \to \mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma/I]]$.

- *The quotient.* The subgroup $\pi^{-1}(I) = M \rtimes s(I)$ is $\mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}/k^{ur})$. Its maximal pro-$p$ quotient kills $s(I)$, which has order prime to $p$, and so kills the closed normal subgroup generated by $s(I)$. That subgroup is $H_0 = \varepsilon_IM \cdot s(I)$. Indeed, it contains each $m\,s(x)m^{-1}s(x)^{-1}$ with $m \in M$, $x \in I$, which is $m - x \cdot m$ in additive notation on $M$, so it contains $\varepsilon_IM$. Conversely, $\varepsilon_IM \cdot s(I)$ is a closed subgroup containing $s(I)$, and it is normal in $M \rtimes s(I)$, because $\varepsilon_IM$ is a $\Lambda$-submodule and $m\,s(x)m^{-1} = (m - x \cdot m)\,s(x)$. The quotient $\pi^{-1}(I)/H_0 \cong M/\varepsilon_IM$ is pro-$p$. So the fixed field of $H_0$ is the largest subextension of $T^{ab}$ that is a pro-$p$ extension of $k^{ur}$, that is, $\Omega \cap T^{ab}$. In particular $H_0$ is normal in $E$ and $E/H_0 = \mathrm{Gal}((\Omega \cap T^{ab})/k)$.
- *The lifts act trivially on it.* By (F2), $\beta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma/I$, so $\beta_\Lambda$ induces the identity on $\Lambda/\varepsilon_I\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[\Gamma/I]]$, that is, $\beta_\Lambda(\lambda) - \lambda \in \varepsilon_I\Lambda$ for all $\lambda$. For $x \in (\varepsilon\Lambda)^n$ this gives $\varphi_\beta(\iota(x)) - \iota(x) = \iota(\beta_\Lambda(x) - x) \in \varepsilon_I\,\iota((\varepsilon\Lambda)^n) \subseteq \varepsilon_IM$, and $\varphi_\beta$ is the identity on $eM$. So $\varphi_\beta(m) \equiv m$ modulo $\varepsilon_IM$ for all $m \in M$. Also $s(\beta(\gamma)) = s(\beta(\gamma)\gamma^{-1})s(\gamma) \in s(I)s(\gamma)$. Hence $\tilde\beta(m\,s(\gamma)) \equiv m\,s(\gamma)$ modulo the normal subgroup $H_0$. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** The section of part 2 is an injective homomorphism, so it maps each finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ isomorphically onto a finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(E)$ whose image under $r$ is the given one. In the example, $\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ contains the $(\ell - 1)$-th roots of unity, hence a primitive $p$-th root of unity, so $u$ exists and has order $p$. Since $\beta_{u,0}^j = \beta_{u^j,0}$, the automorphism $\beta_{u,0}$ has order $p$, and so does $\tilde\beta_{u,0}$. As $\pi\tilde\beta_{u,0} = \beta_{u,0}\pi$, it acts on tame inertia $I$ by $\tau \mapsto \tau^u$, and part 3 gives the rest. For $p = 2$, $-1 \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ and $\beta_{-1,0}$ has order $2$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What is new.* The cited lifting theorem lifts each tame automorphism separately. Here the lifts are chosen compatibly, as a group, at the cost of fixing the splitting $s$ and the basis $\iota$, on which the section depends. The section does not obviously descend to outer automorphism groups: for $\gamma \in \Gamma$, the lift of conjugation by $\gamma$ acts on $(\varepsilon\Lambda)^n$ by $x \mapsto \gamma x\gamma^{-1}$, while conjugation by $s(\gamma)$ acts by $x \mapsto \gamma x$.
- *Tsujimura's question for $G_K$.* For odd $p$ and $\mu_p \subset K$, an automorphism of $G_K$ of $p$-power order with finitely many outer conjugates induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$, for the $p$ and $K$ covered by [that entry](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md). Its remark on case (a) leaves open how such an automorphism acts on tame inertia, by some $u$ with $u_\ell = 1$ unless $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$. Part 4 shows that on $E$, finite order $p$ and triviality on $\mathrm{Gal}((\Omega_K \cap T^{ab})/K)$, the largest common quotient of $E$ and $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$, do not exclude a twist $\tau \mapsto \tau^u$ by a $p$-th root of unity. An argument that excludes it for $G_K$ must therefore use more than $E$ together with these two properties, for instance the nonabelian structure of $P$ or the finite-conjugacy hypothesis. This entry does not decide whether $\tilde\beta_{u,0}$ is induced by an automorphism of $G_K$, nor whether its class in $\mathrm{Out}(E)$ has finitely many conjugates under the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$, which is not determined here.
- *Finite-order lifts to $G_k$.* For odd $p$, a tame automorphism preserving the mod-$p$ cyclotomic character lifts to $G_k$, but whether a tame automorphism of finite order has a lift of finite order is open ([remark there](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md)). Part 4 shows that, when $\mu_p \subset k$, the obstruction is not visible on $G_k/[P,P]$.
- *The case $p = 2$.* Every finite $k/\mathbb{Q}_2$ satisfies the hypothesis. For the unramified fields $U_f$ of the [Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md), part 2 lifts the shifts $\alpha_a$ to $E$ as a homomorphism $a \mapsto \tilde\alpha_a$ from $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. This adds nothing to the question for $G_{U_f}$ itself, which stays open.
- *Without $\mu_p$.* Step 4 of the cited proof, and so part 1, needs $\mu_p \subset k$. When $\mu_p \not\subset k$, the tame automorphisms that lift to $E$ are those preserving the mod-$p$ cyclotomic character, by [the cyclotomic criterion](cyclotomic-criterion-for-tame-lifting-modulo-wild-commutators.md), whose proof goes through Jannsen's classification of the module $M$ rather than an explicit basis. Whether that subgroup has a homomorphic section is not decided here.
