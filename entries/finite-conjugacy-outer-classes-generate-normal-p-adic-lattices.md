**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ finite with $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P=P_K$ its wild inertia, $\Phi(P)=\overline{P^p[P,P]}$, $V=P/\Phi(P)$ and $\Gamma=G/P$. Wild inertia and its Frattini subgroup are characteristic. Give $A=\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ and $O=\operatorname{Out}(G)$ their natural profinite topologies; these exist because $G$ is topologically finitely generated [@kondo2025, p. 17, paragraph preceding Lemma 2.13]. Write
$$\mathcal F=\{x\in O: x\text{ has finitely many conjugates in }O\},$$
the *FC-centre*. This set need not be closed in a general profinite group.

**Theorem (proved here).**

1. The automorphisms inducing the identity on both $\Gamma$ and $V$ form a closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup $A_0\subseteq A$. Its image $W\subseteq O$ is closed, normal and pro-$p$, and contains $\mathcal F$.
2. For any $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\mathcal F$, their closed normal closure in $O$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p^r$ for some finite $r\ge0$, and is centralized by an open normal subgroup of $O$. Every element of this closure belongs to $\mathcal F$.
3. The set $\mathcal F$ is a torsion-free abelian normal subgroup. Its closure is abelian and pro-$p$. Every nonidentity $x\in\mathcal F$ generates a closed subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$. In particular $Z(O)$ is a torsion-free abelian pro-$p$ group.

**Proof.**

*1. A pro-$p$ ambient subgroup.* The identity conditions defining $A_0$ are closed: for each $g\in G$ and $u\in P$ they require $\alpha(g)g^{-1}\in P$ and $\alpha(u)u^{-1}\in\Phi(P)$, respectively. Evaluation is continuous in the natural topology of $A$. Conjugating these conditions by any automorphism of $G$ preserves them, so $A_0$ is normal.

Let $B\subseteq A_0$ be the kernel of the action on $E=G/\Phi(P)$. Nishio's pro-$p$ kernel theorem, with $(J,P,i,s)=(G,P,2,1)$, says that $B$ is pro-$p$ [@nishio2026finite, Theorem 1.2 and proof, pp. 6–7]. Its hypotheses allow $P$ to have infinite rank; only $G$ must be topologically finitely generated.

An automorphism $\alpha\in A_0$ induces an automorphism of $E$ fixing its normal elementary abelian subgroup $V$ pointwise and acting identically on $E/V=\Gamma$. In additive notation for $V$, set
$$c_\alpha(\gamma)=\alpha(e)e^{-1}\in V,\qquad e\in E\text{ mapping to }\gamma.$$
This is independent of the lift: replacing $e$ by $ve$ conjugates $\alpha(e)e^{-1}$ by $v\in V$, which has no effect. Multiplication in $E$ gives
$$c_\alpha(\gamma\delta)=c_\alpha(\gamma)+\gamma c_\alpha(\delta),\qquad c_{\alpha\beta}=c_\alpha+c_\beta,$$
where the second identity uses that $\alpha$ fixes $V$. The kernel of $\alpha\mapsto c_\alpha$ is $B$. Consequently the profinite quotient $A_0/B$ is abelian of exponent $p$, so is pro-$p$. An extension of two pro-$p$ groups is pro-$p$, proving the assertion for $A_0$. Its image $W$ is a compact normal pro-$p$ subgroup of the Hausdorff group $O$, hence closed.

For $x\in\mathcal F$, [tame innerness](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md) supplies a representative $\alpha\in A$ inducing the identity on $\Gamma$. It still represents $x$, so has finite outer conjugacy. The [wild Frattini theorem](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-wild-frattini.md) then gives $\alpha|_V=1$. Thus $\alpha\in A_0$ and $x\in W$.

*2. Normal closures have open centre and no torsion.* Let $S$ be the union of the conjugacy classes of $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, a finite conjugation-invariant set, and put $H=\overline{\langle S\rangle}$. Then $H$ is a topologically finitely generated closed normal subgroup of $O$, and $H\subseteq W$ makes it pro-$p$.

For $s\in S$, its centralizer $C_O(s)$ is closed of finite index, hence open. Therefore
$$C=\bigcap_{s\in S}C_O(s)$$
is open and, because $S$ is conjugation-invariant, normal in $O$. It centralizes $H$ by continuity. In particular $Z(H)$ contains the open subgroup $H\cap C$ and is open in $H$. Also every $h\in H$ has at most $[O:C]$ conjugates, so $H\subseteq\mathcal F$. Corollary 1 of tame innerness excludes every nonidentity finite-order element of $\mathcal F$, so $H$ is torsion-free.

*3. A transfer argument makes $H$ abelian.* We give the argument to avoid assuming that an open centre alone forces commutativity. Put $Z=Z(H)$ and $m=[H:Z]$. Choose representatives $T$ for the finitely many cosets $Zt$. For $h\in H$, write uniquely
$$th=z_{t,h}t_h,\qquad z_{t,h}\in Z,\quad t_h\in T.$$
Right multiplication by $h$ permutes $T$. The continuous map
$$v:H\longrightarrow Z,\qquad v(h)=\prod_{t\in T}z_{t,h}$$
is a homomorphism: the identity $z_{t,hk}=z_{t,h}z_{t_h,k}$, followed by reindexing the product, gives $v(hk)=v(h)v(k)$. For $z\in Z$, centrality gives $t_z=t$ and $z_{t,z}=z$, so $v(z)=z^m$.

Since $H$ is torsion-free, $\ker(v)\cap Z=1$. Thus $\ker(v)$ injects into the finite group $H/Z$, and is finite. Torsion-freeness now forces $\ker(v)=1$. Hence $H$ embeds in the abelian group $Z$, so is abelian. A topologically finitely generated abelian pro-$p$ group is a finitely generated $\mathbb Z_p$-module; being torsion-free, it is free of finite rank. This proves part 2, including $r=0$ when $H=1$. Conjugation on $H\cong\mathbb Z_p^r$ factors through the finite quotient $O/C$.

*4. Passing to all finite-conjugacy classes.* Applying part 2 to $x,y\in\mathcal F$ shows that they commute and that $xy$ and $x^{-1}$ lie in $\mathcal F$. Conjugation preserves $\mathcal F$ by definition, and tame innerness excludes its nonidentity torsion. Its closure is abelian by continuity and pro-$p$ because it is a closed subgroup of $W$. For $1\ne x\in\mathcal F$, its closed cyclic subgroup is torsion-free, pro-$p$ and generated by one element, hence is $\mathbb Z_p$. Finally $Z(O)$ is closed and contained in $\mathcal F$, so it is torsion-free, abelian and pro-$p$. $\square$

**Scope.** This gives a structural reduction of the remaining infinite-order case in [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535). Any nontrivial finite-conjugacy class would give a nonzero normal $\mathbb Z_p$-lattice with finite conjugation action, consisting entirely of finite-conjugacy classes. The theorem neither constructs nor excludes such a lattice. It does not assert that $\mathcal F$ is closed or that its whole closure is torsion-free. The cases $p=2$ and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\le2$ are not covered. The deductions are proved here from the linked results, with no novelty claim; the other sources behind those entries were not reopened.
