**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, let $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite of degree $n$ with residue field of order $q$, let $T = k_{tr}$ be its maximal tamely ramified extension, and put $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(T/k)$, $V = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/T)$ and $\pi : G_k \to \Gamma$ the restriction. Let $I \subseteq \Gamma$ be tame inertia. Choose generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\Gamma$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, where $\tau$ generates $I$ and $\sigma$ lifts Frobenius (see [generators and relations for $G_k$](local-galois-group-presentations.md)). Write $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. As $I \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$ (see [wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), $x \mapsto \tau^x$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' \to I$.

The extension $k(\mu_p)/k$ has degree dividing $p - 1$, so it lies in $T$, and the mod-$p$ cyclotomic character factors as $\bar\chi : \Gamma \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$. Let $d$ be the order of $\bar\chi(\tau)$. As $\bar\chi$ identifies $\Gamma/\ker\bar\chi$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(k(\mu_p)/k)$ and $I$ maps onto its inertia subgroup, $d$ is the ramification index of $k(\mu_p)/k$. From $\bar\chi(\tau) = \bar\chi(\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}) = \bar\chi(\tau)^q$, $d$ divides $q - 1$.

Every automorphism $\Theta$ of $G_k$ maps $V$ onto itself, because $V$ is the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G_k$ (see [wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). So it induces $\bar\Theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ with $\pi \circ \Theta = \bar\Theta \circ \pi$. Say that $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ *lifts* if $\beta = \bar\Theta$ for some $\Theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$. Automorphisms are continuous, and $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ and $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ carry the profinite topology recalled in [Tsujimura's question](p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), defined by the characteristic open subgroups of $G_k$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here by combining the cited results.

1. *Lifting criterion.* $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ lifts if and only if $\bar\chi \circ \beta = \bar\chi$.
2. *Explicit form.* Every $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ is $\beta_{u,a} : \tau \mapsto \tau^u$, $\sigma \mapsto \sigma\tau^a$ for unique $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ and $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and every such pair occurs. $\beta_{u,a}$ lifts if and only if $u \equiv 1$ and $a \equiv 0 \pmod d$.
3. *The image in tame outer automorphisms.* Let $U_d = \{u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times : u \equiv 1 \bmod d\}$, and let $\overline{q^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}} \subseteq U_d$ be the closure of the powers of $q$. The image $\mathcal O$ of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma)$ sits in an exact sequence
$$1 \to \mathbb{Z}/\tfrac{q-1}{d} \to \mathcal O \to U_d/\overline{q^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}} \to 1,$$
where the second map sends $\beta_{u,a}$ to the class of $u$, and the first sends $a$ to the class of $\beta_{1, da}$.
4. *Not finitely generated.* For every $r \ge 1$, $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ has a continuous quotient isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^r$. In particular it is not topologically finitely generated.

**Examples.**

- If $\mu_p \subset k$, then $d = 1$, and every automorphism of $\Gamma$ lifts.
- For $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$, $q = p$ and $d = p - 1$, so $\mathcal O \cong U_{p-1}/\overline{p^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}}$. This group is infinite, since the map $\varepsilon$ in the proof of part 4 factors through it. A lift of $\beta_{u,0}$ with $u \notin \overline{p^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}}$ is an outer automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ that induces an outer automorphism of $\Gamma$. The outer automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ constructed by Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, and those of [order two](involutive-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) and of [finite abelian subgroups](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md), are the identity on $\Gamma$.
- For $k$ unramified of degree $f \ge 2$, $d = p - 1$ and $(q - 1)/d = (p^f - 1)/(p - 1) > 1$. The shift $\beta_{1,1} : \sigma \mapsto \sigma\tau$ does not lift, since $p - 1 > 1$. The shift $\beta_{1, p-1}$ lifts, and its class in $\mathrm{Out}(\Gamma)$ is nontrivial. In the [2-adic Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md), by contrast, $d = 1$ and the question is whether $\beta_{1,1}$ itself lifts.

**Proof of part 1.**

*Necessity.* Let $\Theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ and $\beta = \bar\Theta$. Every automorphism of $G_k$ preserves the $p$-adic cyclotomic character $\chi$ (see [invariants of $G_K$](local-field-galois-invariants.md)), so $\chi \circ \Theta = \chi$. Reducing modulo $p$ gives $\bar\chi \circ \pi \circ \Theta = \bar\chi \circ \pi$, that is, $\bar\chi \circ \beta \circ \pi = \bar\chi \circ \pi$. As $\pi$ is onto, $\bar\chi \circ \beta = \bar\chi$.

*Sufficiency.* Suppose $\bar\chi \circ \beta = \bar\chi$. Let $\mu_T$ be the group of $p$-power roots of unity in $T$, of order $p^s$, and $\chi_T : \Gamma \to (\mathbb{Z}/p^s)^\times$ the action on it. Step 3 of the [cyclotomic criterion](cyclotomic-criterion-for-tame-lifting-modulo-wild-commutators.md) shows that $\chi_T \circ \beta = \chi_T$. Put $\alpha = \chi_T^{-1}$, so $\alpha \circ \beta^{-1} = \alpha$. By part 1 of [uniqueness of Demuškin formations](uniqueness-of-demuskin-formations.md), $(G_k, \pi)$ is a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n, s, \alpha$. By the lemma below, so is $(G_k, \beta \circ \pi)$. Part 3 of that entry gives an isomorphism $\Theta : G_k \to G_k$ with $\pi \circ \Theta = \beta \circ \pi$, which is a lift of $\beta$.

*Lemma (re-marking).* Let $(X, \varphi)$ be a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n, s, \alpha$, and let $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$. Then $(X, \beta \circ \varphi)$ is a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n, s, \alpha \circ \beta^{-1}$.

*Proof.* Put $\varphi' = \beta \circ \varphi$ and $\alpha' = \alpha \circ \beta^{-1}$. The map $\varphi'$ is onto, with the same pro-$p$ kernel as $\varphi$. The open normal subgroups $\mathcal H \subseteq \ker\alpha' = \beta(\ker\alpha)$ correspond to the open normal subgroups $\mathcal H_0 = \beta^{-1}(\mathcal H) \subseteq \ker\alpha$, and $\varphi'^{-1}(\mathcal H) = \varphi^{-1}(\mathcal H_0)$. So for $(X, \varphi')$ and $\mathcal H$, the subgroup in the definition is the same subgroup $X_{\mathcal H_0}$ of $X$ as for $(X, \varphi)$ and $\mathcal H_0$. Let $\bar\beta : G_0 = \Gamma/\mathcal H_0 \to G = \Gamma/\mathcal H$ be the isomorphism induced by $\beta$. An element $g \in G$ acts on the cohomology of $\tilde X_{\mathcal H_0}$, for $\varphi'$, by conjugation by any $x \in X$ with $\varphi'(x) \in g$, that is, with $\varphi(x) \in \bar\beta^{-1}(g)$. So it acts as $\bar\beta^{-1}(g)$ acts for $\varphi$, and likewise $\rho \in \Gamma$ acts for $\varphi'$ as $\beta^{-1}(\rho)$ acts for $\varphi$. Now check the three conditions of the definition.

1. The group $\tilde X_{\mathcal H_0}$ is the same, and $|G| = |G_0|$.
2. The map $X_{\mathcal H_0} \to \mathcal H$ given by $\varphi'$ is $\varphi$ followed by the isomorphism $\beta : \mathcal H_0 \to \mathcal H$. So inflation along it has the same image in $H^1(\tilde X_{\mathcal H_0}, \mathbb{F}_p)$ as inflation from $H^1(\mathcal H_0, \mathbb{F}_p)$ along $\varphi$. The orthogonal complement $W$ and the quotient are therefore the same groups, with the same cup product. A subgroup is stable under $G$ for $\varphi'$ exactly when it is stable under $G_0$ for $\varphi$, and the $\mathbb{F}_p[G]$-module structure is obtained from the $\mathbb{F}_p[G_0]$-module structure through the ring isomorphism $\mathbb{F}_p[G_0] \to \mathbb{F}_p[G]$ induced by $\bar\beta$. Freeness of rank $n$ and a decomposition into two totally isotropic submodules are carried over.
3. $\rho \in \Gamma$ acts on $H^2(\tilde X_{\mathcal H_0}, \mathbb{Z}/p^s)$ for $\varphi'$ as $\beta^{-1}(\rho)$ does for $\varphi$, that is, as multiplication by $\alpha(\beta^{-1}(\rho)) = \alpha'(\rho)$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** By Step 1 of the [cyclotomic criterion](cyclotomic-criterion-for-tame-lifting-modulo-wild-commutators.md), every $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ maps $I$ onto itself and induces the identity on $\Gamma/I$. As $\beta$ restricts to an automorphism of $I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, $\beta(\tau) = \tau^u$ with $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$, and $\beta(\sigma) = \sigma\tau^a$ with $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, both unique. Conversely, for any such $u, a$ the substitution preserves the relation, because $\sigma\tau^a\tau^u\tau^{-a}\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{qu}$. One checks on $\sigma$ and $\tau$ that
$$\beta_{u,a} \circ \beta_{u',a'} = \beta_{uu',\, a + ua'},$$
so each $\beta_{u,a}$ has the inverse $\beta_{u^{-1}, -u^{-1}a}$. Since $\sigma, \tau$ generate $\Gamma$ topologically, $\bar\chi \circ \beta_{u,a} = \bar\chi$ exactly when $\bar\chi(\tau)^u = \bar\chi(\tau)$ and $\bar\chi(\sigma)\bar\chi(\tau)^a = \bar\chi(\sigma)$, that is, when $u \equiv 1$ and $a \equiv 0$ modulo $d$. This is the explicit test of the cyclotomic criterion, and part 1 finishes the proof. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Inner automorphisms of $G_k$ induce inner automorphisms of $\Gamma$, so $\Theta \mapsto \bar\Theta$ induces $\mathrm{Out}(G_k) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma)$. Let $S \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ be the subgroup of $\beta$ with $\bar\chi \circ \beta = \bar\chi$. It contains the inner automorphisms of $\Gamma$, because $\bar\chi$ takes values in an abelian group, and by part 1 it is the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$. So $\mathcal O = S/\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)$.

*Inner automorphisms.* Every element of $\Gamma$ is $\sigma^b\tau^y$ with $b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, by the lemma in the [Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md), and $\sigma^b\tau\sigma^{-b} = \tau^{q^b}$, with $q^b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ defined by continuity. So conjugation by $\sigma^b$ is $\beta_{q^b, 0}$. As $\sigma^{-1}\tau\sigma = \tau^{1/q}$, conjugation by $\tau^y$ fixes $\tau$ and sends $\sigma$ to $\sigma\tau^{y/q}\tau^{-y}$, so it is $\beta_{1,\, y(1-q)/q}$. By the composition rule, conjugation by $\sigma^b\tau^y$ is $\beta_{q^b,\, q^b y(1-q)/q}$, and as $y$ runs through $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ the second index runs through $(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, because $q$ is a unit there. Writing $\overline{q^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}} = \{q^b : b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\}$, which is the closure of the powers of $q$, this gives
$$\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma) = \{\beta_{v, a} : v \in \overline{q^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}},\ a \in (q-1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'\}.$$
Here $\overline{q^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}} \subseteq U_d$ because $d$ divides $q - 1$.

*The sequence.* By part 2, $S = \{\beta_{u,a} : u \in U_d,\ a \in d\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'\}$. By the composition rule, $\beta_{u,a} \mapsto u$ is a homomorphism from $S$ onto $U_d$, with kernel $\{\beta_{1,a} : a \in d\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'\}$, and it maps $\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)$ onto $\overline{q^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}}$. So $\mathcal O \to U_d/\overline{q^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}}$ is onto. Its kernel is the image of $\{\beta_{u,a} \in S : u \in \overline{q^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}}\}$, and each such element is $\beta_{u,0} \circ \beta_{1, u^{-1}a}$ with $\beta_{u,0}$ inner, so the kernel is the image of $\{\beta_{1,a} : a \in d\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'\}$. By the description of $\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)$ and the uniqueness of the indices in part 2, $\beta_{1,a}$ is inner exactly when $a \in (q-1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. So the kernel is $d\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'/(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. Multiplication by $d$, which is prime to $p$, maps $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ isomorphically onto $d\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ and $\frac{q-1}{d}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ onto $(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'/m\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' \cong \mathbb{Z}/m$ for $m$ prime to $p$. So the kernel is cyclic of order $(q-1)/d$, generated by the class of $\beta_{1,d}$. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** For $\Theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$, let $u(\Theta)$ be the $u$ of $\bar\Theta = \beta_{u,a}$. By the composition rule, $u : \mathrm{Aut}(G_k) \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ is a homomorphism.

*Continuity modulo $m$.* Let $m \ge 1$ be prime to $p$, let $f_m$ be the order of $q$ modulo $m$, and let $D_m$ be the closed subgroup of $\Gamma$ generated by $\tau^m$ and $\sigma^{f_m}$. It is normal, because $\sigma\tau^m\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{qm}$ and $\tau\sigma^{f_m}\tau^{-1} = \sigma^{f_m}\tau^{q^{-f_m} - 1}$ with $q^{-f_m} - 1 \in m\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. Its intersection with $I$ is the closure of $\langle\tau^m\rangle$, and it has finite index. Let $C_m$ be the intersection of all open normal subgroups of $\Gamma$ of index at most $(\Gamma : D_m)$. There are finitely many of them, because $\Gamma$ is topologically finitely generated, so $C_m$ is open. It is characteristic, and $C_m \cap I \subseteq D_m \cap I$. Then $N_m = \pi^{-1}(C_m)$ is an open subgroup of $G_k$, and it is characteristic, because every $\Theta$ satisfies $\pi \circ \Theta = \bar\Theta \circ \pi$ with $\bar\Theta(C_m) = C_m$. If $\Theta$ induces the identity on $G_k/N_m = \Gamma/C_m$, then $\tau^{u(\Theta)} \equiv \tau$ modulo $C_m$. The image of $\tau$ in $\Gamma/C_m$ has order divisible by $m$, since $I/(C_m \cap I)$ maps onto $I/(D_m \cap I) \cong \mathbb{Z}/m$. So $u(\Theta) \equiv 1 \pmod m$. Hence $\Theta \mapsto u(\Theta) \bmod m$ is constant on cosets of an open subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$, so it is continuous.

*The quotient.* Let $r \ge 1$, and let $L$ be a set of $r + 1$ odd primes not dividing $pd$. For $\ell \in L$, write $(\cdot / \ell)$ for the Legendre symbol, and define
$$\varepsilon : \mathrm{Aut}(G_k) \to E = \prod_{\ell \in L}\{\pm 1\}, \qquad \Theta \mapsto \big((u(\Theta)/\ell)\big)_{\ell \in L},$$
where $u(\Theta)$ is reduced modulo $\ell$. It is a continuous homomorphism, by the previous paragraph with $m = \prod_{\ell \in L}\ell$.

- *$\varepsilon$ is onto.* The map $U_d \to \prod_{\ell \in L}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^\times$ is onto, because the condition $u \equiv 1 \bmod d$ involves only the components at primes dividing $d$, and $L$ avoids them. By part 2, each $\beta_{u,0}$ with $u \in U_d$ lifts.
- *Inner automorphisms.* If $\Theta$ is conjugation by $g$, then $\bar\Theta$ is conjugation by $\pi(g) = \sigma^b\tau^y$, which is $\beta_{q^b, a}$ for some $a$ by the computation in part 3. Since $\ell - 1$ is even, $q^b$ modulo $\ell$ is a square exactly when $q^{b_0}$ is, where $b_0 \in \{0, 1\}$ is $b$ modulo $2$. So $\varepsilon(\Theta) = \big((q/\ell)^{b_0}\big)_\ell$, and $\varepsilon(\mathrm{Inn}(G_k)) \subseteq \{1, e_q\}$ with $e_q = ((q/\ell))_{\ell}$.

So $\varepsilon$ induces a continuous surjection from $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ onto $E/\{1, e_q\}$, which is an $\mathbb{F}_2$-vector space of dimension at least $r$. It has a quotient isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^r$. If $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ were topologically generated by $r'$ elements, so would be each of its finite continuous quotients, which fails for $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{r'+1}$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Finite order.* Part 1 gives some lift of $\beta$, not one of finite order. Whether an automorphism of $\Gamma$ of finite order, in the set described by part 2, has a lift of finite order is not settled here. The automorphisms of finite order constructed so far for odd $p$ act trivially on $\Gamma$ (see [finite subgroups of outer automorphism groups](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md)).
- *The kernel.* $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ is an extension of the group $S$ of part 3 by the group of automorphisms that induce the identity on $\Gamma$. This entry says nothing about the latter.
- *Presentations.* By part 4 of [uniqueness of Demuškin formations](uniqueness-of-demuskin-formations.md), Jannsen and Wingberg's presentation of $G_k$ holds for every generator pair of $\Gamma$, with an isomorphism compatible with the maps to $\Gamma$. Applied to the pairs $(\sigma, \tau)$ and $(\beta(\sigma), \beta(\tau))$, this gives another route to part 1, along the lines of the heuristic paragraph in the [Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md). That route also needs a check that the substitution $\sigma \mapsto \beta(\sigma)$, $\tau \mapsto \beta(\tau)$, $x_i \mapsto x_i$ carries the relator for one pair to the relator for the other, with matching choices of the lift of $\alpha$. The re-marking lemma avoids this check.
- *The case $p = 2$.* The proof needs Koch's characterization and Wingberg's uniqueness, which assume $p$ odd [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, §I, p. 99]. When $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is ramified, as for the unramified fields of the Frobenius shift problem, Schneider writes that there is no sensible notion of a Demuškin formation (see [generators and relations](local-galois-group-presentations.md)). So the method does not reach that problem.
- *Literature.* The statement was not found in the sources opened for this entry. Kumpitsch's 2022 Frankfurt thesis on outer automorphisms of these groups could not be opened, and a November 2026 search was not exhaustive.
