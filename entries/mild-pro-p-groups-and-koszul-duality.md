**Setting.** Fix a prime $p$ and a finitely generated pro-$p$ group $G$. Write $H=H^\bullet(G,\mathbb F_p)$ for continuous cohomology with trivial coefficients, and

$$B=\operatorname{gr}\mathbb F_p[[G]]=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}I^n/I^{n+1},$$

where $I$ is the augmentation ideal of the completed group algebra. This is the restricted universal enveloping algebra of the graded restricted Lie algebra associated to the $p$-Zassenhaus filtration. See [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, §3.1–3.2 and Proposition 3.4, pp. 7–9]. Quadratic algebras, their duals $H^!$, and Koszulity are defined in the [Koszulity conjecture entry](positselski-koszulity-conjecture.md).

**Mildness.** Take a finite minimal pro-$p$ presentation $G=S/R$, with $S$ free on $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ and $R$ the closed normal subgroup generated by $r_1,\ldots,r_m$. Under the Magnus substitution $x_j\mapsto1+X_j$, let $\rho_i$ be the first nonzero homogeneous part of $r_i-1$, of degree $s_i\ge2$. Put

$$A=\mathbb F_p\langle X_1,\ldots,X_d\rangle/(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m),\qquad h_A(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(A_n)z^n.$$

The presentation is *strongly free* if

$$h_A(z)=\frac{1}{1-dz+\sum_{i=1}^m z^{s_i}}.$$

A group admitting such a presentation is *mild with respect to the $p$-Zassenhaus filtration*; the empty sequence of relators is allowed [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Definition 4.4, p. 11]. All uses of mildness here refer to this filtration.

**Theorem (Mináč–Pasini–Quadrelli–Tân).** If $G$ is mild and $H$ is quadratic, then $H$ and $B$ are Koszul and

$$H^!\cong B\cong\mathbb F_p\langle X_1,\ldots,X_d\rangle/(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m).$$

Here every $\rho_i$ has degree two, $d=\dim H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)$ and $m=\dim H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)$, and

$$h_B(z)=\frac{1}{1-dz+mz^2}.$$

These are [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 4.1, Proposition 4.5 and Theorem 4.6 with its proof, pp. 10–11]. For $m\ge1$ the group has $\operatorname{cd}(G)=2$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 4.5, p. 11]. Conversely, if $G$ is finitely generated, $\operatorname{cd}(G)=2$ and $H$ is Koszul, then $G$ is mild [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 4.7, pp. 11–12]. The theorem and converse hold also at $p=2$.

**A cup-product criterion.** For a finitely generated $G$ with $H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)\ne0$, a decomposition $H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)=U\oplus W$ with

$$U\cup U=0,\qquad U\otimes W\xrightarrow{\ \cup\ }H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)\ \text{surjective}$$

implies mildness [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 5.8, p. 15]. This is a sufficient criterion; no choice of presentation is part of its hypothesis.

**Galois application.** Let $K$ contain a primitive $p$th root of unity and also $\sqrt{-1}$ if $p=2$. If $G_K(p)$ is mild, the [norm residue theorem](norm-residue-isomorphism-theorem.md) supplies quadratic cohomology, and the theorem proves the mild case of Positselski's conjecture and the corresponding quadratic-duality and graded-algebra Koszulity conjectures [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Introduction, Conjectures 1.1–1.2 and the paragraph following Theorem 1.3, pp. 1–3]. Infinite [Demushkin groups](demushkin-group.md) are examples; for odd $p$, so is $S/\langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle$ when $S$ is finitely generated free pro-$p$ and $r\in S_{(2)}\setminus S_{(3)}$, where $S_{(n)}$ denotes its Zassenhaus filtration [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Examples 5.1–5.2, p. 12].
