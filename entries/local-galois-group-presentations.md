title: Generators and relations for the absolute Galois group of a p-adic field
kind: theorem
summary: 'For odd $p$, Jannsen and Wingberg present $G_K$ for $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $n$ by $n + 3$ generators and explicit relations, which depend only on $n$, the residue field and the tame action on $p$-power roots of unity. Diekert treats $p = 2$ when $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is unramified.'
tags: [local-fields, galois-groups]
---

**Statement for odd $p$.** Let $p \ne 2$, let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite of degree $n$ with residue field of order $q$, let $T$ be the maximal tamely ramified extension of $K$, and let $\mathcal{G} = \mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$. Following Hasse and Iwasawa, $\mathcal{G}$ has generators $\sigma, \tau$ with the single defining relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$. The $p$-power roots of unity in $T$ form a group $\mu_T$ of finite order $p^s$ with $s \ge 1$, and there are $g, h \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ with $\sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^g$ and $\tau(\zeta) = \zeta^h$ for $\zeta \in \mu_T$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 71]. Then $G_K$ is isomorphic to the profinite group with generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_n$ and the following defining conditions [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 71]:

1. The closed normal subgroup generated by $x_0, \dots, x_n$ is a pro-$p$ group.
2. $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$.
3. For even $n$, $x_0^\sigma = (x_0, \tau)^g x_1^{p^s} [x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4] \cdots [x_{n-1}, x_n]$. For odd $n$, $x_0^\sigma = (x_0, \tau)^g x_1^{p^s} [x_1, y_1][x_2, x_3][x_4, x_5] \cdots [x_{n-1}, x_n]$.

Here $(x_0, \tau) = (x_0^{h^{p-1}} \tau x_0^{h^{p-2}} \tau \cdots x_0^{h} \tau)^{\pi/(p-1)}$, where $\pi \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the element with $\pi\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{Z}_p$, and $y_1$ is an explicit element of the subgroup generated by $x_1, \sigma, \tau$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, pp. 71–72]. The general form allows any generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\mathcal{G}$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, and takes the exponents from any lift $\beta : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ of the inverse of the character $\alpha : \mathcal{G} \to (\mathbb{Z}/p^s)^\times$ through which $\mathcal{G}$ acts on $\mu_T$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, Theorem 2]. The form above is the case described in [@jannsenwingberg1982, §1.4 a)].

**What determines $G_K$.** For odd $p$, then, $G_K$ depends only on $n$, $q$, $s$ and the character $\alpha$, that is, on $n$ and the action of $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$ on $\mu_T$. The proof uses Koch's description of $G_K$ as a Demuškin formation over $\mathcal{G}$ with invariants $n$, $p^s$ and $\alpha^{-1}$, together with a uniqueness theorem: two Demuškin formations over $\mathcal{G}$ with the same invariants are isomorphic [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 72 and proof of Theorem 2]. With local class field theory, this gives the [classification of $p$-adic fields with isomorphic absolute Galois groups](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) for odd $p$. Diekert's theorem below does the same for $p = 2$ when $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is unramified.

**Examples.** The group $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ has the four generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, x_1$. For $K = \mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)$, the group $G_K$ has generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_{p-1}$, with condition 1, condition 2 for $q = p$, and the relation $x_0^\sigma = (x_0 \tau)^{\pi} x_1^p [x_1, x_2] \cdots [x_{p-2}, x_{p-1}]$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, §1.4 a)].

**With roots of unity.** If $\zeta_p \in K$, then $K(\mu_T)/K$ is unramified [@jardenritter1979, p. 2], so $\tau$ acts trivially on $\mu_T$ and $h = 1$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, §1.4 d)]. Jarden and Ritter write the data as $n$, the residue degree $f$, the number $r + d$ with $p^{r+d} = |\mu_T|$, and a Frobenius exponent $c$ with $1 \le c \le p^{r+d} - 1$ such that Frobenius sends $\zeta_{p^{r+d}}$ to $\zeta_{p^{r+d}}^c$. (Their paper calls $c$ by the letter $s$.) They state that these four numbers determine $G_K$ when $\zeta_p \in K$, and $\zeta_4 \in K$ if $p = 2$ [@jardenritter1979, p. 6]. The definitions are at [@jardenritter1979, p. 2]. This is the input to the [Jarden–Ritter theorem](jarden-ritter-theorem.md).

**The case $p = 2$.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ have degree $n$ and residue field of order $2^f$, and suppose that $q = |\mu_T| > 2$. This means that $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is unramified, the case $\sqrt{-1} \in K$ included, and it forces $n$ to be even [@diekert1984, §3, p. 166]. Diekert proves that $G_K$ is then isomorphic to the profinite group with generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_n$ and the following defining conditions [@diekert1984, Theorem 3.1]:

1. The closed normal subgroup generated by $x_0, \dots, x_n$ is a pro-$2$ group.
2. $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{2^f}$.
3. $\sigma x_0 \sigma^{-1} = (x_0\tau)^{\pi g} x_1^q [x_1, x_2] \cdots [x_{n-1}, x_n]$, where $\pi \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the element with $\pi\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{Z}_2$, and $g \in \mathbb{Z}_2$ satisfies $\varphi(\zeta) = \zeta^g$ for $\zeta \in \mu_T$ and a Frobenius automorphism $\varphi$.

This is the same description as for odd $p$ and even $n$ [@diekert1984, p. 152]. As there, the proof shows that $G_K$ and this group are Demuškin formations over $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$ with the same invariants $n$, $q$ and $\alpha$, and applies the uniqueness theorem [@diekert1984, p. 166]. The new difficulty at $p = 2$ is a symplectic module over $\mathbb{Z}/q$, which corresponds to the Kummer group with the Hilbert symbol. For odd $p$ this module is hyperbolic and determined by its reduction modulo $p$, but for $p = 2$ that fails in general [@diekert1984, p. 153]. Jenkner states Diekert's hypothesis as the condition that $K(\zeta_{2p})/K$ has no wild ramification [@jenkner1992, p. 221].

Diekert notes that Zel'venskii also gave generators and relations in this case, building on Jakovlev's work, which contains several errors [@diekert1984, p. 152]. Jarden and Ritter credit the case $\zeta_4 \in K$ to Zel'venskii [@jardenritter1979, p. 6], and Yamagata states it when the $2$-power roots of unity in $T$ have order at least $4$ [@yamagata1976, §1]. Zel'venskii's papers have not been opened for this entry.

**When $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified.** This includes $K = \mathbb{Q}_2$. Schneider writes that $G_K$ is still unknown in this case, and that there is no sensible notion of a Demuškin formation for it [@schneider2016, Introduction, p. 1]. She proposes a conjectural presentation of $G_K$ and takes a first step towards proving it [@schneider2016, Introduction, pp. 2–3]. For $K = \mathbb{Q}_2$, Roe and Turturean give a presentation with generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, x_1$, the condition that $x_0, x_1$ generate a pro-$2$ closed normal subgroup, the tame relation and one further explicit relation [@roeturturean2026, Theorem 1.2]. Their project page states that the manuscript has not yet been externally peer reviewed. They list the extension to every finite $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ as a further question [@roeturturean2026, §11].

**History.** Jakovlev gave generators and relations earlier, but with several errors that needed extensive correction. The correction was sketched only for even $n$, and gave a complicated relation as a recursively formed limit. Jakovlev also used three relations where two suffice [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 72]. Jarden and Ritter report that, by a paper of Koch, Jakovlev's system is not a system of generators and relations for $G_K$, but that Koch proves that $n, f, r + d, c$ determine $G_K$ [@jardenritter1979, p. 6, footnote 2]. Yamagata, citing Jakovlev and Zel'venskii, states that for odd $p$ the group $G_K$ is determined by $n$, $f$, $|\mu_T|$ and two numbers describing the action of the tame generators on $\mu_T$ [@yamagata1976, §1]. For odd $p$ that statement follows from the Jannsen–Wingberg theorem.

**Further results.**

- For every $p$, the group $G_K$ needs exactly $n + 2$ generators [@jardenshusterman2023, Theorem 2.1], and it is finitely presented, needing exactly $n + 1$ relations [@jardenshusterman2023, Theorems 3.4 and 4.4]. These proofs do not use the presentation above, so they include $p = 2$.
- Condition 1 is not a finite set of relations, so the presentation above is not a finite presentation as it stands [@jardenshusterman2023, Remark 5.2].
- For odd $p$, Jannsen and Wingberg use the explicit relations to show that $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ has outer automorphisms, answering a question of Jarden and Ritter [@jannsenwingberg1982, §1.4 e)]. Since $\mathbb{Q}_p$ has no field automorphisms other than the identity, these do not come from field automorphisms.
