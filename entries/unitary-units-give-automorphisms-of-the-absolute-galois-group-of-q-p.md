**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd. Use the Setting of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) with $K=\mathbb Q_p$, so $d=1$ and $q=p$. So $G=G_{\mathbb Q_p}$ is $F/(\varrho)$ with $F=P\rtimes\mathcal G$, where $\mathcal G=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle$ maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q_p^{\mathrm{tr}}/\mathbb Q_p)$ and $P$ is normally generated by $x_0,x_1$. Also $P_K$ is wild inertia, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\mathcal G]]$, $\omega:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ gives the action on $\mu_p$, $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, $\bar P=P/P^{(2)}$ and $\bar V=P_K/P_K^{(2)}$, and $u=\bar ge_h-\sigma\in\Lambda$ is as in the Lemma there.

Here $s=1$. The field $\mathbb Q_p^{\mathrm{tr}}$ contains $\mu_p$, as $\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p)/\mathbb Q_p$ is totally ramified of degree $p-1$, which is prime to $p$. It does not contain $\mu_{p^2}$, because $\mathbb Q_p(\mu_{p^2})/\mathbb Q_p$ is totally ramified of degree $p(p-1)$ ([the cyclotomic tower](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)), so it is wildly ramified. So the relator is
$$\varrho=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa\,x_1^{p}\,[x_1,y_1],$$
the $p^s$-central series of a pro-$p$ group $H$ is its $p$-central series $H^{(i)}$, and $\Lambda_1=\Lambda$, $\omega_1=\omega$ and $u_1=u$.

By part 1 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), applied with $d=1$, which is odd, $y_1\in P$ and $\bar y_1=\delta\bar x_1$ in $\bar P$ for a unique $\delta\in\Lambda$. By step (c) of its proof, $y_1$ lies in the closed subgroup $F_H$ generated by $x_1$ and the pro-$2$ subgroup $H\subseteq\mathcal G$ generated by $\sigma^{\pi_2}$ and $\tau^{\pi_2}$. Put $\delta_0=\delta-\delta^*$. By part 1 of the tame-group-algebra theorem, which has no condition on the degree, $\bar P$ is free over $\Lambda$ on $\bar x_0,\bar x_1$, and $\bar V=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0\oplus\Lambda\bar x_1$ with $\Lambda\bar x_1\cong\Lambda$ and $\rho\bar x_0=\omega(\rho)\bar x_0$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, and let $\mathcal U'$ be the set of $v\in\Lambda^\times$ with
$$v^*\delta_0v=\delta_0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\omega(v)=1.$$

1. $\mathcal U'$ is a subgroup of $\Lambda^\times$. It contains every central unit $v$ with $vv^*=1$ and $\omega(v)=1$.
2. For every $v\in\mathcal U'$ there is an automorphism $\theta_v$ of $G$ with $\theta_v(\rho)\in\rho P_K^{(2)}$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$ and $\theta_v(x_0)\in x_0P_K^{(2)}$, such that the class of $\theta_v(x_1)$ in $\bar V$ is $v\bar x_1$.
3. The automorphism $\Phi(v)$ of $G/P_K^{(2)}$ induced by $\theta_v$ fixes the image of $\mathcal G$ and acts on $\bar V$ $\Lambda$-linearly, by $\bar x_0\mapsto\bar x_0$ and $\mu\bar x_1\mapsto\mu v\bar x_1$. So it depends only on $v$, $\Phi(v)\Phi(v')=\Phi(v'v)$, and $\Phi$ is injective. If $v\ne1$, then $\theta_v$ is not inner.

**Proof of part 1.** If $v,w\in\mathcal U'$, then $(vw)^*\delta_0(vw)=w^*(v^*\delta_0v)w=\delta_0$ and $\omega(vw)=1$. Multiplying $v^*\delta_0v=\delta_0$ on the left by $(v^*)^{-1}=(v^{-1})^*$ and on the right by $v^{-1}$ gives $(v^{-1})^*\delta_0v^{-1}=\delta_0$, and $\omega(v^{-1})=1$. So $\mathcal U'$ is a subgroup. If $v$ is central, so is $v^*$, as $*$ is an anti-automorphism. Then $v^*\delta_0v=v^*v\delta_0=vv^*\delta_0$, which is $\delta_0$ if $vv^*=1$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Write $N=\bar P=P/P^{(2)}$ and $M=P^{(2)}/P^{(3)}$ additively. These are the modules $N_s$ and $M_s$ of the proof of part 2 of the tame-group-algebra theorem, with $s=1$. So they are compact $\Lambda$-modules, $M$ is central in $P/P^{(3)}$ and has exponent $p$, and the commutator induces a continuous biadditive map $b:N\times N\to M$ with $b(\rho x,\rho y)=\rho\,b(x,y)$ and $b(y,x)=-b(x,y)$. By fact (i) of the mixing theorem with $s=1$, $x\mapsto x^p$ induces a $\Lambda$-linear map $\wp:N\to M$.

Let $v\in\mathcal U'$. Choose $w\in P$ with $\bar w=(v-1)\bar x_1$, and put $y=x_1w$, so that $\bar y=v\bar x_1$. Let $\psi_0$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$, fixes $x_0$ and maps $x_1$ to $y$.

*(a) The image of $y_1$.* As $\psi_0$ fixes $\mathcal G$ and maps $x_0,x_1$ into the normal subgroup $P$, it maps $P$ into $P$, hence $P^{(2)}$ into $P^{(2)}$. It induces an endomorphism of $N$ that commutes with $\mathcal G$, hence is $\Lambda$-linear by continuity, and maps $\bar x_0\mapsto\bar x_0$ and $\bar x_1\mapsto v\bar x_1$. As $\bar y_1=\delta\bar x_1$, the class of $\psi_0(y_1)$ is $\delta v\bar x_1$.

*(b) The defect.* Put $A=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa$, which $\psi_0$ fixes, as it is built from $x_0$ and $\mathcal G$. So $\psi_0(\varrho)=A\,y^p\,[y,\psi_0(y_1)]$. The elements $x_1^p$, $y^p$, $[x_1,y_1]$ and $[y,\psi_0(y_1)]$ lie in $P^{(2)}$, with classes $\wp(\bar x_1)$, $\wp(v\bar x_1)=v\wp(\bar x_1)$, $b(\bar x_1,\delta\bar x_1)$ and $b(v\bar x_1,\delta v\bar x_1)$ in $M$. As $M$ is central in $P/P^{(3)}$ and all factors lie in $P$,
$$\psi_0(\varrho)\equiv\varrho\cdot D\pmod{P^{(3)}},\qquad D=m_1+m_2,$$
with $m_1=(v-1)\wp(\bar x_1)$ and $m_2=b(v\bar x_1,\delta v\bar x_1)-b(\bar x_1,\delta\bar x_1)$. Both lie in $uM$:

- As $\omega(v-1)=0$, $v-1\in\ker\omega=u\Lambda$ by Lemma (b) of the tame-group-algebra entry with $r=1$. So $m_1\in u\Lambda M=uM$.
- By Lemma (c) there, with $\lambda=v$ and $\mu=\delta v$, $m_2\equiv b(\bar x_1,c\bar x_1)$ modulo $uM$, where $c=v^*\delta v-\delta$. Now $c-c^*=v^*\delta v-v^*\delta^*v-\delta+\delta^*=v^*\delta_0v-\delta_0=0$. For $c=c^*$, antisymmetry and Lemma (c) give $b(\bar x_1,c\bar x_1)=-b(c\bar x_1,\bar x_1)\equiv-b(\bar x_1,c^*\bar x_1)=-b(\bar x_1,c\bar x_1)$ modulo $uM$. So $2b(\bar x_1,c\bar x_1)\in uM$. As $M$ has odd exponent $p$ and $uM$ is a subgroup, $b(\bar x_1,c\bar x_1)=\tfrac{p+1}2\cdot2b(\bar x_1,c\bar x_1)\in uM$, and $m_2\in uM$.

*(c) The correction.* Choose $w_0\in M$ with $uw_0=-D$ and $z\in P^{(2)}$ with class $w_0$, and let $\psi$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that agrees with $\psi_0$ except that $\psi(x_0)=x_0z$. Since $\psi$ and $\psi_0$ agree on $x_1$ and on $\mathcal G$, they agree on $F_H$, so $\psi(y_1)=\psi_0(y_1)$. By the Computation of the tame-group-algebra entry, with $r=1$, $N=P^{(3)}$, $A=M$, $X=x_0P^{(3)}$ and $a=w_0$, $\psi(A)\equiv A\cdot uw_0$ modulo $P^{(3)}$. So
$$\psi(\varrho)\equiv\varrho\cdot(uw_0+D)=\varrho\pmod{P^{(3)}}.$$

*(d) The lift.* The paragraph *A lifting step* of the mixing theorem applies to $\psi$. It needs that $\psi$ is the identity on $\mathcal G$, maps each $x_i$ into $P$, satisfies $\psi(\varrho)\in\varrho P^{p,3,s}$, and that the classes of the $\psi(x_i)$ generate $\bar P$ as a $\Lambda$-module. The last holds here, as the classes are $\bar x_0$ and $v\bar x_1$, and $v$ is a unit. That paragraph is the last two steps of the proof of part 2 of the tame-group-algebra theorem: $\psi$ induces an automorphism of $G/P_K^{(3)}$ that is the identity on $\Gamma$, and [Wingberg's lifting theorem](wingberg-lifting-for-the-p-s-central-series-of-wild-inertia.md), here with $s=1$, gives $\theta_v\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)$, inducing the identity on $\Gamma$, that agrees with it modulo $P_K^{(2)}$. Reading the generators in $G$: $\theta_v(\rho)\in\rho P_K^{(2)}$, $\theta_v(x_0)\in x_0zP_K^{(2)}=x_0P_K^{(2)}$, and $\theta_v(x_1)\in yP_K^{(2)}$, whose class in $\bar V$ is the image of $v\bar x_1$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** $G/P_K^{(2)}$ is generated by $\bar V$ and the image of $\mathcal G$, which $\Phi(v)$ fixes. So the restriction of $\Phi(v)$ to $\bar V$ commutes with $\mathcal G$ and is $\Lambda$-linear, and by part 2 it fixes $\bar x_0$ and maps $\bar x_1\mapsto v\bar x_1$. As $\bar V=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0+\Lambda\bar x_1$, this determines $\Phi(v)$. Both $\Phi(v)\Phi(v')$ and $\Phi(v'v)$ fix $\mathcal G$ and $\bar x_0$, and $\Phi(v)\Phi(v')(\bar x_1)=\Phi(v)(v'\bar x_1)=v'v\bar x_1$, so they agree. If $\Phi(v)=1$, then $(v-1)\bar x_1=0$, so $v=1$ as $\Lambda\bar x_1\cong\Lambda$. Suppose $\theta_v=\mathrm{inn}(g)$. As $\theta_v$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$, the image of $g$ in $\Gamma$ is central, hence trivial, because $\Gamma$ has trivial centre (part 3 of the tame-group-algebra theorem). So $g\in P_K$, conjugation by $g$ is trivial on the abelian group $\bar V$, and $\Phi(v)$ is trivial on $\bar V$. So $v=1$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The withdrawn argument.* Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg construct an outer automorphism of $G_{\mathbb Q_p}$ from the central unit $\varepsilon=1-2E$. They take the endomorphism $\psi$ of $F$ that fixes $\sigma,\tau,x_0$ and sends $x_1$ to $x_1^\varepsilon$. They argue that $\psi$ induces an automorphism $\psi_K$ of $G_K(p)/G_K(p)^3$ for every finite tamely ramified Galois $K/\mathbb Q_p$, using a congruence for the Demuškin relation of $G_K(p)$ modulo $F_K(p)^3$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 421–423]. Their errata, crediting S. Ishii, say that the construction contains a mistake: this $\psi$ does not induce the automorphisms $\psi_K$, the argument is incomplete, and Ishii showed that it cannot be the case [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2026errata, item for pp. 422–423]. Ishii's own account was not found.
- *What changes here.* The proof above does not use the groups $G_K(p)$ or that congruence. It works with the single relator $\varrho$ modulo the third $p$-central term $P^{(3)}$ of the free group $P$, and it also moves $x_0$, by $z\in P^{(2)}$, to absorb the defect $D\in uM$. This is the method of part 2 of the tame-group-algebra theorem, where $x_1$ is not moved. Nothing is claimed here about $D$ for their $\psi$.
- *Their conclusion survives.* Their $\varepsilon$ lies in $\mathcal U'$. They show that $E$ is a central idempotent [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 421–422], so $\varepsilon$ is central with $\varepsilon^2=1$. Also $E^*=E$: at each level, applying $*$ to $\frac1e\sum_i\omega(\tau)^{-i}\tau^{2i}$ gives $\frac1e\sum_i\omega(\tau)^{i}\tau^{-2i}$, the same sum reindexed by $i\mapsto-i$, as $\omega(\tau)^e=1$. So $\varepsilon\varepsilon^*=1$. Also $\omega(E)=\frac1e\sum_i\omega(\tau)^i=0$, as $\omega(\tau)\ne1$ and $\omega(\tau)^e=1$, so $\omega(\varepsilon)=1$. So part 2 gives an automorphism acting by $\varepsilon$ on $\Lambda\bar x_1$, which is not inner by part 3 if $\varepsilon\ne1$, and $E\ne0$ as shown in part 1 (b) of [the involution entry](involutive-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md). Here $E$ is the limit, over tamely ramified levels whose ramification index $e$ is divisible by $p-1$, of $\frac1e\sum_{i<e}\omega(\tau)^{-i}\tau^{2i}$.
- *The condition $\omega(v)=1$.* It is what makes the change of $x_1^{p}$ absorbable by $x_0$. Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg instead require $\varepsilon\lambda_K=\lambda_K$ at every level [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423], and the group $\mathcal U$ of [nonabelian finite subgroups](nonabelian-finite-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) asks the same of $v$. That condition implies $\omega(v)=1$, because $\omega(\lambda_K)=1$, so $\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal U'$.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$ and $K=\mathbb Q_p$ only. For $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$, the automorphisms of the tame-group-algebra and mixing entries are built in the same way, from the single relator modulo $P^{p,3,s}$, and do not use the computation of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg. Degree $2$ is not treated here. Everything is proved from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened, except the pages of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg and the errata cited in these remarks. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
