**Setting.** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$. A *Deligne–Mumford curve* $\mathcal X$ over $K$ is an irreducible, separated, smooth, quasi-compact and geometrically connected Deligne–Mumford stack of dimension one over $K$. Its stabilizer at the generic point is a finite group $I_\eta$, the *generic inertia*. Removing it gives the *rigidification* $\mathcal X \to \mathcal X_{\mathrm{rig}}$, a gerbe banded by $I_\eta$, and $\mathcal X$ has a coarse space $\mathcal X_{\mathrm{coarse}}$, a smooth curve [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, §3 introduction, p. 20, and Proposition 3.2, p. 21]. A *stacky curve* is one with a dense open subscheme, so that $\mathcal X = \mathcal X_{\mathrm{rig}}$. These are Mochizuki's orbicurves [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, p. 21].

Let $\mathcal X$ be a stacky curve, of genus $g$ with $r$ cusps and with stabilizers of orders $n_1, \dots, n_k \ge 2$ at its orbifold points. Its geometric fundamental group $\Delta_{\mathcal X}$ is the *profinite Fenchel group* with presentation
$$\Big\langle \alpha_1, \beta_1, \dots, \alpha_g, \beta_g, \gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_r, \delta_1, \dots, \delta_k \;\Big|\; \prod_{i=1}^g [\alpha_i, \beta_i] \prod_{j=1}^r \gamma_j \prod_{l=1}^k \delta_l = 1,\ \delta_1^{n_1} = \dots = \delta_k^{n_k} = 1 \Big\rangle$$
[@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Definition 2.1, p. 6, and Proposition 3.4, p. 21]. It is *hyperbolic* if $\chi = 2 - 2g - r - \sum_i (1 - 1/n_i) < 0$, and *affine* if $r \ge 1$. A Deligne–Mumford curve is hyperbolic or affine exactly when its rigidification is [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Definition 3.1, pp. 20–21, and pp. 21–22]. For example, the projective line with three orbifold points of orders $2, 3, 7$ is hyperbolic, although its coarse space is not.

Write $\Pi_{\mathcal X}$ for the étale fundamental group, an extension of $G_K$ by $\Delta_{\mathcal X}$. There is an exact sequence $1 \to I_\eta \to \Pi_{\mathcal X} \to \Pi_{\mathcal X_{\mathrm{rig}}} \to 1$, and similarly for geometric fundamental groups [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 3.6, p. 22]. Put $\Pi_{\mathcal X}^{\Delta - m} = \Pi_{\mathcal X}/\Delta_{\mathcal X}^{(m)}$, where $\Delta^{(m)}$ is the $m$-th closed derived subgroup [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, (3.4), p. 23].

**Theorem 1** (number fields). Let $K$ be a number field, and let $\mathcal X, \mathcal Y$ be Deligne–Mumford curves over $K$ with $\mathcal X$ hyperbolic. Then $\mathcal X \cong \mathcal Y$ over $K$ if and only if $\Pi_{\mathcal X} \cong \Pi_{\mathcal Y}$ over $G_K$ [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Theorem 3.18, p. 28]. For the rigidifications more is true: every isomorphism $\Pi_{\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{rig}}} \to \Pi_{\mathcal X_{\mathrm{rig}}}$ over $G_K$ comes from a unique isomorphism $\mathcal Y_{\mathrm{rig}} \to \mathcal X_{\mathrm{rig}}$ [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 3.10 and its proof, pp. 24–25].

The proof has three steps.

1. *Inertia is group-theoretic.* The stack inertia groups at closed points are the maximal finite nontrivial closed subgroups of $\Delta_{\mathcal X}$, and $I_\eta$ is its unique maximal finite closed normal subgroup [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 3.10 (i), p. 24]. So an isomorphism $\Pi_{\mathcal X} \to \Pi_{\mathcal Y}$ over $G_K$ induces one of the rigidified groups.
2. *Rigidified curves.* Hoshi proved that if $\mathcal X_1, \mathcal X_2$ are hyperbolic orbicurves over generalized sub-$p$-adic fields, then every isomorphism $\Pi_{\mathcal X_1} \to \Pi_{\mathcal X_2}$ that maps $\Delta_{\mathcal X_1}$ onto $\Delta_{\mathcal X_2}$ and induces on absolute Galois groups the map given by an isomorphism of algebraic closures that restricts to one of the base fields comes from a unique isomorphism of stacks [@hoshi2022quasitripods, Theorem 4.5, p. 21]. His orbicurves have trivial generic inertia [@hoshi2022quasitripods, Definition 3.5, p. 17]. Number fields are generalized sub-$p$-adic, and over them the condition on the base field is automatic for isomorphisms over $G_K$ [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, p. 25].
3. *The gerbe.* There is an isomorphism $\mathcal X \cong [U/H]$ over $\mathcal X_{\mathrm{rig}}$, for a scheme $U$ and a finite quotient $H = \Pi_{\mathcal X}/\pi_1(U)$, both built from the exact sequence above [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 3.15, p. 26]. So when the induced isomorphism of rigidifications is geometric, $\mathcal X \cong \mathcal Y$ over it [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Corollary 3.16, p. 27].

Hoshi's theorem is stated over all generalized sub-$p$-adic fields, while Theorem 1 is stated only over number fields. This entry does not check whether the rest of the proof needs number fields.

**Theorem 2** (geometrically solvable quotients). Let $K$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, and let $\mathcal X, \mathcal X'$ be stacky curves over $K$ with $\mathcal X$ affine and hyperbolic, of type $(g, r)$ and with orbifold orders $n_1, \dots, n_k$. Suppose that $(g, r) \ne (0, 1)$, or that $n_1 \cdots n_k \ge 3\,\mathrm{lcm}(n_1, \dots, n_k)$. Then
$$\mathcal X \cong \mathcal X' \text{ over } K \iff \Pi_{\mathcal X}^{\Delta - 5} \cong \Pi_{\mathcal X'}^{\Delta - 5} \text{ over } G_K.$$
More precisely, for every $j \ge 1$, an isomorphism $\Pi_{\mathcal X}^{\Delta - j} \to \Pi_{\mathcal X'}^{\Delta - j}$ over $G_K$ that is induced by an isomorphism of the $(j+4)$-step quotients comes from an isomorphism $\mathcal X \to \mathcal X'$ and a compatible isomorphism of the maximal geometrically $j$-step solvable coverings. With $j \ge 2$ this holds in type $(0,1)$ without the condition, so the 6-step form holds for all affine hyperbolic stacky curves [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Theorem 3.24, p. 30]. The source also assumes that $\Delta_{\mathcal X}$ is not perfect. That is automatic here, since a nontrivial profinite Fenchel group is perfect only if $(g, r) = (0, 0)$ [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 2.15, p. 13].

The proof chooses torsion-free open normal subgroups of $\Pi_{\mathcal X}^{\Delta - (j+4)}$ that contain the image of $\Delta^{(j)}$. They form a cofinal family, and the corresponding coverings are affine hyperbolic curves with $r \ge 3$ and type other than $(0,3)$ and $(0,4)$. Yamaguchi's 4-step theorem gives isomorphisms of these coverings, and they glue [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Lemma 3.23 and proof of Theorem 3.24, pp. 30–31].

**Failure with generic inertia.** The source records that no $m$-step form holds for all Deligne–Mumford curves [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Theorem 1.2, p. 3, and §3.3.3, p. 32]. The simplest instance is checked here. Suppose $I_\eta$ is perfect and nontrivial. Then $I_\eta = I_\eta^{(m)} \subseteq \Delta_{\mathcal X}^{(m)}$ for every $m$. A surjection of profinite groups maps closed derived subgroups onto closed derived subgroups, so the exact sequence above gives $\Pi_{\mathcal X}^{\Delta - m} \cong \Pi_{\mathcal X_{\mathrm{rig}}}^{\Delta - m}$ over $G_K$ for every $m$. But $\mathcal X \not\cong \mathcal X_{\mathrm{rig}}$, since only one of them has trivial generic inertia. An example is $\mathcal X = X_0 \times BA_5$, for a hyperbolic curve $X_0$ over $K$ and the constant alternating group $A_5$. Its generic inertia is $A_5$, and its rigidification is $X_0$.

**Profinite Fenchel groups.** Theorem 2 rests on the following group theory.

- Every non-perfect profinite Fenchel group $\Delta$ has a torsion-free open characteristic subgroup $N$ with $\Delta/N$ solvable of derived length at most $3$. Derived length at most $2$ is possible exactly when $(g, r) \ne (0, 0)$, or when $(g, r) = (0, 0)$ and $\Delta^{(1)}$ satisfies the *abelian-period condition*. For $k \ge 2$ orbifold points, this condition says that each $n_i$ divides $\mathrm{lcm}(n_j : j \ne i)$. By convention it holds for $k = 0$ and fails for $k = 1$ [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Definition 2.10, p. 10, and Theorem 2.18, p. 14].
- For non-perfect $\Delta$, hyperbolicity holds exactly when $\Delta^3 = \Delta/\Delta^{(3)}$ has derived length $3$ and is not isomorphic to the symmetric group $S_4$. For hyperbolic $\Delta$, affineness is also detected by $\Delta^3$, through an equation in the Chen ranks of a subgroup [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 2.29, p. 19]. The quotient $\Delta^2$ does not detect hyperbolicity [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, p. 19].

*Example: derived length 3 is needed.* Proved here. Let $\Delta$ have signature $(0, 0; \{2, 8, 3\})$, so $\chi = 2 - (1/2 + 7/8 + 2/3) = -1/24$. Write $x, z, y$ for the images of $\delta_1, \delta_2, \delta_3$ in a finite quotient $G = \Delta/N$, so that $xzy = 1$. If $N$ is torsion-free, then $x, y, z$ have orders $2, 3, 8$, because each $\delta_l^j$ with $0 < j < n_l$ is a nontrivial torsion element: in a hyperbolic profinite Fenchel group, $\delta_l$ has order exactly $n_l$ [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 2.6 and its proof, pp. 7–8].

1. *No metabelian quotient.* Suppose $G$ is metabelian, with $x, y, z$ of orders $2, 3, 8$. The abelianization $\Delta^{\mathrm{ab}} = \langle a, b \mid 2a = 3b = 8(a + b) = 0 \rangle$ is $\mathbb{Z}/2$: the element $a + b$ has order dividing $6$ and $8$, so $2b = 0$, and with $3b = 0$ this gives $b = 0$. So $y$ lies in the abelian normal subgroup $G'$, and so does $\sigma(y) = xyx^{-1}$. As $x^2 = 1$, we get $(yx)^2 = y\sigma(y)$, a product of two commuting elements of order dividing $3$. So $z^{-6} = (yx)^6 = 1$, contradicting that $z$ has order $8$. Hence no torsion-free open normal subgroup has metabelian quotient. This agrees with Theorem 2.18, since $\Delta^{(1)}$ has signature $(0, 0; \{4, 3, 3\})$ by item 2, and $4$ does not divide $\mathrm{lcm}(3, 3)$.
2. *A quotient of derived length 3.* The source's construction gives an explicit one, with signatures computed by its Proposition 2.3 [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 2.3, pp. 6–7, and proof of Theorem 2.18, pp. 14–15]. By item 1, $M = \Delta^{(1)}$ is the kernel of the surjection $\Delta \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ sending $\delta_1, \delta_2$ to $1$ and $\delta_3$ to $0$. It has index $2$ and signature $(0, 0; \{4, 3, 3\})$. Since $M^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$, with the generator of order $4$ mapping to $0$, the group $M^{(1)}$ has index $3$ and signature $(0, 0; \{4, 4, 4\})$. Its abelianization is $(\mathbb{Z}/4)^3/\langle(1,1,1)\rangle \cong (\mathbb{Z}/4)^2$, and each generator maps to an element of order $4$. So the abelian-period condition holds, and $N = M^{(2)}$ is torsion-free [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 2.12 (i), p. 10]. As $M = \Delta^{(1)}$, we have $N = \Delta^{(3)}$, a normal subgroup. Thus the maximal 3-step solvable quotient $\Delta^3 = \Delta/N$ has order $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 16 = 96$, and its derived length is exactly $3$, by item 1. Since $\chi(N) = 96 \cdot (-1/24) = -4$, $N$ is the profinite fundamental group of a closed surface of genus $3$.

**A caveat in the source.** Checked here. Proposition 2.6 says that the periods, the Euler characteristic and affineness are isomorphism invariants of every nontrivial profinite Fenchel group. Its proof sets aside only the trivial groups of the source's Table 1 [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Proposition 2.6 and its proof, pp. 7–8]. The statement fails for some other groups that are not hyperbolic: $\mathbb{Z}/2$ has signatures $(0, 0; \{2, 2\})$ and $(0, 1; \{2\})$, both listed in the source's Table 1 [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, Table 1, p. 8]. They differ in all three invariants: the periods are $\{2, 2\}$ and $\{2\}$, the Euler characteristics are $1$ and $1/2$, and only the second is affine. Theorems 1 and 2 concern hyperbolic curves.

**Status.** These are results of a May 2026 RIMS preprint. This entry checks the group-theoretic example and the counterexample above. It does not independently check the gerbe construction of Proposition 3.15, Corollary 3.16, or the gluing step in the proof of Theorem 3.24, which relies on compatibility results of Yamaguchi [@collasphilipyamaguchi2026, p. 31].
