**Conjecture** (Shafarevich). Let $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ be the maximal abelian extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, which by the Kronecker–Weber theorem is $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n : n \ge 1)$. Then $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ is a free profinite group [@barysorokerfehm2012, Conjecture 3.1]. Harbater gives the same statement as the original form of the conjecture [@harbater2009, §1, p. 1]. Bary-Soroker and Fehm cite it through a survey of Dèbes and Deschamps, and Shafarevich's own statement has not been seen for this entry.

*The rank.* This is checked here. If $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ is free, its rank is countably infinite. A free profinite group on a set $S$ that converges to $1$ has, for each $s \in S$, a surjection onto $\mathbb{Z}/2$ sending $s$ to $1$ and the rest of $S$ to $0$ [@harbater2009, §2, p. 3], and these have distinct kernels. As $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is countable, it has only countably many finite extensions in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, so $S$ is countable. On the other hand, let $a$ be a prime $\ell$ or a quotient $\ell/\ell'$ of distinct primes. Then $x^4 - a$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, by Eisenstein's criterion at $\ell$ applied to $x^4 - \ell$ or to $y^4 - \ell\ell'^3$, where $y = \ell' x$. If it had a root $c \in \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$, then $\mathbb{Q}(c)$ would be abelian, hence normal, of degree $4$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. It would contain all four roots $\pm r, \pm ir$, with $r$ real, and so $\mathbb{Q}(r, i)$, which has degree $8$. So $x^4 - a$ has no root in $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Now $\sqrt{\ell} \in \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$, so each $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}(\sqrt[4]{\ell})$ is quadratic over $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$. If two of them coincided, for $\ell \ne \ell'$, Kummer theory would give $\sqrt{\ell}/\sqrt{\ell'} = c^2$ with $c \in \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and $c$ would be a root of $x^4 - \ell/\ell'$. So $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ has infinitely many quadratic extensions. A profinite group generated by $e$ elements has at most $2^e$ homomorphisms to $\mathbb{Z}/2$, so $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ is not finitely generated.

**Evidence.**

The [PAC realization characterization](projective-profinite-groups-and-pac-fields.md) explains projectivity and gives a direct example showing that it does not imply freeness as a profinite group.

- Iwasawa proved that the maximal prosolvable quotient of $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ is a free prosolvable group, and Tate that $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ has cohomological dimension $1$ [@barysorokerfehm2012, §3.1, p. 4]. So $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ is projective, meaning that every finite embedding problem for it has a weak solution [@harbater2009, §2, p. 3]. Iwasawa's 1953 paper in the Annals has not been opened for this entry.
- The [geometric Shafarevich conjecture](geometric-shafarevich-conjecture.md), the analogue for global function fields, is a theorem of Harbater and Pop [@barysorokerfehm2012, §3.1, p. 4].

$G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is an extension of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb{Q}) \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$ by $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$, so Bary-Soroker and Fehm see a proof as a step towards understanding $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@barysorokerfehm2012, §3.1, p. 4].

**A route through ampleness.** Call a field ample, or large, if every smooth curve over it with a rational point has infinitely many rational points [@harbater2009, §3, p. 10]. Bary-Soroker and Fehm ask whether $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is ample, and note that by Pop's results a positive answer would prove the conjecture [@barysorokerfehm2012, §3.1, Question I]. The deduction runs as follows. By Pop, every finite split embedding problem over a large Hilbertian field has a proper solution. If the absolute Galois group is moreover projective, it is then $\omega$-free, and for a countable field Iwasawa's criterion makes it free of countable rank [@harbater2009, §3, p. 10]. The field $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is countable and has projective absolute Galois group. It is Hilbertian, as an abelian extension of the Hilbertian field $\mathbb{Q}$ [@harbater2009, proof of Proposition 3.2, p. 11]. That $\mathbb{Q}$ is Hilbertian is Hilbert's irreducibility theorem, which is standard and not checked in a source here.

Pop's theorem applies in the same way to countable Hilbertian PAC fields, which are large with projective absolute Galois group [@harbater2009, proof of Proposition 3.2, p. 11]. But Frey proved that $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is not PAC [@barysorokerfehm2012, §3.2]. Ampleness passes to algebraic extensions [@barysorokerfehm2012, Proposition 2.2], yet it is not even known whether the maximal solvable extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ is ample [@barysorokerfehm2012, §3.2].

**What a proof would say.** This is checked here.

- *For anabelian geometry.* Free profinite groups of the same rank are isomorphic [@harbater2009, §2, p. 3]. With the rank computed above and the [geometric Shafarevich conjecture](geometric-shafarevich-conjecture.md), the conjecture would give $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}} \cong G_{F^{\mathrm{ab}}} \cong G_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p(t)}$ for every prime $p$ and every global function field $F$. So $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ would determine neither the field nor its characteristic, while $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ determines $\mathbb{Q}$ by the [Neukirch–Uchida theorem](neukirch-uchida-theorem.md). Everything $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ records about $\mathbb{Q}$ would then lie in how the free group $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ sits inside $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. For global function fields this is already known unconditionally.
- *For inverse Galois theory.* A free profinite group of countably infinite rank maps onto every finite group: send finitely many generators to generators of the group and the rest to $1$. So the conjecture implies that every finite group is a Galois group over $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$. This would also follow from the inverse Galois problem over $\mathbb{Q}$, because the inverse Galois property passes from a field to its maximal abelian extension [@harbater2009, Proposition 3.1(a)].

**Variants.** Shafarevich's conjecture has been generalized to the statement that for every global field $K$ the absolute Galois group of the maximal cyclotomic extension of $K$ is free, and further to the same for the maximal abelian extension $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Both are proved for function fields and open for number fields [@harbater2009, §1, p. 1]. In dimension two, let $K = k(x, y)$ with $k$ algebraically closed. Harbater notes that $G_{K^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ is free exactly when it is projective and quasi-free. Projectivity would follow if every Sylow $\ell$-subgroup of $G_{K^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ were free pro-$\ell$, a condition that would imply Bogomolov's conjecture that the commutator subgroup of every Sylow $\ell$-subgroup of $G_K$ is free pro-$\ell$. Bogomolov's conjecture and the quasi-freeness of $G_{K^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ are open, even for $K = \mathbb{C}(x, y)$. Pop has proposed that $G_{K^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ is free for every function field $K$ over an algebraically closed field, or over a field containing all roots of unity [@harbater2009, §1, p. 2]. The local analogue holds: for $k$ separably closed, the maximal abelian extension of $k((x, y))$ has free absolute Galois group, of rank equal to its cardinality [@harbater2009, Theorem 4.5(b)].

**Status.** Open, as reported by Harbater [@harbater2009, §1, p. 1] and by Bary-Soroker and Fehm [@barysorokerfehm2012, §3.1]. A brief literature search in November 2026 found no later proof, but it was not exhaustive.
