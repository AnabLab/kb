**Setting.** Let $X$ be a compact Riemann surface of genus $g$, let $p \in X$, and let $J_p \subset \mathbb{Z}\pi_1(X,p)$ be the augmentation ideal. Hain put a mixed Hodge structure on each $J_p/J_p^{s+1}$, using Chen's theorem that integration identifies $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(J_p/J_p^{s+1}, \mathbb{C})$ with the closed iterated integrals of length at most $s$ [@kaenders2001, §1, (1.1), p. 2]. The weight filtration is the $J_p$-adic filtration, $W_{-l} = J_p^l/J_p^{s+1}$, and $J_p/J_p^2 = H_1(X)$ is pure of weight $-1$ [@kaenders2001, §1, p. 2]. The Hodge filtration comes from that of differential forms: $\int w_1 \cdots w_r$ with $w_j \in F^{p_j}$ lies in $F^{p_1 + \dots + p_r}$ [@hain2002, §9.2, p. 21].

Write $H^1 = H^1(X)$. Multiplication maps $J_p/J_p^2 \otimes J_p/J_p^2$ onto $J_p^2/J_p^3$, and the dual map identifies $(J_p^2/J_p^3)^*$ with $K = \ker(\cup : H^1 \otimes H^1 \to H^2(X))$, a Hodge structure of weight $2$ [@kaenders2001, §1, p. 2]. Dualizing $0 \to J_p^2/J_p^3 \to J_p/J_p^3 \to J_p/J_p^2 \to 0$ therefore gives an extension of mixed Hodge structures
$$0 \to H^1 \to (J_p/J_p^3)^* \to K \to 0,$$
whose class is written $m_p \in \mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{MHS}}(K, H^1)$ [@kaenders2001, §1, (1.2) and p. 3].

**Theorem (Hain, Pulte).** Let $g \ge 2$. Part 1 fails for $g = 1$, as shown below.

1. The map $\mathrm{Pic}^0(X) \to \mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{MHS}}(K, H^1)$ that takes the class of $p - p'$ to $m_p - m_{p'}$ is well defined and injective [@kaenders2001, Theorem 2.1, p. 7].
2. *Pointed Torelli theorem.* Let $(Y, r)$ be another pointed compact Riemann surface. If a ring isomorphism $\mathbb{Z}\pi_1(X,p)/J_p^3 \to \mathbb{Z}\pi_1(Y,r)/J_r^3$ is an isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures, then there is a biholomorphism $f : X \to Y$ with $f(p) \in a_Y(r)$ [@kaenders2001, Theorem 2.3, p. 7].

Here $(X,p) \cong (X,p')$ means that an automorphism of $X$ maps $p$ to $p'$, and the *set of alternatives* for $p$ is
$$a_X(p) = \{p\} \cup \{p' \in X : m_{p'} = -m_p \text{ and } (X,p) \not\cong (X,p')\}.$$
By part 1, $a_X(p)$ has at most two points, and up to automorphisms of $X$ there is at most one pair of distinct points $p, p'$ with $a_X(p) = \{p, p'\} = a_X(p')$ [@kaenders2001, Corollary 2.2, p. 7]. The sign comes from the classical Torelli theorem, which writes an isomorphism of polarized Hodge structures $H^1(Y) \to H^1(X)$ as $\pm f^*$ for a biholomorphism $f : X \to Y$, with no control over the sign [@kaenders2001, proof of Theorem 2.8, p. 8]. Kaenders writes out the argument for the punctured case below.

Kaenders credits both parts to Hain, *The geometry of the mixed Hodge structure on the fundamental group* (Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 46, Part 2, 1987), and to Pulte, *The fundamental group of a Riemann surface: mixed Hodge structures and algebraic cycles* (Duke Math. J. 57, 1988) [@kaenders2001, Introduction, p. 1, and §2.1, p. 7]. Neither paper was opened for this entry. Hain reports that Pulte reworked Harris's work on the harmonic volume in the language of mixed Hodge theory [@hain2002, §10.4, p. 24]. The resulting Harris–Pulte theorem says that the Abel–Jacobi image of the cycle $C_p - C_p^-$ in the Jacobian of $X$ is the image of $2m_p$ under an injective map. See [the Harris–Pulte theorem](harris-pulte-theorem.md), which also records Hain and Matsumoto's $\ell$-adic version for curves over subfields of $\mathbb{C}$, where the Galois action on $\pi_1$ replaces the mixed Hodge structure.

**The alternatives.** Kaenders reports that no pointed compact Riemann surface with $|a_X(p)| = 2$ was known. Pulte showed that such an $(X,p)$ must have zero harmonic volume, and that if $a_X(p) = \{p, p'\} = a_X(p')$, then $(g - 1)(p + p') - K_X = 0$ in $\mathrm{Pic}^0(X)$, where $K_X$ is a canonical divisor. B. Harris proved that a generic curve has nonzero harmonic volume [@kaenders2001, Remark 2.4, p. 7].

*Hyperelliptic curves.* If $X$ is hyperelliptic, then $a_X(p) = \{p\}$ for every $p$, as Kaenders notes [@kaenders2001, Remark 2.4, p. 7]. In detail, as checked here: let $\iota$ be the hyperelliptic involution. Since $X/\iota \cong \mathbb{P}^1$, the invariants of $\iota^*$ on $H^1(X, \mathbb{Q})$ are $H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, \mathbb{Q}) = 0$, so $\iota^* = -1$ on $H^1$, and $\iota^* \otimes \iota^* = 1$ on $K$. By functoriality, $\iota : (X, p) \to (X, \iota(p))$ gives an isomorphism between the extensions for $\iota(p)$ and for $p$ that is $-1$ on $H^1$ and the identity on $K$, so $m_{\iota(p)} = -m_p$. If $m_{p'} = -m_p$, then $m_{p'} - m_{\iota(p)} = 0$, so $p' - \iota(p)$ is linearly equivalent to $0$ by part 1, and $p' = \iota(p)$ because distinct points of a curve of positive genus are not linearly equivalent. As $(X, p) \cong (X, \iota(p))$, no alternative remains.

*Genus $1$.* Kaenders states part 1 without a genus hypothesis, but it fails for $g = 1$, as checked here. For an elliptic curve $E$ and $a \in E$, translation by $a$ is a biholomorphism $(E, p) \to (E, p + a)$ that is homotopic to the identity, so it acts trivially on $H^1$ and on $K$. By functoriality, $m_{p+a} = m_p$. So the map of part 1 is zero, while $\mathrm{Pic}^0(E) \cong E$ is not. Part 2 holds trivially for $g = 1$: the Hodge structure $H^1$ determines $E$, and translations act transitively on base points. For $g = 0$ there is nothing to prove.

**Punctured curves (Kaenders).** Let $q \in X$ and $U = X \setminus \{q\}$, with base point $p \in U$. The inclusion $U \subset X$ identifies $H_1(U)$ with $H_1(X)$, and now $(J_p^2/J_p^3)^* \cong H^1 \otimes H^1$ [@kaenders2001, §1, p. 2]. So the mixed Hodge structure on $\mathbb{Z}\pi_1(U,p)/J_p^3$ gives an extension class $m_{pq} \in \mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{MHS}}(H^1 \otimes H^1, H^1)$, which maps to $m_p$ under restriction to $K$ [@kaenders2001, §1, (1.2), pp. 2–3].

- *The extra part.* Let $Q \subset H^1 \otimes H^1$ be the rank-one sublattice orthogonal to $K$ for the form $b(x_1 \otimes x_2, y_1 \otimes y_2) = (x_1 \cup y_2)(y_1 \cup x_2)$. It is a Hodge structure isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}(-1)$, and $K_{\mathbb{Q}} \oplus Q_{\mathbb{Q}} = H^1_{\mathbb{Q}} \otimes H^1_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Restricting $m_{pq}$ to $Q$ gives a class $k_{pq} \in \mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{MHS}}(Q, H^1)$, and Carlson's isomorphism $\Psi : \mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{MHS}}(Q, H^1) \to \mathrm{Pic}^0(X)$ gives
$$\Psi(k_{pq}) = 2g\,q - 2p - K_X \in \mathrm{Pic}^0(X)$$
[@kaenders2001, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, p. 3]. The proof expresses both sides through iterated integrals and Riemann's constant [@kaenders2001, Theorem 1.3, p. 3, and §§1.2–1.4, pp. 4–6]. Kaenders also notes that $k_{pp}$ makes sense, with $\Psi(k_{pq}) = 2g(q - p) + \Psi(k_{pp})$ [@kaenders2001, §1.4, p. 6], so $\Psi(k_{pp}) = (2g - 2)p - K_X$.
- *Injectivity.* For fixed $p$, the map $\mathrm{Pic}^0(X) \to \mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{MHS}}(H^1 \otimes H^1, H^1)$ taking the class of $q - q'$ to $m_{pq} - m_{pq'}$ is well defined and injective [@kaenders2001, Theorem 2.5, p. 7]. The map $(p, q) \mapsto m_{pq}$ on $(X \times X) \setminus \Delta$ is injective and extends to the diagonal [@kaenders2001, Proposition 2.6, p. 7]. Its proof uses part 1 above to recover $p$ [@kaenders2001, proof of Proposition 2.6, p. 8].
- *Punctured pointed Torelli theorem.* The intersection form gives polarizations on the graded quotients $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{-1} = H_1$ and $\mathrm{Gr}^W_{-2} = H_1 \otimes H_1$. Let $Y$ be a compact Riemann surface with points $s \ne r$. If a ring isomorphism $\mathbb{Z}\pi_1(X \setminus \{q\}, p)/J_p^3 \to \mathbb{Z}\pi_1(Y \setminus \{s\}, r)/J_r^3$ is an isomorphism of graded polarized mixed Hodge structures, then there is a biholomorphism $f : X \to Y$ with $(f(p), f(q)) \in A_Y(r, s)$. Here $A_Y(r, s)$ consists of $(r, s)$ and the pairs $(r', s')$ with $m_{r's'} = -m_{rs}$ and $(Y \setminus \{s\}, r) \not\cong (Y \setminus \{s'\}, r')$, and it has at most two elements [@kaenders2001, Corollary 2.7 and Theorem 2.8, p. 8].
- *Proof of the last statement.* The isomorphism induces an isomorphism of polarized Hodge structures $\lambda^* : H^1(Y) \to H^1(X)$, so the classical Torelli theorem gives a biholomorphism $f$ with $f^* = \pm\lambda^*$. Since the isomorphism respects products, its effect on $(J^2/J^3)^* = H^1 \otimes H^1$ is $\lambda^* \otimes \lambda^* = f^* \otimes f^*$. After transport by $f$, the two extensions are compared by a map that is $\pm 1$ on $H^1$ and the identity on $H^1 \otimes H^1$, so they agree up to sign [@kaenders2001, proof of Theorem 2.8, pp. 8–9]. Kaenders says that the proof follows those of Pulte and Hain in the compact case [@kaenders2001, p. 8].
