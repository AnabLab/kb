**Koszul algebras.** Let $k$ be a field. A positively graded algebra $A = k \oplus A_1 \oplus A_2 \oplus \cdots$ is *Koszul* if $\mathrm{Tor}^A_{ij}(k, k) = 0$ for all $i \ne j$, where $i$ is the homological grading and $j$ the grading induced from $A$. The conditions for $i = 1, 2$ say exactly that $A$ is quadratic, that is, generated by $A_1$ with relations in degree $2$. Priddy introduced the notion for algebras with finite-dimensional components, and Positselski and Vishik extended it to the infinite-dimensional case [@positselski2014, §0.1].

Let $A = T(V)/(\Omega)$ be quadratic, with $V$ finite-dimensional and $\Omega \subseteq V \otimes V$. Its quadratic dual is $A^! = T(V^*)/(\Omega^\perp)$, where $\Omega^\perp \subseteq V^* \otimes V^*$ is the annihilator of $\Omega$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Definition 2.3]. By Priddy's theorem, $A$ is Koszul exactly when $\mathrm{Ext}^\bullet_A(k, k) \cong A^!$, and $A$ is Koszul exactly when $A^!$ is [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Theorem 2.10 and p. 9]. So a Koszul algebra can be recovered from its cohomology [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, p. 2]. A sufficient condition is the PBW property: for some ordered basis of $V$ and some admissible order on monomials, every critical monomial of degree $3$ is confluent under the rewriting rules given by the relations [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, §2.4, p. 11].

**Conjecture** (Positselski). Let $\ell$ be a prime and $K$ a field containing a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity. Then the algebra $K^M_*(K)/\ell$ is Koszul. Equivalently, the Galois cohomology algebra $H^*(G_K, \mathbb{Z}/\ell)$ is Koszul [@positselski2014, §0.1] [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Conjecture 1.3].

Positselski states the Galois form with coefficients $\mu_\ell^{\otimes n}$ in degree $n$. When $\mu_\ell \subset K$, these coefficients are $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$ compatibly with cup products, and the [norm residue isomorphism theorem](norm-residue-isomorphism-theorem.md) identifies $H^*(G_K, \mathbb{Z}/\ell)$ with $K^M_*(K)/\ell$ as graded algebras (part 1 there). Mináč, Pasini, Quadrelli and Tân present the conjecture as a special case of a more general one that Positselski formulated [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, p. 2].

**Origin.** Positselski and Vishik showed that the Bloch–Kato conjecture would follow from its low-degree part if $K^M_*(K)/\ell$ were known to be Koszul, by an argument that applies directly to fields with no extensions of degree prime to $\ell$. Given the Merkurjev–Suslin theorem, the Galois form of the conjecture implies the Bloch–Kato conjecture for $K$ [@positselski2014, §0.1]. Rost and Voevodsky have since proved that conjecture. For $K$ containing a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity it makes $H^*(G_K, \mathbb{Z}/\ell)$ quadratic, which is the condition $i = 1, 2$ of Koszulity, so the Koszulity conjecture strengthens it [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, p. 2].

**Known cases.**

- *At most two relations.* For $K$ as in the conjecture with $K^\times/K^{\times\ell}$ finite, and also $\sqrt{-1}\in K$ at $\ell=2$, if $G_K(\ell)$ has at most two defining relations, its cohomology is [universally Koszul](universal-koszulity-with-few-relations.md) [@quadrelli2021few, Corollary 1.3].
- *Virtual cohomological dimension at most one.* Pál–Quick prove Koszulity for every prime in this case [@palquick2025, Theorem 1.11]; see [formality of Galois cochains in virtual dimension one](galois-cochain-formality-in-virtual-dimension-one.md).
- *Local and global fields.* Positselski proves the conjecture for every algebraic extension of $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $\mathbb{F}_p((z))$, $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{F}_p(z)$ [@positselski2014, §0.4]: see [@positselski2014, §3, Theorem 1 (1)] for the local case and [@positselski2014, §5, Theorem] for the global case. Mináč, Pasini, Quadrelli and Tân note that his results give the PBW property for these fields [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, p. 4]. He also proves that $K^M_*(K)/\ell$ is Koszul for global fields without a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, outside the scope of the conjecture, while noting that there are few general reasons to expect Koszulity in that case [@positselski2014, §0.4 and §7, Theorem].
- *Henselian discretely valued fields.* If $K$ is a Henselian discrete valuation field with residue field $\kappa$ and $\ell \ne \mathrm{char}\, \kappa$, then $K^M_*(K)/\ell$ is Koszul whenever $K^M_*(\kappa)/\ell$ is [@positselski2014, §3, Theorem 1 (2)].
- *Elementary type.* If $G$ is a pro-$\ell$ group of elementary type, then $H^\bullet(G, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is PBW, hence Koszul [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Theorem A]. The same holds for $H^\bullet(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ whenever the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient $G_K(\ell)$ is of elementary type, since inflation from $G_K(\ell)$ is then an isomorphism [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Remark 1.4]. So [Efrat's elementary type conjecture](elementary-type-conjecture.md) would prove the Koszulity conjecture for every $K$ that contains a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity and has $K^\times/K^{\times \ell}$ finite. Unconditionally, for $K$ containing a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity with $K^\times/K^{\times \ell}$ finite, $H^\bullet(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is PBW if $K$ is finite, pseudo algebraically closed, of relative transcendence degree $1$ over a pseudo algebraically closed field, an algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, of transcendence degree $1$ over a local field, or $\ell$-rigid, and for $\ell = 2$ also if $K$ is an algebraic extension of a global field of characteristic not $2$, or Pythagorean [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Theorem D].
- *Demushkin groups.* For every [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) $G$, the algebra $H^\bullet(G, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is PBW, hence Koszul, and its quadratic dual is the restricted universal enveloping algebra of the restricted Lie algebra attached to the $\ell$-Zassenhaus filtration of $G$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Theorem C].

Mináč, Pasini, Quadrelli and Tân conjecture the stronger statement that $H^\bullet(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is PBW for every $K$ containing a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Conjecture 1.6].

**Example: $\ell$-adic fields.** The following is checked here, and agrees with the theorems of Positselski and of Mináč–Pasini–Quadrelli–Tân cited above. Let $\ell$ be odd, and let $K/\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ be finite of degree $n$ with $\zeta_\ell \in K$. By [maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md), $G_K(\ell)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $d = n + 2$, and by part 3 of the norm residue entry, inflation $H^*(G_K(\ell), \mathbb{F}_\ell) \to H^*(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is an isomorphism. So $V = H^1(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ has dimension $d$, and $H^2(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is a line, which we identify with $\mathbb{F}_\ell$, such that the cup product $V \times V \to \mathbb{F}_\ell$ is nondegenerate. Graded commutativity gives $x \cup x = -x \cup x$, so $x \cup x = 0$ as $\ell$ is odd, and the form is alternating. Choose a symplectic basis $x_1, \dots, x_d$, so that $d$ is even, $x_{2i-1} \cup x_{2i} = 1 = -x_{2i} \cup x_{2i-1}$, and all other products of basis vectors are $0$. Let $X_1, \dots, X_d$ be the dual basis of $V^*$, with $V^* \otimes V^*$ paired with $V \otimes V$ by $(f \otimes g)(v \otimes w) = f(v)g(w)$.

By part 1 of the norm residue entry, $H^*(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell) = T(V)/(\Omega)$ with $\Omega$ the kernel of the cup product $m : V \otimes V \to \mathbb{F}_\ell$. A linear form on $V \otimes V$ vanishes on $\ker m$ exactly when it is a multiple of $m$, so $\Omega^\perp$ is the line spanned by $m$. Evaluating on basis tensors shows that $m$ corresponds to $r = \sum_{i=1}^{d/2} (X_{2i-1} \otimes X_{2i} - X_{2i} \otimes X_{2i-1})$. Hence

$$H^*(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)^! \cong \mathbb{F}_\ell\langle X_1, \dots, X_d \rangle / (r), \qquad r = \sum_{i=1}^{d/2} (X_{2i-1}X_{2i} - X_{2i}X_{2i-1}).$$

This algebra has a single quadratic relator, in which $X_1X_2$ occurs as a monomial and $X_1^2$ does not, so it is PBW [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Lemma 2.15], hence Koszul. So $H^*(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is Koszul, since a quadratic algebra is Koszul exactly when its quadratic dual is [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, p. 9].

**Reformulation: quasi-formality of cochains.** For an augmented DG-algebra $A^\bullet$ over a field, Positselski calls the differentials $d_r$, $r \ge 2$, of its algebraic Eilenberg–Moore spectral sequence, whose $E_2$ page is $\mathrm{Tor}^{H^*(A^\bullet)}(k, k)$, its *tensor Massey products*. He calls $A^\bullet$ *quasi-formal* if they all vanish, and notes that formal DG-algebras are quasi-formal [@positselski2017, §2, p. 12 and Proposition 2.1]. He calls $A^\bullet$ *of $K(\pi, 1)$ type* if it is connected by quasi-isomorphisms of augmented DG-algebras to the cobar construction of a conilpotent coalgebra [@positselski2017, p. 23]. His main theorem says that $H^*(A^\bullet)$ is Koszul exactly when $A^\bullet$ is quasi-formal and of $K(\pi, 1)$ type [@positselski2017, Theorem 4.3].

Let $G$ be a profinite group, $k$ a field of characteristic $\ell$, and $G^{(\ell)}$ the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of $G$. The cochain DG-algebra of $G$ is the cobar construction of the coalgebra $k(G)$ of locally constant functions on $G$. Its maximal conilpotent subcoalgebra is $k(G^{(\ell)})$ [@positselski2017, Example 6.3, pp. 34–35]. So the cochain DG-algebra is of $K(\pi, 1)$ type exactly when inflation $H^*(G^{(\ell)}, k) \to H^*(G, k)$ is an isomorphism [@positselski2017, Remark 4.5]. That holds for $G = G_K$ when $K$ contains a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity [@positselski2017, p. 35], as in part 3 of the norm residue entry. Combining these, as is done here: *for $K$ containing a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, $H^*(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$ is Koszul exactly when the cochain DG-algebra of $G_K$ over $\mathbb{F}_\ell$ is quasi-formal.* So the conjecture is a statement about the cochains of $G_K$, not only about the ring $H^*(G_K, \mathbb{F}_\ell)$, which the norm residue theorem already describes. Positselski notes that the conjecture implies the vanishing of the tensor Massey products in $H^*(G_K, \mathbb{Z}/\ell)$, but may have no direct implications for the vanishing of tuple Massey products [@positselski2017, p. 13], the subject of the [Massey vanishing conjecture](massey-vanishing-conjecture.md).

**Koszul but not formal.** Let $\ell \ne p$ be primes and $F$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ or $\mathbb{F}_p((z))$ containing a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, and also $\sqrt{-1}$ if $\ell = 2$. Then $G_F^{(\ell)}$ is a semidirect product of two copies of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, with one acting on the other by multiplication by the order $q$ of the residue field, and $H^*(G_F, k)$ is the exterior algebra on two generators of degree $1$, which is Koszul [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, Example 2.9 (a)]. Yet the cochain DG-algebra of $G_F$ is not formal. By Positselski's criterion it would be formal only if the coalgebra $k(G_F^{(\ell)})$ were isomorphic to its associated graded coalgebra, which is cocommutative, while $k(G_F^{(\ell)})$ is not, as $G_F^{(\ell)}$ is not commutative [@positselski2017, Theorem 6.2 and Example 6.3, pp. 34–35]. This answers negatively a question of Hopkins and Wickelgren, whether the cochain DG-algebras of absolute Galois groups are formal [@positselski2017, p. 4] [@merkurjevscavia2025, p. 831].

As his method seemed to need a field without all roots of unity of $\ell$-power order, Positselski asked for a field containing them all whose cochains are not formal [@merkurjevscavia2025, Question 1.1, p. 831] [@positselski2017, pp. 35–36]. Merkurjev and Scavia answer this for every prime [@merkurjevscavia2025, Theorem 1.2, p. 832]: see [non-formality of Galois cochains](non-formality-of-galois-cochains.md). The fields answering the question contain all roots of unity of $\ell$-power order, so the conjecture predicts that their cohomology is Koszul, and then, by the reformulation above, that their cochains are quasi-formal though not formal. Their non-formality is detected by four classes with vanishing adjacent cup products whose Massey product, in the usual tuple sense, is not defined [@merkurjevscavia2025, Theorem 1.3, p. 832]. This is consistent with Positselski's remark on tuple products. The sources opened here do not say whether the cohomology of these fields is Koszul.

**Stronger forms.** Positselski also proposes module Koszulity conjectures [@positselski2014, §0.2–0.3]. Suppose that $\ell$ is odd and $K$ contains a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, or that $\ell = 2$ and $\sqrt{-1} \in K$. Then $K^M_*(K)/\ell$ is the quotient of the exterior algebra $\Lambda$ on $K^\times/K^{\times \ell}$ by the ideal $J_K$ generated by the Steinberg symbols, which lie in degree $2$. If $J_K$ is a Koszul module over $\Lambda$ in the appropriately shifted grading, then both the Koszulity of $K^M_*(K)/\ell$ and a version of [Bogomolov's freeness conjecture](bogomolov-positselski-freeness-conjecture.md) follow [@positselski2014, §0.2]. The shift matters because $J_K$ is generated in degree $2$. The condition is that $J_K$ has a linear graded resolution over $\Lambda$ starting in degree $2$, that is, $\mathrm{Tor}^{\Lambda}_{i}(\mathbb{F}_\ell, J_K)$ is concentrated in internal degree $i + 2$ for every $i \ge 0$. Positselski's convention takes Koszul modules to be generated in degree $1$ [@positselski2014, §0.2], so it applies after lowering degrees by one. Feuerpfeil writes $M(s)_n = M_{n+s}$ and asks for $J_K(2)$ to be Koszul in the sense $\mathrm{Tor}^{\Lambda}_{i,j} = 0$ for $i \ne j$ [@feuerpfeil2026, p. 3, p. 6 and Definition 2.9]. Without the shift the condition fails, under either convention, for every $K$ with $J_K \ne 0$, since $\mathrm{Tor}^{\Lambda}_{0}(\mathbb{F}_\ell, J_K) = J_K/\Lambda_+ J_K$ is nonzero in degree $2$. Positselski proves the module conjectures for local and global fields, except that for one of them over global fields his proof assumes $\{c, c\} = 0$ for the element $c$ involved [@positselski2014, §0.4]. Feuerpfeil shows that for torsion-free oriented pro-$\ell$ groups of elementary type, the kernel of the inflation map from the exterior algebra on $H^1$ onto $H^\bullet$, shifted by $2$ as above, is a Koszul module over that exterior algebra. So the elementary type conjecture would give the module condition on $J_K$, in the same shifted sense, for every $K$ as above with $K^\times/K^{\times \ell}$ finite [@feuerpfeil2026, Theorem C and p. 4].

**Status.** Open, as far as the sources opened here show. Positselski calls it a conjecture in 2017 [@positselski2017, p. 13], and Mináč, Pasini, Quadrelli and Tân prove it only in the cases above [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2021, pp. 2–4].
