**Massey products and unipotent matrices.** Let $G$ be a profinite group acting trivially on $\mathbb F_p$, and let $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)$. A defining system for $\langle\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\rangle$ is a family of continuous cochains $a_{ij}$, for $1\le i<j\le n+1$ and $(i,j)\ne(1,n+1)$, such that $a_{i,i+1}$ represents $\alpha_i$ and $\delta a_{ij}=\sum_{l=i+1}^{j-1}a_{il}\cup a_{lj}$. The product is the set of classes of the $2$-cocycles $\sum_{k=2}^n a_{1k}\cup a_{k,n+1}$ over all defining systems. It is *defined* if a defining system exists, and it *contains zero* if some defining system gives the zero class [@minactan2015kernel, Definition 7.1, pp. 16–17].

Let $U_{n+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ be the group of upper unitriangular $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ matrices. By Dwyer's theorem, if $\rho:G\to U_{n+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ is a continuous homomorphism, with entries $\rho_{ij}$, then $\langle-\rho_{12},\ldots,-\rho_{n,n+1}\rangle$ is defined and contains zero [@minactan2015kernel, Theorem 7.2 and Corollary 7.3, p. 17]. Conjugating $\rho$ by a diagonal matrix multiplies its superdiagonal entries by arbitrary elements of $\mathbb F_p^\times$, so the signs do not matter. The same matrix description of definedness and vanishing is spelled out for triple products in [triple Massey products in Galois cohomology](triple-massey-products-in-galois-cohomology.md).

**Theorem (Mináč–Tân).** Let $p$ be a prime, $n\ge3$ and $F$ a field. Assume that $F$ contains a primitive $p$th root of unity if $\mathrm{char}\,F\ne p$, and that $-1$ is a square in $F$ if $p=2$. Let $G$ be $G_F$ or its maximal pro-$p$ quotient $G_F(p)$. Then for every $\chi\in H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)$, the $n$-fold Massey product $\langle\chi,\ldots,\chi\rangle$ is defined and contains zero [@minactan2015kernel, Theorem 8.1, p. 17].

The source calls this a generalization of an earlier proposition of Mináč and Tân [@minactan2015kernel, Remark 8.3, p. 19].

**Outline of the proof** [@minactan2015kernel, proof of Theorem 8.1, pp. 17–19]. Inflation identifies $H^1(G_F(p),\mathbb F_p)$ with $H^1(G_F,\mathbb F_p)$, and a representation of $G_F(p)$ inflates to $G_F$, so it is enough to treat $G=G_F(p)$. In characteristic $p$, $G_F(p)$ is free pro-$p$ and the products vanish. Otherwise $\chi=\chi_a$ is the Kummer character of some $a\in F^\times\setminus F^{\times p}$. A representation into $U_{m+1}$ with constant superdiagonal restricts, on its upper left corner, to one into $U_{n+1}$ for every $n<m$, so it is enough to treat $n=p^s$. Put $N=p^s+1$, let $X\in M_N(\mathbb F_p)$ have ones at the positions $(i,i+1)$ and zeros elsewhere, and put $B=1+X$. Then $B$ has order $p^{s+1}$ and all its superdiagonal entries equal $1$. Fix compatible primitive roots of unity $\zeta_{p^r}$. In each case below there is a representation of a finite quotient of $G$ whose superdiagonal entries are all $\chi_a$, which gives the claim.

- *Case 0: $\zeta_{p^{s+1}}\in F$.* Then $L=F(a^{1/p^{s+1}})$ is cyclic of degree $p^{s+1}$ over $F$. Send its generator $\sigma$, with $\sigma(a^{1/p^{s+1}})=\zeta_{p^{s+1}}a^{1/p^{s+1}}$, to $B$.
- *If $\zeta_{p^{s+1}}\notin F$,* let $p^k$, with $1\le k\le s$, be the largest power with $\zeta_{p^k}\in F$. At $p=2$, $k\ge2$ because $-1$ is a square. There are two cases.
- *Case 1: $X^{p^{s+1}}-a$ is irreducible over $F(\zeta_{p^{s+1}})$.* Then $L=F(\zeta_{p^{s+1}},a^{1/p^{s+1}})$ is Galois over $F$ with group $\langle\sigma,\tau\mid\tau^{p^{s+1}}=\sigma^{p^{s+1-k}}=1,\ \sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^{1+p^k}\rangle$, where $\tau$ moves only the radical and $\sigma$ moves only the root of unity. There is a unique $A\in U_N(\mathbb F_p)$ whose last column is the last standard basis vector and with $ABA^{-1}=B^{1+p^k}$, and it has order $p^{s+1-k}$ [@minactan2015kernel, Lemma 3.4 and Proposition 3.6, pp. 9–11]. Send $\tau\mapsto B$ and $\sigma\mapsto A$. The superdiagonal entries of $A$ are $0=\chi_a(\sigma)$.
- *Case 2: $X^{p^{s+1}}-a$ is reducible over $F(\zeta_{p^{s+1}})$.* Then $a$ is a $p$th power there, and Kummer theory gives $\chi_a=m\chi_{\zeta_{p^k}}$ for some $0<m<p$. So one may take $a=\zeta_{p^k}$. Then $L=F(\zeta_{p^{s+1+k}})$ is cyclic of degree $p^{s+1}$, and its generator goes to $B$.

**The cyclic group of order $p$.** Let $G=\mathbb Z/p$ and let $\chi$ be the identity map. For odd $p$, the source quotes the earlier paper for the fact that the $p$-fold product $\langle\chi,\ldots,\chi\rangle$ is defined but does not contain zero [@minactan2015kernel, Example 8.2, p. 19]. For $p=2$, the $4$-fold product is not defined [@minactan2015kernel, Example 8.2, p. 19]. As checked here: modulo the corner entry, any matrix in $U_5(\mathbb F_2)$ with all superdiagonal entries $1$ squares to a matrix whose entries at $(1,3)$, $(2,4)$ and $(3,5)$ equal $1$. So it cannot be the image of an element of order $2$.

**Consequences.** Mináč and Tân draw the following consequences [@minactan2015kernel, Remark 8.3, pp. 19–20].

- If $L$ is a field with $G_L\cong\mathbb Z/2$, then $-1$ is not a square in $L$. Otherwise the theorem would make the $4$-fold product defined.
- No absolute Galois group has order $4$. A subgroup $H$ of order $2$ has fixed field $K$ with $G_K\cong\mathbb Z/2$, so $-1$ is not a square in $K$, hence not in $F$. Then $L=F(\sqrt{-1})$ has $G_L$ of order $2$ and contains a square root of $-1$, which is impossible.

As checked here, the theorem with $n=p$ and the cyclic example also show that for odd $p$, $G_F(p)$ is never cyclic of order $p$ when $F$ contains a primitive $p$th root of unity. The source says that the earlier proposition was used in the same way to explain why $G_F$ and $G_F(p)$ have no elements of odd prime order, which is part of Artin–Schreier's theorem and of Becker's theorem [@minactan2015kernel, Remark 8.3, p. 19]. For the full statement about finite absolute Galois groups, see [Artin–Schreier's theorem](artin-schreier-theorem.md).
