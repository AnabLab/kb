**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $g \ge 1$. Let $F$ be the free pro-$p$ group on $x_1, \dots, x_{2g}$, write $(a, b) = a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$, and let $D = F/R$, where $R$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by
$$r = (x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{2g-1}, x_{2g}).$$
This is the pro-$p$ surface group of genus $g$, the [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $2g$ with $q = 0$ (see [symplectic similitudes lift](symplectic-similitudes-lift-to-pro-p-surface-groups.md)).

For a pro-$p$ group $G$ put $G_1 = G$ and let $G_{k+1} = [G_k, G]$ be the closed subgroup generated by the commutators $(a, b)$ with $a \in G_k$, $b \in G$. The quotients $\mathrm{gr}_k G = G_k/G_{k+1}$, written additively, form a graded Lie algebra $\mathrm{gr}\, G = \bigoplus_{k \ge 1} \mathrm{gr}_k G$ over $\mathbb{Z}_p$. For $a \in G_i$ and $b \in G_j$, the bracket $[\bar a, \bar b]$ is the class of $(a, b)$ in $\mathrm{gr}_{i+j} G$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.8.1) and the paragraph after (3.8.4), pp. 220–221]. Moreover $\bigcap_k G_k = 1$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.8.2), p. 220].

Write $V = \mathrm{gr}_1 D = D^{\mathrm{ab}}$. It is a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module with basis the classes $\xi_1, \dots, \xi_{2g}$ of the $x_j$, because $r$ is a product of commutators.

Let $L$ be the free Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ on $X_1, \dots, X_{2g}$, graded by degree. Let $\rho = \sum_{i=1}^{g} [X_{2i-1}, X_{2i}] \in L_2$, and let $\mathfrak{g} = L/(\rho)$, where $(\rho)$ is the ideal generated by $\rho$. Write $\mathrm{ad}(a)^m b = [a, [a, \dots, [a, b] \dots]]$ with $m$ brackets, and put
$$Y_{j,m} = \mathrm{ad}(X_1)^m X_j \quad (2 \le j \le 2g,\ m \ge 0), \qquad S = \{Y_{2,0}\} \cup \{Y_{j,m} : 3 \le j \le 2g,\ m \ge 0\}.$$
So $Y_{j,m}$ has degree $m + 1$, and $Y_{j,0} = X_j$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Presentation.* The graded Lie algebra homomorphism $\beta : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathrm{gr}\, D$ with $\beta(X_j) = \xi_j$ exists and is an isomorphism. Moreover $\mathfrak{g} = \mathbb{Z}_p X_1 \oplus \mathfrak{s}$ as $\mathbb{Z}_p$-modules, where $\mathfrak{s}$ is an ideal that is a free Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ with free generating set (the image of) $S$. Each $\mathrm{gr}_k D$ is a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of finite rank. For $E = \mathbb{Q}_p$ or $\mathbb{F}_p$, $\mathrm{gr}\, D \otimes E = E X_1 \oplus \mathfrak{s}_E$, with $\mathfrak{s}_E = \mathfrak{s} \otimes E$ free over $E$ on $S$.

From now on $g \ge 2$, and $E$ is $\mathbb{Q}_p$ or $\mathbb{F}_p$.

2. *Centralizers in $\mathrm{gr}\, D$.*
   - (a) If $d \ge 1$ and $z \in \mathrm{gr}_d D \otimes E$ satisfies $[z, \xi_3] = [z, \xi_4] = 0$, then $z = 0$.
   - (b) If $I$ and $J$ are graded ideals of $\mathrm{gr}\, D \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ with $[I, J] = 0$, then $I = 0$ or $J = 0$.
3. *Centralizers in $D$.*
   - (a) For every $i \ge 1$, the centre of $D/D_{i+1}$ is $D_i/D_{i+1}$. In particular $D$ is centre-free.
   - (b) For every $i \ge 1$, the map $\mathrm{ad}_i : \mathrm{gr}_i D \to \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(V, \mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D)$, $z \mapsto [z, -]$, is injective with torsion-free cokernel.
   - (c) Every nontrivial closed normal subgroup of $D$ has trivial centralizer in $D$.
4. *The Andreadakis filtration.* Automorphisms are continuous, and $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ carries the profinite topology defined by the characteristic open subgroups of $D$ [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Notations and conventions, p. 3]. Identify $D$ with $\mathrm{Inn}(D)$ through $d \mapsto c_d$, $c_d(x) = dxd^{-1}$, which is injective by 3(a). For $i \ge 1$ let $K_i$ be the kernel of $\mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{Aut}(D/D_{i+1})$.
   - (a) For $\kappa \in K_i$, the class of $\kappa(x)x^{-1}$ in $\mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D$ depends only on the image of $x$ in $V$, and defines $J_i(\kappa) \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(V, \mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D)$. The map $J_i$ is a homomorphism with kernel $K_{i+1}$. For $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, $J_i(\alpha\kappa\alpha^{-1}) = \mathrm{gr}_{i+1}(\alpha) \circ J_i(\kappa) \circ \mathrm{gr}_1(\alpha)^{-1}$. So $K_i/K_{i+1}$ is a torsion-free abelian group.
   - (b) $K_i \cap D = D_i$, and $J_i$ maps $D_iK_{i+1}/K_{i+1}$ onto $\mathrm{ad}_i(\mathrm{gr}_i D)$.
   - (c) $DK_i/DK_{i+1} \cong K_i/D_iK_{i+1}$, and this is a torsion-free abelian group.
   - (d) $\bigcap_i K_i = 1$ and $\bigcap_i DK_i = D$.
   - (e) If $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ acts on $V$ as multiplication by $\lambda \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, then it acts on $\mathrm{gr}_k D$ as multiplication by $\lambda^k$, and $J_i(\alpha\kappa\alpha^{-1}) = \lambda^i J_i(\kappa)$ for $\kappa \in K_i$.

Presentations of this kind are standard in the literature on pro-$\ell$ surface groups; for instance Takao recalls one for the graded Lie algebra of a pro-$\ell$ braid group on a proper curve [@takao2012, §2.2, (9)–(11), pp. 10–11]. The proof below is self-contained apart from the inputs listed next.

**Inputs.**

- *Free pro-$p$ groups.* $\mathrm{gr}\, F$ is the free Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ on the classes of $x_1, \dots, x_{2g}$ [@labute1967lie, proof of Lemme 2, p. 154].
- *Free Lie algebras.* Over an integral domain $A$, the free Lie algebra $L_A(S)$ on a set $S$ is a free $A$-module, its enveloping algebra is the algebra $A\langle S\rangle$ of noncommutative polynomials in $S$, and the canonical map $L_A(S) \to A\langle S\rangle$ is injective [@labute1967lie, §2, p. 145]. So $L_A(S)$ is the Lie subalgebra of $A\langle S\rangle$ generated by $S$, with bracket $[a, b] = ab - ba$.
- *Elimination.* In the free Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ on $X_1, \dots, X_{2g}$, the Lie subalgebra generated by $T = \{\mathrm{ad}(X_1)^n X_i : n \ge 0,\ 2 \le i \le 2g\}$ is free with free generating set $T$, and it is a complement of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell X_1$ as a module [@takao2012, proof of Lemma 2.1, p. 11]. Takao writes $X_1^n X_i$ for $\mathrm{ad}(X_1)^n X_i$ [@takao2012, Notations in §1, p. 6], and attributes the statement to Bourbaki. It applies with $\ell = p$.
- *The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff product*, used without citation. Let $\mathfrak{n}$ be a finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff series defines a group law $a * b$ on the set $\mathfrak{n}$, with identity $0$ and inverse $a \mapsto -a$. Write $c(a, b) = (-a) * (-b) * a * b$ for the group commutator. Then $a * b - a - b$ and $c(a, b) - [a, b]$ are finite sums of rational multiples of iterated brackets in $a$ and $b$, of degree at least $2$ and at least $3$ respectively, and each of these brackets contains both $a$ and $b$. Every Lie algebra automorphism of $\mathfrak{n}$ is an automorphism of $(\mathfrak{n}, *)$.

**Proof of part 1.**

*Step 1: elimination in $\mathfrak{g}$.* Let $T = \{Y_{j,m} : 2 \le j \le 2g,\ m \ge 0\}$ and let $L_T \subseteq L$ be the Lie subalgebra it generates. By elimination, $L_T$ is free on $T$ and $L = \mathbb{Z}_p X_1 \oplus L_T$. Since $\mathrm{ad}(X_1)$ is a derivation and maps $T$ into $T$, it preserves $L_T$, so $L_T$ is an ideal of $L$. Now $[X_1, X_2] = Y_{2,1}$, so
$$\rho = Y_{2,1} + \sum_{i=2}^{g} [Y_{2i-1,0}, Y_{2i,0}],$$
and by the Leibniz rule, for $m \ge 0$,
$$\mathrm{ad}(X_1)^m \rho = Y_{2,m+1} - f_m, \qquad f_m = -\sum_{i=2}^{g} \sum_{l=0}^{m} \binom{m}{l} [Y_{2i-1,l}, Y_{2i,m-l}].$$
Each $f_m$ lies in the Lie subalgebra generated by $S$, since $2i - 1 \ge 3$ for $i \ge 2$. Let $J \subseteq L_T$ be the ideal of $L_T$ generated by the elements $\mathrm{ad}(X_1)^m \rho$, $m \ge 0$. This generating set is mapped into itself by the derivation $\mathrm{ad}(X_1)$, so $J$ is stable under $\mathrm{ad}(X_1)$, and hence $J$ is an ideal of $L$. It contains $\rho$ and lies in $(\rho)$, so $J = (\rho)$. Therefore $\mathfrak{g} = \mathbb{Z}_p X_1 \oplus L_T/J$.

The set $T$ is the disjoint union of $S$ and $\{Y_{2,m+1} : m \ge 0\}$, and $J$ is generated by the elements $Y_{2,m+1} - f_m$. Let $L(S)$ be the free Lie algebra on $S$. The homomorphism $L_T \to L(S)$ that fixes $S$ and sends $Y_{2,m+1}$ to $f_m$ kills $J$. The homomorphism $L(S) \to L_T/J$ induced by $S \subseteq T$ is surjective, since $Y_{2,m+1} \equiv f_m$ modulo $J$. The two induced maps are inverse to each other, as one checks on generators. So $\mathfrak{s} := L_T/J$ is free on $S$. It is an ideal of $\mathfrak{g}$, and $X_1$ acts on it by the derivation $\delta = \mathrm{ad}(X_1)$ with
$$\delta(Y_{j,m}) = Y_{j,m+1} \ (j \ge 3), \qquad \delta(Y_{2,0}) = Y_{2,1} = f_0 = -\sum_{i=2}^{g} [Y_{2i-1,0}, Y_{2i,0}].$$
There are finitely many elements of $S$ in each degree, so each $\mathfrak{g}_k$ is a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of finite rank. For $E = \mathbb{Q}_p$ or $\mathbb{F}_p$, $L \otimes E$ and $L(S) \otimes E$ satisfy the universal properties of the free Lie algebras over $E$ on the same generators, so tensoring the decomposition gives $\mathfrak{g} \otimes E = EX_1 \oplus \mathfrak{s}_E$ with $\mathfrak{s}_E$ free on $S$ over $E$.

*Step 2: $\beta$ exists and is onto.* The image of $F_k$ in $D$ is $D_k$, so $\mathrm{gr}\, F \to \mathrm{gr}\, D$ is a surjective homomorphism of graded Lie algebras. Identify $\mathrm{gr}\, F$ with $L$, sending $X_j$ to the class of $x_j$ (Inputs). The class of $r$ in $\mathrm{gr}_2 F$ is $\rho$, because $r$ is the product of the commutators $(x_{2i-1}, x_{2i})$. As $r \mapsto 1$ in $D$, the map $L \to \mathrm{gr}\, D$ kills $\rho$, hence the ideal $(\rho)$. It induces a surjective $\beta : \mathfrak{g} \to \mathrm{gr}\, D$ with $\beta(X_j) = \xi_j$.

*Step 3: $\beta$ is injective.* Fix $n \ge 2$. Let $\mathfrak{n} = (\mathfrak{g}/\bigoplus_{k > n} \mathfrak{g}_k) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$, a finite-dimensional nilpotent graded Lie algebra. For $k \le n$ we regard $\mathfrak{g}_k$ as a lattice in $\mathfrak{n}_k$, which we may since $\mathfrak{g}_k$ is free. Let $\mathfrak{n}_{\ge k} = \bigoplus_{k \le l \le n} \mathfrak{n}_l$. By the properties of $*$ above, each $\mathfrak{n}_{\ge k}$ is a normal subgroup of $(\mathfrak{n}, *)$, and for $a \in \mathfrak{n}_{\ge i}$ and $b \in \mathfrak{n}_{\ge j}$,
$$a * b \equiv a + b \pmod{\mathfrak{n}_{\ge i+j}}, \qquad c(a, b) \equiv [a, b] \pmod{\mathfrak{n}_{\ge i+j+1}}. \tag{$\dagger$}$$
For $t \in \mathbb{Q}_p^\times$ let $\sigma_t$ multiply $\mathfrak{n}_l$ by $t^l$. It is a Lie algebra automorphism, hence an automorphism of $(\mathfrak{n}, *)$.

(i) *Solving the relation.* For $u = (u_1, \dots, u_{2g}) \in \mathfrak{n}^{2g}$ put $\mathcal{R}(u) = c(u_1, u_2) * c(u_3, u_4) * \cdots * c(u_{2g-1}, u_{2g})$. We claim that there is $u$ with $u_j - X_j \in \mathfrak{n}_{\ge 2}$ for all $j$ and $\mathcal{R}(u) = 0$. We show by induction on $k = 3, \dots, n+1$ that some $u$ with $u_j - X_j \in \mathfrak{n}_{\ge 2}$ has $\mathcal{R}(u) \in \mathfrak{n}_{\ge k}$. For $k = 3$ take $u_j = X_j$: by ($\dagger$), $\mathcal{R}(X) \equiv \sum_i [X_{2i-1}, X_{2i}] = \rho = 0$ modulo $\mathfrak{n}_{\ge 3}$. Suppose that $\mathcal{R}(u) \in \mathfrak{n}_{\ge k}$ with $3 \le k \le n$, and let $\varepsilon \in \mathfrak{n}_k$ be its degree-$k$ component. Let $\delta_j \in \mathfrak{n}_{k-1}$ and $u'_j = u_j + \delta_j$. Expand each iterated bracket in $c(u'_{2i-1}, u'_{2i})$ multilinearly. The terms containing no $\delta$ give $c(u_{2i-1}, u_{2i})$. In the remaining terms, $[\delta_{2i-1}, u_{2i}] + [u_{2i-1}, \delta_{2i}] \equiv [\delta_{2i-1}, X_{2i}] + [X_{2i-1}, \delta_{2i}]$, and every other term has degree at least $k+1$: $[\delta_{2i-1}, \delta_{2i}]$ has degree $2k - 2 \ge k + 1$, and a bracket of degree at least $3$ containing a $\delta$ has degree at least $(k-1) + 2$. So
$$c(u'_{2i-1}, u'_{2i}) \equiv c(u_{2i-1}, u_{2i}) + [\delta_{2i-1}, X_{2i}] + [X_{2i-1}, \delta_{2i}] \pmod{\mathfrak{n}_{\ge k+1}}.$$
In the product of the $g$ factors, which lie in $\mathfrak{n}_{\ge 2}$, a change of one factor by an element of $\mathfrak{n}_{\ge k}$ changes the product by the same element modulo $\mathfrak{n}_{\ge k+2}$. Indeed, by the properties of $*$, for $x, a \in \mathfrak{n}_{\ge 2}$ and $\eta \in \mathfrak{n}_{\ge k}$, the terms of $x * (a + \eta) - x * a - \eta$ are brackets containing both $\eta$ and $x$, of degree at least $k + 2$, and likewise for $(a + \eta) * x$. Hence
$$\mathcal{R}(u') \equiv \mathcal{R}(u) + \sum_{i=1}^{g} \big([\delta_{2i-1}, X_{2i}] + [X_{2i-1}, \delta_{2i}]\big) \pmod{\mathfrak{n}_{\ge k+1}}.$$
Since $\mathfrak{g}$ is generated by $\mathfrak{g}_1$, $\mathfrak{n}_k = \sum_j [\mathfrak{n}_{k-1}, X_j]$, so $-\varepsilon = \sum_j [a_j, X_j]$ with $a_j \in \mathfrak{n}_{k-1}$. Take $\delta_{2i-1} = a_{2i}$ and $\delta_{2i} = -a_{2i-1}$. Then the sum above is $\sum_j [a_j, X_j] = -\varepsilon$, and $\mathcal{R}(u') \in \mathfrak{n}_{\ge k+1}$. At $k = n + 1$ we get $\mathcal{R}(u) = 0$.

(ii) *A pro-$p$ group inside $(\mathfrak{n}, *)$.* Let $\Lambda = \bigoplus_{k \le n} \mathfrak{g}_k \subseteq \mathfrak{n}$, a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-lattice closed under brackets. Choose $h \ge 0$ such that $p^h$ times each coefficient of the series for $a * b$ and $c(a, b)$ in degrees at most $n$ lies in $\mathbb{Z}_p$; there are finitely many such coefficients. Fix an integer $N \ge h + 1$ and put $\Gamma_{N'} = p^{N'}\Lambda$ for $N' \ge N$. An iterated bracket of degree $m$ in elements of $p^{e_1}\Lambda, \dots, p^{e_m}\Lambda$ lies in $p^{e_1 + \dots + e_m}\Lambda$. Since every term of the two series beyond $a + b$ contains both arguments, for $a \in p^{N'}\Lambda$ and $b \in p^{N''}\Lambda$ with $N', N'' \ge N$,
$$a * b - a - b \in p^{N' + N'' - h}\Lambda, \qquad c(a, b) \in p^{N' + N'' - h}\Lambda.$$
So $\Gamma_N$ is a subgroup, as it also contains $-a$ with $a$. Each $\Gamma_{N'}$ is normal in $\Gamma_N$, because $b * a * (-b) = a * c(a, -b) \in \Gamma_{N'}$ for $a \in \Gamma_{N'}$ and $b \in \Gamma_N$. The map $\Gamma_{N'} \to p^{N'}\Lambda/p^{N'+1}\Lambda$, $a \mapsto a \bmod p^{N'+1}\Lambda$, is a homomorphism, since $2N' - h \ge N' + 1$, and its kernel is $\Gamma_{N'+1}$. So $(\Gamma_N : \Gamma_{N'})$ is a power of $p$. With the $p$-adic topology, $*$ is continuous, $\Gamma_N$ is compact, and the $\Gamma_{N'}$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of $0$. Hence $\Gamma_N$ is a pro-$p$ group.

(iii) *A homomorphism from $D$.* Take $u$ as in (i). The components of the $u_j$ lie in $p^{-f}\Lambda$ for some $f \ge 0$. With $t = p^{N+f}$, each $\sigma_t(u_j)$ lies in $\Gamma_N$, since the degree-$l$ component is multiplied by $t^l$ with $l \ge 1$. By the universal property of $F$ there is a continuous homomorphism $\psi : F \to \Gamma_N$ with $\psi(x_j) = \sigma_t(u_j)$. Then $\psi(r) = \mathcal{R}(\sigma_t u) = \sigma_t(\mathcal{R}(u)) = 0$, so $\psi$ factors through a continuous homomorphism $\bar\psi : D \to \Gamma_N$.

(iv) *Leading terms.* Since $D_{k+1}$ is topologically generated by the commutators $(a, b)$ with $a \in D_k$, and $\mathfrak{n}_{\ge k+1} \cap \Gamma_N$ is a closed subgroup, ($\dagger$) and induction give $\bar\psi(D_k) \subseteq \mathfrak{n}_{\ge k}$. For $k \le n$ and $d \in D_k$, let $\lambda_k(d) \in \mathfrak{n}_k$ be the degree-$k$ component of $\bar\psi(d)$. By ($\dagger$), $\lambda_k$ is additive and vanishes on $D_{k+1}$. It is continuous, so it induces a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear map $\lambda_k : \mathrm{gr}_k D \to \mathfrak{n}_k$. For $d \in D_a$ and $d' \in D_b$ with $a + b \le n$, ($\dagger$) gives $\lambda_{a+b}((d, d')) = [\lambda_a(d), \lambda_b(d')]$. So the $\lambda_k$ respect brackets, and $\lambda_1(\xi_j) = tX_j$.

(v) *Conclusion.* The composite $\lambda \circ \beta$ is $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear on $\bigoplus_{k \le n} \mathfrak{g}_k$, respects brackets, and sends $X_j$ to $tX_j$. Since $\mathfrak{g}_k$ is spanned by iterated brackets of the $X_j$, $\lambda(\beta(z)) = t^k z$ for $z \in \mathfrak{g}_k$. This is injective because $\mathfrak{g}_k$ is torsion-free, so $\beta$ is injective on $\mathfrak{g}_k$ for $k \le n$. As $n$ was arbitrary, $\beta$ is an isomorphism. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Fix a total order on $S$. In $E\langle S\rangle$, graded by word length, order the words of each length lexicographically. For a nonzero homogeneous $f$, let $\mathrm{lw}(f)$ be the largest word occurring in $f$. If $f, f'$ are nonzero and homogeneous, then $\mathrm{lw}(ff') = \mathrm{lw}(f)\,\mathrm{lw}(f')$, with coefficient the product of the two leading coefficients. Indeed, for words $w, w_1$ of one length and $w', w'_1$ of another, $ww' < w_1w'_1$ when $w < w_1$, or when $w = w_1$ and $w' < w'_1$. For a word $w$ let $\nu(w) \in \mathbb{N}^{(S)}$ count its letters.

*Commuting words.* If nonempty words $w, w'$ satisfy $ww' = w'w$, then they are powers of a common word. This follows by induction on $|w| + |w'|$: if $|w| \le |w'|$ then $w' = ww''$, and $ww'' = w''w$. Hence $\nu(w)/|w| = \nu(w')/|w'|$.

*Lemma W1.* If $a \in L_E(S)$ commutes with two distinct $s, t \in S$, then $a = 0$. Each word-length component $a_m$ of $a$, with $m \ge 1$, commutes with $s$, so if $a_m \ne 0$ then $\mathrm{lw}(a_m)$ commutes with $s$ and $\nu(\mathrm{lw}(a_m)) = m\,\nu(s)$, that is, $\mathrm{lw}(a_m) = s^m$. Likewise $\mathrm{lw}(a_m) = t^m$, which is impossible.

*Lemma W2.* Let $u, v \in L_E(S)$ be nonzero and homogeneous for word length, of lengths $a$ and $b$, with $[u, v] = 0$. Then $[u, [s, v]] \ne 0$ for some $s \in S$. Suppose not. Put $P = \mathrm{lw}(u)$ and $Q = \mathrm{lw}(v)$. Since $S$ has at least two elements, there is $s \in S$ with $Q \ne s^b$. The words $sQ$ and $Qs$ are distinct, since otherwise $\nu(Q)/b = \nu(s)$ and $Q = s^b$. So $sv - vs$ has leading word $R = \max(sQ, Qs)$ with nonzero coefficient, because every other word of $sv$ is smaller than $sQ$ and every other word of $vs$ is smaller than $Qs$. Now $uv = vu$ gives $PQ = QP$, and $u(sv - vs) = (sv - vs)u$ gives $PR = RP$. Hence
$$\frac{\nu(Q)}{b} = \frac{\nu(P)}{a} = \frac{\nu(R)}{b+1} = \frac{\nu(Q) + \nu(s)}{b+1},$$
so $\nu(Q) = b\,\nu(s)$, that is, $Q = s^b$, a contradiction.

*Proof of 2(a).* By part 1, $\mathrm{gr}\, D \otimes E = EX_1 \oplus \mathfrak{s}_E$, with $\xi_j$ corresponding to $X_j$. Write $z = \gamma X_1 + y$ with $\gamma \in E$ and $y \in \mathfrak{s}_E$; here $\gamma = 0$ unless $d = 1$. Inside $\mathfrak{s}_E = L_E(S)$, graded by word length in $S$,
$$[z, X_3] = \gamma\,\delta(Y_{3,0}) + [y, Y_{3,0}] = \gamma\,Y_{3,1} + [y, Y_{3,0}].$$
The first term has word length $1$ and the second has only components of length at least $2$. So $[z, X_3] = 0$ forces $\gamma = 0$ and $[y, Y_{3,0}] = 0$. Likewise $[y, Y_{4,0}] = 0$. As $g \ge 2$, $Y_{3,0} = X_3$ and $Y_{4,0} = X_4$ are distinct elements of $S$, so $y = 0$ by Lemma W1.

*Proof of 2(b).* Work in $\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = \mathbb{Q}_p X_1 \oplus \mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, and suppose that $I, J \ne 0$. First, $I \cap \mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \ne 0$. Take a nonzero homogeneous $z \in I$. If $z \notin \mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, then $z$ has degree $1$ and $z = \gamma X_1 + y$ with $\gamma \ne 0$. Then $[z, X_3] = \gamma Y_{3,1} + [y, Y_{3,0}]$ is a nonzero element of $I \cap \mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, as its word-length-$1$ component is $\gamma Y_{3,1}$. Likewise $J \cap \mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \ne 0$. Take nonzero $u \in I \cap \mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ and $v \in J \cap \mathfrak{s}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, and let $u_0, v_0$ be their components of lowest word length in $S$, say $a$ and $b$. From $[u, v] = 0$, the component of length $a + b$ gives $[u_0, v_0] = 0$. For $s \in S$, $[s, v] \in J$, so $[u, [s, v]] = 0$. Its component of length $a + b + 1$ is $[u_0, [s, v_0]]$, so $[u_0, [s, v_0]] = 0$ for every $s \in S$. This contradicts Lemma W2. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.**

(a) Classes of $D_i$ are central in $D/D_{i+1}$ because $(D_i, D) \subseteq D_{i+1}$. Conversely, let $x \in D$ be central modulo $D_{i+1}$, and suppose that $x \notin D_i$. Let $d < i$ be largest with $x \in D_d$, and let $\bar x \ne 0$ be its class in $\mathrm{gr}_d D$. For $j = 3, 4$, $(x, x_j) \in D_{i+1} \subseteq D_{d+2}$, so $[\bar x, \xi_j] = 0$ in $\mathrm{gr}_{d+1} D$. As $\mathrm{gr}\, D$ is torsion-free, 2(a) with $E = \mathbb{Q}_p$ gives $\bar x = 0$, a contradiction. If $x$ is central in $D$, then $x \in D_i$ for all $i$, so $x = 1$.

(b) Injectivity follows from 2(a) with $E = \mathbb{Q}_p$. If $\mathrm{ad}_i(z) \in p\,\mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D)$, then the image $\bar z$ of $z$ in $\mathrm{gr}_i D \otimes \mathbb{F}_p$ satisfies $[\bar z, \xi_3] = [\bar z, \xi_4] = 0$ in $\mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D \otimes \mathbb{F}_p$, which is $\mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D/p$ since $\mathrm{gr}\, D$ is free. By 2(a) with $E = \mathbb{F}_p$, $z = pz'$. So if $p\varphi = \mathrm{ad}_i(z)$, then $p(\varphi - \mathrm{ad}_i(z')) = 0$ and $\varphi = \mathrm{ad}_i(z')$. Hence the cokernel has no $p$-torsion, and no other torsion as it is a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module.

(c) Let $M \ne 1$ be a closed normal subgroup and $C$ its centralizer, a closed normal subgroup. For a closed normal subgroup $H$ put $\mathrm{gr}(H) = \bigoplus_b (H \cap D_b)D_{b+1}/D_{b+1} \subseteq \mathrm{gr}\, D$. It is a graded ideal, since $(H \cap D_b, D_a) \subseteq H \cap D_{a+b}$, and it is nonzero if $H \ne 1$, since $\bigcap_b D_b = 1$. For $m \in M \cap D_b$ and $w \in C \cap D_a$, $(m, w) = 1$, so $[\mathrm{gr}(M), \mathrm{gr}(C)] = 0$. Their $\mathbb{Q}_p$-spans in $\mathrm{gr}\, D \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ are graded ideals that commute, and the span of $\mathrm{gr}(M)$ is nonzero because $\mathrm{gr}\, D$ is torsion-free. By 2(b), $\mathrm{gr}(C) = 0$, so $C = 1$. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** The inner automorphisms form a normal subgroup, since $\alpha c_d \alpha^{-1} = c_{\alpha(d)}$.

(a) Let $\kappa \in K_i$. Then $\kappa(x)x^{-1} \in D_{i+1}$ for all $x$. The automorphism $\mathrm{gr}(\kappa)$ of $\mathrm{gr}\, D$ is the identity on $\mathrm{gr}_1 D$, and $\mathrm{gr}\, D$ is generated by $\mathrm{gr}_1 D$, so $\kappa$ acts trivially on $D_{i+1}/D_{i+2}$. We have
$$\kappa(xy)(xy)^{-1} = \kappa(x)x^{-1} \cdot x\big(\kappa(y)y^{-1}\big)x^{-1},$$
and conjugation acts trivially on $D_{i+1}/D_{i+2}$. So $x \mapsto \kappa(x)x^{-1}D_{i+2}$ is a continuous homomorphism $D \to \mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D$. It factors through $D^{\mathrm{ab}} = V$ and is $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear; this is $J_i(\kappa)$. Next, $\kappa\kappa'(x)x^{-1} = \kappa\big(\kappa'(x)x^{-1}\big) \cdot \kappa(x)x^{-1}$, and $\kappa$ fixes $\kappa'(x)x^{-1}$ modulo $D_{i+2}$. So $J_i(\kappa\kappa') = J_i(\kappa) + J_i(\kappa')$. Clearly $J_i(\kappa) = 0$ exactly when $\kappa \in K_{i+1}$. Finally, $\alpha\kappa\alpha^{-1}(x)x^{-1} = \alpha\big(\kappa(y)y^{-1}\big)$ with $y = \alpha^{-1}(x)$, which gives the equivariance. As $\mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D$ is torsion-free, so is $\mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D)$, and hence $K_i/K_{i+1}$.

(b) $c_d \in K_i$ means that $dxd^{-1}x^{-1} \in D_{i+1}$ for all $x$, that is, $d$ is central modulo $D_{i+1}$. By 3(a) this means $d \in D_i$. For $d \in D_i$, $dxd^{-1}x^{-1} = (d^{-1}, x^{-1})$, whose class in $\mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D$ is $[-\bar d, -\bar x] = [\bar d, \bar x]$. So $J_i(c_d) = \mathrm{ad}_i(\bar d)$.

(c) The subgroup $DK_{i+1}$ is normal in $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$. The map $K_i \to DK_i/DK_{i+1}$ is onto with kernel $K_i \cap DK_{i+1} = (K_i \cap D)K_{i+1} = D_iK_{i+1}$, by the modular law, since $K_{i+1} \subseteq K_i$, and by (b). By (a) and (b), $J_i$ embeds $K_i/D_iK_{i+1}$ into $\mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D)/\mathrm{ad}_i(\mathrm{gr}_i D)$, which is torsion-free by 3(b).

(d) If $\kappa \in K_i$ for all $i$, then $\kappa(x)x^{-1} \in \bigcap_i D_{i+1} = 1$. For the second statement, evaluation $\alpha \mapsto \alpha(x)$ is continuous, since $\alpha\beta(x) \in \alpha(x)U$ when $\beta$ acts trivially on $D/U$ for a characteristic open $U$. So each $K_i$ is closed. The map $d \mapsto c_d$ is continuous, since $c_u$ acts trivially on $D/U$ for $u \in U$, so its image $D$ is compact. Let $x \in \bigcap_i DK_i$. The sets $D \cap xK_i$ are nonempty, closed and decreasing, so they have a common element $d$. Then $x^{-1}d \in \bigcap_i K_i = 1$, so $x \in D$.

(e) $\mathrm{gr}(\alpha)$ is a graded Lie algebra automorphism that is multiplication by $\lambda$ on $\mathrm{gr}_1 D$, which generates, so it is multiplication by $\lambda^k$ on $\mathrm{gr}_k D$. By (a), $J_i(\alpha\kappa\alpha^{-1}) = \lambda^{i+1} J_i(\kappa)\lambda^{-1}$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Genus $1$.* For $g = 1$, part 1 says $\mathrm{gr}\, D = V$, as $D \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^2$. Parts 2 and 3 fail for $g = 1$, since $D$ is abelian.
- *Use for Tsujimura's question.* For $D$ with $g \ge 2$, part 3(c) is the property of a free pro-$p$ group $F$ that Tsujimura's Lemma 3.3 uses, that nontrivial closed normal subgroups have trivial centralizers. Part 4 gives the analogues of his Proposition 1.2 (ii), (iii) and (v)–(vii), with $\mathrm{Hom}(F^{\mathrm{ab}}, F_{i+1}/F_{i+2})$ replaced by $\mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D)$ [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Proposition 1.2, p. 4, and Lemma 3.3, p. 9]. The analogue of his Proposition 1.2 (iv), $[K_i, K_j] \subseteq K_{i+j}$, is not proved here. His Proposition 1.2 (i), surjectivity of $\mathrm{Aut}(F) \to \mathrm{GL}(F^{\mathrm{ab}})$, is replaced for $D$ by [symplectic similitudes lift](symplectic-similitudes-lift-to-pro-p-surface-groups.md). Whether the rest of his argument goes through is the [open question](demushkin-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md).
- *Rank.* By part 1, the rank of $\mathrm{gr}_k D$ is the rank of $\mathfrak{s}_k$, plus $1$ for $k = 1$, where $\mathfrak{s}$ is free on $S$, and $S$ has $2g - 1$ elements of degree $1$ and $2g - 2$ of each degree $m \ge 2$.
