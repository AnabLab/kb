**Setting.** Let $k$ be a number field, $\ell$ a prime and $C$ a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $k$. Let $\Delta_C$ be the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of $\pi_1(C \otimes_k \bar k)$, let $1 \to \Delta_C \to \Pi_C \to G_k \to 1$ be the induced extension, and let $\rho_C : G_k \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_C)$ be the outer representation. Put $\Gamma_C = \rho_C(G_k)$, and let $\Omega_C$ be the fixed field of $\ker \rho_C$, so that $\Gamma_C = \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_C/k)$ [@hoshi2015, Definition 1.1, p. 652]. Write $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ for $\Omega_C$ when $C$ is the tripod $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$. For an abelian variety $A$ over $k$ the same construction gives the $\ell$-adic Tate module, and $\Omega_A = k(A[\ell^\infty])$ [@hoshi2015, Remarks 1.1.1 and 1.3.6 (i), pp. 652–654].

Hoshi records these general facts [@hoshi2015, Proposition 1.2, pp. 653–654]. The group $\Gamma_C$ has an open pro-$\ell$ subgroup, and it is pro-$\ell$ if $G_k$ acts on $\Delta_C^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{F}_\ell$ through a pro-$\ell$ quotient. For a prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $k$ not above $\ell$, $C$ has good reduction at $\mathfrak{p}$ exactly when $\Omega_C/k$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$. Finally $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \subseteq \Omega_C$, by part 1 of the Hoshi–Mochizuki theorem in [injectivity of outer Galois representations](injectivity-of-outer-galois-representations.md).

**Moderate points.** Since $\Delta_C$ is center-free [@hoshi2015, proof of Lemma 2.1, p. 658], the part of $\Pi_C$ over $\ker \rho_C$ has a unique splitting with normal image, and Hoshi puts $\Phi_C = \Pi_C / \mathrm{Im}(s_C)$ for that splitting $s_C$. This gives an extension $1 \to \Delta_C \to \Phi_C \to \Gamma_C \to 1$ with $\Pi_C = \Phi_C \times_{\Gamma_C} G_k$ [@hoshi2015, Lemma 2.1 and Definition 2.2, pp. 658–659]. As checked here, $\Phi_C$ is the image of $\Pi_C$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta_C)$ under conjugation. The kernel $Z$ of $\Pi_C \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Delta_C)$ is normal, meets $\Delta_C$ trivially because $\Delta_C$ is center-free, and lies over $\ker \rho_C$. An element of $\Pi_C$ over $\ker \rho_C$ acts on $\Delta_C$ as conjugation by some $\delta \in \Delta_C$, and multiplying it by $\delta^{-1}$ gives an element of $Z$. So $Z$ maps isomorphically onto $\ker \rho_C$, and it is the image of $s_C$, by uniqueness.

A point $x \in C(k)$ gives a section $s_x : G_k \to \Pi_C$, well defined up to conjugation by $\Delta_C$. Hoshi calls $x$ *$\ell$-moderate* if $s_x$ arises from a splitting of $\Phi_C \to \Gamma_C$ [@hoshi2015, Definition 2.4 (1), p. 659]. Since $\Pi_C = \Phi_C \times_{\Gamma_C} G_k$, this means that the conjugation action $G_k \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Delta_C)$ through $s_x$ kills $\ker \rho_C$, as checked here. That kernel always lies in $\ker \rho_C$, since composing with $\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta_C) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_C)$ gives $\rho_C$. The same definitions apply over every number field. A closed point $x$ is $\ell$-moderate if every point of $C \otimes_k \kappa(x)$ over $x$ that is rational over $\kappa(x)$ is $\ell$-moderate for that curve over $\kappa(x)$, where $\kappa(x)$ is the Galois closure over $k$ of the residue field [@hoshi2015, Definitions 2.3 and 2.4 (2), p. 659].

For $x \in C(k)$ and $U = C \setminus \{x\}$, the following are equivalent [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.5, pp. 659–660]:

- $x$ is $\ell$-moderate;
- $\Omega_U = \Omega_C$;
- $\Omega_U / \Omega_C$ is finite;
- the conjugation action $G_k \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Delta_C)$ through $s_x$ has kernel exactly $\ker \rho_C$.

The last condition was introduced by Matsumoto, who studied it for proper curves [@hoshi2015, Introduction, p. 649, and Remark 2.5.1, p. 660].

*Examples.* Every nontrivial $\ell$-power torsion point of an elliptic curve $E$ is an $\ell$-moderate point of $E \setminus \{O\}$ [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.7, p. 662]. For the tripod, call a finite set $S \subset \mathbb{P}^1(\bar k)$ admissible if it is reached from $\{0, 1, \infty\}$ by finitely many steps of two kinds: replace $S$ by $\{x : x^\ell \in S\}$, with $\infty^\ell = \infty$, or by its image under the Möbius transformation sending three distinct elements of $S$ to $0$, $1$ and $\infty$. A *tripod $\ell$-unit* is an element other than $0$ and $\infty$ of an admissible set [@hoshi2015, Definitions 1.4 and 1.6, pp. 655–656]. The points of the tripod given by tripod $\ell$-units are $\ell$-moderate [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.8, pp. 662–663]. Let $S \subset \mathbb{P}^1(k)$ be finite and contain $0$, $1$ and $\infty$. Anderson and Ihara proved that $\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S}$ is generated over $k$ by the elements other than $0$ and $\infty$ of the sets reached from $S$ by the same two kinds of step. In particular $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ is generated by the tripod $\ell$-units, whose products are Anderson and Ihara's higher circular $\ell$-units [@hoshi2015, Theorem 1.5, Definition 1.6 and Remark 1.6.1, pp. 655–656].

*Dependence on $\ell$.* The point $\zeta_\ell$ of the tripod over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)$ is $\ell$-moderate, but it is not $\ell'$-moderate for any prime $\ell' \ne \ell$, because $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty, \zeta_\ell\}$ has bad reduction at the prime above $\ell$. So its pro-$\ell'$ kernel field is ramified there, while that of the tripod is not [@hoshi2015, Remark 2.8.1, p. 663].

**Question** (Hoshi). Let $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ be the compositum of the fields $\kappa(x)$ for the $\ell$-moderate closed points $x$ of $C$. Is $\Omega_C = k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$? [@hoshi2015, Introduction, p. 649, and Remark 2.6.2, p. 662]

The model is the abelian case, where $\Omega_A$ is generated by torsion points. A naive analogue takes the points of $C$ that are $\ell$-power torsion under an embedding of the compactification of $C$ into its Jacobian. Hoshi observes that these cannot generate $\Omega_C$, because by Raynaud's theorem there are only finitely many of them, while $\Omega_C/k$ is infinite [@hoshi2015, Introduction, p. 648]. Hoshi states this for every hyperbolic curve, but the finiteness argument needs the compactification to have genus at least two. In genus one the finiteness fails. For $C = E \setminus \{O\}$, with $E$ embedded in itself as its own Jacobian, the $\ell^{2r} - 1$ nonzero points of $E[\ell^r](\bar k)$ lie on $C$ for every $r \ge 1$. These points are $\ell$-moderate, by the examples above, so they are among the generators in the question below.

**Known.**

- *One inclusion.* Every $\ell$-moderate point is defined over $\Omega_C$, that is, $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq \Omega_C$ [@hoshi2015, Theorem 3.1, p. 664]. In the proof, an $\ell$-moderate point $x$ yields a pro-$\ell$ section of $\Pi_C \to G_k$, after a base change inside $\Omega_C$, whose restriction to $G_{\kappa(x)}$ is the section of $x$. Two lemmas from Hoshi's work on the birational section conjecture then show that the section comes from a $k$-rational point of the compactification of $C$, which must be $x$ [@hoshi2015, proof of Theorem 3.1, p. 664]. So the question is whether $\Omega_C \subseteq k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$.
- *Cases of equality.* Equality holds in each of these cases [@hoshi2015, Theorem B, pp. 649–650, Corollary 3.3 and Example 3.4, pp. 666–670]:
  1. $C \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus S$ for an admissible $S$ with $S \setminus \{\infty\} \subseteq k$, such as the tripod;
  2. $\ell$ is odd and $C$ is the affine Fermat curve $s^{\ell^n} + t^{\ell^n} + 1 = 0$, for some $n \ge 1$;
  3. $\ell$ is odd and $C$ is the projective Fermat curve $s^{\ell^n} + t^{\ell^n} + u^{\ell^n} = 0$, for some $n \ge 1$ with $(\ell, n) \ne (3, 1)$;
  4. $\ell = 3$, $k$ contains a primitive $3^n$-th root of unity, and $C$ is the modular curve $Y(3^n)$, for some $n \ge 1$;
  5. $\ell = 3$, $k$ contains a primitive $3^n$-th root of unity, and $C$ is the modular curve $X(3^n)$, for some $n \ge 2$.

  Case 1 follows from the inclusion above and the Anderson–Ihara theorem [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.3 and its proof, pp. 666–667]. The other cases use two tools. The first passes to finite étale covers of a restricted kind. Let $X \to C$ be a connected finite étale cover that arises from an open subgroup of the geometrically pro-$\ell$ group $\Pi_C$, with $X$ geometrically connected over $k$. Then a closed point of $X$ is $\ell$-moderate exactly when its image in $C$ is, and $\Omega_X$ is a finite extension of $\Omega_C$ [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.2 (i) and (ii), pp. 665–666]. Hoshi states this only for such covers, not for arbitrary finite étale covers, which correspond to open subgroups of the full $\pi_1(C)$.

  The second tool passes from an open subcurve to the whole curve. If $U \subseteq X$ is a nonempty open subscheme of a hyperbolic curve, then $\Omega_X \subseteq \Omega_U$ [@hoshi2015, Remark 1.1.2, p. 653]. Suppose that $\Omega_U = \Omega_X$. Then every $\ell$-moderate closed point of $U$ is $\ell$-moderate on $X$, so equality for $U$ gives equality for $X$. Hoshi argues this way when $X$ is the compactification of $U$ [@hoshi2015, Example 3.4 (ii), p. 668]. As checked here, the argument needs only $\Omega_U = \Omega_X$. Let $P$ be an $\ell$-moderate closed point of $U$, and put $k' = \kappa(P)$. Base change to $k'$ restricts the outer representations to $G_{k'}$, so it replaces each $\Omega$ by its compositum with $k'$, and $\Omega_{U_{k'}} = \Omega_{X_{k'}}$. For a $k'$-rational point $Q$ over $P$, Remark 1.1.2 and Proposition 2.5 give
  $$\Omega_{X_{k'}} \subseteq \Omega_{X_{k'} \setminus \{Q\}} \subseteq \Omega_{U_{k'} \setminus \{Q\}} = \Omega_{U_{k'}} = \Omega_{X_{k'}},$$
  so $Q$ is $\ell$-moderate on $X_{k'}$, and $P$ is $\ell$-moderate on $X$. If moreover $\Omega_U = k_U^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$, then Theorem 3.1 gives $\Omega_X = \Omega_U = k_U^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq k_X^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq \Omega_X$.

  *Case 2.* Hoshi uses the map $u \mapsto s^{\ell^n}$ from the affine Fermat curve $X$ to the tripod [@hoshi2015, Example 3.4 (i), p. 667]. As printed, this does not map $X$ to the tripod, because the $k$-rational point $(s, t) = (0, -1)$ of $X$ goes to $u = 0$. The argument works for a smaller curve, as checked here. Put $m = \ell^n$, which is odd, and let $V \subset X$ be the open curve where $st \ne 0$. In the coordinates $x = -s$ and $y = -t$, the curve $X$ is $x^m + y^m = 1$. The map $u = x^m = -s^m$ has $1 - u = y^m = -t^m$, so it sends $V$ to the tripod. It extends to a map of degree $m^2$ from the smooth projective Fermat curve $F \supset X$ to $\mathbb{P}^1$. The full preimages of $0$, $1$ and $\infty$ are the $m$ points where $s = 0$, the $m$ points where $t = 0$ and the $m$ points at infinity of $X$, each with ramification index $m$. These $3m$ points are exactly the points of $F$ outside $V$, and there is no other ramification, since a value $u \ne 0, 1, \infty$ has the $m^2$ distinct preimages with $x^m = u$ and $y^m = 1 - u$. So $V$ is finite étale over the tripod. It is geometrically connected, and over $\bar k$ it is Galois with group $\mu_m \times \mu_m$, acting on $x$ and $y$. That is an $\ell$-group, so the open subgroup of $\pi_1$ of the tripod that corresponds to $V$ contains the kernel of $\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^1_{\bar k} \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}) \to \Delta_{\mathrm{tpd}}$, and $V$ arises from an open subgroup of $\Pi_{\mathrm{tpd}}$.

  Now let $a$ be a tripod $\ell$-unit other than $1$. The points of $V$ over $a$ have $x^m = a$ and $y^m = 1 - a$. Such $x$ and $y$ are again tripod $\ell$-units. For $x$, apply the first step $n$ times to an admissible set containing $a$. For $y$, first apply the Möbius step $w \mapsto 1 - w$, which sends $1$, $0$ and $\infty$ to $0$, $1$ and $\infty$; every admissible set contains these three points, by induction on the steps. So these points are defined over $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$, which the tripod $\ell$-units generate, and they are $\ell$-moderate by Proposition 2.8 and Corollary 3.2 (i). Corollary 3.2 (i) then gives $\Omega_V = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$, and Corollaries 3.2 (ii) and 3.3 give $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} = k_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq k_V^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq \Omega_V$, where the first field is $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ for the tripod. This is Hoshi's argument with $V$ in place of $X$. Finally $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \subseteq \Omega_X \subseteq \Omega_V$, by Proposition 1.2 (vi) and Remark 1.1.2, so $\Omega_X = \Omega_V$, and the second tool gives equality for $X$.

  *Cases 3 to 5.* Since $\zeta_3 \in k$ in case 4, the curve $Y(3)$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{1, \zeta_3, \zeta_3^2, \infty\}$, and $Y(3^n)$ is a cover of $Y(3)$ of the kind in Corollary 3.2. For $n \ge 2$, Hoshi also uses the positive answer to the Anderson–Ihara question for $\ell = 3$ [@hoshi2015, Theorem 1.9, pp. 657–658, and Example 3.4 (iv), pp. 669–670]. Cases 3 and 5 pass from the open curves of cases 2 and 4 to their compactifications by the second tool [@hoshi2015, p. 650, and Example 3.4 (ii) and (v), pp. 668–670].
- *Transfer principles and further cases.* Both tools hold in general, and equality also descends along a finite extension $k'/k$ contained in $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$; see the [transfer principles](moderate-point-equality-transfer-principles.md). In particular, equality passes from $C$ to every geometrically connected finite étale cover $X$ whose Galois closure over $\bar k$ has $\ell$-power degree, and then $\Omega_X = \Omega_C \cdot \kappa(x)$ for every $\ell$-moderate closed point $x$ of $X$. This gives equality for $Y(3^n)$ without the Anderson–Ihara answer, and for twisted Kummer covers of the tripod. With these principles, the once-punctured elliptic curves $y^2 = x^3 - Dx$ at $\ell = 2$ and $y^2 = x^3 + D$ at $\ell = 3$ satisfy equality over every number field, with $\Omega_C$ equal to $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(D^{1/4})$ and $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(\sqrt{D}, \sqrt[3]{4D})$ respectively; see [elliptic curves with extra automorphisms](moderate-point-equality-for-elliptic-curves-with-extra-automorphisms.md).
- *Consequences of a positive answer.* Equality for $C$ implies that $\Omega_C$ is finite over $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$, hence that $C$ has infinitely many $\ell$-moderate points, since $\Omega_C/k$ is infinite. Any $\ell$-moderate point is defined over $\Omega_C$, which lies in the maximal pro-$\ell$ extension of the finite extension $k_C^0$ of $k$ cut out by the action on $\Delta_C^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{F}_\ell$. So as soon as $C$ has an $\ell$-moderate point, which a positive answer guarantees, $C$ has a point over that pro-$\ell$ extension, and hence over the maximal solvable extension of $k_C^0$ [@hoshi2015, Remark 3.1.1, pp. 664–665].
- *Moderate points are not torsion in the Jacobian.* For $\ell \ge 5$, the Fermat curve $x^\ell + y^\ell + z^\ell = 0$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ satisfies the equality, so it has infinitely many $\ell$-moderate points. By Raynaud's theorem, then, the difference of two $\ell$-moderate points need not be torsion in the Jacobian [@hoshi2015, Remarks 2.6.3 and 3.4.1, pp. 662 and 670].

**Every case of equality in Hoshi's list has the field of the tripod.** This is checked here. In each case of the list above, $\Omega_C = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$. Hoshi shows this along the way in cases 2–5 [@hoshi2015, Example 3.4, pp. 667–670]. In case 2 it rests on the corrected argument above, which gives $\Omega_X = \Omega_V = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$. In case 1, $S$ contains $\{0, 1, \infty\}$, and both are admissible [@hoshi2015, Lemma 1.7 (i), p. 656]. Hoshi's Lemma 1.7 (v), applied with his $S$, $T'$, $S'$ and $T$ equal to $\{0, 1, \infty\}$, $\{0, 1, \infty\}$, $S$ and $S$, shows that the generators that the Anderson–Ihara theorem attaches to $S$ and to $\{0, 1, \infty\}$ generate the same field over $k$. By that theorem, these fields are $\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus S}$ and $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ [@hoshi2015, Lemma 1.7 (v) and Theorem 1.5, pp. 655–657].

So Hoshi's cases say nothing about any $C$ with $\Omega_C \ne \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$. That includes every $C$ with bad reduction at some prime $\mathfrak{p}$ not above $\ell$. Indeed, the tripod has good reduction at $\mathfrak{p}$, because $0$, $1$ and $\infty$ stay distinct modulo every prime, so $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$ while $\Omega_C$ is not, by Hoshi's Proposition 1.2 (ii) above. For example, if an elliptic curve $E$ over $k$ has bad reduction at $\mathfrak{p}$, so does $E \setminus \{O\}$, since a good model of $E \setminus \{O\}$ would have as its compactification a good model of $E$.

The further cases in the bullet on transfer principles include such curves, for example $y^2 = x^3 - 3x$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ minus its origin, at $\ell = 2$. In every case now known, though, $\Omega_C$ is a finite extension of $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$, as checked here. Each case starts from some $\mathbb{P}^1_{k'} \setminus S$, with $k'$ equal to $k$ or a finite extension of it and $S$ admissible with $S \setminus \{\infty\} \subseteq k'$, whose field is $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k'}$ by the paragraph above. It then uses three moves: covers, which give finite extensions [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.2 (ii), p. 666]; open subcurves with the same field; and descent to $k$, which does not change the field. Since $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k'} = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot k'$ is finite over $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$, so is $\Omega_C$. So the question is open, as far as the sources opened here show, for every $C$ with $\Omega_C$ infinite over $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$.

**The abelian analogue of the inclusion fails.** This is checked here. For an abelian variety $A$ over $k$, a point of $A(k)$ is $\ell$-moderate exactly when it is torsion, of any order [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.6, pp. 660–661]. So the $\ell$-moderate closed points of $A$ generate the field $k(A_{\mathrm{tors}})$ of all torsion points, while $\Omega_A = k(A[\ell^\infty])$. These differ in general. Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $k$, and let $m \ne \ell$ be a rational prime such that $E$ has good reduction at every prime above $m$, which holds for all but finitely many $m$. Then $k(E[\ell^\infty])/k$ is unramified above $m$, by Proposition 1.2 (ii) above. But $k(E[m^r])$ contains the $m^r$-th roots of unity, by the [Weil pairing](weil-pairing-and-cyclotomic-character.md) [@sutherland2025weil, Corollary 23.24, p. 13]. Once $m^{r-1}(m-1) > [k:\mathbb{Q}]$, the extension $k(\mu_{m^r})/k$ is ramified above $m$, because $m$ has ramification index $m^{r-1}(m-1)$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{m^r})$, and this index is at most $[k:\mathbb{Q}]$ times the ramification index above $m$ in $k(\mu_{m^r})/k$. So $E$ has $\ell$-moderate points that are not defined over $\Omega_E$. The proof of the inclusion for curves rests on the theory of pro-$\ell$ sections of hyperbolic curves, which has no counterpart for abelian varieties.

**Relation to the Anderson–Ihara question.** For the tripod over $\mathbb{Q}$, the Anderson–Ihara theorem describes $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/\mathbb{Q}}$ by moderate points, for every prime $\ell$. The [Anderson–Ihara question](anderson-ihara-question.md) asks for an arithmetic description: is $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/\mathbb{Q}} = \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{un}\text{-}\ell}$, the maximal Galois extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ that is unramified at the finite primes other than $\ell$, contains $\zeta_\ell$, and is pro-$\ell$ over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)$ [@hoshi2015, Definition 1.8 and Remark 1.8.1, p. 657]? As checked here, $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{un}\text{-}\ell}$ is the field called $\Omega_p$ there, with $p = \ell$. It is a pro-$\ell$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)$ unramified at every finite prime not above $\ell$, so it lies in $\Omega_p$ by maximality. Conversely, the conjugates of $\Omega_p$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ are extensions of the same kind, so they lie in $\Omega_p$. Hence $\Omega_p$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$, and it lies in $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{un}\text{-}\ell}$ by maximality.

Suppose that question has a positive answer for $\ell$, as it does for odd regular $\ell$ [@hoshi2015, Theorem 1.9, pp. 657–658]. Hoshi shows that if $C$ is proper over a field $k \subseteq \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{un}\text{-}\ell}$, with $C(k)$ nonempty and $\Omega_C = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/\mathbb{Q}}$, then the $\ell$-moderate closed points of $C$ are exactly its points over $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{un}\text{-}\ell}$ [@hoshi2015, Lemma 3.5, pp. 670–671]. For odd regular $\ell$ and $(\ell, n) \ne (3, 1)$, this applies to the projective Fermat curve of degree $\ell^n$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ [@hoshi2015, Remark 3.5.1, p. 671].

Hoshi also applies this to Fermat's equation. Let $\ell \ge 5$, suppose the Anderson–Ihara question has a positive answer for $\ell$, and let $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{un}\text{-}\ell} \setminus \{0, 1\}$ satisfy $a^\ell + b^\ell = 1$. Let $C$ be the tripod over $\mathbb{Q}(a^\ell)$. Then the section of $\Pi_C \to G_{\mathbb{Q}(a^\ell)}$ given by the point $a^\ell$ has image in $\Phi_C$ that is not open, which is what it means for $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty, a^\ell\}$ over $\mathbb{Q}(a^\ell)$ not to be [quasi-$\ell$-monodromically full](monodromically-full-hyperbolic-curve.md) [@hoshi2015, Theorem C, p. 650, and Corollary 3.6, p. 672]. A positive answer to the Anderson–Ihara question for every $\ell$, together with a positive answer to a [problem of Matsumoto and Tamagawa](matsumoto-tamagawa-problem-on-monodromic-fullness.md) on monodromic fullness, would imply Fermat's Last Theorem. Hoshi answered that problem in the negative in a separate paper [@hoshi2015, Remark 3.6.1, pp. 672–673].

**Status.** Hoshi wrote that he did not know whether $\Omega_C \subseteq k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ holds in general [@hoshi2015, Introduction, p. 649]. In a later paper he calls a $k$-rational point *moderate* if it is $p$-moderate for some prime $p$. He proves there that if $E$ is an elliptic curve over a number field $k$, then $E \setminus \{O\}$ has only finitely many moderate $k$-rational points, and that the same holds for a nonsplit one-dimensional torus with its identity removed [@hoshi2016moderate, Introduction, p. 2, and Theorem A, p. 2]. This is the analogue of the finiteness of torsion points over a fixed number field. Since it concerns points over a fixed field, it neither answers the question nor conflicts with the infinitely many $\ell$-moderate closed points, over growing fields, that a positive answer would give. A literature search in November 2026 found no case of equality beyond Hoshi's and no counterexample, but it was not exhaustive. The further cases above are checked in this knowledgebase; that search did not find them in print.
