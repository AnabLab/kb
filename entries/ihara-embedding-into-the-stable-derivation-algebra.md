**Setting.** Fix a prime $\ell$. As in the [Deligne–Ihara conjecture](deligne-ihara-conjecture.md), let $\phi_\ell : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathrm{Out}\, \pi^{(\ell)}$ be the outer action on the pro-$\ell$ completion of $\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C}) \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\})$, let $I^m_\ell G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ be Ihara's filtration, and let $\mathfrak{g}^\ell = \bigoplus_{m \ge 1} I^m_\ell G_{\mathbb{Q}}/I^{m+1}_\ell G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, a graded Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. The action factors through the Galois group $G_\ell$ of the maximal extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ unramified outside $\ell$, and Hain and Matsumoto write $\mathrm{Gr}^{>0}_{I_\ell} G_\ell$ for the corresponding graded Lie algebra [@hainmatsumoto2003, §8.4, p. 27]. It is $\mathfrak{g}^\ell$, since the filtration of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is the preimage of that of $G_\ell$. By the Deligne–Ihara theorem, $\mathfrak{g}^\ell \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is free on Soulé elements $\sigma_3, \sigma_5, \dots$, with $\sigma_m$ of degree $m$, so its degree-$w$ part has dimension $f_w$, the dimension in degree $w$ of a free graded Lie algebra on one generator in each odd degree at least $3$. The values of $f_w$ for $w \le 12$ are tabulated in that entry.

**The stable derivation algebra.** Let $\mathbb{L} = \bigoplus_{w \ge 1} \mathbb{L}_w$ be the free graded Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ on $x$ and $y$, both of degree $1$. For $f \in \mathbb{L}$, let $D_f$ be the derivation of $\mathbb{L}$ with $D_f(x) = 0$ and $D_f(y) = [y, f]$. Then $[D_f, D_g] = D_h$ with $h = [f, g] + D_f(g) - D_g(f)$. The stable derivation algebra is $\mathfrak{D} = \bigoplus_{w \ge 1} \mathfrak{D}_w$, where $\mathfrak{D}_w$ is the space of $D_f$ with $f \in \mathbb{L}_w$ such that

- (0) $f \in [\mathbb{L}, \mathbb{L}]$,
- (i) $f(x, y) + f(y, x) = 0$,
- (ii) $f(x, y) + f(y, z) + f(z, x) = 0$ for $x + y + z = 0$,
- (iii) $\sum_{i \in \mathbb{Z}/5} f(x_{i,i+1}, x_{i+1,i+2}) = 0$ in $\mathfrak{P}^{(5)}$.

Here $f(\alpha, \beta)$ is the image of $f$ under $x \mapsto \alpha$, $y \mapsto \beta$, and $\mathfrak{P}^{(5)}$ is the graded Lie algebra on generators $x_{i,j}$, $1 \le i, j \le 5$, with relations $x_{i,i} = 0$, $x_{i,j} = x_{j,i}$, $\sum_k x_{i,k} = 0$ for each $i$, and $[x_{i,j}, x_{k,l}] = 0$ when $\{i, j\} \cap \{k, l\} = \emptyset$. Each $D \in \mathfrak{D}$ determines $f$, and (iii) implies (i) [@furusho2003mzv, §2.1, p. 7, and §2.2, pp. 8–9]. Each $\mathfrak{D}_w$ is finite-dimensional, being isomorphic to a subspace of $\mathbb{L}_w$.

Ihara defined it through the Lie algebras $\mathfrak{P}^{(n)}$ of the pure braid groups: the symmetric special outer derivations of $\mathfrak{P}^{(n)}$ form a tower that he proved to be stable for $n \ge 5$, and he identified its stable term with $\mathfrak{D}$ [@furusho2003mzv, §2.1, pp. 7–8, and §2.2, p. 8]. Drinfeld studied the same Lie algebra independently as the graded Lie algebra version of the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group, where it appears as $\bigoplus_n \mathfrak{grt}_1^n(\mathbb{Q})$ [@furusho2003mzv, §0, p. 2, and §2.2, p. 9]. Conditions (i) and (ii), with $z = -x - y$, are equations 1 and 2 in the definition of $\mathfrak{grt}_1$ in the [freeness theorem's setting](freeness-of-the-grothendieck-teichmuller-lie-algebra.md), the bracket is the Ihara bracket used there, and the pentagon equation is written in $\mathfrak{P}^{(5)}$ instead of in the Drinfeld–Kohno Lie algebra.

**Low weights.** By computations of Ihara, Matsumoto and Tsunogai, for $w \le 12$ [@furusho2003mzv, Example 2.3.2, pp. 9–10]:

| $w$ | 1, 2, 4, 6 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{D}_w$ | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| basis | | $D_{f_3}$ | $D_{f_5}$ | $D_{f_7}$ | $[D_{f_3}, D_{f_5}]$ | $D_{f_9}$ | $[D_{f_3}, D_{f_7}]$ | $D_{f_{11}}$, $[D_{f_3}, [D_{f_3}, D_{f_5}]]$ | $[D_{f_3}, D_{f_9}]$, $[D_{f_5}, D_{f_7}]$ |

Here $f_w \in \mathbb{L}_w$, with $f_3 = [x, [x, y]] + [y, [x, y]]$, the element found by hand in weight $3$ in the freeness theorem's **Small weights**. The row of dimensions is $f_w$ for every $w \le 12$. These computations have not been checked here.

**Theorem (Ihara).** For every prime $\ell$, $\mathfrak{g}^\ell$ is contained in $\mathcal{D} \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$, where $\mathcal{D}$ is a $\mathbb{Z}$-Lie algebra of outer derivations of the free $\mathbb{Z}$-Lie algebra on two generators, which Hain and Matsumoto call the stable derivation algebra [@hainmatsumoto2003, Remark 8.6, p. 27]. Furusho states the rational form: there is an injective homomorphism of graded Lie algebras
$$\Phi_\ell : \mathfrak{g}^\ell \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell \hookrightarrow \mathfrak{D} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}_\ell,$$
sending degree $w$ to weight $w$, where he describes the source as the graded Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ constructed from the image of $\phi_\ell$ [@furusho2003mzv, §0, p. 2, and §2.4, pp. 10–11]. That description is brief, but he credits Hain and Matsumoto with proving the generation conjecture for this Lie algebra [@furusho2003mzv, §2.4, p. 11], and they prove it for $\mathfrak{g}^\ell \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ [@hainmatsumoto2003, Conjecture 1, p. 2, and §8.4, p. 27], so the source is $\mathfrak{g}^\ell \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$. Neither source gives the proof of the embedding. Furusho refers to Ihara's address at the 1990 International Congress of Mathematicians for details [@furusho2003mzv, §2.4, p. 11], and that paper has not been opened here.

**Ihara's conjecture.** Ihara conjectured that $\Phi_\ell$ is an isomorphism for every prime $\ell$, so that $\mathfrak{D}$, which is defined combinatorially, would be a common $\mathbb{Q}$-structure for all the $\mathfrak{g}^\ell \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$. Furusho lists it with the conjectures that these Lie algebras are free, on one generator in each odd degree $m \ge 3$. He attributes all three to Ihara's 1990 address and a 1999 preprint of Ihara, and partly to Deligne [@furusho2003mzv, §0, p. 2, and Conjecture 2.4.1, p. 11]. Hain and Matsumoto report that Ihara also asked whether $\mathcal{D}$ is generated by derivations $D_{2m+1}$ analogous to the $\sigma_{2m+1}$ [@hainmatsumoto2003, Remark 8.6, p. 27]. The conjecture now holds, by item 5 below.

**Consequences.** These are checked here, from the theorem, the Deligne–Ihara theorem and the definitions above. Dimensions over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ of $\mathfrak{D}_w \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ equal dimensions over $\mathbb{Q}$ of $\mathfrak{D}_w$.

1. *Lower bound.* $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{D}_w \ge f_w$ for every $w$, since $\Phi_\ell$ is injective and graded and its source has dimension $f_w$ in degree $w$. This route to the lower bound is not independent of motives, since the freeness half of the Deligne–Ihara theorem comes from Brown's theorem.
2. *Equivalent forms of Ihara's conjecture.* The following are equivalent:
   - (a) $\Phi_\ell$ is an isomorphism for some prime $\ell$;
   - (b) $\Phi_\ell$ is an isomorphism for every prime $\ell$;
   - (c) $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{D}_w = f_w$ for every $w$;
   - (d) $\mathfrak{D}$ is free on homogeneous elements, one in each odd weight at least $3$;
   - (e) $\mathfrak{D}$ is generated by homogeneous elements, one in each odd weight at least $3$.

   *Proof.* By item 1, $\Phi_\ell$ is surjective in degree $w$ exactly when $\dim \mathfrak{D}_w = f_w$, a condition that does not involve $\ell$. So (a), (b) and (c) are equivalent. Clearly (d) implies (e). Under (e), $\mathfrak{D}$ is a quotient of the free graded Lie algebra on generators of the given weights, so $\dim \mathfrak{D}_w \le f_w$, and item 1 gives (c). Under (c), $\Phi_\ell$ is an isomorphism, so $\mathfrak{D} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is free on one generator in each odd degree at least $3$, and $H_1(\mathfrak{D}) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell = H_1(\mathfrak{D} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$, where $H_1 = \mathfrak{D}/[\mathfrak{D}, \mathfrak{D}]$, is one-dimensional in each odd weight at least $3$ and zero in other weights. Choose homogeneous $d_w \in \mathfrak{D}_w$ lifting a basis of $H_1(\mathfrak{D})_w$ for odd $w \ge 3$. In a positively graded Lie algebra, the image of any section of the projection to $H_1$ generates [@hainmatsumoto2003, Proposition 2.1]. So the map from the free graded Lie algebra on generators $e_w \mapsto d_w$ to $\mathfrak{D}$ is onto in each weight, and it is injective because both sides have dimension $f_w$ in weight $w$. This gives (d). $\square$

   By the identification of $\mathfrak{D}$ with $\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})$ above, (d) is form (a) of the **Equivalent forms** in the [freeness theorem](freeness-of-the-grothendieck-teichmuller-lie-algebra.md), applied to graded pieces, so Ihara's conjecture is equivalent to it. Given Brown's theorem, then, the freeness of $\mathfrak{D}$, and even its generation by one element in each odd weight, already gives Ihara's conjecture.
3. *Weights up to $12$.* By the table, $\dim \mathfrak{D}_w = f_w$ for $w \le 12$. So $\Phi_\ell$ is bijective in degrees up to $12$, for every $\ell$. Furusho reports that Tsunogai verified the three conjectures by computation in this range of weights for $\mathfrak{D}$ [@furusho2003mzv, §2.4, p. 11].
4. *Depth one and Soulé characters.* For $D = D_f \in \mathfrak{D}_m$, Ihara's coefficient $c_m(D) \in \mathbb{Q}$ is defined by $f \equiv c_m(D)\, (\mathrm{ad}\, x)^{m-1}(y)$ modulo $\mathfrak{F}^2\mathbb{L}_m$, where $\mathfrak{F}^1\mathbb{L} = \mathbb{Q}y \oplus \mathbb{Q}[x, y] \oplus \mathbb{Q}[x, [x, y]] \oplus \mathbb{Q}[y, [x, y]] \oplus \cdots$ is spanned by the Lie monomials other than $x$, and $\mathfrak{F}^2\mathbb{L} = [\mathfrak{F}^1\mathbb{L}, \mathfrak{F}^1\mathbb{L}]$ [@furusho2003mzv, §2.2, p. 8]. The linear form $c_m$ on $\mathfrak{D}_m$ is nonzero exactly when $m$ is odd and at least $3$, and its pullback by $\Phi_\ell$ is $\kappa_m/(m-1)!$, where $\kappa_m : \mathfrak{g}^\ell_m \to \mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is induced by the $m$-th Soulé character. Both statements are due to Ihara [@furusho2003mzv, §5, pp. 21–22]. Now, writing Lie elements as noncommutative polynomials, every Lie monomial other than $x$ is a combination of words containing $y$, so every word in an element of $\mathfrak{F}^2\mathbb{L}$ contains $y$ at least twice, while $(\mathrm{ad}\, x)^{m-1}(y)$ has coefficient $1$ on the word $x^{m-1}y$. So $c_m(D_f)$ is the coefficient of $x^{m-1}y$ in $f$. Hence, for $\sigma \in \mathfrak{g}^\ell_m$, the element $f$ with $\Phi_\ell(\sigma) = D_f$ has coefficient $\kappa_m(\sigma)/(m-1)!$ on $x^{m-1}y$. This is the leading coefficient used to normalize generators in the freeness theorem of $\mathfrak{grt}_1$, so $\Phi_\ell(\sigma)$ is a nonzero multiple of an element with leading coefficient $1$ exactly when $\kappa_m(\sigma) \ne 0$. For example, $f_5$ as given by Furusho has coefficient $2$ on $x^4y$ [@furusho2003mzv, Example 2.3.2, p. 10].
5. *Ihara's conjecture holds.* By the [Deligne–Drinfeld theorem](freeness-of-the-grothendieck-teichmuller-lie-algebra.md), $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})_w = f_w$ for every $w$. Through the identification of $\mathfrak{D}$ with $\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})$ above, this is (c) of item 2. So $\Phi_\ell$ is an isomorphism for every prime $\ell$, and $\mathfrak{D}$ is free on one generator in each odd weight at least $3$. That theorem also says that any homogeneous elements of $\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})$, one in each odd weight $m \ge 3$ and each with nonzero coefficient on $x^{m-1}y$, freely generate it. By item 4, then, any $D_m \in \mathfrak{D}_m$ with $c_m(D_m) \ne 0$, one for each odd $m \ge 3$, freely generate $\mathfrak{D}$. This answers Ihara's question reported above positively, for derivations $D_{2m+1}$ normalized in this way.

**De Rham counterpart.** From the Drinfeld associator, Furusho constructs a canonical surjective $\mathbb{Q}$-linear map $\Phi_{DR}$ from the graded dual of $\mathfrak{D}$ onto the new-zeta space, which is the graded space of multiple zeta values modulo constants, $\pi^2$ and products of multiple zeta values of positive weight [@furusho2003mzv, Definition 1.3.1]. It strictly preserves depth filtrations [@furusho2003mzv, Theorem 4.2.2, p. 17], and it sends $c_m$ to the class of $-\zeta(m)$ [@furusho2003mzv, §5, p. 22]. So the dimension of the new-zeta space in weight $w$ is at most $\dim \mathfrak{D}_w$ [@furusho2003mzv, Corollary 4.3.1]. If $\mathfrak{D}$ is generated by one element in each odd weight at least $3$, the dimension of the space of multiple zeta values of weight $w$ is at most Zagier's conjectured value $d_w$ [@furusho2003mzv, Proposition 4.3.7, p. 20]. By item 5 that hypothesis now holds and $\dim \mathfrak{D}_w = f_w$, so the new-zeta space has dimension at most $f_w$ in weight $w$, and Furusho's route gives Zagier's upper bound. Furusho notes that Goncharov had announced a proof of this upper bound by the theory of mixed Tate motives [@furusho2003mzv, §0, p. 3, footnote 1]. Furusho conjectures that $\Phi_{DR}$ is an isomorphism, and sets it beside $\Phi_\ell$, whose dual is likewise expected to be an isomorphism: $c_m$ goes to $-\zeta(m)$ on the de Rham side and to $\kappa_m/(m-1)!$ on the $\ell$-adic side [@furusho2003mzv, Conjecture 4.3.8 and §5, pp. 21–22].

**Status.** Ihara's conjecture holds, by item 5, as a consequence of the Deligne–Drinfeld theorem. The proof of that theorem was completed in 2026, in preprints that had not been refereed; see that entry for the sources and what was checked. Until then Ihara's conjecture was open, since by item 2 it is equivalent to the Deligne–Drinfeld conjecture. Brown's theorem had determined the structure of the source of $\Phi_\ell$, and the 2026 upper bound on $\dim \mathfrak{D}_w$ completed the proof.

**Sources.** Furusho's paper appeared in Publ. RIMS in 2003; the locations given are those of arXiv version 5, of 28 May 2001. Not opened: Ihara's papers, including his 1990 address and his 1999 preprint, Tsunogai's 1997 paper, and Drinfeld's paper.
