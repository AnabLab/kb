**Question** (Anderson–Ihara). Let $p$ be a prime, let $\pi^{(p)}$ be the pro-$p$ completion of the geometric fundamental group of $U = \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{Q}} \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$, and let $\rho_p : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathrm{Out}(\pi^{(p)})$ be the outer Galois representation. Let $\Omega^*_p \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ be the fixed field of $\ker \rho_p$, and $\Omega_p$ the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_p)$ unramified outside $p$. Is $\Omega^*_p = \Omega_p$? [@ishii2026, §1, p. 2]

Ishii states the question this way, citing Anderson and Ihara's 1988 paper in the Annals, p. 272. He reports that they proved $\Omega^*_p$ to be a nonabelian infinite pro-$p$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty})$, unramified outside $p$ and generated by $p$-units that they call higher circular $p$-units [@ishii2026, §1, p. 2]. Sharifi records that $\Omega^*_p$ is a pro-$p$ extension of $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_p)$ unramified outside $p$ for every prime $p$ [@sharifi2002, §1, p. 1]. So $\Omega^*_p \subseteq \Omega_p$, and the question asks whether this inclusion is an equality. Anderson and Ihara's paper has not been opened for this entry.

For $p = 2$, "unramified outside $p$" has to allow ramification at the real place, as checked here. The module $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(U_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}, \mathbb{Q}_2(1))$ has trivial Galois action, as for every prime in place of $2$ [@hainmatsumoto2003, §8.4, p. 27], and it is $2$-dimensional, so $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ acts on the abelianization of $\pi^{(2)}$, tensored with $\mathbb{Q}_2$, through the cyclotomic character. Complex conjugation therefore acts by $-1$, so it is not in $\ker \rho_2$, and $\Omega^*_2$ is not real.

**Known cases.**

- *Odd regular primes: yes.* Call $p$ regular if it does not divide the class number of $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_p)$. Sharifi proves that if $p$ is odd and regular and the [Deligne–Ihara conjecture](deligne-ihara-conjecture.md) holds for $p$, then $\Omega^*_p = \Omega_p$ [@sharifi2002, Theorem 1]. That conjecture is now a theorem, so the answer is yes for every odd regular prime [@ishii2026, §1, p. 2 and Theorem 1.1]. Sharifi's theorem gives more: $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_p/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty}))$ is a free pro-$p$ group on Soulé elements $\sigma_m$, one for each odd $m \ge 3$, and Ihara's graded Lie algebra is free over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ on the same elements [@sharifi2002, §1, p. 2, and Theorem 1].
- *How the regular case is proved.* For regular $p$, $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_p/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_p))$ is a free pro-$p$ group on $(p+1)/2$ generators [@ishii2026, Remark 1.1.1]. Sharifi deduces that $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_p/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty}))$ is free on elements that agree with Soulé elements on the maximal abelian quotient, so that it is free on Soulé elements and maps onto $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega^*_p/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty}))$. The Deligne–Ihara conjecture makes this map injective, so it is an isomorphism and $\Omega^*_p = \Omega_p$ [@sharifi2002, §3, proof of Theorem 1, p. 8].

**Irregular primes.** The opened sources do not settle the question here, and Ishii states the affirmative answer only for odd regular $p$ [@ishii2026, §1, p. 2]. Two results constrain it.

- Let $p$ be irregular, and suppose Greenberg's conjecture in multivariable Iwasawa theory holds for $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$. Then $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega^*_p/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty}))$ is not generated by Soulé elements, and Ihara's graded Lie algebra is not generated over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ by them [@sharifi2002, Theorem 3], since the Deligne–Ihara conjecture is now known. McCallum proved Greenberg's conjecture for a large class of irregular primes [@sharifi2002, §1, p. 3]. So the description of the regular case fails for these $p$. This does not decide whether $\Omega^*_p = \Omega_p$.
- Let $p$ be odd, and suppose that the map from the free pro-$p$ group on generators $s_m$ to $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega^*_p/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^\infty}))$, $s_m \mapsto \sigma_m$, is not surjective. Let $m$ be the least degree in which its truncation fails to be surjective. Then the subfield of $\Omega^*_p$ cut out by the action on $\pi^{(p)}$ modulo the $(m+2)$-nd term of its lower central series contains a nontrivial elementary abelian $p$-extension of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)$ with Tate twist $m$. If $m$ is even, then $p$ divides the Bernoulli number $B_m$. If $m$ is odd, then $p$ divides $B_{p-m}$ and Vandiver's conjecture fails at $p$ [@sharifi2002, Theorem 11].

For odd irregular $p$, the group $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_p/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_p))$ is not generated by $(p+1)/2$ elements [@ishii2026, Remark 1.1.1]. A pro-$p$ group needs exactly as many generators as the $\mathbb{F}_p$-dimension of its maximal elementary abelian quotient. So, as checked here, an affirmative answer for an odd irregular $p$ requires the maximal elementary abelian $p$-extension of $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_p)$ inside $\Omega^*_p$ to have degree greater than $p^{(p+1)/2}$.

**Other curves.** Ishii notes that, using Oda's prediction in the form of a theorem of Takao, or part 1 of the Hoshi–Mochizuki theorem in [injectivity of outer Galois representations](injectivity-of-outer-galois-representations.md), together with a lemma of Hoshi, one can construct hyperbolic curves of higher genus whose pro-$p$ outer representations have the same kernel as that of $U$. He gives the Fermat curve of degree $p$ as an example, citing Hoshi [@ishii2026, §1, pp. 2–3]. Hoshi works over an arbitrary number field $k$. For an odd prime $\ell$ and a positive integer $n$ with $(\ell, n) \ne (3, 1)$, he shows that the projective Fermat curve $s^{\ell^n} + t^{\ell^n} + u^{\ell^n} = 0$ over $k$ and $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$ have pro-$\ell$ outer representations with the same kernel [@hoshi2015, Example 3.4(ii), p. 668]. With $k = \mathbb{Q}$, $\ell = p$ and $n = 1$, this is the smooth projective Fermat curve of degree $p$, for $p \ge 5$. The bound on $p$ is needed: for $p = 2$ the Fermat conic has trivial geometric fundamental group, and for $p = 3$ the Fermat cubic has genus one and is not hyperbolic. Hoshi gets the same conclusion for the affine Fermat curve $s^{\ell^n} + t^{\ell^n} + 1 = 0$, for every odd $\ell$ and every $n \ge 1$ [@hoshi2015, Example 3.4(i), pp. 667–668]. So the affine Fermat curve of degree $p$ works for every odd $p$, including $p = 3$. For all these curves the question is the same.

**A CM elliptic analogue.** Let $K$ be an imaginary quadratic field of class number one, $E$ an elliptic curve over $K$ with complex multiplication by $\mathcal{O}_K$ defined over $K$, and $X = E \setminus \{O\}$. Let $p \ge 5$ split in $K$. Assume that $p$ does not divide the class number of the ray class field $K(p)$, that only two primes of $K(p^2)$ lie above $p$, and an analogue of the Deligne–Ihara conjecture. Then the fixed field of the kernel of the pro-$p$ outer representation of $X$ is the compositum of $K(E[p])$ and the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $K(p)$ unramified outside $p$ [@ishii2026, Theorem A]. Ishii writes that the theorem depends critically on that analogue, which he states as a working conjecture [@ishii2026, §1, p. 2]. For $p = 5$ the first two assumptions hold exactly for $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})$, and for $p = 7$ only for $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-19})$ [@ishii2026, Example 1.2].
