**Setting.** Let $k$ be a perfect field with algebraic closure $\bar k$, let $X$ be a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $k$, and let $\Sigma$ be a set of primes invertible in $k$. For $n \ge 1$, let $X_n$ be the $n$-th configuration space of $X$, roughly the complement of the diagonals in $X^n$, so that $X_1 = X$. Write $\Delta^\Sigma_{X_n}$ for the maximal pro-$\Sigma$ quotient of $\pi_1(X_n \otimes_k \bar k)$. The exact sequence $1 \to \pi_1(X_n \otimes_k \bar k) \to \pi_1(X_n) \to G_k \to 1$ induces the outer representation
$$\rho^\Sigma_{X_n} : G_k \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta^\Sigma_{X_n}).$$
When $\Sigma$ is the set of all primes, write $\Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_{X_n}$ and $\rho^{\mathrm{prf}}_{X_n}$ [@hoshimochizuki2011, Theorem C, p. 7].

**Theorem (Hoshi–Mochizuki).** Let $\Sigma$ be either a single prime or the set of all primes, with every element of $\Sigma$ invertible in $k$.

1. The kernel of $\rho^\Sigma_{X_n}$ does not depend on $n$, and it is contained in the kernel of the corresponding representation $\rho^\Sigma_{U}$ for $U = \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$.
2. If $\Sigma$ is the set of all primes, so that $k$ has characteristic $0$, the kernel of $\rho^{\mathrm{prf}}_{X_n}$ is contained in the kernel of the restriction map $G_k \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, where $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ is taken inside $\bar k$.
3. In particular, if $k$ is a number field or a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, then
$$\rho^{\mathrm{prf}}_X : G_k \to \mathrm{Out}(\pi_1(X \otimes_k \bar k))$$
is injective.

This is [@hoshimochizuki2011, Theorem C, p. 7], proved as [@hoshimochizuki2011, Corollary 6.2 and Corollary 6.3 (i), pp. 63–65]. Corollary 6.3 (i) also gives injectivity of $\rho^{\mathrm{prf}}_{X_n}$ for every $n$. Part 3 follows from part 2 because $G_k \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is injective for these $k$ [@hoshimochizuki2011, proof of Corollary 6.3, p. 65]. That injectivity is checked here. For a number field, $\bar k = \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. For $k$ finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, every finite extension of $k$ is generated over $k$ by an element of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, by Krasner's lemma, so $k \bar{\mathbb{Q}} = \bar k$.

**History.** For $U$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, injectivity is a well-known result of Belyi, and it is the input that turns part 1 into part 2 [@hoshimochizuki2011, proof of Corollary 6.2, p. 63]. Matsumoto proved injectivity for affine hyperbolic curves: Hoshi and Mochizuki say that his Theorem 2.1 corresponds to their Corollary 6.3 for affine $X$, whose injectivity statement for $n = 1$ generalizes it [@hoshimochizuki2011, p. 64]. His Theorem 2.2 corresponds to their Corollary 6.2 for $k \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ and $X$ of positive genus with at least one cusp defined over $k$ [@hoshimochizuki2011, p. 63]. Matsumoto's paper (J. Reine Angew. Math. 474 (1996)) has not been opened for this entry. Boggi asserted a result corresponding to a special case of part 1, and Hoshi and Mochizuki write that they could not follow its proof [@hoshimochizuki2011, Introduction, p. 8].

**Outline of the proof.** Let $\mathrm{Out}^{\mathrm{FC}}$ denote outer automorphisms that, roughly speaking, preserve fibre subgroups and cuspidal inertia groups. Over an algebraically closed field, the map $\mathrm{Out}^{\mathrm{FC}}(\Delta^\Sigma_{X_{n+1}}) \to \mathrm{Out}^{\mathrm{FC}}(\Delta^\Sigma_{X_n})$ induced by forgetting a factor is injective for $n \ge 1$. The proof reduces this to $n = 1$, which for proper $X$ rests on the nodal degeneration argument [@hoshimochizuki2011, Theorem 6.1 and its proof, pp. 61–63]. Hence the kernel of $\rho^\Sigma_{X_n}$ does not depend on $n$. Let $k'$ be the minimal Galois extension of $k$ over which every cusp of $X$ is defined. The kernel lies in $G_{k'}$, and the action of $G_{k'}$ on $\Delta^\Sigma_{X_3}$ induces, through a construction of Mochizuki using the diagonal of $X_2$, the outer action of $G_{k'}$ on $\Delta^\Sigma_U$. This gives part 1, and part 2 follows from part 1 and Belyi's injectivity [@hoshimochizuki2011, proof of Corollary 6.2, pp. 63–64].

**Why proper curves are harder.** The case $n = 1$ of the injectivity above, for the projection from $X_2$ to $X$, was already known for affine $X$ from Mochizuki's earlier work, and extending it to proper $X$ was the motivation of the paper. The affine proof studies degenerations in which a moving point on an affine curve collides with a cusp, and it relies on reconstruction algorithms that depend on the positivity of a period matrix. A proper curve has no cusps, so one must instead let a moving point on a stable curve collide with a node. There the symmetry switching the two branches of the node would force such a positivity to be invariant under multiplication by $-1$, so the earlier method cannot apply [@hoshimochizuki2011, Introduction, pp. 4–5]. Hoshi and Mochizuki handle this case with their theorem on nodally nondegenerate outer representations [@hoshimochizuki2011, Theorem A, pp. 2–3].

**Belyi's case.** Here is an argument for it, checked here except for one standard step: a lift of $\sigma \in G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ to $\pi_1(U)$ carries the open subgroup of $\Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_U$ belonging to a connected finite étale cover $Y \to U_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}$ to the subgroup belonging to the Galois conjugate cover. That step has not been checked against a source opened for this entry.

Suppose $\rho^{\mathrm{prf}}_U(\sigma) = 1$, with $U$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then every open subgroup is carried to a conjugate of itself, so every such $Y$ is isomorphic over $U_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}$ to its conjugate, and hence so are their smooth compactifications over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. By Belyi's theorem, a smooth projective curve $C$ over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ admits a finite morphism $t : C \to \mathbb{P}^1$ whose critical values lie in $\{0, 1, \infty\}$ [@koeck2004, Theorem 3.3, p. 10]. The proof composes a morphism $C \to \mathbb{P}^1$ defined over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ with polynomials over $\mathbb{Q}$ and with fractional linear maps that move rational points to $0$, $1$ and $\infty$ [@koeck2004, proof of Theorem 3.3 and Lemmas 3.4–3.6, pp. 10–11]. These maps are defined over $\mathbb{Q}$, so $t$ is defined over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. Then $t^{-1}(U_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})$ is such a cover $Y$, with compactification $C$. Take $C$ to be an elliptic curve with $j$-invariant $a \in \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. Its conjugate by $\sigma$ has $j$-invariant $\sigma^{\pm 1}(a)$, depending on conventions, and is isomorphic to $C$, so $\sigma(a) = a$. Since $a$ is arbitrary, $\sigma = 1$.

**The pro-$\ell$ analogue fails.** Part 2 needs all primes. For a single prime $\ell$, Ihara showed that the pro-$\ell$ outer representation $\rho^{\{\ell\}}_U$ of $U$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ factors through the Galois group of the maximal algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ unramified outside $\ell$, as Hain and Matsumoto report [@hainmatsumoto2003, §1, pp. 2–3]. So it is not injective. This is checked here. For an odd prime $q \ne \ell$, the quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\pm q})$ with $\pm q \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ has discriminant $\pm q$, so it is ramified at $q$, and the restriction of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ to the maximal extension unramified outside $\ell$ has nontrivial kernel. Part 1 still holds for $\Sigma = \{\ell\}$: the kernel of the pro-$\ell$ representation of any hyperbolic curve over $k$ lies inside that of $U$.

**Part 2 is sharp.** Let $k_0$ be a number field algebraically closed in a field $k$, such as $k = k_0(t)$, let $X_0$ be a hyperbolic curve over $k_0$, and let $X = X_0 \otimes_{k_0} k$. The geometric fundamental group of a curve does not change under extension of algebraically closed fields of characteristic $0$, compatibly with the Galois actions. This standard fact is not checked against a source here. Granting it, $\rho^{\mathrm{prf}}_X$ is $\rho^{\mathrm{prf}}_{X_0}$ composed with the surjection $G_k \to G_{k_0}$, so its kernel is exactly $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar k / k\bar{\mathbb{Q}})$, by part 3 over $k_0$. That is the kernel of $G_k \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. For example, over $k = k_0(t)$ injectivity fails for every hyperbolic curve defined over $k_0$, since $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(t)$ is not algebraically closed.

**Further consequences in the same paper.** Let $k$ be a number field or a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and let $k_0$ be $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{Q}_p$ accordingly.

- *Arithmetic and geometric automorphisms.* Let $\mathrm{Aut}(X_{\bar k}/k_0)$ be the group of $k_0$-linear automorphisms of the scheme $X \otimes_k \bar k$. It contains $G_k$, and acts by outer automorphisms on $\Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_{X_n}$ compatibly with $\rho^{\mathrm{prf}}_{X_n}$. This action is injective [@hoshimochizuki2011, Corollary 6.3 (ii), (iii), pp. 64–65]. The proof combines the injectivity on $\mathrm{Aut}(X_{\bar k}/\bar k)$, which Hoshi and Mochizuki call well known, citing the injectivity part of [Mochizuki's Hom theorem](mochizuki-hom-theorem-for-hyperbolic-curves.md), with part 3 and the fact that $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/k_0)$ is slim, meaning that every open subgroup has trivial centralizer [@hoshimochizuki2011, §0, p. 8, and proof of Corollary 6.3, p. 65].
- *Galois action versus monodromy.* Let $X$ have type $(g, r)$, and let $\rho_{g,r} : \Pi_{\mathcal{M}_{g,r}} \to \mathrm{Out}(\pi_1(X \otimes_k \bar k))$ be the universal monodromy representation of the moduli stack of $r$-pointed smooth curves of genus $g$ over $k$, with geometric part $\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{g,r}}$. For any $k$ of characteristic $0$, the elements of $G_k$ whose outer action on $\pi_1(X \otimes_k \bar k)$ lies in $\rho_{g,r}(\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{g,r}})$ lie in the kernel of $G_k \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. So for number fields and finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the images of $\rho^{\mathrm{prf}}_X$ and of $\rho_{g,r}$ on $\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{g,r}}$ meet trivially. This generalizes a theorem of Matsumoto and Tamagawa for $r \ge 1$ [@hoshimochizuki2011, Corollary 6.4, pp. 66–67].
- *Congruence subgroup problem.* Over such $k$, the congruence subgroup problem for $(g, r)$ has an affirmative answer, meaning that $\rho_{g,r}$ is injective on $\Delta_{\mathcal{M}_{g,r}}$, exactly when $\rho_{g,r}$ is injective on all of $\Pi_{\mathcal{M}_{g,r}}$ [@hoshimochizuki2011, Corollary 6.5, p. 67].

**Related.** The [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) entry records a remark of Saïdi that uses this injectivity, to explain why Hoshi's construction of nongeometric pro-$p$ sections is not expected to extend to profinite sections.
