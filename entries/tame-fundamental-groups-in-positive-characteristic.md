**Setting.** Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p \ge 0$ and $U$ a smooth connected curve over $k$, with smooth compactification $X$ and $S = X - U$. Let $g$ be the genus of $X$ and $n$ the number of points of $S$ [@tamagawa2003tame, Introduction, p. 47]. The tame fundamental group $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)$ is the quotient of $\pi_1(U)$ that classifies coverings at most tamely ramified over $S$. The curve $U$ is hyperbolic if $2 - 2g - n < 0$ [@tamagawa2003tame, §5 (A), p. 93], as in [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md).

**Characteristic $0$.** If $p = 0$, then $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U) = \pi_1(U)$ is the profinite completion of the fundamental group of a Riemann surface of type $(g,n)$, so it carries almost no information about $U$ beyond $(g,n)$ [@huyangzong2024, §1.1.1, p. 1]. It does not even determine $(g, n)$: for $n \ge 1$ it is free profinite of rank $2g + n - 1$, so it depends only on $2g + n$ (see [geometric fundamental groups of curves](geometric-fundamental-groups-of-curves.md), which records the presentation from SGA 1). In characteristic $p > 0$, for $X$ proper of genus $g \ge 2$, the prime-to-$p$ quotient of $\pi_1(X)$ is still the prime-to-$p$ completion of the surface group, and the maximal pro-$p$ quotient is free pro-$p$ of rank the $p$-rank of the Jacobian. Pop and Saïdi wrote in 2003 that the structure of the whole group $\pi_1(X)$ was not known in any single case [@popsaidi2003, Introduction, p. 107]. The absolute Galois group of the function field of $X$, by contrast, is free in every characteristic and determines only the cardinality of $k$ ([function fields of curves](function-fields-of-curves-have-free-galois-groups.md)).

**Theorem (Tamagawa).**

1. *Characteristic and type.* For $i = 1, 2$ let $k_i$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p_i > 0$ and $U_i$ a curve over $k_i$ of type $(g_i, n_i)$. If $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_1) \cong \pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_2)$, then $g_1 = g_2$; $p_1 = p_2$ unless $g_i = 0$ and $n_i \le 1$ for both $i$; and $n_1 = n_2$ unless $\{(g_1,n_1),(g_2,n_2)\} = \{(0,0),(0,1)\}$ [@tamagawa2003tame, Theorem (4.1), p. 85].
2. *Genus $0$.* Suppose $k_1 \cong k_2$, and that for some $i$ we have $g_i = 0$, $n_i > 1$, and either $U_i$ is defined over the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$ in $k_i$ or $n_i \le 4$. Then $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_1) \cong \pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_2)$ if and only if $U_1 \cong U_2$ as schemes [@tamagawa2003tame, Corollary (5.9), pp. 97–98].

The isomorphism in part 2 is one of schemes, not necessarily of $k$-schemes [@tamagawa2003tame, Abstract, p. 47]. Tamagawa had proved earlier that the full étale fundamental group determines $(g,n)$, and in genus $0$ over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ the curve, using wild ramification. The tame quotient can be recovered group-theoretically from $\pi_1(U)$, so the tame theorem is the stronger one [@tamagawa2003tame, Introduction, pp. 47–48].

**Reading off $p$, $g$ and $n$.**

- *The characteristic.* Let $b^{(1)} = 2g + n - 1 + b^{(2)}$, where $b^{(2)}$ is $1$ if $n = 0$ and $0$ otherwise, and let $\gamma$ be the $p$-rank of the Jacobian of $X$. Then
  $$\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell^{\,b^{(1)}} \times \mathbb{Z}_p^{\,\gamma},$$
  and $\gamma \le g \le b^{(1)}$, with $\gamma = b^{(1)}$ only when $b^{(1)} = 0$. So $p$ is the unique prime at which the rank of the abelianization drops, unless $(g,n)$ is $(0,0)$ or $(0,1)$ [@tamagawa2003tame, Lemma (4.3) and its proof, pp. 85–86].
- *The genus, by averages of $p$-ranks.* For a profinite group $\Pi$ and an integer $N$, let $\Pi(N)$ be the kernel of $\Pi \to \Pi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/N$. For $N$ prime to $p$ put
  $$\gamma^{\mathrm{av}}_N = \frac{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}\big(\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)(N)^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{F}_p\big)}{\#\big(\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Z}/N\big)},$$
  and let $g' = g$ if $n > 1$ and $g' = g - 1$ if $n \le 1$. Unless $(g,n)$ is $(0,0)$ or $(0,1)$,
  $$\lim_{f \to \infty} \gamma^{\mathrm{av}}_{p^f - 1} = g'$$
  [@tamagawa2003tame, Theorem (0.5), p. 51, and Corollary (4.11), p. 91]. Geometrically, $\gamma^{\mathrm{av}}_N$ is the $p$-rank of the compactification $X(N)$ of the covering of $U$ cut out by $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)(N)$, divided by its degree $N^{b^{(1)}}$ [@tamagawa2003tame, Remark (4.8), p. 88]. An effective form gives $g'$ as the unique integer in an explicit interval once $f$ is large enough [@tamagawa2003tame, Corollary (4.10), pp. 90–91]. Comparing $U$ with the coverings $U(m)$ cut out by $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)(m)$ determines $n$, and then $b^{(1)}$ gives $g$ [@tamagawa2003tame, Theorem (4.14) and the end of the proof of (4.1), pp. 92–93].
- *The input.* For most prime-to-$p$ cyclic coverings, the generalized Hasse–Witt invariants are as large as possible. For étale coverings this follows from Raynaud's theory of theta divisors, and Tamagawa extends that theory to the ramified case [@tamagawa2003tame, Introduction, pp. 48–50].

*Check, done here, for proper curves.* Let $n = 0$ and $g \ge 1$, so $b^{(1)} = 2g$ and $X(N) \to X$ is étale of degree $N^{2g}$. The Riemann–Hurwitz formula gives $g_{X(N)} - 1 = N^{2g}(g - 1)$, and a $p$-rank is at most the genus. Hence
$$\gamma^{\mathrm{av}}_N \le \frac{N^{2g}(g-1) + 1}{N^{2g}} = g - 1 + N^{-2g},$$
which matches Tamagawa's upper bound for $N = p^f - 1$ [@tamagawa2003tame, Theorem (4.9) (i), p. 89]. So the limit formula says that these coverings have asymptotically maximal $p$-rank. For $g = 1$ the covering is multiplication by $N$ on the elliptic curve $X$, so $X(N) \cong X$, and $\gamma^{\mathrm{av}}_N = \gamma / N^2 \to 0 = g'$.

*Check, done here: characteristic $0$ is detected too.* If $b^{(1)} > 0$, then $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)$ is not isomorphic to the fundamental group of any curve over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. Such a group is the profinite completion of a surface group whose abelianization is $\mathbb{Z}^{b'}$ for some $b'$. Its abelianization is therefore $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{b'}$, of the same $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-rank at every prime $\ell$. By the first bullet, $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ has rank $b^{(1)}$ at primes $\ell \ne p$ and rank $\gamma < b^{(1)}$ at $p$.

**Inertia and the constant field.** Let $U$ be hyperbolic. The set of inertia subgroups of the points over $S$ can be recovered group-theoretically from $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)$, and every isomorphism $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_1) \cong \pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_2)$ matches these sets [@tamagawa2003tame, Theorem (5.2), p. 94]. The quotient $\pi_1(X)$ can be recovered as well [@tamagawa2003tame, Corollary (4.19), p. 93]. An inertia group $I$ is canonically $\varprojlim_{p \nmid m} \mu_m(k)$, so $(I \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} (\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})') \sqcup \{*\}$, with $(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})'$ the prime-to-$p$ part, is identified with the algebraic closure $F$ of $\mathbb{F}_p$ in $k$: its multiplicative group is the group of roots of unity in $k$, and $*$ is $0$. Tamagawa recovers the field structure of this set from $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)$ [@tamagawa2003tame, §5 (B) and Proposition (5.3), p. 94]. So the profinite group alone yields a copy of $F$ with its addition. In genus $0$ this lets one compute the coordinates of the cusps group-theoretically, which is the core of the proof of part 2 [@huyangzong2024, §1.3.2, p. 4], [@tamagawa2003tame, proof of (5.8) and (5.9), p. 98].

**Refinements.**

- *Homomorphisms.* For two hyperbolic curves of the same type over algebraically closed fields of the same characteristic, every open continuous homomorphism between their tame fundamental groups is surjective [@huyangzong2024, §4.1.1 and Lemma 4.1, p. 27]. Hu, Yang and Zong prove the following. Let $U_1, U_2$ be curves of type $(0,n)$ over algebraically closed fields of characteristic $p$, with $U_1$ defined over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$. There is an open continuous homomorphism $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_1) \to \pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_2)$ if and only if the minimal models of $U_1$ and $U_2$ are isomorphic as schemes, and then every such homomorphism is an isomorphism [@huyangzong2024, Theorem 4.3 (ii), pp. 29–30]. Here the minimal model of $U$ is its model over the smallest algebraically closed subfield of $k$ over which $U$ is defined, and it has the same tame fundamental group [@huyangzong2024, §4.1.1, p. 27]. The same holds for the maximal prosolvable quotients [@huyangzong2024, Remark 4.3.1, p. 33]. The new difficulty is that Tamagawa's reconstructions compare isomorphic groups. Under a surjection $\phi$, the preimage of an open subgroup need not be isomorphic to it, and inequalities between limits of $p$-averages, as in the second bullet above, take the place of equalities [@huyangzong2024, §1.3.2, p. 4, and Proposition 3.8, p. 16].
- *Finite quotients.* Since $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)$ is topologically finitely generated, its isomorphism class is determined by its set of finite quotients [@yang2025finite, §1.1.2, p. 2]. For a profinite group $\Pi$ let $D^{(1)}_c(\Pi)$ be the closed subgroup generated by commutators and $c$-th powers, and $D^{(i)}_c = D^{(1)}_c \circ D^{(i-1)}_c$. Let $U_1$ have genus $0$ and be defined over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$. Yang constructs explicitly an integer $c$, depending only on $U_1$, such that the minimal models of $U_1$ and $U_2$ are isomorphic as schemes if and only if the finite groups $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_j)/D^{(9)}_c(\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_j))$, $j = 1,2$, are isomorphic [@yang2025finite, §1.2.1 and Corollary 1.4, pp. 3–4]. His Theorem 1.3 is a sharper form with two finite groups [@yang2025finite, Theorem 1.3, p. 4]. The second group must depend on both curves: by a theorem in another of Yang's preprints, for a curve $U_2$ of type $(0,n)$, every finite quotient of $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_2)$ is also a quotient of $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U_1)$ for some curve $U_1$ of type $(0,n)$ not isomorphic to $U_2$ [@yang2025finite, Remark 1.3.2, p. 4].
