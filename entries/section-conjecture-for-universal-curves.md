**Setting.** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$, and let $g \ge 3$, $n \ge 0$ and $m \ge 1$ be integers. If $m > 1$, assume that $k$ contains the $m$th roots of unity and fix a primitive one, so that the moduli stack $\mathcal{M}_{g,n/k}[m]$ of smooth projective curves of genus $g$ with $n$ distinct marked points and a level $m$ structure is geometrically connected [@hain2011, §5.1.2, p. 11]. For $m = 1$ this is $\mathcal{M}_{g,n/k}$ itself. Let $K = k(\mathcal{M}_{g,n}[m])$ be its function field, and let $C$ be the restriction of the universal complete curve to $\mathrm{Spec}\, K$, the *generic curve*. It is a smooth projective curve of genus $g$ over $K$, so a proper [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md). Restricting the $n$ tautological sections gives $n$ points $x_1, \dots, x_n \in C(K)$, the *tautological points* [@hain2011, §1, pp. 2–3]. Write $\chi_\ell : G_k \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ for the $\ell$-adic cyclotomic character.

**Theorem (Hain).**

1. *Rational points.* Suppose that $\chi_\ell$ has infinite image for some prime $\ell$, and that $\chi_2$ does if $g = 3$. If $g = 3$, suppose also that $n \ge 1$ or $m \ge 3$, as in Hain's list of examples [@hain2011, §1, pp. 2–3]. Then $C(K) = \{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$. In particular $C(K) = \emptyset$ when $n = 0$. This is Hain's Theorem 1 on topologically ample families [@hain2011, Theorem 1, p. 3], which applies because the universal curve over $\mathcal{M}_{g,n/k}[m]$ is topologically ample under these hypotheses [@hain2011, §11, p. 38]. Hain notes that for the universal curve itself the statement follows directly from results in Teichmüller theory due to Hubbard and to Earle and Kra, and that his algebraic proof also applies to ample subvarieties of dimension at least $2$ [@hain2011, §1, p. 2].
2. *No sections.* Suppose that $n = 0$, $g \ge 5$ and $\chi_\ell$ has infinite image for some prime $\ell$. Then the sequence
$$1 \to \pi_1(C \otimes_K \bar K) \to \pi_1(C) \to G_K \to 1$$
does not split [@hain2011, Theorem 2, p. 3].
3. *Unipotent sections.* Suppose that $n \ge 1$, $g \ge 5$, $\chi_\ell$ has infinite image, and $H^1(G_k, \mathbb{Q}_\ell(r))$ is finite dimensional for all $r > 1$. Let $\pi^{\mathrm{un}}$ be the $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-unipotent completion of $\pi_1(C \otimes_K \bar K, \bar x_1)$, and form the extension $1 \to \pi^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathbb{Q}_\ell) \to G_K \ltimes \pi^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathbb{Q}_\ell) \to G_K \to 1$ using the action of $G_K$ given by the tautological point $x_1$. Then there is a natural bijection between $C(K)$ and the $\pi^{\mathrm{un}}(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$-conjugacy classes of splittings of this extension [@hain2011, Theorem 3, p. 3]. Hain notes that the hypotheses hold when $k$ is a number field, and when $k$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with $p \ne \ell$ [@hain2011, §1, p. 4].

For $n \ge 1$, Hain writes that he "can only prove a weaker version" than part 2 [@hain2011, §1, p. 3]. Part 3 doesn't give the profinite statement, because two sections of $\pi_1(C) \to G_K$ that are not conjugate might still induce conjugate splittings of the unipotent extension.

**The section conjecture for the generic curve.** Hain states that he proves the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) for the restriction of the universal curve over $\mathcal{M}_g$ to its generic point, for all $g \ge 5$, when $k$ has characteristic $0$ [@hain2011, §1, p. 2]. The conjecture concerns fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$. Within that range, the deduction is checked here.

Let $k$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, $g \ge 5$, $n = 0$ and $m \ge 1$.

- *$K$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$.* For $g \ge 3$, the open substack of $\mathcal{M}_{g,n}[m]$ of curves with trivial automorphism group is a smooth quasi-projective variety over $k$ [@hain2011, §11, p. 38], and $K$ is its function field.
- *$\chi_\ell$ has infinite image for every $\ell$.* The algebraic closure $k_0$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $k$ is a number field. Since $k_0$ is algebraically closed in $k$, finite extensions of $k_0$ are linearly disjoint from $k$, so $[k(\mu_{\ell^r}) : k] = [k_0(\mu_{\ell^r}) : k_0] \ge (\ell - 1)\ell^{r-1}/[k_0 : \mathbb{Q}]$, which is unbounded in $r$.
- So part 1 gives $C(K) = \emptyset$, and part 2 shows that $\pi_1(C) \to G_K$ has no section. The map from $C(K)$ to conjugacy classes of sections is the bijection $\emptyset \to \emptyset$.

**The birational analogue.** Under the same hypotheses, the restriction map $G_{K(C)} \to G_K$ has no section either, so the [birational section conjecture](birational-section-conjecture.md) also holds for $C$. The argument is written out here. Fix a separable closure $\Omega$ of $K(C)$ and let $\bar K$ be the algebraic closure of $K$ in $\Omega$. Then $\Omega$ defines a geometric point $\bar\eta$ of $C$ over its generic point. The morphisms $\mathrm{Spec}\, K(C) \to C \to \mathrm{Spec}\, K$ induce homomorphisms $q : G_{K(C)} \to \pi_1(C, \bar\eta)$ and $p : \pi_1(C, \bar\eta) \to G_K$, and $p \circ q$ is the restriction map $\sigma \mapsto \sigma|_{\bar K}$. If $s$ were a section of the restriction map, then $p \circ (q \circ s) = \mathrm{id}$, so $q \circ s$ would be a section of $p$, contradicting part 2. The [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) entry records the same observation, that a birational section gives a section of the fundamental group sequence.

**Method.** The main tool is weighted completion of profinite groups, developed by Hain and Matsumoto [@hain2011, §1, p. 2].

- *Weighted completion.* For a family of curves $f : C \to T$, such as the universal curve over $T = \mathcal{M}_{g,n/k}[m]$, set $H = H^1_{\text{ét}}(C_{\bar\eta}, \mathbb{Q}_\ell(1))$. Completing $\pi_1(C)$ and $\pi_1(T)$ with respect to their monodromy representations into $\mathrm{GSp}(H)$ gives $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-proalgebraic groups $\mathcal{G}_C \to \mathcal{G}_T$. These are extensions of $\mathrm{GSp}(H)$ by prounipotent groups, and their Lie algebras carry natural weight filtrations [@hain2011, §1, p. 4]. A section of $\pi_1(C) \to \pi_1(T)$ induces a section of $\mathcal{G}_C \to \mathcal{G}_T$ [@hain2011, Proposition 7.1 (ii), p. 18]. Over the generic point, a section of $\pi_1(C_K) \to G_K$ induces a section of the corresponding completions [@hain2011, Proposition 7.3, p. 19].
- *Rational points.* A $K$-point gives a $\mathrm{GSp}(H)$-invariant section of a homomorphism $\mathfrak{d}_{g,n+1} \to \mathfrak{d}_{g,n}$ of graded two-step nilpotent Lie algebras, built from the weight $-1$ and $-2$ parts of the completed mapping class groups [@hain2011, Corollary 11.6, p. 39, and §13.1, p. 43]. In weight $-1$ such a section has the form $(u_1, \dots, u_n) \mapsto (\sum_j a_j u_j, u_1, \dots, u_n)$ on the copies of $H$. Compatibility with the bracket forces $a_i^2 = a_i$ and $a_i a_j = 0$ for $i \ne j$. The component $\mathbf{c}$ of the bracket from $\Lambda^2 \Lambda^3_0 H$ to $\Lambda^2_0 H$, which is nonzero for $g \ge 4$ and absent for $g = 3$, forces $\sum_j a_j = 1$ as well [@hain2011, Lemma 10.5 and proof of Proposition 10.4, pp. 34–35]. So for $g \ge 4$ the only sections are the $n$ tautological ones, and there are none when $n = 0$ [@hain2011, Proposition 10.8, p. 36]. For $g = 3$, where $\mathbf{c} = 0$, an extra section appears, and Hain rules it out using an integral structure with $\ell = 2$ [@hain2011, §10.3, p. 36]. The section determines the class $\kappa_x \in H^1_{\text{ét}}(T, R^1 f_* \mathbb{Q}_\ell(1))$ of the cycle $(2g-2)x - \omega_{C/T}$, so $\kappa_x$ equals the class $\kappa_j$ of some tautological point $x_j$ [@hain2011, Corollary 12.6, p. 41, and §13.1, p. 43]. A $K$-point with $\kappa_x = \kappa_j$ is $x_j$ [@hain2011, Proposition 13.2, p. 43].
- *Sections.* Let $\pi^{\mathrm{un}}$ be the $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-unipotent completion of the geometric fundamental group. Its weight filtration $W_{-r}\pi^{\mathrm{un}}$ is its lower central series [@hain2011, §15, p. 48]. A section of $\pi_1(C) \to G_K$ would induce a section of $\mathcal{G}_C/W_{-3}\pi^{\mathrm{un}} \to \mathcal{G}_K$, whose kernel is the two-step nilpotent quotient $\pi^{\mathrm{un}}/W_{-3}\pi^{\mathrm{un}}$ [@hain2011, proof of Theorem 2, p. 55]. Hain computes these sections with his variant of Kim's non-abelian cohomology. Sections modulo $W_{-2}$ form a principal homogeneous space under $H^1(G_K, H) = \mathbb{Q}_\ell\kappa_1 \oplus \dots \oplus \mathbb{Q}_\ell\kappa_n$ [@hain2011, §18, p. 54]. Such a section lifts modulo $W_{-3}$ exactly when a connecting map $\delta$ to $H^2$ vanishes on it [@hain2011, §14.2, p. 46]. Hain computes $\delta$ explicitly, and its zeros are exactly the $n$ classes $\kappa_j/(2g-2)$ [@hain2011, Lemma 18.2 and Corollary 18.3, p. 55]. When $n = 0$ there are none, which proves part 2. Reading the formulas with $n = 0$, as is done here, Hain's identification makes the sections modulo $W_{-2}$ a single point [@hain2011, §18, p. 54]. Lemma 18.2 then gives its obstruction as $(2g-2)\mathbf{c}$, which is nonzero because $\mathbf{c}$ is a basis of $H^2(\mathfrak{g}_K, \Lambda^2_0 H)$ in this case [@hain2011, Proposition 17.1, pp. 52–53].
- *Where $g \ge 5$ enters.* To pass from $\mathcal{M}_g[m]$ to its generic point, Hain shows that restriction $H^1_{\text{ét}}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n/k}[m], \mathbb{V}) \to H^1(G_K, V)$ is an isomorphism, and that the corresponding map on $H^2$ is injective, for the relevant local systems [@hain2011, Proposition 16.3, p. 50]. This rests on a result for Zariski open subsets of powers of the universal curve, over an algebraically closed field, that holds for $g = 3$ and $g \ge 5$ [@hain2011, Theorem 16.1, p. 49]. Hain attributes this invariance under passing to open subsets to a corollary of his density theorem, whose proof uses Goresky–MacPherson stratified Morse theory and Putman's result on Picard groups of level coverings of $\mathcal{M}_{g,n}$ for $g \ge 5$ [@hain2011, §1, pp. 4–5]. The bracket argument above needs only $g \ge 4$, but Proposition 16.3 and the proofs of parts 2 and 3 assume $g \ge 5$.

**Hodge theory.** Hodge theory supplies part of the input:

- The presentations of the weight-graded Lie algebras of the completed mapping class groups refine computations in Hain's earlier paper on infinitesimal presentations of Torelli groups (J. Amer. Math. Soc. 10 (1997)) [@hain2011, §9, p. 25]. There the weight filtration was constructed using Hodge theory. Hain checks for $g \ge 3$ that it agrees with the filtration that weighted completion gives [@hain2011, Remark 8.5, p. 22].
- The presentation of the weight-graded Lie algebra of the unipotent fundamental group of a curve follows from Labute's results, or from Hodge theory through Deligne, Griffiths, Morgan and Sullivan [@hain2011, §9.2, p. 26].
- A lemma on extensions of variations of mixed Hodge structure shows that the group of sections of the relative Jacobian is finitely generated when the monodromy local system has no global sections, with no assumption on $k$. For $k$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, Hain uses the [Mordell–Weil theorem](mordell-weil-theorem.md) instead [@hain2011, Proposition 11.2, p. 37, and Appendix A, p. 57]. Finite generation is part of the definition of a topologically ample family [@hain2011, Definition 11.1 (iv), p. 37].

**Small genus.**

- *$g = 2$.* Part 1 fails. For $m + n \ge 2$, the generic curve over $k(\mathcal{M}_{2,n}[2m])$ has exactly $6 + 2n$ rational points. Besides the tautological points, there are the six Weierstrass points, which are rational because the level is even, and the $n$ hyperelliptic conjugates of the tautological points. That there are no others follows from Earle and Kra's result [@hain2011, §1, p. 3 and footnote 2].
- *$g = 3, 4$.* Part 1 holds, but Hain's paper does not prove part 2 in these genera. Over $k$ finitely generated over $\mathbb Q$, [generic descent combined with the bracket obstruction](generic-curve-nonsplitting-in-small-genus.md) proves non-splitting for $g=4$ at every full level. Without level, non-splitting is known for every $g\ge3$, even after geometrically abelianizing, by Li, Litt, Salter and Srinivasan [@lilittsaltersrinivasan2023, Corollary 6.1.3, p. 31]. The linked entry gives the deductions and the distinction between the level hypotheses.

**Positive characteristic.** Watanabe carries Hain's method over to $\mathbb{F}_p$, using specialization of fundamental groups. Let $\ell \ne p$ be a prime and $K$ the function field of $\mathcal{M}_{g,n/\mathbb{F}_p}[\ell^m]$. If $g \ge 4$, or if $g = 3$, $p \ge 3$ and $\ell = 2$, then the only $K$-rational points of the generic curve are the $n$ tautological points [@watanabe2019, Theorem 1, p. 2]. With $\mathbb{F}_q = \mathbb{F}_p(\zeta_{\ell^m})$ and $L$ the function field of $\mathcal{M}_{g/\mathbb{F}_q}[\ell^m]$, the fundamental group sequence of the generic curve over $L$ does not split for $g \ge 4$ [@watanabe2019, Theorem 2, p. 2], so the section conjecture, which Watanabe states for finitely generated infinite fields, holds for that curve [@watanabe2019, §1, p. 2, and Corollary 3, p. 3]. Here the bound is $g \ge 4$, unlike Hain's $g \ge 5$.

**Context.** Hain says that the work was inspired by Ellenberg and Kim, who independently tried to bound the rational points of hyperbolic curves over number fields by bounding conjugacy classes of sections of $\pi_1(C)/L^r \to G_K$, where $L^r$ is the $r$th term of the lower central series of the geometric fundamental group [@hain2011, §1, p. 2]. He presents the result as a case in which Kim's non-abelian cohomology finds all the rational points of a curve [@hain2011, §1, p. 5]. Under the hypotheses of part 2 on $k$, and for every $n \ge 0$, $g \ge 5$ and $2 \le r \le 5$, the map from $C(K)$ to conjugacy classes of sections of the extension of $G_K$ by $(\pi^{\mathrm{un}}/W_{-r-1}\pi^{\mathrm{un}})(\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ is a bijection [@hain2011, Theorem 18.4, p. 55].
