**Setting.** Use the notation of [the lower central series of pro-$p$ surface groups](lower-central-series-of-pro-p-surface-groups.md): $p$ is a prime, $g \ge 2$, $D$ is the pro-$p$ surface group of genus $g$, the [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $2g$ with $q = 0$, and $V = D^{\mathrm{ab}}$, a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of rank $2g$. Automorphisms are continuous, and $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ is a profinite group. Since $D$ is centre-free, identify $d \in D$ with the inner automorphism $c_d(x) = dxd^{-1}$, so that $D \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(D)$; then $\alpha c_d \alpha^{-1} = c_{\alpha(d)}$. Let $K_i$ be the kernel of $\mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{Aut}(D/D_{i+1})$, where $(D_i)$ is the closed lower central series.

Let $\pi : \mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V)$ be $\alpha \mapsto \alpha^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Its image is the group of symplectic similitudes ([symplectic similitudes lift](symplectic-similitudes-lift-to-pro-p-surface-groups.md)); in particular it contains $-I$ and $(1+p)I$. For $m \ge 1$ let $\Gamma_m$ be the kernel of $\mathrm{GL}(V) \to \mathrm{GL}(V/p^mV)$. For a pro-$p$ group $G$, its Frattini subgroup $\Phi(G)$ is the closure of $G^p[G, G]$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, §3.9, p. 224]. So $D/\Phi(D) = V/pV$. Let $\bar\pi : \mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(V/pV)$ be the reduction of $\pi$, $P = \ker\bar\pi$ and $\bar G = \bar\pi(\mathrm{Aut}(D))$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p$ be a prime and $g \ge 2$.

1. If $N$ is a closed normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ that is isomorphic to $D$ as a profinite group, then $N = D$.
2. Every continuous automorphism of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ maps $D$ onto $D$, and is conjugation by an element of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$. So conjugation $\mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Aut}(D))$ is an isomorphism onto the continuous automorphisms, and $\mathrm{Out}(\mathrm{Aut}(D)) = 1$.

By the classification, every Demushkin group with $q = 0$ and rank $n$ is such a $D$ with $n = 2g$ (see [symplectic similitudes lift](symplectic-similitudes-lift-to-pro-p-surface-groups.md)). So this answers both questions of [the problem](demushkin-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md) for Demushkin groups with $q = 0$ and rank at least $4$, for every $p$.

**Inputs.**

- From [the lower central series](lower-central-series-of-pro-p-surface-groups.md), for $g \ge 2$: $D$ is centre-free (3(a)); every nontrivial closed normal subgroup of $D$ has trivial centralizer in $D$ (3(c)); and the statements 4(a)–(e) on the Johnson homomorphisms $J_i : K_i/K_{i+1} \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D)$, namely equivariance, torsion-freeness of $K_i/K_{i+1}$ and of $DK_i/DK_{i+1}$, $K_i \cap D = D_i$, $\bigcap_i K_i = 1$, $\bigcap_i DK_i = D$, and that an $\alpha$ with $\pi(\alpha) = \lambda I$ acts on $K_i/K_{i+1}$ by conjugation as multiplication by $\lambda^i$.
- From [the inner part of the first Johnson quotient](inner-part-of-the-first-johnson-quotient-for-pro-p-surface-groups.md): every $\mathbb{Z}_p$-submodule of $K_1/K_2$ of rank $2g$ that is stable under conjugation by $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ lies in $DK_2/K_2$.
- *Frattini argument.* A family of elements of a pro-$p$ group $G$ generates $G$ topologically if and only if its image generates $G/\Phi(G)$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.9.1), p. 225].
- *Open normal subgroups.* If $G$ is a pro-$p$ group with $\mathrm{cd}_p G = n < \infty$, and $N$ is a closed normal subgroup with $\mathrm{cd}_p N = n$ and $H^n(N, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ finite, then $N$ is open in $G$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (3.3.10), p. 177].
- *Demushkin groups,* as recorded in [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md): an infinite Demushkin group has cohomological dimension $2$ and $H^2 \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$, and an open subgroup $H$ of a Demushkin group $G$ of rank $n(G) \ge 2$ is a Demushkin group with $n(H) - 2 = (G : H)(n(G) - 2)$.

**Three lemmas.**

*Lemma A.* For a topologically finitely generated pro-$p$ group $G$, the kernel $P_G$ of $\mathrm{Aut}(G) \to \mathrm{GL}(G/\Phi(G))$ is a pro-$p$ group.

*Proof.* The subgroups $P_G \cap \ker(\mathrm{Aut}(G) \to \mathrm{Aut}(G/U))$, for characteristic open $U \subseteq \Phi(G)$, are open and normal in $P_G$ and form a basis at $1$, so it suffices that the image of $P_G$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(Q)$, $Q = G/U$, is a $p$-group. Since $\Phi(G)$ is the closure of $G^p[G,G]$, $\Phi(Q) = \Phi(G)/U$, and the image consists of automorphisms acting trivially on $Q/\Phi(Q)$. Let $\beta$ be one of them, of prime order $\ell \ne p$. Fix $y_1, \dots, y_d \in Q$ whose images form a basis of $Q/\Phi(Q)$, and let $S$ be the set of tuples $(z_1, \dots, z_d)$ with $z_j \in y_j\Phi(Q)$. Then $\beta$ permutes $S$, and $|S| = |\Phi(Q)|^d$ is prime to $\ell$, so $\beta$ fixes some tuple in $S$. That tuple generates $Q$ by the Frattini argument, so $\beta = 1$, a contradiction. A finite group without elements of prime order $\ell \ne p$ is a $p$-group. $\square$

*Lemma B.* $\bar G$ acts irreducibly on $V/pV$ and has no nontrivial normal $p$-subgroup.

*Proof.* Let $e_i, f_i \in V$ be the classes of $x_{2i-1}, x_{2i}$, and $\omega = \sum_i e_i \wedge f_i \in \Lambda^2 V$. As shown in the setting of [the inner part](inner-part-of-the-first-johnson-quotient-for-pro-p-surface-groups.md), $A \in \mathrm{GL}(V)$ is a symplectic similitude with multiplier $1$ exactly when $\Lambda^2(A)\omega = \omega$. The following elements of $\mathrm{GL}(V)$ fix $\omega$, as one checks on its terms $e_j \wedge f_j$, so they lie in $\pi(\mathrm{Aut}(D))$, and their reductions lie in $\bar G$: $u_j : f_j \mapsto f_j + e_j$; $u'_j : e_j \mapsto e_j + f_j$; $e_i \mapsto e_{\sigma(i)}$, $f_i \mapsto f_{\sigma(i)}$ for a permutation $\sigma$; and $e_j \mapsto f_j$, $f_j \mapsto -e_j$; each fixing the basis vectors not mentioned. Let $W \ne 0$ be a $\bar G$-stable subspace of $V/pV$ and $0 \ne w \in W$. If the $f_j$-coordinate $\gamma$ of $w$ is nonzero, then $u_jw - w = \gamma e_j \in W$; if its $e_j$-coordinate $\gamma$ is nonzero, then $u'_jw - w = \gamma f_j \in W$. So $W$ contains the class of a basis vector, and then, by the last two kinds of maps, the classes of all basis vectors. If $M$ is a normal $p$-subgroup of $\bar G$, then the orbits of $M$ on $V/pV$ have $p$-power size, so the number of vectors fixed by $M$ is divisible by $p$; as $0$ is one of them, $M$ fixes a nonzero vector. The fixed space is $\bar G$-stable since $M$ is normal, so it is $V/pV$, and $M = 1$. $\square$

*Lemma C.* Let $p = 2$. Give $V/2V$ the basis formed by the reductions of the $e_i, f_i$ of Lemma B, and the form $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ of [the theorem on transitive matrix spaces](transitive-matrix-spaces-under-symplectic-conjugation-over-f2.md) with respect to this basis. Then $\bar G \subseteq \mathrm{Sp}(V/2V)$, and $\bar G$ contains the transvection $t_u$ for every $u \in V/2V$. Hence $\bar G$ acts transitively on the nonzero vectors of $V/2V$, by part 1 of that theorem.

*Proof.* On $V$ let $\langle x, y\rangle = x^{\mathsf T}\Omega y$, in coordinates for the basis of classes of $x_1, \dots, x_{2g}$, with $\Omega$ as in [symplectic similitudes lift](symplectic-similitudes-lift-to-pro-p-surface-groups.md). This form is alternating, with $\langle e_i, f_j\rangle = \Omega_{2i-1,2j} = \delta_{ij}$ and $\langle e_i, e_j\rangle = \langle f_i, f_j\rangle = 0$, so it reduces modulo $2$ to the form above. Note that $\Omega^{-1} = -\Omega$. If $B \Omega B^{\mathsf T} = \mu\Omega$ with $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$, then inverting gives $B^{-\mathsf T}\Omega B^{-1} = \mu^{-1}\Omega$, that is, $\langle B^{-1}x, B^{-1}y\rangle = \mu^{-1}\langle x, y\rangle$. As $\mu^{-1} \equiv 1 \pmod 2$, the reduction of $B^{-1}$, and hence that of $B$, lies in $\mathrm{Sp}(V/2V)$. So $\bar G \subseteq \mathrm{Sp}(V/2V)$. For $u \in V$ let $T_u(x) = x + \langle x, u\rangle u$. As the form is alternating,
$$\langle T_ux, T_uy\rangle = \langle x, y\rangle + \langle x, u\rangle\big(\langle y, u\rangle + \langle u, y\rangle\big) + \langle x, u\rangle\langle y, u\rangle\langle u, u\rangle = \langle x, y\rangle,$$
that is, $T_u^{\mathsf T}\Omega T_u = \Omega$. So $T_u$ is invertible, and inverting $\Omega = T_u^{-\mathsf T}\Omega T_u^{-1}$ gives $T_u\Omega T_u^{\mathsf T} = \Omega$. By part 2 of [the lifting theorem](symplectic-similitudes-lift-to-pro-p-surface-groups.md), $T_u = \pi(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, and $\bar\pi(\alpha) = t_{\bar u}$, where $\bar u$ is the reduction of $u$. Every element of $V/2V$ is such a $\bar u$. $\square$

**Proof of 1.** Let $N$ be as in part 1 and fix an isomorphism $\theta : N \to D$.

*Step 1: conjugation $\phi : \mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{Aut}(N)$, $\phi(\alpha)(n) = \alpha n\alpha^{-1}$, is injective and continuous.* First, $N \cap D \ne 1$. Otherwise $[N, D] \subseteq N \cap D = 1$, since both are normal. Then for $n \in N$ and $d \in D$, $c_{n(d)} = nc_dn^{-1} = c_d$, so $n(d) = d$ as $D$ is centre-free; so $N = 1$, which is absurd. Let $\alpha \in \ker\phi$. Then $\alpha$ commutes with $c_m$ for every $m \in M = N \cap D$, so $\alpha(m) = m$. For $x \in D$ and $m \in M$, $xmx^{-1} \in M$, so $\alpha(x)m\alpha(x)^{-1} = \alpha(xmx^{-1}) = xmx^{-1}$. So $x^{-1}\alpha(x)$ centralizes $M$, a nontrivial closed normal subgroup of $D$, and $x^{-1}\alpha(x) = 1$ by 3(c). So $\alpha = 1$. For continuity, let $U$ be a characteristic open subgroup of $N$ and $n_1, \dots, n_k$ topological generators of $N$. The $\alpha$ with $\phi(\alpha)$ trivial on $N/U$ are those with $\alpha n_j\alpha^{-1} \in n_jU$ for all $j$, an open set.

*Step 2: let $\bar\phi : \mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{GL}(N/\Phi(N))$ be $\phi$ followed by reduction. Then $\ker\bar\phi = P$, $\bar\phi$ maps onto the image $\bar G_N$ of $\mathrm{Aut}(N)$ in $\mathrm{GL}(N/\Phi(N))$, $N/\Phi(N)$ is an irreducible $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$-module, and $N \subseteq P$.* By Step 1, $\phi$ maps $\ker\bar\phi$ injectively and continuously into $P_N$, which is pro-$p$ by Lemma A. So $\ker\bar\phi$ is pro-$p$, its image in $\bar G$ is a normal $p$-subgroup, and that image is trivial by Lemma B. So $\ker\bar\phi \subseteq P$, and $|\bar\phi(\mathrm{Aut}(D))| \ge (\mathrm{Aut}(D) : P) = |\bar G|$. Transport by $\theta$ identifies $\bar G_N$ with $\bar G$, so $|\bar G_N| = |\bar G|$, and as $\bar\phi(\mathrm{Aut}(D)) \subseteq \bar G_N$, equality holds throughout. By Lemma B transported by $\theta$, $\bar G_N$ acts irreducibly on $N/\Phi(N)$. Finally, for $n \in N$, $\phi(n)$ is an inner automorphism of $N$, which is trivial on the abelian quotient $N/\Phi(N)$.

*Step 3: $\pi(N) = 1$, that is, $N \subseteq K_1$.* Put $H = \pi(N)$; then $H \subseteq \Gamma_1$ by Step 2. Suppose $H \ne 1$, and let $m \ge 1$ be largest with $H \subseteq \Gamma_m$, which exists as $\bigcap_m \Gamma_m = 1$. The map $\Gamma_m \to \mathrm{M}_{2g}(\mathbb{F}_p)$, $1 + p^mA \mapsto A \bmod p$, is a homomorphism with kernel $\Gamma_{m+1}$, and $B(1 + p^mA)B^{-1} = 1 + p^mBAB^{-1}$ for $B \in \mathrm{GL}(V)$. Let $W \ne 0$ be the image of $H$. The composite $\psi : N \to H \to W$ is a surjective homomorphism onto an elementary abelian $p$-group, so it factors through $\bar\psi : N/\Phi(N) \to W$, and $\bar\psi(\alpha \cdot x) = \pi(\alpha)\bar\psi(x)\pi(\alpha)^{-1}$ for $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, where $\alpha$ acts on $N/\Phi(N)$ through $\bar\phi$.

*If $p$ is odd.* Choose $c \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ with $\pi(c) = -I$. Conjugation by $-I$ is trivial, so $c$ acts trivially on $W$. On the other hand, $\bar\pi(c) = -1$ is central in $\bar G \cong \mathrm{Aut}(D)/P$ and is not $1$, as $p$ is odd. By Step 2, $\bar\phi(c)$ is a central element of $\bar G_N$ with $\bar\phi(c)^2 = 1 \ne \bar\phi(c)$. As $p$ is odd, $N/\Phi(N)$ is the direct sum of the $(\pm 1)$-eigenspaces of $\bar\phi(c)$, which are $\bar G_N$-stable, so by irreducibility $\bar\phi(c) = -1$. Then $\bar\psi(x) = c \cdot \bar\psi(x) = \bar\psi(\bar\phi(c)x) = -\bar\psi(x)$ for all $x$, so $\bar\psi = 0$ as $p$ is odd, contradicting $W \ne 0$.

*If $p = 2$.* The map $\bar\psi$ is injective, since its kernel is an $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$-stable subspace of the irreducible module $N/\Phi(N)$ (Step 2) and $\bar\psi \ne 0$. So $\dim W = 2g$. Conjugation by $\pi(\alpha)$ acts on $\mathrm{M}_{2g}(\mathbb{F}_2) = \mathrm{End}(V/2V)$ as conjugation by $\bar\pi(\alpha)$, and $\bar\pi(\alpha)$ ranges over $\bar G$, so $W$ is stable under conjugation by $\bar G$. By Lemma C, $\bar G$ acts transitively on the nonzero vectors of $V/2V$. Transport by $\theta$ identifies $\bar G_N$ acting on $N/\Phi(N)$ with $\bar G$ acting on $D/\Phi(D) = V/2V$, so $\bar\phi(\mathrm{Aut}(D)) = \bar G_N$ acts transitively on the nonzero vectors of $N/\Phi(N)$. Applying $\bar\psi$, $\bar G$ acts transitively on $W \setminus \{0\}$ by conjugation. As $\dim W = 2g \ge 4$, this contradicts part 2 of [the theorem on transitive matrix spaces](transitive-matrix-spaces-under-symplectic-conjugation-over-f2.md), which applies to $\bar G$ by Lemma C.

*Step 4: put $\phi_\theta(\alpha) = \theta \circ \phi(\alpha) \circ \theta^{-1} \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$. Then $\phi_\theta$ is an injective continuous homomorphism with $\phi_\theta(N) = D$, and $\phi_\theta(D) \subseteq K_1$.* The first claims follow from Step 1, and $\phi_\theta(n) = c_{\theta(n)}$. The reduction of $\pi(\phi_\theta(\alpha))$ is $\bar\phi(\alpha)$ transported by $\theta$, so $\bar\pi \circ \phi_\theta$ has kernel $P$, which contains $D$. Put $H' = \pi(\phi_\theta(D)) \subseteq \Gamma_1$ and suppose $H' \ne 1$. Define $m'$, $W' \ne 0$ and $\bar\psi' : D/\Phi(D) = V/pV \to W'$ from $d \mapsto \pi(\phi_\theta(c_d))$ as in Step 3. Since $\alpha c_d\alpha^{-1} = c_{\alpha(d)}$, we get
$$\bar\psi'(\bar\pi(\alpha)v) = B\bar\psi'(v)B^{-1}, \qquad B = \pi(\phi_\theta(\alpha)).$$
*If $p$ is odd*, take $\alpha = c$. Then $\bar\pi(c)v = -v$, and $B \equiv -I \pmod p$ by Step 3, since $\bar\phi(c) = -1$. Conjugation by an element of $-\Gamma_1$ is trivial on $\Gamma_{m'}/\Gamma_{m'+1}$. So $-\bar\psi'(v) = \bar\psi'(v)$, and $\bar\psi' = 0$, a contradiction.

*If $p = 2$.* By the identity just displayed, the kernel of $\bar\psi'$ is stable under $\bar\pi(\mathrm{Aut}(D)) = \bar G$. As $V/2V$ is an irreducible $\bar G$-module (Lemma B) and $\bar\psi' \ne 0$, $\bar\psi'$ is injective, so $\dim W' = 2g$. Conjugation by $B$ acts on $\Gamma_{m'}/\Gamma_{m'+1} = \mathrm{End}(V/2V)$ as conjugation by the reduction $\bar\pi(\phi_\theta(\alpha))$ of $B$. As $\alpha$ varies, these reductions range over all of $\bar G$, because $\bar\pi \circ \phi_\theta$ is $\bar\phi$ transported by $\theta$, and $\bar\phi$ maps onto $\bar G_N$ (Step 2). So $W'$ is stable under conjugation by $\bar G$. Since $\bar G$ acts transitively on the nonzero vectors of $V/2V$ (Lemma C), the identity $\bar\psi'(\bar\pi(\alpha)v) = B\bar\psi'(v)B^{-1}$ shows that $\bar G$ acts transitively on $W' \setminus \{0\}$ by conjugation. As $\dim W' = 2g \ge 4$, this again contradicts part 2 of [the theorem on transitive matrix spaces](transitive-matrix-spaces-under-symplectic-conjugation-over-f2.md).

*Step 5: $N \not\subseteq K_2$, and $N^{\mathrm{ab}} \to K_1/K_2$ is injective.* First a lemma: let $G \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ be a subgroup, $d \ge 1$, and $Q \subseteq K_d$ a closed subgroup normalized by $G$, with $Q \not\subseteq K_{d+1}$, $Q^{\mathrm{ab}}$ free of finite rank over $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and $Q/\Phi(Q) = Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/p$ irreducible under conjugation by $G$. Then the map $h : Q^{\mathrm{ab}} \to K_d/K_{d+1}$ is injective. Indeed, $K_d/K_{d+1}$ is a torsion-free abelian pro-$p$ group, as $J_d$ embeds it continuously in $\mathrm{Hom}(V, \mathrm{gr}_{d+1} D)$; $J_d$ is continuous because evaluation $\kappa \mapsto \kappa(x)$ is (proof of 4(d) of the lower central series entry). So $h$ is a $G$-equivariant $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear map, and $L = \ker h$ is a $G$-stable submodule with $Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/L$ torsion-free. Then $L \cap pQ^{\mathrm{ab}} = pL$, so $L/pL$ embeds $G$-equivariantly in $Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/p$. If $L/pL = Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/p$, then $L$ has full rank, $Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/L$ is torsion-free of rank $0$, and $h = 0$, contradicting $Q \not\subseteq K_{d+1}$. So $L/pL = 0$, and $L = 0$ by Nakayama's lemma.

Let $r$ be largest with $N \subseteq K_r$; it exists as $N \subseteq K_1$, $N \ne 1$ and $\bigcap_i K_i = 1$. Apply the lemma to $G = \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ and $Q = N$, using Step 2 and $N^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong V$: $h_N : N^{\mathrm{ab}} \to K_r/K_{r+1}$ is injective. Let $s$ be largest with $\phi_\theta(D) \subseteq K_s$, using Step 4. Apply the lemma to $G = \phi_\theta(\mathrm{Aut}(D))$ and $Q = \phi_\theta(D) \cong D$. Here $\phi_\theta(\alpha)\phi_\theta(c_d)\phi_\theta(\alpha)^{-1} = \phi_\theta(c_{\alpha(d)})$, so $Q/\Phi(Q)$ is $V/pV$ with $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ acting through $\bar\pi$, which is irreducible by Lemma B. So $h_Q : \phi_\theta(D)^{\mathrm{ab}} \to K_s/K_{s+1}$ is injective.

Let $\lambda = 1 + p$ and choose $a \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ with $\pi(a) = \lambda I$. By 4(e), $a$ acts on $K_r/K_{r+1}$ by conjugation as $\lambda^r$, so by injectivity of $h_N$, conjugation by $a$ acts on $N^{\mathrm{ab}}$ as $\lambda^r$. Transporting by $\theta$, $\pi(\phi_\theta(a)) = \lambda^rI$. By 4(e) again, $\phi_\theta(a)$ acts on $K_s/K_{s+1}$ as $\lambda^{rs}$, so by injectivity of $h_Q$, conjugation by $\phi_\theta(a)$ acts on $\phi_\theta(D)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ as $\lambda^{rs}$. But $\phi_\theta(a)\phi_\theta(c_d)\phi_\theta(a)^{-1} = \phi_\theta(c_{a(d)})$, and $a$ acts on $D^{\mathrm{ab}} = V$ as $\lambda$. So $\lambda^{rs} = \lambda$. As $\lambda$ is not a root of unity, $rs = 1$, so $r = 1$. Thus $h_N : N^{\mathrm{ab}} \to K_1/K_2$ is injective and $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$-equivariant, and conjugation by $a$ acts on $N^{\mathrm{ab}}$ as $\lambda$.

*Step 6: $N \subseteq DK_i$ for every $i \ge 1$.* For $i = 1$ this is $N \subseteq K_1$. For $i = 2$: the image of $N$ in $K_1/K_2$ is $h_N(N^{\mathrm{ab}})$, a submodule of rank $2g$ stable under conjugation by $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$. By [the inner part of the first Johnson quotient](inner-part-of-the-first-johnson-quotient-for-pro-p-surface-groups.md) it lies in $DK_2/K_2$, so $N \subseteq DK_2$. Suppose $N \subseteq DK_i$ with $i \ge 2$. The quotient $DK_i/DK_{i+1} \cong K_i/D_iK_{i+1}$ is abelian, so $N \to DK_i/DK_{i+1}$ induces an $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$-equivariant $\mathbb{Z}_p$-linear map $k : N^{\mathrm{ab}} \to DK_i/DK_{i+1}$. Conjugation by $a$ acts on $N^{\mathrm{ab}}$ as $\lambda$ (Step 5) and on $DK_i/DK_{i+1}$, a quotient of $K_i/K_{i+1}$, as $\lambda^i$ (4(e)). So $(\lambda^i - \lambda)k = 0$. Here $\lambda^i - \lambda = \lambda(\lambda^{i-1} - 1) \ne 0$, and $DK_i/DK_{i+1}$ is torsion-free by 4(c), so $k = 0$ and $N \subseteq DK_{i+1}$.

*Step 7: $N = D$.* By Step 6 and 4(d), $N \subseteq \bigcap_i DK_i = D$. So $N$ is a nontrivial closed normal subgroup of $D$. As $N \cong D$ is an infinite Demushkin group, $\mathrm{cd}_p N = 2 = \mathrm{cd}_p D$ and $H^2(N, \mathbb{Z}/p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$. So $N$ is open in $D$, and the index formula gives $2g - 2 = (D : N)(2g - 2)$. As $g \ge 2$, $N = D$. $\square$

**Proof of 2.** Let $\sigma$ be a continuous automorphism of $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$. Then $\sigma(D)$ is a closed normal subgroup isomorphic to $D$, so $\sigma(D) = D$ by part 1. Since $d \mapsto c_d$ is a homeomorphism of $D$ onto its image, there is a continuous automorphism $\tau$ of $D$ with $\sigma(c_d) = c_{\tau(d)}$. For $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, applying $\sigma$ to $\alpha c_d\alpha^{-1} = c_{\alpha(d)}$ gives $c_{\sigma(\alpha)(\tau(d))} = c_{\tau(\alpha(d))}$. So $\sigma(\alpha) = \tau\alpha\tau^{-1}$, and $\sigma$ is conjugation by $\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$. Conjugation $\mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Aut}(D))$ is injective, since an automorphism commuting with all $c_d$ fixes every $d$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The case $p = 2$.* Only Steps 3 and 4 treat odd $p$ and $p = 2$ differently. For odd $p$ they use the element $c$ with $\pi(c) = -I$, a nontrivial central element of $\bar G$. For $p = 2$ this element is trivial modulo $2$, and they use instead [the theorem on transitive matrix spaces](transitive-matrix-spaces-under-symplectic-conjugation-over-f2.md), whose proof is a Sylow argument and a rank count. Lemmas A and B, Steps 1, 2 and 5–7 and the inputs hold for every $p$; in Step 5, $\lambda = 1 + p$ is an integer greater than $1$, so it is not a root of unity. For free pro-$2$ groups, Tsujimura instead identifies $N/\Phi(N)$ with the standard module of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_2)$ or its dual, using the automorphisms of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_2)$, shows that neither maps nontrivially to $\mathrm{End}$ of the standard module, and treats rank $2$ separately [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Lemmas 2.2–2.4 and proofs of Propositions 3.8 and 3.10, pp. 7–12]. The argument here identifies no module, so it needs no information on the automorphisms of $\bar G$.
- *Other Demushkin groups.* The proof uses $q = 0$ throughout: $V$ is free, all symplectic similitudes lift, and the lower central series is computed for the surface relation. Demushkin groups with $q \ne 0$, including $G_K(p)$ for $p$-adic fields $K$ containing $\zeta_p$, are not covered. The argument already breaks at Lemma B. For a Demushkin group $D'$ of rank $n$ with $q \ne 0, 2$, the relation can be taken to be $x_1^q(x_1, x_2) \cdots (x_{n-1}, x_n)$ ([symplectic similitudes lift](symplectic-similitudes-lift-to-pro-p-surface-groups.md)), so the torsion subgroup of $D'^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is cyclic of order $q$, generated by the class of $x_1$. Its image in $D'/\Phi(D')$ is a line preserved by every automorphism of $D'$, so the analogue of the irreducibility in Lemma B fails.
- *Comparison with the free case.* Steps 1–7 are Tsujimura's Lemma 3.3, Lemma 3.5, Proposition 3.8, Proposition 3.10, Lemmas 1.7 and 3.11, Proposition 3.12 and the proof of Theorem 3.1 [@tsujimura2026freeaut, pp. 6–13], with these replacements: the lifting of symplectic similitudes for his Proposition 1.2 (i); [the lower central series](lower-central-series-of-pro-p-surface-groups.md) for his Proposition 1.2 (ii), (iii), (v)–(vii) and for internal indecomposability; [the inner part](inner-part-of-the-first-johnson-quotient-for-pro-p-surface-groups.md) for his Lemma 1.4 and Proposition 1.5; Lemma B for his Lemma 2.1 (i); Lemma C and [the theorem on transitive matrix spaces](transitive-matrix-spaces-under-symplectic-conjugation-over-f2.md) for his Lemmas 2.2–2.4 when $p = 2$; and, for the final step, openness of normal subgroups of maximal cohomological dimension and the index formula for Demushkin groups in place of the corresponding facts for free pro-$p$ groups. His Proposition 1.2 (iv) is not needed.
