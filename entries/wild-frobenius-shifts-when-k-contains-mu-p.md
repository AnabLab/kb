**Setting.** Use the Setting and notation of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite of degree $d$ with residue field of order $q$, $G=G_K$, $P_K$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P_K$, and $G=F/(\varrho)$ with $F=P\rtimes\mathcal G$ free among semidirect products of $\mathcal G$ with pro-$p$ groups. Here $\mathcal G$ is generated by $\sigma,\tau$ with the single relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^q$ and maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma$, and $\tau$ generates tame inertia $I\cong\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$. The integers $h,\kappa\in\mathbb Z_p$ satisfy $\tau(\zeta)=\zeta^h$ and $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^\kappa$ for $\zeta\in\mu_T$, the group of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, of order $p^s$. We also use $\Lambda_s$, $\omega_s$, $u_s=\kappa e_h-\sigma$ and the $p^s$-central series $P_K^{p,i,s}$ from there. Let $I_K\subseteq G$ be the inertia group, the inverse image of $I$. Write $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$ and $x^y=yxy^{-1}$.

Assume now that $\mu_p\subseteq K$, and let $p^m$ be the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K$. Then $1\le m\le s$. Put $k=\mathbb Z/p^m$ and $H^1(G,k)=\operatorname{Hom}(G,k)$, continuous homomorphisms. Since $[\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p):\mathbb Q_p]=p-1$ divides $d$, the degree $d$ is even and at least $2$, so
$$\varrho=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa\,x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d].$$

*Two congruences.*

- (i) $h\equiv1\pmod{p^s}$. The extension $K(\mu_T)/K$ lies in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$, so it is tamely ramified, and its ramification index is prime to $p$. Its Galois group acts faithfully on $\mu_T\cong\mathbb Z/p^s$ and fixes $\mu_p\subseteq K$, so it embeds in the kernel of $(\mathbb Z/p^s)^\times\to(\mathbb Z/p)^\times$, a $p$-group. The ramification index divides the degree, a power of $p$, so it is $1$, and the inertia element $\tau$ fixes $\mu_T$. This is the fact recorded in [presentations](local-galois-group-presentations.md), "With roots of unity". In particular $\omega_s(\gamma)=1$ for $\gamma\in I$.
- (ii) $\kappa\equiv1\pmod{p^m}$, because $\sigma\in\Gamma$ fixes $\mu_{p^m}\subseteq K$.

*The unramified character.* Let $f\in H^1(G,k)$ be the composite of $G\to G/I_K$ with the homomorphism to $k$ sending the image of $\sigma$ to $1$. This exists and is unique, because $y\mapsto\sigma^yI$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb Z}\to\Gamma/I=G/I_K$ (proof of part 3 of the cited entry). So $f(\sigma)=1$, and $f$ vanishes on $I_K$, hence on $\tau$ and on every $x_i$.

**Theorem (proved here).**

1. *Constraint.* Let $A_m$ be the set of automorphisms $\alpha$ of $G$ that induce the identity on $\Gamma$ and on the image of $I_K$ in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}/p^m$. For $\alpha\in A_m$ there is a unique $\nu(\alpha)\in k$ with
$$\psi\circ\alpha=\psi+\nu(\alpha)\,\psi(x_0)\,f\qquad\text{for all }\psi\in H^1(G,k).$$
Equivalently, $\alpha(\sigma)\sigma^{-1}$ and $x_0^{\nu(\alpha)}$ have the same image in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}/p^m$.
2. *Realization.* For every $\nu\in\mathbb Z_p$ there is an automorphism $\alpha_\nu$ of $G$ with
$$\alpha_\nu(\sigma)\in\sigma x_0^{\nu}P_K^{p,2,s},\qquad\alpha_\nu(\tau)\in\tau P_K^{p,2,s},\qquad\alpha_\nu(x_i)\in x_iP_K^{p,2,s}\quad(0\le i\le d).$$
It induces the identity on $\Gamma$ and on $P_K/P_K^{p,2,s}$, it lies in $A_m$, and $\nu(\alpha_\nu)=\nu\bmod p^m$.
3. *The invariant.* $A_m$ is a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, and $\nu:A_m\to k$ is a surjective homomorphism vanishing on conjugation by elements of $P_K$. For every $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, $\theta A_m\theta^{-1}=A_m$, and there is $\lambda_\theta\in k^\times$, independent of $\alpha$, with $\nu(\theta\alpha\theta^{-1})=\lambda_\theta\,\nu(\alpha)$ for all $\alpha\in A_m$.

In particular, if $\nu\notin p^m\mathbb Z_p$, then $\alpha_\nu$ acts nontrivially on $H^1(G,k)$, hence on $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$. It is therefore not inner, and its outer class does not lie in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Aut}(G^{\mathrm{ab}})$, although $\alpha_\nu$ is trivial on $\Gamma$ and on $P_K/P_K^{p,2,s}$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $d\ge4$, which for $\mu_p\subseteq K$ is the same as $d\ge3$. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$.

- Some $\iota_g\circ\alpha$, where $\iota_g$ is conjugation by $g\in G$, lies in $A_m$, and $\nu(\iota_g\circ\alpha)$ does not depend on the choice of $g$. Call it $\nu(\alpha)$.
- If $\iota_g\circ\alpha$ is the identity on $G/[P_K,P_K]$ for some $g$, then $\nu(\alpha)=0$. So the conclusion of the corollary of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), proved there for $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, fails for $K$ if some $\alpha_\nu$ with $\nu\notin p^m\mathbb Z_p$ has finitely many outer conjugates.
- The conjugates of the outer class of $\alpha$ have invariants $\lambda_\theta\nu(\alpha)$, which lie in the finite set $k^\times\nu(\alpha)$. So the finiteness of the conjugacy class gives no condition on $\nu(\alpha)$ through this invariant.

**Proof of part 1.** Let $\alpha\in A_m$.

*Step 1: the shape of $\alpha$ on characters.* Each $\psi\in H^1(G,k)$ factors through $G^{\mathrm{ab}}/p^m$, and $\psi\circ\alpha-\psi$ is a continuous homomorphism $G\to k$. It vanishes on $I_K$, because $\alpha$ fixes the image of $I_K$ in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}/p^m$. So it factors through $G/I_K$, which is topologically generated by the image of $\sigma$. Hence $\psi\circ\alpha-\psi=\ell(\psi)f$ with $\ell(\psi)=\psi(\alpha(\sigma)\sigma^{-1})$, and $\ell:H^1(G,k)\to k$ is $k$-linear. As $\alpha$ is the identity on $\Gamma$, $\alpha(\sigma)\sigma^{-1}\in P_K\subseteq I_K$, so $\ell(f)=0$. It remains to show $\ell(\psi)=\ell(\chi_0)\psi(x_0)$, with $\chi_0$ as in Step 2.

*Step 2: the maximal pro-$p$ quotient.* Let $E$ be the free pro-$p$ group on $\bar\sigma,\bar x_0,\dots,\bar x_d$. Let $s_0:\mathcal G\to E$ be the homomorphism with $\sigma\mapsto\bar\sigma$ and $\tau\mapsto1$, which respects the relation. Let $\mathcal G$ act on $E$ by conjugation through $s_0$. The universal property of $F$ gives a homomorphism $F\to E\rtimes\mathcal G$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$ and maps $x_i$ to $(\bar x_i,1)$. Composing with $E\rtimes\mathcal G\to E$, $(e,\rho)\mapsto e\,s_0(\rho)$, which is a homomorphism because $\mathcal G$ acts through $s_0$-conjugation, gives a continuous $\Phi:F\to E$ with $\Phi(\sigma)=\bar\sigma$, $\Phi(\tau)=1$ and $\Phi(x_i)=\bar x_i$.

Every continuous homomorphism $\beta$ from $F$ to a pro-$p$ group kills $\tau$, which generates a group of order prime to $p$. So $\beta=\beta_0\circ\Phi$, where $\beta_0$ is the homomorphism on $E$ with $\bar\sigma\mapsto\beta(\sigma)$ and $\bar x_i\mapsto\beta(x_i)$: both sides agree on $\sigma,\tau,x_0,\dots,x_d$, which topologically generate $F$. Let $\bar\varrho=\Phi(\varrho)$, let $R\subseteq E$ be the closed normal subgroup it generates, and put $G(p)=E/R$. Then $\Phi$ induces a surjection $G\to G(p)$, and by what was just shown every continuous homomorphism from $G$ to a pro-$p$ group factors uniquely through it. So $G(p)$ is the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $G$, whose kernel is characteristic. Hence $\alpha$ induces an automorphism $\bar\alpha$ of $G(p)$, and inflation identifies $H^1(G(p),k)$ with $H^1(G,k)$, compatibly with $\alpha$ and $\bar\alpha$.

As $\Phi$ is continuous and $\Phi(\tau)=1$,
$$\bar\varrho=(\bar x_0^{\bar\sigma})^{-1}\,\bar x_0^{\kappa h'}\,\bar x_1^{p^s}[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]\cdots[\bar x_{d-1},\bar x_d],\qquad h'=\frac{h+h^2+\cdots+h^{p-1}}{p-1}\in\mathbb Z_p.$$
Here the power $\pi/(p-1)$ in $(x_0,\tau)$ acts on the pro-$p$ group $E$ through its $\mathbb Z_p$-component $(p-1)^{-1}$. By (i), $h'\equiv1\pmod{p^s}$, and by (ii), $\kappa\equiv1\pmod{p^m}$. As $m\le s$, $\kappa h'-1=p^ma_0$ for some $a_0\in\mathbb Z_p$.

*Step 3: Labute's cup product formula.* Put $Q_0=p^m$, and let $E_1=E$, $E_{i+1}=E_i^{Q_0}(E_i,E)$ (closed subgroups) be the descending $Q_0$-central series, with $\mathrm{gr}_i=E_i/E_{i+1}$. Labute writes $(x,y)=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, so $[x,y]=(x^{-1},y^{-1})$. The image of $(x,y)$ in $\mathrm{gr}_{i+j}$, for $x\in E_i$ and $y\in E_j$, is the bracket of the images, and $\mathrm{gr}$ is a Lie algebra over $k$. The map $x\mapsto x^{Q_0}$ induces $\pi\cdot:\mathrm{gr}_1\to\mathrm{gr}_2$ [@labute1967, §2, Proposition 1 and the paragraph before Proposition 2, pp. 112–113]. For odd $p$, $\binom{Q_0}{2}\equiv0\pmod{Q_0}$, so $\pi\cdot$ is additive on $\mathrm{gr}_1$ [@labute1967, Proposition 2 (2) and the Remarks after it, p. 113]. Let $\xi_\sigma,\xi_0,\dots,\xi_d$ be the images of the generators, a basis of $\mathrm{gr}_1\cong k^{d+2}$.

Write $\bar\varrho$ as the product of $(\bar\sigma^{-1},\bar x_0)=\bar\sigma\bar x_0^{-1}\bar\sigma^{-1}\bar x_0$, $(\bar x_0^{a_0})^{Q_0}$, $(\bar x_1^{p^{s-m}})^{Q_0}$ and the $[\bar x_{2i-1},\bar x_{2i}]=(\bar x_{2i-1}^{-1},\bar x_{2i}^{-1})$. Each factor lies in $E_2$, so $\bar\varrho\in E_2$ and $R\subseteq E_2$, and the class of $\bar\varrho$ in the abelian group $\mathrm{gr}_2$ is the sum of the classes of the factors:
$$\bar\varrho\equiv-[\xi_\sigma,\xi_0]+\sum_{i=1}^{d/2}[\xi_{2i-1},\xi_{2i}]+a_0\,\pi\cdot\xi_0+p^{s-m}\,\pi\cdot\xi_1.$$
Order the basis as $\xi_\sigma,\xi_0,\xi_1,\dots,\xi_d$. In Labute's notation $\bar\varrho\equiv\sum a_i\,\pi\cdot\xi_i+\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}[\xi_i,\xi_j]$, this means $a_{\sigma0}=-1$, $a_{2i-1,2i}=1$ for $1\le i\le d/2$, all other $a_{ij}$ with $i<j$ are $0$, and $a_\sigma=0$.

Let $f,\chi_0,\dots,\chi_d\in H^1(G(p),k)=H^1(E,k)$ be the basis dual to $\xi_\sigma,\xi_0,\dots,\xi_d$. Under the identification with $H^1(G,k)$, the first element is the unramified character $f$, as both vanish on the $x_i$ and send $\sigma$ to $1$. The transgression $H^1(R,k)^E\to H^2(G(p),k)$ is bijective [@labute1967, §1.4, pp. 111–112], and $\bar\varrho$ defines $\bar r:H^2(G(p),k)\to k$, $\bar r(a)=\mathrm{tg}^{-1}(a)(\bar\varrho^{-1})$. Labute's Proposition 3 gives $\bar r(\chi_i\cup\chi_j)=a_{ij}$ for $i<j$, $-a_{ji}$ for $i>j$, and $\binom{Q_0}{2}a_i$ for $i=j$, for the cup product with respect to the multiplication $k\times k\to k$ [@labute1967, Proposition 3, p. 113]. Hence:

- $\bar r(f\cup\chi_0)=-1$, $\bar r(f\cup\chi_i)=0$ for $i\ge1$, $\bar r(f\cup f)=0$ and $\bar r(\chi_1\cup\chi_2)=1$;
- $\bar r(\psi\cup\psi')=-\bar r(\psi'\cup\psi)$ for all $\psi,\psi'$, as the diagonal values $\binom{Q_0}{2}a_i$ vanish in $k$.

Moreover, $\bar r$ is an isomorphism. It is onto, since it takes the value $1$. It is injective, because an $E$-invariant $u\in H^1(R,k)$ satisfies $u(e\bar\varrho e^{-1})=u(\bar\varrho)$ for $e\in E$ [@labute1967, §1.1, p. 109], and these conjugates topologically generate $R$, so $u(\bar\varrho^{-1})=0$ forces $u=0$.

*Step 4: comparison.* Cup products are natural, so $\bar\alpha^*(a\cup b)=\bar\alpha^*a\cup\bar\alpha^*b$. As $H^2(G(p),k)\cong k$ is cyclic, $\bar\alpha^*$ acts on it as multiplication by some $\lambda\in k^\times$. By Step 1, $\bar\alpha^*\psi=\psi+\ell(\psi)f$. For $\psi,\psi'\in H^1$, using $\bar r(f\cup f)=0$ and antisymmetry,
$$\lambda\,\bar r(\psi\cup\psi')=\bar r(\psi\cup\psi')+\ell(\psi)\,\bar r(f\cup\psi')-\ell(\psi')\,\bar r(f\cup\psi).$$
For $\psi=\chi_1$, $\psi'=\chi_2$ this gives $\lambda=1$. For $\psi=\chi_i$ with $i\ge1$ and $\psi'=\chi_0$ it gives $0=-\ell(\chi_i)$. With $\ell(f)=0$, writing $\psi=\psi(\sigma)f+\sum_{i\ge0}\psi(x_i)\chi_i$ gives $\ell(\psi)=\ell(\chi_0)\psi(x_0)$. Put $\nu(\alpha)=\ell(\chi_0)$. It is unique, because $\chi_0(x_0)=1$ and $f$ has order $p^m$. For the equivalent form: $\psi(\alpha(\sigma)\sigma^{-1})=\ell(\psi)$ and $\psi(x_0^{\nu(\alpha)})=\nu(\alpha)\psi(x_0)$ agree for every $\psi$, and the continuous homomorphisms to $k$ separate the points of the profinite abelian group $G^{\mathrm{ab}}/p^m$, which has exponent dividing $p^m$, by Pontryagin duality. $\square$

As $\bar r(f\cup\psi)=-\psi(x_0)$, part 1 can be written as $\psi\circ\alpha=\psi-\nu(\alpha)\,\bar r(f\cup\psi)\,f$: a symplectic transvection along $f$ for the cup product of $G(p)$.

**Proof of part 2.** Let $Q=G/P_K^{p,3,s}$, $\bar P=P_K/P_K^{p,3,s}$, $N_s=P_K/P_K^{p,2,s}$ and $M=P_K^{p,2,s}/P_K^{p,3,s}$. Then $M$ is abelian and central in $\bar P$, since $[P_K^{p,2,s},P_K]\subseteq P_K^{p,3,s}$, it has exponent dividing $p^s$, and it is normal in $Q$. Write $M$ additively, with $Q$ acting by conjugation; $\bar P$ acts trivially. Images in $Q$ keep their names.

*(a) Inertia fixes $x_0$ modulo $P_K^{p,2,s}$.* Apply the Computation of the cited entry with $r=s$, $N=P^{p,2,s}$ and $A=P/P^{p,2,s}$, which is abelian, central in itself, of exponent dividing $p^s$ and normal in $F/N$, and with $X=1$ and $a$ the class of $x_0$. Since $(1,\tau)=\tau^\pi=1$, the image of $(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa$ in $A$ is $u_s$ times the class of $x_0$. The factors $x_1^{p^s}$ and $[x_i,x_j]$ lie in $P^{p,2,s}$. By (F1) of [Wingberg's lifting theorem](wingberg-lifting-for-the-p-s-central-series-of-wild-inertia.md), $P^{p,2,s}$ maps onto $P_K^{p,2,s}$, so $N_s$ is the quotient of $A$ by the closed $\Lambda_s$-submodule generated by the image of $\varrho$, as in the proof of part 1 there. So $\Lambda_su_s$ kills the class $\tilde x_0\in N_s$ of $x_0$. By Lemma (b) there, $\Lambda_su_s=\ker\omega_s$, so $\rho\tilde x_0=\omega_s(\rho)\tilde x_0$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$. By (i), $\gamma x_0\gamma^{-1}\in x_0P_K^{p,2,s}$ for every $\gamma\in I$.

*(b) A lift of $x_0$ fixed by $\tau$.* For $\gamma\in I$ put $\eta(\gamma)=x_0^{-1}\gamma x_0\gamma^{-1}$, computed in $Q$. By (a), $\eta(\gamma)\in M$, and $\eta$ is continuous. As $M$ is central in $\bar P$, $\gamma\delta x_0\delta^{-1}\gamma^{-1}=\gamma x_0\gamma^{-1}\cdot\gamma\eta(\delta)\gamma^{-1}=x_0\,\eta(\gamma)\,\gamma\eta(\delta)$, so $\eta(\gamma\delta)=\eta(\gamma)+\gamma\eta(\delta)$.

We find $n\in M$ with $\eta(\gamma)=\gamma n-n$ for all $\gamma\in I$. Let $U\subseteq M$ be an open subgroup stable under $G$. Such $U$ form a basis at $0$, closed under finite intersections: the stabilizer in $G$ of an open subgroup is open, by continuity of the action and compactness, so it has finitely many distinct conjugates, whose intersection is open and $G$-stable. The group $I$ acts on the finite $p$-group $M/U$ through a finite quotient. Its kernel is an open subgroup of $I\cong\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$, so it is the closure of $\langle\tau^{m'}\rangle$ for some $m'$ prime to $p$. On this kernel $\eta$ modulo $U$ is a homomorphism to a finite $p$-group from a group whose finite quotients have order prime to $p$, so $\eta(\tau^{m'})\in U$. Put $S=\sum_{i=0}^{m'-1}\eta(\tau^i)$. Summing $\eta(\tau^{i+1})=\eta(\tau)+\tau\eta(\tau^i)$ over $0\le i<m'$ gives $S+\eta(\tau^{m'})=m'\eta(\tau)+\tau S$. So $n_U=-m'^{-1}S$, with $m'$ invertible on $M$, satisfies
$$\eta(\tau)-(\tau-1)n_U=m'^{-1}\eta(\tau^{m'})\in U.$$
The sets $\{n\in M:\eta(\tau)-(\tau-1)n\in U\}$ are closed and nonempty, and they shrink with $U$. By compactness some $n$ lies in all of them, so $\eta(\tau)=\tau n-n$. The set of $\gamma\in I$ with $\eta(\gamma)=\gamma n-n$ is closed and is a subgroup, by the cocycle identity and $\eta(\gamma^{-1})=-\gamma^{-1}\eta(\gamma)$. It contains $\tau$, so it is $I$.

Put $z=x_0n^{-1}\in\bar P$. For $\gamma\in I$, $\gamma z\gamma^{-1}=x_0\,\eta(\gamma)\,(\gamma n)^{-1}$, which in additive notation on the central factor is $x_0\cdot(\eta(\gamma)-\gamma n)=x_0\cdot(-n)=z$. So $z$ commutes with $\tau$, and $z\equiv x_0$ modulo $M$.

*(c) A second section.* Fix $\nu\in\mathbb Z_p$; $z^\nu$ is defined because $\bar P$ is pro-$p$. Since $z^\nu$ commutes with $\tau$, $(\sigma z^\nu)\tau(\sigma z^\nu)^{-1}=\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^q$ in $Q$. As $\mathcal G$ is defined by the single relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^q$, there is a homomorphism $s':\mathcal G\to Q$ with $s'(\sigma)=\sigma z^\nu$ and $s'(\tau)=\tau$. Composed with $Q\to\Gamma$ it is the identification $\mathcal G=\Gamma$, so $s'$ is injective and $Q=\bar P\rtimes s'(\mathcal G)$. Apply the universal property of $F$ to $\bar P\rtimes\mathcal G$, with $\mathcal G$ acting by conjugation through $s'$, and compose with the isomorphism $(y,\rho)\mapsto y\,s'(\rho)$ onto $Q$. This gives a continuous homomorphism $\psi:F\to Q$ with $\psi|_{\mathcal G}=s'$ and $\psi(x_i)=x_i$ for all $i$.

*(d) The relator.* Since $n$ is central in $\bar P$, $z^\nu=x_0^\nu n^{-\nu}$, so $z^\nu x_0z^{-\nu}=x_0$ in $Q$. Hence $\psi(x_0^\sigma)=\sigma z^\nu x_0z^{-\nu}\sigma^{-1}=x_0^\sigma$. As $\psi$ is continuous and fixes $\tau$ and every $x_i$, it fixes $(x_0,\tau)$ and $x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d]$. So $\psi(\varrho)$ is the image of $\varrho$ in $Q$, which is $1$.

*(e) An automorphism of $Q$.* So $\psi$ kills $(\varrho)$, and it maps $P$, the closed normal subgroup generated by the $x_i$, into the normal subgroup $\bar P$. So it induces a continuous homomorphism $G\to Q$ mapping $P_K$ into $\bar P$, hence $P_K^{p,3,s}$ into $\bar P^{p,3,s}$, by induction along the series. By (F1) applied to the projection $P_K\to\bar P$, $\bar P^{p,3,s}$ is the image of $P_K^{p,3,s}$, which is trivial. This gives an endomorphism $\psi_3$ of $Q$, which induces the identity on $\Gamma$, as $\psi_3(\sigma)\in\sigma\bar P$ and $\psi_3(\tau)=\tau$.

$\psi_3$ is onto. Its image $J$ contains the $x_i$ and $s'(\mathcal G)$, so $J\cap\bar P$ is a closed subgroup normalized by $s'(\mathcal G)$ and containing the $x_i$. Its image in $\bar V=P_K/P_K^{(2)}$ is therefore a closed subgroup stable under the action of $\mathcal G$. Here $s'(\rho)$ and $\rho$ act on $\bar V$ in the same way, since they differ by an element of $\bar P$. This image is thus a closed $\Lambda$-submodule containing the $\bar x_i$, which is all of $\bar V$ by part 1 of the cited entry. As $P_K^{p,3,s}\subseteq P_K^{(2)}$, $\bar V$ is the Frattini quotient of the pro-$p$ group $\bar P$, so $J\cap\bar P=\bar P$. As $J$ also maps onto $\Gamma$, $J=Q$. A continuous surjective endomorphism of a topologically finitely generated profinite group is an automorphism, as shown in the proof of part 2 of the cited entry, and $Q$ is topologically generated by $\sigma,\tau,x_0,\dots,x_d$. So $\psi_3$ is an automorphism.

*(f) The lift.* By [Wingberg's lifting theorem for the $p^s$-central series](wingberg-lifting-for-the-p-s-central-series-of-wild-inertia.md) there is an automorphism $\alpha_\nu$ of $G$, inducing the identity on $\Gamma$, that agrees with $\psi_3$ modulo $P_K^{p,2,s}$. As $z^\nu\equiv x_0^\nu$ modulo $M$, it has the three stated properties.

*(g) Its properties.* Conjugation by $P_K$ is trivial on the abelian group $N_s$, and $\alpha_\nu(g)\in gP_K$ for every $g\in G$. So $\alpha_\nu$ maps the class of $gx_ig^{-1}$ in $N_s$ to that of $g\,\alpha_\nu(x_i)\,g^{-1}$, which is the same class. These classes topologically generate $N_s$, because $P_K$ is the closed normal subgroup of $G$ generated by $x_0,\dots,x_d$ (Setting of [even degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md)). So $\alpha_\nu$ is the identity on $N_s$.

For $A_m$: $P_K^{p,2,s}$ is generated by $p^s$-th powers and commutators, and $m\le s$, so it maps to $0$ in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}/p^m$. Hence $\alpha_\nu$ fixes the images of $P_K$ there. The images of $\tau$ and $\alpha_\nu(\tau)$ there are $0$, because each generates a group of order prime to $p$ and $G^{\mathrm{ab}}/p^m$ has exponent $p^m$. As $I_K$ is generated by $P_K$ and $\tau$, $\alpha_\nu\in A_m$. Finally, write $\alpha_\nu(\sigma)\sigma^{-1}=\sigma x_0^\nu y\sigma^{-1}$ with $y\in P_K^{p,2,s}$. Then $\nu(\alpha_\nu)=\chi_0(\sigma x_0^\nu y\sigma^{-1})=\nu$ modulo $p^m$, as $\chi_0$ is a homomorphism killing $y$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Compositions and inverses of elements of $A_m$ lie in $A_m$. For $\alpha,\beta\in A_m$ and $\psi\in H^1(G,k)$, using $f(x_0)=0$,
$$\psi\circ\alpha\circ\beta=\big(\psi+\nu(\alpha)\psi(x_0)f\big)\circ\beta=\psi+\big(\nu(\alpha)+\nu(\beta)\big)\psi(x_0)f.$$
So $\nu$ is a homomorphism. It is onto by part 2. For $g\in P_K$, conjugation by $g$ lies in $A_m$ and fixes every $\psi$, so its invariant is $0$.

Let $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$. It maps $P_K$ and $I_K$ onto themselves and induces on $G/I_K$ an automorphism fixing the Frobenius element ([local Galois invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md)). So $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$ and on the image of $I_K$ in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}/p^m$, and $f\circ\theta=f$, as both vanish on $I_K$ and agree on $\sigma$. The automorphism $\bar\theta$ of $G(p)$ induced by $\theta$ acts on the cyclic group $H^2(G(p),k)$ by some $\lambda_\theta\in k^\times$. In the form after the proof of part 1, with $\theta^*\psi=\psi\circ\theta$,
$$\psi\circ\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}=(\theta^{-1})^*\big(\theta^*\psi-\nu(\alpha)\,\bar r(f\cup\theta^*\psi)\,f\big)=\psi-\nu(\alpha)\,\bar r(f\cup\theta^*\psi)\,f.$$
Here $(\theta^{-1})^*f=f$, and $f\cup\theta^*\psi=\theta^*f\cup\theta^*\psi=\bar\theta^*(f\cup\psi)$, so $\bar r(f\cup\theta^*\psi)=\lambda_\theta\,\bar r(f\cup\psi)$. Hence $\nu(\theta\alpha\theta^{-1})=\lambda_\theta\nu(\alpha)$. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** By [tame innerness](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md), some $\alpha'=\iota_{g_0}\circ\alpha$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$. It represents the outer class of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates. By [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), it is the identity on $P_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, hence on the image of $P_K$ in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}/p^m$. As in step (g), the images of $\tau$ and $\alpha'(\tau)$ there are $0$. So $\alpha'\in A_m$. If $\iota_{g_1}\circ\alpha$ also lies in $A_m$, then $\iota_{g_1g_0^{-1}}$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$, whose centre is trivial (proof of part 3 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). So $g_1g_0^{-1}\in P_K$, and part 3 gives $\nu(\iota_{g_1}\circ\alpha)=\nu(\alpha')$.

If $\iota_g\circ\alpha$ is the identity on $G/[P_K,P_K]$, then it induces the identity on $\Gamma$, so it lies in $A_m$ and has invariant $\nu(\alpha)$. It also induces the identity on $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, because $[P_K,P_K]\subseteq[G,G]$, so it fixes every $\psi$, and $\nu(\alpha)=0$. In particular, an $\alpha_\nu$ with $\nu\notin p^m\mathbb Z_p$ and finitely many outer conjugates would violate that conclusion for $K$.

For $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, $\theta\alpha'\theta^{-1}\in A_m$ represents the conjugate outer class, and part 3 gives it the invariant $\lambda_\theta\nu(\alpha)$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Contrast.* Over $\mathbb Q_p$, which does not contain $\mu_p$ for odd $p$, every automorphism of $G$ fixes $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$ ([automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb Q_p}$](automorphisms-of-g-qp-fix-abelianization.md)). For $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $d\ge3$, [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md) shows that automorphisms with finitely many outer conjugates fix $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Its proof kills the cocycle class with mixing automorphisms built from $\lambda\in\ker\omega$ with nonzero augmentation, and these do not exist when $\omega=1$. Part 2 shows that for $\mu_p\subseteq K$, automorphisms trivial on $\Gamma$ and on $P_K/P_K^{p,2,s}$ can move a Frobenius lift in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Part 1 shows that, modulo $p^m$, they can move it only along $x_0$.
- *Why $x_0$.* In the cup product of $G(p)$, $f$ pairs nontrivially only with $\chi_0$ (Step 3). So the direction $x_0$ is the cup-product dual of the unramified character, and the transvections of part 1 are intrinsic up to the scalar $\bar r$. Part 3 is the reason finite outer conjugacy gives no information on $\nu$: conjugation changes $\nu(\alpha)$ only by a unit.
- *What is not decided.* Whether some $\alpha_\nu$ with $\nu\notin p^m\mathbb Z_p$ has finitely many outer conjugates. Whether $\nu(\alpha)=0$ for every $\alpha$ with finitely many outer conjugates, which is the case $\mu_p\subseteq K$ of the corollary of the cocycle theorem modulo $p^m$. Whether $\alpha_\nu$ can be chosen trivial on all of $P_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, rather than on $P_K/P_K^{p,2,s}$. The map $\nu\mapsto\alpha_\nu$ is not claimed to be a homomorphism.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, every finite $K/\mathbb Q_p$ containing $\mu_p$, every $m$ and $s$; parts 1–3 need no bound on $d$. The case $p=2$ is not treated.
- *Sources.* Labute's paper was opened at §1.1–§2, pp. 109–113, for the transgression, the $q$-central series, and Proposition 3. His standing hypotheses there are $R\subseteq F^q(F,F)$ with $q=p^g$ and $k=\mathbb Z_p/q\mathbb Z_p$, which hold here with $q=p^m$. The presentation and the lifting theorem are used through the linked entries, whose sources were not reopened. The construction was not found in the sources opened; no novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
