**Setting.** Let $k/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite with $\mu_p\subset k$, let $T=k_{tr}$, and put
$$
G=G_k,\qquad P=\mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/T),\qquad
\Gamma=G/P,\qquad A=[P,P].
$$
All commutator subgroups are closed. Let $D=G_k(p)=\mathrm{Gal}(k(p)/k)$ be the full [maximal pro-$p$ quotient](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md), and put $R=\ker(G\to D)$ and $E=G/A$. Thus $E=\mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}/k)$, where $T^{ab}$ is the maximal abelian extension of $T$ in $\bar k$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Every continuous $\beta\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ lifts to
$$
J=G/(A\cap R)=\mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}k(p)/k)
$$
by an automorphism inducing the identity on $D$. In particular, for $k=U_f$, the unramified extension of $\mathbb Q_2$ of degree $f$, the [Frobenius shift](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md) lifts to $\mathrm{Gal}(T^{ab}U_f(2)/U_f)$ and fixes its full maximal pro-$2$ quotient pointwise.

**Proof.** We first refine the lift to $E$, then check compatibility with $D$.

*A lift fixing $E(p)$.* Use the splitting $E=M\rtimes\Gamma$, where $M=P^{ab}$, from Step 2 of [the tame lifting proof](tame-automorphisms-lift-modulo-wild-commutators.md). Put
$$
N=\ker\bigl(\Gamma\to\widehat{\mathbb Z}\to\mathbb Z_p\bigr).
$$
The group $N$ is pro-prime-to-$p$ and $\Gamma/N=\mathbb Z_p$. Let $e$ be its central averaging idempotent in $\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$. Then
$$
M=eM\oplus(1-e)M,\qquad M_N\cong eM,
$$
where $M_N$ denotes continuous coinvariants. The last identification is proved by averaging in the [abelianized wild inertia entry](abelianized-wild-inertia-from-unramified-cyclotomic-data.md): the closed subgroup generated by $(n-1)m$, with $n\in N$, is exactly $(1-e)M$.

Every homomorphism from $E=M\rtimes\Gamma$ to a pro-$p$ group kills the chosen section of $N$ and therefore kills $(1-e)M$. Conversely, the quotient
$$
E(p)\cong eM\rtimes(\Gamma/N)
$$
is pro-$p$. This identifies the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $E$, with quotient map $(m,\gamma)\mapsto(em,\gamma N)$.

Step 1 of the tame lifting proof shows that $\beta$ preserves $N$ and induces the identity on $\Gamma/N$. Steps 3–5 construct a $\beta$-semilinear automorphism $\varphi$ of $M$ that is the identity on $eM$: take the identity on that summand and the isomorphism constructed in Step 5 on $(1-e)M$. Here semilinearity means $\varphi(\gamma m)=\beta(\gamma)\varphi(m)$. Consequently
$$
b(m,\gamma)=(\varphi(m),\beta(\gamma))
$$
is an automorphism of $E$ lifting $\beta$ and inducing the identity on $E(p)$.

*The joint quotient.* The common quotient of $E$ and $D$ induced by $G$ is
$$
C=G/(AR)=E(p).
$$
Indeed it is pro-$p$, and every map from $E$ to a pro-$p$ group, pulled back to $G$, kills both $A$ and $R$, hence their product. There is a natural isomorphism
$$
J\xrightarrow{\sim} E\times_C D.
$$
Its kernel is trivial by the definition of $A\cap R$. To check surjectivity, choose a lift $g\in G$ of the $E$-coordinate of a compatible pair $(x,d)$. The element $d\rho(g)^{-1}$ lies in $\ker(D\to C)=\rho(A)$, where $\rho:G\to D$. Choose $a\in A$ with $\rho(a)=d\rho(g)^{-1}$. Then $ag$ maps to $(x,d)$. The resulting continuous bijection is a homeomorphism by compactness.

Since $b$ induces the identity on $C$, the automorphism $(b,\mathrm{id}_D)$ preserves this fiber product and lifts $\beta$. Its inverse is $(b^{-1},\mathrm{id}_D)$. Finally $A\cap R\subseteq R$, so the full maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $J$ is indeed $D$. This proves the assertion, including the dyadic specialization. $\square$

**Scope.** The compatibility on $E(p)$ is essential: separate lifts on $E$ and $D$ would not by themselves define an automorphism of their joint quotient. Here the compatible lift retains all finite $p$-group quotients of $G_k$, as well as the entire wild-abelian quotient $E$. Lifting it through $A\cap R$ to $G_k$ remains a separate question; this result does not settle the remaining [dyadic classification cases](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md).
