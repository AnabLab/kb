**Setting.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_k$ and residue field $F$ of order $q$. Write $r : G_k \to G_F$ for the reduction map, whose kernel is the inertia group $I_k$. Let $\Sigma$ be a nonempty set of primes and $X$ a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $k$, with smooth compactification $X^{\mathrm{cpt}}$. Let $\Delta_X$ be the maximal pro-$\Sigma$ quotient of $\pi_1(X_{\bar k})$, and $\Pi_X$ the quotient of $\pi_1(X)$ by the kernel of $\pi_1(X_{\bar k}) \to \Delta_X$, so that $1 \to \Delta_X \to \Pi_X \to G_k \to 1$ is exact. A *pro-$\Sigma$ section* is a continuous section $s$ of $\Pi_X \to G_k$, considered up to conjugation by $\Delta_X$. A $k$-rational point $x$ gives the section $s_x$ of its decomposition group, and the *pro-$\Sigma$ section map* $\varphi_{X,\Sigma}$ sends $x$ to the class of $s_x$ [@saidi2012, §2.1, p. 6]. A section is *geometric* if its image lies in a decomposition group at a $k$-rational point of $X^{\mathrm{cpt}}$; for proper $X$ these are Saïdi's point-theoretic sections, the classes in the image of $\varphi_{X,\Sigma}$ [@saidi2012, Definition 2.2, p. 6].

Following Hoshi, $s$ is *unramified* if the composite $I_k \to G_k \xrightarrow{s} \Pi_X \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Delta_X)$, whose last map is conjugation, is trivial, and *potentially unramified* if its image is finite [@hoshi2014conditional, Definition A.1, p. 33]. Good reduction is meant as in [the Oda–Tamagawa criterion](oda-good-reduction-criterion.md): $X^{\mathrm{cpt}}$ extends to a smooth proper curve over $\mathcal{O}_k$ in which the closure of the cusps is finite étale.

**Theorem.**

*Ramification, for any hyperbolic $X$ (Hoshi).*

1. If $p \in \Sigma$, no pro-$\Sigma$ section is potentially unramified. If $X$ does not have good reduction, no pro-$\Sigma$ section is unramified [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition A.2, pp. 33–34].
2. Let $p \notin \Sigma$. Then $s$ is unramified exactly when every connected finite étale cover of $X$ that corresponds to an open subgroup of $\Pi_X$ containing $s(G_k)$ has good reduction [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition A.4, (1) ⇔ (4), p. 35].
3. Let $p \notin \Sigma$, and let $X$ have good reduction. Then $s$ is unramified exactly when it is potentially unramified [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition A.9, p. 39]. It is ramified exactly when, for some $\ell \in \Sigma$, the pro-$\ell$ section it induces is ramified. Then the image of inertia in the geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ fundamental group of the smooth model of $X$ is a nontrivial closed subgroup of a cuspidal inertia group [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition A.6, p. 37]. In particular, if $X$ is proper, every pro-$\Sigma$ section is unramified [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition A.8, p. 39]. Hoshi regards this as a Galois-section version of the valuative criterion of properness [@hoshi2014conditional, Remark A.8.1, p. 39].
4. Let $p \notin \Sigma$, and let $s$ be geometric at a point $x \in X(k)$, not a cusp. Then $s$ is unramified exactly when the hyperbolic curve $X \setminus \{x\}$ has good reduction [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition A.7, p. 38].

*Proper curves with good reduction.* Now let $p \notin \Sigma$, and let $X$ be proper with good reduction, with smooth proper model $\mathcal{X}$ over $\mathcal{O}_k$ and special fibre $\mathcal{X}_s$. Define $\Delta_{\mathcal{X}_s}$ and $\Pi_{\mathcal{X}_s} \to G_F$ for $\mathcal{X}_s$ over $F$ in the same way. Specialization gives a homomorphism $\mathrm{Sp} : \Pi_X \to \Pi_{\mathcal{X}_s}$ over $r$ that restricts to an isomorphism $\Delta_X \to \Delta_{\mathcal{X}_s}$, and the square formed by $\mathrm{Sp}$ and $r$ is cartesian [@saidi2010goodsections, diagram (4.5) and Lemma 4.2.2, p. 46]. The proof notes that the outer action $G_k \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_X)$ factors through $r$ [@saidi2010goodsections, proof of Lemma 4.2.2, p. 46].

5. *(Reduction to the residue field.)* Sending $s$ to the section $\bar s$ of $\Pi_{\mathcal{X}_s} \to G_F$ with $\mathrm{Sp} \circ s = \bar s \circ r$ is a bijection from classes of pro-$\Sigma$ sections of $X$ to classes of sections of $\Pi_{\mathcal{X}_s} \to G_F$. It sends the class of $s_x$, for $x \in X(k)$, to the class of $s_{\bar x}$, where $\bar x \in \mathcal{X}_s(F)$ is the reduction of $x$.
6. *(Consequences.)*
   - (a) $X$ always has pro-$\Sigma$ sections, whether or not $X(k)$ is empty.
   - (b) A pro-$\Sigma$ section $s$ is geometric exactly when $\bar s$ is the section of an $F$-rational point of $\mathcal{X}_s$. So every pro-$\Sigma$ section of $X$ is geometric exactly when every section of $\Pi_{\mathcal{X}_s} \to G_F$ comes from an $F$-rational point of $\mathcal{X}_s$.
   - (c) The image of $\varphi_{X,\Sigma}$ has at most $|\mathcal{X}_s(F)|$ elements. If $X(k) \ne \emptyset$, then $\varphi_{X,\Sigma}$ is not injective. Saïdi states that this map is not injective in general [@saidi2010goodsections, §4.2, p. 45].
   - (d) If $\mathcal{X}_s(F) = \emptyset$, then $X(k) = \emptyset$ and no pro-$\Sigma$ section of $X$ is geometric. This is Saïdi's proof that $\varphi_{X,\Sigma}$ need not be surjective [@saidi2010goodsections, Proposition 4.2.1 and its proof, pp. 45 and 47].
7. *(An explicit family.)* Let $p$ be odd, and let $c \in \mathcal{O}_k^\times$ reduce to a nonsquare in $F$. The smooth projective curve $X$ over $k$ with affine equation $y^2 = c\,(x^{q+p} - x^{p+1} + 1)$ has genus $(q+p)/2 - 1 \ge 2$, good reduction and $\mathcal{X}_s(F) = \emptyset$. So it has pro-$\Sigma$ sections for every nonempty $\Sigma$ not containing $p$, and none of them is geometric. Over $\mathbb{Q}_3$, with $c = -1$, this is the genus $2$ curve $y^2 = -(x^6 - x^4 + 1)$.

Items 5 to 7 are proved below. The proofs are checked here.

**Proof of 5.** Since the square is cartesian, $\Pi_X = \Pi_{\mathcal{X}_s} \times_{G_F} G_k$, with $\mathrm{Sp}$ the first projection and $\Delta_X$ the subgroup $\Delta_{\mathcal{X}_s} \times \{1\}$. So a section $s$ is the same as a continuous homomorphism $t = \mathrm{Sp} \circ s : G_k \to \Pi_{\mathcal{X}_s}$ over $r$, through $s(\sigma) = (t(\sigma), \sigma)$. Let $\tau \in I_k$. Then $t(\tau) \in \Delta_{\mathcal{X}_s}$, and for $\delta \in \Delta_{\mathcal{X}_s}$,
$$s(\tau)\,(\delta, 1)\,s(\tau)^{-1} = (t(\tau)\,\delta\,t(\tau)^{-1}, 1).$$
By item 3, $s$ is unramified, so $t(\tau)$ is central in $\Delta_{\mathcal{X}_s}$. The group $\Delta_X \cong \Delta_{\mathcal{X}_s}$ is slim [@saidi2010goodsections, Lemma 4.1.1, p. 44], so it has trivial centre, and $t(\tau) = 1$. Hence $t = \bar s \circ r$ for a unique section $\bar s$ of $\Pi_{\mathcal{X}_s} \to G_F$, which is continuous because $r$ is a quotient map. Conversely, a section $\bar s$ defines the section $s(\sigma) = (\bar s(r(\sigma)), \sigma)$. For $\delta \in \Delta_X$ we have $\mathrm{Sp} \circ (\delta s \delta^{-1}) = \mathrm{Sp}(\delta)\, (\mathrm{Sp} \circ s)\, \mathrm{Sp}(\delta)^{-1}$, and $\mathrm{Sp}$ maps $\Delta_X$ bijectively onto $\Delta_{\mathcal{X}_s}$. So conjugacy classes correspond.

For the second claim, $x$ extends to $x_{\mathcal{O}} \in \mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_k)$ by the valuative criterion of properness, and $\bar x$ is its closed point. For a proper scheme over a henselian local ring, restriction to the closed fibre is an equivalence between the categories of finite étale covers [@stacks2026, Lemma 58.9.1, Tag 0A48]. So $\pi_1(\mathcal{X}_s) \to \pi_1(\mathcal{X})$ and $G_F \to \pi_1(\mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}_k)$ are isomorphisms, and the specialization map is induced by $\pi_1(X) \to \pi_1(\mathcal{X}) \cong \pi_1(\mathcal{X}_s)$. Saïdi cites Grothendieck's specialization theory for it [@saidi2010goodsections, p. 46]. Apply $\pi_1$ to the morphisms $x$, $x_{\mathcal{O}}$ and $\bar x$ from $\mathrm{Spec}\, k \to \mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}_k \leftarrow \mathrm{Spec}\, F$ to $X \to \mathcal{X} \leftarrow \mathcal{X}_s$. This gives $\mathrm{Sp} \circ s_x = s_{\bar x} \circ r$ up to conjugation, on the full fundamental groups and hence on the geometrically pro-$\Sigma$ quotients. $\square$

**Proof of 6.** (a) $G_F \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is topologically generated by Frobenius $\phi$, and every element $f$ of a profinite group determines a continuous homomorphism from $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ sending $1$ to $f$. Choosing $f \in \Pi_{\mathcal{X}_s}$ over $\phi$ gives a section $\bar s$, and item 5 gives $s$. Saïdi makes this argument over finite fields, where it shows that the analogue of the section conjecture fails, even for the full fundamental group, for proper hyperbolic curves without rational points [@saidi2012, p. 6].

(b) If $s$ is conjugate to $s_x$, then $\bar s$ is conjugate to $s_{\bar x}$ by item 5. Conversely, suppose $\bar s$ is conjugate to $s_{\bar y}$ for some $\bar y \in \mathcal{X}_s(F)$. Since $\mathcal{X}$ is smooth over $\mathcal{O}_k$, Hensel's lemma lifts $\bar y$ to some $y_{\mathcal{O}} \in \mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_k)$, as in Saïdi's use of lifting smooth rational points [@saidi2010goodsections, proof of Proposition 1.6.6, p. 18]. If $y \in X(k)$ is its generic point, then $s$ and $s_y$ have conjugate images $s_{\bar y}$, so they are conjugate by item 5.

(c) By item 5, the class of $s_x$ depends only on $\bar x$, which gives the bound. Now let $x \in X(k)$, with reduction $\bar x$. Since $\mathcal{X}$ is smooth of relative dimension $1$, there are an open neighbourhood $U$ of $\bar x$ in $\mathcal{X}$ and an étale map $g : U \to \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathcal{O}_k}$; after a translation by an element of $\mathcal{O}_k$, $g(\bar x) = 0$. For each $a$ in the maximal ideal of $\mathcal{O}_k$, the étale $\mathcal{O}_k$-scheme $U \times_{\mathbb{A}^1, a} \mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}_k$ has the $F$-rational point $\bar x$ in its closed fibre. By Hensel's lemma it has an $\mathcal{O}_k$-point through it, which gives a point $y_a \in X(k)$ with reduction $\bar x$ and $g(y_a) = a$. These points are distinct, so infinitely many points of $X(k)$ share the class of $s_x$.

(d) Every point of $X(k) = \mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_k)$ has a closed point in $\mathcal{X}_s(F) = \emptyset$. Since $X$ is proper, it has no cusps, so a geometric section would come from a point of $X(k)$. $\square$

**Proof of 7.** Put $f(x) = x^{q+p} - x^{p+1} + 1$ and let $\bar f \in F[x]$ be its reduction. For $a \in F$ we have $a^q = a$, so $a^{q+p} = a^{p+1}$ and $\bar f(a) = 1$. Since $p$ divides $q + p$, the derivative is $\bar f'(x) = -x^p$, so a repeated root of $\bar f$ would be $0$, but $\bar f(0) = 1$. So $\bar f$ is squarefree, monic and of even degree $q + p$. Glue $\mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}_k[x, y]/(y^2 - c f(x))$ and $\mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal{O}_k[u, v]/(v^2 - c f^*(u))$ along $u = 1/x$, $v = y\, u^{(q+p)/2}$, where $f^*(u) = u^{q+p} f(1/u) = 1 - u^{q-1} + u^{q+p}$ also has squarefree reduction and constant term $1$. Both coordinate rings are free $\mathcal{O}_k$-modules, with bases $x^i, x^i y$ and $u^i, u^i v$. The result is finite over $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathcal{O}_k}$ through $x$, hence proper. Its fibres are smooth, since in characteristic different from $2$ a singular point of $y^2 = h(x)$ has $y = 0$ and $h(x) = h'(x) = 0$. It is flat with smooth fibres, so it is a smooth proper model of $X$, of genus $(q+p-2)/2$. In the special fibre, an affine point would need $\bar c\, \bar f(a) = \bar c$ to be a square, and a point with $u = 0$ would need $v^2 = \bar c$. Neither is possible, so $\mathcal{X}_s(F) = \emptyset$, and items 6 (a) and (d) apply. $\square$

**Goodness does not see these sections.** Saïdi calls a section *good* if, for every finite étale neighbourhood $X_i[s]$ of $s$, pulling back along the induced section maps the $\Sigma$-adic completion of $\mathrm{Pic}(X_i[s])$ to zero in $H^2(G_k, \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\Sigma(1))$. It is *uniformly good* if this holds after every finite extension of $k$ [@saidi2010goodsections, Definition 1.4.1, p. 12]. Geometric sections of proper hyperbolic curves are uniformly good [@saidi2010goodsections, Proposition 1.5.2, p. 13]. If $p \notin \Sigma$, every pro-$\Sigma$ section of a proper hyperbolic curve over $k$ is uniformly good [@saidi2010goodsections, Proposition 1.6.3 (i), p. 14; Proposition 4.3.1, p. 47]. So this necessary condition does not detect the nongeometric sections of item 6 (d). For $p \in \Sigma$, Saïdi shows that goodness is equivalent to each $X_i[s]$ having a point over the maximal tamely ramified extension of $k$, among other conditions [@saidi2010goodsections, Proposition 1.6.6, p. 17].

**Context.**

- *$p \in \Sigma$.* Then $\varphi_{X,\Sigma}$ is injective [@saidi2012, §2.3, p. 7], but Hoshi constructed nongeometric pro-$p$ sections over $p$-adic fields; see [explicit nongeometric pro-$p$ sections](explicit-nongeometric-pro-p-sections.md) and the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md). Saïdi notes that whether every section is geometric for $\Sigma$ the set of all primes was not known [@saidi2010goodsections, Remark 4.3.2, p. 48].
- *Intermediate quotients.* In 2021 Saïdi wrote that no nongeometric section of the full arithmetic fundamental group of a hyperbolic curve over a $p$-adic field was known [@saidi2023further, §0, p. 1]. For a proper hyperbolic curve with potentially good reduction and a rational point, he constructs nongeometric sections, after a finite extension of $k$, of certain geometrically pro-nilpotent-by-abelian quotients, including the geometrically metabelian quotient. He also constructs nongeometric sections of geometrically $m$-step solvable pro-$p$ quotients for some curves when $p$ is large [@saidi2023further, Theorem A, Corollary B and Theorem C, p. 4].
- *The method.* Sections of $\Pi_X$ correspond to lifts of $G_k \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_X)$ to $\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta_X)$, so they exist whenever the image of $G_k$ is a free pro-$\Sigma$ group. This is Hoshi's method for $\Sigma = \{p\}$, and Saïdi notes that it is unlikely to work when $\Sigma$ is the set of all primes, because $G_k \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_X)$ is then injective [@saidi2012, Remarks 2.6 (i)–(ii), pp. 9–10]. For $p \notin \Sigma$ and good reduction, the outer action factors through $G_F \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, a free profinite group, so it lifts to $\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta_X)$. Item 6 (a) is this argument.
- *Global use.* Hoshi's condition that a section over a number field be unramified almost everywhere, in his [criteria over small number fields](birational-sections-over-small-number-fields.md), rests on this local analysis. Item 1 is why those criteria use pro-$\ell$ sections for a single prime $\ell$.

**Sources.** Hoshi's appendix is cited from the July 2012 manuscript, and Saïdi's *Good sections* from arXiv:1010.1313v1. Saïdi's 2021 preprint cites a 2012 paper of his in Adv. Math. for the prime-to-$p$ examples; that paper was not opened, as the bibliography note says. The standard facts used in the proofs (the valuative criterion of properness, Hensel's lemma for smooth and étale schemes, local étale coordinates on smooth curves, and the smoothness of hyperelliptic models away from characteristic $2$) are not cited at a location.
