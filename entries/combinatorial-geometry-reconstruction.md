**Geometry and normalization.** For $K \subset L$, the points of $G(L/K)$ are transcendental elements modulo equality of their relative algebraic closures over $K$, with closure induced by algebraic dependence. Write $L^i$ for the perfection and put

$$C_K=L^i\cap\overline K,$$

where $\overline K$ is the algebraic closure of $K$ in a chosen algebraic closure of $L$. The geometry is unchanged by replacing $K\subset L$ with $C_K\subset L^i$ [@gismatullin2008, Introduction, pp. 1–2].

**Reconstruction (Gismatullin).** If $\mathrm{trdeg}(L/K),\mathrm{trdeg}(L'/K')\ge5$, every isomorphism $G(L/K)\to G(L'/K')$ is induced by a field isomorphism $L^i\to L'^i$ carrying $C_K$ onto $C_{K'}$. Thus the geometries are isomorphic exactly when these normalized field extensions are isomorphic [@gismatullin2008, Theorem 4.2(ii), p. 8].

The natural map from automorphisms of $L^i$ preserving $C_K$ **setwise** to $\mathrm{Aut}(G(L/K))$ is surjective. Its kernel is trivial in characteristic $0$; in characteristic $p>0$ it is the infinite cyclic group generated by $x\mapsto x^p$ [@gismatullin2008, Theorem 4.2(iii), pp. 8–9].

**Explicit interpretation.** In this transcendence-degree range, for function fields over algebraically closed constants, fixed first-order formulas recover a domain, an equivalence relation and the graphs of addition and multiplication, after choosing parameters. The quotient is a field isomorphic to the perfection. Topaz spells out this interpretation, while warning that his full mod-$\ell$ reconstruction is not effective because of earlier lattice-recovery steps [@topaz2016, Theorem 6.1 and following explanation, pp. 26–27].

**Anabelian role.** This replaces the fundamental theorem of projective geometry in [mod-$\ell$ reconstruction](mod-l-reconstruction-from-rational-quotients.md) and supplies its transcendence-degree bound. Evans–Hrushovski proved the algebraically closed case; Gismatullin extended it to arbitrary extensions [@topaz2016, §1.3, p. 6]. The original Evans–Hrushovski papers have not been opened for this entry.
