**Setting.** A *generalized global field* is a finitely generated infinite field [@tsujimura2026gs, Definition 1.7, p. 8]. Fix one, $K$, and an irreducible normal scheme $X$ of finite type over $\mathbb Z$ with function field $K$. Write $|X|$ for its set of closed points. Dirichlet density of subsets of $|X|$ is defined using $N(v) = |k(v)|$ [@tsujimura2026gs, Definition 1.8, p. 8].

Let $G$ be a semi-abelian variety over $K$. An open $U \subseteq X$ is *$G$-admissible* if $G$ extends to a semi-abelian scheme $\mathcal G$ over $U$ that is an extension of an abelian scheme by a torus. Such $U$ exist, and for a prime $l$ invertible on $U$ the Tate module $T_l G$ is a representation of $\Pi_U = \pi_1(U)$ [@tsujimura2026gs, Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.2, p. 13]. For a finitely generated subgroup $\Gamma \subseteq G(K)$, $\widehat\Gamma$ is its profinite completion if $\mathrm{char}\, K = 0$, and its pro-prime-to-$p$ completion if $\mathrm{char}\, K = p > 0$. Likewise $\mathcal G_{k(v)}(k(v))'$ is $\mathcal G_{k(v)}(k(v))$ itself, or its prime-to-$p$ part [@tsujimura2026gs, Definition 3.12 and Corollary 3.13, p. 17].

**Theorem** (Tsujimura). Let $Z \subseteq |X|$ have Dirichlet density $0$, and let $|X| = S_1 \sqcup \dots \sqcup S_r$ be a finite partition. Then there is one $j \le r$ such that, for every semi-abelian variety $G$ over $K$:

1. *Cohomology.* For every $G$-admissible open $U \subseteq X$ and every prime $l$ invertible on $U$, the restriction map
$$H^1(\Pi_U, T_l G) \to \prod_{v \in |U| \cap (S_j \setminus Z)} H^1(G_{k(v)}, T_l G)$$
is injective [@tsujimura2026gs, Theorem B, p. 3, and Theorem 3.10, p. 16].
2. *Points.* For every finitely generated $\Gamma \subseteq G(K)$ and every $G$-admissible open $U \subseteq X$ over which each element of $\Gamma$ extends to a section of $\mathcal G$, the map
$$\widehat\Gamma \to \prod_{v \in |U| \cap (S_j \setminus Z)} \mathcal G_{k(v)}(k(v))'$$
is injective. Such $U$ exist [@tsujimura2026gs, Corollary C, pp. 3–4, and Corollary 3.13, p. 17].

The piece $S_j$ does not depend on $G$, $U$, $l$ or $\Gamma$. Tsujimura stresses that this uniformity cannot be seen in the multiplicative case, which concerns a single group [@tsujimura2026gs, Introduction, p. 3].

**The multiplicative case.** For $G = \mathbb G_m$, item 2 says: if $\Gamma \subseteq K^\times$ is finitely generated and consists of units on $U$, then $\widehat\Gamma \to \prod_{v \in |U| \cap (S_j \setminus Z)} k(v)^\times$ is injective. This is Grunewald and Segal's theorem on congruence topologies, in a form that allows the exceptional set $Z$ in every dimension and asks only that it have Dirichlet density $0$ [@tsujimura2026gs, Theorem 0.1 and the paragraph before it, p. 2]. Tsujimura regards it as a refinement of work of Schmidt, Chevalley and Wehrfritz [@tsujimura2026gs, Introduction, p. 2]. For tori, constructing the central regular elements of the proof outline below is almost trivial, so Tsujimura's §1 alone gives a simpler proof of Grunewald and Segal's theorem [@tsujimura2026gs, Introduction, p. 4, and Remark 3.13.1, p. 17].

Lemma 4.2 of Tachihara and Tsujimura's [purely mono-anabelian reconstruction](purely-mono-anabelian-reconstruction-of-number-fields.md) follows from the case $X = \mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal O_F$, for a number field $F$. They use it to cut $F^\times$ out of its Kummer container [@tachiharatsujimura2026, Lemma 4.2 and Remark 4.2.2, pp. 39–40]. The [theorem on algebraic elements of Kummer completions](algebraic-elements-of-kummer-completions-of-number-fields.md) gives a way to do that step without it.

**Proof outline.**

- *Abstract principle.* A profinite group acts on finitely many finitely generated free $\mathbb Z_l$-modules $M_l$. The system has the *ECR property* if, for every $m$, the image of the group contains a central element $z$ with each $z_l - 1$ injective and $\mathrm{Im}(z_l - 1) \subseteq l^m M_l$ [@tsujimura2026gs, Definitions 1.4 and 1.5, p. 6]. If $z$ is central and $z - 1$ is injective, a cocycle $c$ representing a nonzero class has $c(z) \notin \mathrm{Im}(z - 1)$ [@tsujimura2026gs, Lemma 1.3, p. 5]. A counting argument then gives one $\gamma$ with $c(\gamma) \notin \mathrm{Im}(\gamma - 1)$ for finitely many nonzero classes at once [@tsujimura2026gs, Proposition 1.6, p. 7]. Since $H^1(\widehat{\mathbb Z}, M) = M/(\mathrm{Frob} - 1)M$, the Chebotarev density theorem in a finite quotient of $\Pi_U$ gives a set of positive Dirichlet density of closed points at which all these classes stay nonzero [@tsujimura2026gs, Proposition 1.9 and its proof, pp. 8–9]. Chebotarev is the only arithmetic input of this step.
- *Central regular elements.* For abelian varieties these come from the semisimplicity of Tate modules (Faltings, Zarhin, Mori) and the Weil conjectures. Semisimplicity splits the $\mathbb Q_l$-Lie algebra of the $l$-adic image into its derived algebra and its centre. Purity, or for tori the cyclotomic character, shows that the centre acts by a nonzero character on every irreducible constituent, and exponentials of a suitable central element give the elements $z$ [@tsujimura2026gs, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3, Lemmas 2.7 and 2.8, and Proposition 2.9, pp. 10–12]. Over number fields Bogomolov's homothety theorem gives another proof of this step [@tsujimura2026gs, Remark 2.9.1, p. 12].
- *Main results.* Finitely many nonzero classes $\xi_i \in H^1(\Pi_U, T_{l_i} G_i)$ stay nonzero simultaneously on a set containing one of positive Dirichlet density [@tsujimura2026gs, Theorem A, p. 3, and Theorem 3.9, p. 15]. The semi-abelian case reduces to tori and abelian varieties through $0 \to T_l T \to T_l G \to T_l A \to 0$ [@tsujimura2026gs, Lemma 3.7 and Proposition 3.8, p. 15]. Item 1 follows: if no piece worked, choosing a failing class for each piece and applying Theorem A would give a set of positive density inside $Z$ [@tsujimura2026gs, proof of Theorem 3.10, p. 16]. Item 2 follows from item 1 because the Kummer map $\varprojlim_n \Gamma/l^n\Gamma \to H^1(\Pi_U, T_l G)$ is injective, using that $\mathcal G(U)$ is finitely generated [@tsujimura2026gs, Lemma 3.11, p. 16, and proof of Corollary 3.13, p. 17].

**Serre's congruence subgroup problem.** Let $A$ be an abelian variety over a number field $k$, so that $A(k)$ is finitely generated by the Mordell–Weil theorem [@serre1964congruence, §3.1, p. 14]. Let $S$ be a finite set of places of $k$ containing the archimedean ones. For a finite set $I$ of places outside $S$ and open subgroups $U_i$ of the compact groups $A(k_i)$, the elements of $A(k)$ lying in every $U_i$ form an *$S$-congruence subgroup*. Serre's condition $(C_S)$ asks that every subgroup of finite index in $A(k)$ contain an $S$-congruence subgroup [@serre1964congruence, §3.1, p. 14].

- *Serre's results.* He proved $(C_S)$ for every $S$ when $A$ has sufficiently many complex multiplications and when $A$ is an elliptic curve [@serre1964congruence, Theorems 4 and 5, pp. 16–17]. He recalled that Chevalley had settled the analogous problem for the multiplicative group, that is, for $S$-units [@serre1964congruence, §3.1, Remark 2, p. 15].
- *The general case in 1964.* Serre called the general case open [@serre1964congruence, Introduction, p. 3]. He noted that it would follow if $V_p(A)$ were always a semisimple module over the Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_p$ of the $p$-adic Galois image, or if $\mathfrak g_p$ always contained the homotheties [@serre1964congruence, §3.5, p. 17].

The case $r = 1$ of item 2 gives a strong form of $(C_S)$.

**Corollary.** Let $G$, $\Gamma$ and $U$ be as in item 2, and let $\Sigma \subset |U|$ be finite. Every subgroup $\Delta \subseteq \Gamma$ of finite index, prime to $p$ if $\mathrm{char}\, K = p > 0$, contains the kernel of $\Gamma \to \prod_{v \in V} \mathcal G_{k(v)}(k(v))'$ for some finite $V \subset |U| \setminus \Sigma$.

*Proof.* The set $U' = U \setminus \Sigma$ is open, because closed points are closed. It is $G$-admissible, and every element of $\Gamma$ extends over it, by restriction. Apply item 2 to $U'$ with $r = 1$, $S_1 = |X|$ and $Z = \emptyset$, so that $j = 1$.

- *An embedding.* Put $P_v = \mathcal G_{k(v)}(k(v))'$ and $P = \prod_{v \in |U'|} P_v$. Each $P_v$ is finite, of order prime to $p$ in characteristic $p$, so $\Gamma \to P_v$ extends continuously to $\widehat\Gamma$. The resulting map $\varphi : \widehat\Gamma \to P$ is injective by item 2. Since $\widehat\Gamma$ is compact and $P$ is Hausdorff, $\varphi$ is a homeomorphism onto its image.
- *An open subgroup.* The quotient map $\Gamma \to \Gamma/\Delta$ also extends to $\widehat\Gamma$, since $\Gamma/\Delta$ is finite, of order prime to $p$ in characteristic $p$. Its kernel $\bar\Delta$ is open in $\widehat\Gamma$. So $\varphi(\bar\Delta) = \varphi(\widehat\Gamma) \cap O$ for some open $O \subseteq P$, and $1 \in O$. Each $P_v$ is discrete, so $O$ contains $W = \ker(P \to \prod_{v \in V} P_v)$ for some finite $V \subset |U'|$.
- *Conclusion.* Let $\gamma \in \Gamma$ map to $1$ in $P_v$ for all $v \in V$. Then $\varphi(\gamma) \in \varphi(\widehat\Gamma) \cap W \subseteq \varphi(\bar\Delta)$. As $\varphi$ is injective, the image of $\gamma$ in $\widehat\Gamma$ lies in $\bar\Delta$, so $\gamma$ maps to $0$ in $\Gamma/\Delta$, that is, $\gamma \in \Delta$. $\square$

The same argument, applied to item 2 for an arbitrary partition, gives $V \subseteq S_j \setminus Z$.

*Back to Serre's condition.* Take $X = \mathrm{Spec}\, \mathcal O_k$, $G = A$ and $\Gamma = A(k)$, choose $U$ as in item 2, and let $\Sigma$ be the set of finite places in $S$ that lie in $U$. Let $\Delta \subseteq A(k)$ have finite index, and let $V$ be as in the corollary. Since $A$ has no torus part, $\mathcal G$ is an abelian scheme $\mathcal A$ over $U$. For $v \in V$, let $\mathcal O_v$ be the ring of integers of $k_v$. Since $\mathcal A$ is proper over $U$, $A(k_v) = \mathcal A(\mathcal O_v)$, and the kernel $U_v$ of reduction $A(k_v) \to \mathcal A(k(v))$ is open. The places in $V$ lie outside $S$, and the $S$-congruence subgroup given by $V$ and the $U_v$ is the kernel in the corollary, so it lies in $\Delta$. Hence $(C_S)$ holds for every abelian variety over a number field and every $S$, with kernels of reduction as the open subgroups.

*Not claimed as new.* Tsujimura deduces from Faltings's semisimplicity theorem that $V_l$ is a semisimple module over the Lie algebra of an open subgroup of the $l$-adic image, which is the Lie algebra Serre considers [@tsujimura2026gs, discussion after Definition 2.6, p. 11]. So by Serre's remark, $(C_S)$ already follows from Faltings's theorem. When and where the general case was first written down has not been checked here. The partition statement goes further: for any finite partition of the primes, the congruence conditions can be imposed at primes from one piece, the same piece for all semi-abelian varieties.

**Open question.** Tsujimura asks whether analogues of Theorems A and B hold for higher $l$-adic cohomology of smooth proper varieties. His proof uses the Weil conjectures and semisimplicity for abelian varieties, and the general semisimplicity it would need is the open Grothendieck–Serre conjecture [@tsujimura2026gs, Introduction, p. 3].
