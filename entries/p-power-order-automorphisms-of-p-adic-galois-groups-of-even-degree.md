**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd and $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of even degree $d = 2g + 2$, $g \ge 0$, with residue field of order $q$. Use the notation of the Setting of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md): $G = G_K$ with inertia $I_K$ and wild inertia $P_K$, the fields $K_n$ and $L_n = K_n(\mu_p)$, $\Gamma_n = \mathrm{Gal}(L_n/K)$, $\tilde K = K^{\mathrm{ur}}(\mu_p)$, its maximal pro-$p$ extension $\Omega_K$, and for finite $L/K$ the maximal pro-$p$ extension $L(p)$, $V_L = G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathbb{Q}_p$ and the span $I_L \subseteq V_L$ of the image of $I_K \cap G_L$. As shown there, for $n$ prime to $p$ every automorphism $\eta$ of $G$ maps $G_{L_n}$ onto itself and induces the identity on $\Gamma_n$, which is abelian of order prime to $p$, and the induced automorphism $\eta_*$ of $V_{L_n}$ commutes with the action of $\Gamma_n$.

*A presentation.* Let $T$ be the maximal tamely ramified extension of $K$, put $\mathcal{G} = \mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$, and choose generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\mathcal{G}$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 71]. The $p$-power roots of unity in $T$ form a group $\mu_T$ of order $p^s$ with $s \ge 1$, on which $\mathcal{G}$ acts through a character $\alpha : \mathcal{G} \to (\mathbb{Z}/p^s)^\times$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, pp. 71 and 75]. Choose $h, \kappa \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ with $\tau(\zeta) = \zeta^h$ and $\sigma(\zeta) = \zeta^\kappa$ for $\zeta \in \mu_T$, subject to two conditions.

- *$h^{p-1} = 1$.* Since $\alpha(\tau) = \alpha(\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}) = \alpha(\tau)^q$, the order of $\alpha(\tau)$ divides $q - 1$. By [multiplicative groups](multiplicative-group-of-a-p-adic-field.md), applied to $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times = \mu_{p-1} \times (1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p)$. Write $\alpha(\tau)$ as the image of $hu$ with $h \in \mu_{p-1}$ and $u \in 1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$. As $p - 1$ divides $q - 1$, the image of $u$ has order dividing $q - 1$, which is prime to $p$, in the image of $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$, a group of order $p^{s-1}$. So that image is $1$, and $\alpha(\tau)$ is the image of $h$.
- *$\kappa$ is not a root of unity.* The elements of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ in the residue class of $\alpha(\sigma)$ modulo $p^s$ are infinitely many. The roots of unity in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ are the $p - 1$ elements of $\mu_{p-1}$, because $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ is torsion-free: by the same entry $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p \cong \mu_{p^\infty}(\mathbb{Q}_p) \times \mathbb{Z}_p$, and $\zeta_p \notin \mathbb{Q}_p$ as $[\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p) : \mathbb{Q}_p] = p - 1 \ge 2$ ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)).

Write $x^y = yxy^{-1}$ and $[x, y] = xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 74], and let $\pi \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ be the element with $\pi\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{Z}_p$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 72], whose image in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is $1$ and in every other $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is $0$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, §2.1, p. 77]. Then $G$ is isomorphic to the profinite group with generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_d$ and the defining conditions

- (A) the closed normal subgroup generated by $x_0, \dots, x_d$ is a pro-$p$ group;
- (B) $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$;
- (C) $x_0^\sigma = (x_0, \tau)^\kappa\, x_1^{p^s}[x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4] \cdots [x_{d-1}, x_d]$, where $(x_0, \tau) = (x_0^{h^{p-1}}\tau x_0^{h^{p-2}}\tau \cdots x_0^{h}\tau)^{\pi/(p-1)}$

[@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 71]. Jannsen and Wingberg prove this for any generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\mathcal{G}$ as above and any map $\beta : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ lifting $\alpha^{-1}$, not necessarily a homomorphism [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 74 and Theorem 2, p. 75], and obtain the form above with $\beta(\sigma) = \kappa^{-1}$ and $\beta(\tau^i) = h^{p-1-i}$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, §1.4 a), p. 76]. These values lift $\alpha^{-1}$ because $h^{p-1} = 1$. The presented group is $F/(r)$, where $F$ is generated by $\mathcal{G}$ and $x_0, \dots, x_d$ subject to (A) and the relations of $\mathcal{G}$, and $(r)$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by the element $r = (x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0, \tau)^\kappa x_1^{p^s}[x_1, x_2]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, pp. 73–74]. The group $F$ is the semidirect product of $\mathcal{G}$ with the pro-$p$ group $P$ normally generated by the $x_i$, and it is free in the category of such semidirect products [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 74]. So for any $w_0, \dots, w_d \in P$ there is a unique continuous endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and maps $x_i$ to $w_i$. If it fixes $r$, it induces an endomorphism of $F/(r)$. Moreover $r \in P$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, §2.1, p. 77]. Fix an isomorphism and identify $G$ with $F/(r)$.

*Where the generators lie.* Let $P'$ be the closed normal subgroup of $G$ generated by $x_0, \dots, x_d$. It is pro-$p$ by (A), and $G/P' = F/P = \mathcal{G}$, as $r \in P$. Since $P_K$ is the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), $P' \subseteq P_K$, and $G/P_K \cong \mathcal{G}$ has no nontrivial closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup: its inverse image would be a larger closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G$. Neither has $G/P' \cong \mathcal{G}$, which contains $P_K/P'$, so $P_K = P'$. Next, the closed subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle$ of $G/P_K = \mathcal{G}$ is normal and procyclic, so it lies in $I_K/P_K$, the largest such subgroup, which is isomorphic to $\prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$ (same entry). As $\mathcal{G} \subseteq F$ maps isomorphically onto $G/P'$, the subgroup $\langle\tau\rangle$ of $G$ lies in $I_K$ and has order prime to $p$. Finally, $\tau$ acts trivially on $K_n$ and on $\mu_p$ through an element of $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, so $\tau^{p-1} \in G_{L_n}$ for every $n$.

*Two families of automorphisms.*

- *Transvections.* For $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, let $T_k$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, maps $x_2$ to $x_2x_1^k$ and fixes every other $x_i$. Since $x_1^{p^s}[x_1, x_2x_1^k] = x_1^{p^s}x_1x_2x_1^kx_1^{-1}x_1^{-k}x_2^{-1} = x_1^{p^s}[x_1, x_2]$, it fixes $r$ and induces an endomorphism $T_k$ of $G$. The endomorphisms $T_kT_l$ and $T_{k+l}$ agree on the generators, and $T_0 = \mathrm{id}$. So every $T_k$ is an automorphism, and $k \mapsto T_k$ is a homomorphism $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathrm{Aut}(G)$.
- *Symplectic automorphisms*, when $g \ge 1$. Let $\Pi$ be the free group on $\alpha_1, \beta_1, \dots, \alpha_g, \beta_g$, put $\delta = [\alpha_1, \beta_1]\cdots[\alpha_g, \beta_g]$, let $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ be the group of automorphisms of $\Pi$ fixing $\delta$, and let $\iota : \Pi \to P$ map $\alpha_i, \beta_i$ to $x_{2i+1}, x_{2i+2}$. For $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ let $\Psi(\varphi)$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$, fixes $x_0, x_1, x_2$, and maps $x_{2i+1}, x_{2i+2}$ to $\iota(\varphi(\alpha_i)), \iota(\varphi(\beta_i))$. Then $\Psi(\varphi) \circ \iota = \iota \circ \varphi$, as both agree on the letters, so $\Psi(\varphi)$ maps $[x_3, x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d] = \iota(\delta)$ to $\iota(\varphi(\delta)) = \iota(\delta)$, fixes $r$, and induces an endomorphism of $G$. As $\Psi(\varphi)\Psi(\varphi')$ and $\Psi(\varphi\varphi')$ agree on the generators, $\Psi$ is a homomorphism $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi) \to \mathrm{Aut}(G)$. These are Kondo's automorphisms [@kondo2025, discussion after Lemma 2.15, p. 19], written for this presentation. As in the Setting of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), the automorphisms of $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ induced by $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ form a group containing the integral symplectic group $\mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$ for the basis $[\alpha_1], [\beta_1], \dots, [\alpha_g], [\beta_g]$, because every mapping class of the once-punctured surface of genus $g$ lifts to $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ and these classes act on $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ through all of $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$ [@kondo2025, p. 19 and Theorem 2.17, p. 20]. This does not depend on the commutator convention in $\delta$: the automorphism of $\Pi$ inverting every letter exchanges the two versions of $\delta$ and acts on $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ as $-1$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p]$ even. Let $\theta$ be an automorphism of $G_K$ of finite order $p^a$ whose class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has only finitely many conjugates, for instance because it lies in a finite normal subgroup.

1. For every $n$ prime to $p$, $\theta$ acts trivially on $V_{L_n}$ and induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(L_n(p)/K)$.
2. $\theta$ maps $G_{\Omega_K}$ onto itself and induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$, hence on $G_K(p)$, on $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ur}}/K)$ and on $\mathrm{Gal}(K(\mu_{p^\infty})/K)$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here, by combining the Theorem with the cited entries. Let $p$ be odd and $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of any degree.

1. Every automorphism of $G_K$ of finite $p$-power order whose class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has only finitely many conjugates induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$.
2. Every $f \in \mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ of order $p$ with only finitely many conjugates is the class of an automorphism of $G_K$ of order $p$, and every such automorphism induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$.
3. *Tsujimura's question.* Let $N$ be a rival copy of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ of type (a) in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 2, with $M = N \cap G_K$, and suppose that $p$ divides $(G_K : M)$. Then the automorphism of order $p$ in $NG_K$, lying in neither $G_K$ nor $N$, that the Corollary of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) provides induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$.

*Proof.* (1) If $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] = 1$, then $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] \le p - 2$, and part 2 of [small degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-small-degree.md) applies, with no condition on conjugates. If $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 3$ is odd, the hypotheses of part 2 of the Theorem of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) hold for every odd $p$. If $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p]$ is even, part 2 of the Theorem here applies. (2) By part 3 (a) of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), which assumes $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] \le 2$ or finitely many conjugates, $f$ is the class of an automorphism of order $p$. Every such automorphism has class $f$, so part 1 applies to it. (3) The automorphism has order $p$, and its class lies in $NG_K/G_K$, a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ by part 2 (a) of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md). So part 1 applies. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** Fix $n$ prime to $p$, and write $L = L_n$, $\Gamma = \Gamma_n$ and $N = |\Gamma|$. Let $A = \theta_*$, so that $A^{p^a} = 1$ and $A$ commutes with $\Gamma$ (Setting). Let $\gamma_\sigma, \gamma_\tau \in \Gamma$ be the images of $\sigma, \tau$, so that $\gamma_\tau^{p-1} = 1$. For $x \in P$, write $\bar x$ for its image in $V_L$; then $\gamma\bar x$ is the image of $sxs^{-1}$ for any lift $s \in G$ of $\gamma \in \Gamma$.

*Step 1: generators of $I_L$.* Step 1 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) uses about its generators only that they normally generate $P_K$, which holds here. So $I_L \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]^d$ as $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]$-modules, and $I_L$ is the $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]$-span of $\bar x_0, \dots, \bar x_d$. Let $M = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{d} \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]e_i$, let $\varpi : M \to I_L$ be the surjection of $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]$-modules with $\varpi(e_i) = \bar x_i$, and let $R$ be its kernel. Then $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p} R = (d+1)N - dN = N$.

*Step 2: $R$ is generated by the relation.* All factors of (C) other than $(x_0, \tau)^\kappa$ lie in $P \subseteq G_L$, hence so does $(x_0, \tau)^\kappa$. Their images in $V_L$ are as follows.

- $x_0^\sigma = \sigma x_0 \sigma^{-1}$ has image $\gamma_\sigma \bar x_0$.
- Put $y = x_0^{h^{p-1}}\tau x_0^{h^{p-2}}\tau\cdots x_0^{h}\tau$. Moving the $\tau$ to the right gives $y = z\tau^{p-1}$ with $z = \prod_{i=0}^{p-2} \tau^i x_0^{h^{p-1-i}}\tau^{-i} \in P$. As $\tau^{p-1} \in G_L$, $y \in G_L$. The image of $\tau^{p-1}$ in the pro-$p$ group $G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$ is trivial, because $\langle\tau\rangle$ has order prime to $p$. So $y$ has image $\sum_{i=0}^{p-2} h^{p-1-i}\gamma_\tau^i \bar x_0$. The map $G_L \to G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$ is a homomorphism to an abelian pro-$p$ group, on which $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ acts through $\mathbb{Z}_p$, where $\pi/(p-1)$ becomes $1/(p-1)$. Hence $(x_0, \tau)^\kappa$ has image $\kappa\Lambda\bar x_0$ with $\Lambda = \frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-2} h^{p-1-i}\gamma_\tau^i \in \mathbb{Z}_p[\Gamma]$.
- $x_1^{p^s}$ has image $p^s\bar x_1$, and each commutator of two elements of $G_L$ has image $0$.

So $\rho = (\gamma_\sigma - \kappa\Lambda)e_0 - p^s e_1$ lies in $R$. The map $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma] \to R$, $\lambda \mapsto \lambda\rho$, is injective, because the $e_1$-coordinate of $\lambda\rho$ is $-p^s\lambda$. By dimensions, $R = \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]\rho$. In particular $R \subseteq \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]e_0 \oplus \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]e_1$.

*Step 3: isotypic parts.* Let $E = \mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_N)$, which is unramified over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and for each character $\varepsilon$ of $\Gamma$ let $e_\varepsilon \in E[\Gamma]$ be its idempotent, as in Step 3 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md). Extend $\varepsilon$ linearly to $E[\Gamma]$, so that $e_\varepsilon\lambda = \varepsilon(\lambda)e_\varepsilon$. Put $I_\varepsilon = e_\varepsilon(I_L \otimes E)$, $e_i^\varepsilon = e_\varepsilon e_i$ and $\bar x_i^\varepsilon = e_\varepsilon \bar x_i$. Then $\varpi$ induces an isomorphism from $\bigoplus_i Ee_i^\varepsilon$ modulo $E\rho_\varepsilon$ onto $I_\varepsilon$, where
$$\rho_\varepsilon = e_\varepsilon\rho = a_\varepsilon e_0^\varepsilon - p^s e_1^\varepsilon, \qquad a_\varepsilon = \varepsilon(\gamma_\sigma) - \kappa\,\varepsilon(\Lambda).$$
Put $t = \varepsilon(\gamma_\tau)$. Then $t^{p-1} = 1$ and $h^{p-1} = 1$, so $h^{p-1-i} = h^{-i}$ and $u = th^{-1}$ satisfies $u^{p-1} = 1$. Hence $\varepsilon(\Lambda) = \frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{p-2} u^i$, which is $1$ if $u = 1$ and $(u^{p-1} - 1)/((p-1)(u - 1)) = 0$ otherwise. So $a_\varepsilon$ is $\varepsilon(\gamma_\sigma)$ or $\varepsilon(\gamma_\sigma) - \kappa$. The first is a root of unity, and the second is nonzero because $\kappa \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ is not a root of unity. So $a_\varepsilon \ne 0$ for every $\varepsilon$.

Consequently $I_\varepsilon = C_\varepsilon \oplus S_\varepsilon$, where $C_\varepsilon = \varpi(Ee_0^\varepsilon \oplus Ee_1^\varepsilon \oplus Ee_2^\varepsilon)$ has dimension $2$ and basis $\bar x_1^\varepsilon, \bar x_2^\varepsilon$, since $\bar x_0^\varepsilon = (p^s/a_\varepsilon)\bar x_1^\varepsilon$, and $S_\varepsilon = \varpi(\bigoplus_{i \ge 3} Ee_i^\varepsilon)$, on which $\varpi$ is injective. Thus $S_\varepsilon \cong \Pi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes E$ via $[\alpha_i] \mapsto \bar x_{2i+1}^\varepsilon$ and $[\beta_i] \mapsto \bar x_{2i+2}^\varepsilon$, and $S_\varepsilon = 0$ if $g = 0$.

*Step 4: the two families on $I_\varepsilon$.* Every automorphism of $G$ acts on $I_L$ commuting with $\Gamma$ (Setting), so it preserves each $I_\varepsilon$. The image of $T_k(x_2) = x_2x_1^k$ is $\bar x_2 + k\bar x_1$, and $T_k$ fixes the other $x_i$. So $T_k$ acts as the identity on $S_\varepsilon$ and as $1 + kN_\varepsilon$ on $C_\varepsilon$, where $N_\varepsilon(\bar x_1^\varepsilon) = 0$ and $N_\varepsilon(\bar x_2^\varepsilon) = \bar x_1^\varepsilon$. As in Step 2 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), $\Psi(\varphi)$ fixes $x_0, x_1, x_2$ and maps $x_{2i+1}$ to a word in the surface generators $x_3, \dots, x_d \in G_L$, whose image is the sum of the images of its letters weighted by their exponent sums. So $\Psi(\varphi)$ acts as the identity on $C_\varepsilon$ and as $\varphi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes 1$ on $S_\varepsilon$.

*Step 5: finitely many conjugates.* Step 4 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) uses only that the class of $\theta$ has finitely many conjugates. It shows that the automorphisms of $G$ whose action on $V_L$ commutes with $A$ form a subgroup $Y$ of finite index in $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$. So $\{k : T_k \in Y\} = m\mathbb{Z}$ for some $m \ge 1$. If $g \ge 1$, then $\Psi^{-1}(Y)$ has finite index in $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$, so $\Gamma_0 = \{\varphi^{\mathrm{ab}} : \varphi \in \Psi^{-1}(Y)\} \cap \mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$ has finite index in $\mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$.

*Step 6: $A = 1$ on $I_L$.* Fix $\varepsilon$; $A$ preserves $I_\varepsilon$. No element $\zeta \ne 1$ of $p$-power order lies in $E$: otherwise $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta) \subseteq E$ would be unramified over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, but it is totally ramified of degree at least $p - 1 \ge 2$ ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)).

- If $g \ge 1$: $A$ commutes on $I_\varepsilon$ with $\mathrm{id} \oplus X$ for every $X \in \Gamma_0$. By the span fact of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), it commutes with $0 \oplus Z$ for every $E$-linear endomorphism $Z$ of $S_\varepsilon$. Taking $Z = \mathrm{id}$, $A$ preserves $C_\varepsilon$ and $S_\varepsilon$. On $S_\varepsilon$ it commutes with every endomorphism, so it is a scalar $\mu \in E^\times$ with $\mu^{p^a} = 1$, and $\mu = 1$.
- If $g = 0$, then $C_\varepsilon = I_\varepsilon$.

In both cases $A$ preserves $C_\varepsilon$ and commutes there with $1 + mN_\varepsilon$, hence with $N_\varepsilon$, whose matrix in the basis $\bar x_1^\varepsilon, \bar x_2^\varepsilon$ is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. The matrices commuting with it are those of the form $c + bN_\varepsilon$, so $A|_{C_\varepsilon} = c + bN_\varepsilon$ with $c, b \in E$ and $c \ne 0$. As $N_\varepsilon^2 = 0$,
$$1 = A^{p^a}|_{C_\varepsilon} = c^{p^a} + p^a c^{p^a - 1} b\, N_\varepsilon,$$
so $b = 0$ and $c^{p^a} = 1$, whence $c = 1$. So $A$ is the identity on $I_\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon$, hence on $I_L \otimes E = \bigoplus_\varepsilon I_\varepsilon$ and on $I_L$.

*Step 7: the rest.* Steps 6, 7 and 8 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) use only that $A$ is the identity on $I_L$, that $\theta$ has order $p^a$ and induces the identity on $G/I_K$, and that $\zeta_p \in L$. They give that $A$ is the identity on $V_L$, that $\theta$ induces the identity on the Demushkin group $G_L(p)$, of rank $[L:\mathbb{Q}_p] + 2 \ge 4$, and that $\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(L(p)/K)$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** The proof of part 2 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) uses only its part 1, which holds here. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What is new.* For $p \ge 5$ and even degree $d \ge 4$, the Theorem is contained in [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md). For $p \ge 5$ and $d = 2$, [small degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-small-degree.md) gives more, with no condition on conjugates. The new case is $p = 3$ with $d$ even, where the proof also gives a different argument for $p \ge 5$. It contains every $3$-adic field with $\zeta_3 \in K$, since then $2 = [\mathbb{Q}_3(\zeta_3) : \mathbb{Q}_3]$ divides $d$, and every quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_3$, such as $\mathbb{Q}_3(\zeta_3)$ and $\mathbb{Q}_9$. For these fields, part 3 (a) of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) gave only triviality of automorphisms of order $3$ on $G_K(3)$.
- *Method.* For even $d$, the complementary generators $x_0, x_1, x_2$ give a block of dimension $2$ in each isotypic part. The other entry controls it by degrees: a $p$-power root of unity $\zeta \ne 1$ has degree at least $p - 1$ over $E$, which exceeds $2$ only for $p \ge 5$. Here the transvections $T_k$, which fix every generator but one and preserve the relation exactly, act on that block by unipotent matrices with nonzero nilpotent part, and an element of finite order commuting with them is a scalar. For odd $d$ the block has dimension $1$, and the degree argument works for every odd $p$.
- *The choice of $\kappa$.* The proof needs $a_\varepsilon \ne 0$, so that $\bar x_1^\varepsilon \ne 0$ and $N_\varepsilon \ne 0$. For other admissible choices this can fail. For example, if $\sigma$ and $\tau$ act trivially on $\mu_T$, one may take $h = \kappa = 1$, and then $a_\varepsilon = 0$ for the trivial character, so that $T_k$ acts trivially on that isotypic part. The freedom in Jannsen and Wingberg's Theorem 2 allows $\kappa$ to avoid the roots of unity.
- *What remains.* As in [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), nothing is said about the kernel $G_{\Omega_K}$, in particular about the action on tame inertia at primes $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$, and whether $G_K$ has any automorphism of order $p$ whose class has finitely many conjugates remains open for every odd $p$. The case $p = 2$ is not treated: the order-$p$ Demushkin input used in Step 7 is stated there for odd $p$.
