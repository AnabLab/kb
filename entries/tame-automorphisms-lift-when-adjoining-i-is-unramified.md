**Setting.** Let $k/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite of degree $n$, with residue field of order $2^f$. Let $T = k_{tr}$ be its maximal tamely ramified extension, $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(T/k)$, and $\pi : G_k \to \Gamma$ the restriction. Its kernel $V = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/T)$ is the wild inertia group, which is the largest closed normal pro-$2$ subgroup of $G_k$ (see [wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). So every automorphism $\Theta$ of $G_k$ maps $V$ onto itself and induces $\bar\Theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ with $\pi \circ \Theta = \bar\Theta \circ \pi$. Say that $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ *lifts* if $\beta = \bar\Theta$ for some $\Theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$. All automorphisms are continuous.

By Hasse and Iwasawa, $\Gamma$ has generators $\sigma, \tau$ with the single defining relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{2^f}$ [@diekert1984, §3, p. 166], where $\tau$ generates the tame inertia group $I$. Put $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne 2}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. As $I \cong \prod_{\ell \ne 2}\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), $x \mapsto \tau^x$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' \to I$. For $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ and $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, let $\beta_{u,a}$ be the automorphism $\tau \mapsto \tau^u$, $\sigma \mapsto \sigma\tau^a$ of $\Gamma$. By part 2 of [the odd-$p$ entry](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md), whose proof of this point uses only the tame relation and Step 1 of [lifting modulo wild commutators](tame-automorphisms-lift-modulo-wild-commutators.md), every automorphism of $\Gamma$ is $\beta_{u,a}$ for unique $u, a$, every pair occurs, and $\beta_{u,a} \circ \beta_{u',a'} = \beta_{uu', a + ua'}$.

Let $\mu_T$ be the group of $2$-power roots of unity in $T$, of order $2^s$, and $\chi_T : \Gamma \to (\mathbb{Z}/2^s)^\times$ the character by which $\Gamma$ acts on it. Diekert writes $q = 2^s$ and defines a character $\alpha$ by $\rho(\zeta) = \zeta^{\alpha(\rho)^{-1}}$ for $\zeta \in \mu_T$ [@diekert1984, §3, p. 166]. It is written $\psi = \chi_T^{-1}$ here, because $\alpha$ is the Frobenius shift in the [problem entry](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md).

*The hypothesis $s \ge 2$.* It holds exactly when $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is unramified, the case $\sqrt{-1} \in k$ included, as Diekert notes [@diekert1984, §3, p. 166]. Indeed, finite subextensions of $T/k$ have odd ramification index. If $s \ge 2$, then $\sqrt{-1} \in T$, so $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ has degree at most $2$ and odd ramification index, hence is unramified. Conversely, an unramified extension of $k$ lies in $T$. If $s \ge 2$, the extension $k(\mu_T)/k$ lies in $T$ and its degree divides $|(\mathbb{Z}/2^s)^\times| = 2^{s-1}$, so its ramification index is odd and a power of $2$, hence $1$. So $I$ acts trivially on $\mu_T$, that is, $\chi_T(\tau) = 1$, which Diekert also remarks [@diekert1984, Bemerkung after Theorem 3.1, p. 166].

**Diekert's Demuškin formations.** Diekert extends Koch's and Wingberg's definition to $q = 2^s$ with $s \ge 2$ [@diekert1984, §2, p. 160]. For a profinite group $Z$ let $\tilde Z$ be its maximal pro-$2$ quotient, and write $H^i(Z)$ for $H^i(Z, \mathbb{Z}/2)$. Let $X$ be a profinite group with a surjection $\varphi : X \to \Gamma$ with pro-$2$ kernel, and put $X_{\mathcal H} = \varphi^{-1}(\mathcal H)$ for an open subgroup $\mathcal H$ of $\Gamma$. If $\mathcal H$ is normal, conjugation in $X$ makes $\Gamma/\mathcal H \cong X/X_{\mathcal H}$, and $\Gamma$ through it, act on the cohomology of $\tilde X_{\mathcal H}$. Let $n \ge 1$, $s \ge 2$ and $\psi : \Gamma \to (\mathbb{Z}/2^s)^\times$ a character. Then $(X, \varphi)$ is a *Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n$, $2^s$, $\psi$* if for every open normal subgroup $\mathcal H \subseteq \ker\psi$, with $G = \Gamma/\mathcal H$ [@diekert1984, 2.1 Definition, p. 160]:

1. $\tilde X_{\mathcal H}$ is a Demushkin group of rank $n|G| + 2$, and the torsion subgroup of its abelianization has order $2^s$.
2. View $H^1(\mathcal H)$ inside $H^1(\tilde X_{\mathcal H})$ by inflation along $\varphi$, and let $H^1(\mathcal H)^\perp$ be its orthogonal complement for the cup product. Then the $\mathbb{F}_2[G]$-module $H^1(\mathcal H)^\perp/H^1(\mathcal H)$ is free of rank $n$, and the cup product induces a hyperbolic form on it: the quotient is the direct sum of two totally isotropic subspaces.
3. $H^2(\tilde X_{\ker\psi}, \mathbb{Z}/2^s)$ is isomorphic, as a $\Gamma$-module, to $\mathbb{Z}/2^s$ with $\rho \in \Gamma$ acting as multiplication by $\psi(\rho)$.

Diekert proves the following.

- *(D1) Uniqueness over $\Gamma$.* If $(X, \varphi_X)$ and $(Y, \varphi_Y)$ are Demuškin formations over $\Gamma$ with the same invariants, then there is an isomorphism of profinite groups $\theta : Y \to X$ with $\varphi_X \circ \theta = \varphi_Y$ [@diekert1984, 2.2 Eindeutigkeitssatz, p. 160]. The proof ends by choosing an element of a projective limit of nonempty finite sets of isomorphisms $Y/Y^i_{\mathcal H} \to X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$ compatible with the maps to $\Gamma/\mathcal H$, and notes that it gives an isomorphism over $\Gamma$ [@diekert1984, p. 165].
- *(D2) Koch's characterization for $p = 2$.* If $s \ge 2$, then $(G_k, \pi)$ is a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n$, $2^s$ and $\psi = \chi_T^{-1}$ [@diekert1984, 3.3 Satz, p. 167, with the notation of p. 166].

**Theorem.** Suppose that $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is unramified, that is, $s \ge 2$. This is proved here from Diekert's theorems.

1. *Every tame automorphism lifts.* Every automorphism of $\Gamma$ lifts to an automorphism of $G_k$.
2. *Tame image of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$.* The map $\mathrm{Out}(G_k) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma)$ is onto. Writing $\overline{2^{f\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}} \subseteq \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ for the closure of the powers of $2^f$, there is an exact sequence
$$1 \to \mathbb{Z}/(2^f - 1) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma) \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times/\overline{2^{f\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}} \to 1,$$
where the second map sends the class of $\beta_{u,a}$ to the class of $u$, and the first sends $a$ to the class of $\beta_{1,a}$.
3. *Not finitely generated.* For every $r \ge 1$, $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ has a continuous quotient isomorphic to $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^r$. In particular it is not topologically finitely generated.

Now let $f \ge 1$ and let $U_f$, $T$, $P = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2/T)$, $\Gamma_f = \mathrm{Gal}(T/U_f)$, $\pi : G_{U_f} \to \Gamma_f$ and the Frobenius shift $\alpha$ be as in the [problem entry](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md). Put $k' = U_f(\sqrt{-1})$, $\Pi = G_{U_f}$ and $\Pi' = G_{k'}$, an open normal subgroup of index $2$ in $\Pi$. For $x \in \Pi$, let $c_x \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Pi')$ be conjugation by $x$, and let $\iota \in \mathrm{Out}(\Pi')$ be the class of $c_x$ for any $x \in \Pi \setminus \Pi'$, that is, for any lift to $\Pi$ of the nontrivial automorphism of $k'/U_f$.

4. *The Frobenius shift at index two.* The restriction $\pi|_{\Pi'} : \Pi' \to \Gamma_f$ is onto, and its kernel is the wild inertia group of $k'$. Every automorphism of $\Gamma_f$, in particular $\alpha$, lifts through it: there is $\Phi' \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Pi')$ with $\pi \circ \Phi' = \alpha \circ \pi$ on $\Pi'$.
5. *A reformulation of the problem.* Through $\pi|_{\Pi'}$, automorphisms of $\Pi'$ induce automorphisms of $\Gamma_f$, which gives a homomorphism $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi') \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_f)$. The class $\iota$ has order $2$ and lies in its kernel. Automorphisms $\Phi$ of $\Pi$ preserve $P$, by the lemma in the problem entry, and induce $\bar\Phi \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma_f)$ with $\pi \circ \Phi = \bar\Phi \circ \pi$, which gives $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_f)$. Its image equals the image of the centralizer of $\iota$ in $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi')$. In particular, $\alpha$ lifts to $G_{U_f}$ if and only if its class in $\mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_f)$ is the image of an outer automorphism of $G_{U_f(\sqrt{-1})}$ that commutes with $\iota$.

By part 4, the image of all of $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi')$ in $\mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_f)$ is everything. So the [Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md) asks whether the class of $\alpha$, for $f \ge 2$, survives when $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi')$ is replaced by the centralizer of $\iota$. Part 5 does not decide this.

**Examples.**

- $k = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})$, with $n = 2$ and $f = 1$. Every automorphism of $\Gamma$ lifts, and the kernel in part 2 is trivial, so the image of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ is $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times/\overline{2^{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}}$. This group is infinite, because the map $\varepsilon$ in the proof of part 3 factors through it.
- $k = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{3})$. It does not contain $\sqrt{-1}$, but $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is unramified and $s = 2$ [@diekert1984, 4.4 Beispiel, p. 170], so parts 1–3 apply.
- $k = U_f(\sqrt{-1})$ for every $f \ge 1$, which is part 4. For odd $e$ and the tamely ramified fields $K_c = T^{H_c}$ of the reduction in the [problem entry](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md), $\Pi' \cap \pi^{-1}(H_c) = G_{K_c(\sqrt{-1})}$, and $\alpha(H_c) = H_{c+1}$. So a lift $\Phi'$ as in part 4 maps $G_{K_c(\sqrt{-1})}$ onto $G_{K_{c+1}(\sqrt{-1})}$. The reduction in the problem entry needs the same for the fields $K_c$ themselves.

**Proof of part 1.**

*Lemma (re-marking).* Let $(X, \varphi)$ be a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n$, $2^s$, $\psi$, and let $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$. Then $(X, \beta \circ \varphi)$ is a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n$, $2^s$, $\psi \circ \beta^{-1}$.

*Proof.* This is the re-marking lemma of [the odd-$p$ entry](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md), checked against Diekert's definition. Put $\varphi' = \beta \circ \varphi$ and $\psi' = \psi \circ \beta^{-1}$. The map $\varphi'$ is onto, with the same pro-$2$ kernel as $\varphi$, and $\ker\psi' = \beta(\ker\psi)$. The open normal subgroups $\mathcal H \subseteq \ker\psi'$ correspond to the open normal subgroups $\mathcal H_0 = \beta^{-1}(\mathcal H) \subseteq \ker\psi$, and $\varphi'^{-1}(\mathcal H) = \varphi^{-1}(\mathcal H_0)$. So for $(X, \varphi')$ and $\mathcal H$, the group in the definition is the group $X_{\mathcal H_0}$ for $(X, \varphi)$ and $\mathcal H_0$. Let $\bar\beta : G_0 = \Gamma/\mathcal H_0 \to G = \Gamma/\mathcal H$ be the isomorphism induced by $\beta$. An element $g \in G$ acts on the cohomology of $\tilde X_{\mathcal H_0}$, for $\varphi'$, by conjugation by any $x \in X$ with $\varphi(x) \in \bar\beta^{-1}(g)$. So it acts as $\bar\beta^{-1}(g)$ acts for $\varphi$, and likewise $\rho \in \Gamma$ acts for $\varphi'$ as $\beta^{-1}(\rho)$ acts for $\varphi$.

1. The group $\tilde X_{\mathcal H_0}$ is the same, and $|G| = |G_0|$.
2. The map $X_{\mathcal H_0} \to \mathcal H$ given by $\varphi'$ is $\varphi$ followed by the isomorphism $\beta : \mathcal H_0 \to \mathcal H$. So inflation from $H^1(\mathcal H)$ along $\varphi'$ has the same image as inflation from $H^1(\mathcal H_0)$ along $\varphi$. The orthogonal complement, the quotient and the form on it are therefore the same. The $\mathbb{F}_2[G]$-module structure is obtained from the $\mathbb{F}_2[G_0]$-module structure through the ring isomorphism $\mathbb{F}_2[G_0] \to \mathbb{F}_2[G]$ induced by $\bar\beta$, which carries free modules of rank $n$ to free modules of rank $n$. The two totally isotropic subspaces are unchanged.
3. For $\varphi'$, condition 3 concerns $\tilde X$ for $\varphi'^{-1}(\ker\psi') = \varphi^{-1}(\ker\psi)$, the same group as for $\varphi$. An isomorphism of $\Gamma$-modules from its $H^2(\cdot, \mathbb{Z}/2^s)$ to $\mathbb{Z}/2^s$ with the action through $\psi$, for $\varphi$, is the same map of groups as an isomorphism to $\mathbb{Z}/2^s$ with the action through $\psi \circ \beta^{-1} = \psi'$, for $\varphi'$, because $\rho$ acts for $\varphi'$ as $\beta^{-1}(\rho)$ does for $\varphi$. $\square$

*Proof of part 1.* Let $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$. By Step 1 of [lifting modulo wild commutators](tame-automorphisms-lift-modulo-wild-commutators.md), $\beta(I) = I$ and $\beta(\gamma) \in \gamma I$ for every $\gamma \in \Gamma$. Since $\chi_T(\tau) = 1$, $\chi_T$ is trivial on $I$, so $\chi_T \circ \beta^{-1} = \chi_T$ and $\psi \circ \beta^{-1} = \psi$. By (D2), $(G_k, \pi)$ is a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n$, $2^s$, $\psi$, and by the lemma so is $(G_k, \beta \circ \pi)$. By (D1), applied to $(X, \varphi_X) = (G_k, \pi)$ and $(Y, \varphi_Y) = (G_k, \beta \circ \pi)$, there is an automorphism $\Theta$ of $G_k$ with $\pi \circ \Theta = \beta \circ \pi$. So $\beta = \bar\Theta$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Inner automorphisms of $G_k$ induce inner automorphisms of $\Gamma$, so $\Theta \mapsto \bar\Theta$ induces $\mathrm{Out}(G_k) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma)$, and it is onto by part 1. The proof of part 3 of [the odd-$p$ entry](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md), with its $q$ equal to $2^f$, computes
$$\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma) = \{\beta_{v,a} : v \in \overline{2^{f\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}},\ a \in (2^f - 1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'\}.$$
That computation uses only the tame relation, the composition rule and the lemma in the [problem entry](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md) that every element of $\Gamma$ is $\sigma^b\tau^y$ with $b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$; that lemma is proved there for every $p$. The rest of that proof, with $d = 1$ and $S = \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$, gives the sequence: $\beta_{u,a} \mapsto u$ is a homomorphism from $\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)$ onto $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ mapping $\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)$ onto $\overline{2^{f\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}}$, and the kernel of the induced map on $\mathrm{Out}(\Gamma)$ is the image of $\{\beta_{1,a}\}$, which is $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'/(2^f - 1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' \cong \mathbb{Z}/(2^f - 1)$, as $2^f - 1$ is odd. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** The proof of part 4 of [the odd-$p$ entry](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md) applies with $p = 2$, $q = 2^f$ and $d = 1$. It defines the homomorphism $u : \mathrm{Aut}(G_k) \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ by $\bar\Theta = \beta_{u(\Theta), a}$, shows that $u$ is continuous modulo every $m$ prime to $p$, and composes it with Legendre symbols at a set $L$ of $r + 1$ odd primes not dividing $pd$, here any $r + 1$ odd primes. Those steps use only the structure of $\Gamma$, the composition rule, the formula for $\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)$ above, and the fact that automorphisms of $G_k$ induce automorphisms of $\Gamma$. The one step that uses liftability is the surjectivity of the Legendre-symbol map, for which it needs every $\beta_{u,0}$ with $u \in U_d$ to lift. Here $U_1 = \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$, and part 1 gives the lifts. So $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ maps continuously onto an $\mathbb{F}_2$-vector space of dimension at least $r$. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** Let $V'$ be the wild inertia group of $k'$.

- *Onto.* $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is totally ramified of degree $2$ ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)) and $U_f/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is unramified, so $k'/U_f$ has ramification index $2$. Finite subextensions of $T/U_f$ have odd ramification index, so $k' \not\subseteq T$, that is, $P \not\subseteq \Pi'$. As $\Pi'$ has index $2$ in $\Pi$, $\Pi'P = \Pi$, so $\pi(\Pi') = \pi(\Pi) = \Gamma_f$.
- *Kernel.* The kernel of $\pi|_{\Pi'}$ is $\Pi' \cap P$, a closed normal pro-$2$ subgroup of $\Pi'$, so it lies in $V'$, the largest one ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). Conversely, $V'$ is characteristic in $\Pi'$, being the largest closed normal pro-$2$ subgroup, and $\Pi'$ is normal in $\Pi$, so $V'$ is a closed normal pro-$2$ subgroup of $\Pi$. Hence $V' \subseteq P$, the largest one in $\Pi$, and $V' \subseteq \Pi' \cap P$. So $V' = \Pi' \cap P$.
- *Lifting.* Let $T'$ be the fixed field of $V'$, the maximal tamely ramified extension of $k'$. As $V' \subseteq P$, $T \subseteq T'$, and restriction $r : \mathrm{Gal}(T'/k') \to \Gamma_f$ satisfies $r \circ \pi' = \pi|_{\Pi'}$, where $\pi' : \Pi' \to \mathrm{Gal}(T'/k')$ is restriction. Both $\pi'$ and $\pi|_{\Pi'}$ are onto with kernel $V'$, so $r$ is an isomorphism. Since $\sqrt{-1} \in k'$, part 1 applies to $k'$. Given $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma_f)$, it gives $\Phi' \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Pi')$ with $\pi' \circ \Phi' = (r^{-1}\beta r) \circ \pi'$, and then $\pi \circ \Phi' = r\pi'\Phi' = \beta r \pi' = \beta \circ \pi$ on $\Pi'$. $\square$

**Proof of part 5.** Let $\rho : \mathrm{Aut}(\Pi') \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma_f)$ send $\Phi'$ to the automorphism induced through $\pi|_{\Pi'}$, which exists because its kernel $V'$ is characteristic in $\Pi'$. For $x \in \Pi$ and $g \in \Pi'$, $\pi(c_x(g)) = \pi(x)\pi(g)\pi(x)^{-1}$, so $\rho(c_x)$ is conjugation by $\pi(x)$. Three facts are used.

- *(a) $\Pi$ is slim*: every open subgroup of $\Pi$ has trivial centralizer in $\Pi$. This is [@mochizuki2012, Theorem 1.7 (ii), p. 14], with the definition at [@mochizuki2012, §0, p. 8].
- *(b) Every automorphism of $\Pi$ maps $\Pi'$ onto itself.* $\Pi'$ is the kernel of the action of $\Pi$ on $\{\pm 1, \pm\sqrt{-1}\}$, that is, of the cyclotomic character modulo $4$, and every automorphism of $\Pi$ preserves the cyclotomic character ([invariants of $G_K$](local-field-galois-invariants.md)).
- *(c) $\Gamma_f$ has trivial centre.* By the lemma in the [problem entry](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md), the centralizer of $\tau$ is $I$. If $\tau^a$ with $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ commutes with $\sigma_f$, then $\tau^{2^f a} = \tau^a$, so $(2^f - 1)a = 0$ in the torsion-free group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and $a = 0$.

*The class $\iota$.* Fix $s_0 \in \Pi \setminus \Pi'$. Then $c_{s_0}^2 = c_{s_0^2}$ with $s_0^2 \in \Pi'$, so $\iota^2 = 1$. If $c_{s_0} = c_h$ with $h \in \Pi'$, then $h^{-1}s_0$ centralizes $\Pi'$, so $s_0 = h$ by (a), which is false. So $\iota$ has order $2$. As $\rho(c_{s_0})$ is inner, $\iota$ maps to $1$ in $\mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_f)$. For $\Phi' \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Pi')$ and $y \in \Pi'$, $\Phi' c_y \Phi'^{-1} = c_{\Phi'(y)}$, so $\rho$ induces $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi') \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_f)$.

*One inclusion.* Let $\Phi \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)$. By (b), $\Phi' = \Phi|_{\Pi'}$ is an automorphism of $\Pi'$, and $\rho(\Phi') = \bar\Phi$. Moreover $\Phi' c_{s_0} \Phi'^{-1} = c_{\Phi(s_0)}$, and $\Phi(s_0) \in \Pi \setminus \Pi' = s_0\Pi'$, so the class of $\Phi'$ commutes with $\iota$.

*The other inclusion.* Let $\Phi' \in \mathrm{Aut}(\Pi')$ have class commuting with $\iota$, and put $\beta = \rho(\Phi')$. Then $\Phi' c_{s_0}\Phi'^{-1} = c_{s_0}c_h = c_{s_1}$ for some $h \in \Pi'$, with $s_1 = s_0h \in \Pi \setminus \Pi'$. Squaring gives $c_{\Phi'(s_0^2)} = \Phi' c_{s_0}^2 \Phi'^{-1} = c_{s_1^2}$, so $s_1^2 = \Phi'(s_0^2)$ by (a). Define $\Phi : \Pi \to \Pi$ by $\Phi(g) = \Phi'(g)$ and $\Phi(gs_0) = \Phi'(g)s_1$ for $g \in \Pi'$. For $g, h \in \Pi'$, using $s_0h = c_{s_0}(h)s_0$ and $\Phi'(c_{s_0}(h)) = c_{s_1}(\Phi'(h))$:
$$\Phi(gs_0 \cdot h) = \Phi'(g\,c_{s_0}(h))\,s_1 = \Phi'(g)\,s_1\Phi'(h)s_1^{-1}\,s_1 = \Phi(gs_0)\Phi(h),$$
$$\Phi(gs_0 \cdot hs_0) = \Phi'(g\,c_{s_0}(h)\,s_0^2) = \Phi'(g)\,s_1\Phi'(h)s_1^{-1}\,s_1^2 = \Phi(gs_0)\Phi(hs_0),$$
and $\Phi(g \cdot h) = \Phi(g)\Phi(h)$, $\Phi(g \cdot hs_0) = \Phi(g)\Phi(hs_0)$ are clear. So $\Phi$ is a homomorphism. It maps $\Pi'$ onto $\Pi'$ and $s_0\Pi'$ onto $s_1\Pi' = \Pi \setminus \Pi'$ bijectively, and it is continuous because $\Pi'$ is open, so it is an automorphism of the profinite group $\Pi$. On $\Pi'$, $\pi \circ \Phi = \beta \circ \pi$. Applying $\rho$ to $\Phi' c_{s_0} \Phi'^{-1} = c_{s_1}$ gives that conjugation by $\beta(\pi(s_0))$ and by $\pi(s_1)$ agree on $\Gamma_f$, so $\pi(s_1) = \beta(\pi(s_0))$ by (c). Hence $\pi \circ \Phi = \beta \circ \pi$ on all of $\Pi$, and $\bar\Phi = \beta$.

So the two images in $\mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_f)$ agree. Finally, the automorphisms of $\Gamma_f$ that lift to $\Pi$ form a subgroup containing the inner ones, since $\pi$ is onto, so $\alpha$ lifts to $\Pi$ exactly when its class lies in the image of $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi)$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Comparison with odd $p$.* For odd $p$, an automorphism of the tame group lifts exactly when it preserves the mod-$p$ cyclotomic character ([the odd-$p$ entry](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md)), and every one lifts when $\mu_p \subset k$. Here the corresponding condition, preserving $\psi$, holds for every automorphism, because $\psi$ is trivial on tame inertia once $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is unramified.
- *What is left for the Frobenius shift.* When $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is ramified, as for $U_f$, Schneider writes that there is no sensible notion of a Demuškin formation (see [generators and relations](local-galois-group-presentations.md)), so (D1) and (D2) are not available for $G_{U_f}$ itself. Part 5 moves the problem to $G_{U_f(\sqrt{-1})}$, where they are, at the cost of an equivariance condition for the group $\mathrm{Gal}(k'/U_f)$ of order $2$. The splitting arguments used for odd $p$ in [prime-to-$p$ abelian subgroups](prime-to-p-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) need finite groups of order prime to $p$, and this group has order $p = 2$.
