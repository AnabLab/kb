**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$. Use the Setting and notation of [finite normal subgroups in degree two](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups-have-order-prime-to-p-in-degree-two.md), called the order-$p$ entry below. So $G=G_K=F/(\varrho)$ with $F=P\rtimes\mathcal G$, wild inertia $P_K$ is the image of $P$, $\mathcal G$ is identified with $\Gamma=G/P_K$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, $\omega:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ is the action on $\mu_p$, $I\subseteq\Gamma$ is tame inertia, topologically generated by $\tau$, $q$ is the order of the residue field, and
$$\varrho=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa\,x_1^{p^s}\,[x_1,x_2],\qquad [x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}.$$
Write $V=P_K/P_K^{(2)}$, which the order-$p$ entry calls $\bar V$. By part 1 of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md),
$$V=L\oplus\Lambda\bar x_1\oplus\Lambda\bar x_2,\qquad L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0,\qquad \lambda\bar x_0=\omega(\lambda)\bar x_0,$$
with $\Lambda\bar x_1,\Lambda\bar x_2$ free of rank one. For $\rho\in\Gamma$, $\rho\bar x_j$ is the class of $\rho x_j\rho^{-1}$. An automorphism of $G$ is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$. It then preserves $P_K$, which is characteristic ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), and acts $\Lambda$-linearly on $V$.

*Lower central series.* $P_K$ is free pro-$p$ of infinite rank ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). Let $(P_K)_1=P_K$ and $(P_K)_{j+1}=[P_K,(P_K)_j]$ (closed subgroups), $H=P_K/(P_K)_2$ and $\mathcal L_j=(P_K)_j/(P_K)_{j+1}$, written additively, with the bracket $\mathcal L_1\times\mathcal L_1\to\mathcal L_2$ induced by the commutator. Then $H/pH=V$. The lower central input of [the boundary-twist theorem](boundary-twists-exclude-finite-outer-conjugacy-of-wild-scalars.md) gives: $H$ and $\mathcal L_2$ are torsion-free; $\mathcal L_2/p$ is the degree-two completed free Lie space $\mathcal L_2(V)$ on $V$, functorially for continuous linear maps, so that a continuous linear surjection $\pi:V\to W'$ onto a finite-dimensional space induces $\mathcal L_2(\pi):\mathcal L_2(V)\to\mathcal L_2(W')\subseteq W'\otimes W'$ with $[v,v']\mapsto\pi v\otimes\pi v'-\pi v'\otimes\pi v$; and $v\mapsto[v,\cdot\,]$ is injective from $V$ to $\mathrm{Hom}(V,\mathcal L_2(V))$. The action of an automorphism on $\mathcal L_2(V)$ is $\mathcal L_2$ of its action on $V$, since both preserve brackets.

*Degree-one Johnson maps.* For a continuous automorphism $\theta$ of $P_K$ acting trivially on $H$, let $J_1(\theta):H\to\mathcal L_2$ send $x(P_K)_2$ to $\theta(x)x^{-1}(P_K)_3$. Step 2 of the proof in [the shear-vanishing theorem](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-wild-frattini.md) shows, with no condition on the degree, that $J_1(\theta)$ is a continuous homomorphism, that $J_1(\theta\theta')=J_1(\theta)+J_1(\theta')$, that $J_1(\beta\theta\beta^{-1})=\beta_2J_1(\theta)\beta_H^{-1}$ for every automorphism $\beta$ of $P_K$ with actions $\beta_H$ on $H$ and $\beta_2$ on $\mathcal L_2$, and that $J_1(c_g)=[g(P_K)_2,\cdot\,]$ for conjugation $c_g$ by $g\in P_K$.

*The complementary twist.* Put $w=x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2]\in P$, the final segment of $\varrho$. Let $T$ be the endomorphism of $F$ that is the identity on $\mathcal G$, fixes $x_0$, and maps $x_j\mapsto wx_jw^{-1}$ for $j=1,2$. It fixes the closed subgroup generated by $\mathcal G$ and $x_0$, which contains $(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa$, and
$$T(w)=wx_1^{p^s}w^{-1}\cdot w[x_1,x_2]w^{-1}=w,$$
so $T(\varrho)=\varrho$. The endomorphism $T^-$ defined in the same way with $w^{-1}$ in place of $w$ also fixes $w$, so $TT^-$ and $T^-T$ fix every generator and are the identity by the universal property of $F$. Hence $T$ induces an automorphism of $G$, again written $T$, which is tame-trivial. This is the twist $T'$ of step 2 of the shear-vanishing proof, written there for even degree at least four, and the twist proposed in [research issue 624](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/624).

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$. Let $\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ be tame-trivial with finitely many conjugates in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. Then $\alpha$ acts trivially on $V=P_K/P_K^{(2)}$. Consequently, if $\alpha$ has finite order, then $\alpha=1$; in particular, if its order is finite and prime to $p$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=2$.

1. The only element of finite order of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ with finitely many conjugates is $1$.
2. Every finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ is trivial.
3. Let $N\ne G_K$ be a closed normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ isomorphic to $G_K$. Then $N\cap G_K$ has infinite index in both $G_K$ and $N$, and is not topologically finitely generated.

**Proof of the Theorem.** Let $A$ be the action of $\alpha$ on $V$, and $A_H$, $A_2$ its actions on $H$, $\mathcal L_2$.

*Step 0: the shape of $A$.* By Step 1 of the proof in [the degree-two tame-quotient theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-on-the-tame-quotient-in-degree-two.md), $A$ maps $L$ onto itself, so $A\bar x_0=\varepsilon\bar x_0$ with $\varepsilon\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. Step 2 of the proof in the order-$p$ entry uses only that $\alpha$ is tame-trivial and that its class has finitely many conjugates. It gives $a\in\Lambda^\times$ and $c_1,c_2\in\mathbb F_p$ with
$$A\bar x_j=c_j\bar x_0+a\bar x_j,\qquad A(\lambda\bar x_j)=\omega(\lambda)c_j\bar x_0+\lambda a\bar x_j\qquad(j=1,2,\ \lambda\in\Lambda).$$
So $A$ maps $L+\Lambda\bar x_1$ into itself, and onto it, since $A(\lambda a^{-1}\bar x_1)\in\lambda\bar x_1+L$. Hence $A^{-1}(L+\Lambda\bar x_1)=L+\Lambda\bar x_1$. As the tame twist of $\alpha$ is the identity, the twisted commutation (T) of Step 3 of the degree-two tame-quotient proof reads, for some $b\ge0$,
$$a\,z^{p^b}=z^{p^b}a\qquad(z\in\Lambda).\tag{T}$$

*Step 1: the Johnson identity.* The twist $T$ acts trivially on $H$: it commutes with conjugation by $\mathcal G$, fixes $x_0$, and $T(x_j)x_j^{-1}=[w,x_j]\in(P_K)_2$; and $H$ is topologically generated as a $\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$-module by the classes of $x_0,x_1,x_2$, as $P_K$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by them. Put $J=J_1(T)$. With $z=[x_1,x_2]\in(P_K)_2$ and $[yz,x]=y[z,x]y^{-1}[y,x]$,
$$[w,x_1]=x_1^{p^s}[z,x_1]x_1^{-p^s}\in(P_K)_3,\qquad [w,x_2]=x_1^{p^s}[z,x_2]x_1^{-p^s}[x_1^{p^s},x_2]\equiv[x_1^{p^s},x_2]\pmod{(P_K)_3}.$$
So $J(\bar x_0)=J(\bar x_1)=0$ and $J(\bar x_2)=p^s[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]$, writing $\bar x$ also for classes in $H$. Since $T$ fixes $\mathcal G$, the conjugation rule makes $J$ equivariant for $\Gamma$. So $J$ maps a set of topological generators of $H$ into the closed subgroup $p^s\mathcal L_2$, and $J(H)\subseteq p^s\mathcal L_2$. Multiplication by $p^s$ is a continuous bijection from the torsion-free compact group $\mathcal L_2$ onto $p^s\mathcal L_2$, hence a homeomorphism. So $J=p^sj$ for a continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant homomorphism $j:H\to\mathcal L_2$ with
$$j(\bar x_0)=j(\bar x_1)=0,\qquad j(\bar x_2)=[\bar x_1,\bar x_2].$$

The conjugates $[T]^k[\alpha][T]^{-k}$, $k\ge0$, of the class of $\alpha$ are not all distinct, so some $n>0$ has $[T]^n$ commuting with $[\alpha]$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G)$. Thus $\alpha T^n\alpha^{-1}T^{-n}=c_g$ for some $g\in G$. The left side is tame-trivial, so the image of $g$ in $\Gamma$ is central, hence trivial, as $\Gamma$ has trivial centre (part 3 of the units entry). So $g\in P_K$. Both sides act trivially on $H$, and the rules for $J_1$ give
$$np^s\big(A_2\,j\,A_H^{-1}-j\big)=[\bar g,\cdot\,],\qquad \bar g=g(P_K)_2\in H.$$
Write $np^s=p^ku$ with $k\ge1$ and $u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$, so $[\bar g,H]\subseteq p^k\mathcal L_2$. Suppose $\bar g=p^ig_i$ with $g_i\in H$ and $0\le i<k$. Then $p^i[g_i,H]\subseteq p^k\mathcal L_2$, so $[g_i,H]\subseteq p^{k-i}\mathcal L_2\subseteq p\mathcal L_2$ by torsion-freeness. Hence the image of $g_i$ in $V$ brackets every element of $V$ to $0$ in $\mathcal L_2(V)$, so it is $0$ by injectivity, and $g_i\in pH$. By induction $\bar g=p^kg_k$ with $g_k\in H$. As $\mathrm{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ is torsion-free, $A_2jA_H^{-1}-j=[u^{-1}g_k,\cdot\,]$. Reduce modulo $p$. Let $\bar\jmath:V\to\mathcal L_2(V)$ be the reduction of $j$ and $h\in V$ that of $u^{-1}g_k$. Then
$$\mathcal L_2(A)\,\bar\jmath\,A^{-1}-\bar\jmath=[h,\cdot\,].\tag{1}$$
Here $\bar\jmath$ is continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant, kills $L+\Lambda\bar x_1$, and $\bar\jmath(\rho\bar x_2)=[\rho\bar x_1,\rho\bar x_2]$ for $\rho\in\Gamma$.

*Step 2: $a$ is a group element.* Let $U$ be an open normal subgroup of $\Gamma$ with $\tau\notin U$. Write $\Gamma_U=\Gamma/U$ and $\lambda\mapsto\bar\lambda$ for $\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p[\Gamma_U]$. Let $W_U=\mathbb F_p[\Gamma_U]e_1\oplus\mathbb F_p[\Gamma_U]e_2$ be free, with basis $\{te_1,te_2:t\in\Gamma_U\}$, and let $\pi_U:V\to W_U$ kill $L$ and send $\lambda\bar x_j$ to $\bar\lambda e_j$. It is a continuous linear surjection. Let $A_U:W_U\to W_U$ map $\mu e_j\mapsto\mu\bar ae_j$. By Step 0, $\pi_UA=A_U\pi_U$, so $\mathcal L_2(\pi_U)\mathcal L_2(A)=\mathcal L_2(A_U)\mathcal L_2(\pi_U)$. Put $\delta_U=\mathcal L_2(\pi_U)\circ\bar\jmath$ and $h_U=\pi_U(h)$. Applying $\mathcal L_2(\pi_U)$ to (1) gives, for every $v\in V$,
$$\mathcal L_2(A_U)\big(\delta_U(A^{-1}v)\big)-\delta_U(v)=h_U\otimes\pi_Uv-\pi_Uv\otimes h_U.\tag{2}$$

Take $v=\bar x_1$ and $v=\tau\bar x_1$. Then $v$ and $A^{-1}v$ lie in $L+\Lambda\bar x_1$, which $\bar\jmath$ kills. So $h_U$ commutes in $W_U\otimes W_U$ with $e_1$ and with $\bar\tau e_1$, which are distinct basis vectors as $\tau\notin U$. If $h_U\otimes e_1=e_1\otimes h_U$, then for each basis vector $f\ne e_1$ the coefficient of $f\otimes e_1$ gives that the $f$-coordinate of $h_U$ is $0$, so $h_U\in\mathbb F_pe_1$. Likewise $h_U\in\mathbb F_p\bar\tau e_1$. Hence $h_U=0$.

Now take $v=A\bar x_2$ in (2). The left side becomes $\mathcal L_2(A_U)(\delta_U(\bar x_2))-\delta_U(A\bar x_2)$, and $\delta_U(\bar x_2)$ is the image of $[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]$, so
$$[\bar ae_1,\bar ae_2]=\delta_U(A\bar x_2)=\delta_U(a\bar x_2),$$
as $A\bar x_2=c_2\bar x_0+a\bar x_2$ and $\bar\jmath(L)=0$. Here $[y,y']=y\otimes y'-y'\otimes y$. The maps $\lambda\mapsto\delta_U(\lambda\bar x_2)$ and $\lambda\mapsto\sum_t\lambda_t[te_1,te_2]$, where $\bar\lambda=\sum_{t\in\Gamma_U}\lambda_tt$, are continuous and linear, and agree at every $\rho\in\Gamma$, whose span is dense in $\Lambda$. So they agree. With $\bar a=\sum_ta_tt$ this gives
$$\sum_{t,t'\in\Gamma_U}a_ta_{t'}\,[te_1,t'e_2]=\sum_{t\in\Gamma_U}a_t\,[te_1,te_2].$$
The tensor $te_1\otimes t'e_2$ occurs only in $[te_1,t'e_2]$, with coefficient $1$. Comparing its coefficients gives $a_ta_{t'}=0$ for $t\ne t'$ and $a_t^2=a_t$. As $\bar a$ is a unit, it is nonzero, so $\bar a=t_U$ for a unique $t_U\in\Gamma_U$.

The open normal subgroups not containing $\tau$ are cofinal, as $\tau\ne1$. For $U'\subseteq U$ in this family, $t_{U'}$ maps to $t_U$, because distinct elements of $\Gamma_U$ are linearly independent in $\mathbb F_p[\Gamma_U]$. So the $t_U$ define $\gamma\in\Gamma=\varprojlim\Gamma/U$ with $\bar\gamma=\bar a$ for cofinally many $U$, and $a=\gamma$ in $\Lambda$.

*Step 3: $a=1$.* By (T) with $z=\tau$ and with $z=\sigma$, the element $\gamma$ commutes in $\Lambda$ with $\tau^{p^b}$ and $\sigma^{p^b}$. Distinct elements of $\Gamma$ stay distinct in $\Lambda$, so $\gamma$ commutes with them in $\Gamma$. As $I\cong\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$ with $\tau\mapsto1$ and $p^b$ is a unit there, $\tau^{p^b}$ topologically generates $I$, so $\gamma$ centralizes $\tau$. Write $\gamma=\tau^x\sigma^y$ with $x\in\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$ and $y\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, as in the proof of part 3 of the units entry. Since $I$ is abelian, $\tau=\gamma\tau\gamma^{-1}=\sigma^y\tau\sigma^{-y}=\tau^{q^y}$, so $q^y=1$ in $\big(\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell\big)^\times$. For $M\ge1$, $N_M=q^M-1$ is prime to $p$, and $q$ has order exactly $M$ modulo $N_M$. So the continuous homomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb Z}\to(\mathbb Z/N_M)^\times$, $y\mapsto q^y\bmod N_M$, has kernel $M\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. Hence $y\in\bigcap_MM\widehat{\mathbb Z}=0$. Then $\gamma=\tau^x$, and $\tau^x=\sigma^{p^b}\tau^x\sigma^{-p^b}=\tau^{q^{p^b}x}$ gives $(q^{p^b}-1)x=0$. So $x=0$, as $\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$ is torsion-free. Thus $\gamma=1$ and $a=1$.

*Step 4: the cyclotomic line.* By Steps 0 and 3, $\alpha$ acts on $W=V/L$ as the identity, that is, as multiplication by the central unit $z=1$, and on $L$ by $\varepsilon$. The Theorem of [unitarity of central scalars](unitarity-of-central-scalars-on-wild-frattini.md), which has no condition on the degree, gives $\varepsilon=\omega(1)=1$. So $A=1+E$, where $E(\bar x_0)=0$ and $E(\lambda\bar x_j)=\omega(\lambda)c_j\bar x_0$. Thus $E(V)\subseteq L$, $E(L)=0$ and $A^{-1}=1-E$.

*Step 5: the shear.* Let $\pi:V\to\mathbb F_p^3$ send $\bar x_0\mapsto X_0$ and $\lambda\bar x_j\mapsto\mathrm{aug}(\lambda)X_j$ for $j=1,2$, as in the coordinates of the Setting of the shear-vanishing theorem. It induces $\mathcal L_2(\pi)$ into the tensor square of $\mathbb F_p^3$, where $[X_0,X_1]$, $[X_0,X_2]$, $[X_1,X_2]$ are linearly independent. Write $\pi(h)=r_0X_0+r_1X_1+r_2X_2$. Evaluate (1) at $\bar x_1$: as $A^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-c_1\bar x_0$ is killed by $\bar\jmath$, $[h,\bar x_1]=0$. Applying $\mathcal L_2(\pi)$ gives $r_0[X_0,X_1]-r_2[X_1,X_2]=0$, so $r_0=r_2=0$. Evaluate (1) at $\bar x_2$: $\bar\jmath(A^{-1}\bar x_2)=\bar\jmath(\bar x_2-c_2\bar x_0)=[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]$, so
$$[h,\bar x_2]=[\bar x_1+c_1\bar x_0,\bar x_2+c_2\bar x_0]-[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]=c_1[\bar x_0,\bar x_2]+c_2[\bar x_1,\bar x_0].$$
Applying $\mathcal L_2(\pi)$ gives $r_1[X_1,X_2]=c_1[X_0,X_2]-c_2[X_0,X_1]$, so $c_1=c_2=0$. Hence $E=0$ and $A=1$.

*Consequence.* If $\alpha$ has finite order, part 2 of [finite-order detection](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-on-the-frattini-quotient-of-wild-inertia.md) gives $\alpha=1$. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** (1) Let $x\in\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ have finite order $p^kr$ with $p\nmid r$ and finitely many conjugates. The conjugates of $y=x^{p^k}$ are the $p^k$-th powers of those of $x$, so there are finitely many, and $y$ has order $r$. By Corollary (b) of the degree-two tame-quotient theorem, $y$ has a tame-trivial representative $\alpha$ of order dividing $r$. By the Theorem, $\alpha=1$, so $y=1$ and $x$ has order $p^k$. By part 1 of the Corollary of the order-$p$ entry, $x=1$.

(2) Every element of a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has finite order, and all its conjugates lie in that subgroup. So (1) applies.

(3) By part 2 of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), either $N\cap G_K$ is as stated, or $NG_K/G_K$ is a nontrivial finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. The second is excluded by (2). $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* In degree $2$ there is no surface pair, so neither the boundary twists of [the boundary-twist theorem](boundary-twists-exclude-finite-outer-conjugacy-of-wild-scalars.md) nor its central-scalar input is available. The single twist $T$ along $x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2]$ does both jobs. Its Johnson identity vanishes on the free summand $\Lambda\bar x_1$, and two distinct translates $e_1$, $\bar\tau e_1$ there kill the inner term without a spare generator. At $\bar x_2$ the identity says that $a$ is group-like, as proposed in research issue 624. There the finite order $m$ of $\alpha$ was to finish, since $a^m=1$ in the torsion-free group $\Gamma$. Step 3 instead uses (T), so the Theorem needs no finite-order hypothesis. Once $A=1+E$, the shear computation is that of step 2 of the shear-vanishing proof.
- *A second proof in the order-$p$ case.* For $\theta$ of $p$-power order with finitely many outer conjugates, Step 1 of the order-$p$ entry makes $\alpha=\theta^{p^{a-1}}$ tame-trivial of order $p$. The Theorem then gives $\alpha=1$, without the character argument of Step 4 there.
- *Scope.* Odd $p$ and every quadratic $K/\mathbb Q_p$, ramified or unramified, with every $s$. This includes $K=\mathbb Q_3(\mu_3)$. With the corollary of [finite outer conjugacy on the tame quotient](frobenius-shifts-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates-are-inner-on-the-tame-quotient.md) for degree at least $3$, every finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ is trivial for every odd $p$ and every $K$ of degree at least $2$.
- *What remains.* Not decided here: classes of infinite order with finitely many conjugates; whether a tame-trivial automorphism with finite outer conjugacy that acts trivially on $V$ must be inner, which in degree at least $3$ is also open; the integral analogue on $P_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, proved for degree at least $3$ in [the abelianized wild inertia entry](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), whose proof was not checked for degree $2$; rival copies of type (b); $K=\mathbb Q_p$, where there is no second complementary generator; and $p=2$.
- *Sources.* Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
