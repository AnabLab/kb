**Setting.** A finite group $Q$ is *supersolvable* if it has a chain $1 = Q_0 \subseteq Q_1 \subseteq \dots \subseteq Q_m = Q$ of normal subgroups of $Q$ with cyclic quotients $Q_{i+1}/Q_i$. Subgroups, quotients and finite direct products of supersolvable groups are supersolvable; this standard fact is not checked in a source here. For a profinite group $G$, let $G^{\mathrm{ss}}$ be its quotient by the intersection of the open normal subgroups $U$ with $G/U$ supersolvable. This is the maximal prosupersolvable quotient of $G$.

Fix an algebraic closure $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. For a number field $K \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, let $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ be the fixed field of the kernel of $G_K \to G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$. So $G_K^{\mathrm{ss}} = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ss}}/K)$, and a finite Galois extension $M/K$ lies in $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ exactly when $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)$ is supersolvable. A finite abelian group is supersolvable, since a chain of subgroups with steps of prime index is a chain of normal subgroups with cyclic quotients. Hence $K^{\mathrm{ab}} \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ss}}$.

For a prime $\ell$, let $E_\ell(K)$ be the maximal abelian extension of $K$ of exponent dividing $\ell - 1$, so $E_2(K) = K$. Let $\Omega_\ell(K)$ be the maximal pro-$\ell$ extension of $E_\ell(K)$. Since $(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^\times$ has exponent $\ell - 1$, $\mu_\ell \subset E_\ell(K)$.

Karshon and Shusterman call an algebraic extension $\Omega/\mathbb{Q}$ *$\ell$-sealed with respect to* a finite set $S$ of rational places if $\mu_\ell \subset \Omega$ and no $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$-extension of $\Omega$ has every place of $S(\Omega)$ split completely [@karshonshusterman2026, Definition 3.1, p. 3]. They call $\Omega$ *abundant* if, for all rational primes $p$ outside a set of density zero, every completion $\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}$ at a place over $p$ contains $\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{tame}}$ or the $\mathbb{Z}_p^2$-extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@karshonshusterman2026, Definition 6.1, p. 11]. Their main theorem says that if $\Omega_i/K_i$ are Galois, $\ell$-sealed and abundant, then every isomorphism $G_{\Omega_1/K_1} \to G_{\Omega_2/K_2}$ is induced by a unique isomorphism of field extensions $\Omega_1/K_1 \to \Omega_2/K_2$ [@karshonshusterman2026, Theorem 9.4, p. 19]. They suggest the prosupersolvable variant as a direction for future research, noting that the maximal pro-$\ell$ extension of $K(\mu_\ell)$ is prosupersolvable over $K$ [@karshonshusterman2026, §1, p. 2].

**Theorem.** This is proved here by combining the cited results. Let $K_1, K_2$ be number fields and $\alpha : G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}} \to G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{ss}}$ an isomorphism of profinite groups.

1. For every prime $\ell$, $\alpha$ maps $\mathrm{Gal}(K_1^{\mathrm{ss}}/\Omega_\ell(K_1))$ onto $\mathrm{Gal}(K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}/\Omega_\ell(K_2))$. So it induces an isomorphism $\alpha_\ell : \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K_1)/K_1) \to \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K_2)/K_2)$.
2. Each $\alpha_\ell$ is induced by a unique isomorphism of field extensions $\sigma_\ell : \Omega_\ell(K_1)/K_1 \to \Omega_\ell(K_2)/K_2$. In particular $K_1 \cong K_2$.
3. The restrictions $\sigma_\ell|_{K_1} : K_1 \to K_2$ are the same for all $\ell$.

So the answer to the first question of [the prosupersolvable problem](prosupersolvable-neukirch-uchida-problem.md) is yes.

**Why Theorem 9.4 does not apply directly.** This is proved here. Let $\Omega \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ be Galois over $K$ and abundant. Then, for every prime $\ell$, $\Omega$ is not $\ell$-sealed with respect to any finite $S$. This is clear by definition if $\mu_\ell \not\subset \Omega$, so assume $\mu_\ell \subset \Omega$.

- *A Galois layer with a prime $p \nmid \ell(\ell-1)$.* Choose $p \nmid \ell(\ell - 1)$ outside the exceptional set of density zero, and a place $\mathfrak{p}$ of $K$ over $p$. Both $\mathbb{Q}_p^{\mathrm{tame}}$, which contains the unramified extension with group $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, and the $\mathbb{Z}_p^2$-extension have degree divisible by $p^\infty$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. So $p$ divides $[\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}} : K_{\mathfrak{p}}]$ for every $\mathfrak{P}$ over $\mathfrak{p}$, and hence $[\Omega : K]$ [@karshonshusterman2026, Lemma 5.5, p. 8]. Take a finite Galois $L/K$ inside $\Omega$ with $\mu_\ell \subset L$ and $p \mid [L:K]$, put $G = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$, and let $h \in G$ have order $p$.
- *A free Kummer module.* By Chebotarev, there is a prime $\mathfrak{Q}$ of $L$, not over $S$, whose restriction to $K$ splits completely in $L$, so the primes $g\mathfrak{Q}$ for $g \in G$ are distinct. Approximation of $\ell$-th power classes at the places of $L$ over $S$ and over the prime below $\mathfrak{Q}$ [@karshonshusterman2026, Proposition 3.2, p. 3] gives $a \in L^\times$ that is an $\ell$-th power at every place of $L$ over $S$, with $v_{\mathfrak{Q}}(a) \equiv 1$ and $v_{g\mathfrak{Q}}(a) \equiv 0 \pmod \ell$ for $g \ne 1$. For $x = \sum_g c_g g \in \mathbb{F}_\ell[G]$ put $x \cdot a = \prod_g g(a)^{c_g}$. Then $v_{g\mathfrak{Q}}(x \cdot a) \equiv c_g \pmod \ell$, so $x \mapsto x \cdot a$ embeds $\mathbb{F}_\ell[G]$ into $L^\times/L^{\times\ell}$. Call its image $V$.
- *The Kummer extension is not supersolvable.* Let $N = L(b^{1/\ell} : b \in V)$, which is Galois over $K$. By Kummer theory $A = \mathrm{Gal}(N/L) \cong \mathrm{Hom}(V, \mu_\ell)$, compatibly with the action of $G$. As $V$ is free over $\mathbb{F}_\ell[\langle h \rangle]$, so is $A$, so $h$ has a primitive $p$-th root of unity as an eigenvalue on $A$. It does not lie in $\mathbb{F}_\ell$, because $p \nmid \ell - 1$. If $\mathrm{Gal}(N/K)$ had a chain $(H_i)$ as in the definition, the groups $A \cap H_i$ would be $h$-stable subspaces of $A$, with each quotient $(A \cap H_{i+1})/(A \cap H_i)$ cyclic and of exponent $\ell$, so of dimension at most $1$. Then $h$ would be triangular over $\mathbb{F}_\ell$, a contradiction. So $N \not\subseteq K^{\mathrm{ss}}$.
- *Conclusion.* If $a$ were an $\ell$-th power in $\Omega$, then, as $\Omega$ is Galois over $K$ and contains $\mu_\ell$, it would contain every $\ell$-th root of every $g(a)$, hence $N$. So $\Omega(a^{1/\ell})$ is a $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$-extension of $\Omega$, and every place of $S(\Omega)$ splits completely in it, since $a$ is an $\ell$-th power there.

Karshon and Shusterman use abundance only to show that their bijection of places preserves residue characteristics. The proof below replaces it by comparing the quotients $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K)/K)$ for all primes $\ell$.

**Proof.**

*Step 1: part 1.* For a profinite group $G$, let $H_\ell(G)$ be the closed subgroup generated by commutators and $(\ell-1)$-th powers. Let $N_\ell(G)$ be the intersection of the open normal subgroups $U$ of $H_\ell(G)$ with $H_\ell(G)/U$ an $\ell$-group. Both are defined from the group alone, so $\alpha$ maps $N_\ell(G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}})$ onto $N_\ell(G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{ss}})$. Put $G = G_K^{\mathrm{ss}}$.

- *Its fixed field is $\Omega_\ell(K) \cap K^{\mathrm{ss}}$.* $G/H_\ell(G)$ is the maximal abelian quotient of exponent dividing $\ell - 1$. Since $K^{\mathrm{ab}} \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ss}}$, the fixed field of $H_\ell(G)$ is $E_\ell(K)$. The fixed field of $N_\ell(G)$ is then the compositum of the finite Galois extensions of $E_\ell(K)$ inside $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ whose groups are $\ell$-groups.
- *$\Omega_\ell(K) \subseteq K^{\mathrm{ss}}$.* $\Omega_\ell(K)$ is Galois over $K$, because $E_\ell(K)$ is and its maximal pro-$\ell$ extension is canonical. Let $M \subseteq \Omega_\ell(K)$ be finite Galois over $K$, put $Q = \mathrm{Gal}(M/K)$, and let $P = \mathrm{Gal}(M/M \cap E_\ell(K))$. Then $P \cong \mathrm{Gal}(M E_\ell(K)/E_\ell(K))$ is a normal $\ell$-subgroup, and $Q/P$ is abelian of exponent $e \mid \ell - 1$. We show by induction on $|P|$ that $Q$ is supersolvable. If $P = 1$, $Q$ is abelian. Otherwise $W = \{x \in Z(P) : x^\ell = 1\}$ is a nontrivial normal subgroup of $Q$, and an $\mathbb{F}_\ell$-vector space on which $Q$ acts through $Q/P$. As $X^e - 1$ has $e$ distinct roots in $\mathbb{F}_\ell$, the commuting operators from $Q/P$ are simultaneously diagonalizable over $\mathbb{F}_\ell$. So $W$ contains a normal subgroup $W_1$ of $Q$ of order $\ell$. By induction $Q/W_1$ has a chain as in the definition, and its inverse image, with $1 \subseteq W_1$ added, is one for $Q$.

So $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ss}}/\Omega_\ell(K)) = N_\ell(G)$, which proves part 1.

*Step 2: $\Omega_\ell(K)$ and its local fields.* Write $\Omega = \Omega_\ell(K)$ and $E = E_\ell(K)$.

- *$\Omega$ is $\ell$-sealed with respect to $S = \emptyset$.* Let $\Omega'/\Omega$ be cyclic of degree $\ell$, and $\Omega''$ its Galois closure over $E$. Since $\Omega/E$ is Galois, $\Omega''$ is a compositum of cyclic extensions of $\Omega$ of degree $\ell$, so $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega''/E)$ is pro-$\ell$ and $\Omega'' \subseteq \Omega$. So $\Omega$ has no $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$-extension, and it contains $\mu_\ell$. This makes it $\ell$-sealed with respect to $\emptyset$ [@karshonshusterman2026, Example 3.4 (1), p. 4]. Hence $\mu_\ell \subset \Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}$ and $\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}^\times = \Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}^{\times\ell}$ for every place $\mathfrak{P}$ of $\Omega$ [@karshonshusterman2026, Lemma 3.5, p. 4].

Let $\mathfrak{P}$ be a finite place of $\Omega$ over the place $\mathfrak{p}$ of $K$. As in the source, $\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}$ and $E_{\mathfrak{P}}$ denote unions of completions of finite subextensions, and the decomposition group $D_{\mathfrak{P}} \subseteq \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/K)$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}/K_{\mathfrak{p}})$ [@karshonshusterman2026, §2, p. 2].

- *$E_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is finite over $K_{\mathfrak{p}}$, of degree prime to $\ell$.* Put $m = \ell - 1$. $E_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is a union of finite abelian extensions $L$ of $K_{\mathfrak{p}}$ of exponent dividing $m$. Each has $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K_{\mathfrak{p}}) \cong K_{\mathfrak{p}}^\times/\mathrm{Nm}(L^\times)$ [@milnecft, Chapter I, Theorem 1.1 (b), p. 20], so $\mathrm{Nm}(L^\times) \supseteq K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times m}$ and $[L : K_{\mathfrak{p}}] \le (K_{\mathfrak{p}}^\times : K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times m})$. This index is finite: $K_{\mathfrak{p}}^\times \cong \mathbb{Z} \times U$, and $U^m$ contains the open subgroup of $a \in U$ with $|1 - a| < |m|^2$ [@milnecft, Chapter I, 1.7, p. 22], so it has finite index in the compact group $U$. As the degrees of these $L$ are bounded, $E_{\mathfrak{P}}/K_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is finite abelian of exponent dividing $\ell - 1$, so of degree prime to $\ell$.
- *$\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is the maximal pro-$\ell$ extension $E_{\mathfrak{P}}(\ell)$.* $\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}/E_{\mathfrak{P}}$ is Galois with group $D_{\mathfrak{P}} \cap \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/E)$, which is pro-$\ell$. If $\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}} \ne E_{\mathfrak{P}}(\ell)$, the nontrivial pro-$\ell$ group $\mathrm{Gal}(E_{\mathfrak{P}}(\ell)/\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}})$ has a quotient of order $\ell$. By Kummer theory this gives an element of $\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}^\times$ that is not an $\ell$-th power, which is impossible.
- *The $\ell$-Sylow subgroup.* So $P_{\mathfrak{P}} = \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{\mathfrak{P}}/E_{\mathfrak{P}}) \cong G_{E_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\ell)$ is a normal pro-$\ell$ subgroup of $D_{\mathfrak{P}}$ of index prime to $\ell$. Every pro-$\ell$ subgroup of $D_{\mathfrak{P}}$ lies in it, so every isomorphism between such decomposition groups maps $P_{\mathfrak{P}}$ onto $P_{\mathfrak{P}'}$.

*Step 3: the bijection of places.* Let $\Omega_i = \Omega_\ell(K_i)$. For a finite place $\mathfrak{P}$ of $\Omega_1$, Step 2 shows that $D_{\mathfrak{P}} \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\Xi/F)$ for the local field $F = K_{1,\mathfrak{p}}$ and $\Xi = \Omega_{1,\mathfrak{P}}$, with $\mu_\ell \subset \Xi$ and $\Xi^\times = \Xi^{\times\ell}$. Karshon and Shusterman's Neukirch theorem for $\ell$-sealed extensions [@karshonshusterman2026, Theorem 5.8, p. 9], applied to $\alpha_\ell(D_{\mathfrak{P}}) \subseteq \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_2/K_2)$, gives a unique finite place $\beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})$ of $\Omega_2$ with $\alpha_\ell(D_{\mathfrak{P}}) \subseteq D_{\beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})}$.

Their proof of Proposition 7.2 uses abundance only through Corollary 6.8, to show that residue characteristics are preserved and so that the places used on both sides lie over the same set $R$ of primes [@karshonshusterman2026, proof of Proposition 7.2, pp. 14–15]. With $S = \emptyset$ and $R$ the set of all rational primes, the rest of the proof applies verbatim. It shows the following.

- $\beta_\ell$ is a bijection from the finite places of $\Omega_1$ to those of $\Omega_2$, with $\alpha_\ell(D_{\mathfrak{P}}) = D_{\beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})}$ and $\beta_\ell(g\mathfrak{P}) = \alpha_\ell(g)\beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})$.
- For every finite subextension $L/K_1$ of $\Omega_1/K_1$, $\beta_\ell$ descends to a bijection from the finite places of $L$ to those of the corresponding field $\alpha_\ell(L)$, satisfying parts (1), (3) and (4) of Proposition 7.2. In particular, for $L$ Galois over $K_1$, $\alpha_\ell$ maps the decomposition group in $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K_1)$ at a place to that at its image. For $L = K_1$ the descent is a bijection $b_\ell$ from the finite places of $K_1$ to those of $K_2$.
- The restriction $G_{\Omega_1/L} \to G_{\Omega_2/\alpha_\ell(L)}$ of $\alpha_\ell$ gives the same bijection $\beta_\ell$. Indeed it maps $D_{\mathfrak{P}} \cap G_{\Omega_1/L}$ into $D_{\beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})}$, so this follows from the uniqueness in Theorem 5.8.

*Step 4: places over $\ell$ go to places over $\ell$.* Let $\mathfrak{P}$ lie over the rational prime $p$, and put $F = E_{\mathfrak{P}}$, a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ containing $\mu_\ell$. Then $P_{\mathfrak{P}} \cong G_F(\ell)$, and we compare $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_\ell} \mathrm{Hom}(P_{\mathfrak{P}}, \mathbb{Z}/\ell)$.

- *If $p \ne \ell$, the dimension is at most $2$.* The $\ell$-Sylow subgroups of $G_F$ are isomorphic to a semidirect product $\mathbb{Z}_\ell \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ [@karshonshusterman2026, Lemma 6.6 (1), p. 12], which is topologically generated by two elements. The image of one of them in the pro-$\ell$ quotient $G_F(\ell)$ is an $\ell$-Sylow subgroup [@karshonshusterman2026, Lemma 6.5 (2), p. 12], so it is all of $G_F(\ell)$.
- *If $p = \ell$, the dimension is $[F : \mathbb{Q}_\ell] + 2 \ge 3$.* $G_F(\ell)$ is a Demushkin group with invariants $([F:\mathbb{Q}_\ell] + 2, q)$ [@serre1963, Théorème 4.2, p. 148], and its abelianization is $\mathbb{Z}/q \times \mathbb{Z}_\ell^{[F:\mathbb{Q}_\ell]+1}$ [@serre1963, §8, pp. 152–153]. See also [maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md).

By Steps 2 and 3, $\alpha_\ell$ maps $P_{\mathfrak{P}}$ isomorphically onto $P_{\beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})}$. So $\mathfrak{P}$ lies over $\ell$ if and only if $\beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})$ does, and $b_\ell$ maps the places of $K_1$ over $\ell$ onto those of $K_2$ over $\ell$.

*Step 5: $b_\ell$ does not depend on $\ell$.* Let $\ell, r$ be primes with $\ell \mid r - 1$, and suppose that $\mathfrak{q} = b_\ell(\mathfrak{p})$ and $\mathfrak{q}' = b_r(\mathfrak{p})$ differ for some finite place $\mathfrak{p}$ of $K_1$.

- *A test field.* By the Grunwald–Wang theorem there is a cyclic extension $M/K_2$ of degree $\ell$ with local degree $1$ at $\mathfrak{q}$ and $\ell$ at $\mathfrak{q}'$ [@milnecft, Chapter VIII, Corollary 2.5, p. 232]. So the decomposition groups of $M/K_2$, which depend only on the place of $K_2$, are $1$ at $\mathfrak{q}$ and $\mathrm{Gal}(M/K_2)$ at $\mathfrak{q}'$.
- *It lies in both quotients.* $M \subseteq \Omega_\ell(K_2)$, since $M E_\ell(K_2)/E_\ell(K_2)$ is an $\ell$-extension. $M \subseteq E_r(K_2) \subseteq \Omega_r(K_2)$, since $\ell \mid r - 1$. Let $M_1 \subseteq K_1^{\mathrm{ss}}$ be the fixed field of $\alpha^{-1}(\mathrm{Gal}(K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}/M))$. By Step 1, $M_1 \subseteq \Omega_\ell(K_1) \cap \Omega_r(K_1)$. The isomorphism $\bar\alpha : \mathrm{Gal}(M_1/K_1) \to \mathrm{Gal}(M/K_2)$ induced by $\alpha$ is also the one induced by $\alpha_\ell$ and by $\alpha_r$.
- *Contradiction.* $M_1/K_1$ is abelian, so its decomposition group $D$ at $\mathfrak{p}$ is well defined. Step 3 for $\alpha_\ell$ gives $\bar\alpha(D) = 1$, and for $\alpha_r$ it gives $\bar\alpha(D) = \mathrm{Gal}(M/K_2)$.

So $b_\ell = b_r$ whenever $\ell \mid r - 1$. For any two primes $\ell \ne \ell'$, choose a prime $r \equiv 1 \pmod{\ell\ell'}$ by Dirichlet's theorem. Then $b_\ell = b_r = b_{\ell'}$. Write $b$ for this common bijection.

*Step 6: residue characteristics, and part 2.* Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a finite place of $K_1$ over $p$. By Step 4 for $\ell = p$, $b(\mathfrak{p}) = b_p(\mathfrak{p})$ lies over $p$. Since $\beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})$ restricts to $b(\mathfrak{P}|_{K_1})$, every $\beta_\ell$ preserves residue characteristics. This is part (2) of Proposition 7.2.

So all of Proposition 7.2 holds for $\alpha_\ell$ and for its restrictions over finite subextensions, with $S = \emptyset$ and $R$ the set of all rational primes. In Karshon and Shusterman's proofs of Corollary 7.3, Theorem 8.2, Lemma 9.3 and Theorem 9.4 [@karshonshusterman2026, pp. 15–19], abundance enters only through Proposition 7.2.

- Corollary 7.3 applies Proposition 7.2.
- Theorem 8.2 applies Corollary 7.3 to $\Omega_i$ over finite subextensions $\widetilde K_i$ and $\widetilde K_i'$.
- Lemma 9.3 applies Theorem 8.2 over finite subextensions $L_i$, and uses the set $R$ and parts (2) and (4) of Proposition 7.2.
- Theorem 9.4 applies Lemma 9.3 over finite subextensions $L_i'$, together with the set $R$ and part (3) of Proposition 7.2.

The set $S$ for which $\Omega_i$ is $\ell$-sealed is used throughout, and here it is $\emptyset$. So these proofs apply verbatim to $\alpha_\ell$ and give $\sigma_\ell$. The uniqueness does not use abundance [@karshonshusterman2026, Remark 9.5, p. 19]. As $K_1 \subseteq \Omega_\ell(K_1)$, $\sigma_\ell$ restricts to an isomorphism $K_1 \to K_2$.

*Step 7: part 3.* As $\sigma_\ell$ induces $\alpha_\ell$, $D_{\sigma_\ell(\mathfrak{P})} = \sigma_\ell D_{\mathfrak{P}} \sigma_\ell^{-1} = \alpha_\ell(D_{\mathfrak{P}}) = D_{\beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})}$. By the uniqueness in Theorem 5.8, $\sigma_\ell(\mathfrak{P}) = \beta_\ell(\mathfrak{P})$, so $\sigma_\ell|_{K_1}$ maps each finite place $\mathfrak{p}$ to $b(\mathfrak{p})$.

For primes $\ell, \ell'$, let $\tau = (\sigma_\ell|_{K_1})^{-1} \circ \sigma_{\ell'}|_{K_1}$, an automorphism of $K_1$ that fixes every finite place. Suppose $\tau \ne 1$, and let $B$ be its fixed field. By Chebotarev, some finite place of $B$ splits completely in the cyclic extension $K_1/B$. The group $\mathrm{Gal}(K_1/B) = \langle \tau \rangle$ permutes the places over it simply transitively, so $\tau$ moves them, a contradiction. So $\tau = 1$. $\square$

**What remains.** Whether $\alpha$ itself is induced by an isomorphism $K_1^{\mathrm{ss}} \to K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}$ is not settled here. Part 3 reduces this question to one about automorphisms. This reduction is proved here.

Extend $\sigma_\ell|_{K_1}$ to an isomorphism $\tilde\sigma : \bar{\mathbb{Q}} \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. It maps $K_1^{\mathrm{ss}}$ onto $K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}$, so $\alpha' = c_{\tilde\sigma}^{-1} \circ \alpha$ is an automorphism of $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$, where $c_{\tilde\sigma}(g) = \tilde\sigma g \tilde\sigma^{-1}$. On $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K_1)/K_1)$, $\alpha'$ is conjugation by $\tilde\sigma^{-1}\sigma_\ell$, which is the identity on $K_1$ by part 3. So $\alpha'$ is inner on every quotient $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K_1)/K_1)$.

Moreover, $\alpha$ is induced by a field isomorphism exactly when $\alpha'$ is inner. If $\alpha' = c_g$, then $\alpha = c_{\tilde\sigma g}$. Conversely, suppose $\alpha$ is induced by $\sigma : K_1^{\mathrm{ss}} \to K_2^{\mathrm{ss}}$, and put $t = \tilde\sigma^{-1}\sigma$. Then $t$ and $\tilde\sigma^{-1}\sigma_\ell$ both induce $\alpha'$ on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K_1)/K_1)$. By the uniqueness in part 2 they agree on $\Omega_\ell(K_1)$, so $t$ fixes $K_1$, $t \in G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$, and $\alpha' = c_t$.

Uniqueness of a field isomorphism inducing $\alpha$, when one exists, is equivalent to $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$ having trivial centre. If $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ both induce $\alpha$, then they agree on every $\Omega_\ell(K_1)$ by part 2, so $\sigma^{-1}\sigma'$ fixes $K_1$ and is a central element of $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$. Conversely, if $z$ is central and $\sigma$ induces $\alpha$, then so does $\sigma z$. By part 2, applied to the identity of $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{ss}}$, a central element acts trivially on every $\Omega_\ell(K_1)$, and in particular on $K_1^{\mathrm{ab}}$.

The fields $\Omega_\ell(K)$ need not contain every finite Galois subextension of $K^{\mathrm{ss}}/K$. A finite quotient of $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_\ell(K)/K)$ has a normal Sylow $\ell$-subgroup with abelian quotient. A group $(\mathbb{Z}/7 \times \mathbb{Z}/13) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/3$ in which $\mathbb{Z}/3$ acts nontrivially on both factors is supersolvable but has no such Sylow subgroup for any $\ell$. So a Galois extension of $K$ with this group lies in $K^{\mathrm{ss}}$ but in no $\Omega_\ell(K)$.

**Status of the inputs.** The proof rests on Karshon and Shusterman's arXiv preprint of January 2026. Its Theorem 9.4 and the results leading to it are used as stated, apart from the modification described in Step 6. They have not been independently audited here, beyond the reading needed to check where abundance is used.
