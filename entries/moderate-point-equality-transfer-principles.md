**Setting.** Let $k$ be a number field, $\ell$ a prime and $C$ a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $k$, with $\Delta_C$, $\Pi_C$, $\rho_C$ and the kernel field $\Omega_C$ as in [Hoshi's question on moderate points](l-moderate-points-and-pro-l-kernel-fields.md). For a closed point $x$ of $C$, $\kappa(x) \subseteq \bar k$ is the Galois closure over $k$ of its residue field, and $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ is the compositum of the fields $\kappa(x)$ for the $\ell$-moderate closed points $x$ of $C$ [@hoshi2015, Definition 2.3, p. 659, and Remark 2.6.2, p. 662]. Say that $C$ *satisfies equality* if $\Omega_C = k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$. The inclusion $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq \Omega_C$ always holds [@hoshi2015, Theorem 3.1, p. 664]. Throughout, "moderate" means $\ell$-moderate.

The proofs below use these results of Hoshi.

- **(H1)** Let $X \to C$ be a connected finite étale cover that arises from an open subgroup of $\Pi_C$, with $X$ geometrically connected over $k$. If a closed point $x$ of $X$ lies over $c \in C$, then $x$ is moderate exactly when $c$ is, and in that case $\Omega_X = \Omega_C \cdot \kappa(x)$. Moreover $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq k_X^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ and $\Omega_C \subseteq \Omega_X$ [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.2 (i), (ii), pp. 665–666].
- **(H2)** For $x \in C(k)$, the point $x$ is moderate if and only if $\Omega_{C \setminus \{x\}} = \Omega_C$, if and only if $\Omega_{C \setminus \{x\}}/\Omega_C$ is finite [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.5, pp. 659–660].
- **(H3)** If $U \subseteq C$ is a nonempty open subscheme, then $\Omega_C \subseteq \Omega_U$ [@hoshi2015, Remark 1.1.2, p. 653].
- **(H4)** If $C$ satisfies equality, then $C$ has a moderate closed point, because $\Omega_C/k$ is infinite [@hoshi2015, Remark 3.1.1 (i), pp. 664–665].

One more fact is checked here. For a finite extension $K/k$ inside $\bar k$, $\Omega_{C_K} = \Omega_C K$. Indeed $C_K$ has the same geometric fundamental group, and $\Pi_{C_K} = \Pi_C \times_{G_k} G_K$, so $\rho_{C_K}$ is the restriction of $\rho_C$ to $G_K$ and has kernel $\ker \rho_C \cap G_K$.

**Theorem.**

1. *Covers.* Let $X \to C$ be a finite étale morphism with $X$ geometrically connected over $k$, such that the Galois closure of $X \otimes_k \bar k \to C \otimes_k \bar k$ has $\ell$-power degree. Then $\Omega_X = \Omega_C \cdot \kappa(x)$ for every moderate closed point $x$ of $X$. If $C$ satisfies equality, then so does $X$, and $X$ has moderate closed points.
2. *Open subcurves.* Let $U \subseteq X$ be a nonempty open subscheme of a hyperbolic curve $X$ over $k$ with $\Omega_U = \Omega_X$. Then every moderate closed point of $U$ is a moderate closed point of $X$. If $U$ satisfies equality, then so does $X$.
3. *Descent.* Let $k' \subseteq \bar k$ be a finite extension of $k$ with $k' \subseteq k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$. If the curve $C_{k'}$ over $k'$ satisfies equality, then so does $C$, and $\Omega_C = \Omega_{C_{k'}}$.

Part 2 is Hoshi's argument for passing to a compactification [@hoshi2015, Example 3.4 (ii), p. 668], stated in general; the problem entry already records it. Parts 1 and 3 are not stated in Hoshi's paper, which proves the inclusions of (H1) and the finiteness statement that if $\Omega_C/k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ is finite, so is $\Omega_X/k_X^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.2 (iii), p. 666].

**Proof of 1.** First, $X \to C$ arises from an open subgroup of $\Pi_C$. Let $N$ be the kernel of $\pi_1(C \otimes_k \bar k) \to \Delta_C$, the intersection of the open normal subgroups with $\ell$-group quotient. The normal core $V$ of $\pi_1(X \otimes_k \bar k)$ in $\pi_1(C \otimes_k \bar k)$ corresponds to the Galois closure, so the quotient by $V$ is an $\ell$-group and $N \subseteq V \subseteq \pi_1(X \otimes_k \bar k) \subseteq \pi_1(X)$. So $\pi_1(X)$, an open subgroup of $\pi_1(C)$, is the preimage of an open subgroup of $\Pi_C = \pi_1(C)/N$. The first claim is then (H1), since a moderate $x$ lies over a moderate $c$.

Suppose that $C$ satisfies equality. By (H4), $C$ has a moderate closed point $c$. A finite étale morphism from a nonempty scheme to the connected curve $C$ is surjective, so some closed point $x$ of $X$ lies over $c$, and $x$ is moderate by (H1). For any moderate $x$, (H1) gives
$$\Omega_X = \Omega_C \cdot \kappa(x) = k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \cdot \kappa(x) \subseteq k_X^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell},$$
using $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq k_X^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ from (H1) and $\kappa(x) \subseteq k_X^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ by definition. The reverse inclusion is Theorem 3.1.

**Proof of 2.** Let $P$ be a moderate closed point of $U$, put $k_1 = \kappa(P)$, and let $Q \in U(k_1)$ lie over $P$. Then $Q$ is moderate on $U_{k_1}$ [@hoshi2015, Definition 2.4 (2), p. 659]. By base change, $\Omega_{U_{k_1}} = \Omega_U k_1 = \Omega_X k_1 = \Omega_{X_{k_1}}$. By (H3) twice and (H2) for $Q$ on $U_{k_1}$,
$$\Omega_{X_{k_1}} \subseteq \Omega_{X_{k_1} \setminus \{Q\}} \subseteq \Omega_{U_{k_1} \setminus \{Q\}} = \Omega_{U_{k_1}} = \Omega_{X_{k_1}},$$
so $Q$ is moderate on $X_{k_1}$ by (H2). This holds for every $k_1$-rational point over $P$, so $P$ is moderate on $X$. The field $\kappa(P)$ is the same in $U$ and in $X$, so $k_U^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq k_X^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$. If $U$ satisfies equality, then $\Omega_X = \Omega_U = k_U^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq k_X^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq \Omega_X$.

**Proof of 3.** *Step 1: moderateness of a geometric point does not depend on the field.* Let $z \in C(\bar k)$, and let $K \subseteq K'$ be finite extensions of $k$ in $\bar k$ containing the field of definition of $z$. View $z$ as a rational point of $C_K$ and of $C_{K'}$. By base change, applied over $K$ to $C_K$ and to $C_K \setminus \{z\}$, the fields for $K'$ are the composita with $K'$ of those for $K$. Since $K \subseteq \Omega_{C_K}$,
$$[\Omega_{C_K \setminus \{z\}} K' : \Omega_{C_K} K'] \le [\Omega_{C_K \setminus \{z\}} : \Omega_{C_K}] \le [K' : K] \cdot [\Omega_{C_K \setminus \{z\}} K' : \Omega_{C_K} K'].$$
So by (H2), $z$ is moderate on $C_K$ exactly when it is moderate on $C_{K'}$. Call $z$ moderate in that case.

*Step 2: conjugate points.* Let $z \in C(\bar k)$ and $\tau \in G_k$. Choose $K$ finite Galois over $k$ containing the field of definition of $z$. The automorphism of the scheme $C_K = C \times_k \operatorname{Spec} K$ that is the identity on $C$ and is induced by $\tau$ on $K$ lies over an automorphism of $\operatorname{Spec} K$. It maps $C_K \setminus \{z\}$ isomorphically onto $C_K \setminus \{z'\}$, where $z'$ is $\tau(z)$ or $\tau^{-1}(z)$ according to convention. By transport of structure, it identifies the extensions $1 \to \Delta \to \Pi \to G_K \to 1$ of these two curves up to conjugation by $\tau$ on $G_K$. So $\Omega_{C_K \setminus \{z'\}} = \tau^{\pm 1}(\Omega_{C_K \setminus \{z\}})$, while $\Omega_{C_K} = \Omega_C K$ is Galois over $k$, so the degree in (H2) is unchanged. Since $\tau$ is arbitrary, $z$ is moderate if and only if every $G_k$-conjugate of $z$ is.

*Step 3.* Let $x'$ be a moderate closed point of $C_{k'}$, with image $x$ in $C$, and let $z \in C(\bar k)$ lie over $x'$. Since $x'$ is moderate, the $\kappa_{k'}(x')$-rational point $z$ of $C_{\kappa_{k'}(x')}$ is moderate, where $\kappa_{k'}$ is the Galois closure over $k'$. The geometric points over $x$ form one $G_k$-orbit, so all of them are moderate by Step 2. They are the $\kappa(x)$-rational points over $x$, so $x$ is moderate on $C$ by Step 1 and Definition 2.4 (2). The residue field of $x'$ is $k'(z) = k' \cdot k(z)$, which lies in the Galois extension $k' \kappa(x)$ of $k'$, so $\kappa_{k'}(x') \subseteq k' \kappa(x)$. Hence
$$k'^{\,\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}_{C_{k'}} \subseteq k' \cdot k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} = k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}.$$
Since $k' \subseteq k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq \Omega_C$, base change gives $\Omega_{C_{k'}} = \Omega_C k' = \Omega_C$. If $C_{k'}$ satisfies equality, then $\Omega_C = \Omega_{C_{k'}} = k'^{\,\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}_{C_{k'}} \subseteq k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell} \subseteq \Omega_C$.

**Example: twisted Kummer covers of the tripod.** Let $m = \ell^n$ with $n \ge 1$, let $a \in k^\times$, and let $C_a = \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus (\{0, \infty\} \cup \{t : a t^m = 1\})$. The map $t \mapsto a t^m$ is ramified only at $0$ and $\infty$, and the preimages of $0$, $1$, $\infty$ are $0$, $\{a t^m = 1\}$, $\infty$. So it restricts to a finite étale map from $C_a$ to the tripod $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$, and over $\bar k$ it is Galois with group $\mu_m$, acting by $t \mapsto \zeta t$. The tripod satisfies equality [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.3, p. 666], so $C_a$ does, by part 1.

As checked here, $\Omega_{C_a} = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(a^{1/m})$. Let $b \ne 1$ be a tripod $\ell$-unit [@hoshi2015, Definition 1.6, p. 656]; for example, the root step applied to $\{0, 1, \infty\}$ shows that a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity is one. Its closed point on the tripod is moderate [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.8, p. 662], so a closed point $x$ of $C_a$ over it is moderate by (H1), and $\Omega_{C_a} = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot \kappa(x)$. The field $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ is generated by the tripod $\ell$-units [@hoshi2015, Definition 1.6, p. 656]. Applying the root step $n$ times to an admissible set containing $b$, or containing $1$, shows that the $m$-th roots of $b$ and of $1$ are tripod $\ell$-units. Since $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ is Galois over $k$, it also contains the $m$-th roots of every conjugate $b'$ of $b$. Each geometric point over $x$ is a $t$ with $a t^m = b'$ for some such $b'$, so $\kappa(x) \subseteq \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(a^{1/m})$, a Galois extension of $k$ because $\mu_m \subseteq \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$. Conversely, dividing one such $t$ by an $m$-th root of the corresponding $b'$ gives an $m$-th root of $1/a$ in $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot \kappa(x)$.

If some prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $k$ not above $\ell$ has $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)$ prime to $\ell$, then $\Omega_{C_a} \ne \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$. For a prime $\mathfrak{P}$ over $\mathfrak{p}$ in $k(\alpha)$, $\alpha^m = a$, with ramification index $e$, we have $e \, v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a) = m \, v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\alpha)$, so $m$ divides $e$. Thus $\Omega_{C_a}$ is ramified at $\mathfrak{p}$, while $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ is not, because the tripod has good reduction at $\mathfrak{p}$ [@hoshi2015, Proposition 1.2 (ii), pp. 653–654]. For example, $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{Q}} \setminus (\{0, \infty\} \cup \{t : 2t^\ell = 1\})$ with $\ell$ odd satisfies equality with a kernel field different from the tripod's.

[Once-punctured elliptic curves with $j = 0$ or $1728$](moderate-point-equality-for-elliptic-curves-with-extra-automorphisms.md) give examples of genus one, using all three parts.

**Remark on Hoshi's modular curves.** For $\zeta_{3^n} \in k$, Hoshi derives equality for $Y(3^n)$, $n \ge 2$, from equality for $Y(3)$, using the positive answer to the Anderson–Ihara question at $\ell = 3$ to show $\Omega_{Y(3^n)} = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ [@hoshi2015, Example 3.4 (iii), (iv), pp. 668–670]. As checked here, part 1 gives equality for $Y(3^n)$ without that answer. The cover $Y(3^n) \to Y(3)$ arises from an open subgroup of $\Pi_{Y(3)}$ [@hoshi2015, Example 3.4 (iv), pp. 669–670], and that is all part 1 uses beyond equality for $Y(3)$. The Anderson–Ihara answer is still needed for the identification $\Omega_{Y(3^n)} = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$, and through it for $X(3^n)$.
