**Statement.** Let $p$ be a prime and $(K, v)$ a valued field with value group $vK$ and residue field $Kv$. The valuation $v$ is tamely branching at $p$ if $\mathrm{char}\, Kv \ne p$, $vK \ne p\,vK$, and $p^2$ divides the order of $G_{Kv}$ whenever $(vK : p\,vK) = p$ [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Definition 3.28]. Here the order of a profinite group is a supernatural number. Then $K$ admits a henselian valuation tamely branching at $p$ if and only if $G_K$ has a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$, not isomorphic to $C_2$, $\mathbb{Z}_p$ or $\mathbb{Z}_2 \rtimes C_2$, that contains a nontrivial closed abelian subgroup $N$ normal in $P$ [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Theorem 3.29].

The right-hand side depends only on the isomorphism class of $G_K$. So if $G_K \cong G_L$, then $K$ has a henselian valuation tamely branching at $p$ exactly when $L$ does. For example, the only henselian valuations on $\mathbb{Q}_p$ are the trivial one and the $p$-adic one, neither tamely branching at $p$, so no field with absolute Galois group isomorphic to $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ has such a valuation. This step is used in the proof of the [Galois characterization of $p$-adically closed fields](galois-characterization-of-p-adically-closed-fields.md) [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §5, Step 1.5].

**Where $N$ comes from.** Let $v$ be henselian and tamely branching at $p$, let $P$ be a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G_K$ with fixed field $L$, and let $w$ be the unique prolongation of $v$ to $L$. The inertia group $I_L$ of $w$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p^r$ with $r = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} wL/p\,wL \ge 1$, and $P \cong I_L \rtimes G_{Lw}$. So $I_L$ is a nontrivial normal abelian closed subgroup of $P$, and $P$ is not one of the excluded groups. This is the "only if" direction [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Lemma 3.32].

**The converse.** The first case is $G_K \cong \mathbb{Z}_p \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_p$, assuming also $\sqrt{-1} \in K$ when $p = 2$ [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Proposition 3.33]. Then $\mathrm{char}\, K \ne p$, $\zeta_p \in K$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} K^\times/K^{\times p} = 2$. Kummer theory shows that every $x \in K^\times \setminus K^{\times p}$ is $p$-rigid, meaning $K^{\times p} + xK^{\times p} \subseteq \bigcup_{i=0}^{p-1} x^i K^{\times p}$. The construction of valuations from $p$-rigid elements then gives a valuation with non-$p$-divisible value group, which is shown to be henselian and tamely branching at $p$. The general case reduces to this one: one applies the first case to the fixed field of a subgroup of $N$, or of a subgroup generated by $N$ and one more element, that is a semidirect product $\mathbb{Z}_p \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_p$, and then passes the valuation down to $K$ by a "henseling down" theorem [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Corollary 3.37].

**Why the restrictions.** Gitin, Koenigsmann and Stock give examples showing why only tamely branching valuations can be recovered [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Remark 3.31]:

- Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$ with no nontrivial henselian valuation, such as $\mathbb{Q}$. The field of Puiseux series over $k$ has absolute Galois group isomorphic to $G_k$, so its $t$-adic valuation, whose value group is divisible, cannot be read off from the group.
- If $v$ is henselian, $\mathrm{char}\, Kv \ne p$, $(vK : p\,vK) = p$ and $p$ does not divide the order of $G_{Kv}$, then the Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G_K$ are $\mathbb{Z}_p$. An example is $\mathbb{C}((t))$ with its $t$-adic valuation, whose absolute Galois group is $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. But $\mathbb{Z}_p$ is also the absolute Galois group of the algebraic extension $\bigcup_{p \nmid n} \mathbb{F}_{q^n}$ of a finite field, which has only the trivial valuation.
- For $p = 2$, the $t$-adic valuation on $\mathbb{R}((t))$ is henselian with $(vK : 2\,vK) = 2$ and $G_{Kv} \cong C_2$, so it is not tamely branching at $2$, and $G_K$ has Sylow $2$-subgroup $\mathbb{Z}_2 \rtimes C_2$. Take the intersection $L$ of two distinct real closures of $\mathbb{Q}$ in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, whose [involutions](involutions-and-real-places.md) lie in one Sylow $2$-subgroup of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Two distinct involutions generate an infinite group, by the [Artin–Schreier theorem](artin-schreier-theorem.md), and here $G_L \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \rtimes C_2$ although $L$ has no nontrivial henselian valuation.

For $p > 2$, if $p$ divides the order of $G_{Kv}$ then so does $p^2$, by the Artin–Schreier theorem [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Remark 3.31].

**History.** The following is reported by Gitin, Koenigsmann and Stock [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §3.4]. Neukirch showed in 1968 that a subfield $K$ of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ admits a henselian valuation if $G_K$ is prosolvable and $\mathrm{Br}(K)$ is neither a $p$-group for any $p$ nor a $\{2, 3\}$-group [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Theorem 3.30]. Pop generalized this, keeping prosolvability, and Koenigsmann used Pop's form in 1995 to show that fields with absolute Galois group isomorphic to $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ are henselian. Engler and Koenigsmann proved a $p$-henselian analogue, extending earlier work of Engler–Nogueira and Efrat for $p = 2$. Koenigsmann then gave a criterion assuming that the Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G_K$ are solvable, and removed that assumption in 2003. Engler and Prestel present the result in their book. Gitin, Koenigsmann and Stock write that the proofs of Koenigsmann and of Engler and Prestel need additional arguments for $p = 2$, and they give a complete proof [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §1].

Neukirch's proof of the characterization of $p$-adic fields in his [local theory](neukirch-local-theory.md) also begins by showing that a field is henselian.

**Status of the sources.** The original papers of Neukirch, Pop, Engler, Nogueira, Efrat and Koenigsmann and the book of Engler and Prestel have not been opened for this entry. The attributions above follow the account of Gitin, Koenigsmann and Stock.
