**Setting.** Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. Curves over $K$ are smooth, proper and connected. For a nonconstant morphism $h$ of curves and a point $y$ of its source, $e_y(h)$ is the ramification index, and for a point $P$ of its target, $h^*(P) = \sum_{y \in h^{-1}(P)} e_y(h)\, y$; this divisor has degree $\deg h$. Let $X$ be a curve and $Q \in X(K)$. Say that $Q$ satisfies the *four-fibre hypothesis* on $X$ if $X$ and $S = \{Q\}$ satisfy the hypothesis of part 1 of [even ramification over four points](even-ramification-over-four-points-gives-tamagawas-criterion.md). That is, there are a nonconstant morphism $f : X \to \mathbb{P}^1$ and distinct $b_1, b_2, b_3, b_4 \in \mathbb{P}^1(K)$ with $f^{-1}(b_4) \subseteq \{Q\}$ such that, for each $i \le 3$, either $f^{-1}(b_i) \subseteq \{Q\}$ or $e_x(f)$ is even for every $x \in f^{-1}(b_i)$. Call such an $f$ a *witness* for $Q$, and write $\Sigma(X)$ for the set of these $Q$. By that theorem, $X \setminus \{Q\}$ lies over a tripod when $Q \in \Sigma(X)$.

For $\lambda \in K \setminus \{0, 1\}$, let $E_\lambda$ be the smooth projective cubic $y^2 = x(x-1)(x-\lambda)$, with point at infinity $O$ and involution $\sigma(x, y) = (x, -y)$. The function $x : E_\lambda \to \mathbb{P}^1$ has degree $2$ and satisfies $x \circ \sigma = x$ and $x^{-1}(\infty) = \{O\}$. For $b \in \{0, 1, \lambda\}$, $x^{-1}(b) = \{(b, 0)\}$, so $e_{(b,0)}(x) = 2$. The fixed points of $\sigma$ are $O$, $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$ and $(\lambda,0)$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $X$ be a curve of genus $g \ge 2$ and let $Q \in X(K)$.

1. *Characterization.* $Q \in \Sigma(X)$ if and only if (a) or (b) holds.
   - (a) There is a nonconstant morphism $h : X \to E$ to a curve $E$ of genus $1$ with $h^{-1}(h(Q)) = \{Q\}$.
   - (b) There are a connected finite étale double cover $\pi : Y \to X$, with nontrivial automorphism $\iota$ over $X$, an element $\lambda \in K \setminus \{0, 1\}$ and a nonconstant morphism $h : Y \to E_\lambda$ such that $h \circ \iota = \sigma \circ h$ and $h^{-1}(O) = \pi^{-1}(Q)$.
2. *Degrees.* Every witness for $Q$ has even degree $m$ with $2 \le m \le 4g - 2$. Every $h$ as in (a) has degree at most $2g - 1$, and every $h$ as in (b) has degree at most $4g - 2$.
3. *Finiteness.* $\Sigma(X)$ is finite.

**Lemma.** Let $C$ be a curve of genus at least $1$ and let $n \ge 1$. There is a finite set $\mathcal{M}_n(C)$ of morphisms of degree $n$ from $C$ to curves of genus $1$ with the following property. Every morphism $h : C \to E$ of degree $n$ to a curve $E$ of genus $1$ equals $\psi \circ h_0$ for some $h_0 : C \to E_0$ in $\mathcal{M}_n(C)$ and some isomorphism $\psi : E_0 \to E$.

*Proof.* Let $E$ be a curve of genus $1$ and $O_E \in E(K)$. The canonical map $E \to J_E$ attached to $O_E$ is a closed immersion [@milne1986jacobian, Proposition 2.3, p. 7] into an abelian variety of dimension $1$ [@milne1986jacobian, Proposition 2.1, p. 5], so it is an isomorphism. Transporting the group law makes $E$ an abelian variety with zero $O_E$. Under this isomorphism a divisor $\sum_P n_P P$ of degree $0$ on $E$ has class equal to the point $\sum_P n_P P$ computed in the group law [@milne1986jacobian, p. 6]. Write $[P]$ for the divisor of a point $P$, and $\lambda_E : E \to E^\vee$ for the homomorphism $\varphi_{\mathcal{L}([O_E])}$, which on points is $a \mapsto t_a^* \mathcal{L}([O_E]) \otimes \mathcal{L}([O_E])^{-1}$ [@milne1986abelian, start of §9, p. 14, and the definition of $\mathrm{NS}(A)$ before Corollary 12.8, p. 22]. Since $t_a^{-1}(O_E) = \{-a\}$, the point $\lambda_E(a)$ of $E^\vee(K) = \mathrm{Pic}^0(E)$ is the class of the divisor $[-a] - [O_E]$.

Fix $c_0 \in C(K)$, let $J$ be the Jacobian of $C$ and $j : C \to J$ the canonical map attached to $c_0$. Then $J(K) = \mathrm{Pic}^0(C)$, and a divisor $\sum_x n_x x$ of degree $0$ on $C$ has class $\sum_x n_x\, j(x)$ in $J(K)$ [@milne1986jacobian, p. 6]. Let $\lambda_J : J \to J^\vee$ be the canonical polarization, which is an isomorphism [@milne1986jacobian, Theorem 6.6, p. 21, and Summary 6.11, p. 24]. For $M \in J^\vee(K) = \mathrm{Pic}^0(J)$, $\lambda_J^{-1}(M)$ is minus the class of $j^* M$ in $\mathrm{Pic}^0(C) = J(K)$ [@milne1986jacobian, Lemma 6.9 and Remark 6.10 (c), p. 23]. Let $\alpha \mapsto \alpha^\dagger = \lambda_J^{-1} \circ \alpha^\vee \circ \lambda_J$ be the Rosati involution of $\lambda_J$ on $\mathrm{End}^0(J) = \mathrm{End}(J) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ [@milne1986abelian, start of §17, p. 35]. Let $\mathrm{Tr}$ be the trace on $\mathrm{End}^0(J)$. It is the trace of the action on $V_\ell J = \mathbb{Q}_\ell \otimes T_\ell J$ for a prime $\ell$ [@milne1986abelian, definition before Proposition 12.9, and Proposition 12.9, p. 23], so it is $\mathbb{Q}$-linear.

Let $h : C \to E$ be a morphism of degree $n$ to a curve $E$ of genus $1$, and give $E$ the group law with zero $O_E = h(c_0)$. Then $h = \varphi_h \circ j$ for a unique homomorphism $\varphi_h : J \to E$ [@milne1986jacobian, Proposition 6.1, p. 19]. Its image is closed and irreducible, and it is not a point because $h$ is nonconstant, so $\varphi_h$ is surjective. Let $N_h \subseteq J(K)$ be the group of $K$-points of $\ker \varphi_h$, and put $\varphi_h^\dagger = \lambda_J^{-1} \circ \varphi_h^\vee \circ \lambda_E : E \to J$, a homomorphism.

*Step 1: $\varphi_h \circ \varphi_h^\dagger = n_E$, multiplication by $n$ on $E$.* Let $a \in E(K)$. On points, $\varphi_h^\vee$ is pullback of invertible sheaves [@milne1986abelian, start of §11, p. 17], so $\varphi_h^\vee(\lambda_E(a)) = \varphi_h^* \mathcal{L}([-a] - [O_E])$. Since $\varphi_h \circ j = h$, its pullback by $j$ is $h^* \mathcal{L}([-a] - [O_E]) = \mathcal{L}(h^*(-a) - h^*(O_E))$. By the description of $\lambda_J^{-1}$ above,
$$\varphi_h^\dagger(a) = -\sum_{x \in h^{-1}(-a)} e_x(h)\, j(x) + \sum_{x \in h^{-1}(O_E)} e_x(h)\, j(x).$$
Applying the homomorphism $\varphi_h$, which sends $j(x)$ to $h(x)$, and using $\sum_{x \in h^{-1}(P)} e_x(h) = n$, gives $\varphi_h(\varphi_h^\dagger(a)) = -n(-a) + n O_E = na$. Two morphisms of varieties over $K$ that agree on $K$-points are equal, so $\varphi_h \circ \varphi_h^\dagger = n_E$.

*Step 2: $e_h = \varphi_h^\dagger \circ \varphi_h \in \mathrm{End}(J)$ lies in a finite set $F_n$ that depends only on $C$, $c_0$ and $n$.*
- *$e_h$ is fixed by $\dagger$.* Let $\mathcal{N} = \varphi_h^* \mathcal{L}([O_E])$. Then $\varphi_h^\vee \circ \lambda_E \circ \varphi_h = \varphi_{\mathcal{N}}$ [@milne1986abelian, proof of Lemma 16.2, p. 30], so $e_h = \lambda_J^{-1} \circ \varphi_{\mathcal{N}}$. Elements of this form are fixed by $\dagger$ [@milne1986abelian, Proposition 17.2, p. 35].
- *$\mathrm{Tr}(e_h e_h^\dagger) = 2n^2$.* By Step 1, $e_h^2 = \varphi_h^\dagger \circ n_E \circ \varphi_h = n e_h$. The action of $e_h$ on $V_\ell J$ is the composite of the actions of $\varphi_h$ and $\varphi_h^\dagger$. A composite of linear maps $V \to W \to V$ has the same trace as the composite $W \to V \to W$ in the other order, so $\mathrm{Tr}(e_h)$ is the trace of $n$ on $V_\ell E$, which has dimension $2$ [@milne1986abelian, Remark 8.4, p. 14]. So $\mathrm{Tr}(e_h) = 2n$, and $\mathrm{Tr}(e_h e_h^\dagger) = \mathrm{Tr}(e_h^2) = n\, \mathrm{Tr}(e_h) = 2n^2$.
- *Finiteness.* The quadratic form $q(\alpha) = \mathrm{Tr}(\alpha \alpha^\dagger)$ on $\mathrm{End}^0(J)$ satisfies $q(\alpha) > 0$ for $\alpha \ne 0$ [@milne1986abelian, Theorem 17.3, p. 35]. Choose a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_r$ of $\mathrm{End}(J)$, which is a free $\mathbb{Z}$-module of finite rank [@milne1986abelian, Theorem 12.5, p. 21], and let $S$ be the symmetric rational matrix with $q(\sum_i x_i \alpha_i) = x^{\mathsf T} S x$. Then $x^{\mathsf T} S x > 0$ for nonzero $x \in \mathbb{Q}^r$, so $x^{\mathsf T} S x \ge 0$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}^r$ by continuity. If $S$ were singular, its kernel, being defined over $\mathbb{Q}$, would contain a nonzero rational $x$, with $x^{\mathsf T} S x = 0$. So $S$ is positive definite, and $x^{\mathsf T} S x \ge c\, |x|^2$ for some $c > 0$. Hence $F_n = \{\alpha \in \mathrm{End}(J) : q(\alpha) = 2n^2\}$ is finite, and $e_h \in F_n$.

*Step 3: for each $e \in F_n$, the groups $N_h$ with $e_h = e$ form a finite set.* Let $G$ be the group of $K$-points of $\ker e$. If $a \in G$, then $\varphi_h^\dagger(\varphi_h(a)) = 0$, so $n \varphi_h(a) = \varphi_h(\varphi_h^\dagger(\varphi_h(a))) = 0$ by Step 1, and $na \in N_h$. So $nG \subseteq N_h \subseteq G$. In characteristic $0$, $\ker e$ is reduced and its identity component is an abelian variety $G^0$ [@milne1986abelian, start of §8, p. 12]. As $\ker e$ is of finite type, it has finitely many components, each a translate of $G^0$, so $G^0(K)$ has finite index in $G$. Multiplication by $n$ on $G^0$ is an isogeny [@milne1986abelian, Theorem 8.2, p. 13], hence surjective, so $nG \supseteq n\,G^0(K) = G^0(K)$, and $G/nG$ is finite. The group $N_h$ corresponds to a subgroup of the finite group $G/nG$, and $G$ and $n$ depend only on $e$, so there are finitely many possibilities.

*Step 4: $N_h$ determines $h$ up to an isomorphism of the target.* Let $h' : C \to E'$ be another nonconstant morphism to a curve of genus $1$ with $N_{h'} = N_h$. Let $\Gamma \subseteq E \times E'$ be the image of $(\varphi_h, \varphi_{h'}) : J \to E \times E'$ with its reduced structure. It is a connected closed subgroup, so it is an abelian variety [@milne1986abelian, unnumbered lemma at the top of p. 20]. The projection $p : \Gamma \to E$ is a surjective homomorphism. If $p(\varphi_h(a), \varphi_{h'}(a)) = 0$, then $a \in N_h = N_{h'}$, so $(\varphi_h(a), \varphi_{h'}(a)) = 0$. So $\ker p$ has one $K$-point, and being reduced in characteristic $0$ [@milne1986abelian, start of §8, p. 12] it is trivial. Its fibres are therefore points, so $\dim \Gamma = \dim E$, and $p$ is an isogeny of degree $1$, that is, a finite flat morphism of degree $1$ [@milne1986abelian, Proposition 8.1 and the definition of degree after it, p. 12]. So $p$ is an isomorphism, and so is the second projection $p'$ by the same argument. Then $\alpha = p' \circ p^{-1} : E \to E'$ is an isomorphism with $\alpha \circ \varphi_h = \varphi_{h'}$, so $\alpha \circ h = h'$.

By Steps 2 and 3, the groups $N_h$, for $h$ of degree $n$, form a finite set. For each of them choose one such $h$, and let $\mathcal{M}_n(C)$ consist of these choices. Step 4 gives the property, since composing with an isomorphism does not change the degree. $\square$

**Pole lemma.** Let $\phi : Z \to X$ be a finite surjective morphism of curves, $f \in K(X)$ and $h : Z \to E_\lambda$ a nonconstant morphism with $h^* x = \phi^* f$. Then $h^{-1}(O) = \phi^{-1}(f^{-1}(\infty))$. Indeed, $x$ is regular on $E_\lambda \setminus \{O\}$ and has a pole at $O$, so $h^* x$ has a pole at $z$ exactly when $h(z) = O$. Likewise $\phi^* f$ has a pole at $z$ exactly when $f$ has one at $\phi(z)$.

**Proof of part 1.** Write $L = K(X)$.

*(a) implies $Q \in \Sigma(X)$.* This is part 2 (a) of [even ramification over four points](even-ramification-over-four-points-gives-tamagawas-criterion.md), with $S = \{Q\}$, $\varphi = h$ and $P = h(Q)$.

*(b) implies $Q \in \Sigma(X)$.* Since $x \circ h \circ \iota = x \circ \sigma \circ h = x \circ h$, the function $h^* x$ is fixed by $\iota$. As $K(Y)/L$ is Galois with group generated by $\iota$, $h^* x = \pi^* f$ for some $f \in L$, which is nonconstant because $h$ and $x$ are. By the pole lemma, $\pi^{-1}(f^{-1}(\infty)) = h^{-1}(O) = \pi^{-1}(Q)$, so $f^{-1}(\infty) = \{Q\}$ as $\pi$ is surjective. Let $b \in \{0, 1, \lambda\}$, $z \in f^{-1}(b)$ and $y \in \pi^{-1}(z)$. Then $x(h(y)) = b$, so $h(y) = (b, 0)$. As $\pi$ is étale, $e_y(\pi) = 1$, and so
$$e_z(f) = e_y(f \circ \pi) = e_y(x \circ h) = e_y(h) \, e_{(b,0)}(x) = 2 e_y(h).$$
So $f$ is a witness for $Q$, with $b_4 = \infty$ and $(b_1, b_2, b_3) = (0, 1, \lambda)$.

*$Q \in \Sigma(X)$ implies (a) or (b).* Let $f$ be a witness. As $f$ is surjective, $f^{-1}(b_4)$ is nonempty, so $f^{-1}(b_4) = \{Q\}$. For $i \le 3$, $f^{-1}(b_i)$ is nonempty and disjoint from $\{Q\}$, so $e_x(f)$ is even for every $x \in f^{-1}(b_i)$. Compose $f$ with an automorphism of $\mathbb{P}^1$, which changes neither fibres nor ramification indices, so that $(b_1, b_2, b_3, b_4) = (0, 1, \lambda, \infty)$ with $\lambda \in K \setminus \{0, 1\}$. The degree $m$ of $f$ is $\sum_{x \in f^{-1}(0)} e_x(f)$, a sum of even numbers, so $m$ is even. The only pole of $f$ is $Q$, of order $e_Q(f) = m$.

Put $u = f(f - 1)(f - \lambda) \in L^\times$. Then $v_Q(u) = -3m$ is even. For $x \ne Q$, at most one of $v_x(f)$, $v_x(f - 1)$, $v_x(f - \lambda)$ is nonzero, and if one is, it equals $e_x(f)$, which is even. So $v_x(u)$ is even for every $x \in X$.

- *If $u = w^2$ with $w \in L$,* then $(f, w)$ satisfies the equation of $E_\lambda$, so $[f : w : 1]$ defines a rational map $X \to E_\lambda$, which extends to a morphism $h$ because $X$ is a smooth curve. As $h^* x = f$, $h$ is nonconstant, and by the pole lemma with $\phi = \mathrm{id}$, $h^{-1}(O) = f^{-1}(\infty) = \{Q\}$. This is (a), with $E = E_\lambda$ and $h(Q) = O$.
- *If $u$ is not a square in $L$,* let $L' = L(s)$ with $s^2 = u$, a field of degree $2$ over $L$, and let $\pi : Y \to X$ be the normalization of $X$ in $L'$. Since $v_x(u)$ is even at every $x \in X$, the argument of Step 2 in the proof of part 1 of [even ramification over four points](even-ramification-over-four-points-gives-tamagawas-criterion.md) shows that every point of $Y$ has ramification index $1$ over its image. That argument uses only this parity and $\operatorname{char} K \ne 2$. So $\pi$ is a finite étale double cover, and $Y$ is connected because $L'$ is a field. The automorphism $s \mapsto -s$ of $L'/L$ gives the nontrivial automorphism $\iota$ of $Y$ over $X$. As before, $[\pi^* f : s : 1]$ defines a nonconstant morphism $h : Y \to E_\lambda$ with $h^* x = \pi^* f$. The morphisms $h \circ \iota$ and $\sigma \circ h$ are both given by $(\pi^* f, -s)$, so they agree on a dense open subset, hence everywhere. By the pole lemma with $\phi = \pi$, $h^{-1}(O) = \pi^{-1}(Q)$. This is (b). $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Normalize a witness $f$ as in the proof of part 1. Its degree $m$ is even and positive. By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula, $2g - 2 = -2m + \sum_{x \in X} (e_x(f) - 1)$. The point $Q$ contributes $m - 1$. For $b \in \{0, 1, \lambda\}$, every index in $f^{-1}(b)$ is at least $2$, so $f^{-1}(b)$ has at most $m/2$ points, and its contribution $m - \# f^{-1}(b)$ is at least $m/2$. Hence $2g - 2 \ge -2m + (m - 1) + 3m/2 = m/2 - 1$, that is, $m \le 4g - 2$. For $h$ as in (a), $e_Q(h) = \deg h$, and the Riemann–Hurwitz formula for $h$ gives $2g - 2 \ge \deg h - 1$, so $\deg h \le 2g - 1$. For $h$ as in (b), the witness $f$ built in the proof of part 1 satisfies $f \circ \pi = x \circ h$, so $2 \deg f = 2 \deg h$ and $\deg h = \deg f \le 4g - 2$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.**

*Step 1: finitely many double covers.* Let $\pi : Y \to X$ be a connected finite étale double cover. As $\operatorname{char} K \ne 2$, $K(Y) = L(\sqrt{u})$ for some $u \in L^\times$. For $x \in X$ and $y \in \pi^{-1}(x)$, $e_y(\pi) = 1$ gives $v_x(u) = v_y(u) = 2 v_y(\sqrt{u})$. So $\operatorname{div}(u) = 2D$, where $D$ has degree $0$ and $2D$ is principal. If $u' \in L^\times$ has $\operatorname{div}(u') = 2D'$ with $D' - D = \operatorname{div}(w)$, then $\operatorname{div}(u'/(u w^2)) = 0$. So $u' = c u w^2$ with $c \in K^\times$, which is a square in $K$, and $L(\sqrt{u'}) = L(\sqrt{u})$. Hence $K(Y)$ is determined by the class of $D$ in the $2$-torsion of $\mathrm{Pic}^0(X)$. That group is $J_X(K)$ [@milne1986jacobian, p. 6], whose $2$-torsion has $2^{2g}$ elements [@milne1986abelian, Remark 8.4, p. 14]. As $Y$ is normal and finite over $X$, it is the normalization of $X$ in $K(Y)$. So up to isomorphism over $X$ there are finitely many such covers $\pi_j : Y_j \to X$. Let $\iota_j$ be the nontrivial automorphism of $Y_j$ over $X$; it is unique because such automorphisms correspond to automorphisms of $K(Y_j)/L$. By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula, $Y_j$ has genus $2g - 1 \ge 1$.

*Step 2: case (a).* Let $h : X \to E$ be as in (a), of degree $n$. By part 2, $n \le 2g - 1$. By the lemma, $h = \psi \circ h_0$ with $h_0 \in \mathcal{M}_n(X)$ and $\psi$ an isomorphism, so $h_0^{-1}(h_0(Q)) = \{Q\}$. A morphism of curves of degree $1$ is an isomorphism, and $X$ does not have genus $1$, so $n \ge 2$. Hence $e_Q(h_0) = n \ge 2$, and $Q$ is a ramification point of $h_0$. In characteristic $0$, $h_0$ is separable and so has finitely many ramification points. So the points satisfying (a) lie in the union, over $n \le 2g - 1$ and $h_0 \in \mathcal{M}_n(X)$, of the ramification points of $h_0$, which is finite.

*Step 3: case (b).* Let $\pi$, $\iota$, $\lambda$ and $h$ be as in (b). By Step 1 there are $j$ and an isomorphism $\beta : Y_j \to Y$ over $X$. Then $\beta^{-1} \iota \beta$ is a nontrivial automorphism of $Y_j$ over $X$, so it equals $\iota_j$. Put $h' = h \circ \beta$, of degree $n = \deg h \le 4g - 2$ by part 2. Then $h' \circ \iota_j = \sigma \circ h'$ and $h'^{-1}(O) = \beta^{-1}(\pi^{-1}(Q)) = \pi_j^{-1}(Q)$. By the lemma, $h' = \psi \circ h_0$ with $h_0 : Y_j \to E_0$ in $\mathcal{M}_n(Y_j)$ and $\psi : E_0 \to E_\lambda$ an isomorphism. Let $\tau = \psi^{-1} \sigma \psi$, an automorphism of $E_0$. Then
$$h_0 \circ \iota_j = \psi^{-1} \circ h' \circ \iota_j = \psi^{-1} \circ \sigma \circ h' = \tau \circ h_0.$$
Since $h_0$ is surjective, $\tau$ is the only automorphism of $E_0$ with $h_0 \circ \iota_j = \tau \circ h_0$, so $\tau$ depends only on $j$ and $h_0$. Its fixed points are the images under $\psi^{-1}$ of the four fixed points of $\sigma$, and $c = \psi^{-1}(O)$ is one of them. Now $\pi_j^{-1}(Q) = h'^{-1}(O) = h_0^{-1}(c)$, so $Q \in \pi_j(h_0^{-1}(\mathrm{Fix}\,\tau))$, a finite set that depends only on $(j, h_0)$. There are finitely many $j$, and for each $j$ and each $n \le 4g - 2$ the set $\mathcal{M}_n(Y_j)$ is finite, so the points satisfying (b) lie in a finite set.

By part 1, $\Sigma(X)$ is finite. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Fields.* The theorem holds over every algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, for instance $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $\mathbb{C}_p$ or $\mathbb{C}$. Let $X$ be a smooth proper curve of genus at least $2$ over a finite extension $k$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Part 1 of [even ramification over four points](even-ramification-over-four-points-gives-tamagawas-criterion.md) is applied over an algebraic closure $\bar k$ in part 3 there. So, by part 3 here, the four-fibre construction applies to $X \setminus \{Q\}$, and shows that it is of A-qLT-type, for only finitely many $Q \in X(\bar k)$. For every other point $Q$, rational over some finite extension of $k$, it gives nothing, and whether $X \setminus \{Q\}$ is of A-qLT-type is not settled here. Positive characteristic is not treated: Steps 3 and 4 of the proof of the lemma use that kernels are reduced, and Step 4 fails as written for a power of Frobenius, which has trivial kernel on points.
- *The lemma.* Kani states, for a curve of genus $2$, that it has only finitely many isomorphism classes of elliptic subcovers of bounded degree, as the genus-two case of a theorem of Tamme valid for arbitrary genus [@kani1994elliptic, Corollary 1.10 and the sentence before it, p. 4]. The proof of the lemma above does not use these results. The bound on the degree is needed: composing one map with the multiplication maps of its target gives maps of infinitely many different degrees, which no isomorphism of targets relates.
- *Scope.* The theorem concerns only the four-fibre hypothesis. Tamagawa's criterion allows any finite étale cover and any dominant morphism to a tripod (see [the problem](a-qlt-problem-for-p-adic-hyperbolic-curves.md)), so a point outside $\Sigma(X)$ may still give a curve $X \setminus \{Q\}$ that lies over a tripod by another construction.
- *A point of $\Sigma(X)$ that is not a Weierstrass point.* This is checked here. Let $X$ be the curve of genus $2$ given by $y^2 = c(x^2 - a_1)(x^2 - a_2)(x^2 - a_3)$, with $c \ne 0$ and $a_1, a_2, a_3$ distinct and nonzero. Its Weierstrass points are the ramification points of the double cover $(x, y) \mapsto x$, which is unramified over $\infty$ because the degree $6$ is even. They are the six points $(\pm\sqrt{a_i}, 0)$. Let $R = (0, r)$ with $r^2 = -c a_1 a_2 a_3$, so $r \ne 0$ and $R$ is not a Weierstrass point. Let $E$ be the cubic $v^2 = c(t - a_1)(t - a_2)(t - a_3)$, which has genus $1$, and $h : X \to E$ the morphism given by $(t, v) = (x^2, y)$. The function $x^2$ has poles at the two points of $X$ at infinity, so $h$ sends them to the point at infinity of $E$. The only affine point with $x^2 = 0$ and $y = r$ is $R$, so $h^{-1}(h(R)) = \{R\}$. So (a) holds and $R \in \Sigma(X)$. Hence the single-cusp curve $X \setminus \{R\}$ lies over a tripod by part 1 of [even ramification over four points](even-ramification-over-four-points-gives-tamagawas-criterion.md), although the hyperelliptic double cover gives no admissible $f$ for it. If $c$, the $a_i$ and $r$ lie in a finite extension $k$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, part 3 there shows that $X \setminus \{R\}$ over $k$ is of A-qLT-type.
