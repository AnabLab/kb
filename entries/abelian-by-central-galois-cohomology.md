**Statement.** For a group $G$ and a prime $\ell$, put $G^{(1,n)} = G$ and $G^{(i+1,n)} = (G^{(i,n)})^{\ell^n}[G^{(i,n)}, G]$, and put $G^{c,n} = G/G^{(3,n)}$. This descending central series differs from the [Zassenhaus filtration](abelian-by-central-galois-group.md) in general. Let $K, L$ be fields containing the $\ell^n$-th roots of unity, let $G_K$ and $G_L$ be their maximal pro-$\ell$ Galois groups, and let $\Psi : G_K \to G_L$ be a continuous homomorphism. Then $\Psi$ induces an isomorphism $G^{c,n}_K \to G^{c,n}_L$ if and only if it induces an isomorphism $H^*(G_L, \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n) \to H^*(G_K, \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)$. This is due to Chebolu, Efrat and Mináč [@bogomolovtschinkel2012, Theorem 12].

**Relation to Milnor K-theory.** By the Merkurjev–Suslin theorem, the second cohomology of the absolute Galois group with $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n$ coefficients is $K^M_2(K)/\ell^n$. Bogomolov and Tschinkel explain that the Bloch–Kato conjecture implies that the abelian-by-central quotient captures the $\ell$-part of the cohomology, and they present this theorem as a related incarnation of that principle [@bogomolovtschinkel2012, §3].

**A formal description.** If $\mu_n \subset K$ and the characteristic of $K$ is prime to $n$, then the Merkurjev–Suslin theorem gives a functorial description of the second graded piece of the mod-$n$ central series of the absolute Galois group of $K$ as a group of functions on $K \setminus \{0,1\}$, compatibly with Kummer theory [@topaz2017abc, Theorem 1].

**Theorems A and B.** The same paper proves two companion results. Let $q = p^d$ be a prime power, let $F$ be a field containing a primitive $q$-th root of unity, write $H^*(-)$ for cohomology with $\mathbb{Z}/q$ coefficients, and let $G_F^{[3]}$ be the quotient of the absolute Galois group $G_F$ by the third term of its descending $q$-central sequence. Inflation maps the subring of $H^*(G_F^{[3]})$ generated by degree $1$ isomorphically onto $H^*(G_F)$ (Theorem A). Conversely, $G_F^{[3]}$ is uniquely determined by $H^1(G_F)$, $H^2(G_F)$, the cup product $H^1(G_F) \times H^1(G_F) \to H^2(G_F)$ and the Bockstein map $H^1(G_F) \to H^2(G_F)$ (Theorem B) [@cheboluefratminac2012, §1, p. 2]. For $q = 2$, the Bockstein map is $\psi \mapsto \psi \cup \psi$ [@cheboluefratminac2012, §2, p. 3], the group $G_F^{[3]}$ is the W-group of $F$, and in this case Theorem A had been proved by Adem, Karagueuzian and Mináč [@cheboluefratminac2012, p. 2]. See [W-groups and Witt rings](w-groups-and-witt-rings.md).

**Attribution.** In the original paper this is Theorem C, stated for absolute Galois groups, and Remark 8.2 there says that it also holds for maximal pro-$\ell$ Galois groups [@cheboluefratminac2012, Theorem C and Remark 8.2].

**Root-of-unity description.** The Bockstein in Theorem B uses trivial coefficients throughout $0\to\mathbb Z/q\to\mathbb Z/q^2\to\mathbb Z/q\to0$. It is cup product with the Kummer class of a primitive $q$th root of unity; see [the formula and its character-lifting interpretation](bockstein-and-roots-of-unity.md).

**Minimal quotient.** Efrat and Mináč identify a [minimal quotient for inflation](minimal-galois-cohomology-quotient.md). It is determined by the degree-one cup product without a separate Bockstein map [@efratminac2017functors, Theorems A–B and Corollary 7.3(1)].
