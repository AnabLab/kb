**Setting.** Fix a prime $p$, a power $q=p^f$ with $f\ge1$, and
$$G=\langle x,y,z\mid[x,y]=1\rangle_{\text{pro-}p}=\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p,$$
with $p$-Zassenhaus filtration $G_{(n)}$. The kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_q$ is as in [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md). There, $G$ *detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F_q$* if every $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$ has $\rho(g)\ne1$ for some continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_q)$, and by its Lemma 1, detecting every degree $m\le n-1$ over $\mathbb F_q$ gives the property over $\mathbb F_q$. Put $N(q)=(q+1)(q^2-q+3)$.

Before this entry, the knowledgebase had the following. [Polynomial factor algebras](polynomial-factors-double-the-unipotent-detection-range.md) give the property over $\mathbb F_q$ for $n\le2q+2$. [The prime-field theorem](zp2-free-product-zp-has-the-kernel-unipotent-property-up-to-size-2p-plus-4.md) gives it over $\mathbb F_p$, and hence over $\mathbb F_q$, for $n\le2p+4$. [The fixed-field theorem](no-fixed-finite-field-detects-every-zassenhaus-degree-of-zp2-free-product-zp.md) shows that it fails over $\mathbb F_q$ for every $n\ge N(q)+4$, and for every $n\ge N(q)+3$ if $p=2$.

*Notation.* We use the notation of the prime-field theorem: $\bar R=\mathbb F_p\langle X,Y,Z\rangle/(XY-YX)$ with its components $P^{(k)}$ by number of letters $Z$; the pairs $a_l=(X_l,Y_l)$; the map $\kappa$, injective on $\bar R^{(k)}$; the multidegree $\delta(S)$ of a $k$-subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and the components $\kappa_\delta$; the ring $\mathcal T_k$ generated by the coordinates of the differences $d_l=a_{l-1}-a_l$; the space $\mathcal W_m$, defined by (W0) and (W1); the map $\bar\mu:\bar R\to A(G)$; and the representations $\rho_{S,v}$. We regard polynomials with coefficients in $\mathbb F_p$ as elements of $\mathbb F_q[a_0,\ldots,a_k]$, and we now allow $v=(v_0,\ldots,v_k)\in(\mathbb F_q^2)^{k+1}$. The construction of $\rho_{S,v}$ there uses only that the matrices $A$ and $B$ commute, together with Step 2 of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md), which holds over every finite field of characteristic $p$. So $\rho_{S,v}:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_q)$ is a continuous homomorphism.

*(E) over $\mathbb F_q$.* For $P\in\bar R_m$ and $\rho=\rho_{S,v}$ with $|S|=k$ and $v\in(\mathbb F_q^2)^{k+1}$,
$$\kappa_\rho(\bar\mu(P))=\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(k)})(v_0,\ldots,v_k),$$
where $\kappa_\rho$ is the corner functional on $A_m(G)^{\mathbb F_q}$ of the invisible-ideal theorem. The proof of (E) in the prime-field theorem applies verbatim. It uses the linear extension of $\rho$ to the group algebra and Step 3 of the counterexample, which holds over every field. Likewise, its identity (Z) holds for every $v\in(\mathbb F_q^2)^{k+1}$ whenever every multihomogeneous component of $\kappa(P^{(k)})=\Phi(d_1,\ldots,d_k)$ vanishes on $(\mathbb F_q^2)^{k+1}$.

*Two derivations.* On $\mathbb F_q[a_0,\ldots,a_k]$ put $E=\sum_l\partial/\partial X_l$ and $E'=\sum_l\partial/\partial Y_l$. If $f\in\mathcal T_k$, then $f(a_0+t,\ldots,a_k+t)=f(a_0,\ldots,a_k)$. Taking $t=(T,0)$, respectively $t=(0,T)$, and comparing the coefficients of $T$ gives $Ef=0$ and $E'f=0$. For a pair $a=(X,Y)$ put
$$\Lambda(a)=X^qY-XY^q=XY\prod_{c\in\mathbb F_q^\times}(X-cY),$$
where the factorization is the homogenization of $\prod_{c\in\mathbb F_q^\times}(T-c)=T^{q-1}-1$, as in (B2) of the fixed-field theorem. So $\Lambda(a)$ is a product of $q+1$ pairwise non-associate linear forms. As $q=0$ in $\mathbb F_q$, $E(\Lambda(a_l))=-Y_l^q$ and $E'(\Lambda(a_l))=X_l^q$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. For every power $q$ of $p$ and every $m\le2q+4$, $G$ detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F_q$, by representations $\rho_{S,v}$ with $v$ over $\mathbb F_q$. So $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_q$, and over every finite field containing $\mathbb F_q$, for every $n\le2q+5$. For $q=p$ this means $n\le2p+5$ over $\mathbb F_p$: $n\le9$ if $p=2$ and $n\le11$ if $p=3$. For $q=4,8,9$ it means $n\le13,21,23$.

**Corollary.** Let $K$ be the field of Corollary 1 of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md), which contains a primitive $p$th root of unity and has $G_K(p)\cong G$. Then $G_K(p)$ satisfies the equality of the [kernel-unipotent conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md) for every $n\le2p+5$. For $p=2$ this leaves $10\le n\le17$ open.

**Proposition (where undetected elements live).** This is proved here. Let $m\le N(q)+1$, and let $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$ satisfy $\rho(g)=1$ for every continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_q)$. Then every $P\in\mathcal W_m$ with $\bar\mu(P)=\mathrm{in}_m(g)$, and by Lemma 2 of the prime-field theorem there is one, has $P^{(k)}=0$ for $k\le2$ and for $k\ge m-2q-1$. So $P$ is a combination of words with $k$ letters $Z$, where $3\le k\le m-2q-2$, and such $g$ exist only if $m\ge2q+5$. For $q=p$, the part $k\le2$ is the proposition of the prime-field theorem.

**Lemma A (forms vanishing at the $\mathbb F_q$-points).** Let $h\in\mathbb F_q[a_0,\ldots,a_k]$ be multihomogeneous of multidegree $\delta$, with $h(v)=0$ for all $v\in(\mathbb F_q^2)^{k+1}$. If $\delta_l\le q$ for every $l$, then $h=0$. If $\delta_l\ge q+1$ for exactly one $l$, then $\Lambda(a_l)$ divides $h$.

*Proof.* This is Lemma 3 of the prime-field theorem with $\mathbb F_q$ in place of $\mathbb F_p$, and its proof goes through unchanged. A binary form vanishing on $\mathbb F_q^2$ is divisible by $r'Y-r''X$ for every nonzero $r=(r',r'')\in\mathbb F_q^2$, by the argument there, which works over any field. For representatives $r$ of the $q+1$ lines these forms are pairwise non-proportional, and for the representatives $(1,t)$ and $(0,1)$ their product is $-X\prod_{t\in\mathbb F_q}(Y-tX)=-X(Y^q-X^{q-1}Y)=\Lambda(a)$. So the binary forms of degree $d$ vanishing on $\mathbb F_q^2$ are $0$ for $d\le q$, and are the multiples of $\Lambda$ for $d\ge q+1$. The tensor-product argument there then applies as written. $\square$

**Lemma B (translation invariance in both coordinates).** Let $k\ge0$ and $D\le2q+1$. Let $f\in\mathbb F_q[a_0,\ldots,a_k]$ be homogeneous of degree $D$, with $Ef=E'f=0$, and suppose that every multihomogeneous component of $f$ vanishes on $(\mathbb F_q^2)^{k+1}$. Then $f=0$.

*Proof.* As $D<2q+2$, no multidegree of degree $D$ has two entries at least $q+1$. By Lemma A, the components with every $\delta_l\le q$ vanish, and those with $\delta_l\ge q+1$ are divisible by $\Lambda(a_l)$. Collecting the latter by $l$ gives
$$f=\sum_{l=0}^k\Lambda(a_l)\,g_l,$$
with each $g_l$ homogeneous of degree $e=D-q-1\le q$. If $e<0$, then $f=0$. Otherwise write $g_l=g_l^0+u_l$, where $g_l^0$ is $g_l$ with $X_l=Y_l=0$ and every monomial of $u_l$ has positive degree in the pair $a_l$. For a monomial $M$, write $\deg_l M$ for its degree in $X_l,Y_l$.

As $E$ is a derivation and $E(\Lambda(a_l))=-Y_l^q$,
$$0=Ef=\sum_l\Bigl(-Y_l^qg_l^0+\tau_l\Bigr),\qquad\tau_l=-Y_l^qu_l+\Lambda(a_l)E(g_l).$$
All terms are homogeneous of degree $q+e\le2q$. Every monomial of $Y_l^qg_l^0$ has $\deg_l=q$, and every monomial of $\tau_l$ has $\deg_l\ge q+1$.

(i) *$\tau_l=0$.* Let $M$ be a monomial with $\deg_lM\ge q+1$. Then $\deg_{l'}M\le e-1<q$ for every $l'\ne l$, so $M$ does not occur in the $l'$th summand, whose monomials all have $\deg_{l'}\ge q$. Nor does $M$ occur in $Y_l^qg_l^0$. So the coefficient of $M$ in $Ef$ is its coefficient in $\tau_l$, and $\tau_l=0$.

(ii) *$g_l^0$ is a combination of the $Y_{l'}^q$, $l'\ne l$, and is $0$ if $e<q$.* Let $M$ be a monomial of $g_l^0$, so that $\deg_lM=0$, and consider $Y_l^qM$. It does not occur in $\tau_l$. Suppose that it occurs in the $l'$th summand for some $l'\ne l$. Then $\deg_{l'}(Y_l^qM)\ge q$, while $\deg_l(Y_l^qM)=q$ and the total degree is $q+e\le2q$. So $e=q$, $\deg_{l'}(Y_l^qM)=q$, and $M$ is a monomial in $X_{l'},Y_{l'}$ alone. As $\tau_{l'}$ has $\deg_{l'}\ge q+1$, $Y_l^qM$ occurs in $Y_{l'}^qg_{l'}^0$, so $Y_l^qM=Y_{l'}^qM'$ with $\deg_{l'}M'=0$. Comparing the factors in $a_{l'}$ gives $M=Y_{l'}^q$. If $e<q$, the supposition is impossible. So for every monomial $M$ of $g_l^0$ other than the $Y_{l'}^q$, the coefficient of $Y_l^qM$ in $Ef$ is minus the coefficient of $M$ in $g_l^0$, which is therefore $0$.

The same argument with $E'$, using $E'(\Lambda(a_l))=X_l^q$, gives $X_l^qu_l+\Lambda(a_l)E'(g_l)=0$, and shows that $g_l^0$ is a combination of the $X_{l'}^q$ with $l'\ne l$. The monomials $Y_{l'}^q$ and $X_{l''}^q$ are all distinct, so $g_l^0=0$ and $g_l=u_l$.

(iii) *$u_l=0$.* Now (i) and its analogue for $E'$ read
$$Y_l^q\,u_l=\Lambda(a_l)\,E(u_l),\qquad X_l^q\,u_l=-\Lambda(a_l)\,E'(u_l).$$
In the unique factorization domain $\mathbb F_q[a_0,\ldots,a_k]$, each of the linear factors $X_l$ and $X_l-cY_l$ ($c\in\mathbb F_q^\times$) of $\Lambda(a_l)$ divides $Y_l^qu_l$ and is prime to $Y_l$, so it divides $u_l$. The factor $Y_l$ divides $X_l^qu_l$ and is prime to $X_l$, so it divides $u_l$. These $q+1$ factors are pairwise non-associate primes, so $\Lambda(a_l)$ divides $u_l$. As $\deg u_l=e\le q<q+1$, $u_l=0$.

So every $g_l=0$, and $f=0$. $\square$

**Lemma C (two letters $Z$).** Let $\Phi\in\mathbb F_q[d_1,d_2]$ be homogeneous of degree $D\le3q+2$, with
$$\Phi(s_0v_0-s_1v_1,\ s_1v_1-s_2v_2)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_q[s_0,s_1,s_2]\tag{Z}$$
for all $v_0,v_1,v_2\in\mathbb F_q^2$. Then $\Phi=0$.

*Proof.* For nonzero $r\in\mathbb F_q^2$ write $\theta_r(d)=r'd''-r''d'$, so that $\theta_r(r)=0$, and fix $\bar r\notin\mathbb F_qr$. Take $(v_0,v_1,v_2)$ to be $(r,r,\bar r)$, $(\bar r,r,r)$ or $(r,\bar r,r)$, and $\ell$ to be $\theta_r(d_1)$, $\theta_r(d_2)$ or $\theta_r(d_1+d_2)$, respectively. In the three cases the pair $(d_1,d_2)=(s_0v_0-s_1v_1,s_1v_1-s_2v_2)$ is $\bigl((s_0-s_1)r,\ s_1r-s_2\bar r\bigr)$, $\bigl(s_0\bar r-s_1r,\ (s_1-s_2)r\bigr)$ and $\bigl(s_0r-s_1\bar r,\ s_1\bar r-s_2r\bigr)$, with $d_1+d_2=(s_0-s_2)r$ in the third. So $\ell$ vanishes on it. This linear map $\mathbb F_q^3\to\mathbb F_q^4$ is injective: if the pair is $0$, the independence of $r$ and $\bar r$ gives $s_0=s_1=s_2=0$ in each case. So its image is $\ker\ell$. Complete $\ell$ to a basis $\ell,y_2,y_3,y_4$ of the linear forms and write $\Phi=\ell\,\Phi_1+\Phi_0(y_2,y_3,y_4)$. Then (Z) says that $\Phi_0$, composed with an invertible linear substitution in $s_0,s_1,s_2$, is $0$. So $\Phi_0=0$, and $\ell$ divides $\Phi$.

Letting $r$ run over representatives of the $q+1$ lines gives $3(q+1)$ such forms. They are pairwise non-proportional: $\theta_r(d_1)$ involves only $d_1$, $\theta_r(d_2)$ only $d_2$, and $\theta_r(d_1+d_2)$ both, while $\theta_r$ and $\theta_s$ are not proportional for distinct lines. So their product, of degree $3q+3>D$, divides $\Phi$, and $\Phi=0$. $\square$

These are the first three cases of the proof of part (a) of the proposition in the fixed-field theorem, whose other factors of $\Psi_q$ are not needed here.

**Lemma D (detection in each number of letters $Z$).** Let $m\le2q+4$, $P\in\mathcal W_m$, and $k\ge0$ with $P^{(k)}\ne0$. Then $\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(k)})(v)\ne0$ for some $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ with $|S|=k$ and some $v\in(\mathbb F_q^2)^{k+1}$.

*Proof.* Put $f=\kappa(P^{(k)})$, which is nonzero and homogeneous of degree $D=m-k$, and suppose that every multihomogeneous component of $f$ vanishes on $(\mathbb F_q^2)^{k+1}$. If $k\le1$, the cases $k=0$ and $k=1$ of the proof of Lemma 4 of the prime-field theorem give $f=0$, since they use only the test vectors $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. If $k=2$, then $f=\Phi(d_1,d_2)$ by (W1), (Z) holds for all $v\in(\mathbb F_q^2)^3$, and $D=m-2\le2q+2$, so $\Phi=0$ by Lemma C. If $k\ge3$, then $D\le2q+1$, and $f\in\mathcal T_k$ by (W1), so $Ef=E'f=0$, and $f=0$ by Lemma B. Each case contradicts $f\ne0$. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** Let $m\le2q+4$ and $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$. By Lemma 2 of the prime-field theorem, $\mathrm{in}_m(g)=\bar\mu(P)$ with $P\in\mathcal W_m$. By Jennings' theorem, as quoted in the Setting of [induced subgraphs decide the property](small-induced-subgraphs-decide-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md), $\mathrm{in}_m(g)\ne0$, so $P\ne0$ and some $P^{(k)}\ne0$. Lemma D gives $S$ and $v$ over $\mathbb F_q$ with $\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(k)})(v)\ne0$. For $\rho=\rho_{S,v}$, (E) gives $\kappa_\rho(\mathrm{in}_m(g))\ne0$, so $\rho(g)=1+\kappa_\rho(\mathrm{in}_m(g))E_{1,m+1}\ne1$ by formula (1) of the invisible-ideal theorem. This proves detection in every degree $m\le2q+4$, and Lemma 1 of the invisible-ideal theorem gives the property over $\mathbb F_q$ for $n\le2q+5$. Over a finite field $\mathbb F\supseteq\mathbb F_q$ it follows because $U_n(\mathbb F_q)\subseteq U_n(\mathbb F)$, and every continuous homomorphism to $U_n(\mathbb F)$ kills $G_{(n)}$, by Step 1 of the counterexample. $\square$

The corollary follows from the case $q=p$ and Corollary 1 of the counterexample.

**Proof of the proposition.** By (E) and formula (1), $\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(k)})(v)=0$ for every $S$ and every $v$ over $\mathbb F_q$. The cases $k\le1$ of Lemma D give $P^{(0)}=P^{(1)}=0$. For $k=2$, (Z) holds for all $v\in(\mathbb F_q^2)^3$. From (Z) alone, the proof of part (a) of the proposition in the fixed-field theorem, from its choice of $v_0,v_1,v_2$ for each factor $\ell$ of $\Psi_q$ onwards, shows that the polynomial $\Psi_q$ of degree $N(q)$ divides $\Phi$. As $\deg\Phi=m-2<N(q)$, $\Phi=0$, so $P^{(2)}=0$. For $k\ge3$ with $m-k\le2q+1$, Lemma B applies as in Lemma D, and $P^{(k)}=0$. As $P\ne0$ by Jennings' theorem, some $k$ with $3\le k\le m-2q-2$ remains, so $m\ge2q+5$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What is new in the argument.* The case $k\ge3$ of Lemma 4 of the prime-field theorem uses only $E$, and needs $D\le2p$, so that no monomial is shared by two runs. In the top degree $D=2q+1$, two runs can share the monomials $Y_l^qY_{l'}^q$, and $E$ alone does not decide. For instance $f=\Lambda(a_0)Y_1^q-\Lambda(a_1)Y_0^q$ has $Ef=0$, and both of its components vanish at the $\mathbb F_q$-points, but $E'f=X_0^qY_1^q-X_1^qY_0^q\ne0$. Using $E'$ as well removes these. The last step of the prime-field proof also needs $q=p$: it shows that the $X_l$-adic valuation $j$ of $g_l$ satisfies $j\equiv-1\pmod p$, and then uses $j\le\deg g_l\le p-1$ to get $j=p-1=\deg g_l$. For $q>p$ only $j\le q-1$ is available, which does not force $j=\deg g_l$. Step (iii) of Lemma B replaces it by divisibility by $\Lambda(a_l)$, which works for every $q$.
- *Where the proof stops.* For $m=2q+5$ all cases except $k=3$ still go through, the case $k=2$ by Lemma C or the proposition. For $k=3$, $D=2q+2$, and a component of multidegree with two entries $q+1$ may be divisible by $\Lambda(a_l)\Lambda(a_{l'})$. Lemma B is sharp as stated. The computer check below found nonzero forms with $Ef=E'f=0$ and vanishing components for $(q,k,D)=(2,3,6)$ and $(4,1,10)$. So going further needs more than the two derivatives, for instance the full invariance $f\in\mathcal T_k$, which in characteristic $p$ is stronger, or the bracket structure of initial forms.
- *Computer check.* This is not part of the proof. For each $(q,k,D)$ below, a calculation over $\mathbb F_q$ computed the space of homogeneous $f$ of degree $D$ in $k+1$ pairs whose multihomogeneous components vanish at all $\mathbb F_q$-points, and then the subspace with $Ef=0$, or with $Ef=E'f=0$. With both derivatives the subspace was $0$ for $(q,k,D)=(2,1,5)$, $(2,2,5)$, $(2,3,5)$, $(3,1,7)$, $(3,2,7)$, $(3,3,7)$, $(4,1,9)$, $(4,2,9)$, $(5,1,11)$, $(5,2,11)$, $(8,1,17)$ and $(9,1,19)$, as Lemma B says. With $E$ alone it had dimension $3$ for $(2,1,5)$, $(4,1,9)$ and $(8,1,17)$, $6$ for $(2,2,5)$, and $1$ for $(3,1,7)$ and $(5,1,11)$. This matches steps (i) and (ii) without $E'$: they leave $g_l^0=\sum_{l'\ne l}c_{l,l'}Y_{l'}^q$ with $c_{l',l}=-c_{l,l'}$, and step (iii) without $E'$ leaves $u_l=c_l(X_l^q-X_lY_l^{q-1})$ with $2c_l=0$.
- *What remains open.* Over $\mathbb F_q$ the property is not decided for $2q+6\le n<N(q)+4$, or $N(q)+3$ if $p=2$. By the proposition, a failure at such an $n$ with $n\le N(q)+2$ needs an element of some degree $m\le n-1$, missed by $U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_q)$, whose initial form has between $3$ and $m-2q-2\le n-2q-3$ letters $z$.

**Status.** The theorem, the corollary, the proposition and the lemmas are proved here. They were not found in the sources opened for [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md), and no separate literature search was made. The calculations in the remarks are not part of the proof.
