**Setting.** Let $k$ be a number field with algebraic closure $\bar k$, let $p$ be a prime, and fix an embedding $\iota_v : \bar k \to \mathbb{C}_p$. Let $k_v$ be the closure of $k$ in $\mathbb{C}_p$, so that $G_{k_v}$ is identified with the decomposition group in $G_k$ of the place $v$ of $\bar k$ given by $\iota_v$. For a smooth geometrically connected $k$-variety $X$, let $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ be the Berkovich analytic space attached to $X_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ [@andre2003, §7.1].

An étale covering $\mathcal{Y} \to X^{\mathrm{an}}$, possibly of infinite degree, is *tempered* if there are a finite étale covering $\mathcal{T} \to X^{\mathrm{an}}$ and a possibly infinite topological covering $\mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{T}$ that factors through $\mathcal{Y}$ over $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ [@andre2003, §4.3]. The tempered fundamental group $\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ classifies tempered coverings. It is a prodiscrete topological group whose profinite completion is $\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\mathbb{C}_p}) = \pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k})$ [@andre2003, Proposition 4.4.1 and §7.1]. André explains why neither the topological nor de Jong's étale fundamental group is a close analogue of the fundamental group of a complex manifold: the first is trivial for $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{C}_p} \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, and the second is huge even for $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ [@andre2003, §4.2]. For an elliptic curve $E_j$ with invariant $j$, the tempered group is $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \times \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ if $|j|_p \le 1$ and $\mathbb{Z} \times \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ if $|j|_p > 1$, so it detects bad reduction [@andre2003, §4.6]. For a curve with good reduction, the map from the tempered group to the product of the maximal prime-to-$p$ and pro-$p$ quotients of $\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}$ is surjective. André concludes that the properties of the tempered group used below are lost in those quotients [@andre2003, §4.7 and Proposition 4.7.1].

Let $X$ be a curve. Then $\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ embeds into $\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k})$ [@andre2003, §4.5], and $G_{k_v}$ acts on it by outer automorphisms, compatibly with the outer action of $G_k$ on $\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k})$ [@andre2003, Proposition 5.1.1 and §5.2]. If $X$ is hyperbolic, $\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ is its own normalizer in $\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k})$, and $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ embeds into $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k})$ [@andre2003, Proposition 6.2.1 and Corollary 6.2.2]. Write $\overline{\mathrm{Out}}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ for its closure. If $X$ is affine and hyperbolic, then $G_k$ also embeds into $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k})$, by the [injectivity theorem](injectivity-of-outer-galois-representations.md) of Belyi and Matsumoto [@andre2003, Theorem 2.3.1]. So $G_{k_v}$ lies in both $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ and $G_k$.

**Theorem (André).** Let $X$ be an affine hyperbolic curve over $k$.

1. If $X = \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, then $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}}) \cap G_k = \overline{\mathrm{Out}}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}}) \cap G_k = G_{k_v}$ [@andre2003, Theorem 7.2.1].
2. If some finite étale covering of $X_{\bar k}$ admits a nonconstant rational function omitting at least three values, then $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}}) \cap G_k = G_{k_v}$. If moreover the base point $x$ is $k$-rational, then $G_{k_v}$ is the subgroup of $G_k$ whose action on $\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k}, x)$ preserves $\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}}, x)$ [@andre2003, Theorem 7.2.3].

The hypothesis of part 2 holds for an elliptic curve minus one point. André mentions heuristic arguments suggesting that it fails for some affine hyperbolic curves [@andre2003, §3.3, Remarks 1 and 2]. Both parts depend on $\iota_v$ only through the place $v$ [@andre2003, §7.2, Remark 3]. For $X = \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, the image of $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ is not dense in any open subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k})$ [@andre2003, Corollary 7.2.2].

*Proof of part 1.* Let $\sigma \in G_k$ lie in the closure. André shows that $|j|_p \le 1$ implies $|\sigma(j)|_p \le 1$ for every $j \in \bar k \setminus \{0,1\}$. By Belyi's theorem, there is a morphism $E_j \to \mathbb{P}^1$ unramified outside $0, 1, \infty$. Since the outer action of $\sigma$ is approximated by automorphisms of the tempered group of $X^{\mathrm{an}}$, one obtains an isomorphism between the tempered groups of $E_j$ and of its conjugate $E_{\sigma(j)}$. The tempered group detects good reduction, as above, so $E_{\sigma(j)}$ has good reduction when $E_j$ does [@andre2003, proof of Theorem 7.2.1]. The rest is standard and checked here. The elements of the intersection form a group, so $\sigma^{-1}$ has the same property. Hence $\sigma$ maps the valuation ring $\{j \in \bar k : |j|_p \le 1\}$ onto itself, so $\sigma$ fixes $v$ and lies in its decomposition group $G_{k_v}$.

*Part 2.* Part 2 follows from part 1 by passing to finite extensions of $k$, to finite étale coverings of $X$, and along a dominant map from a covering to $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$ [@andre2003, Lemma 7.2.4]. One step uses that a closed subgroup of $G_k$ containing $G_{k_v}$ with finite index equals $G_{k_v}$. André attributes this to Neukirch and cites Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's book [@andre2003, Sublemma 7.2.5], which has not been opened for this entry.

**The archimedean analogue.** Fix $\iota : \bar k \to \mathbb{C}$ and let $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ be the complex manifold of $X_{\mathbb{C}}$. For an affine hyperbolic curve, $\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ is free of finite rank at least $2$. It embeds into its profinite completion $\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k})$, and so does its outer automorphism group, because a free group of rank greater than $1$ is its own normalizer in its profinite completion [@andre2003, §3.2 and Lemma 3.2.1]. If $\iota(k) \subset \mathbb{R}$, complex conjugation gives $G_{\mathbb{R}} \subseteq \mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(X^{\mathrm{an}}) \cap G_k$ [@andre2003, §3.1].

3. For $X = \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, the intersection of $G_k$ with $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$, or with its closure, is $G_{\mathbb{R}}$ if $\iota(k) \subset \mathbb{R}$ and trivial otherwise [@andre2003, Theorem 3.3.1].
4. Under the hypothesis of part 2, and if $\iota(k) \subset \mathbb{R}$, then $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}(X^{\mathrm{an}}) \cap G_k = G_{\mathbb{R}}$ [@andre2003, Theorem 3.3.2]. André reports that Tamagawa and a referee pointed out that part 4 also follows from a theorem of Matsumoto and Tamagawa comparing mapping class group and Galois actions, without the hypothesis on rational functions [@andre2003, §3.3, footnote 12].

*Proof of part 3.* This follows André's proof, and every step is checked here. One may take $k = \mathbb{Q}$. Write $c$ for complex conjugation. By Nielsen's theorem, which André cites, $\mathrm{Out}(F_2) \to GL_2(\mathbb{Z})$, the action on the abelianization, is an isomorphism [@andre2003, proof of Theorem 3.3.1]. Complex conjugation reverses the orientation of $\mathbb{C}$, so it sends small loops around $0$ and $1$ to their inverses in homology, and its image in $GL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is $-1$. As $-1$ is central and centralizers are closed, every element of the closure commutes with $c$ in $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})$, and so, by Belyi's injectivity, every element $g$ of the intersection with $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ commutes with $c$ in $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Then $g$ maps the real closed field $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{alg}} = \bar{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \mathbb{R}$ fixed by $c$ onto itself. A field automorphism of $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{alg}}$ maps nonzero squares, which are the positive elements, to nonzero squares, so it preserves the ordering. It fixes $\mathbb{Q}$, which is dense in $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{alg}}$, so it is the identity. Hence $g \in \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{alg}}) = \{1, c\}$. André cites the self-centralizing property of $G_{\mathbb{R}}$ from the same book instead.

**The local Grothendieck–Teichmüller group.** For $r \ge 4$, let $\mathcal{M}_{0,r}$ be the moduli space of genus $0$ curves with $r$ ordered marked points. Let $\Gamma^{\mathrm{temp}}_{0,r}$ be the image of $\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(\mathcal{M}_{0,r,\mathbb{C}_p}^{\mathrm{an}})$ in its profinite completion, which is $\Gamma^{\mathrm{alg}}_{0,r} = \pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{M}_{0,r,\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})$ [@andre2003, §§8.4–8.5]. Let $GT_p^{(r)}$ be the group of outer automorphisms of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{temp}}_{0,r}$ that commute with the symmetric group $\mathfrak{S}_r$ and, for a single $\lambda \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$, send the conjugacy class of the local monodromy at each boundary component to its $\lambda$-th power. It embeds into the analogous group $\widehat{GT}^{(r)}$ for $\Gamma^{\mathrm{alg}}_{0,r}$, and $\widehat{GT}^{(r)} \cong \widehat{GT}$ for $r \ge 5$ by the theorem of Harbater and Schneps [@andre2003, Theorem 8.4.1, §8.6 and Proposition 8.6.2], recorded in the [definition of $\widehat{GT}$](grothendieck-teichmuller-group.md). Write $\widehat{GT}_p^{(r)}$ for the closure of $GT_p^{(r)}$ in $\widehat{GT}^{(r)}$. André defines $GT_p$ as the intersection of the images of all $GT_p^{(r)}$ in $GT_p^{(4)}$, and the *local Grothendieck–Teichmüller group at $p$* as its closure $\widehat{GT}_p$, a closed subgroup of $\widehat{GT}$ [@andre2003, Notation 8.6.1, Definition 8.6.3 and §8.6]. Here $\widehat{GT}_p$ is a closure, not a profinite completion [@andre2003, Notation 8.6.1].

5. $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = G_{\mathbb{Q}} \cap GT_p = G_{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \widehat{GT}_p$, and $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = G_{\mathbb{Q}} \cap GT_p^{(r)} = G_{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \widehat{GT}_p^{(r)}$ for every $r \ge 4$. This follows from part 1, because $\widehat{GT}_p^{(4)}$ lies in $\overline{\mathrm{Out}}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}$ of $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{C}_p} \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$ [@andre2003, Theorem 8.7.1 and its proof].
6. If $G_{\mathbb{Q}} = \widehat{GT}$, then $GT_p$, $\widehat{GT}_p$, $GT_p^{(r)}$ and $\widehat{GT}_p^{(r)}$ all equal $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ for $r \ge 5$. In particular, $GT_p^{(5)}$ would be a finitely generated profinite group [@andre2003, Corollary 8.7.2]. André finds it tempting to try to disprove this, and notes that it is not known whether $\mathrm{Out}\,\pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{C}_p} \setminus \{0,1,\infty\})$ is profinite [@andre2003, §8.7, after Corollary 8.7.2, and §6.2, Remark after Corollary 6.2.2].
7. The archimedean analogues $GT_\infty$, $\widehat{GT}_\infty$, $GT^{(r)}_\infty$ and $\widehat{GT}^{(r)}_\infty$, defined with $\mathbb{C}$ and $\pi_1^{\mathrm{top}}$, all equal $G_{\mathbb{R}}$ for $r \ge 4$. The proof is the argument for part 3, with the fact that $G_{\mathbb{R}}$ is its own centralizer in $\widehat{GT}$, which André attributes to Lochak and Schneps [@andre2003, §8.7, Remark 5].

So the archimedean analogue of the equality predicted by the [Grothendieck–Teichmüller conjecture](grothendieck-teichmuller-conjecture.md) holds. At $p$, the equality $\widehat{GT}_p = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ would follow from the conjecture by part 6, and the sources opened here do not prove it. André also shows that Drinfeld's presentation of $\widehat{GT}$, through local monodromies at $0$ and $1$, has no tempered analogue: for $p = 2$ one cannot choose local monodromies $x, y, z$ at $0, 1, \infty$ in the tempered group with $xyz = 1$ [@andre2003, §8.7, Remark 6].

**$G_{k_v}$ as an automorphism group.** Let $\mathcal{V}_k$ be the category of smooth geometrically connected $k$-varieties, and $\mathcal{T}$ the category of separated topological groups with continuous homomorphisms up to inner automorphisms. The functors $X \mapsto \pi_1^{\mathrm{alg}}(X_{\bar k})$ and $X \mapsto \pi_1^{\mathrm{temp}}(X^{\mathrm{an}})$ from $\mathcal{V}_k$ to $\mathcal{T}$ give homomorphisms $G_k \to \mathrm{Aut}\,\underline{\pi}^{\mathrm{alg}}_{\bar k}$ and $G_{k_v} \to \mathrm{Aut}\,\underline{\pi}^{\mathrm{temp}}_{\mathbb{C}_p}$ [@andre2003, §9.1]. Pop showed, in unpublished work, that the first is an isomorphism, as Oda and Matsumoto had conjectured [@andre2003, Theorem 9.2.1]. As a consequence of Pop's theorem and part 1, André proves that the second is an isomorphism [@andre2003, Theorem 9.2.2]. This statement therefore rests on an unpublished result. It is a local analogue of the proved variants listed under the [Grothendieck–Teichmüller conjecture](grothendieck-teichmuller-conjecture.md).

**Retraction onto $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$.** Let $X = \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{C}_p} \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, with étale fundamental group $\Pi_X$ and tempered fundamental group $\Pi_X^{\mathrm{tp}}$. Tsujimura puts $GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}} = \widehat{GT} \cap \mathrm{Out}(\Pi_X^{\mathrm{tp}})$ inside $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi_X)$ [@tsujimura2020, Definition 2.1 and Remark 2.1.1]. It is a closed subgroup of $\widehat{GT}$, and $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \subseteq GT_p \subseteq GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}}$ [@tsujimura2020, Remarks 2.1.2 and 2.1.3].

- *Tsujimura.* $GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}}$ acts naturally on $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ by field automorphisms that are continuous for the $p$-adic topology. This gives a surjection $\phi : GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}} \to G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ whose restriction to $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ is the identity [@tsujimura2020, Corollaries 2.3 and 2.4 and the proof of Corollary 2.4]. The key input is that the tempered group of $Y \setminus \{y\}$, together with its map to that of $Y$, determines $y \in Y(\mathbb{C}_p)$, for any connected finite étale covering $Y \to X$. Its proof uses [resolution of nonsingularities](resolution-of-nonsingularities.md) results of Tamagawa and Lepage [@tsujimura2020, Theorem 2.2 and the paragraph before it]. Over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, Tsujimura does not know whether $\widehat{GT}$ itself acts naturally on $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@tsujimura2020, Introduction, p. 6].
- *Mochizuki and Tsujimura.* In a preprint, they prove that the various $p$-adic versions of $\widehat{GT}$ in the literature coincide. The metrized Grothendieck–Teichmüller group $GT^{\mathrm{M}}$ equals $GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}}$, so $GT^{\mathrm{M}} = GT_p = GT^{\mathrm{G}} = GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}}$, where $GT_p$ is André's group. This group is commensurably terminal in $\widehat{GT}$, meaning that it equals its commensurator there [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Introduction, Theorem G; Theorem 3.16 and Corollary 3.17]. Their proof starts from $\phi$: since $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \subseteq GT^{\mathrm{M}}$, it suffices to show that $\ker\phi \subseteq GT^{\mathrm{M}}$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, proof of Theorem 3.16]. The action of $GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}}$ on $X(\mathbb{C}_p)$ factors through $\phi$, and $X(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p)$ is the set of points fixed by some open subgroup of $GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}}$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Proposition 3.18 and its proof]. The groups $GT^{\mathrm{M}}$ and $GT^{\mathrm{G}}$ come from earlier work in combinatorial anabelian geometry, cited there as [CbTpIII], which has not been opened for this entry.

*Consequences, checked here.*

- $G_{\mathbb{Q}} \cap GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}} = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$. Indeed, $G_{\mathbb{Q}} \subseteq \widehat{GT}$ inside $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi_X)$, so $G_{\mathbb{Q}} \cap GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}} = G_{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \mathrm{Out}(\Pi_X^{\mathrm{tp}})$. This is $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ by part 1 for $k = \mathbb{Q}$. Tsujimura's embedding of $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi_X^{\mathrm{tp}})$ into $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi_X)$ is André's [@tsujimura2020, Remark 2.1.1].
- Put $N = \ker\phi$, which is normal in $GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}}$. Since $\phi$ is the identity on $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, we have $N \cap G_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = 1$, and $g\,\phi(g)^{-1} \in N$ for every $g$. So $GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}} = N \rtimes G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ as an abstract group. By the first point, $N \cap G_{\mathbb{Q}} \subseteq N \cap G_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = 1$.
- If $G_{\mathbb{Q}} = \widehat{GT}$, then $GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}} = G_{\mathbb{Q}} \cap GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}} = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, so $N = 1$. Thus a nontrivial element of $N$ would refute the Grothendieck–Teichmüller conjecture. By the equalities of Mochizuki and Tsujimura, $N = 1$ is equivalent to André's $GT_p$ being $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$.

**Status.** Whether $N = 1$, that is, whether $GT_p^{\mathrm{tp}} = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, is not settled in the sources opened here. Mochizuki and Tsujimura's paper is a preprint.
