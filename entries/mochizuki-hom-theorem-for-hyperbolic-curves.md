**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime. A field is *sub-$p$-adic* if it is isomorphic to a subfield of a finitely generated field extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@mochizuki1999, Definition 15.4 (i)]. Examples are the finitely generated extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, number fields, and the compositum in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ of all number fields of degree at most $n$ [@mochizuki1999, p. 77].

Every field $F$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ is sub-$p$-adic for every $p$. This is checked here. Choose a transcendence basis $t_1, \dots, t_d$, so that $F$ is finite over $E = \mathbb{Q}(t_1, \dots, t_d)$, which embeds in $E' = \mathbb{Q}_p(t_1, \dots, t_d)$. Write $F = E[x]/(h)$ with $h$ irreducible over $E$, and let $h_1$ be an irreducible factor of $h$ over $E'$. Then $x \mapsto x$ defines a map $F \to E'[x]/(h_1)$, which is injective because $F$ is a field, and $E'[x]/(h_1)$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

A *hyperbolic pro-curve* over $K$ is a projective limit of [hyperbolic curves](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $K$ with birational transition morphisms [@mochizuki1999, Definition 15.4 (ii)]. A *smooth pro-variety* is a projective limit of smooth geometrically connected $K$-varieties with birational transition morphisms [@mochizuki1999, Definition 16.4]. Hyperbolic curves, and spectra of function fields of dimension one, are hyperbolic pro-curves. Smooth varieties, and spectra of function fields of any dimension, are smooth pro-varieties [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, remark (2) after Theorem A]. The groups $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X \supseteq \Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_X$ and their pro-$p$ versions $\Pi_X \supseteq \Delta_X$ are as in the entry on hyperbolic curves.

**Theorem A.** Let $K$ be sub-$p$-adic, let $X$ be a smooth pro-variety over $K$, and let $Y$ be a hyperbolic pro-curve over $K$. Let $\mathrm{Hom}^{\mathrm{dom}}_K(X, Y)$ be the set of dominant $K$-morphisms $X \to Y$. Let $\mathrm{Hom}^{\mathrm{open}}_{G_K}(\Pi_X, \Pi_Y)$ be the set of open continuous homomorphisms $\Pi_X \to \Pi_Y$ over $G_K$, modulo composition with inner automorphisms by elements of $\Delta_Y$, and define $\mathrm{Hom}^{\mathrm{open}}_{G_K}(\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X, \Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_Y)$ in the same way with $\Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_Y$. Then the natural maps
$$\mathrm{Hom}^{\mathrm{dom}}_K(X, Y) \to \mathrm{Hom}^{\mathrm{open}}_{G_K}(\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X, \Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_Y) \to \mathrm{Hom}^{\mathrm{open}}_{G_K}(\Pi_X, \Pi_Y)$$
are bijective [@mochizuki1999, Theorem A and Theorem 16.5]. The substance is the bijectivity of the composite, and the bijectivity of the first map follows formally from it [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, remark (1) after Theorem A, and Remark after Theorem 16.5].

**The Grothendieck conjecture for hyperbolic curves.** Let $K$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, and let $X, Y$ be hyperbolic curves, or spectra of function fields of dimension one, over $K$. Then the $K$-isomorphisms $X \to Y$ correspond bijectively to the isomorphisms $\Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_X \to \Pi^{\mathrm{prf}}_Y$ over $G_K$ modulo $\Delta^{\mathrm{prf}}_Y$, and also to the isomorphisms $\Pi_X \to \Pi_Y$ over $G_K$ modulo $\Delta_Y$. Mochizuki notes that many authors mean exactly this by "the Grothendieck conjecture", and that it is a special case of Theorem A [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, remark (3) after Theorem A]. The deduction is checked here. By the paragraph above, $K$ is sub-$p$-adic. If $\theta$ is an isomorphism with inverse $\theta'$, Theorem A gives dominant morphisms $\phi : X \to Y$ and $\phi' : Y \to X$ inducing them. Then $\phi' \circ \phi$ and $\mathrm{id}_X$ both induce the class of $\theta' \circ \theta = \mathrm{id}$, so they are equal by injectivity, and likewise $\phi \circ \phi' = \mathrm{id}_Y$.

**History.** Mochizuki's historical overview credits Nakamura with the genus-$0$ case over number fields, Pop with the birational case, and Tamagawa with the affine case in early 1995; Mochizuki's earlier work on proper hyperbolic curves over number fields removed the affineness hypothesis in characteristic $0$ [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, pp. 5–6]. For precise scope, [Tamagawa's theorem](tamagawa-grothendieck-conjecture-for-affine-curves.md) gives the relative isomorphism correspondence over fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ [@tamagawa1997, Theorem (0.3), pp. 136–137]. Its positive-characteristic statements reconstruct affine curves over finite fields as schemes, with no base field fixed [@tamagawa1997, Theorems (0.5)–(0.6), p. 138].

Positive characteristic needs a Frobenius qualification. For a hyperbolic curve $X/k$ in characteristic $p>0$, relative Frobenius identifies the fundamental groups of $X$ and its twists $X^{(m)}$, $m\ge0$, over $G_k$ [@stix2002thesis, §4.1.2, pp. 61–62]. For nonisotrivial $X$ these twists are pairwise nonisomorphic over $k$ [@stix2002thesis, Corollary B.2.4, p. 106]. Over fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{F}_p$, [Stix's isomorphism correspondence](grothendieck-conjecture-over-finitely-generated-fields-of-characteristic-p.md) inverts relative Frobenius and assumes at least one curve is not isotrivial [@stix2002thesis, Theorem 5.1.1 and Corollary 5.1.2, p. 71]. The isotrivial case requires a modified correspondence [@stix2002thesis, Remark (1) following Corollary 5.1.2, p. 72].

In characteristic $0$, Theorem A extends Tamagawa's affine result to proper curves and pro-curves, pro-$p$ geometric fundamental groups, dominant morphisms instead of isomorphisms, a source of any dimension that need not be hyperbolic, and any sub-$p$-adic base field [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, pp. 6–7]. Mochizuki regards the last generalization as the most important: in his view the Grothendieck conjecture for hyperbolic curves is a local result in $p$-adic Hodge theory, not a global one akin to the Tate conjecture [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, p. 7].

**Local to global.** For curves, the case of a number field is deduced from the local case. For $K$ inside a finitely generated extension $L$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the proof base-changes to $L$, where the result is proved first, and then descends the morphism to $K$. Descent works because morphisms between hyperbolic curves have no moduli, and because a morphism is determined by its effect on geometric fundamental groups [@mochizuki1999, Corollary 15.5 and its proof]. For a number field, $L$ can be its completion at a prime above $p$.

**Further results in the same paper.**

- *Function fields (Theorem B).* Let $L, M$ be function fields of any dimension over a sub-$p$-adic field $K$ that is algebraically closed in both. Then $K$-morphisms $\mathrm{Spec}\, L \to \mathrm{Spec}\, M$ correspond bijectively to open continuous homomorphisms $G_L \to G_M$ over $G_K$, modulo inner automorphisms by elements of $\ker(G_M \to G_K)$ [@mochizuki1999, Theorem B and Theorem 17.1]. In characteristic $0$ this generalizes Pop's results, which needed the homomorphisms to be isomorphisms and $K$ to be finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, p. 4]. The proof is an induction on the transcendence degree of $M$ through intermediate function fields. It needs profinite groups, because maximal pro-$p$ quotients are not well behaved in fibrations [@mochizuki1999, proof of Theorem 17.1 and the Remark after it]. This is the relative form recorded in [Pop's theorem for finitely generated fields](pop-mochizuki-finitely-generated-fields.md). Corry–Pop later proved a [geometrically pro-$p$ version in every dimension](birational-pro-p-hom-theorem.md) using a different argument [@corrypop2009, §1.2, p. 3].
- *Truncation (Theorem A′).* Put $\Delta\{0\} = \Delta$ and let $\Delta\{i\}$ be the closed commutator subgroup of $\Delta\{i-1\}$, with $\Pi^n = \Pi/\Delta\{n\}$ and $\Delta^n = \Delta/\Delta\{n\}$. Let $K$ be sub-$p$-adic, $X$ a smooth variety and $Y$ a hyperbolic curve over $K$, and $n \ge 5$. Then every open continuous homomorphism $\theta : \Pi^n_X \to \Pi^n_Y$ over $G_K$ induces a dominant morphism $X \to Y$. The map it induces on geometric fundamental groups agrees, up to inner automorphisms by elements of $\Delta_Y$, with the map $\Delta^{n-3}_X \to \Delta^{n-3}_Y$ induced by $\theta$ [@mochizuki1999, Theorem A′ and Theorem 18.1]. Theorem A″ treats pro-objects, with a larger loss [@mochizuki1999, Theorem 18.2]. The key Hodge–Tate step uses only the quotient $\Delta_X/[\Delta_X, [\Delta_X, \Delta_X]]$ [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, p. 13], and Mochizuki does not claim that the bounds are optimal [@mochizuki1999, Remark after Theorem 18.2].
- *Sections (Theorem C).* For a hyperbolic curve $X$ over a sub-$p$-adic field $K$, the map from $X(K)$ to the set of sections of $\Pi_X \to G_K$ modulo conjugation by $\Delta_X$ is injective [@mochizuki1999, Theorem C and Theorem 19.1]. In the proof, two points $a, b \in X(K)$ with the same section give hyperbolic curves $X \setminus \{a\}$ and $X \setminus \{b\}$ with compatible outer Galois actions. These curves are then isomorphic compatibly with their inclusions into $X$, which forces $a = b$ [@mochizuki1999, proof of Theorem 19.1]. Only the isomorphism version, from Mochizuki's earlier preprint, is needed here [@mochizuki1999, Remark after Theorem 19.1]. See [injectivity in the section conjecture](section-conjecture-injectivity.md) for the profinite consequence.
- *Surfaces (Theorem D).* An isomorphism version holds for profinite fundamental groups of hyperbolically fibred surfaces over sub-$p$-adic fields [@mochizuki1999, Theorem D and Theorem a2.4]. Its proof needs the Hom version of Theorem A for curves, not just the isomorphism version [@mochizuki1999, Appendix, remark (1) after Theorem D].

**Outline of the proof.** This follows Mochizuki's own summary [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, pp. 7–12]. The core case has $K$ finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $X$ and $Y$ proper hyperbolic curves, and $\theta : \Pi_X \to \Pi_Y$ surjective over $G_K$. Sections 1–14 prove a version with the function field of $X$ as source, §15 extends it to sub-$p$-adic $K$, and §16 to sources of higher dimension.

1. *Differentials from Galois data.* The $p$-adic étale cohomology of $X \otimes_K \bar K$ is the group cohomology of $\Delta_X$, so $\theta$ induces a $G_K$-equivariant injection $H^1(Y \otimes_K \bar K, \mathbb{Q}_p) \hookrightarrow H^1(X \otimes_K \bar K, \mathbb{Q}_p)$. By [Hodge–Tate comparison](hodge-tate-decomposition.md), tensoring with $\mathbb{C}_p(1)$ and taking $G_K$-invariants recovers the spaces of global differentials. So $\theta$ gives a $K$-linear injection $H^0(Y, \omega_Y) \hookrightarrow H^0(X, \omega_X)$, and hence a rational map between the canonical projective spaces, after passing to coverings that are not hyperelliptic [@mochizuki1999, Introduction, pp. 8–9].
2. *Preservation of relations.* It remains to show that this map carries $X$ into $Y$, that is, that $\theta$ respects the relations among products of differentials defining $Y$ in its canonical embedding. This reduces to the following. Let $L$ be the fraction field of the completed local ring of a stable model of $X$ at the generic point of a component of its special fibre. The resulting $L$-point of $X$ gives a homomorphism $G_L \to \Pi_X$, and its composite with $\theta$ must be shown to come from an $L$-point of $Y$.
3. *Geometricity.* This takes four steps. First, for a $K$-point of $X$, the arithmetic first Chern class of the associated line bundle can be computed from the induced section of $\Pi_X \to G_K$. Applying the same recipe to its composite with $\theta$ again gives the class of a line bundle on $Y$. The reason is that a certain quotient of the Malcev Lie algebra of $\Delta_X$, tensored with $\mathbb{C}_p$, is Hodge–Tate when the section comes from a point, this property passes to the corresponding quotient for $Y$, and it forces the class to come from a line bundle (§§1–5). Second, the same is shown for $L$-points (§§6–7). Third, a line bundle of degree prime to $p$ yields a rational point over a tamely ramified extension (§8). Fourth, applying this along a tower of coverings and using Faltings' $p$-adic Hodge theory, these points converge $p$-adically to an $L$-point that induces the given section (§§9–10).
