**Setting.** Fix a prime $p$ and a finite field $\mathbb F$ of characteristic $p$. For a finite simple graph $\Gamma=(V,E)$, let $G_\Gamma$ be its right-angled Artin pro-$p$ group, as in [right-angled Artin pro-$p$ groups with the kernel-unipotent property](kernel-unipotent-property-for-right-angled-artin-pro-p-groups.md). A continuous homomorphism from $G_\Gamma$ to a pro-$p$ group is the same as a map on $V$ sending adjacent vertices to commuting elements. For $S\subseteq V$ write $\Gamma[S]$ for the induced subgraph and $G_S=G_{\Gamma[S]}$, with $G_\emptyset=1$. Let $\sigma_S:G_S\to G_\Gamma$ and $\tau_S:G_\Gamma\to G_S$ be the continuous homomorphisms that fix every vertex of $S$, with $\tau_S$ killing $V\setminus S$. They are defined by the argument in the proof of 5 implies 1 in that entry, and $\tau_S\sigma_S=\mathrm{id}$ because it fixes the generators. Put $e_S=\sigma_S\tau_S$, the continuous endomorphism of $G_\Gamma$ that fixes $S$ and kills $V\setminus S$.

A finitely generated pro-$p$ group $H$ has the *kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$* if $H_{(n)}=\bigcap_\rho\ker\rho$ over all continuous $\rho:H\to U_n(\mathbb F)$, where $H_{(n)}$ is the [$p$-Zassenhaus filtration](p-zassenhaus-filtration.md). Over $\mathbb F_p$ this is the property in the [kernel-unipotent conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md). The inclusion $\subseteq$ always holds, by item 3 of the filtration entry. As $U_n(\mathbb F_p)\subseteq U_n(\mathbb F)$, the property over $\mathbb F_p$ implies the property over $\mathbb F$.

*Graded algebra.* As in the Setting of [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md), let $I_H$ be the augmentation ideal of the group algebra $\mathbb F_p[H]$ of the underlying abstract group, let $A(H)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}A_k(H)$ with $A_k(H)=I_H^k/I_H^{k+1}$, and put $\mathrm{in}_k(h)=(h-1)+I_H^{k+1}$ for $h\in H_{(k)}$. By Jennings' theorem, $h-1\in I_H^k$ for $h\in H_{(k)}$, and $\mathrm{in}_k(h)\ne0$ if moreover $h\notin H_{(k+1)}$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 3.4, p. 9]. The identity $gh-1=(g-1)+(h-1)+(g-1)(h-1)$ makes $\mathrm{in}_k:H_{(k)}\to A_k(H)$ a homomorphism, and by Jennings' theorem it kills $H_{(k+1)}$. A continuous homomorphism $f:H\to H'$ extends linearly to an algebra homomorphism $\mathbb F_p[H]\to\mathbb F_p[H']$ taking $h-1$ to $f(h)-1$. So it maps $I_H^k$ into $I_{H'}^k$ and induces a graded algebra homomorphism $A(f)$ with $A(f)(\mathrm{in}_k(h))=\mathrm{in}_k(f(h))$, where $f(h)\in H'_{(k)}$ by item 1 of the filtration entry. For a continuous $\rho:H\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$, the corner functional $\kappa_\rho$ on $A_k(H)^{\mathbb F}$ of that Setting is $\kappa_\rho(u+I_H^{k+1})=\tilde\rho(u)_{1,k+1}$, with $\tilde\rho$ the linear extension of $\rho$, and its formula (1) says that $\rho(h)=1+\kappa_\rho(\mathrm{in}_k(h))E_{1,k+1}$ for $h\in H_{(k)}$.

For $G=G_\Gamma$, let $T=\mathbb F_p\langle X_v:v\in V\rangle$ be the free associative algebra, graded by degree, and $\mu:T\to A(G)$ the algebra homomorphism with $\mu(X_v)=\mathrm{in}_1(v)$. It is onto, by the first point of Lemma 3 of the invisible-ideal theorem, whose proof uses only that $V$ generates $G$. For a word $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_d)\in V^d$ put $X_w=X_{w_1}\cdots X_{w_d}$ and $\mathrm{supp}(w)=\{w_1,\ldots,w_d\}$. For $S\subseteq V$ let $\pi_S$ be the algebra endomorphism of $T$ that fixes $X_v$ for $v\in S$ and kills $X_v$ for $v\notin S$. Then $A(e_S)\circ\mu=\mu\circ\pi_S$, since both are algebra homomorphisms and they agree on the generators, as $\mathrm{in}_1(1)=0$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $\Gamma=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph and $G=G_\Gamma$.

1. *Support.* Let $d\ge1$ and $g\in G_{(d)}\setminus G_{(d+1)}$. Then $e_S(g)\notin G_{(d+1)}$ for some $S\subseteq V$ with $|S|\le d$, and for such $S$, $\tau_S(g)\notin(G_S)_{(d+1)}$.
2. *Finite detection.* Let $n\ge2$. Then $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ if and only if $G_S$ has it for every $S\subseteq V$ with $|S|\le n-1$.
3. *Small degrees.* $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ for every $n\le5$.

Part 3 holds at every prime, including $p=2$, for every finite graph, with no realizability hypothesis.

**Corollaries.** These are proved here.

- (a) *Finitely many tests.* Fix $n\ge2$ and $\mathbb F$. Every finite graph gives a group with the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ if and only if the finitely many graphs on exactly $n-1$ vertices do. Indeed, adding isolated vertices makes a graph on fewer vertices an induced subgraph of one on $n-1$ vertices, so the "only if" direction of part 2 gives all graphs on at most $n-1$ vertices, and then the "if" direction gives every graph.
- (b) *Galois groups.* If $K$ contains a primitive $p$th root of unity and $G_K(p)\cong G_\Gamma$ for a finite graph $\Gamma$, then $G_K(p)$ satisfies the equality of the [conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md) for every $n\le5$. For example, for $r\ge2$ this applies to $(\mathbb Z_p\times F_r)*\mathbb Z_p$, the group of a star with $r$ leaves and an isolated vertex added, which is $G_K(p)$ for such a field $K$ by Corollary 2(ii) of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md). That corollary gives its failure for $n\ge n_0$, through a retraction onto $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$, as recorded in the first remark of [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md), where the lower degrees are left open.
- (c) *The first failing degree.* Let $n_{\min}(p)$ be the least $n$ for which some finite graph gives a group without the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_p$. Then $6\le n_{\min}(p)\le n_0$, with $n_0$ as in [the classification](kernel-unipotent-property-for-right-angled-artin-pro-p-groups.md), and some graph on at most $n_{\min}(p)-1$ vertices fails in degree $n_{\min}(p)$. The lower bound is part 3, the upper bound is the failure of $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$ for $n\ge n_0$, and the last claim is part 2.

**Proof of part 1.** Put $x=\mathrm{in}_d(g)$, which is nonzero by Jennings' theorem. Since $\mu$ is onto, $x=\sum_{w\in V^d}a_w\mu(X_w)$ with $a_w\in\mathbb F_p$. For $R\subseteq V$ put $y_R=\sum_{\mathrm{supp}(w)=R}a_w\mu(X_w)$. A word of length $d$ has at most $d$ distinct letters, so $y_R=0$ when $|R|>d$. Since $\pi_S(X_w)$ is $X_w$ if $\mathrm{supp}(w)\subseteq S$ and $0$ otherwise,
$$A(e_S)(x)=\mu\Bigl(\pi_S\Bigl(\sum_wa_wX_w\Bigr)\Bigr)=\sum_{R\subseteq S}y_R\qquad(S\subseteq V).$$
Möbius inversion on subsets gives
$$y_R=\sum_{S\subseteq R}(-1)^{|R\setminus S|}A(e_S)(x).$$
Indeed, after substituting the previous display, the coefficient of $y_{R'}$ on the right, for $R'\subseteq R$, is $\sum_{R'\subseteq S\subseteq R}(-1)^{|R\setminus S|}=(1-1)^{|R\setminus R'|}$, which is $1$ for $R'=R$ and $0$ otherwise. Now $e_V=\mathrm{id}$, so $x=A(e_V)(x)=\sum_Ry_R\ne0$. Hence $y_R\ne0$ for some $R$, and $|R|\le d$. By the inversion formula, $A(e_S)(x)\ne0$ for some $S\subseteq R$. But $A(e_S)(x)=\mathrm{in}_d(e_S(g))=(e_S(g)-1)+I_G^{d+1}$, and Jennings' theorem puts $h-1$ in $I_G^{d+1}$ for every $h\in G_{(d+1)}$. So $e_S(g)\notin G_{(d+1)}$, with $|S|\le|R|\le d$. Finally, $\sigma_S$ maps $(G_S)_{(d+1)}$ into $G_{(d+1)}$, by item 1 of the filtration entry. So $\tau_S(g)\in(G_S)_{(d+1)}$ would give $e_S(g)=\sigma_S(\tau_S(g))\in G_{(d+1)}$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** *Only if.* Let $S\subseteq V$ and $h\in G_S\setminus(G_S)_{(n)}$. If $\sigma_S(h)$ were in $G_{(n)}$, then $h=\tau_S(\sigma_S(h))$ would be in $(G_S)_{(n)}$, by item 1 of the filtration entry. So $\sigma_S(h)\notin G_{(n)}$, and if $G$ has the property, some continuous $\rho:G\to U_n(\mathbb F)$ has $\rho(\sigma_S(h))\ne1$. Then $\rho\sigma_S$ does not kill $h$.

*If.* Let $g\in G\setminus G_{(n)}$. Since $G_{(1)}=G$, there is a largest $d$ with $g\in G_{(d)}$, and $1\le d\le n-1$. By part 1 there is $S$ with $|S|\le d\le n-1$ and $\tau_S(g)\notin(G_S)_{(d+1)}$. As $d+1\le n$, also $\tau_S(g)\notin(G_S)_{(n)}$. By hypothesis some continuous $\rho:G_S\to U_n(\mathbb F)$ has $\rho(\tau_S(g))\ne1$, so $\rho\tau_S$ does not kill $g$. With the inclusion $\subseteq$, $G$ has the property. $\square$

**Lemmas for part 3.** For a nonempty set $R\subseteq V$, define the *Lie monomials on $R$* in $T$ recursively: $X_v$ is a Lie monomial on $\{v\}$, and if $m_1,m_2$ are Lie monomials on disjoint nonempty sets $R_1,R_2$, then $[m_1,m_2]=m_1m_2-m_2m_1$ is a Lie monomial on $R_1\cup R_2$. Let $\mathrm{Lie}_R\subseteq T$ be their $\mathbb F_p$-span. Call a word *$R$-multilinear* if each vertex of $R$ occurs in it exactly once and no other vertex occurs. Every element of $\mathrm{Lie}_R$ is a combination of $R$-multilinear words. For a homogeneous $P\in T$ of degree $|R|$, let $P_R$ be the sum of the terms of $P$ whose words are $R$-multilinear. Let $\mathcal P_k$ be the space of homogeneous $P\in T$ of degree $k$ with $P_R\in\mathrm{Lie}_R$ for every $R\subseteq V$ with $|R|=k$. For instance, every element of degree $1$ lies in $\mathcal P_1$.

*Lemma 1 (a Jacobson identity).* For elements $z_1,\ldots,z_p$ of an associative $\mathbb F_p$-algebra, with $\mathrm{ad}(z)(y)=zy-yz$,
$$\sum_{\sigma\in S_p}z_{\sigma(1)}\cdots z_{\sigma(p)}=\sum_{\tau\in S_{p-1}}\mathrm{ad}(z_{\tau(1)})\cdots\mathrm{ad}(z_{\tau(p-1)})(z_p).$$

*Proof.* It suffices to prove this in the free $\mathbb F_p$-algebra on $z_1,\ldots,z_p$. Write $\mathrm{ad}(z)=L_z-R_z$, with $L_z(y)=zy$ and $R_z(y)=yz$; left and right multiplications commute. Expanding the term of $\tau$, the choice of $R$ at the positions in a set $B\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ gives $(-1)^{|B|}uz_pv$, where $u$ is the product of the $z_{\tau(i)}$ with $i\notin B$ in increasing order of $i$, and $v$ the product of the $z_{\tau(i)}$ with $i\in B$ in decreasing order of $i$. Conversely, fix a word $uz_pv$ in which each $z_i$ occurs once, with $v$ of length $\ell$. The pairs $(\tau,B)$ that produce it correspond to the interleavings of the sequence $u$ with the reverse of $v$. There are $\binom{p-1}\ell$ of them, each with sign $(-1)^\ell$. Since $\binom{p-1}\ell=\prod_{i=1}^\ell\frac{p-i}i\equiv(-1)^\ell\pmod p$, each such word has coefficient $1$ on the right. Every word on the left has this form exactly once. $\square$

*Lemma 2 (initial forms).* (a) If $P\in\mathcal P_i$ and $Q\in\mathcal P_j$, then $[P,Q]\in\mathcal P_{i+j}$ and $P^p\in\mathcal P_{pi}$. (b) For every $k\ge1$ and $h\in G_{(k)}$, $\mathrm{in}_k(h)\in\mu(\mathcal P_k)$.

*Proof.* (a) Let $|R|=i+j$. A word of $PQ$ is a word of $P$ followed by a word of $Q$, and it is $R$-multilinear exactly when these are $R_1$- and $(R\setminus R_1)$-multilinear for some $R_1\subseteq R$ with $|R_1|=i$. So $(PQ)_R=\sum_{R_1}P_{R_1}Q_{R\setminus R_1}$, and similarly for $QP$, so that $[P,Q]_R=\sum_{R_1}[P_{R_1},Q_{R\setminus R_1}]$. A bracket of Lie monomials on disjoint sets is a Lie monomial on their union, so this lies in $\mathrm{Lie}_R$. Now let $|R|=pi$. In the same way, $(P^p)_R$ is the sum of $P_{R_1}\cdots P_{R_p}$ over all sequences $(R_1,\ldots,R_p)$ of pairwise disjoint $i$-element sets with union $R$. These sets are distinct, so group the sequences by the set $\{R_1,\ldots,R_p\}$. For each such set, Lemma 1 with $z_l=P_{R_l}$ writes the sum over its $p!$ orderings as a sum of iterated brackets of the $P_{R_l}\in\mathrm{Lie}_{R_l}$, which lie in $\mathrm{Lie}_R$.

(b) Induction on $k$. Since $G_{(2)}$ is open (item 4 of the filtration entry) and $V$ generates a dense subgroup, $V$ generates $G$ modulo $G_{(2)}$. As $\mathrm{in}_1$ is a homomorphism killing $G_{(2)}$, $\mathrm{in}_1(G)$ is spanned by the $\mathrm{in}_1(v)=\mu(X_v)$, and so lies in $\mu(\mathcal P_1)$. Let $k\ge2$. By the proof of item 1 of the filtration entry, $G_{(k)}$ is the closed subgroup generated by the $p$th powers of elements of $G_{(\lceil k/p\rceil)}$ and the commutators $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ with $x\in G_{(i)}$, $y\in G_{(j)}$ and $i+j=k$. As $G_{(k+1)}$ is open, these elements generate $G_{(k)}$ modulo $G_{(k+1)}$. Since $\mathrm{in}_k$ is a homomorphism killing $G_{(k+1)}$ and $\mu(\mathcal P_k)$ is a subspace, it suffices to treat these elements. For a commutator, $[x,y]-1=x^{-1}y^{-1}(xy-yx)$ and $xy-yx=(x-1)(y-1)-(y-1)(x-1)\in I_G^{k}$, while $x^{-1}y^{-1}-1\in I_G$. So $\mathrm{in}_k([x,y])=\mathrm{in}_i(x)\mathrm{in}_j(y)-\mathrm{in}_j(y)\mathrm{in}_i(x)$. As $i,j<k$, induction writes $\mathrm{in}_i(x)=\mu(P)$ and $\mathrm{in}_j(y)=\mu(Q)$ with $P\in\mathcal P_i$ and $Q\in\mathcal P_j$, so $\mathrm{in}_k([x,y])=\mu([P,Q])\in\mu(\mathcal P_k)$ by (a). For a power, let $m=\lceil k/p\rceil$, so $1\le m<k$ and $pm\ge k$, and let $x\in G_{(m)}$. Then $x^p-1=(x-1)^p\in I_G^{pm}$. If $pm>k$, this lies in $I_G^{k+1}$ and $\mathrm{in}_k(x^p)=0$. If $pm=k$, then $\mathrm{in}_k(x^p)=\mathrm{in}_m(x)^p=\mu(P^p)$ with $P\in\mathcal P_m$ by induction, and $P^p\in\mathcal P_k$ by (a). $\square$

*Lemma 3 (full support).* Suppose that $\Gamma$ has vertex set $S$ with $|S|=d\ge1$, and that $g\in G_{(d)}$ satisfies $e_{S'}(g)\in G_{(d+1)}$ for every $S'\subsetneq S$. Then $\mathrm{in}_d(g)\in\mu(\mathrm{Lie}_S)$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2, $\mathrm{in}_d(g)=\mu(P)$ with $P\in\mathcal P_d$. For $R\subseteq S$ let $P^{(R)}$ be the sum of the terms of $P$ whose words have support exactly $R$, so that $\pi_{S'}(P)=\sum_{R\subseteq S'}P^{(R)}$. For $S'\subsetneq S$, $\mu(\pi_{S'}(P))=A(e_{S'})(\mathrm{in}_d(g))=\mathrm{in}_d(e_{S'}(g))=0$, by Jennings' theorem. The Möbius inversion in the proof of part 1 then gives $\mu(P^{(R)})=0$ for every $R\subsetneq S$. A word of length $d=|S|$ with support $S$ is $S$-multilinear, so $P^{(S)}=P_S\in\mathrm{Lie}_S$, and $\mathrm{in}_d(g)=\sum_R\mu(P^{(R)})=\mu(P_S)$. $\square$

*Lemma 4 (brackets).* (a) For $R\subseteq V$ and $u\in R$, $\mathrm{Lie}_R$ is spanned by the elements $\mathrm{ad}(X_{v_1})\cdots\mathrm{ad}(X_{v_{r}})(X_u)$, over all orderings $(v_1,\ldots,v_r)$ of $R\setminus\{u\}$. (b) If $u,v$ are adjacent, then $\mu(X_u)$ and $\mu(X_v)$ commute. (c) Suppose that $V=S_1\sqcup S_2$ with $S_1,S_2$ nonempty and every vertex of $S_1$ adjacent to every vertex of $S_2$. Then $\mu(\mathrm{Lie}_V)=0$.

*Proof.* (a) Induction on $|R|$. A Lie monomial on $\{u\}$ is $X_u$. Otherwise it is $[m_1,m_2]$ with $m_l$ a Lie monomial on $R_l$; replacing it by $-[m_2,m_1]$ if necessary, $u\in R_2$. By induction $m_2$ is a combination of the stated elements for $R_2$. For a Lie monomial $m_1$ on $R_1$, $\mathrm{ad}(m_1)$ is an integer combination of products $\mathrm{ad}(X_{v_1})\cdots\mathrm{ad}(X_{v_s})$ over orderings of $R_1$, as one sees by induction using $\mathrm{ad}([a,b])=\mathrm{ad}(a)\mathrm{ad}(b)-\mathrm{ad}(b)\mathrm{ad}(a)$. So $[m_1,m_2]=\mathrm{ad}(m_1)(m_2)$ has the stated form.

(b) In $\mathbb F_p[G]$, $(u-1)(v-1)-(v-1)(u-1)=uv-vu=0$.

(c) For $l=1,2$, let $\mathfrak g_l\subseteq A(G)$ be the Lie subalgebra, for the commutator bracket, generated by the $\mu(X_v)$ with $v\in S_l$. By the Jacobi identity the centralizer of an element is a Lie subalgebra. By (b) the centralizer of $\mu(X_v)$, for $v\in S_2$, contains $\mathfrak g_1$. So the centralizer of each element of $\mathfrak g_1$ contains every such $\mu(X_v)$, hence $\mathfrak g_2$, and $[\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_2]=0$. We show by induction on $|R|$ that $\mu(m)=0$ for every Lie monomial $m$ on a set $R$ meeting both $S_1$ and $S_2$. Write $m=[m_1,m_2]$ with $m_l$ on $R_l$. If $R_1$ or $R_2$ meets both $S_1$ and $S_2$, then $\mu(m_1)=0$ or $\mu(m_2)=0$ by induction. Otherwise one of $R_1,R_2$ lies in $S_1$ and the other in $S_2$, as $R$ meets both. Then $\mu(m)=[\mu(m_1),\mu(m_2)]$ lies in $[\mathfrak g_1,\mathfrak g_2]$ or $[\mathfrak g_2,\mathfrak g_1]$, which are $0$. Every Lie monomial on $V$ is of this kind. $\square$

*Lemma 5 (chain representations).* Let $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_d)$ be distinct vertices of $\Gamma$ such that $w_i$ and $w_{i+1}$ are not adjacent for $1\le i<d$. There is a continuous $\rho_w:G\to U_{d+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ with $\rho_w(w_i)=1+E_{i,i+1}$ and $\rho_w(v)=1$ for every other vertex $v$. For every homogeneous $P\in T$ of degree $d$, $\kappa_{\rho_w}(\mu(P))$ is the coefficient of $X_w$ in $P$.

*Proof.* If $w_i$ and $w_j$ are adjacent, then $|i-j|\ge2$, so $E_{i,i+1}E_{j,j+1}=0=E_{j,j+1}E_{i,i+1}$ and their images commute. Images equal to $1$ commute with everything. As $U_{d+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ is a finite $p$-group, $\rho_w$ exists. Its linear extension $\tilde\rho_w$ sends $v-1$ to $E_{i,i+1}$ if $v=w_i$ and to $0$ otherwise. The $(1,d+1)$ entry of a product of $d$ strictly upper triangular matrices of size $d+1$ is the product of the $(j,j+1)$ entries of the $j$th factors. So for $u\in V^d$, the $(1,d+1)$ entry of $\tilde\rho_w((u_1-1)\cdots(u_d-1))$ is $1$ if $u=w$ and $0$ otherwise. As $\mu(X_u)$ is the class of $(u_1-1)\cdots(u_d-1)$, the claim follows by linearity. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** It suffices to work over $\mathbb F_p$. Let $n\le5$ and $g\in G\setminus G_{(n)}$, and let $d$ be largest with $g\in G_{(d)}$, so that $d\le n-1\le4$. Among the sets $S\subseteq V$ with $e_S(g)\notin G_{(d+1)}$, which include $V$, choose one of least size. Then $|S|\le d$ by part 1. Put $g'=\tau_S(g)$. Then $g'\in(G_S)_{(d)}$ by item 1 of the filtration entry, and $g'\notin(G_S)_{(d+1)}$ by part 1. For $S'\subsetneq S$, let $e'_{S'}$ be the endomorphism of $G_S$ that fixes $S'$ and kills $S\setminus S'$. Then $\sigma_Se'_{S'}\tau_S=e_{S'}$, since both fix $S'$ and kill $V\setminus S'$. Hence
$$e'_{S'}(g')=\tau_S\sigma_Se'_{S'}\tau_S(g)=\tau_S(e_{S'}(g))\in(G_S)_{(d+1)},$$
because $e_{S'}(g)\in G_{(d+1)}$ by the minimality of $|S|$. It suffices to find a continuous $\rho:G_S\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)$ with $\rho(g')\ne1$, since then $\rho\tau_S$ does not kill $g$. Such $\rho$ exists if $G_S$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property, because $g'\notin(G_S)_{(d+1)}\supseteq(G_S)_{(n)}$.

*Case $|S|\le3$.* By the theorem of [the classification](kernel-unipotent-property-for-right-angled-artin-pro-p-groups.md), conditions 1 and 2, a graph that is not complete multipartite has an induced subgraph consisting of an edge and a vertex adjacent to neither endpoint. With at most three vertices, it is that graph. If $\Gamma[S]$ is complete multipartite, $G_S$ has the property for every $n$, by the same theorem. Otherwise $G_S=\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$, which has it for $n\le2p+2$, by the first remark there and [polynomial factor algebras double the detection range](polynomial-factors-double-the-unipotent-detection-range.md). Here $n\le5<2p+2$.

*Case $|S|=4$.* Then $d=4$ and $n=5$. Apply Lemmas 3 and 4 to the graph $\Gamma[S]$ and the element $g'$, whose hypotheses were checked above. If the complement of $\Gamma[S]$ is disconnected, $S$ splits as in Lemma 4(c), and then $\mathrm{in}_4(g')\in\mu(\mathrm{Lie}_S)=0$. This contradicts Jennings' theorem, as $g'\notin(G_S)_{(5)}$. Otherwise the complement is one of the six connected graphs on four vertices: the path, the star with three leaves, the $4$-cycle, the paw (a triangle with a pendant edge), the complete graph minus an edge, and the complete graph. So $\Gamma[S]$ is, respectively, a path on four vertices, a triangle with an isolated vertex, two disjoint edges, a path on three vertices with an isolated vertex, an edge with two isolated vertices, or four isolated vertices. In the second, third, fifth and sixth cases every component of $\Gamma[S]$ is a complete graph, so $G_S$ has the property for $n\le2p+2$, as in the previous case.

In the two remaining cases, Lemma 3 gives $\mathrm{in}_4(g')=\mu(P)$ with $P\in\mathrm{Lie}_S$. Name the vertices $a,b,c,z$ as below. By Lemma 4(a) with $u=z$, $P$ is a combination of the six elements $\mathrm{ad}(X_{v_1})\mathrm{ad}(X_{v_2})\mathrm{ad}(X_{v_3})(X_z)$, with $(v_1,v_2,v_3)$ an ordering of $\{a,b,c\}$. Write $D_v$ for $\mathrm{ad}(\mu(X_v))$ on $A(G_S)$, so that $\mu$ maps this element to $D_{v_1}D_{v_2}D_{v_3}(\mu(X_z))$. For adjacent $u,v$, Lemma 4(b) gives $D_u(\mu(X_v))=0$ and $D_uD_v-D_vD_u=\mathrm{ad}([\mu(X_u),\mu(X_v)])=0$.

- *The path with edges $ab$, $bc$, $cz$.* As $D_c(\mu(X_z))=0$, the orderings $(a,b,c)$ and $(b,a,c)$ give $0$, and so does $(a,c,b)$, since $D_aD_cD_b=D_aD_bD_c$. Using $D_aD_b=D_bD_a$ and $D_bD_c=D_cD_b$, the other three orderings give $D_bD_cD_a(\mu(X_z))$. So $\mathrm{in}_4(g')=\lambda\mu(Q)$ with $\lambda\in\mathbb F_p$ and $Q=[X_b,[X_c,[X_a,X_z]]]$. The words of $Q$ beginning with $X_b$ are those of $X_b(X_cX_aX_z-X_cX_zX_a-X_aX_zX_c+X_zX_aX_c)$, so $X_bX_zX_aX_c$ has coefficient $1$. The sequence $w=(b,z,a,c)$ satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma 5, since $bz$, $za$ and $ac$ are not edges. So $\kappa_{\rho_w}(\mathrm{in}_4(g'))=\lambda$, which is nonzero because $\mathrm{in}_4(g')\ne0$. By formula (1), $\rho_w(g')=1+\lambda E_{1,5}\ne1$.
- *The path with edges $ab$, $bc$, and $z$ isolated.* Here $D_b$ commutes with $D_a$ and $D_c$. So the orderings $(a,b,c)$, $(b,a,c)$ and $(a,c,b)$ give $D_bD_aD_c(\mu(X_z))$, and $(c,a,b)$, $(c,b,a)$ and $(b,c,a)$ give $D_bD_cD_a(\mu(X_z))$. So $\mathrm{in}_4(g')=\lambda_1\mu(Q_1)+\lambda_2\mu(Q_2)$, with $Q_1=[X_b,[X_a,[X_c,X_z]]]$, $Q_2=[X_b,[X_c,[X_a,X_z]]]$ and $\lambda_1,\lambda_2\in\mathbb F_p$. The words of $Q_1$ beginning with $X_b$ are those of $X_b(X_aX_cX_z-X_aX_zX_c-X_cX_zX_a+X_zX_cX_a)$, and those of $Q_2$ are as for $Q$ above. Take $w_1=(b,z,c,a)$ and $w_2=(b,z,a,c)$, which satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma 5 since none of $bz$, $zc$, $ca$, $za$ is an edge. The coefficient of $X_{w_1}$ is $1$ in $Q_1$ and $0$ in $Q_2$, and that of $X_{w_2}$ is $0$ in $Q_1$ and $1$ in $Q_2$. So $\kappa_{\rho_{w_l}}(\mathrm{in}_4(g'))=\lambda_l$. As $\mathrm{in}_4(g')\ne0$, some $\lambda_l\ne0$, and then $\rho_{w_l}(g')\ne1$ by formula (1).

In every case $g'$, and hence $g$, is detected. With the inclusion $\subseteq$, $G$ has the property for $n\le5$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What parts 1 and 2 use.* Their proofs use only that $V$ is a finite set of topological generators and that, for each $S\subseteq V$, there is an endomorphism fixing $S$ and killing $V\setminus S$, which retracts $G$ onto the closed subgroup generated by $S$. For $n\le3$, part 2 recovers the proposition on small degrees in [the classification](kernel-unipotent-property-for-right-angled-artin-pro-p-groups.md): the sets $S$ then have at most two vertices, and every graph on at most two vertices is complete multipartite.
- *Degree six.* For $n=6$ the same reduction needs two further inputs, which are not attempted here: elements of degree $5$ with full support on four vertices, which are not multilinear, at least for the four-vertex path and for the three-vertex path with an isolated vertex; and multilinear elements of degree $5$ on graphs with five vertices. Sequences as in Lemma 5 that use all of $S$ exist only when the complement of $\Gamma[S]$ has a Hamiltonian path. For a triangle with an isolated vertex there is none, and detection of $\mathbb Z_p^3*\mathbb Z_p$ comes instead from the polynomial-factor theorem.
- *Sharpness.* For $n\le5$ part 3 makes part 2 vacuous. For $n\ge6$, whether the bound $n-1$ in part 2 can be lowered is not decided here.
