**Setting.** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$ and $\ell$ a prime. For a hyperbolic curve $X = C \setminus D$ over $k$, let
$$\rho_X : G_k \to \mathrm{Out}\big(\pi_1(X \otimes_k \bar k)^{(\ell)}\big)$$
be its pro-$\ell$ outer Galois representation, where $(\cdot)^{(\ell)}$ is the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient. Knowing the kernel of $\rho_X$ is the same as knowing the subfield of $\bar k$ that it fixes. Monodromic fullness and the images $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,r}) \subseteq \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,[r]})$ of the universal monodromy are as in [monodromically full hyperbolic curves](monodromically-full-hyperbolic-curve.md). A curve is *split* if its cusps are $k$-rational.

**Theorem A** (Hoshi) [@hoshi2011monodromic, Theorem A, pp. 48–49, and Theorem 6.1, p. 81]. Let $k$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, and let $X_1, X_2$ be hyperbolic curves of genus $0$ over $k$ that are $\ell$-monodromically full. Suppose that there is a finite Galois extension $k'/k$ of degree prime to $\ell$ over which $X_1$ and $X_2$ both split. This holds, for example, if both are split, or if both have fewer than $\ell$ cusps. Then $X_1 \cong X_2$ over $k$ if and only if $\ker \rho_{X_1} = \ker \rho_{X_2}$.

So, under these hypotheses, the curve is determined by a single subfield of $\bar k$. Hoshi presents the theorem as a genus-$0$ analogue of a theorem of Mochizuki. Let $(E_1, o_1)$ and $(E_2, o_2)$ be elliptic curves over a number field $k$ without complex multiplication over $\bar k$. Then they are isomorphic over $k$ if and only if, for every $n \ge 1$, the representations of $G_k$ on $E_1[n]$ and $E_2[n]$ have the same kernel [@hoshi2011monodromic, Introduction, p. 48]. Mochizuki had also shown that over generalized sub-$\ell$-adic fields the whole pro-$\ell$ outer Galois representation, not just its kernel, determines the curve [@hoshi2011monodromic, Introduction, p. 49].

**Theorem B** (Hoshi) [@hoshi2011monodromic, Theorem B, p. 50, Corollary 7.13, pp. 92–93, and Corollary 8.3, p. 95]. Let $k$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, and let $X_1, X_2$ be hyperbolic curves of type $(0,4)$ over $k$. Suppose that either $k$ is a number field and no element of $\mathfrak{m}_{X_1}$ or $\mathfrak{m}_{X_2}$ is an algebraic unit, or neither curve is NF-isotrivial. Then $X_1 \cong X_2$ over $k$ if and only if $\ker \rho_{X_1} = \ker \rho_{X_2}$ for infinitely many primes $\ell$. Under either hypothesis both curves are $\ell$-monodromically full for all but finitely many $\ell$ (see the [criteria in type $(0,4)$](matsumoto-tamagawa-problem-on-monodromic-fullness.md)), and the condition on $k'$ holds for $\ell > 4$, so Theorem A applies.

**Proof of Theorem A** [@hoshi2011monodromic, §§4–6, pp. 72–83]. Only "if" needs proof. Write $N$ for the common kernel and $r_i$ for the number of cusps of $X_i$, and let $\rho_{0,3}$ be the pro-$\ell$ outer Galois representation of $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$.

1. *Universal monodromy in genus $0$.* The stack $\mathcal{M}_{0,r}$ is the $(r-3)$-rd configuration space of $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$, and every automorphism of $\mathcal{M}_{0,r}$ over $k$ lies over $\mathcal{M}_{0,[r]}$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 4.1, p. 72]. The geometric image $\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,r})$ is the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of $\pi_1(\mathcal{M}_{0,r} \otimes_k \bar k)$, its abelianization is free over $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ of rank $r(r-3)/2$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 4.2, pp. 72–73], and it is slim [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 4.3 (i), p. 73]. By the genus-$0$ case of [Oda's prediction](oda-prediction-on-monodromy-fixed-fields.md), $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,[r]})$ maps onto $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,3})$ with kernel $\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,[r]})$, compatibly with the maps from $G_k$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 1.6, pp. 57–58].
2. *Rigidity of the universal monodromy.* The natural map from $\mathrm{Aut}_k(\mathcal{M}_{0,r})$ to the automorphisms of $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,r})$ over $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,3})$, modulo inner automorphisms by $\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,r})$, is bijective. For $r = 3, 4$ this is Mochizuki's pro-$\ell$ Isom theorem. For $r \ge 5$ Hoshi argues by induction on $r$, forgetting a point, with Nakamura's Galois rigidity of pure sphere braid groups [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 4.3 (iii) and its proof, pp. 73–75]. Moreover, a $k$-point of $\mathcal{M}_{0,[r]}$ is determined by the class of the homomorphism $G_k \to \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,[r]})$ over $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,3})$ that it induces, modulo $\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,[r]})$-conjugation [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 4.3 (v), p. 73].
3. *Intermediate covers.* An open subgroup $H \subseteq \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,[r]})$ that contains $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,r})$ corresponds to a finite étale cover $[\mathcal{M}_{0,r}/Q] \to \mathcal{M}_{0,[r]}$, where $Q \subseteq \mathfrak{S}_r$ is the image of $H$. Let $H_1, H_2$ be two such subgroups, with images $Q_1, Q_2$ of order prime to $\ell$. Then $\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,r})$ is the largest pro-$\ell$ closed subgroup of $H_i \cap \mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,[r]})$, so every isomorphism $H_1 \to H_2$ over $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,3})$ preserves it [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 5.1, p. 78]. By step 2, every such isomorphism comes from an isomorphism $[\mathcal{M}_{0,r}/Q_1] \cong [\mathcal{M}_{0,r}/Q_2]$ over $k$ that lifts to an automorphism of $\mathcal{M}_{0,r}$, up to inner automorphisms [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 5.2, pp. 79–80].
4. *Conclusion* [@hoshi2011monodromic, proof of Theorem 6.1, pp. 81–83]. The identifications $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{X_1}) \cong G_k/N \cong \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{X_2})$ give an isomorphism $\varphi$ over $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,3})$. By monodromic fullness, $H_i = \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{X_i})$ contains $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,r_i})$. Its image $Q_i$ in $\mathfrak{S}_{r_i}$ is a quotient of $\mathrm{Gal}(k'/k)$, since $X_i$ splits over $k'$, so it has order prime to $\ell$. As in step 3, $\varphi$ maps $\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,r_1})$ onto $\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,r_2})$, and comparing the ranks of their abelianizations gives $r_1 = r_2 = r$. By step 3, $\varphi$ comes from an automorphism of $\mathcal{M}_{0,r}$, which lies over $\mathcal{M}_{0,[r]}$. So the classifying points of $X_1$ and $X_2$ in $\mathcal{M}_{0,[r]}(k)$ induce the same class of homomorphisms $G_k \to \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,[r]})$, and by step 2 they are equal. Hence $X_1 \cong X_2$.

Monodromic fullness is what makes $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{X_i})$ the group of a finite étale cover of $\mathcal{M}_{0,[r]}$, so that step 3 applies.

**Profinite versus pro-$\ell$.** Over a number field, the profinite outer Galois representation of every hyperbolic curve is injective, so its kernel does not depend on the curve; see [injectivity of outer Galois representations](injectivity-of-outer-galois-representations.md). By Theorems A and B, the pro-$\ell$ kernel depends strongly on the curve [@hoshi2011monodromic, Introduction, p. 51].

**Finiteness for a given kernel** (Hoshi) [@hoshi2011monodromic, Theorem C, p. 51, and Corollary A.4, pp. 96–97]. Let $k$ be a number field, let $2g - 2 + r > 0$, and let $N \subseteq G_k$ be a closed normal subgroup. Then only finitely many $k$-isomorphism classes of hyperbolic curves of type $(g, r)$ over $k$ have $\ker \rho_X = N$. The kernel determines where $\rho_X$ ramifies, so these curves have $\rho_X$ unramified outside a finite set $\mathfrak{P}$ of primes [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remark A.2, p. 96]. Hoshi then shows that only finitely many curves of type $(g, r)$ have $\rho_X$ unramified outside a given finite $\mathfrak{P}$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Theorem A.3, p. 96]. Enlarge $\mathfrak{P}$ to contain the primes over $\ell$. By the [Oda–Tamagawa criterion](oda-good-reduction-criterion.md) each cusp is defined over an extension of degree at most $r$ unramified outside $\mathfrak{P}$, and by Hermite–Minkowski there are finitely many such extensions, so one finite extension splits all these curves [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma A.5, p. 97]. A curve over a finite extension has finitely many forms over $k$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma A.6, pp. 97–98]. For split curves, the Oda–Tamagawa criterion gives good reduction outside $\mathfrak{P}$, and the theorems of Faltings–Shafarevich, Siegel–Mahler and Faltings–Mordell bound first the compactifications and then the cusps [@hoshi2011monodromic, proof of Theorem A.3, p. 98]. Hoshi writes that the result is likely to be well known, but that he could not find it in the literature [@hoshi2011monodromic, Introduction, p. 51].

**An example.** This is checked here. Let $X_3 = \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{Q}} \setminus \{0, 1, 3, \infty\}$ and $X_4 = \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{Q}} \setminus \{0, 1, 4, \infty\}$. Both are split.

- Let $\ell \ge 5$. For $\lambda = 3$ take $(\mathfrak{p}_0, \mathfrak{q}_0) = (3, 2)$: $3$ divides $3$ but not $1 - 3 = -2$, with $v_3(3) = 1$, and $2$ divides $-2$ but not $3$, with $v_2(-2) = 1$. For $\lambda = 4$ take $(\mathfrak{p}_0, \mathfrak{q}_0) = (2, 3)$: $v_2(4) = 2$, $v_3(1 - 4) = 1$, and $2 \nmid -3$, $3 \nmid 4$. Neither prime lies over $\ell$, and $\ell$ divides none of the valuations. So Hoshi's two-prime criterion [@hoshi2011monodromic, Theorem 7.9, p. 90] makes both curves $\ell$-monodromically full.
- $\mathfrak{m}_{X_3} = \{3, 1/3, -2, -1/2, 3/2, 2/3\}$ and $\mathfrak{m}_{X_4} = \{4, 1/4, -3, -1/3, 4/3, 3/4\}$ are different, so $X_3 \otimes \bar{\mathbb{Q}} \not\cong X_4 \otimes \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 7.10, p. 91].
- By Theorem A, $\ker \rho_{X_3} \ne \ker \rho_{X_4}$ for every prime $\ell \ge 5$: the two curves cut out different fields in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$.

At $\ell = 3$, Theorem A does not apply. $X_3$ is the curve $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \ell, \infty\}$ of Hoshi's negative answer to the Matsumoto–Tamagawa problem, with $k = \mathbb{Q}$ and $\ell = 3$, so it is not even quasi-$3$-monodromically full [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, proof of Theorem A, p. 241]. The opened sources do not decide whether the two kernels differ for $\ell = 2$ or $3$.

**Far from the tripod's field.** This is checked here. Let $X$ be $\ell$-monodromically full of type $(0, r)$ with $r \ge 4$. Composing $\rho_X$ with $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,[r]}) \to \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{0,3})$ gives $\rho_{0,3}$, by step 1. So the field $\Omega_X$ cut out by $\rho_X$ contains the field $\Omega_{0,3}$ cut out by $\rho_{0,3}$, and $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_X/\Omega_{0,3}) = \mathrm{Im}(\rho_X) \cap \mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,[r]})$. This group contains $\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{0,r})$, whose abelianization has rank $r(r-3)/2 \ge 2$. So $\Omega_X$ is an infinite extension of $\Omega_{0,3}$. This contrasts with the curves for which Hoshi proves equality in the [question on $\ell$-moderate points](l-moderate-points-and-pro-l-kernel-fields.md), whose field is that of the tripod.

**Limits.** The opened sources do not say whether Theorem A holds without monodromic fullness or without the condition on $k'$, or for curves of positive genus. The proof uses that $\mathcal{M}_{0,r}$ is a configuration space of $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, through the rigidity in step 2.
