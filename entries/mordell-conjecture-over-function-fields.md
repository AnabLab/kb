**Setting.** Samuel writes in Weil's language. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, $K$ a field of algebraic functions over $k$, that is, a finitely generated extension, and $C$ an absolutely nonsingular curve of genus $g \ge 2$ defined over $K$. Write $C_K$ for the set of points of $C$ rational over $K$. Spreading $C$ out over an affine model $R$ of $K/k$ gives a family $X \to R$ of curves of genus $g$, and the points of $C_K$ correspond to the rational sections $R \to X$ [@samuel1966grauert, p. 55]. The transcendence degree of $K$ over $k$ is arbitrary. Grauert's field of tangent planes below consists of $(\dim R)$-planes, and Samuel proves Theorem 2 below by induction on $\dim_k K$ [@samuel1966grauert, p. 56].

**Theorem** (Grauert; Manin). Let $k$ have characteristic $0$. If $C_K$ is infinite, there are a curve $C'$ defined over $k$ and a $K$-isomorphism $f : C \to C'$. Moreover all but finitely many points of $C_K$ lie in $f^{-1}(C'_k)$, that is, $C'_K \setminus C'_k$ is finite [@samuel1966grauert, p. 55].

Samuel notes that Manin also proved the theorem, by transcendental methods. The final assertion is not explicit in Grauert's paper, and Samuel derives it from Grauert's results [@samuel1966grauert, pp. 55–56]. Conversely, if $C$ is $K$-isomorphic to a curve $C'$ over $k$, then $C_K$ is infinite, because it contains a copy of the infinite set $C'_k$. So in characteristic $0$, $C_K$ is infinite exactly when $C$ is $K$-isomorphic to a curve over $k$. This converse is checked here.

**Mechanism.** Grauert proves two statements about the family $X \to R$ [@samuel1966grauert, p. 56].

- *(G.1)* There is exactly one field $E$ of infinitesimal sections, that is, a section of the bundle of $(\dim R)$-planes tangent to $X$ and transversal to the fibres. Almost all rational sections $R \to X$ are tangent to $E$.
- *(G.2)* For every $j \ge 0$ there are a curve $C^{(j)}$ defined over $K^{p^j}$ and a $K$-isomorphism $C \to C^{(j)}$. Here $K^p$ stands for the intersection of the kernels of the $k$-derivations of $K$, so $K^p = k$ in characteristic $0$.

In characteristic $0$, (G.2) gives the curve $C'$ over $k$. The $K$-points of $C'$ are the rational maps $R \to C'$, which are the rational sections of the trivial family $R \times C' \to R$. For that family $E$ is the horizontal field, so by (G.1) almost all of these sections are constant. This is the finiteness of $C'_K \setminus C'_k$.

**Finiteness of maps.** These hold in every characteristic.

- *Theorem 1.* Let $V$ be a variety and $D$ a curve of genus at least $2$, both defined over an algebraically closed field $k$. Only finitely many separable non-constant rational maps $V \to D$ are defined over $k$. As a corollary, attributed to Schwarz and Klein, $\mathrm{Aut}_k(D)$ is finite [@samuel1966grauert, Théorème 1 and Corollaire, p. 56].
- *Theorem 2* (Severi). Let $K$ be a field of algebraic functions over an algebraically closed field $k$. Only finitely many intermediate fields $k \subset L \subset K$ have transcendence degree $1$ over $k$, genus at least $2$, and $K$ separable over $L$ [@samuel1966grauert, Théorème 2, p. 56, with proof on pp. 56–58].

**Characteristic $p$** (Samuel). Let $k$ have characteristic $p > 0$, and for a curve $D$ over $k$ and $q = p^n$ let $D^q$ be the curve whose equations are those of $D$ with coefficients raised to the $q$-th power.

1. *Frobenius twists.* $D$ is $k$-isomorphic to $D^q$ exactly when $D$ is $k$-isomorphic to a curve defined over $\mathbb{F}_q$ [@samuel1966grauert, Théorème 3, p. 58].
2. *Descent.* Suppose $C_K$ is infinite. Then there are a curve $C'$ over $k$, a finite Galois extension $K'/K$ with group $G$, a $K'$-isomorphism $u : C \to C'$ and an injective homomorphism $s \mapsto j_s$ from $G$ to $\mathrm{Aut}(C')$ with $j_s = u^s \circ u^{-1}$. Thus $C$ is obtained from $C'$ by Galois descent along $K'/K$ through the $j_s$. The points of $C_K$ are the $u^{-1}(x)$ with $x \in C'_{K'}$ and $x^s = j_s(x)$ for all $s \in G$ [@samuel1966grauert, Théorème 4, p. 59]. If $\mathrm{Aut}(C')$ is trivial, $C$ is $K$-isomorphic to $C'$ [@samuel1966grauert, p. 60].
3. *Transcendental moduli.* If $C$ is isomorphic to no curve defined over a finite field, then $C_K$ is infinite exactly when there is a $K$-isomorphism $u$ from $C$ to a curve $C'$ over $k$. Then $C_K \setminus u^{-1}(C'_k)$ is finite [@samuel1966grauert, Théorème 5 (a), p. 60].
4. *Curves over finite fields.* Suppose $C$ is isomorphic to a curve $C'$ over a finite field, let $\mathbb{F}_q$ be a finite field over which $C'$ and all elements of $\mathrm{Aut}(C')$ are defined, and let $f : C' \to C'$ be the Frobenius $x \mapsto x^q$. Then $C_K$ is infinite exactly when there are a finite Galois extension $K'/K$ with group $G$, a $K'$-isomorphism $u : C \to C'$, and an injective homomorphism $s \mapsto j_s$ from $G$ to $\mathrm{Aut}(C')$ with $j_s = u^s \circ u^{-1}$, such that either $K' = K$ or there is a point $z \in C'_{K'} \setminus C'_k$ with $j_s(z) = z^s$ for all $s \in G$. In that case there are finitely many $x_i \in C'_{K'}$ with $x_i^s = j_s(x_i)$ such that every point of $C_K$ is $u^{-1}(f^n(x_i))$ for some $i$ and some $n \ge 0$, or, only when $K' = K$, $u^{-1}(x)$ for some $x \in C'_k$ [@samuel1966grauert, Théorème 5 (b) and its proof, pp. 60–61]. The alternative $K' = K$ is missing from Samuel's printed statement; see the correction below.
5. *Smooth models.* If $C$ has genus at least $2$ over an algebraic closure of $K$ and $C_K$ is infinite, then $C$ has an absolutely nonsingular model over $K$ [@samuel1966grauert, Théorème 6, p. 61]. Samuel leaves open the case in which the genus drops to $0$ or $1$ over the algebraic closure [@samuel1966grauert, p. 62].

**Correction to Théorème 5 (b).** Samuel's printed statement asks for the point $z$ in every case [@samuel1966grauert, Théorème 5 (b), pp. 60–61]. His proof of sufficiency treats $K' = K$ separately: then $C_K$ contains the infinite set $u^{-1}(C'_k)$ [@samuel1966grauert, p. 61]. With that alternative added, item 4 follows from his arguments, as follows.

- *Sufficiency.* If $K' = K$, then $C_K$ contains $u^{-1}(C'_k)$, which is infinite. If $z$ exists, $f$ commutes with each $j_s$, which is defined over $\mathbb{F}_q$, and with the action of $G$, since $(x^q)^s = (x^s)^q$. So each $f^n(z)$ satisfies the condition of item 2, and the points $u^{-1}(f^n(z))$, $n \ge 0$, lie in $C_K$. They are pairwise distinct, because $z$ is transcendental over $k$ [@samuel1966grauert, p. 61].
- *Necessity.* If $C_K$ is infinite, item 2 gives $K'$, $u$ and $j$. Suppose $K' \ne K$. A point $x \in C'_k$ has $x^s = x$, so if also $x^s = j_s(x)$ for all $s \in G$, then $x$ is fixed by every $j_s$. Some $j_s$ is a nontrivial automorphism, and it has only finitely many fixed points [@samuel1966grauert, p. 60]. By item 2 the infinitely many points of $C_K$ are the $u^{-1}(x)$ with $x^s = j_s(x)$ for all $s$, so one such $x$ lies outside $C'_k$, and it serves as $z$.

Without the alternative, the condition is not necessary. Let $k$ be an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $K = k(t)$. Let $C_0$ be a curve of genus at least $2$ defined over a finite field, and let $C$ be $C_0$ regarded over $K$. Then $C_K$ contains the infinite set $(C_0)_k$, but no data as in item 4 come with a point $z$:

1. *Isomorphisms are constant.* Let $K'/K$ be a finite extension and $C'$ a curve over $k$. Then every $K'$-isomorphism $u : C \to C'$ is defined over $k$. Indeed, spread $u$ and its inverse out over a dense open $V$ of a smooth curve over $k$ with function field $K'$. For $v \in V(k)$ this gives $k$-isomorphisms $u_v : C_0 \to C'$. These are separable non-constant rational maps defined over $k$, so by Theorem 1 they take only finitely many values. For each value $\varphi$, the set of $v$ with $u_v = \varphi$ is closed in $V$. It is the complement of the image, under the projection $C_0 \times V \to V$, of the set where $u$ and $\varphi \circ \mathrm{pr}_1$ differ. That set is open because $C'$ is separated, and the projection is open. As $V$ is irreducible, one value $\varphi$ is taken at every $v \in V(k)$. Then $u$ and $\varphi \circ \mathrm{pr}_1$ agree on $C_0 \times V$, so $u = \varphi$ over $K'$.
2. *The group is trivial.* So $u^s = u$, and $j_s$ is the identity for every $s \in G$. As $s \mapsto j_s$ is injective, $G = 1$ and $K' = K$.
3. *No $z$.* A point of $C'_K \setminus C'_k$ is a $k$-isomorphism from $k(C')$ onto a subfield of $K$ [@samuel1966grauert, p. 60]. By Lüroth's theorem every subfield of $k(t)$ of transcendence degree $1$ over $k$ is rational over $k$, so it has genus $0$, while $k(C')$ has genus at least $2$. So $z$ cannot exist.

The corrected necessity and this example are checked here.

**Points not coming from $k$.** In characteristic $p$ the finiteness of $C'_K \setminus C'_k$ fails. For instance, let $C'$ be a curve of genus at least $2$ defined over a finite field and $K = k(C')$. Its generic point $z$ lies in $C'_K \setminus C'_k$. Take $K' = K$ and $u$ the identity in item 4. By the sufficiency argument above, the points $f^n(z)$ for $n \ge 0$ are pairwise distinct points of $C'_K$. None of them lies in $C'_k$, because their coordinates are $q^n$-th powers of those of $z$ and $k$ is perfect. So $C'_K \setminus C'_k$ is infinite. This instance is checked here. Samuel sums up that all the counterexamples come from curves defined over finite fields and their Frobenius endomorphisms [@samuel1966grauert, p. 55].

**Uses in the graph.**

- *Fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$.* The characteristic-$0$ theorem over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, combined with Faltings' theorem over number fields, gives the [Mordell conjecture over fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$](mordell-conjecture-over-finitely-generated-fields.md).
- *Isotriviality in characteristic $p$.* Item 1 is the algebraically closed counterpart of the criterion in [the Grothendieck conjecture over finitely generated fields of characteristic $p$](grothendieck-conjecture-over-finitely-generated-fields-of-characteristic-p.md): there, Frobenius twists of a curve coincide exactly when the curve is isotrivial.
