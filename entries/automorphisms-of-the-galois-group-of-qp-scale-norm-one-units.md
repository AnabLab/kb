**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $G = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, and let $K \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ be the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with nontrivial automorphism $\iota$. Write $U^1_F = 1 + \mathfrak{m}_F$ and
$$N^1 = \{u \in U^1_K : u\,\iota(u) = 1\}.$$
For $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$, $\theta(G_K) = G_K$, because $G_K$ is the only open subgroup of index $2$ containing the inertia group, and $\theta$ preserves inertia (see [invariants recovered from $G_K$](local-field-galois-invariants.md)). Put $\theta_K = \theta|_{G_K}$. It preserves the inertia group of $G_K$, whose image in $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $\mathrm{rec}_K(\mathcal{O}_K^\times)$ by [local class field theory](local-class-field-theory.md). So there is a unique automorphism $\theta_K^\times$ of $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$ with
$$\theta_K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathrm{rec}_K(x)) = \mathrm{rec}_K(\theta_K^\times(x)) \qquad (x \in \mathcal{O}_K^\times).$$

**Theorem.** This is proved here; part 2 adapts a construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg.

1. *Diagonal form.* $\theta_K^\times$ is the identity on $U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, and it maps $N^1$ onto itself by $n \mapsto n^{b(\theta)}$ for a unique $b(\theta) \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. The map $b : \mathrm{Aut}(G) \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ is a homomorphism, with $b(\mathrm{inn}_g) = 1$ for $g \in G_K$ and $b(\mathrm{inn}_g) = -1$ for $g \notin G_K$. Equivalently, if $f : G_K \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ is a surjective homomorphism with $f(sgs^{-1}) = -f(g)$ for $s \notin G_K$ (an anticyclotomic quotient; such $f$ exist and are unique up to $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$), then $f \circ \theta_K = b(\theta)\, f$.
2. *Every residue occurs.* For every $c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ there is $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ with $b(\theta) \equiv c \pmod p$.
3. *Jannsen and Wingberg's level-two automorphism does not lift.* Let $V \subset G$ be the wild inertia group, with $V^1 = V$, $V^2 = V^p[V,V]$ and $V^3 = (V^2)^p[V^2, V]$. The automorphism of $G/V^3$ that Jannsen and Wingberg write down [@jannsenwingberg1982, §5.1, p. 96] is induced by no automorphism of $G$.

**Consequences.**

- For $p \ge 5$, take $c \ne \pm 1$ in part 2. Then $b(\theta) \ne \pm 1$, so $\theta$ is not inner, and $\theta_K^\times$ agrees with neither $\mathrm{id}$ nor $\iota$ on any open subgroup of $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$. Composing with inner automorphisms of $G_K$ does not change $\theta_K^\times$, so the outer class of $\theta_K$ lies outside $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$. By Kondo it lies in the normalizer of $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ [@kondo2025, Proposition 2.5 (3), p. 15] (see [the field-theoretic subgroup](field-theoretic-subgroup-of-outer-automorphisms.md)); Kondo shows such classes fix $\mathbb{Q}_p^\times$ pointwise [@kondo2025, Theorem 2.8, p. 16], which part 1 reproves here for units. He notes that the normalizer is strictly larger than $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$ [@kondo2025, Remark 2.11, p. 17]; part 2 adds that its action on $K^\times$ need not be field-theoretic.
- This gives another proof, for $p \ge 5$, that $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ has outer automorphisms. It uses the same lifting theorem as Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's proof. Their automorphism has $b \equiv 1 \pmod p$; see the remark after the proof of part 2.
- For $p = 3$, part 2 says nothing, since $\mathbb{F}_3^\times = \{\pm 1\}$. Whether some $\theta$ has $b(\theta) \ne \pm 1$ when $p = 3$ is not settled here.
- The application to Hodge–Tate representations is [a two-dimensional representation of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ that is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate](two-dimensional-representation-of-g-qp-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md).

**Proof of part 1.**

*(a) Conjugation.* Local reciprocity commutes with conjugation: the reciprocity maps form a morphism of $G$-modulations, which includes $\mathrm{rec}_K(s(x)) = s\,\mathrm{rec}_K(x)\,s^{-1}$ for $s \in G$ and $x \in K^\times$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Chapter III, §2, p. 170; (7.1.5), p. 375]. Let $s \notin G_K$. Then $\theta(s) = s h$ with $h \in G_K$, and conjugation by $h$ is trivial on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$. So $\theta_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ commutes with conjugation by $s$, and $\theta_K^\times \circ \iota = \iota \circ \theta_K^\times$.

*(b) Units.* $\mu_{p^\infty}(K) = 1$, since $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is totally ramified of degree $p - 1 \ge 2$ ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)) while $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is unramified. So $U^1_K \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^2$ and $U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ ([multiplicative groups](multiplicative-group-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), and square roots are unique in both. Every $u \in U^1_K$ is $u = (u\,\iota(u))^{1/2}\,(u\,\iota(u)^{-1})^{1/2}$ with the first factor $\iota$-fixed, hence in $U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, and the second in $N^1$, and $U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \cap N^1 = \{x : x^2 = 1\} = 1$. So $U^1_K = U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \times N^1$, and $N^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$.

*(c) The cyclotomic character.* Let $\kappa(u) = \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mathrm{rec}_K(u))$ for $u \in U^1_K$, where $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is the $p$-adic cyclotomic character of $G$ restricted to $G_K$. Since $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is a character of $G$, (a) gives $\kappa(\iota u) = \kappa(u)$, so $\kappa(n)^2 = \kappa(n)\kappa(\iota n) = \kappa(n\,\iota(n)) = 1$ for $n \in N^1$. As $U^1_K$ is pro-$p$, $\kappa$ takes values in $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$, which has no element of order $2$, so $\kappa(n) = 1$. The inertia groups of $K$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p$ coincide, and $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is totally ramified with $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ identifying its group with $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)). So $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mathrm{rec}_K(\mathcal{O}_K^\times)) = \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, and taking pro-$p$ Sylow subgroups, $\kappa(U^1_K) = 1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$. By (b), $\kappa$ restricts to a surjection $U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \to 1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$ between groups isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p$, hence an isomorphism.

*(d) Conclusion.* $\theta_K^\times$ preserves $U^1_K$, the pro-$p$ Sylow subgroup of $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$, and by (a) it preserves $U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p} = (U^1_K)^{\iota}$ and $N^1$. The automorphism $\theta_K$ of $G_K$ preserves the cyclotomic character of $G_K$ ([invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md)), so $\kappa \circ \theta_K^\times = \kappa$, and by (c) $\theta_K^\times$ is the identity on $U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$. An automorphism of $N^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ is $n \mapsto n^b$ for a unique $b \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. Since $(\theta\theta')^{\mathrm{ab}}_K = \theta^{\mathrm{ab}}_K\theta'^{\mathrm{ab}}_K$, $b$ is a homomorphism. Conjugation by $g \in G_K$ is trivial on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and by $g \notin G_K$ it is $\iota$ on $\mathcal{O}_K^\times$ by (a), which is $n \mapsto n^{-1}$ on $N^1$.

*(e) The anticyclotomic form.* Homomorphisms $G_K \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ correspond, through $\mathrm{rec}_K$, to continuous homomorphisms $K^\times \to \mathbb{Z}_p$, and by (a) the condition $f(sgs^{-1}) = -f(g)$ becomes $\hat f \circ \iota = -\hat f$ for $\hat f = f \circ \mathrm{rec}_K$. Write $K^\times = p^{\mathbb{Z}} \times \mu_{p^2-1} \times U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \times N^1$. Such an $\hat f$ kills $p$ and $U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, which are $\iota$-fixed, and the torsion, and is determined by its restriction to $N^1 \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$; conversely any homomorphism $N^1 \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ extends this way. So these $f$ form a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of rank $1$, whose generators are the surjective ones. For $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$, $f \circ \theta_K$ again satisfies the sign condition, because $\theta(s) \notin G_K$. On $\mathrm{rec}_K(N^1)$ it equals $b(\theta) f$, since $f(\mathrm{rec}_K(n^{b})) = b\,f(\mathrm{rec}_K(n))$. Both sides are determined by these values, so $f \circ \theta_K = b(\theta) f$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.**

*(a) Generators.* Let $T$ be the maximal tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathcal{G} = \mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_p)$, so $V = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p/T)$. Following Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 419–422], fix a splitting $G = V \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, where $\sigma$ lifts Frobenius, $\tau$ generates tame inertia, $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^p$, and $\sigma$ fixes $\mu_p$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.2)–(7.5.3), p. 410; p. 422]. Let $\omega : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ be the action on $\mu_p$, so $\omega(\sigma) = 1$ and $\omega(\tau)$ is a primitive $(p-1)$-th root of unity [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422]. There are $x_0, x_1 \in V$, generating $V$ as a closed normal subgroup of $G$, such that
$$x_0^{\sigma} = \langle x_0, \tau\rangle^{g}\, x_1^{p}\, [x_1, y_1],\qquad \langle x_0, \tau\rangle = \big(x_0^{h^{p-1}}\tau x_0^{h^{p-2}}\tau \cdots x_0^{h}\tau\big)^{\pi/(p-1)}$$
[@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.14), pp. 419–420]. Here $x^{\rho} = \rho x \rho^{-1}$, $\pi \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the idempotent with $\pi\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{Z}_p$, $y_1 \in V$, and $g, h \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ give the actions of $\sigma, \tau$ on $\mu_p$. The exponent of $x_1$ is $p^s$ with $p^s = |\mu_{p^\infty}(T)|$, and $s = 1$ because $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^2})/\mathbb{Q}_p$ has ramification index $p(p-1)$.

*(b) The anticyclotomic value of $x_1$ is a unit.* Let $f$ be as in part 1 (e), surjective. As $\tau$ generates a procyclic group of order prime to $p$, $f(\tau) = 0$. Since $\sigma^2 \in G_K$ commutes with $\sigma \notin G_K$, $f(\sigma^2) = -f(\sigma^2) = 0$. Put $a = f(x_0)$ and $c_1 = f(x_1)$. All factors of the product defining $\langle x_0, \tau\rangle$ lie in $G_K$, so $f$ of the product is $S a$ with $S = \sum_{j=1}^{p-1} h^j$, and $f(\langle x_0, \tau\rangle) = S a/(p-1)$. As $\sigma \notin G_K$, $f(x_0^\sigma) = -a$. Applying $f$ to the relation gives
$$-a = g S a/(p-1) + p c_1.$$
Now $h \bmod p$ generates $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, because tame inertia maps onto $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)/\mathbb{Q}_p)$, so $S \equiv 0 \pmod p$. Hence $a(1 + gS/(p-1)) = -pc_1$ with $1 + gS/(p-1)$ a unit, and $a \in p\mathbb{Z}_p$. Also $G_K = V \cdot \overline{\langle \sigma^2, \tau\rangle}$, so $f(V) = f(G_K) = \mathbb{Z}_p$. But $f(V)$ is generated by $\pm a, \pm c_1$, because $f(x^\rho) = \pm f(x)$ for $\rho \in G$. So $c_1 \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$.

*(c) A central unit.* Let $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathcal{G}]]$, with the anti-involution $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$ of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 421]. Let $\chi : \mathcal{G} \to \{\pm 1\}$ be the character with $\chi(\sigma) = -1$ and $\chi(\tau) = 1$, whose kernel $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$ is generated by $\sigma^2$ and $\tau$, and extend it to a ring homomorphism $\chi : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$. Put $\sigma_2 = \sigma^{\pi_2}$, where $\pi_2\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{Z}_2$. For an open normal subgroup $\mathcal{N} \subseteq \ker\omega \cap \ker\chi$ of $\mathcal{G}$, let $\Gamma = \mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}$, let $e$ be the order of the image of $\tau$, prime to $p$, and $2^m$ that of $\sigma_2$. In $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma]$ put, for $\xi \in \{1, \omega\}$,
$$T_\xi = \frac1e\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}\xi(\tau)^{-i}\tau^i,\qquad S_- = \frac{1}{2^m}\sum_{i=0}^{2^m-1}(-1)^i\sigma_2^i \ (m \ge 1),\quad S_- = 0\ (m = 0).$$
These are compatible as $\mathcal{N}$ shrinks, so they define elements of $\Lambda$; $T_\omega$ at the level of a finite tamely ramified Galois $K'/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is the element $\lambda_{K'}$ of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422]. At each level:

- $T_1, T_\omega$ are orthogonal idempotents with $\tau T_\xi = \xi(\tau)T_\xi$, and they are central: $\sigma T_\xi \sigma^{-1} = \frac1e\sum_i \xi(\tau)^{-i}\tau^{pi} = T_\xi$, since $i \mapsto pi$ permutes $\mathbb{Z}/e$ and $\xi(\tau)^p = \xi(\tau)$. $S_-$ is an idempotent.
- $P = T_1S_-$ and $Q = T_\omega S_-$ are orthogonal central idempotents. They commute with $\sigma$, as $\sigma_2$ is a power of $\sigma$ in $\Gamma$. For $\tau$: $\tau\sigma_2^i\tau^{-1} = \sigma_2^i\tau^{u-1}$ with $u \equiv p^{-k} \pmod e$ for some $k$, and $\tau^{u-1}T_\xi = \xi(\tau)^{u-1}T_\xi = T_\xi$, because $u \equiv 1$ modulo the order of $\xi(\tau)$, which divides both $e$ and $p - 1$.
- $T_1^* = T_\omega$, $T_\omega^* = T_1$, and $S_-^* = S_-$, because $\omega(\sigma_2) = 1$. As $*$ reverses products, $P^* = S_-T_\omega = Q$ and $Q^* = P$.

For $c \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ let $\eta_c = 1 + (c - 1)P + (c^{-1} - 1)Q$. It is a central unit with inverse $\eta_{c^{-1}}$, and
$$\eta_c\eta_c^* = \big(1 + (c-1)P + (c^{-1}-1)Q\big)\big(1 + (c^{-1}-1)P + (c-1)Q\big) = 1,$$
since the coefficient of $P$ is $(c - 1) + (c^{-1} - 1) + (c-1)(c^{-1}-1) = 0$, and likewise for $Q$. For a finite tamely ramified Galois $K'/\mathbb{Q}_p$ containing $K(\zeta_p)$, with ramification index $e'$ and residue degree $f'$, let $\kappa_{K'} = \sum_{i=0}^{f'-1}\sigma^i$ and $\lambda_{K'}$ be Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's elements [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422], computed in $\mathbb{F}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(K'/\mathbb{Q}_p)]$, through which $\omega$ and $\chi$ factor. (The Demuškin relation of $G_{K'}(p)$ used below needs $\zeta_p \in K'$, and these $K'$ are cofinal.) Then
$$\eta_c\,\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}.$$
Indeed $\sigma^{f'}$ lies in the inertia group, so $\sigma^{f'} = \tau^j$ with $\omega(\tau)^j = \omega(\sigma)^{f'} = 1$, and $\tau\lambda_{K'} = \omega(\tau)\lambda_{K'}$. Hence $\sigma\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} + (\tau^j - 1)\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$, so $\sigma_2$ fixes $\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$ and $S_-\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = 0$. Finally, $\chi(T_1) = 1$, $\chi(T_\omega) = 0$ as $p - 1$ divides $e$ and $\omega(\tau) \ne 1$, and $\chi(S_-) = 1$ as $\chi(\sigma_2) = -1$, which forces $m \ge 1$. So $\chi(\eta_c) = c$.

*(d) The lifting.* Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg let $F = P_2 \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, with $P_2$ the free pro-$p$ $\mathcal{G}$-operator group on $x_0, x_1$, mapping onto $G$. They define $\psi : F \to F$ fixing $\sigma, \tau, x_0$ with $\psi(x_1) \equiv x_1^{\varepsilon}$ modulo the Frattini subgroup, for their central unit $\varepsilon = 1 - 2E$. They show that $\psi$ preserves the Demuškin relation $r_{K'}$ of $G_{K'}(p)$ modulo $F_{K'}(p)^3$ for the finite tamely ramified Galois extensions $K'/\mathbb{Q}_p$, of which those containing $K(\zeta_p)$ suffice. In the limit this gives an automorphism of $G/V^3$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$. By their Theorem (7.5.15), it lifts to an automorphism of $G$ that agrees with it modulo $V^2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15), p. 421, and pp. 422–423].

Their computation of $\psi(r_{K'})$ uses only three properties of $\varepsilon$: it is a central unit; $\varepsilon\varepsilon^* = 1$, which with centrality gives $\varepsilon\delta\varepsilon^* = \delta$ for the $\delta$ with $y_1 \equiv x_1^{\delta}$; and $\varepsilon\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$, which turns $x_1^{p\varepsilon\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}}$ into $x_1^{p\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}}$ (they state the stronger $\varepsilon\lambda_{K'} = \lambda_{K'}$). The other ingredient is the identity $[x^{a}, z]^{\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}} \equiv [x, z^{a^*}]^{\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}}$ modulo $F_{K'}(p)^3$, for $x, z$ in the image of $P_2$ and $a \in \mathbb{F}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(K'/\mathbb{Q}_p)]$; on $F_{K'}(p)^2/F_{K'}(p)^3$, $\mathcal{G}$ acts by conjugation through $\mathrm{Gal}(K'/\mathbb{Q}_p)$, as $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K')$ acts there by inner automorphisms. It holds because $\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}\rho = \rho\,\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \omega(\rho)\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$, which follows as above from $\sigma\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}\sigma = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$ and $\tau\lambda_{K'} = \lambda_{K'}\tau = \omega(\tau)\lambda_{K'}$, using $\tau\sigma^i = \sigma^i\tau^{p^{-i}}$. Then $[x^\rho, z] = [x, z^{\rho^{-1}}]^{\rho}$ gives $[x^\rho, z]^{\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}} = [x, z^{\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}}]^{\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}}$, by bilinearity of commutators from $F_{K'}(p)/F_{K'}(p)^2$ to $F_{K'}(p)^2/F_{K'}(p)^3$, and the general case follows by linearity.

By (c), $\eta_c$ has the three properties. So choose $y \in P_2$ whose image in the Frattini quotient $P_2/\Phi(P_2) = \Lambda\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1$ is $\eta_c\bar x_1$, and let $\psi_c : F \to F$ fix $\sigma, \tau, x_0$ and send $x_1$ to $y$, which exists because $F$ is free in the category of semidirect products over $\mathcal{G}$ with pro-$p$ kernel [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 74]. This is an automorphism, as it is one modulo the Frattini subgroup. The argument just described applies with $\eta_c$ in place of $\varepsilon$. It gives $\theta_c \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and agrees with $\psi_c$ modulo $V^2 = V^p[V,V]$.

*(e) The residue of $b$.* $f$ is a homomorphism on $V$ with $f(\rho v \rho^{-1}) = \chi(\rho) f(v)$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$, so modulo $p$ it factors through $V/V^2$ and satisfies $f(\lambda \bar v) \equiv \chi(\lambda) f(v)$ for $\lambda \in \Lambda$. As $\theta_c(x_1) \equiv \eta_c \bar x_1$ modulo $V^2$, part 1 (e) gives
$$b(\theta_c)\, c_1 = f(\theta_c(x_1)) \equiv \chi(\eta_c)\, c_1 = c\, c_1 \pmod p,$$
and $c_1$ is a unit by (b). So $b(\theta_c) \equiv c \pmod p$. $\square$

*Remark.* Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's own $\varepsilon = 1 - 2E$ has $\chi(\varepsilon) = 1$: their $E$ averages $\tau^{2i}\omega(\tau)^{-i}$, and $\chi$ kills it because $\omega(\tau) \ne 1$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 421]. So their automorphism has $b \equiv 1 \pmod p$. The factor $S_-$ in $P$ and $Q$ is what makes $\eta_c\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$ automatic: $S_-$ kills every element on which $\sigma$ acts trivially, while $\chi(S_-) = 1$.

**Proof of part 3.** Jannsen and Wingberg take the endomorphism $\psi_{JW}$ of $F$ fixing $\sigma, \tau, x_0$ with $\psi_{JW}(x_1) = x_1^{1+p}$ [@jannsenwingberg1982, §5.1, p. 96]. In the indexing used here, write $P_2^1 = P_2$, $P_2^2 = \Phi(P_2)$, $P_2^3 = (P_2^2)^p[P_2^2, P_2]$, and $r = x_0^{-\sigma}\langle x_0, \tau\rangle^g x_1^p[x_1, y_1]$.

- *$\psi_{JW}$ induces an automorphism of $G/V^3$ over $\mathcal{G}$.* It fixes $A = x_0^{-\sigma}\langle x_0, \tau\rangle^g$, and $\psi_{JW}(r) = A\,x_1^{p}x_1^{p^2}[x_1 x_1^p, \psi_{JW}(y_1)]$. Here $x_1^{p^2} \in (P_2^2)^p \subseteq P_2^3$. Since $\psi_{JW} \equiv \mathrm{id}$ modulo $P_2^2$ on generators, it is so on all of $F$, so $\psi_{JW}(y_1) = y_1 v$ with $v \in P_2^2$. As $P_2^2/P_2^3$ is central in $P_2/P_2^3$, $[x_1x_1^p, y_1v] \equiv [x_1, y_1]$ modulo $P_2^3$. So $\psi_{JW}(r) \equiv r$ modulo $P_2^3$, which is Jannsen and Wingberg's congruence [@jannsenwingberg1982, §5.1, p. 96]. As the kernel $N$ of $F \to G$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by $r$, $\psi_{JW}$ maps $NP_2^3$ into itself and induces a surjective endomorphism of $F/NP_2^3 = G/V^3$, which is an automorphism, since $G/V^3$ is topologically finitely generated.
- *No automorphism of $G$ induces it.* Let $\bar I$ be the image of $V$ in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, written additively. Conjugation is trivial on $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and $(p-1)[\tau] = 0$ by the tame relation, so $\pi$ kills $[\tau]$. Abelianizing the relation gives $[x_0] = (gS/(p-1))[x_0] + p[x_1]$, so $[x_0] \in p\mathbb{Z}_p[x_1]$ as in part 2 (b), and $\bar I = \mathbb{Z}_p[x_1]$. The group $\bar I$ is the pro-$p$ Sylow subgroup of the image of inertia in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, because inertia modulo $V$ has order prime to $p$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.2), p. 410]. As $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ maps the image of inertia onto $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, it maps $\bar I$ onto the Sylow subgroup $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$. Hence $\bar I \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$, and $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is injective on $\bar I$. Every $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ preserves $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$, so $\theta^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the identity on $\bar I$. The images of $V^2$ and $V^3$ in $\bar I$ lie in $p\bar I$ and $p^2\bar I$. If $\theta$ induced $\psi_{JW}$ on $G/V^3$, then $[x_1] = \theta^{\mathrm{ab}}[x_1] \equiv (1+p)[x_1]$ modulo $p^2\bar I$, so $p[x_1] \in p^2\mathbb{Z}_p[x_1]$, which is impossible in $\bar I \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ with generator $[x_1]$. $\square$

So Wingberg's lifting theorem in its printed form, that every automorphism of $G/V^3$ over $\mathcal{G}$ lifts to an automorphism of $G$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Satz 2, pp. 100–101], fails for $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$. His $p^s$-central series starts at $Z^0 = Z$, so his $V^2$ is the $V^3$ here [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, p. 100]. Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg note that Satz 2 was stated incorrectly. They state the corrected form, agreement modulo $V^2$, as what Wingberg's proof gives, and note that Jannsen and Wingberg's automorphism might therefore be inner [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15) and the remark after it, p. 421]. Part 3 shows that no automorphism of $G$ induces it modulo $V^3$. Wingberg's proof of Satz 2 refers to the proof of his Satz 3, whose inductive step replaces the map at each level by the reduction of a solution two levels up [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, pp. 111–112].
