**Setting.** Fix a prime $\ell$. A *function field* $F/\kappa$ is a finitely generated extension of an algebraically closed field $\kappa$. Put $W_F = \mathrm{Hom}(F^\times, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$, with the topology of pointwise convergence, and $\widehat F = \varprojlim_e F^\times/F^{\times \ell^e}$, written additively, with $[f]$ the class of $f \in F^\times$. Every character kills the divisible group $\kappa^\times$. Evaluation gives $\widehat F = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(W_F, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ and $\widehat F/\ell^e \widehat F = F^\times/F^{\times \ell^e}$, and evaluation on $W_F$ detects every class modulo $\ell^e$ [@openai2026bp, Lemma 2.1, p. 4]. When $\mathrm{char}\, F \ne \ell$, [Kummer theory](kummer-theory.md) identifies $W_F$ with $\Pi^a_F$ [@openai2026bp, §2, p. 4]. A continuous isomorphism $\varphi : W_L \to W_K$ has the evaluation dual $\Theta : \widehat K \to \widehat L$, $\Theta(h)(\sigma) = h(\varphi\sigma)$ [@openai2026bp, (1), p. 4].

For a valuation $v$ of $F$, with units $U_v$ and principal units $U^1_v = 1 + \mathfrak{m}_v$, put $I_v = U_v^\perp$ and $D_v = (U^1_v)^\perp$ in $W_F$ [@openai2026bp, §3.2, p. 7]. Under Kummer duality these are the minimized inertia and decomposition groups $T^1_v \subseteq Z^1_v$ of the entry on [quasi prime divisors](quasi-prime-divisor.md). A *quasi-prime $r$-divisor* of $F/\kappa$, of transcendence degree $s$, is a composite of $r$ successive quasi prime divisors of the successive residue function fields. It has $vF/v\kappa \cong \mathbb{Z}^r$ and $\mathrm{trdeg}(Fv/\kappa v) = s - r$, and $\kappa v$ is algebraically closed [@openai2026bp, §3.2, (3), p. 8]. Restriction to units gives $D_v/I_v \cong W_{Fv}$, and the class of a $v$-unit $u$ vanishes on $I_v$ and induces on $W_{Fv}$ the class of its residue $\bar u$ [@openai2026bp, Lemma 3.2, p. 8]. This is checked here: since $\kappa v$ is algebraically closed, there is an exact sequence
$$0 \to (Fv)^\times/(\kappa v)^\times \to F^\times/(\kappa^\times U^1_v) \to vF/v\kappa \to 0$$
with free last term, so it splits, and applying $\mathrm{Hom}(-, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ gives $0 \to I_v \to D_v \to W_{Fv} \to 0$.

For a smooth projective curve $C$ over an algebraically closed field, with function field $E$, the *point inertia line* of a point $P$ is $I_P = \mathbb{Z}_\ell \cdot \mathrm{ord}_P \subseteq W_E$.

Fix a normal integral projective model $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^N_l$ of a function field $L/l$, and write $\deg D$ for the degree of a prime divisor $D$ of $X$ in this embedding. Each $\mathrm{ord}_D$ extends continuously to $\widehat L \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell$. For $h \in \widehat L$ and $e \ge 1$ put
$$S_e(h) = \{D : \mathrm{ord}_D(h) \not\equiv 0 \bmod \ell^e\}, \qquad \mathrm{Supp}_X(h) = \{D : \mathrm{ord}_D(h) \ne 0\}$$
[@openai2026bp, §5, p. 15]. Each $S_e(h)$ is finite, because $h$ modulo $\ell^e$ is represented by a rational function, and the $S_e(h)$ increase with $e$ to $\mathrm{Supp}_X(h)$. For $g \in L^\times$ and $m \ge 2$ put $s_m(g) = \sum \deg D$, the sum over the prime divisors $D$ with $\mathrm{ord}_D(g) \not\equiv 0 \bmod m$ [@openai2026bp, §4, p. 12].

**Matching residual curves.** Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be function fields of the same transcendence degree $d \ge 2$, with $\mathrm{char}\, k, \mathrm{char}\, l \ne \ell$. Say that a continuous $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module isomorphism $\varphi : W_L \to W_K$ *matches residual curves* if:

- (M1) for every quasi-prime $(d-1)$-divisor $w$ of $L/l$ there is a quasi-prime $(d-1)$-divisor $v$ of $K/k$ with $\varphi(I_w) = I_v$ and $\varphi(D_w) = D_v$;
- (M2) for $w, v$ as in (M1), if $lw$ is algebraic over a finite field, then so is $kv$, and the isomorphism $W_{Lw} = D_w/I_w \to D_v/I_v = W_{Kv}$ induced by $\varphi$ maps the point inertia lines of the smooth projective curve with function field $Lw$ bijectively onto those of the curve with function field $Kv$.

The manuscript claims that every bracket-compatible isomorphism, the datum of the [Bogomolov–Pop conjecture](bogomolov-pop-conjecture.md), matches residual curves. (M1) is part of its Proposition 3.5, and (M2) is its Proposition 3.7, which applies Pop's residual-curve criterion on both sides [@openai2026bp, Propositions 3.5 and 3.7, pp. 10–12]. **These propositions, and the rest of the manuscript's §3, are not checked here.** For quasi prime divisors themselves, rather than $(d-1)$-divisors, compare the [recovery of quasi-divisorial groups](quasi-divisorial-groups-recoverable.md) from $\Pi^c$.

**Theorem.**

1. *Finite residual tests* [@openai2026bp, Lemma 4.1, p. 12]. There is an integer $G_X \ge 0$, depending only on the embedded variety $X$, with the following property. For $g_1, \dots, g_r \in L^\times$ and $q = \ell^e$, $e \ge 1$, there is a quasi-prime $(d-1)$-divisor $w$ of $L/l$ such that:
   - (a) $lw$ is an algebraic closure of a finite field of characteristic different from $\ell$, and $Lw = lw(C)$ for a smooth projective integral curve $C/lw$ of genus at most $G_X$;
   - (b) $w(g_j) = 0$ for every $j$, and for every $(n_1, \dots, n_r) \in (\mathbb{Z}/q)^r$, $\prod_j g_j^{n_j} \in L^{\times q}$ exactly when $\prod_j \bar g_j^{n_j} \in (Lw)^{\times q}$, where $\bar g_j$ is the residue of $g_j$;
   - (c) for $m = \ell$ and $m = q$, the number of points $P \in C(lw)$ with $\mathrm{ord}_P(\bar g_j) \not\equiv 0 \bmod m$ is $s_m(g_j)$, for each $j$.
2. *Uniform pencil support* [@openai2026bp, Theorem 5.1, p. 15]. Suppose that $\varphi$ matches residual curves. Then for every $t \in K \setminus k$ there is a constant $B_t$ with
$$\sum_{D \in \mathrm{Supp}_X(\Theta[t-a])} \deg D \le B_t \quad \text{for every } a \in k.$$
In particular $\Theta[z]$ has finite support on $X$ for every $z \in K^\times$ [@openai2026bp, Corollary 5.2, p. 17]: for $z \notin k$ take $t = z$ and $a = 0$, and for $z \in k^\times$ the class $[z]$ is $0$.

Part 1 involves no Galois group and no $\varphi$. In part 2, bracket compatibility enters only through (M1) and (M2).

**Proof of part 1.** This is the manuscript's proof [@openai2026bp, proof of Lemma 4.1, pp. 13–15], checked here.

- *Spreading out.* Let $D_1, \dots, D_s$ be the prime divisors occurring in the divisors of the $g_j$. Outside a closed subset $B_X$ of codimension at least $2$, $X$ is smooth, the $D_i$ are smooth and pairwise disjoint, and near each $D_i$ every $g_j$ is a unit times a power of a local equation. On a smooth dense open $U$ where all $g_j$ are units, the root cover $V = \mathrm{Spec}_U\, \mathcal{O}_U[T_1, \dots, T_r]/(T_j^q - g_j)$ is finite étale of degree $q^r$. Let $H \subseteq L^\times/L^{\times q}$ be the subgroup generated by the $[g_j]$. As $\mu_q \subset l$, Kummer theory makes the generic fibre of $V$ a product of $q^r/|H|$ fields of degree $|H|$ over $L$, and as $V$ is normal these are its components. All of this descends to a finitely generated domain $R \subset l$ in which $\ell$ is invertible, and after localizing, every geometric fibre of the model is normal and integral with the Hilbert polynomial $P_X$ of $X$, the $D_{i,R}$ keep their degrees, the local identities hold off a fibrewise codimension-$2$ set, and the components of the root cover have geometrically integral fibres of degree $|H|$. A separating transcendence basis $x_1, \dots, x_d$ of $L/l$ and a primitive element $y$ are spread out too, keeping the reduced minimal polynomial of $y$ irreducible of the same degree and the reductions of the numerators and denominators of the $g_j$ nonzero.
- *A good fibre as a residue field.* Take a closed point $s$ of $\mathrm{Spec}\, R$, with finite residue field of characteristic different from $\ell$, and a valuation $s_0$ of $l$ dominating its local ring. Its residue field is algebraically closed, as that of an algebraically closed field. If it is not algebraic over a finite field, compose with a valuation that gives a transcendence basis independent values. This does not move the centre, because nonzero elements of a finite field have value $0$. So the residue field $\lambda$ is an algebraic closure of a finite field. Extend $s_0$ to $l(x_1, \dots, x_d)$ by the Gauss valuation, with residue field $\lambda(x_1, \dots, x_d)$, and prolong it to $L$. The residue of $y$ satisfies the reduced polynomial, irreducible of degree $[L : l(x_1, \dots, x_d)]$, so the fundamental inequality forces this residue degree and ramification index $1$. The result $s_X$ has $s_X L = s_0 l$, residue field $\lambda(X_s)$, where $X_s$ is the fibre over $s$ base changed to $\lambda$, and it reduces each $g_j$ to its specialization.
- *Cutting down to a curve.* Over the infinite field $\lambda$, intersect $X_s$ with $d-1$ general hyperplanes. The curve $C$ obtained is smooth and integral, misses the bad set, which has dimension at most $d-2$, and meets each $D_{i,s}$ transversally in $\deg D_i$ points, with no point on two of them. By the manuscript's Bertini lemma for quasi-finite maps from integral varieties of dimension at least $2$ [@openai2026bp, Lemma 4.2, p. 13], each successive section, and each component of the root cover restricted to it, stays integral. The hyperplanes can be chosen to avoid associated points, so $P_C(n) = \Delta^{d-1} P_X(n)$ with $\Delta P(n) = P(n) - P(n-1)$. Hence $g(C) = 1 - P_C(0)$ depends only on $P_X$, and this value is $G_X$.
- *The valuation.* Compose $s_X$ with the divisorial valuations of the flag. After the first step the relative value group is the innermost convex copy of $\mathbb{Z}$ and the residue transcendence degree is $d-1$. Every proper coarsening kills this copy of $\mathbb{Z}$, so it is a quasi prime divisor. The later steps are prime divisors of residue function fields over $\lambda$. This gives a quasi-prime $(d-1)$-divisor $w$ with $lw = \lambda$ and $Lw = \lambda(C)$, and (a) holds. Each $g_j$ is a unit at every generic point of the flag, so $w(g_j) = 0$ and $\bar g_j = g_j|_C$.
- *(b).* If $\prod_j g_j^{n_j} = b^q$, then $q\,w(b) = 0$, so $w(b) = 0$ as the value group is torsion-free, and reducing gives a relation among the $\bar g_j$. Conversely, the root cover restricted to $U_s \cap C$ has $q^r/|H|$ components, each integral of degree $|H|$. By Kummer theory over $\lambda(C)$, it has $q^r/|\bar H|$ components of degree $|\bar H|$, where $\bar H$ is generated by the classes of the $\bar g_j$. So $|\bar H| = |H|$, and the two relation subgroups of $(\mathbb{Z}/q)^r$, one inside the other, have the same order.
- *(c).* At each of the $\deg D_i$ points of $C \cap D_{i,s}$, transversality and the local identity give $\mathrm{ord}_P(\bar g_j) = \mathrm{ord}_{D_i}(g_j)$, and elsewhere on $C$ each $\bar g_j$ is a unit. So the count is $s_m(g_j)$, for every $m$ dividing $q$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** This is the manuscript's proof [@openai2026bp, proof of Theorem 5.1, pp. 15–17], checked here, with (M1) and (M2) in place of its Propositions 3.5 and 3.7.

*Step 1: anchors.* Choose five distinct $a_1, \dots, a_5 \in k$ including $0$, and if $\mathrm{char}\, k = 0$ also including $1$ and $\ell$. Put $h_a = \Theta[t-a]$, $N_i = \sum_{D \in S_1(h_{a_i})} \deg D$, which is finite, $c_\ell = 5(1 - 1/\ell) - 2 = 3 - 5/\ell \ge 1/2$, and
$$B = \max\Big\{1, \frac{2G_X - 2 + \sum_{i=1}^5 N_i}{c_\ell}\Big\}.$$
This depends only on $X$, $\Theta$, $t$ and the anchors. The claim is proved with $B_t = 2B$.

*Step 2: independent classes.* Fix $a \in k$, and list the distinct elements among the anchors and $a$ as $a_j$, with $u_j = t - a_j$. For each $j$, the nonconstant function $u_j$ has a zero along some prime divisor of a normal projective model of $K/k$. Its order function gives $o_j \in W_K$ with $o_j(u_j) = e_j \ge 1$ and $o_j(u_h) = 0$ for $h \ne j$, since $u_h = u_j + (a_j - a_h)$ with $a_j - a_h \in k^\times$. So for $q = \ell^e$ large enough, the classes of the $[u_j]$ modulo $q$ are nonzero and pairwise distinct, and any two of them generate a noncyclic group. The required size of $e$ may depend on $a$. Choose $g_j \in L^\times$ representing $h_{a_j}$ modulo $q$, and apply part 1, giving $w$ and $C$. As $\Theta$ is an isomorphism, by (b) the residue classes of the $\bar g_j$ in $(Lw)^\times/(Lw)^{\times q}$ satisfy the same relations as the $[u_j]$ modulo $q$. Let $v$ be the quasi-prime $(d-1)$-divisor of $K/k$ given by (M1). For $\sigma \in D_w$, $[u_j](\varphi\sigma) = h_{a_j}(\sigma) \equiv [g_j](\sigma) \bmod q$, and $[g_j]$ restricted to $D_w$ induces on $W_{Lw}$ the class of $\bar g_j$, since $g_j$ is a $w$-unit. Since $\varphi(D_w) = D_v$ and evaluation on $W_{Lw}$ detects classes modulo $q$, the restrictions of the $[u_j]$ to $D_v$, modulo $q$, are nonzero, pairwise distinct, and generate a noncyclic group.

*Step 3: $v(k(t)) = vk$.* Otherwise $v(k(t))/vk$ is a nonzero subgroup of $vK/vk \cong \mathbb{Z}^{d-1}$. The inequality $\mathrm{rrank}(v(k(t))/vk) + \mathrm{trdeg}(k(t)v/kv) \le 1$ then makes it cyclic and gives $k(t)v = kv$. So an element of $k(t)^\times$ whose value lies in $vk$ is a constant times a principal unit. Characters in $D_v$ kill constants and principal units, so restriction to $D_v$ factors through the cyclic group $v(k(t))/vk$ on $k(t)^\times$, contradicting Step 2.

*Step 4: the residual pencil.* The values $v(u_j) \in vk$ are all equal, say to $\gamma$. Indeed, if $v(u_j) < v(u_h)$, then $v(a_h - a_j) = v(u_j)$, as $u_j = u_h + (a_h - a_j)$, so $u_j/(a_h - a_j) = 1 + u_h/(a_h - a_j)$ is a principal unit and $[u_j]$ vanishes on $D_v$. Next, $v(a_h - a_j) = \gamma$ for $j \ne h$: it is at least $\gamma$, and if larger, $u_j/u_h = 1 + (a_h - a_j)/u_h$ is a principal unit and $[u_j], [u_h]$ agree on $D_v$. Take $b \in k^\times$ with $v(b) = \gamma$ and one listed $a_0$, and let $\tau_0$ and $\beta_j$ be the residues of $(t - a_0)/b$ and $(a_j - a_0)/b$. The $\beta_j$ are pairwise distinct elements of $kv$, and the residue of $u_j/b$ is $\tau_0 - \beta_j$. As $v((t - a_0)/b) = 0$, $\tau_0 \ne 0$. If $\tau_0$ were in $kv$, then $u_0$ would be a constant times a principal unit, and $[u_0]$ would vanish on $D_v$. So $\tau_0 \notin kv$. In characteristic $0$, the anchors $0, 1, \ell$ give $v(\ell) = v(1) = \gamma = 0$, so $\mathrm{char}(kv) \ne \ell$. In characteristic $p > 0$ this holds because $p \ne \ell$.

*Step 5: comparing the residual curves.* By (M2), $kv$ is algebraic over a finite field, and the induced isomorphism $\bar\varphi : W_{Lw} \to W_{Kv}$ matches the point inertia lines of $C$ with those of the smooth projective curve $C'/kv$ with function field $Kv$. Matched generators differ by units of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. Since both residue characteristics differ from $\ell$, the kernel of all point orders on $(Lw)^\times/(Lw)^{\times \ell}$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Pic}(C)[\ell]$, of dimension $2g(C)$, and likewise for $C'$ [@openai2026bp, Lemma 2.4, p. 5] [@stacks2026, Lemma 53.17.1, Tag 0C1Z]. The dual of $\bar\varphi$ matches these kernels, so $g(C') = g(C) \le G_X$. As constants have zero class, $[u_j]$ restricted to $D_v$ is the class of the unit $u_j/b$, which induces $[\tau_0 - \beta_j]$ on $W_{Kv}$. By Step 2, $[\tau_0 - \beta_j] \circ \bar\varphi \equiv [\bar g_j] \bmod q$. So for $m \in \{\ell, q\}$, the number of points of $C'$ where $\tau_0 - \beta_j$ has order $\not\equiv 0 \bmod m$ equals the number of points of $C$ where $\bar g_j$ does, which is $s_m(g_j)$ by (c). As $g_j \equiv h_{a_j} \bmod q$, this is $N_i$ when $m = \ell$ and $a_j = a_i$ is an anchor, and $\sum_{D \in S_e(h_{a_j})} \deg D$ when $m = q$.

*Step 6: the degree bound.* Let $p = \mathrm{char}(kv) > 0$. Write $\tau_0 = \tau^{p^r}$ with $r \ge 0$ maximal, which exists because $\tau_0$ has a nonzero order somewhere. Then $\tau$ is not a $p$-th power, so it gives a separable morphism $\tau : C' \to \mathbb{P}^1$ of some degree $n \ge 1$. With $\beta'_j$ the $p^r$-th root of $\beta_j$ in the perfect field $kv$, the $\beta'_j$ are distinct and $\tau_0 - \beta_j = (\tau - \beta'_j)^{p^r}$. As $p \ne \ell$, the factor $p^r$ does not change which orders vanish modulo $\ell$ or $q$. At a point $P$ over $\beta'_i$, $\mathrm{ord}_P(\tau - \beta'_i)$ is the ramification index $e_P$. So by Step 5 at most $N_i$ points over $\beta'_i$ have $\ell \nmid e_P$, and the others have $e_P \ge \ell$. As $\sum_{P \mapsto \beta'_i} e_P = n$, the fibre has $r_i \le N_i + n/\ell$ points, and $\sum_{P \mapsto \beta'_i}(e_P - 1) = n - r_i \ge (1 - 1/\ell)n - N_i$. The Riemann–Hurwitz formula for separable maps, with the different exponent at $P$ at least $e_P - 1$ even under wild ramification [@stacks2026, Lemma 53.12.4, Tag 0C1F], summed over the five disjoint fibres, gives
$$2g(C') - 2 + 2n \ge 5(1 - 1/\ell)n - \sum_{i=1}^5 N_i.$$
So $c_\ell n \le 2G_X - 2 + \sum_i N_i$, and $n \le B$.

*Step 7: exact supports.* All zeros and poles of $\tau - \beta'_j$ lie over $\beta'_j$ and $\infty$, so there are at most $2n \le 2B$ of them. By Step 5 with $m = q$,
$$\sum_{D \in S_e(h_a)} \deg D \le 2B$$
for every sufficiently large $e$. The bound does not depend on $a$ or $e$. If $\mathrm{Supp}_X(h_a)$ had total degree more than $2B$, a finite subset of it would too, and that subset lies in $S_e(h_a)$ for $e$ large. $\square$

**What was checked here.** Every step above was rebuilt from the manuscript, and no gap was found. In particular:

- *Five anchors.* With four anchors the constant would be $4(1 - 1/\ell) - 2 = 2 - 4/\ell$, which vanishes for $\ell = 2$. With five it is $3 - 5/\ell \ge 1/2$ for every prime, as the manuscript says [@openai2026bp, §1.2, p. 3].
- *Wild ramification.* Step 6 uses only the inequality between the different exponent and $e_P - 1$, not the tame formula, so it holds when $p$ divides some $e_P$.
- *Uniformity.* The required size of $e$ in Step 2 depends on $a$, but $B$ does not, since the residual test is applied afresh for each $a$ and $e$, and $G_X$ depends only on $X$.
- *The anchors $1$ and $\ell$.* In characteristic $0$ they are used only to show $\mathrm{char}(kv) \ne \ell$, which Step 5 needs.

Not re-proved: the manuscript's Bertini lemma, its Lemma 4.2, and the standard spreading-out properties, which it takes from EGA IV and the Stacks project [@openai2026bp, pp. 13–14]. Its Lemma 4.2 is a form of Bertini's irreducibility theorem, and the manuscript writes out an incidence argument for it.

**Role in the manuscript.** The manuscript calls the uniform pencil bound and the incidence argument that recovers curve subfields its two new geometric steps [@openai2026bp, §1.2, p. 3]. The finite supports of Corollary 5.2 and the bound feed into its §6, which recovers curve subfields; that section is not checked here.
