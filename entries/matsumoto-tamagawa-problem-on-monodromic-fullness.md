**Problem** [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Introduction, p. 232]. Matsumoto and Tamagawa asked the following. Let $X$ be a hyperbolic curve over a number field $k$. Are these conditions equivalent?

- (MT1) $X$ is quasi-$\ell$-monodromically full for every prime $\ell$.
- (MT2) $X$ is $\ell$-monodromically full for some prime $\ell$.
- (MT3) $X$ is $\ell$-monodromically full for all but finitely many primes $\ell$.

The terms are defined in [monodromically full hyperbolic curves](monodromically-full-hyperbolic-curve.md). The three conditions mirror the conditions of Serre's theorem for an elliptic curve without complex multiplication: open image at every $\ell$, surjectivity at some $\ell$, and surjectivity at almost all $\ell$ [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Introduction, p. 232]. Clearly (MT3) implies (MT2).

**Theorem** (Hoshi) [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Theorem A, p. 233, and its proof, p. 241]. Let $k$ be a number field.

1. For every odd prime $\ell$, the split curve $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \ell, \infty\}$ of type $(0,4)$ satisfies (MT3), but it is not quasi-$\ell$-monodromically full. So it satisfies (MT2) but not (MT1).
2. For every $r > 4$ there is a split hyperbolic curve of type $(0, r)$ over $k$ that satisfies (MT2) but not (MT1).

So the answer is negative already in genus $0$.

**Why $\ell$ is special.** Let $U = \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$, let $\Delta$ be the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of $\pi_1(U \otimes_k \bar k)$, let $\Phi$ be the image of $\pi_1(U)$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)$, and let $k^{\mathrm{tpd}}$ be the field cut out by the pro-$\ell$ outer Galois representation of $U$. A point $\lambda \in k \setminus \{0,1\} = U(k)$ gives a homomorphism $G_k \to \pi_1(U)$. The curve $X_\lambda = \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \lambda, \infty\}$ is $\ell$-monodromically full exactly when the image of $G_k$ in $\Phi$ is all of $\Phi$, and quasi-full exactly when that image is open [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Definition 1.1 and Lemma 1.3, pp. 235–236].

- *Anderson and Ihara.* Let $E_\ell \subseteq \bar k^\times$ be the group of higher circular $\ell$-units. It is generated by the points other than $0$ and $\infty$ of the finite subsets of $\mathbb{P}^1(\bar k)$ obtained from $\{0, 1, \infty\}$ by repeatedly taking preimages under $t \mapsto t^\ell$ (together with $\infty$) and applying automorphisms $\phi$ of $\mathbb{P}^1$ such that the image set contains $0$, $1$ and $\infty$. For odd $\ell$, $k^{\mathrm{tpd}} = k(E_\ell)$, every element of $E_\ell$ is an $\ell$-th power of an element of $E_\ell$, and $\mu_{\ell^\infty} \subseteq E_\ell$ [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Definition 2.1 and Proposition 2.2, p. 236]. This is the field of the [Anderson–Ihara question](anderson-ihara-question.md).
- *$\ell \in E_\ell$.* Each $1 - \zeta_\ell^i$ lies in $E_\ell$, and $\ell = \prod_{i=1}^{\ell-1} (1 - \zeta_\ell^i)$ [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Lemma 2.4, p. 237].
- *Kummer quotient.* Suppose $\mu_{\ell^\infty} \subseteq k$. The Fermat covers $\mathrm{Spec}\, k[x^{\pm 1}, y^{\pm 1}]/(x^{\ell^n} + y^{\ell^n} - 1) \to U$, $t \mapsto x^{\ell^n}$, define a quotient $\pi_1(U) \to \mathbb{Z}_\ell^2$ that factors through $\Phi$. Their fibre at $\lambda$ is cut out by $\lambda^{1/\ell^n}$ and $(1-\lambda)^{1/\ell^n}$ [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Lemma 2.5, p. 237].
- *Conclusion.* Suppose that $\lambda$ is a root of unity, or that $\ell$ is odd and $\lambda = u\lambda_0$ with $u \in k$ a root of unity and $\lambda_0 \in E_\ell \cap k$. The exact sequence $1 \to \Delta \to \Phi \to \mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{tpd}}/k) \to 1$ allows one to replace $k$ by $k^{\mathrm{tpd}}$, which contains $\mu_{\ell^\infty}$, without changing whether the image is open. Over $k^{\mathrm{tpd}}$ every $\ell^n$-th root of $\lambda$ is already rational, so the fibre field at $\lambda$ is $k^{\mathrm{tpd}}((1-\lambda)^{1/\ell^n})$, and the image of $G_{k^{\mathrm{tpd}}}$ in $(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)^2$ is cyclic for every $n$. So its image in $\mathbb{Z}_\ell^2$ is not open, and $X_\lambda$ is not quasi-$\ell$-monodromically full [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Proposition 2.6 and its proof, pp. 238–239]. For $\lambda = \ell$, none of $\ell$, $1 - \ell$ and $\ell/(\ell - 1)$ is a unit, so the criterion in item 2 below gives (MT3) [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, proof of Theorem A, p. 241].

Part 2 of the theorem removes $r - 4$ suitable rational points from such a curve, using results from Hoshi's paper on monodromically full points of configuration spaces, which has not been opened here [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, proof of Theorem A, p. 241]. A partial compactification of a quasi-full curve is quasi-full [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remark 2.2.5, p. 63], so the result is not quasi-$\ell$-monodromically full.

**Criteria in type $(0,4)$.** Let $k$ be a number field, let $\lambda \in k \setminus \{0, 1\}$, and put $X_\lambda = \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \lambda, \infty\}$. For $a \in k^\times$ write $[a]_\pm$ for the set of finite primes $\mathfrak{p}$ of $k$ with $v_\mathfrak{p}(a) \ne 0$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 7.5, p. 89].

1. *Two primes* [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 7.7 and Theorem 7.9, p. 90]. Let $\ell$ be odd. Suppose that there are distinct finite primes $\mathfrak{p}_0, \mathfrak{q}_0$ of $k$, not above $\ell$, with $\mathfrak{p}_0 \in [\lambda]_\pm \setminus [1-\lambda]_\pm$, $\ell \nmid v_{\mathfrak{p}_0}(\lambda)$, and $\mathfrak{q}_0 \in [1-\lambda]_\pm \setminus [\lambda]_\pm$, $\ell \nmid v_{\mathfrak{q}_0}(1-\lambda)$. Then $X_\lambda$ is $\ell$-monodromically full. Proof: $X_\lambda$ is $\ell$-monodromically full exactly when the fibre at $\lambda$ of the Fermat cover $s^\ell + t^\ell = 1$, $u \mapsto s^\ell$, stays connected over the field $k_\ell$ that the mod-$\ell$ abelianized tripod representation cuts out over $k(\zeta_\ell)$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 7.1 and Lemma 7.4, pp. 83–88]. By Anderson and Ihara, $k_\ell/k(\zeta_\ell)$ is abelian of $\ell$-power degree and unramified outside $\ell$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 7.2, p. 84]. The hypotheses make $k(\zeta_\ell, \lambda^{1/\ell})$ ramified at $\mathfrak{p}_0$ and unramified at $\mathfrak{q}_0$, and the reverse for $(1-\lambda)^{1/\ell}$. So $[k_\ell(\lambda^{1/\ell}, (1-\lambda)^{1/\ell}) : k_\ell] = \ell^2$, and the fibre is connected [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 7.8, p. 90].
2. *No units* [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 7.10 and Corollary 7.11, p. 91]. For a curve $X$ of type $(0,4)$ over $k$ with $X \otimes_k \bar k \cong \mathbb{P}^1_{\bar k} \setminus \{0, 1, \lambda_X, \infty\}$, put $\mathfrak{m}_X = \{\lambda_X, 1/\lambda_X, 1 - \lambda_X, 1/(1-\lambda_X), \lambda_X/(\lambda_X - 1), (\lambda_X - 1)/\lambda_X\}$. This set determines $X \otimes_k \bar k$ up to isomorphism. If $\mathfrak{m}_X$ contains no algebraic unit, then $X$ is $\Sigma$-monodromically full for a cofinite set $\Sigma$ of primes. For $X = X_\lambda$ the hypothesis says that none of $\lambda$, $1 - \lambda$, $\lambda/(\lambda - 1)$ is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_k$. Proof: some $\lambda' \in \mathfrak{m}_X$ then has $[\lambda']_\pm \not\subseteq [1-\lambda']_\pm$ and $[1-\lambda']_\pm \not\subseteq [\lambda']_\pm$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemma 7.6 (v), p. 89], and item 1 applies to $\lambda'$ for all but finitely many $\ell$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remark 7.7.1, p. 90].
3. *Small fields* [@hoshi2011monodromic, Corollary 7.12, pp. 91–92]. Let $k = \mathbb{Q}$, or $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})$ with $d \ne 1, 3$ a squarefree positive integer. Then $X_\lambda$ is $\ell$-monodromically full for some $\ell$ if and only if it is for a cofinite set of $\ell$, if and only if $\lambda \notin \{-1, 2, 1/2\}$, that is, $X_\lambda \not\cong \mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, -1, \infty\}$. That curve has more automorphisms than a general curve of type $(0,4)$, so it is not quasi-full for any $\ell$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, proof of Corollary 7.12, p. 92].
4. *Units and roots of unity* [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Theorem B, p. 233]. Let $k_0 \subseteq k$ be a subfield, and let $X$ be of type $(0,4)$ over $k_0$ with $X \otimes_{k_0} k$ split, so that $\mathfrak{m}_X \subseteq k$. Consider: (1) $\mathfrak{m}_X$ contains no unit of $\mathcal{O}_k$; (2) $X$ is $\Sigma$-monodromically full over $k_0$ for a cofinite $\Sigma$; (3) $X$ is $\ell$-monodromically full over $k_0$ for some $\ell$; (4) $X$ is quasi-$\ell$-monodromically full over $k_0$ for some $\ell$; (5) $\mathfrak{m}_X$ contains no root of unity. Then (1) ⇒ (2) ⇒ (3) ⇒ (4) ⇒ (5), and all five are equivalent if $k$ is imaginary quadratic. In particular (MT2) and (MT3) are equivalent for such $X$ over an imaginary quadratic field.
5. *Moving curves* [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 8.1 and Corollary 8.2, p. 93]. Let $k$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, and let $X$ of type $(0,4)$ over $k$ be *not NF-isotrivial*: no base change of $X$ to a finite extension of $k$ is defined over a number field. Then $X$ is $\Sigma$-monodromically full for a cofinite $\Sigma$. The proof specializes to a closed point where item 2 applies [@hoshi2011monodromic, proof of Corollary 8.2, pp. 93–94]. This fails in type $(0,5)$. Over the function field $k$ of $\mathbb{P}^1_{k_0} \setminus \{0, 1, -1, \infty\}$, with generic point $t$, the curve $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, -1, t, \infty\}$ is not NF-isotrivial. But it is not $\ell$-monodromically full for any $\ell$, because its partial compactification $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, -1, \infty\}$ is not [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remark 8.2.1, pp. 94–95].

**Over $\mathbb{Q}$ and imaginary quadratic fields.** This is checked here. In Hoshi's proof of item 4, the step (5) ⇒ (1) uses only that every unit of $\mathcal{O}_k$ is a root of unity, and the other steps hold over every number field [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, proof of Theorem B, p. 241]. Since $\mathbb{Z}^\times = \{\pm 1\}$, all five conditions are also equivalent for $k = \mathbb{Q}$. So for curves of type $(0,4)$ that split over $\mathbb{Q}$ or over an imaginary quadratic field $k$, (MT1) implies (4), hence (MT3), and (MT2) is equivalent to (MT3). Part 1 of the theorem, with $k$ itself as the base field, shows that (MT2) still does not imply (MT1) for these curves.

**Status.** Hoshi's paper treats only curves of genus $0$ [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Abstract, p. 231]. The opened sources do not say whether (MT2) implies (MT3) in general, or what happens for curves of positive genus.

**Context.** For the Fermat equation, the [question on $\ell$-moderate points](l-moderate-points-and-pro-l-kernel-fields.md) records Hoshi's observation that a positive answer to this problem, together with positive answers to the Anderson–Ihara question for every prime, would imply Fermat's Last Theorem. Monodromic fullness in type $(0,4)$ is also the input for [Hoshi's characterization of curves by pro-$\ell$ kernels](pro-l-kernels-determine-monodromically-full-genus-zero-curves.md).
