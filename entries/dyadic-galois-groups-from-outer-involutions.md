**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite with $\sqrt{-1} \notin K$, inside a fixed algebraic closure, and put $\tilde K = K(\sqrt{-1})$. Then $G_{\tilde K}$ is an open normal subgroup of index $2$ in $G_K$. All groups are profinite and all homomorphisms continuous. For $x \in G_K$, let $c_x \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_{\tilde K})$ be $g \mapsto xgx^{-1}$, and let $\iota_K \in \mathrm{Out}(G_{\tilde K})$ be the class of $c_s$ for any $s \in G_K \setminus G_{\tilde K}$. Two choices of $s$ differ by an element of $G_{\tilde K}$, so $\iota_K$ does not depend on the choice. For an isomorphism $\theta : G_{\tilde K} \to G_{\tilde L}$, write $\theta\iota_K\theta^{-1}$ for the class of $\theta c_s\theta^{-1}$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\tilde L})$, which depends only on $\theta$ and $\iota_K$. Write $K^0$ for the largest subfield of $K$ abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_2$.

Two facts are used throughout.

- *(S) Slimness.* $G_K$ is slim, that is, every open subgroup of $G_K$ has trivial centralizer in $G_K$ [@mochizuki2012, Theorem 1.7 (ii), p. 14], with the definition at [@mochizuki2012, §0, p. 8]. In particular, an element of $G_K$ that commutes with every element of $G_{\tilde K}$ is trivial.
- *(C) Cyclotomic characters.* An isomorphism $\Theta : G_K \to G_L$ of absolute Galois groups of $2$-adic fields carries the cyclotomic character of $K$ to that of $L$, that is, $\chi_L \circ \Theta = \chi_K$ for the $2$-adic cyclotomic characters ([invariants of $G_K$](local-field-galois-invariants.md)).

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *The index-two subgroup is intrinsic.* $G_{\tilde K}$ is the kernel of the action of $G_K$ on the $4$-th roots of unity. If $L/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is finite and $\Theta : G_K \to G_L$ is an isomorphism, then $\sqrt{-1} \notin L$ and $\Theta(G_{\tilde K}) = G_{\tilde L}$. In particular $G_{\tilde K}$ is characteristic in $G_K$.
2. *Outer automorphisms.* $\iota_K$ has order $2$. Restriction $\Phi \mapsto \Phi|_{G_{\tilde K}}$ is an injective homomorphism from $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ onto the group of automorphisms of $G_{\tilde K}$ whose classes commute with $\iota_K$. It induces an isomorphism of groups
$$\mathrm{Out}(G_K) \cong C(\iota_K)/\langle\iota_K\rangle,$$
where $C(\iota_K)$ is the centralizer of $\iota_K$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\tilde K})$.
3. *Isomorphisms.* Let $L/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite with $\sqrt{-1} \notin L$. Restriction is a bijection from the set of isomorphisms $G_K \to G_L$ onto the set of isomorphisms $\theta : G_{\tilde K} \to G_{\tilde L}$ with $\theta\iota_K\theta^{-1} = \iota_L$. So $G_K \cong G_L$ exactly when such a $\theta$ exists.
4. *The classification.* Let $L/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite with $[L:\mathbb{Q}_2] = [K:\mathbb{Q}_2]$ and $L^0 = K^0$. Then $\sqrt{-1} \notin L$, there is an isomorphism $\theta_0 : G_{\tilde K} \to G_{\tilde L}$, and $G_K \cong G_L$ exactly when $\theta_0\iota_K\theta_0^{-1}$ is conjugate to $\iota_L$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\tilde L})$. This condition does not depend on the choice of $\theta_0$.
5. *The ramified case acts trivially on the tame quotient.* Suppose that $\tilde K/K$ is ramified. Let $P_K$ and $P_{\tilde K}$ be the wild inertia groups and $\Gamma_{\tilde K} = G_{\tilde K}/P_{\tilde K}$ the tame quotient. Then $P_{\tilde K} = P_K \cap G_{\tilde K}$, the group $P_K$ is not contained in $G_{\tilde K}$, and for every $s \in P_K \setminus G_{\tilde K}$ the automorphism $c_s$ induces the identity on $\Gamma_{\tilde K}$. Hence $\iota_K$ lies in the kernel $\mathcal{N}_{\tilde K}$ of the map $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\tilde K}) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_{\tilde K})$. Moreover $\mathcal{N}_{\tilde K}$ is isomorphic to the group of automorphisms of $G_{\tilde K}$ that induce the identity on $\Gamma_{\tilde K}$, modulo conjugation by elements of $P_{\tilde K}$.
6. *Diekert's conditions fail over the larger group.* Suppose that $\tilde K/K$ is ramified. Let $T$ be the maximal tamely ramified extension of $\tilde K$, put $\Delta = \mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$, and let $\varphi : G_K \to \Delta$ be restriction. It is onto, with kernel the pro-$2$ group $P_{\tilde K}$. For no $n \ge 1$, $s \ge 2$ and character $\psi : \Delta \to (\mathbb{Z}/2^s)^\times$ does $(G_K, \varphi)$ satisfy condition 2 in Diekert's definition of a Demuškin formation over $\Delta$ with invariants $n$, $2^s$, $\psi$ (the definition is recorded in [the dyadic lifting theorem](tame-automorphisms-lift-when-adjoining-i-is-unramified.md), with $\Delta$ here in place of the tame group there). It fails at $\mathcal{H} = \ker\psi \cap \mathrm{Gal}(T/\tilde K)$.

**What this means for the classification.** By part 2 of the [classification](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), the case left open is $p = 2$ with $\tilde K/K$ ramified, and by part 3 of [the maximal abelian subfield of a 2-adic field](maximal-abelian-subfields-of-2-adic-fields.md) this depends only on $K^0$. Fix the degree $n$ and the field $A = K^0$. By part 4, the groups $G_{\tilde K}$ of all fields $K$ with these invariants are isomorphic to the absolute Galois group $D$ of one field of degree $2n$ containing $\sqrt{-1}$, which has [Diekert's presentation](local-galois-group-presentations.md). Transporting each $\iota_K$ to $D$ gives a well-defined $\mathrm{Out}(D)$-conjugacy class of involutions, and part 2 of the classification holds for these fields exactly when all these classes coincide. By part 5 they all lie in the normal subgroup $\mathcal{N}$ of classes that act trivially on the tame quotient.

Part 6 shows why Diekert's uniqueness theorem cannot simply be applied with the involution built into the base group: the natural candidate violates his freeness condition. Schneider writes that in this case there is no sensible notion of a Demuškin formation [@schneider2016, Introduction, p. 1]; part 6 checks this for that one candidate.

**Examples.**

- $K = \mathbb{Q}_2$. Part 2 gives $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}) \cong C(\iota)/\langle\iota\rangle$ inside $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})})$, where $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})}$ has Diekert's presentation. The computation of $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_2})$ is among the questions recorded in the [Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md).
- $K = U_f$, the unramified extension of degree $f$. Every automorphism of $G_{U_f}$ preserves wild inertia, so part 2, followed by the map to the outer automorphisms of the tame quotient, gives part 5 of [the dyadic lifting theorem](tame-automorphisms-lift-when-adjoining-i-is-unramified.md).
- $K = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt[4]{2})$ and $L = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt[4]{18})$, an open case in the [classification](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) with $K^0 = L^0 = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{2})$. Here $\tilde K^0 = \tilde L^0 = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{-1})$, so $G_{\tilde K} \cong G_{\tilde L}$, and $G_K \cong G_L$ exactly when the two involutions are conjugate. This is not decided here.

**Proof of part 1.** An element of $G_K$ fixes $\sqrt{-1}$ exactly when its cyclotomic character is $1$ modulo $4$, so $G_{\tilde K} = \ker(\chi_K \bmod 4)$. By (C), $\Theta$ maps this kernel onto $\ker(\chi_L \bmod 4)$. The first is a proper subgroup of $G_K$, so the second is a proper subgroup of $G_L$. Hence $\sqrt{-1} \notin L$, and the second kernel is $G_{\tilde L}$. Taking $L = K$ shows that $G_{\tilde K}$ is characteristic. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Fix $s \in G_K \setminus G_{\tilde K}$.

- *Order $2$.* As $s^2 \in G_{\tilde K}$, $\iota_K^2$ is the class of the inner automorphism $c_{s^2}$, so $\iota_K^2 = 1$. If $\iota_K = 1$, then $c_s = c_h$ for some $h \in G_{\tilde K}$, so $h^{-1}s$ commutes with every element of $G_{\tilde K}$, and $h^{-1}s = 1$ by (S). This contradicts $s \notin G_{\tilde K}$.
- *The image.* Let $\Phi \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$. By part 1, $\Phi$ maps $G_{\tilde K}$ onto itself, so $\Phi' = \Phi|_{G_{\tilde K}}$ is an automorphism. For $g \in G_{\tilde K}$, $\Phi'(c_s(\Phi'^{-1}(g))) = \Phi(s)g\Phi(s)^{-1}$, so $\Phi' c_s \Phi'^{-1} = c_{\Phi(s)}$. As $\Phi(s) \in G_K \setminus G_{\tilde K}$, its class is $\iota_K$, so the class of $\Phi'$ commutes with $\iota_K$.
- *Injectivity.* If $\Phi|_{G_{\tilde K}}$ is the identity, then for $x \in G_K$ the same computation gives $c_{\Phi(x)} = \Phi c_x \Phi^{-1} = c_x$ on $G_{\tilde K}$. So $x^{-1}\Phi(x)$ commutes with every element of $G_{\tilde K}$, and $\Phi(x) = x$ by (S).
- *Surjectivity.* This is part 3 with $L = K$.
- *Inner automorphisms.* The restrictions of the inner automorphisms of $G_K$ are the $c_x$ for $x \in G_K = G_{\tilde K} \cup G_{\tilde K}s$. They form $\mathrm{Inn}(G_{\tilde K}) \cup c_s\,\mathrm{Inn}(G_{\tilde K})$, the preimage of $\langle\iota_K\rangle$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_{\tilde K})$. So restriction identifies $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ with the preimage of $C(\iota_K)$ modulo the preimage of $\langle\iota_K\rangle$, which is $C(\iota_K)/\langle\iota_K\rangle$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Fix $s \in G_K \setminus G_{\tilde K}$ and $s' \in G_L \setminus G_{\tilde L}$.

- *Restrictions satisfy the condition.* Let $\Theta : G_K \to G_L$ be an isomorphism. By part 1, $\theta = \Theta|_{G_{\tilde K}}$ is an isomorphism onto $G_{\tilde L}$, and as in part 2, $\theta c_s \theta^{-1} = c_{\Theta(s)}$ with $\Theta(s) \in G_L \setminus G_{\tilde L}$. So $\theta\iota_K\theta^{-1} = \iota_L$.
- *Injectivity.* If $\Theta_1$ and $\Theta_2$ have the same restriction, then $\Theta_2^{-1}\Theta_1$ is an automorphism of $G_K$ that is the identity on $G_{\tilde K}$, hence the identity, by the injectivity in part 2.
- *Surjectivity.* Let $\theta$ satisfy $\theta\iota_K\theta^{-1} = \iota_L$. Then $\theta c_s\theta^{-1} = c_{s'}c_h = c_{s'h}$ for some $h \in G_{\tilde L}$. Put $s_1 = s'h$, an element of $G_L \setminus G_{\tilde L}$. Then:
  - (i) $\theta(c_s(g)) = s_1\theta(g)s_1^{-1}$ for every $g \in G_{\tilde K}$;
  - (ii) $\theta(s^2) = s_1^2$. Indeed both lie in $G_{\tilde L}$, and $c_{\theta(s^2)} = \theta c_{s^2}\theta^{-1} = (\theta c_s\theta^{-1})^2 = c_{s_1^2}$ on $G_{\tilde L}$. So $\theta(s^2)^{-1}s_1^2$ commutes with every element of $G_{\tilde L}$, and it is trivial by (S) for $L$.

  Every element of $G_K$ is uniquely $g$ or $gs$ with $g \in G_{\tilde K}$. Define $\Theta(g) = \theta(g)$ and $\Theta(gs) = \theta(g)s_1$. For $g, h \in G_{\tilde K}$, using $sh = c_s(h)s$, (i) and (ii):
$$\Theta(gs \cdot h) = \theta(g\,c_s(h))\,s_1 = \theta(g)\,s_1\theta(h)s_1^{-1}\,s_1 = \Theta(gs)\Theta(h),$$
$$\Theta(gs \cdot hs) = \theta(g\,c_s(h)\,s^2) = \theta(g)\,s_1\theta(h)s_1^{-1}\,s_1^2 = \Theta(gs)\Theta(hs),$$
  and $\Theta(g \cdot h) = \Theta(g)\Theta(h)$, $\Theta(g \cdot hs) = \Theta(g)\Theta(hs)$ are immediate. So $\Theta$ is a homomorphism. It maps $G_{\tilde K}$ bijectively onto $G_{\tilde L}$ and $G_{\tilde K}s$ bijectively onto $G_{\tilde L}s_1 = G_L \setminus G_{\tilde L}$, and it is continuous on each of these open cosets. A continuous bijection between compact Hausdorff groups is an isomorphism of topological groups, so $\Theta$ is an isomorphism, and it restricts to $\theta$. $\square$

**Proof of part 4.** The field $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})$ is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, so $\sqrt{-1} \in L$ exactly when $\sqrt{-1} \in L^0 = K^0$, which is not the case. By part 3 of [the maximal abelian subfield of a 2-adic field](maximal-abelian-subfields-of-2-adic-fields.md), $\tilde K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}} = K^0(\sqrt{-1})$ and $\tilde L \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ab}} = L^0(\sqrt{-1})$, which are equal. Also $[\tilde K:\mathbb{Q}_2] = 2[K:\mathbb{Q}_2] = [\tilde L:\mathbb{Q}_2]$, and both fields contain $\sqrt{-1}$. So part 2 of the [classification](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), which covers fields containing $\sqrt{-1}$, gives an isomorphism $\theta_0$. The isomorphisms $G_{\tilde K} \to G_{\tilde L}$ are the $\gamma\theta_0$ with $\gamma \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_{\tilde L})$, and $(\gamma\theta_0)c_s(\gamma\theta_0)^{-1} = \gamma(\theta_0 c_s\theta_0^{-1})\gamma^{-1}$. So $(\gamma\theta_0)\iota_K(\gamma\theta_0)^{-1}$ runs through the $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\tilde L})$-conjugacy class of $\theta_0\iota_K\theta_0^{-1}$, which is therefore the same class for every choice of $\theta_0$. By part 3, $G_K \cong G_L$ exactly when $\iota_L$ lies in it. $\square$

**Proof of part 5.**

- *$P_{\tilde K} = P_K \cap G_{\tilde K}$.* By [wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md), $P_{\tilde K}$ is the largest closed normal pro-$2$ subgroup of $G_{\tilde K}$, and $P_K$ that of $G_K$. The group $P_K \cap G_{\tilde K}$ is closed, normal in $G_{\tilde K}$ and pro-$2$, so it lies in $P_{\tilde K}$. Conversely, $P_{\tilde K}$ is characteristic in $G_{\tilde K}$, being the largest such subgroup, and $G_{\tilde K}$ is normal in $G_K$, so $P_{\tilde K}$ is a closed normal pro-$2$ subgroup of $G_K$. Hence $P_{\tilde K} \subseteq P_K$, and $P_{\tilde K} \subseteq P_K \cap G_{\tilde K}$.
- *$P_K \not\subseteq G_{\tilde K}$.* Otherwise $\tilde K$ lies in the fixed field of $P_K$, the maximal tamely ramified extension of $K$, so $\tilde K/K$ is tamely ramified. But it is ramified of degree $2$, equal to the residue characteristic, so it is wildly ramified.
- *Trivial action on $\Gamma_{\tilde K}$.* Let $s \in P_K \setminus G_{\tilde K}$ and $g \in G_{\tilde K}$. Then $sgs^{-1}g^{-1} = s\cdot(gs^{-1}g^{-1})$ lies in $P_K$, which is normal in $G_K$, and $sgs^{-1}g^{-1} = (sgs^{-1})\cdot g^{-1}$ lies in $G_{\tilde K}$, which is normal too. So $c_s(g)g^{-1} \in P_K \cap G_{\tilde K} = P_{\tilde K}$, and $c_s$ induces the identity on $\Gamma_{\tilde K}$.
- *The map to $\mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_{\tilde K})$.* Every automorphism of $G_{\tilde K}$ maps $P_{\tilde K}$ onto itself, being characteristic, and so induces an automorphism of $\Gamma_{\tilde K}$. Inner automorphisms induce inner ones, which gives the map $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\tilde K}) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Gamma_{\tilde K})$. By the previous point, $\iota_K$ maps to $1$.
- *$\Gamma_{\tilde K}$ has trivial centre.* Let $I \subseteq \Gamma_{\tilde K}$ be the image of the inertia group, and $q$ the order of the residue field of $\tilde K$. By [wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md), $I \cong \prod_{\ell \ne 2}\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$, with the conjugation action of $\Gamma_{\tilde K}$ factoring through $\Gamma_{\tilde K}/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, the Galois group of the residue field, so that Frobenius acts as $x \mapsto x^q$. Let $z$ be central, with image $b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. It acts trivially on $I$, so $q^b = 1$ in $\prod_{\ell \ne 2}\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$. Reducing modulo $q^m - 1$, where $q$ has order $m$, gives $b \in m\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ for every $m \ge 1$, so $b = 0$ and $z \in I$. Then $z$ commutes with a lift of Frobenius, which conjugates $z$ to $z^q$, so $z^{q-1} = 1$ in the torsion-free group $I$, and $z = 1$.
- *The kernel.* Let $\mathrm{Aut}^0$ be the group of automorphisms of $G_{\tilde K}$ inducing the identity on $\Gamma_{\tilde K}$. If the class of $\Phi$ lies in $\mathcal{N}_{\tilde K}$, then $\Phi$ induces conjugation by some $\bar x \in \Gamma_{\tilde K}$; for a lift $x \in G_{\tilde K}$, $c_x^{-1}\Phi \in \mathrm{Aut}^0$ has the same class. So $\mathrm{Aut}^0$ maps onto $\mathcal{N}_{\tilde K}$. An inner automorphism $c_x$ lies in $\mathrm{Aut}^0$ exactly when the image of $x$ is central in $\Gamma_{\tilde K}$, that is, when $x \in P_{\tilde K}$. $\square$

**Proof of part 6.** Since $P_{\tilde K}$ is normal in $G_K$, $T/K$ is Galois, and $\varphi$ is onto with kernel $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_2/T) = P_{\tilde K}$. Fix $n$, $s$ and $\psi$. The subgroup $\mathcal{H} = \ker\psi \cap \mathrm{Gal}(T/\tilde K)$ is open and normal in $\Delta$ and contained in $\ker\psi$, so condition 2 must hold for it. Let $F_1 \subseteq T$ be its fixed field and $G = \Delta/\mathcal{H} = \mathrm{Gal}(F_1/K)$.

- *The fields.* $\mathrm{Gal}(F_1/\tilde K)$ embeds in $\Delta/\ker\psi$, hence in $(\mathbb{Z}/2^s)^\times$, so $[F_1:\tilde K] = m$ is a power of $2$. As $F_1 \subseteq T$, the extension $F_1/\tilde K$ is tamely ramified, so its ramification index is odd and divides $m$, hence equals $1$. Let $K_1$ be the unramified extension of $K$ of degree $m$. Since $\tilde K/K$ is totally ramified, $K_1 \cap \tilde K = K$, so $\tilde K K_1$ is unramified of degree $m$ over $\tilde K$, and $F_1 = \tilde K K_1$, both being the unramified extension of $\tilde K$ of degree $m$. Then $F_1 = K_1(\sqrt{-1})$, $\sqrt{-1} \notin K_1$, and $F_1/K_1$ has ramification index $2$. Let $c$ generate $\mathrm{Gal}(F_1/K_1)$, a subgroup of order $2$ of $G$. Put $N = [K_1:\mathbb{Q}_2]$, so that $[F_1:\mathbb{Q}_2] = 2N$.
- *The module.* $X_{\mathcal{H}} = \varphi^{-1}(\mathcal{H}) = G_{F_1}$. By Kummer theory, $a \mapsto \chi_a$, with $g(\sqrt{a}) = \chi_a(g)\sqrt{a}$, is an isomorphism $F_1^\times/F_1^{\times 2} \to H^1(G_{F_1}, \mathbb{Z}/2)$ [@schneider2016, (1.2.4), p. 10]. For $x \in G_K$ one has $\chi_{x(a)}(g) = \chi_a(x^{-1}gx)$, so the action of $G$ through conjugation corresponds to its Galois action on $F_1^\times/F_1^{\times 2}$, possibly composed with $x \mapsto x^{-1}$, which does not affect freeness. By [the multiplicative group](multiplicative-group-of-a-p-adic-field.md), for a finite $E/\mathbb{Q}_2$ the units $\mathcal{O}_E^\times$ are the product of a group of odd order, the cyclic group $\mu_{2^\infty}(E) \ni -1$ and $\mathbb{Z}_2^{[E:\mathbb{Q}_2]}$. So $\mathcal{O}_E^\times/\mathcal{O}_E^{\times 2}$ has dimension $[E:\mathbb{Q}_2] + 1$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$, and $E^\times/E^{\times 2}$ has dimension $[E:\mathbb{Q}_2] + 2$.
- *$H^1(\mathcal{H})$.* The field $T$ is also the maximal tamely ramified extension of $F_1$, and a tamely ramified quadratic extension of a $2$-adic field is unramified. So $H^1(\mathcal{H}, \mathbb{Z}/2)$, inflated to $G_{F_1}$, is spanned by the class of the unramified quadratic extension of $F_1$. Write the unramified quadratic extension of $K_1$ as $K_1(\sqrt{u})$ with $u \in \mathcal{O}_{K_1}^\times$, which is possible because $\sqrt{a}$ generates a ramified extension when $a$ has odd valuation. Then $F_1(\sqrt{u})$ is unramified over $F_1$, and $\sqrt{u} \notin F_1$, since otherwise $K_1(\sqrt{u})$ and $K_1(\sqrt{-1})$ would both equal $F_1$, one unramified and one ramified over $K_1$. So $H^1(\mathcal{H})$ is spanned by $\chi_u$.
- *Its orthogonal complement.* Under Kummer theory the cup product corresponds to the Hilbert symbol $(a, b) = \chi_a(\theta_{F_1}(b))$, where $\theta_{F_1}$ is the reciprocity map [@schneider2016, p. 10 and Theorem 1.13, p. 11]. The reciprocity image of a unit acts trivially on every unramified extension, and that of a prime element acts as Frobenius [@milnecft, I, Theorem 1.1 (a), p. 20, and 1.8, p. 23]. Frobenius is nontrivial on $F_1(\sqrt{u})$, so $(u, b) = (-1)^{v(b)}$, where $v$ is the valuation of $F_1$. As the cup product on $H^1(\cdot, \mathbb{Z}/2)$ is symmetric, $H^1(\mathcal{H})^\perp$ corresponds to the classes of elements of even valuation, that is, to the image of $\mathcal{O}_{F_1}^\times$. Thus
$$Q = H^1(\mathcal{H})^\perp/H^1(\mathcal{H}) \cong \mathcal{O}_{F_1}^\times F_1^{\times 2}/\langle u\rangle F_1^{\times 2},$$
of dimension $(2N + 1) - 1 = 2N$.
- *The norm image is too small.* Writing $Q$ multiplicatively, $(1 + c)$ sends the class of $x \in \mathcal{O}_{F_1}^\times$ to the class of $x\,c(x) = N_{F_1/K_1}(x) \in \mathcal{O}_{K_1}^\times$. So $(1 + c)Q$ lies in the image $\bar J$ of $\mathcal{O}_{K_1}^\times$ in $Q$. If $a \in K_1$ is a square in $F_1$, say $a = (\alpha + \beta\sqrt{-1})^2$ with $\alpha, \beta \in K_1$, then $\alpha\beta = 0$, so $a$ or $-a$ is a square in $K_1$. Hence the kernel of $\mathcal{O}_{K_1}^\times/\mathcal{O}_{K_1}^{\times 2} \to F_1^\times/F_1^{\times 2}$ is spanned by $-1$, which is not a square in $K_1$. So the image $J$ of $\mathcal{O}_{K_1}^\times$ in $F_1^\times/F_1^{\times 2}$ has dimension $(N + 1) - 1 = N$. It contains the nonzero class of $u$, so $\dim \bar J = N - 1$.
- *Conclusion.* A free $\mathbb{F}_2[G]$-module is free over $\mathbb{F}_2[\langle c\rangle]$, and for $M \cong \mathbb{F}_2[\langle c\rangle]^r$ one has $\dim (1 + c)M = r = \frac{1}{2}\dim M$. If $Q$ were free over $\mathbb{F}_2[G]$, this would give $\dim(1 + c)Q = N$, while $\dim(1 + c)Q \le N - 1$. So $Q$ is not a free $\mathbb{F}_2[G]$-module of any rank, and condition 2 fails. $\square$

*A check of part 6.* Take $K = \mathbb{Q}_2$ and $\psi$ trivial on $\mathrm{Gal}(T/\tilde K)$. Then $F_1 = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})$, $K_1 = \mathbb{Q}_2$, $N = 1$ and $c$ is complex conjugation, and one may take $u = 5$, since $\frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$ is a root of $x^2 - x - 1$, which is irreducible modulo $2$. Write $j = \sqrt{-1}$, and $\equiv$ for equality of classes in $F_1^\times/F_1^{\times 2}$, where $-1 = j^2$ is a square.

- $c$ fixes the classes of $j$ and $5$, since $c(j) = -j \equiv j$.
- $j$ and $5$ are independent. Indeed $F_1(\sqrt{j}) = \mathbb{Q}_2(\zeta_8)$ is ramified over $F_1$, because $\zeta_8 - 1$ is a root of the Eisenstein polynomial $(x + 1)^4 + 1$, so $\mathbb{Q}_2(\zeta_8)/\mathbb{Q}_2$ is totally ramified of degree $4$. And $F_1(\sqrt{5})$ is unramified over $F_1$ and nontrivial, because $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{5})$ is unramified over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ and $F_1$ is not.
- $2 + j \not\equiv j^a5^b$ for all $a, b$: otherwise applying $c$ gives $2 - j \equiv j^a5^b$ too, and then $5 = (2 + j)(2 - j) \equiv 1$.

So the classes of $j$, $5$ and $2 + j$ form a basis of the $3$-dimensional space $\mathcal{O}_{F_1}^\times/\mathcal{O}_{F_1}^{\times 2}$, and $Q$ has basis the classes of $j$ and $2 + j$. As $c(2 + j) = 2 - j = 5(2 + j)^{-1} \equiv 5(2 + j)$, $c$ acts trivially on $Q$, so $(1 + c)Q = 0$, in line with the bound $\dim(1 + c)Q \le N - 1 = 0$.

**Remarks.**

- *What is not done here.* Neither $\iota_K$ nor $C(\iota_K)$ is computed, and no new case of the classification is decided. For the [Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md), the [dyadic lifting theorem](tame-automorphisms-lift-when-adjoining-i-is-unramified.md) shows that every automorphism of the tame quotient lifts to $G_{U_f(\sqrt{-1})}$, so by part 2 here the whole difficulty lies in commuting with $\iota_{U_f}$.
