**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite, $X = G_k$, $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(k_{tr}/k)$ the tame Galois group and $\varphi : X \to \Gamma$ the restriction. By Koch's theorem $(X, \varphi)$ is a Demuškin formation over $\Gamma$ with invariants $n = [k : \mathbb{Q}_p]$, $s$ and $\alpha$, where $p^s$ is the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $k_{tr}$ and $\alpha$ is inverse to the character by which $\Gamma$ acts on them ([uniqueness of Demuškin formations](uniqueness-of-demuskin-formations.md), part 1). Following Wingberg, for a profinite group $Z$ put $Z^0 = Z$ and let $Z^i$ be the closure of $(Z^{i-1})^{p^s}[Z^{i-1}, Z]$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, p. 100]. Let $\mathcal U$ be the set of open normal subgroups $\mathcal H \subseteq \ker\alpha$ of $\Gamma$ with $p^{2s} \mid (\Gamma : \mathcal H)$, Wingberg's standing assumption from p. 103 on [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, p. 103]. For $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ put $X_{\mathcal H} = \varphi^{-1}(\mathcal H)$ and $X^i_{\mathcal H} = (X_{\mathcal H})^i$.

For $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ and $j \ge 0$ let $A_{\mathcal H, j}$ be the set of automorphisms $g$ of the group $X/X^j_{\mathcal H}$ that commute with the projection $X/X^j_{\mathcal H} \to \Gamma/\mathcal H$. As recorded in the cited entry, each $X^j_{\mathcal H}$ is open and normal in $X$, so $A_{\mathcal H, j}$ is a finite set, and for $\mathcal H' \subseteq \mathcal H$ in $\mathcal U$ and $j' \ge j$ every $g' \in A_{\mathcal H', j'}$ induces an element of $A_{\mathcal H, j}$, by the argument of its projective-system step. Write $g \mapsto \bar g$ for this map. Every automorphism of $X$ commuting with $\varphi$ induces an element of every $A_{\mathcal H, j}$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here from Wingberg's lemmas. Let $i \ge 1$, and for each $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ let $C_{\mathcal H} \subseteq A_{\mathcal H, i}$ be a subset such that:

- (a) for $\mathcal H' \subseteq \mathcal H$ in $\mathcal U$, every element of $C_{\mathcal H'}$ induces an element of $C_{\mathcal H}$;
- (b) for every $\mathcal H$ in some subset $\mathcal U' \subseteq \mathcal U$, cofinal in the sense that every element of $\mathcal U$ contains one of $\mathcal U'$, some element of $A_{\mathcal H, i+1}$ induces an element of $C_{\mathcal H}$.

Then there is an automorphism $\theta$ of $X$ with $\varphi \circ \theta = \varphi$ that induces an element of $C_{\mathcal H}$ for every $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$.

Conversely, such a $\theta$ induces, for every $\mathcal H$, an element of $A_{\mathcal H, i+1}$ inducing an element of $C_{\mathcal H}$. So (b) for one cofinal $\mathcal U'$ is equivalent to (b) for all of $\mathcal U$.

**Two cases.**

1. *Wingberg's lifting, at every step.* Let $(\psi_{\mathcal H})_{\mathcal H \in \mathcal U}$ be a compatible family with $\psi_{\mathcal H} \in A_{\mathcal H, i+1}$, and $C_{\mathcal H} = \{\bar\psi_{\mathcal H}\} \subseteq A_{\mathcal H, i}$. Then some automorphism of $X$ over $\Gamma$ agrees with every $\psi_{\mathcal H}$ modulo $X^i_{\mathcal H}$. For $i = 1$ this is the argument Wingberg gives for his Satz 2: a given isomorphism at the level of his $V^2$ induces a compatible family at the levels $X^2_{\mathcal H}$, and Lemma 7 is then used as in the proof of Satz 3 [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, proof of Satz 2, p. 112]. Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg state the outcome for $G_k$ as agreement one step lower than the given automorphism [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.15) and the remark after it, p. 421]. For general $i$ the argument is the same, and again one step of precision is lost.
2. *A condition at one level.* Fix $\mathcal H_0 \in \mathcal U$ and a subset $S \subseteq A_{\mathcal H_0, i}$. Put $C_{\mathcal H} = \{g : \bar g \in S\}$ if $\mathcal H \subseteq \mathcal H_0$, and $C_{\mathcal H} = A_{\mathcal H, i}$ otherwise; (a) holds. So *some automorphism of $X$ over $\Gamma$ induces an element of $S$ if and only if, for cofinally many $\mathcal H \subseteq \mathcal H_0$ in $\mathcal U$, some element of $A_{\mathcal H, i+1}$ does.* No compatibility between the elements chosen for different $\mathcal H$ is needed.

Case 2 is used in [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}$ and norm-one units modulo $9$](norm-one-units-of-q9-modulo-nine-under-automorphisms-of-g-q3.md), where the condition is a congruence modulo $9$ that Wingberg's lifting, case 1 with $i = 1$, does not control.

**Wingberg's lemma.** For $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ and $i \ge 1$, let $\phi_i : X \to X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$ be a surjection with kernel $X^i_{\mathcal H}$. Consider the embedding problems of lifting $\phi_i$ along $X/X^{i+1}_{\mathcal H} \to X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$, called $(E_i, \phi_i)$, and along $X/X^{i+2}_{\mathcal H} \to X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$, called $(F_i, \phi_i)$. Wingberg's Lemma 7, stated for two Demuškin formations $X, Y$ over $\Gamma$ with the same invariants and applied here with $Y = X$, says that $(F_i, \phi_i)$ has a surjective solution if and only if $(E_i, \phi_i)$ has one [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, Lemma 7, p. 110]. Every solution of either problem is surjective: its image maps onto $X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$, and the kernel $X^i_{\mathcal H}/X^{i+2}_{\mathcal H}$ lies in the Frattini subgroup of the finite $p$-group $X_{\mathcal H}/X^{i+2}_{\mathcal H}$, hence in that of $X/X^{i+2}_{\mathcal H}$, since the Frattini subgroup of a normal subgroup lies in that of the group.

**Proof of the theorem.**

*Step 1: one level, all steps.* Let $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U'$ and let $g \in A_{\mathcal H, i+1}$ induce $\bar g \in C_{\mathcal H}$. We show that for every $j \ge i$ some $g_j \in A_{\mathcal H, j}$ induces $\bar g$ on $X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$. This is Wingberg's induction in the proof of his Satz 3, started from $\bar g$ instead of from his Lemma 4 [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, proof of Satz 3, pp. 111–112].

Let $\phi_i$ be the composite of $X \to X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$ with $\bar g$. Its kernel is $X^i_{\mathcal H}$, and $(E_i, \phi_i)$ has the solution $X \to X/X^{i+1}_{\mathcal H} \xrightarrow{g} X/X^{i+1}_{\mathcal H}$. Suppose that for some $j \ge i$ we have a surjection $\phi_j : X \to X/X^j_{\mathcal H}$ with kernel $X^j_{\mathcal H}$, lifting $\phi_i$, such that $(E_j, \phi_j)$ is solvable. By Wingberg's lemma $(F_j, \phi_j)$ has a surjective solution $\psi_j : X \to X/X^{j+2}_{\mathcal H}$. Let $\phi_{j+1}$ be $\psi_j$ followed by $X/X^{j+2}_{\mathcal H} \to X/X^{j+1}_{\mathcal H}$. It is a surjective solution of $(E_j, \phi_j)$, so it lifts $\phi_j$ and hence $\phi_i$, and $\psi_j$ solves $(E_{j+1}, \phi_{j+1})$. Its kernel is $X^{j+1}_{\mathcal H}$, as Wingberg shows [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, p. 112]: since $\phi_{j+1}$ lifts $\phi_i$, which commutes with the projections to $\Gamma/\mathcal H$, it maps $X_{\mathcal H}$ into $X_{\mathcal H}/X^{j+1}_{\mathcal H}$, so it kills $X^{j+1}_{\mathcal H}$; and a surjection between finite groups of the same order is bijective. By induction we get such $\phi_j$ for all $j \ge i$. Each induces an automorphism $g_j$ of $X/X^j_{\mathcal H}$. It commutes with the projection to $\Gamma/\mathcal H$, because $\phi_j$ lifts $\phi_i$ and $\bar g$ does, and it induces $\bar g$ on $X/X^i_{\mathcal H}$.

*Step 2: compactness.* Let $\mathcal D$ be the set of pairs $(\mathcal H, j)$ with $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ and $j \ge i$, ordered by $(\mathcal H', j') \ge (\mathcal H, j)$ if $\mathcal H' \subseteq \mathcal H$ and $j' \ge j$. It is directed, because $\mathcal U$ is closed under finite intersections. For $(\mathcal H, j) \in \mathcal D$ let
$$S_{\mathcal H, j} = \{h \in A_{\mathcal H, j} : \text{the element of } A_{\mathcal H, i} \text{ induced by } h \text{ lies in } C_{\mathcal H}\}.$$

- *Projective system.* If $(\mathcal H', j') \ge (\mathcal H, j)$ and $h' \in S_{\mathcal H', j'}$, the element of $A_{\mathcal H, i}$ induced by $h'$ is the one induced by its image in $A_{\mathcal H', i}$, which lies in $C_{\mathcal H'}$, so it lies in $C_{\mathcal H}$ by (a). Hence $h'$ induces an element of $S_{\mathcal H, j}$.
- *Nonempty.* For $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U'$ and $j \ge i$, Step 1 gives $g_j \in S_{\mathcal H, j}$. For general $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$, choose $\mathcal H' \subseteq \mathcal H$ in $\mathcal U'$ and restrict an element of $S_{\mathcal H', j}$.

The $S_{\mathcal H, j}$ form a projective system of nonempty finite sets over a directed set, so its limit is nonempty. Let $(h_{\mathcal H, j})$ be an element. The subgroups $X^j_{\mathcal H}$ for $\mathcal H \subseteq \ker\alpha$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$ in $X$ [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, proof of Satz 3, p. 111]; this remains true for $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ and $j \ge i$, since $\mathcal U$ is cofinal among the open normal subgroups of $\Gamma$ inside $\ker\alpha$ and $X^{j}_{\mathcal H} \subseteq X^{j'}_{\mathcal H}$ for $j \ge j'$. So $X = \varprojlim X/X^j_{\mathcal H}$ over $\mathcal D$, and the family defines a continuous homomorphism $\theta : X \to X$. As in the cited entry's limit step, $\theta$ is injective and surjective, hence an automorphism, and $\varphi \circ \theta$ agrees with $\varphi$ modulo every $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$, so $\varphi \circ \theta = \varphi$. By construction $\theta$ induces $h_{\mathcal H, i} \in C_{\mathcal H}$ for every $\mathcal H$.

*Converse.* If $\theta$ is as in the conclusion, then for each $\mathcal H$ the automorphism it induces on $X/X^{i+1}_{\mathcal H}$ lies in $A_{\mathcal H, i+1}$ and induces an element of $C_{\mathcal H}$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *How Wingberg's lemma is used.* In the induction every surjection $\phi_j$ to which the lemma is applied has kernel $X^j_{\mathcal H}$ and lifts one step further, which is how Wingberg applies it in his own induction [@wingberg1982eindeutigkeit, proof of Satz 3, pp. 111–112]. The only difference is the starting map: Wingberg starts from the isomorphism of his Lemma 4, built from a symplectic isometry, while Step 1 starts from an arbitrary $\bar g$ that lifts one step. The statement of Lemma 7 covers both.
- *Why precision is lost.* Hypothesis (b) asks for one more step than the conclusion controls, as in Wingberg's Satz 2. The lift built in Step 1 agrees with $g$ only modulo $X^i_{\mathcal H}$. That cannot be improved in general: for $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$ and $i = 2$, Jannsen and Wingberg's automorphism of $G/V^3$, in the notation of [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on norm-one units](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md), is induced by no automorphism of $G$ (part 3 there). What case 2 adds is that a condition at one level needs only to be met one step up, by automorphisms chosen separately at each tame level.
- *The case $p = 2$.* Wingberg assumes $p$ odd. Diekert's uniqueness theorem covers $p = 2$ when $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is unramified ([the cited entry](uniqueness-of-demuskin-formations.md), Remarks); whether his proof contains the analogue of Lemma 7 has not been checked here.
