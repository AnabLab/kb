**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the higher cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-higher-cocycle-classes-of-wild-inertia.md): its words $w$, letters, slots $T(w)$, summands $V_w$, coordinates $X_w$ through the maps $\kappa_w$, the rule (R), the classes $u(\alpha)\in H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$, the set $\mathcal S$ of its Lemma 3, the reductions (i')–(v'), and the class $E_n$. Here $d=[K:\mathbb Q_p]=4$, so $r=1$. The single surface pair is $(a,b)=(3,4)$, and the complementary indices are $1$ and $2$. A word $w$ with $T(w)\ne\emptyset$ is *pure* if its letters all lie in $\{3,4\}$ (pure in the pair) or all lie in $\{1,2\}$ (complementary). As there, $\gamma\in\Gamma$ has $\omega(\gamma)=1$ and generates a closed subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$, $\lambda_0=\gamma-1$, and $\lambda_0^*=\gamma^{-1}-1$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=4$, $e=[K(\mu_p):K]\ge2$, and $n\ge2$ with $p\nmid n$ and $e\nmid n-1$. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many outer conjugates and induce the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$.

- (a) If $e\nmid n$, then $u(\alpha)=0$. Equivalently, $c_h\circ\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$ for some $h\in P_n$.
- (b) If $e\mid n$, then $\overline{u(\alpha)}\in\mathbb F_pE_n$. Moreover, there is one $w\in(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma$ with $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}(u(\alpha'))\in\mathbb Z_pw$ for every $\alpha'$ satisfying the hypotheses.

With the higher cocycle theorem, which covers degree at least $5$, this holds in every degree at least $4$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=4$ and $e=[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$. Every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha_e$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_e$ inducing the identity on $G/P_e$ and on $P/P_{e+1}$. Two such $\alpha_e$ differ by $c_k$ with $k\in P_e$, and the classes $u(\alpha_e)$ satisfy $\Phi_{\mathcal L_e}(u(\alpha_e))\in\mathbb Z_pw$ for one $w\in(\mathcal L_e)_\Gamma$. Moreover, $c_h\circ\alpha_e$ induces the identity on $G/P_e^{p^s}P_{e+1}$ for some $h\in P_e$. So every outer class with finitely many conjugates lies in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_e^{p^s}P_{e+1})$.

For example, if $p\ge5$ and $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is unramified of degree $4$, then $e=p-1\ge4$, by the example of the higher cocycle theorem.

**Proof of the theorem.** The proof of the higher cocycle theorem uses $r\ge2$ only in its step 2, through Kondo's matrices $M_{ii'}$ and $M'_{ii'}$ for two pairs. Its step 1 needs only the unit $\gamma$ on the one pair. Its steps 3 to 7 use step 2 only through its conclusion (1), and given (1) they apply verbatim for $r=1$. In step 3 the element $Y'$ is then a sum of elements of the $V_w$ with $w$ complementary. So it suffices to prove (1) there for $r=1$:
$$X_{(a@q,b@q')}=A_{qq'},\qquad X_{(b@q,a@q')}=-A_{qq'}\qquad(q<q'),\qquad X_w=0\text{ for every other }w\text{ pure in the pair},$$
for some $A_{qq'}\in\Lambda$. Fix $v\in\mathcal S$ with image $X\in(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma$. By (i'), what follows applies also to $B^{\otimes n}_\Gamma X$, for every tame-trivial automorphism with action $B$. By step 1 there, which needs only one pair, $X_w=0$ for every word $w$ with $T(w)\ne\emptyset$ that is not pure.

*(2a) Two letters $4$.* Let $w$ be pure in the pair, with letters $4$ at a set $Q_4$ of slots, $|Q_4|\ge2$, and letters $3$ at $Q_3$. Then $X_w=0$. Let $\theta$ be the automorphism of part 2 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md) with $\lambda=\lambda_0$. It is tame-trivial, and its action $B$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_0,\bar x_2,\bar x_3$, and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda_0\bar x_3$ and $B\bar x_4=\bar x_4-\lambda_0^*\bar x_2$. So $B^{-1}$, the action of $\theta^{-1}$, maps $\mu\bar x_1\mapsto\mu\bar x_1-\mu\lambda_0\bar x_3$ and $\mu\bar x_4\mapsto\mu\bar x_4+\mu\lambda_0^*\bar x_2$, and fixes $\bar x_0,\bar x_2,\bar x_3$. In the notation of (R), its nonzero coefficients are $\beta_{ll}=1$, $\beta_{31}=-\lambda_0$ and $\beta_{24}=\lambda_0^*$.

Put $X'=(B^{-1})^{\otimes n}_\Gamma X$. Choose $q\in Q_4$, and let $w^*$ be $w$ with $w_q$ replaced by $2$. It has the complementary letter $2$ and the letter $4$ at $Q_4\setminus\{q\}\ne\emptyset$, so it is not pure, and $X'_{w^*}=0$ by step 1 applied to $X'$. A source $w'$ of $w^*$ for $B^{-1}$ has $w'_q\in\{2,4\}$, $w'_t=4$ for $t\in Q_4\setminus\{q\}$, and $w'_t\in\{3,1\}$ for $t\in Q_3$. It is pure in the pair only if $w'=w$. It is never complementary, because of the letters $4$. Every other source is not pure, so its part of $X$ vanishes by step 1. By (R), the $w^*$-part of $X'$ is therefore the image of the $w$-part of $X$ under $r_{q,\lambda_0^*}$, where $V_w$ and $V_{w^*}$ are both identified with $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^{T(w)}]]$. Choose $t_0\in Q_4\setminus\{q\}$. By Lemma 2 (b) there, $\kappa^{t_0}_w$ commutes with $r_{q,\lambda_0^*}$, so $\kappa^{t_0}_w$ of the $w$-part of $X$ is killed by right multiplication by $\gamma^{-1}-1$ in the coordinate $q$. It vanishes by Lemma 1 (a) of [the higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md), applied to $\gamma^{-1}$. So $X_w=0$.

*(2b) Two letters $3$.* By step 1 of [the degree-one Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-on-wild-inertia.md), every unipotent integral symplectic matrix of the surface lattice $\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb$ is the action on the surface classes of an automorphism $\psi$ as in Lemma 3 (iii) of the higher cocycle theorem that fixes the complementary generators. So (iii') holds for the group generated by $\bar u:\bar a\mapsto\bar a+\bar b$ and $\bar l:\bar b\mapsto\bar b+\bar a$, each fixing $A$ and the other surface generator. This group contains $\bar s=\bar u\bar l^{-1}\bar u$, with $\bar s\bar a=\bar b$, $\bar s\bar b=-\bar a$, fixing $A$. Indeed $\bar l^{-1}(\bar a+\bar b)=\bar b$, $\bar u\bar b=\bar b$, $\bar l^{-1}\bar b=\bar b-\bar a$ and $\bar u(\bar b-\bar a)=-\bar a$. By (R) with integer coefficients, the only source of a word $w$ for $\bar s$ is the word $w^\sigma$ obtained by exchanging the letters $3$ and $4$, so (iii') gives $X_w=\pm X_{w^\sigma}$. If $w$ is pure in the pair with at least two letters $3$, then $w^\sigma$ has at least two letters $4$, and $X_w=0$ by (2a).

*(2c) One letter.* The bullet *One letter* of step 2 of the higher cocycle theorem uses only $\bar u$ and $\bar l$, so $X_{(a@q)}=X_{(b@q)}=0$.

*(2d) One letter of each kind.* By (2a)–(2c), the only words pure in the pair that may have $X_w\ne0$ are $(a@q,b@q')$ and $(b@q,a@q')$, $q<q'$. As in step 2 there, the sources of $(b@q,b@q')$ for $\bar u$ are the four words with letters in $\{a,b\}$ at $q,q'$. Here $(a@q,a@q')$ and $(b@q,b@q')$ have $X_w=0$, so (iii') gives $X_{(a@q,b@q')}+X_{(b@q,a@q')}=0$. Put $A_{qq'}=X_{(a@q,b@q')}$. This is (1) for $r=1$. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** We follow the proof of the corollary of the higher cocycle theorem. For $3\le n\le e$ we show that $\alpha=c_g\circ\alpha_n$ with $\alpha_n$ inducing the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$. For $n=3$ this is the corollary of [the higher Johnson theorem in degrees three and four](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-in-degrees-three-and-four.md), as $e\ge3$. Let $3\le n\le e-1$. Then $e\nmid n-1$, $e\nmid n$ and $p\nmid n$, as $1\le n-1<n<e\le p-1$. Part (a) gives $h\in P_n$ with $c_h\alpha_n$ inducing the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$. The theorem of that entry with $n+1$ in place of $n$, which needs no condition on $e$, shows that $c_h\alpha_n$ is trivial on $P/P_{n+2}$. Take $\alpha_{n+1}=c_h\alpha_n$. Uniqueness up to $c_k$ with $k\in P_e$ follows from Lemma 5 (o) of the higher Johnson theorem, as in the higher cocycle theorem. As $e\mid p-1$, $p\nmid e$, and $e\ge2$, $e\mid e$, $e\nmid e-1$, so part (b) applies to every $\alpha_e$ with $n=e$. Finally, part (b) of the theorem of [the Jannsen–Wingberg relator entry](the-jannsen-wingberg-relator-constrains-wild-cocycle-classes-and-johnson-maps.md), with $n=e$, gives $h\in P_e$ with $c_h\alpha_e$ inducing the identity on $G/P_e^{p^s}P_{e+1}$. This subgroup is characteristic, so the map on outer automorphism groups is defined, and it sends the class of $\alpha$ to the trivial class. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* In the higher cocycle theorem a word with two letters from one pair is killed by moving one of them into a second pair with $M_{ii'}$. With one pair, part 2 of the mixing theorem moves a letter $4$ into the complementary letter $2$, with coefficient $\lambda_0^*=\gamma^{-1}-1$. Any second letter $4$ keeps the new word away from the complementary words, so it has only one pure source. Exchanging the letters by $\bar s$ handles two letters $3$. Then everything proceeds as with several pairs.
- *Degree three.* For odd $d$, part 3 of the mixing theorem moves the letter $3$ into the complementary letter $1$ only with coefficient $\nu^*\delta_0$, with $\delta_0$ from part 1 there. It moves the complementary letter into the pair with coefficient $\nu$. A cocycle class is a single element of $(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma$ and has no input slot, so only the first coefficient enters (2a). It is not known whether right multiplication by $\delta_0$ is injective on $\Lambda$. So the argument above does not reach $d=3$, and the higher cocycle classes in degree three remain open. The Johnson maps avoid this, as [the companion entry](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-in-degrees-three-and-four.md) uses the input $\bar x_1$, moved by $\lambda_0$ alone.
- *What remains.* (1) Degree three. (2) Whether $u(\alpha_e)\in p^sH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_e)$ vanishes, as for degree at least five. (3) The levels with $e\mid n-1$, beyond their reductions. (4) The case $\mu_p\subseteq K$. This continues item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535).
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, every $s$, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]=4$, $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge2$, and $n\ge2$ with $p\nmid n$ and $[K(\mu_p):K]\nmid n-1$ for the theorem; $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$ for the corollary. Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
