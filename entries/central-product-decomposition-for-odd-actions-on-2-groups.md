**Statement (proved here).** Let an odd-order finite group $T$ act on a finite $2$-group $P$. Suppose $Z=\Phi(P)=P^2[P,P]$ is central and elementary abelian, and $T$ fixes $Z$ pointwise. Write
$$
V=P/Z,\qquad e=\sum_{t\in T}t\in\mathbb F_2[T],\qquad
V_0=eV=V^T,\qquad V_1=(1-e)V.
$$
There are $T$-stable subgroups $C,R\subseteq P$ with
$$
C=P^T,\qquad P=CR,\qquad [C,R]=1,\qquad
A=C\cap R=\Phi(R)\subseteq Z,\qquad R/A\cong V_1.
\tag{1}
$$
Thus $P$ is the central product of $C$ and $R$ over $A$: multiplication induces an isomorphism
$$
(C\times R)/\{(a,a^{-1}):a\in A\}\ \cong P.
$$
The action on $C$ is trivial, $A$ is central elementary abelian, and $(R/\Phi(R))^T=0$.

Moreover, for every homomorphism
$$
r:P\rtimes T\longrightarrow D
\tag{2}
$$
to a finite quotient $D$ of a dyadic tame Galois group, one has $r(R)=1$. Consequently (2) factors through
$$
(P\rtimes T)/R\cong (C/A)\times T.
\tag{3}
$$

**Proof.** *The fixed subgroup.* If $xZ\in V_0$, write $t(x)=xz_t$ with $z_t\in Z$. Because $T$ fixes $Z$, the equation for $st(x)$ gives $z_{st}=z_sz_t$. Thus $t\mapsto z_t$ is a homomorphism from an odd-order group to an elementary-abelian $2$-group, and is trivial. Every lift of $V_0$ is therefore fixed. Conversely a fixed element has fixed image in $V$. Hence $C=P^T$ is exactly the inverse image of $V_0$, and contains $Z$.

Let $E$ be the inverse image of $V_1$. The commutator gives a $T$-invariant bilinear map $B:V\times V\to Z$. For $v_0\in V_0$ and $v_1\in V_1$, invariance and oddness give
$$
B(v_0,v_1)=\sum_{t\in T}B(v_0,tv_1)=B(v_0,ev_1)=0.
$$
It follows that $[C,E]=1$, while $P=CE$ and $C\cap E=Z$.

*The moving subgroup.* Put $A=\Phi(E)$. Squares and commutators of $E$ lie in $Z$, so $A\subseteq Z$ is central elementary abelian and fixed by $T$. There is an exact sequence of elementary-abelian $T$-modules
$$
0\longrightarrow Z/A\longrightarrow E/A\longrightarrow V_1\longrightarrow0.
$$
The averaging idempotent splits this sequence: its kernel on $E/A$ maps isomorphically to $V_1$, because the kernel $Z/A$ is fixed and $V_1$ has no fixed vectors. Define $R$ as the inverse image of $(1-e)(E/A)$ in $E$.

Then $E=RZ$ and $R\cap Z=A$. Since $Z$ is central of exponent two, squares and commutators in $RZ$ are precisely those generated by squares and commutators in $R$. Hence
$$
\Phi(R)=\Phi(RZ)=\Phi(E)=A.
$$
Combining this with $P=CE$, $Z\subseteq C$ and $[C,E]=1$ proves (1) and the central-product assertion.

*Tame images.* A finite dyadic tame group has a normal cyclic odd-order inertia image and a cyclic quotient by it. Each of its $2$-subgroups injects into that cyclic quotient, and is cyclic. In particular $r(R)$ is cyclic. Conjugation by $T$ on it is trivial, since the automorphism group of a cyclic $2$-group is a $2$-group.

If $r(R)$ were nontrivial, reduction modulo its squares would give a nonzero $T$-invariant character $R/\Phi(R)\to\mathbb F_2$. Such a character vanishes: for every $v\in V_1$, its value is also its value on $ev=0$. Thus $r(R)=1$. The subgroup $R$ is normal in $P\rtimes T$, because $P=CR$, $[C,R]=1$, and $R$ is $T$-stable. Quotienting the central product by $R$ now gives (3). $\square$

**Checks and scope.** If $V_0=0$, this construction gives $C=A=Z$ and $R=P$. If $V_1=0$, it gives $C=P$ and $R=A=1$. The shared subgroup $A$ need not be trivial, so this is generally a central product rather than a direct product. Centrality and exponent two of $Z$ are used in constructing $R$; no statement about arbitrary Frattini subgroups follows.
