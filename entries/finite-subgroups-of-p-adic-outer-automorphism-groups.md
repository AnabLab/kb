**Setting.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, let $d_k = [k:\mathbb{Q}_p]$, and let $G_k = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar k/k)$. A profinite group is *slim* if every open subgroup has trivial centralizer [@mochizuki2012, §0, p. 8], and $G_k$ is slim [@mochizuki2012, Theorem 1.7 (ii), p. 14]. In particular $G_k$ is center-free, and it is topologically finitely generated (see [generators and relations](local-galois-group-presentations.md)). So $1 \to G_k \to \mathrm{Aut}(G_k) \to \mathrm{Out}(G_k) \to 1$ is an exact sequence of profinite groups, with $G_k$ mapped onto the inner automorphisms $\mathrm{Inn}(G_k)$ [@mochizuki2012, §0, p. 9].

A profinite group is *of MLF-type* if it is isomorphic to the absolute Galois group of a finite extension of some $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ [@nishio2026finite, §0, p. 4]. A finite subgroup $Q \subseteq \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ is *quasi-geometric* if its inverse image $\widetilde Q \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ is of MLF-type [@nishio2026finite, Definition 3.1, p. 14]. Nishio takes the notion from Hoshi, and recalls the following [@nishio2026finite, Introduction, p. 2] [@nishio2026finite, proof of Corollary 3.6, p. 15].

- Extending field automorphisms of $k$ to $\bar k$ gives an injection $\mathrm{Aut}(k) \to \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$, and its image is quasi-geometric. A proof is given below.
- Every subgroup of a quasi-geometric subgroup is quasi-geometric, and so is every $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$-conjugate of one.

**Nishio's problems.** Nishio asks whether every finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ is quasi-geometric (his Problem 1). He also asks the global analogue, whether the inverse image in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ of a finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$, for a number field $K$, is the absolute Galois group of a number field (his Problem 2) [@nishio2026finite, Introduction, p. 2]. He answers Problem 2 yes by the Neukirch–Uchida theorem: the inverse image of $\Delta \subseteq \mathrm{Out}(G_K) \cong \mathrm{Aut}(K)$ is $G_L$ with $L = K^\Delta$ [@nishio2026finite, Introduction, pp. 2–3]. Compare [automorphism towers of number-field Galois groups](automorphism-towers-of-number-field-galois-groups.md).

**Theorem (Nishio, preprint).**

1. *Pro-$p$ kernels.* Let $J$ be a topologically finitely generated profinite group, $P \subseteq J$ a closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup and $s \ge 1$. Put $P^{p,1,s} = P$, and let $P^{p,i+1,s}$ be the closure of $(P^{p,i,s})^{p^s}[P, P^{p,i,s}]$. Then for every $i \ge 2$, the automorphisms of $J$ that preserve $P^{p,i,s}$ and induce the identity on $J/P^{p,i,s}$ form a pro-$p$ group [@nishio2026finite, §0, p. 4; Theorem 1.2, p. 6]. The case $J = P$, $i = 2$, $s = 1$ is the classical fact that the automorphisms of a finitely generated pro-$p$ group acting trivially on its Frattini quotient form a pro-$p$ group [@nishio2026finite, Remark 1.3, p. 7]. Nishio notes that Shun Ishii obtained a similar result independently [@nishio2026finite, Introduction, p. 3].
2. *Finite groups of automorphisms.* Let $p$ be odd, $d_k \ge 3$ and $m = \lfloor (d_k - 1)/2 \rfloor$. Every subgroup of the hyperoctahedral group $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^m \rtimes \mathfrak{S}_m$ whose order is prime to $p$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ [@nishio2026finite, Theorem B, p. 3; Theorem 2.8, p. 13]. The subgroups constructed act trivially on the quotient of $G_k$ by its wild inertia subgroup [@nishio2026finite, proof of Theorem 2.8, p. 14].
3. *Non-quasi-geometric subgroups.* Let $G$ be of MLF-type, $\Gamma \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ a nontrivial finite subgroup and $H \subseteq G$ an open subgroup preserved by $\Gamma$. Then the image in $\mathrm{Out}(H)$ of $\Gamma$, acting on $H$, is a finite subgroup that is not quasi-geometric [@nishio2026finite, Theorem 3.3, p. 14]. Consequently, if $p$ is odd and $d_k \ge 3$, then $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ has a finite subgroup that is not quasi-geometric, and hence one contained in no $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$-conjugate of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$ [@nishio2026finite, Theorem C, p. 3; Corollaries 3.4 and 3.6, p. 15]. So Problem 1 has a negative answer in these cases.

**Method.**

- *Torsion.* A group of MLF-type is torsion-free, because its wild inertia subgroup is free pro-$p$, its tame inertia quotient is $\prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and the quotient by inertia is $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ [@nishio2026finite, Lemma 2.1, p. 8]. So the inverse image of the image of a nontrivial finite $\Gamma \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G)$, which contains $\Gamma$, is not of MLF-type [@nishio2026finite, proof of Theorem 3.3, p. 14].
- *The pairs.* For odd $p$ and $d_k \ge 3$, write the [Jannsen–Wingberg presentation](local-galois-group-presentations.md) with generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \ldots, x_{d_k}$, as in [@nishio2026finite, Proposition 2.4, pp. 9–10]. Its wild relation ends with the product $[x_i, x_{i+1}][x_{i+2}, x_{i+3}] \cdots [x_{d_k-1}, x_{d_k}]$, where $i = 2$ for odd $d_k$ and $i = 3$ for even $d_k$. So it ends with $m$ commutators of pairs of generators.
- *The hyperoctahedral group.* Let $P$ be the wild inertia subgroup and $p^s$ the order of the group of $p$-power roots of unity in the maximal tamely ramified extension of $k$ [@nishio2026finite, Lemma 2.3 (ii), p. 9]. Swapping two of these pairs, or inverting both members of one pair, fixes $\sigma$ and $\tau$ and preserves the relation modulo $P^{p,3,s}$. These substitutions generate a copy of $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^m \rtimes \mathfrak{S}_m$ among the automorphisms of $G_k/P^{p,3,s}$ inducing the identity on $G_k/P$. The copy acts faithfully on $G_k/P^{p,2,s}$, because the images of $x_2, \ldots, x_{d_k}$ in $P/P^{p,2,1}$ are linearly independent [@nishio2026finite, Lemma 2.5 and Theorem 2.7, pp. 10–13].
- *Lifting.* By a lifting theorem for Demuškin formations, from Neukirch–Schmidt–Wingberg and Wingberg, every automorphism of $G_k/P^{p,2,s}$ inducing the identity on $G_k/P$ that comes from one of $G_k/P^{p,3,s}$ lifts to an automorphism of $G_k$ [@nishio2026finite, Proposition 2.6, p. 11]. By part 1 of the theorem, the kernel of the lifting is pro-$p$. So a profinite Schur–Zassenhaus theorem splits off a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ isomorphic to any given subgroup of order prime to $p$ [@nishio2026finite, proof of Theorem 2.8, pp. 13–14].

**Consequences.** These are proved here. Hoshi's 2019 paper, where quasi-geometric subgroups were introduced, was not opened, so some of them may already be there. Consequences 1, 2 and 4 hold for every $p$ and every $k$.

1. *Finite automorphism groups avoid every quasi-geometric subgroup.* Let $\Gamma \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ be finite, with image $\bar\Gamma \subseteq \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$. Then $\Gamma \to \bar\Gamma$ is an isomorphism, and no element of $\bar\Gamma$ other than $1$ lies in a quasi-geometric subgroup. In particular $\bar\Gamma$ meets every $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$-conjugate of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$ only in $1$. This strengthens Nishio's Corollary 3.6.
2. *Order bound.* Let $Q \subseteq \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ be a quasi-geometric finite subgroup. Then $Q \cong \mathrm{Gal}(k''/k')$ for a finite Galois extension $k''/k'$ of finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, with $[k':\mathbb{Q}_p] = d_k/|Q|$. In particular $|Q|$ divides $d_k$, $Q$ is solvable, and if $|Q| = d_k$ then $\widetilde Q \cong G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$.
   - For $k$ Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$ is therefore a quasi-geometric subgroup of the largest possible order $d_k$.
   - For $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$, the order of a quasi-geometric finite subgroup divides $1$. So the only one is $1$, whose inverse image $\mathrm{Inn}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}) \cong G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ is of MLF-type. So Problem 1 for $\mathbb{Q}_p$ asks exactly whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ has no nontrivial element of finite order.
3. *Every prime-to-$p$ group occurs.* Let $p$ be odd, and let $F$ be a nontrivial finite group of order prime to $p$ that acts faithfully on a set of $n$ elements, for instance with $n = |F|$. If $d_k \ge 2n + 1$, then $F$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$, and to a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ that is not quasi-geometric.
4. *The ramification filtration.* No automorphism of $G_k$ of finite order other than the identity preserves the filtration of $G_k$ by its higher ramification groups in upper numbering of index $> 0$. So if $p$ is odd and $d_k \ge 3$, a finite subgroup $\Gamma \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ as in part 2 of the theorem moves this filtration to $|\Gamma|$ distinct filtrations.
5. *$\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ is not an absolute Galois group.* If $p$ is odd and $d_k \ge 5$, then $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ and $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ contain subgroups isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$. So neither is isomorphic, even as an abstract group, to the absolute Galois group of a field. For a number field $K$, by contrast, $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ is the absolute Galois group of the fixed field of $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$ (see [automorphism towers](automorphism-towers-of-number-field-galois-groups.md)).

**Proofs.**

*The image of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$.* Every field automorphism of $k$ is $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linear (see [the field-theoretic subgroup](field-theoretic-subgroup-of-outer-automorphisms.md)). Let $k_0 = k^{\mathrm{Aut}(k)}$, so that $k/k_0$ is Galois with group $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$, and let $c : G_{k_0} \to \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ be conjugation, $c(\psi)(g) = \psi g \psi^{-1}$, which makes sense because $G_k$ is normal in $G_{k_0}$. Then:

- *$c$ is injective,* because its kernel is the centralizer of the open subgroup $G_k$ in $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, which is slim [@mochizuki2012, Theorem 1.7 (ii), p. 14].
- *$c$ is continuous.* For a characteristic open subgroup $V$ of $G_k$, which is normal in $G_{k_0}$, every $\psi \in V$ satisfies $\psi g \psi^{-1} g^{-1} \in V$ for all $g \in G_k$. So $c$ maps $V$ into the kernel of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k) \to \mathrm{Aut}(G_k/V)$, and these kernels form a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$. Being a continuous injection of profinite groups, $c$ is an isomorphism onto its image.
- *The image of $c$ is the inverse image of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$.* Each $\psi \in G_{k_0}$ maps $k$ onto itself, since $k/k_0$ is normal. Conversely, every extension to $\bar k$ of an automorphism of $k$ fixes $\mathbb{Q}_p$, hence lies in $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, and maps $k$ onto $k$, hence fixes $k_0$. So the elements of $G_{k_0}$ are exactly the extensions of automorphisms of $k$. By the definition of $\mathrm{Aut}(k) \to \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$, the image of $c$ therefore maps onto the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$. It contains $c(G_k) = \mathrm{Inn}(G_k)$, the kernel of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k) \to \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$, so it is the whole inverse image.

So the inverse image of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$ is isomorphic to $G_{k_0}$, which is of MLF-type, and the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$ is quasi-geometric. The map $\mathrm{Aut}(k) \to \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ is injective, because $c$ is injective and $G_{k_0}/G_k \cong \mathrm{Aut}(k)$. If $Q$ is quasi-geometric and $\beta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ has class $\bar\beta$, then the inverse image of $\bar\beta Q \bar\beta^{-1}$ is $\beta \widetilde Q \beta^{-1} \cong \widetilde Q$. So conjugates of quasi-geometric subgroups are quasi-geometric.

*Consequence 1.* Since $G_k$ is center-free, $\mathrm{Inn}(G_k) \cong G_k$, which is torsion-free [@nishio2026finite, Lemma 2.1 (iv), p. 8]. So the finite group $\Gamma$ meets $\mathrm{Inn}(G_k)$ only in $1$, and $\Gamma \to \bar\Gamma$ is injective. Let $\gamma \in \Gamma$ with $\gamma \ne 1$, and suppose that its class $\bar\gamma$ lies in a quasi-geometric subgroup $Q$. Then $\gamma \in \widetilde Q$, and $\gamma$ has finite order greater than $1$. But $\widetilde Q$ is of MLF-type, hence torsion-free [@nishio2026finite, Lemma 2.1 (iv), p. 8]. This is a contradiction. The last assertion follows, because the conjugates of the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$ are quasi-geometric, as shown above.

*Consequence 2.* Let $\psi : \widetilde Q \to G_{k'}$ be an isomorphism of profinite groups, with $k'$ finite over some $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. The subgroup $N = \mathrm{Inn}(G_k)$ is closed, being the image of a compact group, and normal in $\widetilde Q$ with $\widetilde Q/N \cong Q$. As a closed subgroup of finite index, it is open. So $\psi(N)$ is an open normal subgroup of $G_{k'}$ of index $|Q|$, that is, $\psi(N) = G_{k''}$ for a Galois extension $k''/k'$ of degree $|Q|$, with
$$\mathrm{Gal}(k''/k') \cong G_{k'}/G_{k''} \cong \widetilde Q/N \cong Q.$$
Now $G_{k''} \cong N \cong G_k$. The residue characteristic and the degree over the prime field are determined by the absolute Galois group, by functorial group-theoretic reconstructions [@nishio2026finite, §2, p. 8]. So $\ell = p$ and $[k'':\mathbb{Q}_p] = d_k$. Hence $d_k = [k'':k'] \cdot [k':\mathbb{Q}_p] = |Q| \cdot [k':\mathbb{Q}_p]$. The absolute Galois group of a field of this type is prosolvable [@mochizuki2012, Remark 1.4.1, p. 12], so its finite quotient $Q$ is solvable. If $|Q| = d_k$, then $k' = \mathbb{Q}_p$.

For $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$, this shows that the only quasi-geometric finite subgroup is $1$. If every finite subgroup is quasi-geometric, then every element of finite order generates the trivial group. Conversely, if there is no nontrivial element of finite order, the only finite subgroup is $1$, which is quasi-geometric.

*Consequence 3.* Since $n \le \lfloor (d_k-1)/2 \rfloor = m$, the action embeds $F$ in $\mathfrak{S}_n \subseteq \mathfrak{S}_m \subseteq (\mathbb{Z}/2)^m \rtimes \mathfrak{S}_m$, with image of order $|F|$, prime to $p$. Part 2 of the theorem gives a subgroup $\Gamma \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ isomorphic to $F$, and consequence 1 shows that $\bar\Gamma \cong F$. Its inverse image contains the nontrivial finite group $\Gamma$, so it is not of MLF-type.

*Consequence 4.* Let $\alpha$ be such an automorphism. Inner automorphisms preserve every filtration by closed normal subgroups, so the class of $\alpha$ is an outer automorphism of the filtered group $G_k$. By [Mochizuki's theorem](mochizuki-local-fields-theorem.md) with $K = K' = k$, it is the class of a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linear automorphism of $k$. So $\alpha$ lies in the inverse image of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$, which is isomorphic to $G_{k_0}$ and hence torsion-free. Since $\alpha$ has finite order, $\alpha = 1$. For the last assertion, the stabilizer of the filtration in $\Gamma$ is therefore trivial, so its orbit under $\Gamma$ has $|\Gamma|$ elements.

*Consequence 5.* As $d_k \ge 5$, we have $m \ge 2$, and the sign changes of the first two coordinates form a subgroup of $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^m \rtimes \mathfrak{S}_m$ isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$, of order $4$, prime to $p$. Part 2 of the theorem and consequence 1 give copies in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ and $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$. By the [Artin–Schreier theorem](artin-schreier-theorem.md), a nontrivial finite subgroup of the absolute Galois group of a field has order $2$. $\square$

**Scope.**

- For $d_k \in \{3, 4\}$ the hyperoctahedral group has order $2$, so Nishio's theorem gives only involutions in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$. Absolute Galois groups can contain involutions, so consequence 5 does not reach these degrees.
- Nishio's construction gives nothing when $p$ is odd and $d_k \le 2$, since then $m = 0$, and it assumes that $p$ is odd [@nishio2026finite, Propositions 2.4 and 2.6, pp. 9–11].
- Problem 1 therefore remains open for odd $p$ with $d_k \le 2$, and for $p = 2$, as far as the sources opened here show. By consequence 2, for $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$ it is the question whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ is torsion-free.
- For outer automorphisms of $G_k$ of infinite order, and the non-normality of $\mathrm{Aut}(k)$, see [the field-theoretic subgroup](field-theoretic-subgroup-of-outer-automorphisms.md).
