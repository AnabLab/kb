**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb Q_2$ be finite of degree $d$, and let $\pi$ be a uniformizer. Put $\pi_0=\pi$, choose $\pi_{s+1}$ with $\pi_{s+1}^2=\pi_s$, and set
$$K_s=K(\pi_s),\qquad K_\infty=\bigcup_sK_s,\qquad K_{2^\infty}=\bigcup_nK(\zeta_{2^n}),\qquad \hat K=K_\infty K_{2^\infty}.$$
As $X^{2^s}-\pi$ is Eisenstein, $K_s/K$ is totally ramified of degree $2^s$ and $\pi_s$ is a uniformizer of $K_s$. The field $\hat K$ is the union of the splitting fields of the polynomials $X^{2^s}-\pi$, so it is Galois over $K$; put $\hat G=\operatorname{Gal}(\hat K/K)$. Recall that for $a,b\in K^\times$ an element $\sqrt b$ lies in $K(\sqrt a)$ only if $b\in K^{\times2}\cup aK^{\times2}$: square $\sqrt b=x+y\sqrt a$ and compare coefficients. In particular $K(\sqrt a)=K(\sqrt b)$ if and only if $ab\in K^{\times2}$.

**Theorem (proved here).**
1. For every $s\ge1$, the only subfield of $K_s$ of degree $2$ over $K$ is $K_1=K(\sqrt\pi)$.
2. The following are equivalent:
   - (a) $K_\infty\cap K_{2^\infty}=K$;
   - (b) $\sqrt\pi\notin K_{2^\infty}$;
   - (c) $\hat G$ is generated by its subgroups $\operatorname{Gal}(\hat K/K_{2^\infty})$ and $\operatorname{Gal}(\hat K/K_\infty)$.
3. Condition (b) depends only on the class of $\pi$ in $K^\times/K^{\times2}$. The uniformizers of $K$ fall into $2^{d+1}$ such classes, and (b) fails for at most three of them. In particular some uniformizer satisfies (a)–(c).

The existence statement in part 3 is [@wang2022weight, Lemma 2.1, p. 3]. Wang reduces it to (b) by quoting a proposition of Liu, which says that $K_\infty\cap K_{2^\infty}$ is $K$ or $K_1$ for every uniformizer. That proposition was not opened here, and parts 1 and 2 replace it. Čoupek quotes Wang's lemma as [@coupek2026crystalline, Lemma 3.1, p. 161], and uses it in the form (c) [@coupek2026crystalline, §3.1, p. 161] to extend his [ramification bounds for mod-$p$ cohomology](prismatic-ramification-bound-for-mod-p-cohomology.md) to $p=2$. For odd $p$, the source quotes Liu for (c) with every uniformizer.

**Example.** Over $K=\mathbb Q_2$, the uniformizers $2$ and $-2$ fail (b), since $\sqrt2=\zeta_8+\zeta_8^{-1}$ and $\sqrt{-2}=\zeta_8+\zeta_8^3$. The uniformizers $\pm6$ and $\pm10$ satisfy (a)–(c). Indeed, odd squares are $1$ modulo $8$, so none of $-1$, $\pm3$, $\pm5$ is a square in $\mathbb Q_2$, and elements of odd valuation are not squares. So $\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt{-1})$, $\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt2)$ and $\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt{-2})$ are distinct, as $-2$, $2$ and $-4$ are not squares. They lie in $\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_8)$, so by the proof of part 3 they are all the quadratic subfields of $K_{2^\infty}$. For $c\in\{\pm6,\pm10\}$ none of $-c$, $2c$, $-2c$ is a square, so $\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt c)$ is none of the three fields. So the choice of uniformizer matters: for $\pi=2$, (c) fails.

**Proof of 1.** Induct on $s$, for all $K$ and $\pi$ at once. For $s=1$, $[K_1:K]=2$. Let $s\ge2$, put $K'=K_1$, and suppose that $E\subseteq K_s$ is quadratic over $K$ with $E\ne K'$. Then $EK'$ is a compositum of two distinct quadratic extensions, so it is Galois over $K$ with group $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$, and $EK'\subseteq K_s$ has degree $2$ over $K'$. Now $K'/\mathbb Q_2$ is finite, $\pi_1$ is a uniformizer of $K'$, and $K_s=K'(\pi_s)$ with $\pi_s^{2^{s-1}}=\pi_1$. By the induction hypothesis for $(K',\pi_1,s-1)$, $EK'=K'(\pi_2)=K_2$. So $K_2/K$ is Galois with group $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$. We show that this is impossible.

The polynomial $X^4-\pi$ is irreducible, being Eisenstein, so the normal extension $K_2$ contains all its roots, and their ratios. Let $\iota$ be a primitive fourth root of unity; then $\iota\in K_2$.
- If $\iota\in K$, every $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K_2/K)$ has $\sigma\pi_2=\iota^{k(\sigma)}\pi_2$, and since $\sigma$ fixes $\iota$, the map $\sigma\mapsto\iota^{k(\sigma)}$ is an injective homomorphism to $\mu_4$. A group of order $4$ embedding in $\mu_4$ is cyclic, not $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$.
- If $\iota\notin K$, then $K(\iota)$ is a quadratic subfield of $K_2$, and $K(\iota)\ne K_1$ because $-\pi$ has odd valuation and so is not a square. So $\iota\notin K_1$, and $K_2=K_1(\iota)$ by degrees. Then $\pi_1=\pi_2^2$ is a square in $K_1(\sqrt{-1})$, so by the recalled fact over $K_1$, $\pi_1$ or $-\pi_1$ is a square in $K_1$. Both have valuation $1$ in $K_1$, which is impossible.

So $E=K'$. $\square$

**Proof of 2.** (a) implies (b) because $K_1\subseteq K_\infty$ and $K_1\ne K$.

(b) implies (a). Let $x\in K_\infty\cap K_{2^\infty}$ and $M=K(x)$. As $K_{2^\infty}/K$ is abelian, $M/K$ is Galois, and $M\subseteq K_s$ for some $s$, so $[M:K]$ divides $2^s$. If $M\ne K$, the $2$-group $\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)$ has a subgroup of index $2$. Its fixed field is a quadratic subfield of $K_s$, so it is $K_1$ by part 1, and $\sqrt\pi\in M\subseteq K_{2^\infty}$. So under (b), $x\in K$.

(a) is equivalent to (c). Put $H=\operatorname{Gal}(\hat K/K_{2^\infty})$ and $J=\operatorname{Gal}(\hat K/K_\infty)$. As $K_{2^\infty}/K$ is Galois, $H$ is normal, so the subgroup generated by $H$ and $J$ is $HJ$, which is closed as the image of the compact group $H\times J$. Restriction maps $\hat G$ onto $\operatorname{Gal}(K_{2^\infty}/K)$ with kernel $H$, so $HJ=\hat G$ exactly when $J$ restricts onto $\operatorname{Gal}(K_{2^\infty}/K)$. The restriction of $J$ is a closed subgroup, and its fixed field is $K_{2^\infty}\cap K_\infty$, because the fixed field of $J$ in $\hat K$ is $K_\infty$. By infinite Galois theory the restriction of $J$ is therefore $\operatorname{Gal}(K_{2^\infty}/K_{2^\infty}\cap K_\infty)$, and it is everything exactly when (a) holds. $\square$

**Proof of 3.** By the recalled fact, $K(\sqrt\pi)$, and hence (b), depends only on the class of $\pi$ modulo $K^{\times2}$. The uniformizers are the $\pi u$ with $u\in\mathcal O_K^\times$, and $\pi u$, $\pi u'$ lie in the same class exactly when $u/u'\in K^{\times2}$, that is, when $u/u'\in\mathcal O_K^{\times2}$, since a square root of a unit is a unit. By the [structure of the multiplicative group](multiplicative-group-of-a-p-adic-field.md), $\mathcal O_K^\times\cong\mu_{q-1}(K)\times\mu_{2^\infty}(K)\times\mathbb Z_2^d$, where $q$ is the order of the residue field. The first factor has odd order, so it consists of squares, and $\mu_{2^\infty}(K)$ is cyclic of even order, as it contains $-1$. So $\mathcal O_K^\times/\mathcal O_K^{\times2}$ has order $2\cdot2^d$, and there are $2^{d+1}$ classes of uniformizers.

The action on $2$-power roots of unity embeds $\operatorname{Gal}(K_{2^\infty}/K)$ as a closed subgroup $\Gamma$ of $\mathbb Z_2^\times$. The same structure theorem for $\mathbb Q_2$ gives $\mathbb Z_2^\times\cong\{\pm1\}\times\mathbb Z_2$. A closed subgroup of this abelian pro-$2$ group is a $\mathbb Z_2$-submodule of a module generated by two elements, so, as $\mathbb Z_2$ is a principal ideal domain, $\Gamma$ is generated by at most two elements. So there are at most four continuous homomorphisms $\Gamma\to\mathbb Z/2$, and at most three open subgroups of index $2$. These correspond to the quadratic subfields of $K_{2^\infty}$, and each class of uniformizers failing (b) gives a different one. As $2^{d+1}\ge4>3$, some class satisfies (b). $\square$
