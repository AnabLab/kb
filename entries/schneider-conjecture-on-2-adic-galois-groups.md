**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ be finite of even degree $n$, with residue field of order $2^s$, such that the maximal tamely ramified extension $T$ of $K$ does not contain a primitive $4$-th root of unity, that is, such that $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified. Let $K' = K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$, and identify $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})/\mathbb{Q}_2)$ with $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$. Schneider puts $K$ in the class $\mathfrak{N}_\alpha$, for an integer $\alpha \ge 2$, if $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})/K')$ is the closed subgroup generated by $-1 + 2^\alpha$. Among the fields of even degree with $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ ramified, those in the union $\mathfrak{N}$ of these classes are exactly those for which $K'$ is not contained in the real subfield of $\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$ [@schneider2016, p. 15 and Remark 2.2]. For $K \in \mathfrak{N}_\alpha$, the largest power $q$ of $2$ such that the $q$-th roots of unity lie in $K(\sqrt{-1})$ is $q = 2^{\alpha + 1}$ [@schneider2016, Remark 2.2 and p. 43].

The group $\mathcal{G} = \mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$ has generators $\sigma, \tau$ with the defining relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{2^s}$, and the $2$-power roots of unity in $T$ are just $\pm 1$ [@schneider2016, p. 43].

**Conjecture** [@schneider2016, Conjecture 3.1]. If $K \in \mathfrak{N}_\alpha$, then $G_K$ is isomorphic to the profinite group $X$ with generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_n$ and the following defining conditions:

1. The closed normal subgroup generated by $x_0, \dots, x_n$ is a pro-$2$ group.
2. $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{2^s}$.
3. $x_0^{2 + q/2}[x_0, x_1]\, x_2^{-\sigma} (x_2, \tau)\, [x_3, x_4] \cdots [x_{n-1}, x_n] = 1$, where $(x_2, \tau) = (x_2\tau)^\pi$ and $\pi \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is the element with $\pi\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{Z}_2$.

Here $[a, b] = a^{-1}b^{-1}ab = a^{-1}a^b$ [@schneider2016, p. 14], so $a^b = b^{-1}ab$. The group $X$ depends only on $n$, $s$ and $q$ [@schneider2016, §1.3 and p. 44]. It has the same kind of generators and the same tame relation as the presentations for odd $p$, and for $p = 2$ when adjoining $\sqrt{-1}$ is unramified, which are described in [generators and relations for the absolute Galois group of a $p$-adic field](local-galois-group-presentations.md).

**Evidence.** All of it is in Schneider's doctoral thesis, which relies on lemmas of Zel'venskii that it cites but does not reprove.

- *The approach.* The separable closure of $K$ is the union of the fields $L(2)$, where $L/K$ runs over finite tamely ramified Galois extensions and $L(2)$ is the maximal $2$-extension of $L$, so it is enough to describe the groups $\mathrm{Gal}(L(2)/K)$. Writing $l$ for the totally ramified part of $L/K$, of degree $e$, Schneider defines subquotients $X_{e,f}$ of $X$ that should be isomorphic to $\mathrm{Gal}(L(2)/l)$. She plans to prove this with a lemma of Zel'venskii: two profinite groups satisfying three stated conditions are isomorphic [@schneider2016, pp. 44–45 and Lemma 3.2].
- *One of the three conditions.* She proves the second condition: the $2$-Sylow subgroup of $X_{e,f}$ is isomorphic to that of $\mathrm{Gal}(L(2)/l)$. Both are [Demushkin groups](demushkin-group.md) with the same rank and the same image of the canonical character [@schneider2016, Theorem 3.3 and its proof]. She reports that the computations for the first condition are complicated and did not succeed [@schneider2016, p. 48].
- *A quotient of $G_K$.* The maximal extension of $K$ without simple ramification is the union of the maximal $2$-extensions $M(2)$ of the extensions $M/K$ that are unramified of odd degree [@schneider2016, Introduction, pp. 1–2]. For $K \in \mathfrak{N}$, Schneider proves that its Galois group over $K$ is isomorphic to the profinite group with generators $x_1, \dots, x_{n+2}$, the relation $x_1^{2+q/2}[x_1, x_2] \cdots [x_{n+1}, x_{n+2}] = 1$, and the condition that the closed normal subgroup generated by $x_1, x_2, x_4, x_5, \dots, x_{n+2}$ is a pro-$2$ group [@schneider2016, Theorem 2.20]. This group depends only on $n$ and $q$.

**Status.** Open as far as the sources opened here show. Roe and Turturean present $G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ [@roeturturean2026, Theorem 1.2], but $\mathbb{Q}_2$ has odd degree, so it is not in $\mathfrak{N}$. They list the extension of their result to every finite $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ as a further question, naming Schneider's partial structural results as a point of comparison [@roeturturean2026, §11, item 1].

**Consequences for the classification.** The following is checked here, using [the maximal abelian subfield of a 2-adic field](maximal-abelian-subfields-of-2-adic-fields.md). For $K \in \mathfrak{N}$ with residue degree $f$, so that $f = s$, write $U_f$ for the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of degree $f$. That entry shows that the maximal subfield $K^0$ of $K$ abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_2$ is $K'U_f$.

- *The conjecture implies part 2 of the [classification of $p$-adic fields with isomorphic absolute Galois groups](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) for fields in $\mathfrak{N}$.* Let $K \in \mathfrak{N}$, and let $L$ have the same degree and $L^0 = K^0$. Since $U_f = K^0 \cap \mathbb{Q}_2^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and $K' = K^0 \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$, the fields $K$ and $L$ have the same $f$ and $K' = L'$. By part 3 of that entry, $L(\sqrt{-1})/L$ is ramified, so $L \in \mathfrak{N}$ with the same $\alpha$ and $q$ as $K$. The conjecture would then give $G_K \cong X \cong G_L$.
- *The conjecture is consistent with part 1.* Part 1 says that $G_K \cong G_L$ forces $K^0 = L^0$. If $K, L \in \mathfrak{N}$ have the same $n$, $s$ and $q$, then $q$ determines $\alpha$, hence $K' = L'$ as the fixed field of the subgroup generated by $-1 + 2^\alpha$, and so $K^0 = K'U_s = L^0$. So the conjecture never predicts an isomorphism that part 1 rules out.
- *No analogue for real $K'$ can use only these data.* $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{10})$ and $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-10})$ have even degree, the same residue degree, ramified $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$, real $K' = \mathbb{Q}_2$ and $q = 4$, by the examples in the same entry. They are abelian and distinct, so their absolute Galois groups are not isomorphic by part 1. So for fields in the classes $\mathfrak{M}_\alpha$, no description of $G_K$ can depend only on $n$, $s$ and $q$.

An example of two non-isomorphic fields in $\mathfrak{N}$ with the same degree and the same $K^0$ is $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt[4]{-2})$ and $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt[4]{-18})$, as checked in the classification entry. The conjecture predicts that their absolute Galois groups are isomorphic.
