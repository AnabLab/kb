**Setting.** Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$, and $K\langle\langle x, y\rangle\rangle$ the algebra of noncommutative formal power series, graded by weight, the number of letters, with the coproduct for which $x$ and $y$ are primitive. For $n \ge 2$ let $\mathfrak{t}_n$ be the degree-completed Lie algebra on symbols $t_{ij} = t_{ji}$, $i \ne j$, with relations $[t_{ij}, t_{kl}] = 0$ for distinct $i, j, k, l$ and $[t_{ij}, t_{ik} + t_{jk}] = 0$ for distinct $i, j, k$. The Grothendieck–Teichmüller Lie algebra $\mathfrak{grt}_1(K)$ is the space of Lie-like $\psi \in K\langle\langle x, y\rangle\rangle$ such that

1. $\psi(x, y) + \psi(y, x) = 0$,
2. $\psi(x, y) + \psi(y, -x-y) + \psi(-x-y, x) = 0$,
3. $\psi(t_{12}, t_{23}) - \psi(t_{12}, t_{23} + t_{24}) + \psi(t_{12} + t_{13}, t_{24} + t_{34}) - \psi(t_{13} + t_{23}, t_{34}) + \psi(t_{23}, t_{34}) = 0$ in $\mathfrak{t}_4$,

with the Ihara bracket $\{\psi, \phi\} = [\psi, \phi] + d_\psi(\phi) - d_\phi(\psi)$, where $d_\psi$ is the derivation with $d_\psi(x) = 0$ and $d_\psi(y) = [y, \psi]$ [@willwacher2026, §§2.1–2.3]. It is graded by weight. The equations are rational and linear in each weight, so $\mathfrak{grt}_1(K)_n = \mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})_n \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} K$ [@willwacher2026, §2.3, (6)], and nothing below depends on $K$. The prounipotent group $GRT_1(K) = \exp(\mathfrak{grt}_1(K))$, with the Ihara product, acts freely and transitively on the set $\mathrm{Ass}_1(K)$ of Drinfeld associators in Willwacher's normalization, whose hexagon equation is $e^{z/2}\Phi(x, y)e^{x/2}\Phi(y, z)e^{y/2}\Phi(z, x) = 1$, where $z = -x-y$ [@willwacher2026, §§2.3–2.4, (8), p. 3]. Furusho defines an associator as a pair $(\mu, \varphi)$ with $\mu \ne 0$, whose hexagon equations have exponents $\mu t_{ij}/2$ [@furusho2011four, Definition 1], and $\mathrm{Ass}_1(K)$ is the case $\mu = 1$. Furusho describes $GRT_1$ as the group-like series satisfying the defining equations of associators with $\mu = 0$, and credits it to Drinfeld [@furusho2011four, Definition 15]. It is a graded pro-unipotent analogue of the profinite [Grothendieck–Teichmüller group](grothendieck-teichmuller-group.md).

**Normalized elements.** For every $k \ge 1$ there is a homogeneous $\sigma_{2k+1} \in \mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})_{2k+1}$ whose coefficient $[x^{2k}y]\sigma_{2k+1}$ of the word $x^{2k}y$ is nonzero. Willwacher attributes this to Drinfeld [@willwacher2026, Lemma 2.1]. A family of such elements is normalized if every $[x^{2k}y]\sigma_{2k+1}$ equals $1$ [@willwacher2026, Definition 3.1]. The $\sigma_{2k+1}$ are not determined beyond their leading coefficients [@willwacher2026, §1].

**Conjecture (Deligne–Drinfeld).** $\mathfrak{grt}_1$ is the completed free Lie algebra on generators $\sigma_3, \sigma_5, \sigma_7, \dots$ of weights $3, 5, 7, \dots$, which are elements as above [@willwacher2026, §1], [@willwacher2015, §9, p. 28].

**Attribution.** Willwacher calls it the Deligne–Drinfeld conjecture [@willwacher2015, §9, p. 28], [@willwacher2026, §1]. Hain and Matsumoto trace a related conjecture of Goncharov on Galois actions to conjectures of Deligne and Ihara and to questions of Drinfeld, citing p. 859 of the translation of Drinfeld's paper [@hainmatsumoto2003, §1, p. 1]. Drinfeld's paper has not been opened for this entry.

**Equivalent forms.** These are checked here. Write $f_n$ for the dimension in weight $n$ of the free Lie algebra on one generator in each odd weight at least $3$. Its values for $n \le 12$ are in the table of the [Deligne–Ihara conjecture](deligne-ihara-conjecture.md): $f_n = 1$ for $n = 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10$, $f_n = 2$ for $n = 11, 12$, and $f_n = 0$ for $n = 1, 2, 4, 6$. The following are equivalent:

- (a) $\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})$ is the completed free Lie algebra on some homogeneous elements, one in each odd weight at least $3$;
- (b) every normalized family freely generates $\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})$;
- (c) $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})_n = f_n$ for every $n$.

*Proof.* Normalized families exist, so (b) implies (a), and (a) implies (c). Some family of elements $\sigma_{2k+1}$ of weight $2k+1$ generates a free Lie subalgebra (see the free half below). That subalgebra has dimension $f_n$ in weight $n$, so under (c) it is everything, which gives (a). For (a) implies (b):

- *Depth.* Call the number of letters $y$ in a word its depth. Every $\psi \in \mathfrak{grt}_1$ has depth at least $1$, meaning that all its words do. Indeed, $y \mapsto 0$ is a Hopf algebra map to $K[[x]]$, so $\psi(x, 0)$ is primitive, hence a multiple of $x$, and $\mathfrak{grt}_1$ has no weight-$1$ part [@willwacher2026, Lemma 2.3 and its proof].
- *Brackets.* For $\psi, \phi$ of depth at least $1$, the term $[\psi, \phi]$ of $\{\psi, \phi\}$ has depth at least $2$, and so do $d_\psi(\phi)$ and $d_\phi(\psi)$, because $d_\psi$ kills $x$ and replaces one letter $y$ of a word by $y\psi - \psi y$, and likewise for $d_\phi$. So every element of the derived subalgebra $\{\mathfrak{grt}_1, \mathfrak{grt}_1\}$ has depth at least $2$, and its coefficient of $x^{2k}y$, a word of depth $1$, is $0$. Hence a normalized $\sigma_{2k+1}$ is nonzero in $H_1 = \mathfrak{grt}_1/\{\mathfrak{grt}_1, \mathfrak{grt}_1\}$.
- *Generation.* Under (a), $H_1$ is one-dimensional in each odd weight at least $3$ and zero in other weights, so the $\sigma_{2k+1}$ map to a basis of $H_1$. Thus $\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})_n$ is spanned by $\sigma_n$, for odd $n \ge 3$, and the brackets $\{\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})_i, \mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})_j\}$ with $i + j = n$. By induction on $n$, the $\sigma_{2k+1}$ generate every weight space. So the graded map from the free Lie algebra on generators $s_{2k+1} \mapsto \sigma_{2k+1}$ is onto in each weight. It is injective because both sides have dimension $f_n$ in weight $n$.

Willwacher's Theorem 1.2 below gives another proof of (a) implies (b). By (c), the conjecture is a statement about the dimensions of the solution spaces of the three linear equations above.

**Small weights.** These are checked here, in the notation $u = [x, y]$. The substitutions $(x, y) \mapsto (y, -x-y)$ and $(x, y) \mapsto (-x-y, x)$ both fix $u$, and $(x, y) \mapsto (y, x)$ sends it to $-u$.

- *Weights $1$ and $2$.* There is no weight-$1$ part [@willwacher2026, Lemma 2.3]. In weight $2$ every Lie element is $cu$, and equation 2 gives $3cu = 0$, so $c = 0$.
- *Weight $3$.* Write $\psi = a[x, u] + b[y, u]$. Equation 1 gives $a = b$. Equation 2 holds for all $a$ and $b$, since its left side is $(a - b - a + b)[x, u] + (b + a - b - a)[y, u]$. So $\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})_3$ lies in the line spanned by $[x, [x, y]] + [y, [x, y]]$. It contains the nonzero $\sigma_3$, so it is that line. The coefficient of $x^2y$ in $[x, [x, y]] + [y, [x, y]]$ is $1$, so this is the normalized $\sigma_3$.
- *Weight $4$.* The Lie elements of weight $4$ have basis $e_1 = [x, [x, u]]$, $e_2 = [y, [x, u]]$, $e_3 = [y, [y, u]]$. For $\psi = ae_1 + be_2 + ce_3$, equation 1 gives $a = c$. In equation 2 each of $e_1, e_2, e_3$ has coefficient $2a - b + 2c$, so with $a = c$ it gives $b = 4a$. So equations 1 and 2 leave the line spanned by $e_1 + 4e_2 + e_3$. The prediction $f_4 = 0$ therefore rests on the pentagon equation 3, which is not checked here.

So (c) holds for $n \le 3$.

**Known results.**

- *The free half.* Brown's work implies that some elements $\sigma_3, \sigma_5, \dots$ as above generate a free Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak{grt}_1$ [@willwacher2015, §9, p. 28], [@willwacher2026, Theorem 1.1]. Willwacher calls this the proof of one half of the conjecture. He notes that it is not stated in this form in Brown's paper, but is a standard consequence of its main results [@willwacher2026, §1]. In a 2026 preprint he proves that every homogeneous family with nonzero leading coefficients generates a free Lie subalgebra [@willwacher2026, Theorem 1.2]. His proof avoids motives and follows Brown's main ideas. It uses Terasoma's theorem that the Brown–Zagier coefficient identity holds for every Drinfeld associator [@willwacher2026, §1 and Theorem 4.2]. In particular $\dim \mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})_n \ge f_n$ for every $n$.
- *The pentagon suffices.* Furusho proved that a group-like series satisfying the pentagon equation satisfies the two hexagon equations for some $\mu$, unique up to sign [@furusho2011four, Theorem 3]. So $GRT_1$ consists of the group-like series that satisfy the pentagon equation and have no quadratic terms [@furusho2011four, Remark 16 (i)]. For $\mathfrak{grt}_1$ it suffices to impose equation 3 and the absence of a term in $[x, y]$ [@willwacher2015, Theorem 6.2 and §6.1, p. 22].
- *Graph complexes.* Willwacher proves that the zeroth cohomology of Kontsevich's graph complex $\mathsf{GC}_2$ is isomorphic to $\mathfrak{grt}_1$ as a Lie algebra, and that its cohomology in negative degrees vanishes [@willwacher2015, Theorem 1.1]. Under this isomorphism, every representative of the class of $\sigma_{2j+1}$ has a nonzero coefficient on the wheel graph with $2j + 1$ spokes, equal up to sign to the coefficient of $x^{2j}y$ in $\sigma_{2j+1}$ [@willwacher2015, Proposition 9.1]. So the conjecture can be stated in terms of graph cohomology.
- *Other groups.* Furusho records inclusions $\mathrm{Gal}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{Z})_1 \subseteq GRT_1 \subseteq DMR_0 \subseteq KRV_0$ of the unipotent motivic Galois group, the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group, Racinet's double shuffle group and the Kashiwara–Vergne group. He asks whether all four are equal [@furusho2011four, Proposition 27 and Question 28].

**Motivic form.** Let $\mathrm{Gal}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{Z})$ be the Tannakian group of the mixed Tate motives unramified over $\mathbb{Z}$, with its canonical fibre functor, and $\mathrm{Gal}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{Z})_1$ its unipotent radical. A canonical splitting $\mathbb{G}_m \to \mathrm{Gal}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{Z})$ grades the Lie algebra of $\mathrm{Gal}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{Z})_1$, which is free on one generator in each degree $-3, -5, -7, \dots$ [@furusho2011four, Definition 11 and Remark 12]. The motivic fundamental group of $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ at the tangential base point $\vec{01}$ gives a graded action $\Psi$ of this group on $\exp$ of the completed free Lie algebra on two variables. Its image lies in $GRT_1$ [@furusho2011four, (10) and Proposition 17]. Brown proved that $\Psi$ is injective, and Furusho calls this a unipotent analogue of Belyi's theorem [@furusho2011four, Theorem 14], [@brown2012, §1, p. 1, and §2.1, p. 3]. Brown calls his faithfulness theorem a motivic analogue of Belyi's injectivity theorem [@brown2014, §1.1, Theorem 1.2]. Furusho writes that $\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi$ and $GRT_1$ are expected to be isomorphic [@furusho2011four, after Proposition 17].

This expectation is equivalent to the conjecture, as checked here, provided that $\Psi$ sends homogeneous elements of degree $-n$ of the Lie algebra to series of weight $n$. That is the normalization in Brown's account, where the forms $dt/t$ and $dt/(1-t)$ dual to the two variables have degree $1$ and the generators of the motivic Lie algebra have degrees $-3, -5, \dots$ [@brown2014, §1.1, p. 2, and §2, p. 9]. Indeed, $\Psi$ is an injective homomorphism of prounipotent groups over $\mathbb{Q}$, so the Lie algebra of $\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi$ is a graded subalgebra of $\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})$ with dimension $f_n$ in weight $n$. Prounipotent groups correspond to their Lie algebras, so $\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi = GRT_1$ exactly when this subalgebra is all of $\mathfrak{grt}_1(\mathbb{Q})$, that is, exactly when (c) holds.

So, in Brown's and Furusho's terms, the profinite [Grothendieck–Teichmüller conjecture](grothendieck-teichmuller-conjecture.md) asks whether $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ fills $\widehat{GT}$, and this conjecture asks whether the unipotent motivic Galois group fills $GRT_1$. The opened sources give no implication between the two.

**Relation to Galois actions.** Over $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, the graded Lie algebra of Ihara's filtration of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is free on Soulé elements $\sigma_3, \sigma_5, \dots$ of degrees $3, 5, \dots$, by the theorem in the [Deligne–Ihara conjecture](deligne-ihara-conjecture.md). Brown deduces it from the faithfulness of the de Rham motivic Galois group, together with a Zariski-dense homomorphism from $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ to the $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-points of the $\ell$-adic motivic Galois group [@brown2019, §2.3, pp. 3–4]. So that Lie algebra has the dimensions $f_n$ that the conjecture predicts for $\mathfrak{grt}_1$. The opened sources do not show that it embeds in $\mathfrak{grt}_1 \otimes \mathbb{Q}_\ell$.

**$p$-adic and archimedean associators.** Drinfeld's associator $\Phi_{\mathrm{KZ}}$, built from the Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equation over $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0, 1\}$, has multiple zeta values as coefficients. It is an associator with $\mu = 2\pi\sqrt{-1}$, but not an element of $GRT_1(\mathbb{C})$. Furusho's $p$-adic Drinfeld associator is not an associator, but it is an element of $GRT_1(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ [@furusho2011four, Example 4, Remark 5 (i) and Remark 18].

**Status.** Open. In 2015 Willwacher wrote that it is unclear whether the free subalgebra is all of $\mathfrak{grt}_1$ [@willwacher2015, §9, p. 28]. In 2026 he still calls Brown's theorem one half of the conjecture [@willwacher2026, §1]. He also writes that results of Brown, Chan, Galatius and Payne on the cohomology of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$ and of moduli of abelian varieties are restricted to a range of weights reflecting the range in which the conjecture is known to hold, and that his Theorem 1.2 removes this restriction [@willwacher2026, Remark 1.3]. The opened sources do not state that range precisely.
