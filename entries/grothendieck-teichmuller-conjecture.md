**Statement.** Let $\mathrm{Ih} : G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \widehat{GT}$, $\sigma \mapsto (\chi(\sigma), f_\sigma)$, be Ihara's injective homomorphism into the [profinite Grothendieck–Teichmüller group](grothendieck-teichmuller-group.md), where $\chi$ is the cyclotomic character. Is $\mathrm{Ih}$ surjective, so that it is an isomorphism?

Dolgushev, Le and Lorenz attribute the question to Ihara's address at the International Congress of Mathematicians [@dolgushevlelorenz2020, §1, p. 2]. Pop and Topaz call the prediction that $\mathrm{Ih}$ is an isomorphism the Grothendieck–Teichmüller conjecture. They call it arguably the most important open problem in the area, and write that surjectivity "remains completely open to this day" [@poptopaz2025, §1, p. 2]. Schneps relates it to Grothendieck's question, in the *Esquisse d'un programme*, of whether $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is the automorphism group of the tower of fundamental groupoids of the moduli spaces of curves [@schneps1997, pp. 183–185 and p. 198]. Ihara's ICM paper and the *Esquisse* have not been opened for this entry.

**Geometric form.** By the theorem of Harbater and Schneps recorded in the [definition entry](grothendieck-teichmuller-group.md), which matches the natural outer action of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with $\mathrm{Ih}$, the conjecture says: every outer automorphism of the profinite fundamental group $\widehat{K}(0,5)$ of $\mathcal{M}_{0,5}$ that sends the conjugacy class of each standard generator $x_{ij}$ to that of $x_{ij}^\lambda$, for a single $\lambda \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$, and commutes with the permutations of the five marked points, comes from $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@harbaterschneps2000, §0.1].

**Reformulations and tests.** These are checked here. Tests 2, 4 and 5 also use that $\mathrm{Ih}$ is continuous, which amounts to $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ acting on each finite quotient of $\widehat{F}_2$ by an open characteristic subgroup through a finite quotient of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. This standard fact is not checked against a source here.

1. *Reduction to $\widehat{GT}^1$.* Write $\lambda : \widehat{GT} \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$ for the first coordinate and $\widehat{GT}^1$ for its kernel. Then $\lambda \circ \mathrm{Ih} = \chi$ [@lochakschneps2006, §1], and $\chi$ is surjective with kernel $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$, since $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_\infty) = \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ by the Kronecker–Weber theorem. So $\mathrm{Ih}$ is surjective exactly when $\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}) = \widehat{GT}^1$. Indeed, if $\mathrm{Ih}(\sigma) \in \widehat{GT}^1$ then $\chi(\sigma) = 1$. Conversely, for $g \in \widehat{GT}$ choose $\sigma$ with $\chi(\sigma) = \lambda(g)$, so that $\mathrm{Ih}(\sigma)^{-1}g \in \widehat{GT}^1$.
2. *Finite quotients.* $\mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})$ is compact, hence closed. So $\mathrm{Ih}$ is surjective exactly when its composite with $\widehat{GT} \to \widehat{GT}/N$ is surjective for every open normal subgroup $N$.
3. *Torsion.* If $\mathrm{Ih}$ is surjective, then every nontrivial element of finite order in $\widehat{GT}$ is conjugate to $c = (-1, 1)$. Indeed, such an element is $\mathrm{Ih}(\sigma)$ with $\sigma \ne 1$ of finite order, since $\mathrm{Ih}$ is injective. By the [Artin–Schreier theorem](artin-schreier-theorem.md) $\sigma$ has order $2$, so by part 1 of [involutions and real places](involutions-and-real-places.md) it is conjugate in $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ to complex conjugation, whose image is $c$ [@bdhp2024, Remark 1.10]. Lochak and Schneps ask whether this holds [@lochakschneps2006, Question 1.4]. Schneps and Pop showed that any further element of finite order becomes trivial in the maximal pro-nilpotent quotient of $\widehat{GT}$ [@schneps1997, §1.4 (4)].
4. *Abelianization.* If $\mathrm{Ih}$ is surjective, then $\widehat{GT}^1$ is the closed derived subgroup of $\widehat{GT}$. Indeed, $\lambda$ is a continuous homomorphism to an abelian group, being read off from the action on the abelianization of $\widehat{F}_2$, so the closed derived subgroup lies in $\widehat{GT}^1$. Conversely, $G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}}$ is the closed derived subgroup of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, so by test 1 and continuity $\widehat{GT}^1 = \mathrm{Ih}(G_{\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}})$ lies in the closure of $\mathrm{Ih}([G_{\mathbb{Q}}, G_{\mathbb{Q}}])$. Since $\mathrm{Ih}$ is surjective, $\mathrm{Ih}([G_{\mathbb{Q}}, G_{\mathbb{Q}}]) = [\widehat{GT}, \widehat{GT}]$. Ihara asked whether this holds. Schneps reports it as unsolved [@schneps1997, §1.4 (2)], and Lochak and Schneps list it again [@lochakschneps2006, Question 1.2].
5. *Outer automorphisms.* If $\mathrm{Ih}$ is surjective, then it is an isomorphism of profinite groups, being a continuous bijection between compact Hausdorff groups. Every automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is inner, because by the [Neukirch–Uchida theorem](neukirch-uchida-theorem.md) it is conjugation by an automorphism of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, which is an element of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. So every continuous automorphism of $\widehat{GT}$ would be inner. Schneps records whether $\mathrm{Out}(\widehat{GT})$ is trivial as a question of Pop, absolutely unsolved [@schneps1997, §1.4 (5)].
6. *Refinements.* Lochak and Schneps describe subgroups $\widehat{GS} \subseteq \mathbb{I} \subseteq \Lambda \subseteq \widehat{GT}$, and Ihara's $\widehat{GTK}$, each defined by further relations expressing geometric properties of the Galois action, such as actions on mapping class groups in every genus, and each containing $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$. They ask whether these groups differ and whether any of them is isomorphic to $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@lochakschneps2006, §2 and Question 2.1]. If $\mathrm{Ih}$ is surjective, all of them equal $\widehat{GT}$, so showing that one is a proper subgroup would refute the conjecture.

**Evidence at finite level.** Bortnovskyi, Dolgushev, Holikov and Pashkovskyi use groups of GT-shadows, finite approximations of the larger group $\widehat{GT}_0$ attached to finite-index normal subgroups of the braid group $B_3$. For a family of such subgroups $\mathsf{K}^{(2^\alpha)}$ with $\alpha \ge 2$, defined through dihedral groups, they show that the finite group $\mathsf{GT}(\mathsf{K}^{(2^\alpha)})$ is a quotient of $\widehat{GT}$, that the composite $G_{\mathbb{Q}} \to \mathsf{GT}(\mathsf{K}^{(2^\alpha)})$ is surjective, and that this group is non-abelian for $\alpha \ge 3$ [@bdhp2024, Theorem 5.3 and Corollary 5.4]. The limit of these groups is an infinite non-abelian quotient of $\widehat{GT}$ onto which $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ maps surjectively. It is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of index $2$ in $\mathbb{Z}_2 \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ [@bdhp2024, Theorem 5.7 and Proposition 5.8]. They also explain how, for every odd prime $\ell < 2^{31}$, earlier work of Ihara, Ichimura–Kaneko and Coleman, with the Kummer–Vandiver conjecture verified in that range, gives another infinite non-abelian quotient of $\widehat{GT}$ onto which $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ maps surjectively [@bdhp2024, §1.6]. By test 2, surjectivity onto every finite quotient of $\widehat{GT}$ would prove the conjecture, and the quotients above are special ones. The paper is a preprint.

**Proved variants.** Ihara asked whether $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is the automorphism group of the geometric fundamental group functor on $\mathbb{Q}$-varieties, and Oda and Matsumoto conjectured that it is. Pop proved this absolute form in an unpublished manuscript [@topaz2018, §1], [@lochakschneps2006, §0]. Pop and Topaz refer to Pop's 2019 paper for its history and for a birational form, in which the category consists of $\mathcal{M}_{0,4}$, $\mathcal{M}_{0,5}$ and the nonempty open affine subvarieties of $\mathcal{M}_{0,5}$, with inclusions and three projections to $\mathcal{M}_{0,4}$. There $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is the automorphism group of the geometric fundamental group functor, and also of its pro-$\ell$ abelian-by-central version [@poptopaz2025, §1.1]. Their paper, accepted for publication in the Journal of the European Mathematical Society, proves the abelian-by-central analogue for complements of line arrangements in the plane [@poptopaz2025, Main Theorem and Theorem A]. They present it as a step between the birational category, which yields $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, and the category with objects $\mathcal{M}_{0,4}$ and $\mathcal{M}_{0,5}$, which yields $\widehat{GT}$ [@poptopaz2025, §1.2]. See also the [abelian-by-central forms](abelian-by-central-ihara-oda-matsumoto.md). All these variants allow far more varieties or morphisms than the conjecture, which concerns only $\mathcal{M}_{0,4}$ and $\mathcal{M}_{0,5}$. The sources opened here do not derive the conjecture from any of them.

**Status.** Open. Pop and Topaz state this in 2025 [@poptopaz2025, §1, p. 2], and Bortnovskyi, Dolgushev, Holikov and Pashkovskyi call it a very challenging problem in 2026 [@bdhp2024, §1].
