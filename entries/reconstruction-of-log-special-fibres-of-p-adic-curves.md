**Setting.** For $i = 1, 2$, let $K_i$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_{p_i}$ with residue field $k_i$, and let $X_i$ be a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) over $K_i$ of type $(g_i, r_i)$, with smooth compactification $Y_i$. Write $\Pi_{X_i}$ for its étale fundamental group, for a chosen geometric basepoint, and $\Delta_{X_i}$ for the kernel of $\Pi_{X_i} \to G_{K_i}$.

Say that $X_i$ has *stable reduction* if $Y_i$, marked by its cusps, extends to a stable pointed curve over $\mathcal{O}_{K_i}$. Its *stable model* $\mathcal{X}_i$ is the complement of the marked points in that curve [@mochizuki2004absolute, §2, pp. 22–23]. Give $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{K_i}$ the log structure of its closed point and $\mathcal{X}_i$ the log structure of the functions invertible on $X_i$, and restrict both to the special fibres. This gives log schemes $\mathcal{X}^{\log}_{i,k_i} \to k_i^{\log}$ [@mochizuki2004absolute, Theorem 2.7, p. 35]. The *dual semi-graph with compact structure* of the special fibre is its dual graph, with a vertex for each irreducible component and an edge for each node, together with an extra edge for each cusp [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 2.3, p. 25]. The *admissible quotient* $\Pi_{X_i} \to \Pi^{\mathrm{adm}}_{X_i}$ is the one whose finite quotients correspond to admissible coverings of the stable model after base change to the rings of integers of tamely ramified extensions of $K_i$. It lies over the Galois group $G^{\log}_{k_i}$ of the maximal tamely ramified extension of $K_i$ [@mochizuki2004absolute, p. 23].

**Theorem (Mochizuki).** Let $\alpha : \Pi_{X_1} \to \Pi_{X_2}$ be an isomorphism of profinite groups. No isomorphism between $K_1$ and $K_2$, or between $G_{K_1}$ and $G_{K_2}$, is given.

1. $\alpha(\Delta_{X_1}) = \Delta_{X_2}$, so $\alpha$ induces an isomorphism $G_{K_1} \to G_{K_2}$, and $p_1 = p_2$ [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 1.3.8, p. 18, and §2, p. 22]. The types agree, and $\alpha$ maps inertia groups of cusps to inertia groups of cusps [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 1.3.9, p. 19].
2. $X_1$ has stable reduction exactly when $X_2$ does [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 2.1, p. 22].
3. Suppose that they do. Then $\alpha$ is compatible with the admissible quotients [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 2.2 (ii), p. 23]. It induces an isomorphism between the dual semi-graphs with compact structure of the special fibres, functorial with respect to finite étale coverings of the $X_i$ [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 2.3, p. 25].
4. The induced isomorphism $\Pi^{\mathrm{adm}}_{X_1} \to \Pi^{\mathrm{adm}}_{X_2}$ lies over an isomorphism $G^{\log}_{k_1} \to G^{\log}_{k_2}$. It arises from a unique commutative diagram of log schemes, made of an isomorphism $\mathcal{X}^{\log}_{1,k_1} \to \mathcal{X}^{\log}_{2,k_2}$ over an isomorphism $k_1^{\log} \to k_2^{\log}$ and of isomorphisms between the universal coverings given by the basepoints [@mochizuki2004absolute, Theorem 2.7, p. 35]. This is functorial with respect to finite étale coverings of the $X_i$ [@mochizuki2004absolute, proof of Theorem 2.7, pp. 34–36].

Mochizuki presents this as recovering the special fibre with its log structure solely from the isomorphism class of the profinite group, at a time when he thought it unlikely that $X_i$ itself could be recovered in general [@mochizuki2004absolute, Introduction, p. 2]. He regards the logarithmic special fibre as a $p$-adic analogue of the topological type [@mochizuki2004absolute, Remark 2.7.1, p. 37].

**How the proof goes.**

- *Stability.* Whether $X_i$ has stable reduction is decided by the action of the inertia subgroup of $G_{K_i}$ on the abelianized geometric fundamental group of $Y_i$ and on the set of conjugacy classes of inertia groups of cusps. Since $\alpha$ preserves inertia subgroups, it preserves this criterion [@mochizuki2004absolute, proof of Lemma 2.1, pp. 22–23].
- *Admissible coverings.* Mochizuki had earlier shown that the admissible quotient can be recovered from $\Pi_X \to G_K$ over a fixed field [@mochizuki1996profinite, Proposition 8.4, p. 35]. That argument needs only that $\alpha$ preserve the inertia and wild inertia subgroups [@mochizuki2004absolute, proof of Lemma 2.2, p. 24].
- *Components and nodes.* These are recovered from the admissible quotient over $G^{\log}_k$ by the theory of the same earlier paper, which for instance counts the nodes of suitable cyclic admissible coverings [@mochizuki1996profinite, Proposition 5.1, p. 20, and Corollary 5.3, p. 21]. From $\alpha$ this needs only that Frobenius elements be preserved, so that the weight filtrations on $\ell$-adic cohomology for $\ell \ne p$ are too [@mochizuki2004absolute, proof of Lemma 2.3, p. 25].
- *Degree one.* The last step applies the logarithmic Grothendieck conjecture for singular stable curves over finite fields [@mochizuki1996profinite, Theorem 7.2, pp. 28–29]. This needs the isomorphism to be of degree one. Over a fixed field this was proved in the earlier paper [@mochizuki1996profinite, Lemma 9.1, p. 39], and Mochizuki reworks the argument for an arbitrary $\alpha$ [@mochizuki2004absolute, p. 26]. After passing to a covering, he uses a cyclic étale covering of the stable model with ordinary new part, constructed after Raynaud, and positivity of the degree [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemmas 2.4 and 2.5, pp. 26–29]. The same argument shows that the isomorphism $G^{\log}_{k_1} \to G^{\log}_{k_2}$ comes from an isomorphism of logarithmic points [@mochizuki2004absolute, Lemma 2.6 and the discussion after it, pp. 32–34].
- *Proper curves with good reduction.* Here he passes to a finite étale Galois covering with bad stable reduction, reconstructs its logarithmic special fibre with the Galois action, and recovers the special fibre of $X_i$ as a quotient of a component that dominates it [@mochizuki2004absolute, proof of Theorem 2.7, pp. 35–36].

**Over a fixed field, and from tempered groups.**

- *The relative version.* For proper hyperbolic curves $X, X'$ with stable reduction over the same finite $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$, Mochizuki had shown that isomorphisms $\Pi_X \to \Pi_{X'}$ compatible with the projections to $G_K$ induce isomorphisms of the logarithmic special fibres over $k^{\log}$, compatibly with the admissible quotients [@mochizuki1996profinite, Theorem 9.2, p. 39]. The theorem above removes both the fixed field and the properness.
- *Geometric tempered groups.* Let $X$ and $X'$ be hyperbolic curves over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathbb{Q}_{p'}$. Every isomorphism of topological groups between their geometric tempered fundamental groups determines, functorially, an isomorphism between the semi-graphs of anabelioids with compact structure of the geometric special fibres of their stable models, and if one exists then $p = p'$ [@mochizuki2006semigraphs, Corollary 3.11, pp. 45–46]. In particular it gives an isomorphism of the dual semi-graphs with compact structure. The vertices and the edges between them are recovered as the conjugacy classes of maximal compact subgroups and of nontrivial intersections of two distinct ones [@mochizuki2006semigraphs, Introduction, p. 2]. The open edges given by the cusps need a further argument, which Mochizuki supplies for curves [@mochizuki2006semigraphs, Remark 3.9.1, p. 43, and p. 45]. No Galois action is used. For Mumford curves, [Lepage](tempered-reconstruction-of-mumford-metrics.md) recovers the edge lengths as well.
- *From profinite to tempered groups.* Applying Lemma 2.3 to corresponding open subgroups, Mochizuki shows that every outer isomorphism between the profinite groups $\Pi_X$ and $\Pi_{X'}$ of hyperbolic curves over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ arises from a unique outer isomorphism between their arithmetic tempered fundamental groups [@mochizuki2006semigraphs, Theorem 6.6 and its proof, pp. 72–73].
- *The profinite geometric group alone does not suffice.* As an abstract profinite group, $\Delta_X$ depends only on the type $(g, r)$ (see [tame fundamental groups](tame-fundamental-groups-in-positive-characteristic.md), which cites this). But curves of one type can have different reductions. For instance, two elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, one with good and one with split multiplicative reduction, each minus its origin, are both of type $(1,1)$. The first has dual semi-graph one vertex of genus $1$ with one open edge, and the second one vertex of genus $0$ with a loop and one open edge. So the reduction is seen by the Galois action or by the tempered topology, and is lost in the profinite completion of the geometric tempered group.

**Questions raised in 2004, and their answer.** Mochizuki noted that an isomorphism $k_1^{\log} \to k_2^{\log}$ as in part 4 need not come from an isomorphism of fields $K_1 \to K_2$. It might, for instance, multiply the section of the log structure given by $p$ by a root of unity, and he did not know whether this happens for the isomorphisms of the theorem [@mochizuki2004absolute, Remark 2.7.2, pp. 37–38]. He also asked whether these isomorphisms preserve more information about the lifting of the logarithmic special fibre to $\mathcal{O}_K$, as he expected for canonical liftings such as Serre–Tate canonical liftings [@mochizuki2004absolute, Remark 2.7.3, p. 38].

The following is checked here, assuming Mochizuki and Tsujimura's preprint. By their [absolute Grothendieck conjecture](absolute-grothendieck-conjecture-for-p-adic-curves.md), applied with $\Sigma$ the set of all primes, $\alpha$ agrees, up to composition with an inner automorphism, with the isomorphism induced by an isomorphism of schemes $f : X_1 \to X_2$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, Theorem 3.12, p. 91]. Also, $f$ lies over an isomorphism of fields $\tau : K_1 \to K_2$ [@mochizukitsujimura2024, proof of Theorem 3.12, p. 91]. So $\Pi_{X_i}$ determines $X_i$ itself, with its lifting, and not just its logarithmic special fibre, which answers the second question for every curve, canonical lifting or not.

For the first, recall how part 4 produces $k_1^{\log} \to k_2^{\log}$ [@mochizuki2004absolute, Theorem 2.7, p. 35]. The isomorphism $\lambda : G^{\log}_{k_1} \to G^{\log}_{k_2}$ induced by $\alpha$ gives an isomorphism of residue fields $\sigma$, and $k_1^{\log} \to k_2^{\log}$ is the unique logarithmic extension of $\sigma$ that matches uniformizers as $\lambda$ matches their Kummer torsors. Here a uniformizer $\pi$ corresponds to the torsor of its roots $\pi^{1/N}$ for $N$ prime to $p$ [@mochizuki2004absolute, Proposition 1.2.3 (ii), p. 13, and Proposition 1.2.4 (iii) and its proof, pp. 14–15]. When $\alpha$ is induced by $f$, up to an inner automorphism, $\lambda$ is induced by an extension of $\tau$ to the algebraic closures, up to an inner automorphism of $G^{\log}_{k_2}$. Then $\sigma$ is the reduction of that extension, the roots of $\pi$ go to the roots of $\tau(\pi)$, and inner automorphisms act trivially on these torsor classes. So the reduction of $\tau : \mathcal{O}_{K_1} \to \mathcal{O}_{K_2}$ is a logarithmic extension of $\sigma$ with the required matching, and by uniqueness it is the isomorphism of part 4. It sends the section given by $p$ to the section given by $\tau(p) = p$. So the phenomenon of the first question does not occur.
