**Setting.** Let $\lambda$ be an algebraically closed field and $N\ge1$. Let $V=\Gamma(\mathbb P^N_\lambda,\mathcal O(1))$ be the space of linear forms, and for $0\ne v\in V$ let $H_v\subset\mathbb P^N$ be its hyperplane. A *variety* is a separated scheme of finite type over $\lambda$. A property holds *for general $H$* if there is a nonempty Zariski open $\Omega\subseteq V\cong\mathbb A^{N+1}_\lambda$ such that it holds for $H_v$ whenever $0\ne v\in\Omega(\lambda)$. As $\lambda$ is infinite, finitely many such conditions can be met at once. For a morphism $f:Y\to\mathbb P^N$, $f^{-1}(H_v)$ means the scheme-theoretic preimage, the zero scheme of $f^*v\in\Gamma(Y,f^*\mathcal O(1))$.

**Theorem.**

1. Let $Y$ be an integral variety of dimension $a\ge2$ and $f:Y\to\mathbb P^N$ a quasi-finite morphism. For general $H$, $f^{-1}(H)$ is geometrically irreducible, in particular nonempty, and has dimension $a-1$.
2. Let $U\subseteq\mathbb P^N$ be a smooth integral locally closed subvariety of dimension $a\ge2$, and $W\to U$ a finite étale morphism with $W$ integral. For general $H$, both $U\cap H$ and $W\times_U(U\cap H)$ are nonempty, smooth and integral of dimension $a-1$.
3. *Curve sections.* Let $X\subseteq\mathbb P^N$ be an integral projective variety of dimension $d\ge2$, and $B\subset X$ a closed subset of dimension at most $d-2$ with $X\setminus B$ smooth. Let $D_1,\dots,D_s$ be prime divisors of $X$ such that the $D_i\setminus B$ are smooth and pairwise disjoint. Let $U\subseteq X$ be a smooth dense open subset and $W_1,\dots,W_m$ integral schemes finite étale over $U$. Then there are hyperplanes $H_1,\dots,H_{d-1}$, each chosen in a nonempty open set depending on the previous ones, such that $C=X\cap H_1\cap\dots\cap H_{d-1}$ is a smooth integral projective curve and:
   - (a) $C\cap B=\varnothing$, and no point of $C$ lies on two of the $D_i$;
   - (b) the scheme $C\cap D_i$ is reduced and consists of exactly $\deg D_i$ points, and at each of them a local equation of $D_i$ restricts to a uniformizer of $C$;
   - (c) each $W_j\times_U(U\cap C)$ is nonempty, smooth and integral.

Part 1 is the irreducibility assertion of Lemma 4.2 of the September 2026 manuscript [@openai2026bp, Lemma 4.2, p. 13], and part 2 contains its smoothness assertion. Part 2 is Lemma 4.2 of the October 2026 mod-$\ell$ manuscript [@openai2026modl, Lemma 4.2, p. 15]. Part 3 is the step "cut down to a curve" in the proofs of Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.3 of those manuscripts, apart from the Hilbert polynomial computation of the genus [@openai2026bp, proof of Lemma 4.1, p. 14] [@openai2026modl, proof of Proposition 4.3, p. 16]. Both manuscripts prove irreducibility by an incidence argument on pairs of points. The proof below instead checks the hypotheses of the Stacks project's Bertini irreducibility theorem.

**Cited inputs.** In the Stacks project's Situation 33.47.2, $X$ is a scheme over a field $k$, $\mathcal L$ an invertible sheaf on it, $\psi:V\to\Gamma(X,\mathcal L)$ a linear map from a finite-dimensional space, and $H_v=Z(\psi(v))$. A property holds for general $v$ if there is a nonempty open $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb A(V)$ such that it holds for every $v$ given by a point of $\Omega$ with values in an extension field $k'$ [@stacks2026, Situation 33.47.2, Tag 0G47].

- (S1) *Bertini irreducibility.* Suppose that $X$ is of finite type and geometrically irreducible over $k$; that there are $v_1,v_2,v_3\in V$ and an irreducible component $Z$ of $H_{v_2}\cap H_{v_3}$ with $Z\not\subset H_{v_1}$ and $\operatorname{codim}(Z,X)=2$; and that every irreducible component of $\bigcap_{v\in V}H_v$ has codimension at least $2$ in $X$. Then for general $v$ the scheme $H_v$ is geometrically irreducible [@stacks2026, Lemma 37.32.3, Tag 0G4F].
- (S2) *Bertini smoothness.* Suppose that $X$ is smooth over $k$, that $\psi(V)$ generates $\mathcal L$, and that the induced morphism $X\to\mathbf P(V)$ is an immersion. Then for general $v$ the scheme $H_v$ is smooth [@stacks2026, Lemma 33.47.3, Tag 0FD6].
- (S3) Irreducible spaces are nonempty by definition [@stacks2026, Definition 5.8.1, Tag 004V]. An irreducible scheme over a separably closed field is geometrically irreducible [@stacks2026, Lemma 33.8.3, Tag 020J].
- (S4) *Dimension theory.* A quasi-finite morphism has finite fibres [@stacks2026, Lemma 29.21.10, Tag 02NH]. If $Z$ is an irreducible closed subset of a scheme $Y$ with generic point $\zeta$, then $\operatorname{codim}(Z,Y)=\dim\mathcal O_{Y,\zeta}$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 28.10.3, Tag 02IZ]. If $Y$ is an integral variety of dimension $a$, then $\dim\mathcal O_{Y,\zeta}=a-\dim Z$: on an affine open $\mathrm{Spec}\,S$ meeting $Z$, with $\mathfrak p$ the prime of $Z$, every nonempty open has dimension $a$ and $\dim Z=\operatorname{trdeg}_\lambda\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 10.116.1, Tag 00P0], so [@stacks2026, Lemma 10.116.3, Tag 00P1] gives $\dim S_{\mathfrak p}=a-\dim Z$. Consequently, if $T$ is an integral variety of dimension $b$ and $s$ is a section of an invertible sheaf on $T$ that is not identically zero, every irreducible component of $Z(s)$ has dimension $b-1$. Locally $s$ is a nonzero element of a domain, a prime minimal over it has height $1$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 10.60.11, Tag 00KV], and the formula applies.

**Proof of part 1.** Apply (S1) with $k=k'=\lambda$, $X=Y$, $\mathcal L=f^*\mathcal O(1)$ and $\psi=f^*$, and write $Y_v=Z(f^*v)=f^{-1}(H_v)$. Here $Y$ is of finite type, and it is geometrically irreducible by (S3). The linear forms have no common zero on $\mathbb P^N$, so $\bigcap_vY_v=\varnothing$ and the last hypothesis of (S1) holds vacuously. Two observations prepare the remaining hypothesis.

- (i) If $T\subseteq Y$ is irreducible and closed of positive dimension, then $f(T)$ is infinite. Otherwise $T$ would lie in finitely many fibres of $f$, which are finite by (S4), whereas an irreducible scheme of finite type over a field of positive dimension has infinitely many points.
- (ii) If $T\subseteq Y$ is an integral closed subvariety of dimension $b\ge1$ and $f(T)\not\subset H_v$, then $f^*v$ is not identically zero on $T$. So every irreducible component of $T\cap Y_v$ has dimension $b-1$, by (S4).

By (i), $f(Y)$ contains two points $P_0\ne P_1$. Choose $v_2$ vanishing at $P_0$ but not at $P_1$. Then $Y_{v_2}$ is nonempty and $f(Y)\not\subset H_{v_2}$, so by (ii) with $T=Y$ its irreducible components $T_1,\dots,T_r$ have dimension $a-1\ge1$. Choose a closed point $y\in T_1$ and put $P=f(y)$. By (i), each $f(T_i)$ contains a point $P_i\ne P$. Let $V_P\subset V$ be the hyperplane of forms vanishing at $P$. The forms in $V_P$ that vanish on $f(T_i)$ lie in the proper subspace of those vanishing at $P_i$. As $\lambda$ is infinite, some $v_3\in V_P$ lies in none of these $r$ subspaces. Then $f(T_i)\not\subset H_{v_3}$ for every $i$, so by (ii) every irreducible component of every $T_i\cap Y_{v_3}$ has dimension $a-2$. Each irreducible component of the finite union $Y_{v_2}\cap Y_{v_3}=\bigcup_i(T_i\cap Y_{v_3})$ is an irreducible component of one of the sets in the union. So all of them have dimension $a-2$, and $y$ lies on one of them, $Z$. By (S4), $\operatorname{codim}(Z,Y)=2$. Finally choose $v_1$ not vanishing at $P$. Then $y\in Z$ and $y\notin Y_{v_1}$.

So (S1) gives a nonempty open $\Omega_1\subseteq V$ such that $Y_v$ is geometrically irreducible, and in particular nonempty, for $v\in\Omega_1(\lambda)$. The forms with $f(Y)\subseteq H_v$ form a proper linear subspace by (i). Off it, (ii) gives $\dim Y_v=a-1$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Apply (S2) to $U$, to $\mathcal O(1)|_U$ and to the restrictions of the linear forms. These generate $\mathcal O(1)$ and induce the immersion $U\subseteq\mathbb P^N$, so $U\cap H$ is smooth for general $H$. The base change $W\times_U(U\cap H)\to U\cap H$ is étale, so $W\times_U(U\cap H)$ is smooth as well, since étale morphisms are smooth and smoothness is stable under composition.

The morphism $W\to U$ is étale, hence open, and finite, hence closed. So its image is all of the irreducible $U$, the generic point of $W$ maps to that of $U$, and the residue field extension there is finite. By (S4), $\dim W=\dim U=a$. The composites $W\to U\subseteq\mathbb P^N$ and $U\subseteq\mathbb P^N$ are quasi-finite, and the preimages of $H$ under them are $W\times_U(U\cap H)$ and $U\cap H$. Part 1 makes both irreducible, hence nonempty, of dimension $a-1$, for general $H$. Smooth schemes are reduced, so both are integral. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Choose $H_1,\dots,H_{d-1}$ one at a time. For $0\le j\le d-1$ put $L_j=H_1\cap\dots\cap H_j$, with $L_0=\mathbb P^N$, and
$$S_j=(X\setminus B)\cap L_j,\quad U_j=U\cap L_j,\quad W_{i,j}=W_i\times_UU_j,\quad E_{i,j}=(D_i\setminus B)\cap L_j,\quad B_j=B\cap L_j.$$
At step $j$, take $H_j$ general, given $H_1,\dots,H_{j-1}$, for the following finitely many conditions.

1. $S_j$ is nonempty, smooth and integral of dimension $d-j$. For $j\le d-1$, $S_{j-1}$ has dimension $d-j+1\ge2$, so this is part 2 applied to $S_{j-1}$, with $W$ the identity cover.
2. Each $U_j$ and each $W_{i,j}$ is nonempty, smooth and integral, by part 2 applied to $W_{i,j-1}\to U_{j-1}$, which is the base change of $W_i\to U$.
3. Each $E_{i,j}$ is smooth, by (S2) applied to the smooth scheme $E_{i,j-1}$ immersed in $\mathbb P^N$, when it is nonempty.
4. $H_j$ contains no irreducible component of $B_{j-1}$. For each component, this excludes a proper linear subspace of forms. Then each component of $B_{j-1}$ of positive dimension meets $H_j$ in a proper closed subset, of smaller dimension, and each zero-dimensional component misses $H_j$. So $\dim B_j\le\dim B_{j-1}-1$, where the empty set has dimension $-1$.
5. $H_j$ contains no associated point of the closed subschemes $D_i\cap L_{j-1}$. There are finitely many such points, and each is avoided by an open condition.

Since $\dim B\le d-2$, condition 4 gives $B_{d-1}=\varnothing$. So $C=X\cap L_{d-1}$ lies in the open subscheme $X\setminus B$ and equals $S_{d-1}$, which is a smooth integral curve by condition 1. It is closed in $\mathbb P^N$, hence projective. For $i\ne i'$, $D_i\cap D_{i'}\subseteq B$, which proves (a). Condition 2 with $j=d-1$ is (c).

For (b), the scheme $C\cap D_i=D_i\cap L_{d-1}$ equals $E_{i,d-1}$, since $B_{d-1}=\varnothing$. By condition 5, each linear equation is a nonzerodivisor on the preceding section of $D_i$, so $0\to\mathcal O_Z(-1)\to\mathcal O_Z\to\mathcal O_{Z\cap H_j}\to0$ is exact for $Z=D_i\cap L_{j-1}$. So the Hilbert polynomial of $D_i\cap L_j$ is $\Delta^jP_{D_i}$, with $\Delta P(n)=P(n)-P(n-1)$. The leading term of $P_{D_i}$ is $\deg D_i\cdot n^{d-1}/(d-1)!$, so $\Delta^{d-1}P_{D_i}$ is the constant $\deg D_i$. Thus $C\cap D_i$ is zero-dimensional of length $\deg D_i$. It is smooth over $\lambda$ by condition 3, hence reduced, so it consists of exactly $\deg D_i$ points. At such a point $P$, $X$ is smooth because $P\notin B$. So $\mathcal O_{X,P}$ is a regular local ring, hence factorial, and the prime of $D_i$ there is generated by one element $\pi$. Then $\mathcal O_{C,P}/(\pi)=\mathcal O_{C\cap D_i,P}=\lambda$, so $\pi$ generates the maximal ideal of the discrete valuation ring $\mathcal O_{C,P}$. $\square$

**How the manuscripts use it.** In the finite residual test, part 3 is applied over the good fibre $X_s$. There $B$ is the fibre of the closed set of codimension at least $2$ off which the model and the $D_i$ are smooth and the local identities $g_j=u\pi^n$ hold, and the $W_j$ are the components of the root cover. Then (b) gives $\mathrm{ord}_P(g_j|_C)=\mathrm{ord}_{D_i}(g_j)$ at the points of $C\cap D_i$. Integrality of the $W_j\times_U(U\cap C)$ is what makes the count of components, and hence the relations among the residues, survive. This is the use in [the finite residual tests](uniform-support-bound-for-kummer-pencils.md), and through it in [the mod-ℓ support bound](uniform-support-bound-for-mod-l-kummer-pencils.md).

**Checks.**

- *Dimension at least $2$ is needed in part 1.* A smooth conic $Y\subset\mathbb P^2$ meets a general line in two points.
- *Quasi-finiteness is needed.* Map $Y=\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1$ to $\mathbb P^2$ by the first projection followed by a conic embedding. The preimage of a general line is two disjoint fibres.
- *Étaleness is needed for smoothness in part 2.* In characteristic $p$, the Frobenius map $\mathbb A^2\to\mathbb A^2$, $(x,y)\mapsto(x^p,y^p)$, composed with $\mathbb A^2\subset\mathbb P^2$, is quasi-finite, so part 1 applies. But the preimage of every line $ax+by+c=0$ in $\mathbb A^2$ is defined by $(a^{1/p}x+b^{1/p}y+c^{1/p})^p$, so it is nonreduced.

**Status.** No gap was found in either manuscript's lemma; the incidence arguments there were not needed and are not checked here. The [manuscripts](manuscripts-claiming-bogomolov-pop.md) are not peer reviewed, and an agent's check does not replace that review.
