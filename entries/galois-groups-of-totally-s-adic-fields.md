**Setting.** Let $K$ be a countable Hilbertian field, such as a number field, with separable closure $K_s$ and absolute Galois group $\mathrm{Gal}(K)$. A *local prime* of $K$ is an equivalence class $v$ of absolute values whose completion $\hat K_v$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, of $\mathbb{F}_p((t))$, or of $\mathbb{R}$. For each $v$ fix a *$v$-closure* $K_v = K_s \cap \hat K_v$, a Henselian closure if $v$ is nonarchimedean and a real closure if $v$ is real [@haranjardenpop2012, Introduction, p. 1]. For a finite set $S$ of local primes, the field of *totally $S$-adic numbers* is

$$K_{\mathrm{tot},S} = \bigcap_{v \in S} \bigcap_{\rho \in \mathrm{Gal}(K)} K_v^\rho.$$

It is the largest Galois extension of $K$ contained in every $K_v$, $v \in S$, since a Galois extension of $K$ inside $K_v$ lies in every conjugate $K_v^\rho$. Complex places can be dropped from $S$ without changing it, so assume $S$ has none [@haranjardenpop2012, §3, p. 12]. For $K = \mathbb{Q}$ and $S$ the real place, it is the field of totally real algebraic numbers. For $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = (\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_e) \in \mathrm{Gal}(K)^e$, let $K_s(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ be the fixed field of $\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_e$ in $K_s$, let $K_s[\boldsymbol{\sigma}]$ be the largest Galois extension of $K$ inside it, put $K_{\mathrm{tot},S}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) = K_s(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) \cap K_{\mathrm{tot},S}$, and let $K_{\mathrm{tot},S}[\boldsymbol{\sigma}]$ be the largest Galois extension of $K$ inside $K_{\mathrm{tot},S}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ [@haranjardenpop2012, Abstract]. "Almost all" refers to the Haar measure on $\mathrm{Gal}(K)^e$. Write $\hat F_e$ for the free profinite group of rank $e$, and $\hat F_\omega$ for that of countable rank.

**Free products over a profinite space.** A family $(G_t)_{t \in T}$ of closed subgroups of a profinite group $G$, indexed by a profinite space $T$, is an *inner free product*, written $G = \coprod^*_{t \in T} G_t$, if $t \mapsto G_t$ is étale continuous, $G_s \cap G_t = 1$ for $s \ne t$, and every continuous map from $\bigcup_t G_t$ to a profinite group that is a homomorphism on each $G_t$ extends uniquely to a homomorphism on $G$ [@haranjardenpop2012, Definition 1.2, p. 6]. Equivalently, $G$ is the free product of a sheaf of profinite groups over $T$, which is unique up to isomorphism; this is due to Melnikov and Haran [@haranjardenpop2012, Definition 1.2, p. 6]. Given profinite groups $G_v$ and profinite spaces $T_v$ for $v$ in a finite set, the free product of the *semi-constant sheaf* $\bigsqcup_v G_v \times T_v$ over $\bigsqcup_v T_v$ is determined up to isomorphism by these data [@haranjardenpop2012, Construction 1.3, p. 7]. A *Cantor space* is a profinite space with a countable basis and no isolated points. All of them are homeomorphic to the Cantor middle third set, and a disjoint union of finitely many of them is again one [@haranjardenpop2012, Definition 1.4, p. 7].

**Theorem.**

1. *(Pop.)* For each $v \in S$ there is a closed subset $R_v \subseteq \mathrm{Gal}(K)$ such that
$$\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S}) = \coprod^*_{v \in S} \coprod^*_{\rho \in R_v} \mathrm{Gal}(K_v^\rho)$$
[@haranjardenpop2012, Introduction, p. 1]. Fried, Haran and Völklein had proved the case where $S$ consists of archimedean places [@haranjardenpop2012, Introduction, p. 1]. Haran, Jarden and Pop report Pop's theorem for global fields [@haranjardenpop2009, Introduction, pp. 91–92], with a unified proof in all characteristics [@haranjardenpop2009, Introduction, p. 95].
2. *(Haran–Jarden–Pop, characteristic $0$.)* Let $\mathrm{char}\, K = 0$, let $e \ge 0$, and let the primes in $S$ be independent. Then for almost all $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in \mathrm{Gal}(K)^e$ there are closed subsets $R_v \subseteq \mathrm{Gal}(K)$, $v \in S$, with
$$\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})) = \mathrm{Gal}(K_s(\boldsymbol{\sigma})) * \coprod^*_{v \in S} \coprod^*_{\rho \in R_v} \mathrm{Gal}(K_v^\rho).$$
Here $R_v$ is a system of representatives of $\mathrm{Gal}(K)/\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}))$, and $\{K_v^\rho : \rho \in R_v\}$ is a closed system of representatives for the $\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}))$-orbits of the conjugates of $K_v$ [@haranjardenpop2009, Theorem 12.4, p. 145]. For almost all $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, also $\mathrm{Gal}(K_s(\boldsymbol{\sigma})) \cong \hat F_e$, by Jarden's free generators theorem [@haranjardenpop2009, Introduction, pp. 91–92].
3. *(Haran–Jarden–Pop, every characteristic.)* Let $e \ge 1$. For almost all $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in \mathrm{Gal}(K)^e$ there are closed $R_v \subseteq \mathrm{Gal}(K)$ with
$$\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})) \cong \hat F_e * \coprod^*_{v \in S} \coprod^*_{\rho \in R_v} \mathrm{Gal}(K_v^\rho), \qquad \mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S}[\boldsymbol{\sigma}]) \cong \hat F_\omega * \coprod^*_{v \in S} \coprod^*_{\rho \in R_v} \mathrm{Gal}(K_v^\rho).$$
The second factor in both is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S})$. It depends only on $K$ and $S$, not on the choice of the fields $K_v$ or on $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, and it is built from the groups $\mathrm{Gal}(K_v)$ in purely group-theoretic terms [@haranjardenpop2012, Main Theorem, p. 2]. The isomorphisms do not map the normal subgroup $\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S})$ onto that factor [@haranjardenpop2012, p. 2].

Part 3 gives the isomorphism type of $\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S}[\boldsymbol{\sigma}])$. The 2009 paper had left this case open, asking for an inner free product with $\mathrm{Gal}(K_s[\boldsymbol{\sigma}]) \cong \hat F_\omega$ as the first factor [@haranjardenpop2009, Remark 12.5, pp. 145–146]. The two extremes were known before. Without $S$, $\mathrm{Gal}(K_s(\boldsymbol{\sigma})) \cong \hat F_e$ and $\mathrm{Gal}(K_s[\boldsymbol{\sigma}]) \cong \hat F_\omega$ for almost all $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, by Jarden [@haranjardenpop2012, Introduction, p. 1]. Without $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ there is Pop's theorem. In between, for $(\boldsymbol{\sigma}, \boldsymbol{\rho}) \in \mathrm{Gal}(K)^{e + |S|}$ chosen at random, the absolute Galois group of $K_s(\boldsymbol{\sigma}) \cap \bigcap_{v \in S} K_v^{\rho_v}$ is $\hat F_e * \ast_{v \in S} \mathrm{Gal}(K_v^{\rho_v})$, a free product of finitely many groups, by Geyer for $e = 0$ and by Jarden in general [@haranjardenpop2009, Introduction, p. 92].

**Method.** In characteristic $0$, the field $M = K_{\mathrm{tot},S}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ is, for almost all $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, *pseudo-$S$-closed*: every absolutely irreducible variety over $M$ with a simple $K_v^\rho$-rational point for all $v$ and $\rho$ has an $M$-rational point. So every finite embedding problem for $\mathrm{Gal}(M)$ that is weakly solvable on each local group has a global weak solution [@haranjardenpop2009, Introduction, p. 93]. The proof also uses three properties of the local groups [@haranjardenpop2009, Introduction, p. 94]:

- They can be recognized group-theoretically, up to conjugation and inclusion, through "big quotients" in every free product $\hat F * \ast_{i=1}^n G_i$ with $\hat F$ finitely generated free and each $G_i$ isomorphic to some $\mathrm{Gal}(K_v)$. Big quotients are defined in [@haranjardenpop2009, Data 7.1, pp. 118–119].
- If $K_v$ is algebraically closed in a field $F$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(F) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(K_v)$, then $K_v$ is an elementary subfield of $F$. The authors attribute this to a combination of results of Efrat, Koenigsmann and Pop with the Ax–Kochen–Ershov and Prestel–Roquette theory. Compare the [Galois characterization of $p$-adically closed fields](galois-characterization-of-p-adically-closed-fields.md).
- For each $v \in S$, the space of $\mathrm{Gal}(M)$-orbits of the conjugates of $\mathrm{Gal}(K_v)$ is homeomorphic to the Cantor middle third set.

The proof shows that the group with its families of local subgroups is a *Cantor group pile* [@haranjardenpop2009, Definition 6.1, pp. 114–115, and proof of Proposition 11.2, pp. 142–143]. An analogue of Iwasawa's characterization of $\hat F_\omega$ by embedding problems then shows that any two Cantor group piles over the same groups are isomorphic [@haranjardenpop2009, Proposition 6.3, p. 115]. The 2012 paper uses another route, valid in every characteristic: a theorem of Pop on Hilbertian ample fields inside $K_{\mathrm{tot},S}$, and a lemma on preimages of subgroups in free products [@haranjardenpop2012, Introduction, pp. 2–3].

**Corollary: only the local groups matter.** This is proved here from the results above. Let $K, K'$ be countable Hilbertian fields of characteristic $0$, let $S, S'$ be finite sets of independent local primes of $K, K'$, none complex, and let $e \ge 0$. Suppose that $\{\mathrm{Gal}(K_v) : v \in S\}$ and $\{\mathrm{Gal}(K'_{v'}) : v' \in S'\}$ represent the same set $\mathcal{C}$ of isomorphism classes of profinite groups. Then
$$\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(K'_{\mathrm{tot},S'}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}'))$$
for almost all $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in \mathrm{Gal}(K)^e$ and almost all $\boldsymbol{\sigma}' \in \mathrm{Gal}(K')^e$. For $e = 0$ this says $\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},S}) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(K'_{\mathrm{tot},S'})$. The sets need not have the same size.

*Proof.* Put $M = K_{\mathrm{tot},S}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ and $K_0 = K_s(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$, and index $K_0$ by a new symbol $0$.

1. For almost all $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, the fields $K_0$ and $M$ satisfy the hypotheses of Haran, Jarden and Pop's §10, and $\mathrm{Gal}(K_0) \cong \hat F_e$ [@haranjardenpop2009, Proposition 12.3 and its proof, pp. 144–145]. Under these hypotheses they show that $\mathrm{Gal}(M)$, with its families of local subgroups, is a Cantor group pile over the groups $\mathrm{Gal}(K_0)$ and $\mathrm{Gal}(K_v)$, $v \in S$ [@haranjardenpop2009, proof of Proposition 11.2, p. 142]. So $\mathrm{Gal}(M)$ is isomorphic to the free product of the semi-constant sheaf $X = (\mathrm{Gal}(K_0) \times \{0\}) \sqcup \bigsqcup_{v \in S} (\mathrm{Gal}(K_v) \times T_v)$, where each $T_v$ is a copy of the Cantor space [@haranjardenpop2009, Data 5.1, p. 108, and Proposition 6.4 (a), p. 118].
2. For each class $c \in \mathcal{C}$ fix a group $\Gamma_c$ in $c$ and isomorphisms $\phi_v : \mathrm{Gal}(K_v) \to \Gamma_c$ for the $v \in S$ in class $c$, and an isomorphism $\phi_0 : \mathrm{Gal}(K_0) \to \hat F_e$. Let $T_c$ be the disjoint union of the $T_v$ over the $v$ in class $c$, which is a Cantor space [@haranjardenpop2012, Definition 1.4, p. 7]. Put $X_{\mathcal{C}} = (\hat F_e \times \{0\}) \sqcup \bigsqcup_{c \in \mathcal{C}} (\Gamma_c \times T_c)$, a semi-constant sheaf over the same base $\{0\} \sqcup \bigsqcup_v T_v$. The map $\Psi : X \to X_{\mathcal{C}}$ with $\Psi(g, t) = (\phi_v(g), t)$ for $t \in T_v$, and $\Psi(g, 0) = (\phi_0(g), 0)$, is a homeomorphism, since it is one on each of the finitely many open-closed pieces $\mathrm{Gal}(K_v) \times T_v$ and these pieces go to a partition of $X_{\mathcal{C}}$ into open-closed pieces. It commutes with the projections to the base and is a group isomorphism on each fibre.
3. Let $(G, \omega)$ and $(G_{\mathcal{C}}, \omega_{\mathcal{C}})$ be the free products of $X$ and $X_{\mathcal{C}}$. Then $\omega_{\mathcal{C}} \circ \Psi$ is continuous and a homomorphism on each fibre of $X$, so it equals $\varphi \circ \omega$ for a unique homomorphism $\varphi : G \to G_{\mathcal{C}}$. Likewise $\omega \circ \Psi^{-1} = \varphi' \circ \omega_{\mathcal{C}}$ for a unique $\varphi'$. By the uniqueness in the universal property [@haranjardenpop2012, Definition 1.2, p. 6], $\varphi'\varphi$ and $\varphi\varphi'$ are the identities. So $\mathrm{Gal}(M) \cong G_{\mathcal{C}}$.
4. Up to isomorphism, $X_{\mathcal{C}}$ depends only on $e$ and $\mathcal{C}$, because all Cantor spaces are homeomorphic. The same steps for $K'$ and $\boldsymbol{\sigma}'$ give a sheaf isomorphic to $X_{\mathcal{C}}$, so $\mathrm{Gal}(K'_{\mathrm{tot},S'}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}')) \cong G_{\mathcal{C}}$ as well. $\square$

This makes explicit, in characteristic $0$, the remark of Haran, Jarden and Pop that the local factor is built from the groups $\mathrm{Gal}(K_v)$ in purely group-theoretic terms. For a real place, $\mathrm{Gal}(K_v)$ has order $2$. For $v$ over an odd prime $p$, $\mathrm{Gal}(K_v)$ is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Gal}(\hat K_v)$, by restriction, since $K_v$ is Henselian; this standard fact is not checked in a source here. So, by part 2 of the [classification of $p$-adic fields with isomorphic absolute Galois groups](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), its class is determined by $[\hat K_v : \mathbb{Q}_p]$ and the maximal subfield of $\hat K_v$ abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

**Example: non-isomorphic fields with isomorphic Galois groups.** This is checked here. Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\beta)$ with $\beta^3 = 2$.

- Modulo $5$, $x^3 - 2 = (x - 3)(x^2 + 3x + 4)$, and $x^2 + 3x + 4$ takes the values $4, 3, 4, 2, 2$ at $x = 0, \dots, 4$, so it has no root. The two factors are coprime modulo $5$, so by Hensel's lemma $x^3 - 2 = (x - a)g(x)$ over $\mathbb{Z}_5$ with $a \equiv 3 \bmod 5$ and $g$ monic quadratic, reducing to $x^2 + 3x + 4$. A root of $g$ in $\mathbb{Q}_5$ would lie in $\mathbb{Z}_5$ and reduce to a root modulo $5$, so $g$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_5$. So $x^3 - 2$ does not split into linear factors over $\mathbb{Q}_5$, and the factor $x - a$ gives a prime $v_1$ of $K$ over $5$ with $\hat K_{v_1} = \mathbb{Q}_5$.
- Embedding $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ into an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_5$ compatibly with $v_1$, the $v_1$-closure of $K$ and the $5$-closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ are both $\bar{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \mathbb{Q}_5$. By the corollary with $S = \{v_1\}$ and $S' = \{5\}$, $\mathrm{Gal}(K_{\mathrm{tot},\{v_1\}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{tot},\{5\}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}'))$ for every $e \ge 0$ and almost all $\boldsymbol{\sigma}, \boldsymbol{\sigma}'$.
- These fields are not isomorphic. The field $\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{tot},\{5\}}$ of totally $5$-adic numbers is mapped to itself by $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q})$. If it contained a cube root of $2$, it would contain all three, since $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q})$ permutes them transitively, and all three would lie in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \mathbb{Q}_5$, which is impossible by the first point. So $\mathbb{Q}_{\mathrm{tot},\{5\}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}')$ contains no cube root of $2$, while $K_{\mathrm{tot},\{v_1\}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ contains $K$, and a field isomorphism maps cube roots of $2$ to cube roots of $2$.

For $e = 0$, the totally $5$-adic numbers form a proper subfield of $K_{\mathrm{tot},\{v_1\}}$ with an isomorphic absolute Galois group: both fields are intersections of conjugates of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}} \cap \mathbb{Q}_5$, under $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{Q})$ and under its subgroup $\mathrm{Gal}(K)$ respectively. By contrast, a number field is determined by its absolute Galois group, by the [Neukirch–Uchida theorem](neukirch-uchida-theorem.md).
