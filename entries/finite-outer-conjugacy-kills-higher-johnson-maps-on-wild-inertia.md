**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the degree-one Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-on-wild-inertia.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$ with augmentation $\epsilon$, $P_j$ is the closed lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$, $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$, and
$$V=H/pH=L\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d,\qquad L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0,\quad V_k=\Lambda\bar x_k\cong\Lambda\ (k\ge1),$$
with coordinates $\pi_k:V\to\Lambda$. The surface pairs are $(a_i,b_i)$ for $1\le i\le r=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor$, with $V_{(i)}=V_{a_i}\oplus V_{b_i}$ and $S=\bigoplus_iV_{(i)}$; the first pair $(a_1,b_1)$ is $(x_2,x_3)$ for odd $d$ and $(x_3,x_4)$ for even $d$. The complementary part is $C_V=V_1$ for odd $d$ and $V_1\oplus V_2$ for even $d$, $A=L\oplus C_V$, $V=A\oplus S$, and $\pi_A:V\to A$ is the projection along $S$. Also $\omega$, $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, $\xi_k\in H$, the element $\gamma\in\mathcal G$ with $\omega(\gamma)=1$ generating a closed subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$, and $\lambda_0=\gamma-1$ are as there. Write $\bar a_i,\bar b_i$ for $\bar x_{a_i},\bar x_{b_i}$, and let $e=[K(\mu_p):K]$, the order of $\omega$. An automorphism is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$; it then acts $\Lambda$-linearly on $V$. Write $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$.

*Lower central input.* By [the boundary-twist theorem](boundary-twists-exclude-finite-outer-conjugacy-of-wild-scalars.md), for every $j$ the group $\mathcal L_j$ is a torsion-free compact $\mathbb Z_p$-module, topologically generated by $j$-fold brackets of elements of $H$, and $\mathcal L_j/p$ is the completed degree-$j$ free Lie space $\mathcal L_j(V)$ inside the completed tensor space $V^{\hat\otimes j}$, with $[u,v]=uv-vu$. An automorphism acting on $V$ by $X$ acts on $\mathcal L_j(V)$ by the restriction $\mathcal L_j(X)$ of $X^{\otimes j}$. The bracket map $H\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ is injective. A tensor in $V^{\hat\otimes j}$ has a *component* in $W_1\hat\otimes\cdots\hat\otimes W_j$ for each choice of summands $W_t\in\{L,V_1,\dots,V_d\}$; if $m$ of the $W_t$ are not $L$, the component has a coordinate in $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^m]]=\Lambda^{\hat\otimes m}$. For a sum $W$ of some of these summands, the *slots in $W$* of a component are the $t$ with $W_t\subseteq W$.

*Multigrading.* (M1) If $W$ is a sum of some of the summands, then $\mathcal L_j(V)\cap W^{\hat\otimes j}=\mathcal L_j(W)$. Indeed, $\mathcal L_j(W)\subseteq\mathcal L_j(V)$, and the projection $\pi_W$ along the other summands induces $\mathcal L_j(\pi_W)$, which maps $\mathcal L_j(V)$ into $\mathcal L_j(W)$ and is the identity on $W^{\hat\otimes j}$. (M2) Let $V=U_1\oplus\cdots\oplus U_q$ with each $U_s$ a sum of summands. The projection $\Pi_{\mathbf m}$ of $V^{\hat\otimes j}$ onto the components with exactly $m_s$ slots in each $U_s$ maps $\mathcal L_j(V)$ into itself. Indeed, for $u\in\mathcal L_{j-1}(V)$ and $v=\sum_sv_s$ with $v_s\in U_s$, $\Pi_{\mathbf m}[u,v]=\sum_s[\Pi_{\mathbf m-\mathbf e_s}u,v_s]$; induct on $j$ and use that $\Pi_{\mathbf m}$ is continuous and $\mathcal L_j(V)$ is the closed span of such $[u,v]$.

*Johnson maps of degree $n-1$.* Let $n\ge2$. For $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with $\theta(x)x^{-1}\in P_n$ for all $x\in P$, Lemma 1 (a) of the degree-one theorem, with $k=n-1$, gives continuous homomorphisms $\partial^\theta_m:\mathcal L_m\to\mathcal L_{m+n-1}$, $zP_{m+1}\mapsto\theta(z)z^{-1}P_{m+n}$, satisfying the bracket recursion there; put $\mathcal J_n(\theta)=\partial^\theta_1:H\to\mathcal L_n$. For two such $\theta,\theta'$, $\theta\theta'(x)x^{-1}=\theta(\theta'(x)x^{-1})\cdot\theta(x)x^{-1}$ and $\theta(z)\in zP_{2n-1}$ for $z\in P_n$, so $\mathcal J_n(\theta\theta')=\mathcal J_n(\theta)+\mathcal J_n(\theta')$. For every automorphism $\beta$, $\beta\theta\beta^{-1}(x)x^{-1}=\beta(\theta(y)y^{-1})$ with $y=\beta^{-1}(x)$, so $\mathcal J_n(\beta\theta\beta^{-1})=\beta_n\mathcal J_n(\theta)\beta_H^{-1}$, where $\beta_j$ is the action of $\beta$ on $\mathcal L_j$. For $g\in P_n$, $\mathcal J_n(c_g)=0$, as $[g,x]\in P_{n+1}$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$, $e=[K(\mu_p):K]$, and $n\ge2$ with $e\nmid n-1$. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ and induce the identity on $G/P_n$. Then $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$ for every $x\in P$, that is, $\mathcal J_n(\alpha)=0$.

For $n=2$ the condition $e\nmid1$ says $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, and the theorem is [the degree-one Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-on-wild-inertia.md). In general $e\nmid n-1$ forces $e\ge2$, so $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$ and $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$. Every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha_2$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_2$ that induces the identity on $G/[P,[P,P]]$ and on $P/P_4$. Two such $\alpha_2$ differ by $c_k$ with $k\in[P,[P,P]]$.

**Lemma 1 (invariants; proved here).** Let $\gamma_1\in\Gamma$ generate an infinite pro-$p$ closed subgroup, $m\ge1$, and $Y$ a compact $\mathbb F_p$-vector space.

- (a) If $z\in\Lambda^{\hat\otimes m}\hat\otimes Y$ is killed by right multiplication by $\gamma_1-1$ in one coordinate, then $z=0$.
- (b) Let $Q\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}$ be nonempty. If $z\in\Lambda^{\hat\otimes m}\hat\otimes Y$ is fixed by right multiplication by $\gamma_1$ in every coordinate in $Q$, then $z=0$.
- (c) For $j\ge2$, $\mathcal L_j(V)^\gamma=0$ and $\mathcal L_j^\Gamma=0$. For $k\ge1$, the fixed points of $\Gamma$ on $A^{\hat\otimes k}$, with the diagonal action, are $0$ if $\omega^k\ne1$ and $\mathbb F_p\bar x_0^{\otimes k}$ if $\omega^k=1$. If $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, then $H^\Gamma=0$.

*Proof.* (a) This is the argument of Lemma 3 (a) of the degree-one theorem, which uses about $\gamma$ only Lemma 2 of [the central-element construction](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md), and that lemma applies to $\gamma_1$: for each continuous functional $f$ on the other factors, $(\mathrm{id}\otimes f)(z)\in\Lambda$ is killed by right multiplication by $\gamma_1-1$, so it is $0$, and at each finite level a tensor killed by all such functionals is $0$.

(b) Choose $q_0\in Q$. The map $h$ of $\Gamma^m$ with $h(g)_q=g_qg_{q_0}^{-1}$ for $q\in Q\setminus\{q_0\}$ and $h(g)_q=g_q$ otherwise is a homeomorphism, and it descends to $(\Gamma/U)^m$ for every open normal $U$, so it induces a continuous linear automorphism $h_*$ of $\Lambda^{\hat\otimes m}$. If $g'$ is $g$ with $g_q$ replaced by $g_q\gamma_1$ for $q\in Q$, then $h(g')$ is $h(g)$ with only the coordinate $q_0$ multiplied on the right by $\gamma_1$. So $(h_*\otimes\mathrm{id}_Y)z$ is fixed by right multiplication by $\gamma_1$ in the coordinate $q_0$, hence killed by right multiplication by $\gamma_1-1$ there, and it is $0$ by (a).

(c) The proof of Lemma 1 (c) of [the theorem modulo the third lower central term](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-the-third-lower-central-term-of-wild-inertia.md) uses about $j$ only that $j\ge2$: a component with $m\ge1$ coordinates in $\Lambda$ has no nonzero point fixed by the left diagonal multiplication by $\gamma$, as $\omega(\gamma)=1$, and the $L^{\otimes j}$-component of a Lie element of degree $j\ge2$ is $0$. So $\mathcal L_j(V)^\gamma=0$, and Lemma 1 (b) there, for the torsion-free compact group $M=\mathcal L_j$, gives $\mathcal L_j^\Gamma=0$. For $A^{\hat\otimes k}$, the same argument kills the fixed points of $\gamma$ in every component with at least one slot in $C_V$, and $\rho$ acts on $L^{\otimes k}$ by $\omega(\rho)^k$. The last assertion is Lemma 3 (c) of the degree-one theorem. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (one generator; proved here).** Let $m\ge2$, $\bar x\in V$, and $\chi:V\to\mathbb F_p$ continuous and linear with $\chi(\bar x)=1$.

- (a) If $\bar y\in\mathcal L_m(V)$ and $[\bar y,\bar x]=0$, then $\bar y=0$.
- (b) If $y\in\mathcal L_m$ and $[y,H]\subseteq p^k\mathcal L_{m+1}$, then $y\in p^k\mathcal L_m$.
- (c) If $g\in P$, $m\ge1$ and $[g,P]\subseteq P_{m+1}$, then $g\in P_m$.

*Proof.* (a) First, if $y\in V^{\hat\otimes l}$ with $l\ge1$ and $y\bar x=\bar xy$, then $y=c\,\bar x^{\otimes l}$ for some $c\in\mathbb F_p$. For $l=1$ apply $\mathrm{id}\otimes\chi$. For $l\ge2$, applying $\mathrm{id}^{\otimes l}\otimes\chi$ gives $y=\bar x\,y_1$ with $y_1=(\mathrm{id}^{\otimes l-1}\otimes\chi)(y)$. Then $\bar xy_1\bar x=\bar x\bar xy_1$, and applying $\chi\otimes\mathrm{id}^{\otimes l}$ gives $y_1\bar x=\bar xy_1$; induct. So $\bar y=c\,\bar x^{\otimes m}$. The map $\chi^{\otimes m}$ kills every $[u,v]$ with $u\in V^{\hat\otimes a}$, $v\in V^{\hat\otimes b}$, as $\chi^{\otimes(a+b)}(uv)=\chi^{\otimes a}(u)\chi^{\otimes b}(v)$, hence it kills $\mathcal L_m(V)$. So $c=\chi^{\otimes m}(\bar y)=0$.

(b) Modulo $p$, $[\bar y,\bar x_1]=0$, so $\bar y=0$ by (a) with $\chi=\epsilon\circ\pi_1$. So $y=py'$, and $[y',H]\subseteq p^{k-1}\mathcal L_{m+1}$ as $\mathcal L_{m+1}$ is torsion-free. Induct on $k$.

(c) If $g\in P_j$ with $j<m$, then $[gP_{j+1},H]=0$ in $\mathcal L_{j+1}$. For $j=1$ this gives $g\in P_2$ by injectivity of the bracket map on $H$; for $j\ge2$ it gives $gP_{j+1}\in\bigcap_kp^k\mathcal L_j=0$ by (b). Induct on $j$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (Lie elements of degree one in a letter; proved here).** Let $E=\Lambda\tilde e\oplus A$ with $\tilde e$ a free generator. Call $P\in\mathcal L_m(E)$, $m\ge2$, *linear* if each of its components has exactly one slot in $\Lambda\tilde e$. If $P$ is linear and its component in $\Lambda\tilde e\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes m-1}$ is $0$, then $P=0$. In particular, the left-normed bracket $[\cdots[[\tilde e,\bar x_0],\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]$ with $m-1$ copies of $\bar x_0$ is the only linear element whose component in $\Lambda\tilde e\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes m-1}$ is $\tilde e\otimes\bar x_0^{\otimes m-1}$.

*Proof.* Open subspaces $U=U_1\oplus U_2$ with $U_1\subseteq\Lambda\tilde e$ and $U_2\subseteq A$ are cofinal, and the image of $P$ in $(E/U)^{\otimes m}$ is a Lie element of the free Lie algebra over $\mathbb F_p$ on the finite-dimensional space $X\oplus Y$, $X=\Lambda\tilde e/U_1$, $Y=A/U_2$, linear in $X$, with zero component in $X\otimes Y^{\otimes m-1}$. It suffices to show that such an element $P$ is $0$. Let $\Delta$ be the algebra homomorphism $T(X\oplus Y)\to T(X\oplus Y)\otimes T(X\oplus Y)$ with $\Delta(w)=w\otimes1+1\otimes w$ for $w\in X\oplus Y$. Brackets of primitive elements are primitive, so $\Delta(P)=P\otimes1+1\otimes P$. On $w_1\otimes\cdots\otimes w_m$, $\Delta$ is $\sum_{S}w_S\otimes w_{S^c}$ over subsets $S\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}$, with $w_S$ the ordered tensor of the $w_t$, $t\in S$. Write $P=\sum_{i=1}^mP_i$ with $P_i\in Y^{\otimes i-1}\otimes X\otimes Y^{\otimes m-i}$, so $P_1=0$. For $2\le j\le m$, take the part of $\Delta(P)$ in $Y^{\otimes j-1}\otimes(X\otimes Y^{\otimes m-j})$, which is $0$. A pair $(P_i,S)$ contributes only if $i\notin S$, $|S|=j-1$ and $i$ is the least element of $S^c$, that is, $\{1,\dots,i-1\}\subseteq S$. So $i\le j$; for $i=j$, $S=\{1,\dots,j-1\}$ and the contribution is $P_j$ itself; the other contributions are images of $P_i$ with $i<j$. By induction on $j$, every $P_j$ is $0$. For the last assertion, each step $z\mapsto[z,\bar x_0]=z\bar x_0-\bar x_0z$ keeps the part with $\tilde e$ first equal to that part of $z$ followed by $\bar x_0$. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (a commutator; proved here).** Let $n\ge2$, let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ satisfy $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in P_n$ for all $x\in P$, let $T\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ be trivial on $P/P_3$, and $N\ge1$. Then $\kappa=T^N\alpha T^{-N}\alpha^{-1}$ satisfies $\kappa(y)y^{-1}\in P_{n+2}$ for all $y\in P$, with class $N\big(\partial^T_n\partial^\alpha_1-\partial^\alpha_3\partial^T_1\big)(yP_2)$ in $\mathcal L_{n+2}$.

*Proof.* Let $x\in P$, and write $\alpha(x)=jx$ and $T^N(x)=fx$ with $j\in P_n$, $f\in P_3$. Put $y=\alpha T^N(x)=\alpha(f)jx$, so $yP_2=xP_2$ and $\kappa(y)=T^N\alpha(x)=T^N(j)fx$. Then $\kappa(y)y^{-1}=T^N(j)\,f\,j^{-1}\alpha(f)^{-1}$. By Lemma 1 (a) of the degree-one theorem, $f'=T^N(j)j^{-1}\in P_{n+2}$ has class $\partial^{T^N}_n(jP_{n+1})=N\partial^T_n(jP_{n+1})$, the factor $N$ coming from additivity and the recursion as in the proof of Lemma 1 (b) there, and $j'=\alpha(f)f^{-1}\in P_{n+2}$ has class $\partial^\alpha_3(fP_4)$, with $fP_4=N\partial^T_1(xP_2)$. So $\kappa(y)y^{-1}=f'\,[j,f]\,j'^{-1}$ with $[j,f]\in P_{n+3}$, and $jP_{n+1}=\partial^\alpha_1(xP_2)$. $\square$

**Lemma 5 (the conditions; proved here).** Let $n\ge2$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Let $\mathcal A_n$ be the set of $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates that induce the identity on $G/P_n$, and $\mathcal S$ the set of continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant $J:H\to\mathcal L_n$ with $p^mJ=\mathcal J_n(\alpha)$ for some $m\ge0$ and $\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$. For such $J$, $m$ and $\alpha$, the recursion of Lemma 1 (a) of the degree-one theorem shows that $\partial^\alpha_3$ maps into $p^m\mathcal L_{n+2}$; put $J^{(3)}=p^{-m}\partial^\alpha_3$, which by the same recursion depends only on $J$.

- (o) Every $\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$ is tame-trivial and $\mathcal J_n(\alpha)$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant. If $c_g$ induces the identity on $G/P_n$, then $g\in P_n$.
- (i) $\theta_nJ\theta_H^{-1}\in\mathcal S$ for every tame-trivial $\theta$.
- (ii) For every tame-trivial $\beta$ there is $N\ge1$ with $\beta_n^NJ\beta_H^{-N}=J$.
- (iii) If $\psi$ is tame-trivial and $\psi_H-1$ is nilpotent, then $\psi_nJ\psi_H^{-1}=J$.
- (iv) Let $T=T^{(1)}_1$ be the boundary twist of the first pair and $D=\mathcal J_3(T)$. Then $\partial^T_nJ=J^{(3)}D$.

*Proof.* (o) $P_n\subseteq P$, so $\alpha$ is tame-trivial. For $\tilde\rho\in G$ over $\rho$, $\alpha(\tilde\rho)=j\tilde\rho$ with $j\in P_n$. For $x\in P$, $\alpha(\tilde\rho x\tilde\rho^{-1})(\tilde\rho x\tilde\rho^{-1})^{-1}=j\,u\,(wj^{-1}w^{-1})$ with $u=\tilde\rho\,\alpha(x)x^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}\in P_n$ and $w=\tilde\rho x\tilde\rho^{-1}$. Modulo $P_{n+1}$, $wj^{-1}w^{-1}\equiv j^{-1}$ and $juj^{-1}\equiv u$, so the class is $\rho\cdot\mathcal J_n(\alpha)(xP_2)$. If $c_g$ induces the identity on $G/P_n$, it is tame-trivial, so $gP\in Z(\Gamma)=1$ (part 3 of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). If $g\in P_j$ with $j<n$, then $g\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}\in P_n\subseteq P_{j+1}$, so $(1-\rho)(gP_{j+1})=0$ in $\mathcal L_j$ for all $\rho$, and $gP_{j+1}=0$ by Lemma 1 (c). Induct on $j$.

(i) $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}$ has finitely many outer conjugates and induces the identity on $G/P_n$, as $P_n$ is characteristic ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). Its $\mathcal J_n$ is $p^m\theta_nJ\theta_H^{-1}$, which is $\Gamma$-equivariant since $\theta_n,\theta_H$ commute with $\Gamma$.

(ii) By Lemma (b) of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), $\beta^N\alpha\beta^{-N}=c_g\alpha$ with $N\ge1$ and $g\in P$. Both sides induce the identity on $G/P_n$, hence so does $c_g$, so $g\in P_n$ by (o) and $\mathcal J_n(c_g)=0$. The rules give $p^m(\beta_n^NJ\beta_H^{-N}-J)=0$, and $\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_n)$ is torsion-free.

(iii) By (ii), $U^NJ=J$ for $U(J')=\psi_nJ'\psi_H^{-1}$. As in the proof of Lemma 4 (ii) of the degree-one theorem, $\psi_n-1$ is nilpotent on $\mathcal L_n$, since $\psi_n$ acts on $n$-fold brackets through $\psi_H$, so $U-1$ is nilpotent on the torsion-free group $\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_n)$, and the binomial argument there gives $UJ=J$.

(iv) By Lemma 2 (a) of [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md), $T$ is tame-trivial, fixes $\mathcal G$ pointwise and is trivial on $P/P_3$, so $D$ and every $\partial^T_l$ are $\Gamma$-equivariant. By Lemma (b) of abelianized wild inertia, $\kappa=T^N\alpha T^{-N}\alpha^{-1}=c_g$ with $N\ge1$ and $g\in P$. By Lemma 4, $[g,P]\subseteq P_{n+2}$, so $g\in P_{n+1}$ by Lemma 2 (c), and $[g,y]P_{n+3}=[\bar g,yP_2]$ with $\bar g=gP_{n+2}\in\mathcal L_{n+1}$. Lemma 4 gives $\operatorname{ad}(\bar g)=N(\partial^T_n\mathcal J_n(\alpha)-\partial^\alpha_3D)$ on $H$. The right side is $\Gamma$-equivariant, since $\partial^\alpha_1=\mathcal J_n(\alpha)$ is by (o) and the recursion carries this to $\partial^\alpha_3$. So $\operatorname{ad}(\rho\bar g-\bar g)=0$ for $\rho\in\Gamma$, hence $\rho\bar g=\bar g$ by Lemma 2 (b) for every $k$, and $\bar g\in\mathcal L_{n+1}^\Gamma=0$ by Lemma 1 (c). So $Np^m(\partial^T_nJ-J^{(3)}D)=0$, and torsion-freeness gives the claim. $\square$

*Reductions.* For $J\in\mathcal S$ let $\bar J:V\to\mathcal L_n(V)$ be its reduction, which is $\Lambda$-linear. By Lemma 1 (a) of the degree-one theorem, $\partial^T_n$ reduces to the map $\delta^{(n)}$ that applies the reduction $\delta$ of $D$ in one slot at a time, and $J^{(3)}$ to the map $\bar J^{(3)}$ that applies $\bar J$ in one slot at a time. So, for every $J\in\mathcal S$:

- (i') $\mathcal L_n(B)\bar JB^{-1}$ is the reduction of an element of $\mathcal S$, if $B$ is the action on $V$ of a tame-trivial automorphism;
- (ii') $\mathcal L_n(B^N)\bar J=\bar JB^N$ for some $N\ge1$, if $B$ is the action of a tame-trivial automorphism;
- (iii') $\mathcal L_n(\bar\Psi)\bar J=\bar J\bar\Psi$ for every $\bar\Psi$ in the group generated by the actions of automorphisms $\psi$ as in (iii);
- (iv') $\delta^{(n)}(\bar Jv)=\bar J^{(3)}(\delta v)$ for $v\in V$.

**Proof of the theorem.** Here $r\ge2$, as $d\ge5$, and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. We show that $\bar J=0$ for every $J\in\mathcal S$. Then $J=pJ'$ with $J'$ continuous, $\Gamma$-equivariant and in $\mathcal S$, as $\mathcal L_n$ is torsion-free and compact; so $J\in\bigcap_kp^k\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_n)=0$. Applied to $\mathcal J_n(\alpha)\in\mathcal S$, this proves the theorem. Fix $J\in\mathcal S$. By (i'), each step below also applies to $\mathcal L_n(B)\bar JB^{-1}$ for every tame-trivial automorphism with action $B$.

*1. Localization.* For $1\le i\le r$, the Setting of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md) gives $\varphi_i\in\operatorname{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ inducing on $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ the integral symplectic matrix that exchanges $\alpha_1,\beta_1$ with $\alpha_i,\beta_i$ (the identity for $i=1$). As in step 1 of the degree-one theorem, $\psi_i=\Psi(\varphi_i)$ is tame-trivial, fixes the complementary generators, and maps each surface generator to a word in the surface generators whose exponent sums are given by this matrix. So it acts on $V$ by fixing $A$, exchanging $\bar a_1,\bar b_1$ with $\bar a_i,\bar b_i$ and fixing the other surface generators. Let $\theta_\gamma$ be the automorphism of part 2 of the tame-group-algebra theorem for the unit $\gamma$; by part 3 there it acts on $V$ by $\mu\bar a_1\mapsto\mu\gamma\bar a_1$, $\mu\bar b_1\mapsto\mu(\gamma^*)^{-1}\bar b_1=\mu\gamma\bar b_1$, and as the identity on the other summands. So $\beta_i=\psi_i\theta_\gamma\psi_i^{-1}$ is tame-trivial, and its action $B_i$ on $V$ is right multiplication by $\gamma$ on the coordinates of $V_{a_i}$ and $V_{b_i}$, and the identity on the other summands. By (ii') there is $N\ge1$ with $\mathcal L_n(B_i^N)\bar J=\bar JB_i^N$. Put $\gamma_1=\gamma^N$; it generates an open subgroup of a closed subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$.

Let $\bar x_l$ be a generator outside the $i$-th pair. Then $B_i^N\bar x_l=\bar x_l$, so $\bar J(\bar x_l)=\mathcal L_n(B_i^N)\bar J(\bar x_l)$. Now $\mathcal L_n(B_i^N)$ preserves every component and acts on its coordinate by right multiplication by $\gamma_1$ in each slot in $V_{(i)}$. By Lemma 1 (b), every component of $\bar J(\bar x_l)$ with a slot in $V_{(i)}$ vanishes. Applying this for every $i$, and (M1),
$$\bar J(\bar x_c)\in\mathcal L_n(A)\ \ (c\text{ complementary}),\qquad\bar J(\bar x_k)\in\mathcal L_n(A\oplus V_{(i)})\ \ (k\in\{a_i,b_i\}).\tag{1}$$

*2. Kondo's automorphisms.* By step 1 of the degree-one theorem, every unipotent integral symplectic matrix $M$ of the surface lattice is the action on the surface classes of an automorphism $\psi$ as in (iii), which fixes $A$; so (iii') holds for the $\Lambda$-linear map $\bar M$ given by $M$ on the surface generators and the identity on $A$. We use $u_i:a_i\mapsto a_i+b_i$, $l_i:b_i\mapsto b_i+a_i$, $d_i=(u_il_i^{-1}u_i)^2$, which is $-1$ on $a_i,b_i$, and, for $i\ne i'$, $M_{ii'}:a_i\mapsto a_i+b_{i'}$, $a_{i'}\mapsto a_{i'}+b_i$ and $M'_{ii'}:b_i\mapsto b_i+a_{i'}$, $b_{i'}\mapsto b_{i'}+a_i$, all other basis vectors being fixed. The matrix $M'_{ii'}$ is symplectic, as $\langle b_i+a_{i'},b_{i'}+a_i\rangle=\langle b_i,a_i\rangle+\langle a_{i'},b_{i'}\rangle=0$ and the other pairings are unchanged, and $(M'_{ii'}-1)^2=0$.

(2a) $\mathcal L_n(\bar d_i)$ multiplies a component by $(-1)^t$, with $t$ its number of slots in $V_{(i)}$. As $\bar J(\bar d_i\bar a_i)=-\bar J(\bar a_i)$, every component of $\bar J(\bar a_i)$, and likewise of $\bar J(\bar b_i)$, has an odd number of slots in $V_{(i)}$.

(2b) Fix $i$ and $i'\ne i$. By (iii') for $M_{ii'}$, $\bar J(\bar a_i)+\bar J(\bar b_{i'})=\mathcal L_n(\bar M_{ii'})\bar J(\bar a_i)$. Take a component with exactly one slot in $V_{b_{i'}}$, at position $s$, and its other slots in summands of $A\oplus V_{(i)}$, at least one in $V_{(i)}$. On the left it is $0$, by (1). On the right, $\bar M_{ii'}$ fixes $A$ and $V_{b_i}$ and maps $\mu\bar a_i$ to $\mu\bar a_i+\mu\bar b_{i'}$, so this component has the same coordinate as the component of $\bar J(\bar a_i)$ with $V_{a_i}$ at position $s$ and the same other summands. Hence every component of $\bar J(\bar a_i)$ with a slot in $V_{a_i}$ and a further slot in $V_{(i)}$ vanishes. In the same way $M'_{ii'}$, evaluated at $\bar b_i$, shows that every component of $\bar J(\bar b_i)$ with a slot in $V_{b_i}$ and a further slot in $V_{(i)}$ vanishes. Let $Z_a$ and $Z_b$ be the sums of the components of $\bar J(\bar a_i)$ and $\bar J(\bar b_i)$ with at least two slots in $V_{(i)}$. Then all slots of $Z_a$ in $V_{(i)}$ lie in $V_{b_i}$, and all those of $Z_b$ lie in $V_{a_i}$. As $\bar u_i$ preserves the number of slots in $V_{(i)}$ and fixes $V_{b_i}$ and $A$, comparing these parts of $\bar J(\bar a_i)+\bar J(\bar b_i)=\mathcal L_n(\bar u_i)\bar J(\bar a_i)$ gives $Z_a+Z_b=\mathcal L_n(\bar u_i)Z_a=Z_a$, so $Z_b=0$. Likewise $\bar l_i$ gives $Z_a=0$. With (1) and (2a):
$$\text{every component of }\bar J(\bar x_k),\ k\in\{a_i,b_i\},\text{ has exactly one slot in }V_{(i)}\text{ and }n-1\text{ slots in }A.\tag{2}$$

*3. One element for all surface generators.* Let $E$ be as in Lemma 3, and for a surface index $k$ let $e_k:E\to V$ be the $\Lambda$-linear map with $\tilde e\mapsto\bar x_k$ that is the identity on $A$. It is an isomorphism onto $V_k\oplus A$, so $\mathcal L_n(e_k)$ is injective with image $\mathcal L_n(V_k\oplus A)$. By (2), (M1) and (M2), $\bar J(\bar a_i)=\mathcal L_n(e_{a_i})P^{aa}+\mathcal L_n(e_{b_i})P^{ab}$ and $\bar J(\bar b_i)=\mathcal L_n(e_{a_i})P^{ba}+\mathcal L_n(e_{b_i})P^{bb}$ for linear $P^{aa},P^{ab},P^{ba},P^{bb}\in\mathcal L_n(E)$. For linear $P$, $\mathcal L_n(\bar u_ie_{a_i})P=\mathcal L_n(e_{a_i})P+\mathcal L_n(e_{b_i})P$, since $\bar u_ie_{a_i}$ agrees with $e_{a_i}$ on $A$ and maps $\mu\tilde e$ to $\mu\bar a_i+\mu\bar b_i$, while $\bar u_ie_{b_i}=e_{b_i}$. So (iii') for $u_i$ at $\bar a_i$ reads
$$\mathcal L_n(e_{a_i})(P^{aa}+P^{ba})+\mathcal L_n(e_{b_i})(P^{ab}+P^{bb})=\mathcal L_n(e_{a_i})P^{aa}+\mathcal L_n(e_{b_i})(P^{aa}+P^{ab}).$$
The two images lie in different components, so $P^{ba}=0$ and $P^{bb}=P^{aa}$. Likewise $l_i$ gives $P^{ab}=0$. So $\bar J(\bar a_i)=\mathcal L_n(e_{a_i})P_i$ and $\bar J(\bar b_i)=\mathcal L_n(e_{b_i})P_i$ with $P_i=P^{aa}$. For $i'\ne i$, (iii') for $M_{ii'}$ at $\bar a_i$ gives $\mathcal L_n(e_{a_i})P_i+\mathcal L_n(e_{b_{i'}})P_{i'}=\mathcal L_n(e_{a_i})P_i+\mathcal L_n(e_{b_{i'}})P_i$, so $P_{i'}=P_i$. Hence there is one linear $P\in\mathcal L_n(E)$ with
$$\bar J(\bar x_k)=\mathcal L_n(e_k)P\qquad\text{for every surface index }k.\tag{3}$$
Let $\varphi\in\Lambda\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes n-1}$ be the coordinate of the component of $P$ in $\Lambda\tilde e\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes n-1}$. By Lemma 3, $\varphi$ determines $P$.

*4. The boundary twist.* Let $\bar a=\bar a_1$ and $\bar b=\bar b_1$. By step 1 of abelianized wild inertia, $\delta$ is $\Lambda$-linear, kills $L$ and every $V_l$ with $l\notin\{a_1,b_1\}$, and $\delta(\bar a)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]=2\bar a\bar b\bar a-\bar b\bar a\bar a-\bar a\bar a\bar b$, $\delta(\bar b)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]$. Let $\mathrm{pr}=\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{b_1}\otimes\pi_A^{\otimes n-1}$ on $V^{\hat\otimes n+2}$, with values in $\Lambda^{\hat\otimes3}\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes n-1}$, defined level by level. Apply it to (iv') at $v=\bar a$, where $\bar J(\bar a)=\mathcal L_n(e_{a_1})P$ by (3).

- Left side. $\delta^{(n)}$ replaces the slot of $\mathcal L_n(e_{a_1})P$ in $V_{a_1}$ by three slots in $V_{(1)}$ and keeps the others in $A$. A word seen by $\mathrm{pr}$ has its first three letters in $V_{a_1},V_{a_1},V_{b_1}$, so only the component with the $V_{a_1}$-slot first contributes, through the term $-\bar a\bar a\bar b$ of $\delta(\bar a)$. As $\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{b_1}$ maps $\mu\cdot\bar a\bar a\bar b$ to $\Delta_3(\mu)$, with $\Delta_3(\rho)=\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\rho$ (property (P) of abelianized wild inertia), the left side gives $-(\Delta_3\otimes\mathrm{id})(\varphi)$.
- Right side. $\bar J^{(3)}(\delta\bar a)$ is obtained from $2\bar a\bar b\bar a-\bar b\bar a\bar a-\bar a\bar a\bar b$ by replacing one letter by $\bar J$ of it, which by (3) has one letter in $V_{a_1}$ or $V_{b_1}$ and $n-1$ letters in $A$. A word seen by $\mathrm{pr}$ has exactly $n-1$ letters in $A$, in its last $n-1$ slots, and these come from the replacement, the only block with letters in $A$. So the replacement fills the last $n$ slots with its surface letter first, the replaced letter is the third, and the first two letters are $\bar a\bar a$. Only $-\bar a\bar a\,\bar J(\bar b)$ qualifies, through the component of $\mathcal L_n(e_{b_1})P$ in $V_{b_1}\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes n-1}$, which has coordinate $\varphi$. So the right side gives $-1\otimes1\otimes\varphi$.

Hence $(\Delta_3\otimes\mathrm{id})(\varphi)=1\otimes1\otimes\varphi$. Applying $\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}$, and $(\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\Delta_3=\mathrm{id}$,
$$\varphi=1\otimes c\qquad\text{with }c=(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id})(\varphi)\in A^{\hat\otimes n-1}.\tag{4}$$

*5. Units on the first pair.* Let $\rho\in\Gamma$, and let $\theta_\rho$ be the automorphism of part 2 of the tame-group-algebra theorem for the unit $\rho$. By part 3 there it acts on $V$ by $B_\rho$: $\mu\bar a_1\mapsto\mu\rho\bar a_1$, $\mu\bar b_1\mapsto\omega(\rho)^{-1}\mu\rho\bar b_1$, the identity on the other summands. By (i'), steps 1 to 3 apply to $\bar J'=\mathcal L_n(B_\rho)\bar JB_\rho^{-1}$, giving a linear $P'$ with $\bar J'(\bar x_k)=\mathcal L_n(e_k)P'$ for every surface index $k$.

- At $\bar a_2$: $B_\rho$ fixes $\bar a_2$, and $B_\rho e_{a_2}=e_{a_2}$, so $\bar J'(\bar a_2)=\mathcal L_n(e_{a_2})P$ and $P'=P$.
- At $\bar a_1$: $B_\rho^{-1}\bar a_1=\rho^{-1}\bar a_1$ and $B_\rho e_{a_1}=e_{a_1}\tilde R_\rho$, where $\tilde R_\rho$ is the $\Lambda$-linear map of $E$ with $\mu\tilde e\mapsto\mu\rho\tilde e$ that fixes $A$. As $B_\rho$ and $e_{a_1}$ are $\Lambda$-linear, $\bar J'(\bar a_1)=\mathcal L_n(e_{a_1})\big(\rho^{-1}\cdot\mathcal L_n(\tilde R_\rho)P\big)$.

So $\mathcal L_n(\tilde R_\rho)P=\rho\cdot P$. On the component in $\Lambda\tilde e\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes n-1}$, $\mathcal L_n(\tilde R_\rho)$ is right multiplication by $\rho$ on $\Lambda$, and $\rho$ acts by left multiplication on $\Lambda$ and diagonally on $A^{\hat\otimes n-1}$. By (4), $\rho\otimes c=\rho\otimes\rho c$, and applying $\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}$ gives $\rho c=c$. So $c$ is fixed by $\Gamma$. As $e\nmid n-1$, $\omega^{n-1}\ne1$, so $c=0$ by Lemma 1 (c). Then $\varphi=0$, so $P=0$ by Lemma 3, and by (3)
$$\bar J(S)=0.\tag{5}$$

*6. The complementary generators.* For complementary $c'$ put $Q_{c'}=\bar J(\bar x_{c'})\in\mathcal L_n(A)$, by (1). *Claim:* let $l$ be a surface index and $B$ a $\Lambda$-linear automorphism of $V$ that fixes $L$ and every summand of $C_V$ other than $V_1$ pointwise, with $B(\mu\bar x_1)=\mu\bar x_1+\mu\lambda_0\bar x_l$. If $Q\in\mathcal L_n(A)$ and $\mathcal L_n(B)Q\in A^{\hat\otimes n}$, then no component of $Q$ has a slot in $V_1$. Indeed, $B$ maps every summand of $A$ into itself except $V_1$, which it maps into $V_1\oplus V_l$. So for a component of $Q$ with $V_1$ at position $s$ and summands of $A$ elsewhere, the component of $\mathcal L_n(B)Q$ with $V_l$ at position $s$ and the same summands elsewhere receives only this contribution, and its coordinate is obtained by right multiplication by $\lambda_0$ in slot $s$. It vanishes, so the component of $Q$ vanishes by Lemma 1 (a).

- *Even $d$.* Part 2 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), with $\lambda=\lambda_0\in\ker\omega$, gives a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $B$ on $V$ fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,4$, with $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda_0\bar x_3$ and $B\bar x_4=\bar x_4-\lambda_0^*\bar x_2$. Then $B^{-1}\bar x_{c'}-\bar x_{c'}\in S$ for $c'=0,1,2$, so by (5), $\bar J'=\mathcal L_n(B)\bar JB^{-1}$ has $\bar J'(\bar x_{c'})=\mathcal L_n(B)Q_{c'}$, which lies in $\mathcal L_n(A)$ by (1) for $\bar J'$. The claim with $l=3$ gives $Q_{c'}\in\mathcal L_n(L\oplus V_2)$. Let $T_1$ be the transvection of step 5 of abelianized wild inertia, fixing $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$; it is tame-trivial with $(T_1)_H=1+N$, $N\xi_2=\xi_1$, $N^2=0$. By (iii'), $\mathcal L_n(\bar T_1)\bar J=\bar J\bar T_1$, where $\bar T_1=1+\bar N$ and $\bar N$ maps $\mu\bar x_2$ to $\mu\bar x_1$ and kills the other summands. For $c'\in\{0,1\}$ this gives $\mathcal L_n(\bar T_1)Q_{c'}=Q_{c'}$. For a component of $Q_{c'}$ with $V_2$ at position $s$ and summands in $L\oplus V_2$ elsewhere, the component of $\mathcal L_n(\bar T_1)Q_{c'}$ with $V_1$ at $s$ and the same summands elsewhere has the same coordinate, and it vanishes because $Q_{c'}$ has no slot in $V_1$. So $Q_{c'}\in\mathcal L_n(L)=0$, as $n\ge2$ and $\dim L=1$. For $c'=2$, $\bar J(\bar T_1\bar x_2)=Q_2+Q_1=Q_2$, so $\mathcal L_n(\bar T_1)Q_2=Q_2$, and the same argument gives $Q_2=0$.
- *Odd $d$.* Part 3 of the mixing theorem, with $\nu=\lambda_0$, gives a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $B$ fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,3$, with $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda_0\bar x_2$. Then $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\lambda_0\bar x_2$ and $B^{-1}\bar x_0=\bar x_0$, and as in the even case the claim with $l=2$ gives $Q_{c'}\in\mathcal L_n(L)=0$ for $c'=0,1$.

So $\bar J$ vanishes on $S$ and on every complementary generator, hence on $V$ by $\Lambda$-linearity. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** By the corollary of [the theorem modulo the third lower central term](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-the-third-lower-central-term-of-wild-inertia.md), $\alpha=c_g\circ\alpha_2$ with $\alpha_2$ inducing the identity on $G/P_3$. It represents the class of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates. As $e\ge3$, $e\nmid2$, and the theorem with $n=3$ gives $\mathcal J_3(\alpha_2)=0$. If $c_k\alpha_2$ is another such representative, then $c_k$ induces the identity on $G/P_3$, so $k\in P_3$ by Lemma 5 (o). Conversely $c_k$ with $k\in P_3$ preserves both properties, since $[k,x]\in P_4$ for $x\in P$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* The new step is the localization in step 1. A power of the unit automorphism $\gamma$ on any one surface pair fixes $\bar J$, by Lemma (b) of abelianized wild inertia, and right multiplication by a power $\gamma_1\ne1$ of $\gamma$ in some coordinates has no nonzero fixed points. So $\bar J$ of a generator has no letters from a pair other than its own, and $\bar J$ of a complementary generator has letters only in $A$. Kondo's matrices $M_{ii'}$ and $M'_{ii'}$ then leave exactly one letter from the generator's own pair, since any further letter would be moved into a second pair. What remains is one linear Lie element $P$, determined by a single coordinate $\varphi$. The boundary-twist equation in the pattern $\bar a\bar a\bar b\,A\cdots A$ makes $\varphi$ constant in its $\Lambda$-factor, the units on the first pair make the constant $\Gamma$-invariant, and the mixing automorphisms and $T_1$ remove the complementary values, as in the degree-one theorem. For $n=2$ this gives a second proof of that theorem, from its lemmas and the Kondo automorphisms of its step 1.
- *Consistency.* For $d\ge5$ and $e\ge3$, every boundary twist $T^{(i)}_t$ and interval twist $T_{I,t}$ of [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md) with $t\ne0$ fixes $\mathcal G$ and is trivial on $P/P_3$, hence on $G/P_3$, and has $\mathcal J_3=tD_I\ne0$ by Lemma 2 (a) and (c) there. So the theorem with $n=3$ shows again that they have infinitely many outer conjugates, as that entry proves for every $d\ge3$.
- *When $e$ divides $n-1$.* Steps 1 to 4, and step 5 up to $\rho c=c$, use only $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. When $\omega^{n-1}=1$, Lemma 1 (c) gives $c=c_0\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}$ with $c_0\in\mathbb F_p$, and Lemma 3 gives $\bar J(\bar x_k)=c_0[\cdots[[\bar x_k,\bar x_0],\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]$ for every surface index $k$. Whether $c_0=0$, and what the complementary values are, is not decided here. For $n=3$ this is the case $e=2$, which includes every $K$ with $p=3$ and $\mu_3\not\subseteq K$.
- *What remains.* (1) The cocycle classes: for $\alpha_2$ as in the corollary, $\rho\mapsto\alpha_2(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}P_4$ is a continuous cocycle $\Gamma\to\mathcal L_3$, and changing $\alpha_2$ by $c_k$ with $k\in P_3$ changes it by a coboundary. Whether its class in $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_3)$ vanishes is not decided here. If it did, the theorem with $n=4$ would apply next when $e\nmid3$; in general the theorem and these cocycle classes alternate. (2) The case $e\mid n-1$ above. (3) Degrees $3$ and $4$, where steps 2, 3 and 5 need a second surface pair. (4) The case $\mu_p\subseteq K$. Innerness of $\alpha$ is equivalent to innerness on every $G/P_j$. This continues item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535).
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, every $s$, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$, and $n\ge2$ with $[K(\mu_p):K]\nmid n-1$ for the theorem; $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$ for the corollary. Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
