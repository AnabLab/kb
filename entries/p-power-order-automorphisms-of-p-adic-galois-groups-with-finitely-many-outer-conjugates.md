**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of degree $d \ge 3$, and $G = G_K$, with inertia group $I_K$ and wild inertia group $P_K$. Let $\omega : G \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ be the action on $\mu_p$. For $n \ge 1$ let $K_n \subset \bar K$ be the unramified extension of $K$ of degree $n$, and put $L_n = K_n(\mu_p)$. Let $\tilde K = K^{\mathrm{ur}}(\mu_p)$ and let $\Omega_K$ be the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $\tilde K$. For a finite extension $L/K$ in $\bar K$ let $L(p)$ be its maximal pro-$p$ extension, put $V_L = G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathbb{Q}_p$, and let $I_L \subseteq V_L$ be the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-span of the image of the inertia group $I_K \cap G_L$. As in [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), write $d = 2g + r$ with $r = 1$ if $d$ is odd and $r = 2$ if $d$ is even, so that $g \ge 1$.

*Every automorphism acts trivially on $\mathrm{Gal}(L_n/K)$.* Let $\eta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$. It preserves $I_K$ and $P_K$, preserves the Frobenius element, which generates $G/I_K \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ topologically, and preserves the cyclotomic character, hence $\omega$ ([invariants recovered from $G_K$](local-field-galois-invariants.md)). Let $n$ be prime to $p$. Then $G_{L_n} = \ker\omega \cap G_{K_n}$, where $G_{K_n}$ is the inverse image of $n\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ in $G$, so $\eta(G_{L_n}) = G_{L_n}$. The group $\Gamma_n = \mathrm{Gal}(L_n/K)$ embeds by restriction in $\mathrm{Gal}(K(\mu_p)/K) \times \mathrm{Gal}(K_n/K)$, on which $\eta$ induces the identity, because $\omega$ embeds the first factor in $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ and the second is a quotient of $G/I_K$. So $\eta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma_n$, which is abelian of order dividing $(p-1)n$, prime to $p$. Write $\eta_*$ for the automorphism of $V_{L_n}$ induced by $\eta$. An element $\gamma \in \Gamma_n$ acts on $V_{L_n}$ through conjugation by a lift $s \in G$, and $\eta(s) = sh$ with $h \in G_{L_n}$, which acts trivially on $G_{L_n}^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Since $\eta(sxs^{-1}) = \eta(s)\eta(x)\eta(s)^{-1}$, $\eta_*$ commutes with $\gamma$.

*Kondo's automorphisms.* Fix Jannsen–Wingberg generators $\sigma, \tau, x_0, \dots, x_d$ of $G$ as in [@kondo2025, Theorem 1.1, p. 8] (see [presentations](local-galois-group-presentations.md)). Among their properties: $G$ is topologically generated by them, $P_K$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by $x_0, \dots, x_d$, and the one relation besides $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{q}$, where $q$ is the order of the residue field, ends with $[x_1, x_2][x_3, x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$ for even $d$ and with $[x_1, x_1'][x_2, x_3]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$ for odd $d$, where $x_1'$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by $\sigma, \tau, x_1$ [@kondo2025, Theorem 1.1, p. 8]. Call $x_0, x_1$, and $x_2$ when $d$ is even, the *complementary* generators; there are $r + 1$ of them. The other $2g$ are the *surface* generators $a_i = x_{2i}$, $b_i = x_{2i+1}$ for odd $d$ and $a_i = x_{2i+1}$, $b_i = x_{2i+2}$ for even $d$, $1 \le i \le g$.

Let $\Pi$ be the free group on letters $\alpha_1, \beta_1, \dots, \alpha_g, \beta_g$, the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface of genus $g$ minus a point, put $\delta = [\alpha_1, \beta_1]\cdots[\alpha_g, \beta_g]$, let $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ be the group of automorphisms of $\Pi$ fixing $\delta$, and let $\iota : \Pi \to G$ be the homomorphism with $\iota(\alpha_i) = a_i$ and $\iota(\beta_i) = b_i$. Kondo shows that each $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ induces an automorphism $\Psi(\varphi)$ of $G$ that fixes $\sigma$, $\tau$ and the complementary generators and maps $a_i, b_i$ to $\iota(\varphi(\alpha_i)), \iota(\varphi(\beta_i))$ [@kondo2025, discussion after Lemma 2.15, p. 19]. Every mapping class of the punctured surface lifts to $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$, and the image of the mapping class group in $\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}) \cong \mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$, for the basis $[\alpha_1], [\beta_1], \dots, [\alpha_g], [\beta_g]$, is $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$ [@kondo2025, p. 19 and Theorem 2.17, p. 20], the integral symplectic group of the intersection form. Write $\mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$ for this subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$. Inner automorphisms of $\Pi$ act trivially on $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$, so the induced automorphisms $\varphi^{\mathrm{ab}}$, for $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$, form a group $H$ containing $\mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p \ge 5$, or let $p = 3$ and $d$ be odd, and let $d \ge 3$. Let $\theta$ be an automorphism of $G_K$ of finite order $p^a$ whose class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has only finitely many conjugates, for instance because it lies in a finite normal subgroup.

1. For every $n$ prime to $p$, $\theta$ acts trivially on $V_{L_n}$ and induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(L_n(p)/K)$.
2. $\theta$ maps $G_{\Omega_K}$ onto itself and induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$. Since $K(p)$, $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and $K(\mu_{p^\infty})$ lie in $\Omega_K$, it induces the identity on $G_K(p)$, on $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ur}}/K)$ and on $\mathrm{Gal}(K(\mu_{p^\infty})/K)$. If $\zeta_p \in K$, then $\tilde K = K^{\mathrm{ur}}$, and $\Omega_K$ is the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here. Keep the hypotheses on $p$ and $d$.

1. *Finite normal subgroups.* Let $f \in \mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ have order $p$ and finitely many conjugates. Then $f$ is the class of an automorphism of $G_K$ of order $p$, and every such automorphism induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$.
2. *Tsujimura's question.* Let $N$ be a rival copy of $G_K$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ of type (a) in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 2, with $M = N \cap G_K$, and suppose that $p$ divides $(G_K : M)$. Then the automorphism of order $p$ in $NG_K$, lying in neither $G_K$ nor $N$, that the Corollary of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) provides induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$.

*Proof.* (1) By part 3 (a) of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), $f$ is the class of an automorphism of order $p$; part 2 of the Theorem applies to each such automorphism. (2) The automorphism provided there has order $p$, and its class lies in $NG_K/G_K$, a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ by part 2 (a) of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), so it has finitely many conjugates, and part 2 of the Theorem applies. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** Fix $n$ prime to $p$ and write $L = L_n$, $\Gamma = \Gamma_n$ and $N = |\Gamma|$. Let $A = \theta_*$, so $A^{p^a} = 1$ and $A$ commutes with $\Gamma$ (Setting).

*Step 1: generators of $I_L$.* By facts (i) and (ii) of [Lubin–Tate representations of non-Galois fields](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), applied to $L$, the map $\log_L \circ \mathrm{rec}_L^{-1}$ identifies $I_L$ with $L_+$. It is $\Gamma$-equivariant, because local reciprocity commutes with conjugation, as recalled in the proof of part 1 (a) of [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on norm-one units](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md), and $\log_L$ commutes with field automorphisms. By the normal basis theorem $L \cong K[\Gamma] \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]^d$, so $I_L \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]^d$ as $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]$-modules. Next, $L/K$ is tamely ramified, so $P_K \subseteq G_L$. The quotient $(I_K \cap G_L)/P_K$ is a closed subgroup of $I_K/P_K \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ ([finite subgroups](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), Method), so it has no nontrivial pro-$p$ quotient, and the images of $I_K \cap G_L$ and of $P_K$ in the pro-$p$ group $G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$ coincide. $P_K$ is the closure of the subgroup generated by the conjugates $gx_ig^{-1}$, $g \in G$, and the image of $gx_ig^{-1}$ in $V_L$ is $\gamma\bar x_i$, where $\bar x_i$ is the image of $x_i$ and $\gamma$ that of $g$ in $\Gamma$. As the closure of a finitely generated subgroup of a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module is its $\mathbb{Z}_p$-span, $I_L$ is the $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]$-span of $\bar x_0, \dots, \bar x_d$. So
$$\pi : M = M^{c} \oplus M^{s} \to I_L, \qquad M^c = \bigoplus_{x_i\ \text{complementary}} \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]e_i,\quad M^s = \Pi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma],$$
sending $e_i$ to $\bar x_i$ and $[\alpha_i] \otimes 1, [\beta_i] \otimes 1$ to $\bar a_i, \bar b_i$, is a surjection of $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]$-modules. Let $R$ be its kernel.

*Step 2: Kondo's automorphisms on $I_L$.* For $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ and $\eta = \Psi(\varphi)$, let $\varphi_M = \mathrm{id}_{M^c} \oplus (\varphi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes 1)$. Then $\pi \circ \varphi_M = \eta_* \circ \pi$. Both sides are $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Gamma]$-linear (Setting), so it suffices to check this on generators. $\eta$ fixes the complementary generators. It maps $a_i$ to the word $\iota(\varphi(\alpha_i))$ in the surface generators, all of which lie in $G_L$, and the image of a word in the abelian group $G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the sum of the images of its letters with their exponent sums as coefficients, which is $\pi(\varphi^{\mathrm{ab}}[\alpha_i] \otimes 1)$; likewise for $b_i$. Hence $\varphi_M(R) \subseteq R$. Moreover $\Psi$ is a homomorphism: $\Psi(\varphi) \circ \iota = \iota \circ \varphi$, as both are homomorphisms $\Pi \to G$ agreeing on the letters, so $\Psi(\varphi)\Psi(\varphi')$ and $\Psi(\varphi\varphi')$ agree on $\sigma$, $\tau$ and every $x_i$, which generate $G$.

*Step 3: isotypic parts.* Let $E = \mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_N)$. Since $p \nmid N$, $N$ divides $p^f - 1$ for some $f$, so $\mu_N$ lies in the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $f$ ([multiplicative groups](multiplicative-group-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), and $E/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is unramified. Every character $\varepsilon$ of $\Gamma$ takes values in $E$, and $e_\varepsilon = N^{-1}\sum_\gamma \varepsilon(\gamma)^{-1}\gamma$ are orthogonal idempotents of $E[\Gamma]$ with sum $1$ and $Ee_\varepsilon = e_\varepsilon E[\Gamma]$ of dimension $1$. Put $I_\varepsilon = e_\varepsilon(I_L \otimes E)$, $M_\varepsilon = e_\varepsilon(M \otimes E) = M^c_\varepsilon \oplus M^s_\varepsilon$ and $R_\varepsilon = e_\varepsilon(R \otimes E)$. Then $\dim_E I_\varepsilon = d$ by Step 1, $M^c_\varepsilon \cong E^{r+1}$, $M^s_\varepsilon = \Pi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes E \cong E^{2g}$, and $\pi$ induces $M_\varepsilon/R_\varepsilon \cong I_\varepsilon$, so $\dim_E R_\varepsilon = 1$.

*Span fact.* Let $\Gamma_0$ be a subgroup of finite index in $\mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$. Then the $E$-span of $\{X - 1 : X \in \Gamma_0\}$ is $\mathrm{End}_E(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes E)$. Indeed, the closure of $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$ in $\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ is $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ [@kondo2026, proof of Lemma 3.3, p. 10], so the closure $\bar\Gamma_0$ is a closed subgroup of finite index, hence open, in $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$. By the Claim in the proof of part 2 of [Lubin–Tate representations of non-Galois fields](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), which holds for any nondegenerate alternating form, the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-span of the $X - 1$ with $X \in \bar\Gamma_0$ is all of $M_{2g}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. A finite-dimensional subspace is closed, so the span over $\Gamma_0$ is the same. Extend scalars to $E$.

*$R_\varepsilon$ lies in $M^c_\varepsilon$.* Write $R_\varepsilon = Ev$ with $v = (v_c, v_s)$. By Step 2, $(v_c, Xv_s) \in Ev$ for every $X \in \mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}) \subseteq H$. If $v_c \neq 0$, this forces $Xv_s = v_s$ for all such $X$, so $Yv_s = 0$ for every $Y$ in the span, which is everything, and $v_s = 0$. If $v_c = 0$, then $v_s \ne 0$ and $Yv_s \in Ev_s$ for every endomorphism $Y$ of $E^{2g}$, which is false as $2g \ge 2$. So $v_s = 0$. Hence $I_\varepsilon = \bar I^c_\varepsilon \oplus \bar I^s_\varepsilon$, where $\bar I^c_\varepsilon = \pi(M^c_\varepsilon)$ has dimension $r$ and $\pi$ maps $M^s_\varepsilon$ isomorphically onto $\bar I^s_\varepsilon$. By Step 2, $\Psi(\varphi)_*$ acts on $I_\varepsilon$ as $\mathrm{id} \oplus \varphi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ in this decomposition.

*Step 4: finitely many conjugates.* Let $\mathcal N \subseteq \mathrm{GL}(V_L)$ be the image of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$. The conjugates of $A$ in $\mathcal N$ are the images of the automorphisms $\eta\theta\eta^{-1}$. Their outer classes lie among the finitely many conjugates $f_1, \dots, f_m$ of the class of $\theta$. With representatives $\theta_j$ of the $f_j$, each $\eta\theta\eta^{-1}$ is $\theta_j c_h$ for some $j$ and an inner automorphism $c_h$, $h \in G$, which acts on $V_L$ through the image of $h$ in $\Gamma$. So $A$ has at most $m|\Gamma|$ conjugates in $\mathcal N$, and its centralizer has finite index in $\mathcal N$. Let $Y \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ be the inverse image of this centralizer, of finite index. As $\Psi$ is a homomorphism, $\Psi^{-1}(Y)$ has finite index in $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$, so its image has finite index in $H$, and $\Gamma_0 = \{\varphi^{\mathrm{ab}} : \varphi \in \Psi^{-1}(Y)\} \cap \mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$ has finite index in $\mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$.

*Step 5: $A = 1$ on $I_L$.* Fix $\varepsilon$. $A$ preserves $I_\varepsilon$, as it commutes with $\Gamma$, and it commutes with $\mathrm{id} \oplus X$ on $I_\varepsilon = \bar I^c_\varepsilon \oplus \bar I^s_\varepsilon$ for every $X \in \Gamma_0$. By the span fact, it commutes with $0 \oplus Z$ for every $E$-linear endomorphism $Z$ of $\bar I^s_\varepsilon$. As in the last paragraph of the proof of part 2 of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), $Z = \mathrm{id}$ shows that $A$ preserves both summands, and then $A = C_\varepsilon \oplus \mu_\varepsilon\,\mathrm{id}$ with $C_\varepsilon \in \mathrm{GL}(\bar I^c_\varepsilon)$ and $\mu_\varepsilon \in E^\times$. Both satisfy $C_\varepsilon^{p^a} = 1$ and $\mu_\varepsilon^{p^a} = 1$. Let $\zeta \ne 1$ be a root of unity of order $p^b$, $b \ge 1$. The extension $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta)/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is totally ramified of degree $(p-1)p^{b-1}$ ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)), and $E/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is unramified, so the ramification index of $E(\zeta)/E$ is at least $(p-1)p^{b-1}$, and $[E(\zeta) : E] \ge p - 1$. Hence $\zeta \notin E$, so $\mu_\varepsilon = 1$. The eigenvalues of $C_\varepsilon$ are roots of its characteristic polynomial, of degree $r$ over $E$. If $p \ge 5$, then $p - 1 > 2 \ge r$; if $p = 3$, then $d$ is odd and $p - 1 = 2 > 1 = r$. Either way no eigenvalue is a root of unity $\zeta \ne 1$ of $p$-power order, so all eigenvalues are $1$. As $x^{p^a} - 1$ has no repeated roots, $C_\varepsilon$ is diagonalizable, so $C_\varepsilon = 1$. Thus $A$ is the identity on every $I_\varepsilon$, hence on $I_L \otimes E = \bigoplus_\varepsilon I_\varepsilon$, hence on $I_L$.

*Step 6: $A = 1$ on $V_L$.* The quotient $V_L/I_L$ is spanned by the image of $G_L/(I_K \cap G_L)$, a subgroup of $G/I_K$ on which $\theta$ induces the identity (Setting). So $(A - 1)^2 = 0$. As the minimal polynomial of $A$ also divides $x^{p^a} - 1$, which has no repeated roots, $A = 1$.

*Step 7: $G_L(p)$.* As $\zeta_p \in L$, $D = G_L(p)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $[L : \mathbb{Q}_p] + 2 \ge 3$ ([maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md)). The kernel of $G_L \to D$ is characteristic in $G_L$, so $\theta$ induces $\bar\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ with $\bar\theta^{p^a} = 1$. The group $D^{\mathrm{ab}} = G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$ modulo torsion is a lattice in $V_L$, on which $\bar\theta$ acts as $A = 1$. By Corollary 1 (1) of [automorphisms of order $p$ of Demushkin groups](order-p-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-act-nontrivially-modulo-torsion.md), $\bar\theta = 1$.

*Step 8: $\mathrm{Gal}(L(p)/K)$.* The subgroup $G_{L(p)} = \ker(G_L \to D)$ is characteristic in $G_L$, which is normal in $G$ and preserved by $\theta$. So $G_{L(p)}$ is normal in $G$ and preserved by $\theta$, which induces an automorphism $\theta_Q$ of $Q = G/G_{L(p)} = \mathrm{Gal}(L(p)/K)$. It is the identity on the normal subgroup $D$, by Step 7, and on $Q/D = \Gamma$. For $x \in Q$ put $z = x^{-1}\theta_Q(x) \in D$. For $y \in D$, $xyx^{-1} = \theta_Q(xyx^{-1}) = \theta_Q(x)\,y\,\theta_Q(x)^{-1} = xzyz^{-1}x^{-1}$, so $z$ centralizes $D$. A Demushkin group of rank at least $3$ has trivial centre ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), part 4), so $z = 1$ and $\theta_Q = \mathrm{id}$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $\Omega'$ be the union of the fields $L_n(p)$ with $p \nmid n$.

- *$\Omega'$ is a field contained in $\Omega_K$.* If $n \mid n'$, then $L_n(p)L_{n'}$ is a pro-$p$ extension of $L_{n'}$, so $L_n(p) \subseteq L_{n'}(p)$; any two integers prime to $p$ divide their product, so the union is directed. Each $L_n$ lies in $\tilde K$, and the Galois group of $L_n(p)\tilde K/\tilde K$ is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of the pro-$p$ group $\mathrm{Gal}(L_n(p)/L_n)$, so $L_n(p) \subseteq \Omega_K$.
- *Every $L_m$ lies in $\Omega'$.* Write $m = p^km'$ with $p \nmid m'$. Then $L_m = L_{m'}K_m$, and $\mathrm{Gal}(L_m/L_{m'})$ embeds in $\mathrm{Gal}(K_m/K_{m'})$, of order $p^k$. So $L_m \subseteq L_{m'}(p)$. Hence $\tilde K = \bigcup_m L_m \subseteq \Omega'$, and $L_m/L_{m'}$ is Galois of $p$-power degree.
- *$\Omega_K \subseteq \Omega'$.* Let $F'/\tilde K$ be finite Galois of $p$-power degree, $F' = \tilde K(\vartheta)$, with minimal polynomial $h$ over $\tilde K$. The roots of $h$ are polynomials in $\vartheta$ with coefficients in $\tilde K$. Choose $m$ such that $L_m$ contains the coefficients of $h$ and of these polynomials. Then $E_0 = L_m(\vartheta)$ is a splitting field over $L_m$ of $h$, which is separable and irreducible over $L_m \subseteq \tilde K$, so $E_0/L_m$ is Galois of degree $[F' : \tilde K]$, a power of $p$. With $m = p^km'$ as above, the conjugates of $E_0$ over $L_{m'}$ are Galois extensions of $p$-power degree of the Galois extension $L_m$, so their compositum is Galois of $p$-power degree over $L_m$, hence over $L_{m'}$. So $E_0 \subseteq L_{m'}(p) \subseteq \Omega'$, and $\vartheta \in \Omega'$. As $\Omega_K$ is the union of such $F'$, $\Omega_K = \Omega'$.

By part 1, each $G_{L_n(p)}$, $p \nmid n$, is preserved by $\theta$, and $g^{-1}\theta(g) \in G_{L_n(p)}$ for every $g \in G$. So $G_{\Omega_K} = \bigcap_{p \nmid n} G_{L_n(p)}$ is preserved by $\theta$, and $g^{-1}\theta(g) \in G_{\Omega_K}$ for every $g$, which is the claim. For the listed fields: $K(p)L_1/L_1$ is pro-$p$, so $K(p) \subseteq L_1(p)$; $K^{\mathrm{ur}} \subseteq \tilde K$; and the cyclotomic character embeds $\mathrm{Gal}(K(\mu_{p^\infty})/K(\mu_p))$ in the pro-$p$ group $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$, so $K(\mu_{p^\infty}) \subseteq L_1(p)$. If $\zeta_p \in K$, then $\tilde K = K^{\mathrm{ur}}$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Comparison.* Part 3 (a) of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) shows that automorphisms of order $p$ whose class has finitely many conjugates are trivial on $G_K(p)$, for every odd $p$, using one Kondo block on $K_+$ and the invariant cyclotomic form. Here the same block is found in every isotypic part of $G_{L_n}^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$. The point is that Kondo's automorphisms fix $\sigma$ and $\tau$ and move only surface generators within the subgroup they generate, so on these parts they act by the same integral symplectic matrix. That gives triviality on the much larger quotient $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$, which has every $\mathrm{Gal}(L_n(p)/K)$, $p \nmid n$, as a quotient; when $\zeta_p \in K$ these are the $\mathrm{Gal}(K_n(p)/K)$. No condition $d \le p - 2$ is needed, so fields containing $\zeta_p$ are included.
- *The excluded cases.* For $p = 3$ and even $d$, the complementary block has dimension $2$, and nothing here prevents $C_\varepsilon$ from having eigenvalues $\zeta_3^{\pm 1}$. The invariant cyclotomic form used in part 3 (a) of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) is $\Gamma$-invariant, so on $V_{L_n}$ it vanishes on every nontrivial isotypic part and cannot help there. For $d \le 2$ there is no symplectic block.
- *What remains for case (a).* The theorem says nothing about the kernel $G_{\Omega_K}$ of $G \to \mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_K/K)$. Since $\Omega_K \supseteq K^{\mathrm{ur}}$, this kernel lies in $I_K$, and its image in tame inertia $I_K/P_K \cong \prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is open. On this procyclic group $\theta$ acts by multiplication by some $u \in \prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ with $u^{p^a} = 1$, so $u_\ell = 1$ unless $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$; whether $u = 1$ is not decided here. Whether $G_K$ has any automorphism of order $p$ whose class has finitely many conjugates remains open, also when $\zeta_p \in K$.
