---
title: Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion for abelian varieties
kind: theorem
summary: 'An abelian variety over a discretely valued field with perfect residue field has good reduction iff inertia acts trivially on $T_\ell A$, for one or every prime $\ell$ other than the residue characteristic, and potentially good reduction iff inertia has finite image (Serre–Tate).'
tags: [abelian-varieties, local-fields, valuations, ramification, galois-groups]
---

**Setting.** Let $K$ be a field with a discrete valuation $v$, valuation ring $\mathcal O_v$ and residue field $k$, and put $p=\operatorname{char}k\ge0$. Neither completeness nor henselianity is assumed. Fix a separable closure $K_s$ and an extension $\bar v$ of $v$ to $K_s$, with inertia group $I=I(\bar v)$ and decomposition group $D(\bar v)$ in $\operatorname{Gal}(K_s/K)$. A set with an action of $\operatorname{Gal}(K_s/K)$ is *unramified at $v$* if $I$ acts trivially on it. This does not depend on $\bar v$, since the inertia groups of two extensions are conjugate [@serretate1968, §1, p. 140].

An abelian variety $A/K$ has *good reduction at $v$* if there is an abelian scheme $\mathcal A$ over $\mathcal O_v$ with $\mathcal A\times_{\mathcal O_v}K\cong A$. For $m$ prime to $\operatorname{char}K$, put $A_m=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z,A(K_s))$, a free $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$-module of rank $2\dim A$. For a prime $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}K$, the Tate module $T_\ell A=\varprojlim_n A_{\ell^n}$ is free of rank $2\dim A$ over $\mathbb Z_\ell$, with a continuous action of $\operatorname{Gal}(K_s/K)$ [@serretate1968, §1, p. 141].

**Theorem (Serre–Tate).** Assume that $k$ is perfect, and let $\ell\ne p$ be a prime. The following are equivalent [@serretate1968, Theorem 1, p. 141]:

- (a) $A$ has good reduction at $v$;
- (b) $A_m$ is unramified at $v$ for every $m\ge1$ prime to $p$;
- (b′) $A_m$ is unramified at $v$ for infinitely many $m$ prime to $p$;
- (c) $T_\ell A$ is unramified at $v$.

Condition (c) holds exactly when $A_{\ell^n}$ is unramified for every $n$ [@serretate1968, proof of Theorem 1, p. 142]. Unwinding the definitions, this says that $I$ fixes the field $K(A[\ell^\infty])\subseteq K_s$, the smallest extension of $K$ over which all $\ell$-power torsion points are defined. Since (a) does not mention $\ell$, (c) holds for one prime $\ell\ne p$ exactly when it holds for all of them [@serretate1968, Corollary 1 to Theorem 1, p. 141]. When $p=0$, every $m$ counts as prime to $p$ and every prime $\ell$ may be used.

The criterion is the implication (c) $\Rightarrow$ (a); the converse was already well known. Serre and Tate named it after Néron, whose minimal models give the proof, and after Ogg, who used a very similar criterion for elliptic curves that was apparently also known to Shafarevich. Grothendieck later generalized it substantially [@serretate1968, Remarks (1)–(2) after Theorem 1 and footnote 2, p. 142].

**Hypotheses.**

- *Perfect residue field.* Serre and Tate assume it because Néron does, and note that, by results announced by Raynaud, it is unnecessary [@serretate1968, footnote 4, p. 141]. Tamagawa quotes the criterion, without proof, over an arbitrary discrete valuation ring [@tamagawa1997, §5, p. 176].
- *Change of field.* Let $K'/K$ be an extension and $v'$ an extension of $v$ such that the inertia group of $v'$ maps onto that of $v$, for example the completion, or a finite extension unramified at $v'$. If $A_{K'}$ has good reduction at $v'$, then $A$ has good reduction at $v$ [@serretate1968, Corollary 4 to Theorem 1, p. 142].
- *Isogenies and duality.* Good reduction passes to quotients by surjective homomorphisms. So $K$-isogenous abelian varieties, and $A$ and its dual, have good reduction together. In an exact sequence $0\to A'\to A\to A''\to0$, $A$ has good reduction exactly when $A'$ and $A''$ do [@serretate1968, Corollaries 2–3 to Theorem 1, pp. 141–142].
- *The prime $p$.* The theorem excludes $\ell=p$. For complete $K$ of characteristic $0$ with perfect residue field of characteristic $p>0$, Andreatta, Iovita and Kim state the $p$-adic replacement, attributing it to Fontaine, Mokrane, Coleman–Iovita and Breuil: $A$ has good reduction exactly when $T_pA$ is crystalline [@andreattaiovitakim2015, Theorem 1.2, p. 2]. Over finite extensions of $\mathbb Q_p$ this is the [Coleman–Iovita criterion](crystalline-tate-module-good-reduction.md), checked there against its sources.

**Proof (Serre–Tate).** Let $\mathcal A$ be the Néron model of $A$. It is a smooth group scheme of finite type over $\mathcal O_v$ with generic fibre $A$, representing $Y\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_K(Y\times_{\mathcal O_v}K,A)$ on smooth $\mathcal O_v$-schemes, and $A$ has good reduction exactly when $\mathcal A$ is proper. The identity component of its special fibre $\tilde A$ is an extension of an abelian variety $B$ by a product $S\times U$ of a torus and a unipotent group. Let $c=[\tilde A:\tilde A^\circ]$ be the index of the identity component, and put $\tilde A_m=\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z,\tilde A(\bar k))$. For $m$ prime to $p$:

- *Lemma 1.* $\tilde A_m$ is an extension of a group of order dividing $c$ by a free $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$-module of rank $\dim S+2\dim B$.
- *Lemma 2.* Reduction gives a $D(\bar v)$-equivariant isomorphism $A_m^{I}\cong\tilde A_m$. Here $L=K_s^I$ has a henselian valuation ring $\mathcal O_L$ with residue field $\bar k$, and $\mathcal O_L$ is a union of étale $\mathcal O_v$-algebras. So $A(L)=\mathcal A(\mathcal O_L)$ by the Néron property, and $\mathcal A(\mathcal O_L)\to\tilde A(\bar k)$ is onto with kernel uniquely $m$-divisible.

If $\mathcal A$ is an abelian scheme, then $\tilde A_m$ has order $m^{2\dim A}=|A_m|$, so $A_m^I=A_m$. This proves (a) $\Rightarrow$ (b). Conversely, under (b′) take $m>c$ with $A_m^I=A_m$. Comparing orders gives $\dim S+2\dim B\ge2\dim A=2(\dim U+\dim S+\dim B)$, so $S=U=0$ and $\tilde A$ is proper. *Lemma 3*, applied to the Néron model itself, then shows that $\mathcal A$ is proper: $\mathcal A$ is smooth and of finite type over $\mathcal O_v$, its generic fibre $A$ is geometrically connected, and its special fibre $\tilde A$ is proper and nonempty, since it contains the identity. This proves (b′) $\Rightarrow$ (a) [@serretate1968, proof of Theorem 1 and Lemmas 1–3, pp. 142–144].

**Potentially good reduction.** $A$ has *potentially good reduction at $v$* if $A_{K'}$ has good reduction at some extension $v'$ of $v$, for some finite extension $K'/K$ [@serretate1968, §2, p. 144]. Keep the hypotheses of the theorem, and let $\rho_\ell:\operatorname{Gal}(K_s/K)\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_\ell A)$ be the $\ell$-adic representation, for $\ell\ne p$.

- $A$ has potentially good reduction at $v$ exactly when $\rho_\ell(I)$ is finite. In that case $\rho_\ell|_I$ does not depend on $\ell$: its kernel is the same for every $\ell\ne p$, and its character is $\mathbb Z$-valued and independent of $\ell$ [@serretate1968, Theorem 2, p. 145].
- If $k$ is finite, and for some $\ell\ne p$ the image of $\rho_\ell$ is abelian, then $A$ has potentially good reduction [@serretate1968, Corollary 1 to Theorem 2, pp. 145–146]. Serre and Tate apply their results to abelian varieties with complex multiplication, which have potentially good reduction everywhere [@serretate1968, introduction, p. 140].
- If $\mathcal O_v$ is henselian with algebraically closed residue field, and $A$ has potentially good reduction, there is a smallest extension $L/K$ inside an algebraic closure over which $A$ acquires good reduction. It is Galois, equal to $K(A_m)$ for every $m\ge3$ prime to $p$, and $\operatorname{Gal}(K_s/L)=\ker\rho_\ell$ for every $\ell\ne p$ [@serretate1968, Corollary 3 to Theorem 2, p. 146]. So the representation $\rho_\ell$ determines the field over which good reduction is attained.

**Role in the graph.** This is the abelian prototype of the curve criteria recorded here. Tamagawa's proof of the [Oda–Tamagawa criterion](oda-good-reduction-criterion.md) begins with it. For a curve $U=X\setminus D$, the abelianization of the geometric pro-$\ell$ fundamental group is $T_\ell(J_X)$ when $D$ is empty, and maps onto $T_\ell(J_X)$ in general. If inertia acts on the group by inner automorphisms, it acts trivially on the abelianization. So the Jacobian $J_X$ has good reduction, and the nonabelian part of the argument then works on a stable model whose dual graph is a tree [@tamagawa1997, §5, p. 176, and proof of Theorem (5.3), p. 177]. Abelian data alone cannot detect good reduction of curves. Over a complete field of characteristic $0$ with perfect residue field of characteristic $p>0$, a smooth proper curve with good reduction has unramified $H_i(X_{\bar K},\mathbb Z_\ell)$ for $i=0,1,2$ and all $\ell\ne p$, but the converse is not always true [@andreattaiovitakim2015, §1, p. 2]. The [crystalline](crystalline-fundamental-group-good-reduction.md) and [rigid](rigid-fundamental-group-good-reduction.md) fundamental-group criteria are $p$-adic counterparts for curves.
