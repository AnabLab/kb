**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite of degree $n$ with $\mu_p \subset K$, which always holds for $p = 2$, and let $q$ be the order of its residue field. Let $K^{ur} \subseteq K_{tr}$ be the maximal unramified and the maximal tamely ramified extensions of $K$, and put $P = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar K/K_{tr})$, $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(K_{tr}/K)$ and $I = \mathrm{Gal}(K_{tr}/K^{ur})$. Then $\Gamma$ is generated by a generator $\tau$ of $I$ and a Frobenius lift $\sigma$ with the single relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$ [@luo2024, Proposition 2.15 (i)]. Every element of $\Gamma$ is $\sigma^y\tau^x$ with $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, $x \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, $I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ and $\Gamma/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, generated by the image of $\sigma$ (as in the lemma of [the Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md)). Let $[P,P]$ be the closed commutator subgroup of $P$ and put

$$E_K = G_K/[P,P] = \mathrm{Gal}(K_{tr}^{ab}/K).$$

Let $N$ be the kernel of $\Gamma \to \Gamma/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{Z}_p$. Its fixed field $F$ is the unramified $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension of $K$, with layers $K_m$ of degree $p^m$. Let $\gamma \in \mathrm{Gal}(F/K)$ be the Frobenius, $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[[\mathrm{Gal}(F/K)]]$ and $\omega_m = \gamma^{p^m} - 1$. Then $\Lambda$ is a complete regular local ring of dimension $2$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = (p, \gamma - 1)$, isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p[[T]]$ by $\gamma \mapsto 1 - T$, and $\Lambda/\omega_m\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(K_m/K)]$ [@serre1960classes, no. 6, Lemme 3, p. 89]. A compact $\Lambda$-module $Y$ with $\mathfrak{m}Y = Y$ is $0$ (proof of [@serre1960classes, Lemme 4, pp. 89–90]), in particular one with $pY = Y$. This is the Nakayama property used below.

The group $\mu_{p^\infty}(K^{ur})$ is finite: otherwise $K(\mu_{p^\infty})$ would be unramified over $K$, but it contains $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})$, an infinite totally ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@milnecft, I, 1.11, p. 24]. Write $p^{s'}$ for its order and $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^{g_K}$ for the action of Frobenius on it. Then $p^{s'} \ge 2$, and $s' \ge 2$ exactly when $\mu_{p^2}$ lies in $K$ or $K(\mu_{p^2})/K$ is unramified. For $p = 2$, $s' = 1$ exactly when $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified.

Let $E_N \subseteq E_K$ be the preimage of $N$, and let $X_K$ be the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $E_N^{ab}$, with $\mathrm{Gal}(F/K) = E_K/E_N$ acting by conjugation. It is a compact $\Lambda$-module.

**Theorem.**

1. $X_K \cong \Lambda^{n-1} \oplus J_K$, where $J_K = \mathrm{Ann}_\Lambda(\mu_{p^\infty}(K^{ur})) = (p^{s'}, \gamma - \tilde g)$ for any $\tilde g \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ lifting $g_K$.
2. Let $L/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite with $\mu_p \subset L$. If $E_K \cong E_L$, then $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] = [L:\mathbb{Q}_p]$, the residue fields have the same order, $|\mu_{p^\infty}(K^{ur})| = |\mu_{p^\infty}(L^{ur})| = p^{s'}$, and $g_K \equiv g_L \pmod{p^{s'}}$.

Whether $\mu_p \subset K$ is visible in $E_K$ too: $E_K^{ab} = G_K^{ab}$, as $[P,P] \subseteq [G_K, G_K]$, and $G_K^{ab} \cong U_K \times \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ by local class field theory [@milnecft, I, 1.10, p. 23], whose torsion is the group of roots of unity in $K$.

**Corollary.** Let $\mu_p \subset K$, and suppose that $p$ is odd, or that $p = 2$ and $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is unramified. Then for every finite $L/\mathbb{Q}_p$, $G_K \cong G_L$ if and only if $E_K \cong E_L$.

**Remarks.** The theorem, the corollary and the remarks are worked out here. The sources opened for this entry do not state them.

- *The quotient sees more than $G_K^{ab}$ and $G_K(p)$.* $K = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{3})$ and $L = \mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{6})$ have degree $2$, residue field $\mathbb{F}_2$, and $K \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty}) = L \cap \mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty}) = \mathbb{Q}_2$, because the quadratic subfields of $\mathbb{Q}_2(\mu_{2^\infty})$ are $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-1})$, $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{2})$ and $\mathbb{Q}_2(\sqrt{-2})$, and none of $\pm 3, \pm 6$ is a square in $\mathbb{Q}_2$. So $G_K(2) \cong G_L(2)$ by [maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md), and $G_K^{ab} \cong G_L^{ab}$, since $q = 2$ makes $G^{ab}$ the product of $G(2)^{ab}$ and $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. But $K(\sqrt{-1}) = K(\sqrt{-3})$ is unramified over $K$, so $s'_K \ge 2$. The unramified quadratic extension of $L$ is $L(\sqrt{-3})$, and $L(\sqrt{-1}) \ne L(\sqrt{-3})$ because neither $3$ nor $18$ is a square in $\mathbb{Q}_2$, so $s'_L = 1$. By part 2, $E_K \not\cong E_L$.
- *The open 2-adic case.* If $p = 2$ and $K(\sqrt{-1})/K$ is ramified, part 1 gives $X_K \cong \Lambda^{n-1} \oplus (2, \gamma - 1)$, which depends only on $n$. So $X_K$ cannot separate the pairs left open in part 2 of [the classification](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). For the resulting classification of $E_K$ itself, see [abelianized wild inertia from unramified cyclotomic data](abelianized-wild-inertia-from-unramified-cyclotomic-data.md).
- *Without $\mu_p$.* For odd $p$ and $\mu_p \not\subset K$, $G_K(p)$ is free pro-$p$ of rank $n + 1$ [@labute1967, §5, p. 130], and the argument of step 3 below gives $X_K \cong \Lambda^n$, which carries no information beyond $n$.

**Proof.**

*Step 1: what $E_K$ determines.* $M = P/[P,P]$ is pro-$p$, because $P$ is, a standard fact not checked in a source here. It is the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $E_K$. Indeed, let $V$ be another, with image $\bar V$ in $\Gamma$. Then $\bar V \cap I$ is pro-$p$ and of order prime to $p$, so trivial, and $[\bar V, I] \subseteq \bar V \cap I = 1$. An element $\sigma^y\tau^x$ commuting with $\tau$ has $q^y = 1$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and since $q$ has order $m$ modulo $q^m - 1$, $y \in m\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ for all $m$, so $y = 0$. Hence $\bar V \subseteq I$, so $\bar V = 1$ and $V \subseteq M$.

So an isomorphism $\theta : E_K \to E_L$ maps $M_K$ onto $M_L$ and induces $\psi : \Gamma_K \to \Gamma_L$. The torsion subgroup of $\Gamma^{ab} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \times \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'/(q-1)$ has order $q - 1$, so $q_K = q_L$. Identifying $\Gamma_K$ and $\Gamma_L$ with the profinite group $\langle\sigma, \tau \mid \sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q\rangle$ through chosen generators, $\psi$ becomes an automorphism of it. The image $b$ of $\psi(\tau)$ in $\Gamma/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ satisfies $b = qb$, so $b = 0$. Thus $\psi(I) \subseteq I$, likewise for $\psi^{-1}$, so $\psi(\tau) = \tau^u$ with $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$. Write $\psi(\sigma) = \sigma^v\tau^h$ with $v \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$. Applying $\psi$ to the relation gives $\tau^{uq^v} = \tau^{uq}$, so $q^{v-1} = 1$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ and $v = 1$ as above. So $\psi$ maps $N_K$ onto $N_L$ and $\gamma_K$ to $\gamma_L$, and $\theta$ maps $E_{N,K}$ onto $E_{N,L}$. It therefore induces an isomorphism $X_K \to X_L$ that is semilinear for the isomorphism $\Lambda_K \to \Lambda_L$ sending $\gamma_K$ to $\gamma_L$. Granting part 1, this proves part 2: $n$ is the rank of $X_K$, and the ideal $J_K$ is determined by the module $X_K$, as its $n$-th Fitting ideal (step 3). Then $\Lambda/J_K \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^{s'}$, with $\gamma$ acting by $g_K$, gives $s'$ and $g_K$.

*Step 2: $X_K$ as a Galois group.* $E_N = G_F/[P,P]$ and $[P,P] \subseteq [G_F, G_F]$, so $E_N^{ab} = G_F^{ab}$ and $X_K = \mathrm{Gal}(F^{ab}(p)/F)$, the Galois group of the maximal abelian pro-$p$ extension of $F$. Let $K(p)$ be the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $K$. It contains $F$, and the maximal pro-$p$ extension $F(p)$ of $F$ is Galois over $K$ with pro-$p$ group, so $F(p) = K(p)$. Hence $X_K = H^{ab}$ for $H = \mathrm{Gal}(K(p)/F)$, the kernel of $\varphi : G_K(p) \to \mathrm{Gal}(F/K)$, with $\gamma$ acting by conjugation.

*Step 3: a presentation with one relation.* By Labute, $G_K(p) = \mathfrak{F}/R$, where $\mathfrak{F}$ is the free pro-$p$ group on $x_1, \dots, x_d$ with $d = n + 2$, and $R$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by one relator $r$ of the following forms, with $(x,y) = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ [@labute1967, p. 106 and §5, Theorems 7–9, pp. 131–132]:

- (A) $x_1^{q'}(x_1,x_2)(x_3,x_4)\cdots(x_{d-1},x_d)$, with $q' = |\mu_{p^\infty}(K)| \ne 2$, or with $p = 2$, $d$ even and $q' = 2 + 2^f$, $f \ge 2$;
- (B) $x_1^2(x_1,x_2)x_3^{2^f}(x_3,x_4)(x_5,x_6)\cdots(x_{d-1},x_d)$, with $p = 2$, $d$ even, $f \ge 2$;
- (C) $x_1^2x_2^4(x_2,x_3)(x_4,x_5)\cdots(x_{d-1},x_d)$, with $p = 2$ and $d$ odd.

Let $\psi : \mathfrak{F} \to \mathrm{Gal}(F/K)$ be $\varphi$ composed with the projection, and write $t_j = \psi(x_j) = \gamma^{a_j}$ with $a_j \in \mathbb{Z}_p$. As $\psi$ is onto, some $a_{j_0}$ is a unit. Let $N' = \ker\psi$, so that $H = N'/R$.

The group $S_k = \Lambda^k \rtimes \mathrm{Gal}(F/K)$, with $(m,c)(m',c') = (m + cm', cc')$, is pro-$p$, as an extension of $\mathrm{Gal}(F/K) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ by the compact pro-$p$ group $\Lambda^k$. So there is a continuous homomorphism $\Theta : \mathfrak{F} \to S_d$ with $\Theta(x_j) = (e_j, t_j)$ [@labute1967, §1.2, p. 111]. Write $\Theta(w) = (D(w), \psi(w))$. Then $D(ww') = D(w) + \psi(w)D(w')$ and $D(x_j) = e_j$, so $D(w^{-1}) = -\psi(w)^{-1}D(w)$ and $D(x^k) = (1 + t + \dots + t^{k-1})D(x)$ for integers $k \ge 1$, where $t = \psi(x)$. The coordinates $D(w)_j$ are the images in $\Lambda$ of the pro-$p$ Fox derivatives $\partial w/\partial x_j$ [@kodanimorishitaterashima2017, §4.1, p. 39], but only the rules above are used. Both $w \mapsto \sum_j D(w)_j(t_j - 1)$ and $w \mapsto \psi(w) - 1$ are continuous, satisfy the same product rule and agree on the $x_j$, so they are equal.

Put $b = a_{j_0}^{-1}$, $y = x_{j_0}^b$ and $y_j = x_jy^{-a_j}$ for $j \ne j_0$. Then $\psi(y) = \gamma$, and the $y_j$ lie in $N'$. They generate $N'$ as a closed normal subgroup: the quotient of $\mathfrak{F}$ by the closed normal subgroup they generate is topologically generated by the image of $y$, as $x_{j_0} = y^{a_{j_0}}$ and $x_j = y_jy^{a_j}$, so it is procyclic pro-$p$, and it maps onto $\mathrm{Gal}(F/K) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$, hence isomorphically. So $N'^{ab}$ is the closed $\Lambda$-span of the classes $\bar y_j$. Let $B : \Lambda^d \to \Lambda^{d-1}$ delete the $j_0$-th coordinate and $\Theta' = (B \times \mathrm{id}) \circ \Theta : \mathfrak{F} \to S_{d-1}$. Then $\Theta'(y) = (0, \gamma)$ and $\Theta'(y_j) = (e_j, t_j)(0, t_j^{-1}) = (e_j, 1)$. On $N'$, $\Theta'$ takes values in $\Lambda^{d-1} \times \{1\}$, where it is additive and $\Lambda$-equivariant, so it induces a $\Lambda$-linear map $\delta : N'^{ab} \to \Lambda^{d-1}$ with $\delta(\bar y_j) = e_j$. The $\Lambda$-linear map $\Lambda^{d-1} \to N'^{ab}$ with $e_j \mapsto \bar y_j$ is onto, and $\delta$ is a left inverse, so both are isomorphisms. The image of $R$ in $N'^{ab}$ is the closed $\Lambda$-span of $\bar r$, and $\delta(\bar r) = B(D(r)) =: v'$. Hence

$$X_K = N'^{ab}/\Lambda\bar r \cong \Lambda^{n+1}/\Lambda v'.$$

Let $J$ be the ideal generated by all coordinates of $D(r)$. It is also generated by those of $v'$. Indeed, $\sum_j D(r)_j(t_j - 1) = \psi(r) - 1 = 0$. Under $\gamma \mapsto 1 - T$, each $t_j - 1 = (1 - T)^{a_j} - 1$ is divisible by $T$, and $t_{j_0} - 1$ is $T$ times a unit, as $a_{j_0}$ is a unit. So $t_j - 1 = (t_{j_0} - 1)c_j$ with $c_j \in \Lambda$, and as $\Lambda$ is a domain, $D(r)_{j_0} = -\sum_{j \ne j_0} D(r)_jc_j$. In particular $J$ is the $n$-th Fitting ideal of $X_K$, the ideal generated by the coordinates of the single relation $v'$ among $n + 1$ generators, and so it depends only on the module $X_K$ [@stacks2026, Tag 07Z8].

*Step 4: $\Lambda/J$ is a cyclic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module.* First, for every $j$, $J$ contains an element congruent modulo $p\Lambda$ to a unit times $t_j - 1$. From the rules, $D((x_a, x_b)) = (t_at_b)^{-1}\big((t_a - 1)e_b - (t_b - 1)e_a\big)$, and $D(w_1w_2\cdots) = \sum_i \psi(w_1\cdots w_{i-1})D(w_i)$, where $\psi$ of a commutator is $1$. Write $\Phi_k(t) = 1 + t + \dots + t^{k-1}$. If $p \mid k$, then $\Phi_k(t)$ lies in $(t - 1)\mathbb{Z}_p[t] + p\mathbb{Z}_p[t]$: with $k = pk'$, $t^k - 1 \equiv (t^{k'} - 1)^p$ modulo $p$, so $\Phi_k \equiv (t-1)^{p-1}\Phi_{k'}^p$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[t]$. Up to unit factors the coordinates of $D(r)$ are:

- (A): $\Phi_{q'}(t_1) - u(t_2 - 1)$, $\ t_1 - 1$, and $t_{2i} - 1$, $t_{2i-1} - 1$ for $i \ge 2$, with $u$ a unit.
- (B): $\Phi_2(t_1) - u(t_2 - 1)$, $\ t_1 - 1$, $\ \Phi_{2^f}(t_3) - u'(t_4 - 1)$, $\ t_3 - 1$, and $t_{2i} - 1$, $t_{2i-1} - 1$ for $i \ge 3$.
- (C): $\Phi_2(t_1)$, $\ \Phi_4(t_2) - u(t_3 - 1)$, $\ t_2 - 1$, and $t_{2i+1} - 1$, $t_{2i} - 1$ for $i \ge 2$.

For example, in case (A) the first coordinate of $D(r)$ is $\Phi_{q'}(t_1) - t_1^{q'-1}t_2^{-1}(t_2 - 1)$ and the second is $t_1^{q'-1}t_2^{-1}(t_1 - 1)$. Each index $j$ occurs in one of two ways. Either a unit times $t_j - 1$ is itself in the list, or the list contains $\Phi_k(t_a) - u(t_j - 1)$ with $p \mid k$ and a unit times $t_a - 1$ in the list: this happens for $j = 2$ in (A), $j = 2, 4$ in (B) and $j = 3$ in (C). In the second case $\Phi_k(t_a) \in (t_a - 1)\Lambda + p\Lambda \subseteq J + p\Lambda$, so $u(t_j - 1) \in J + p\Lambda$. The one remaining index, $j = 1$ in (C), is covered by $\Phi_2(t_1) = (t_1 - 1) + 2 \in J$.

Now let $A = \Lambda/J$. Taking $j = j_0$, $t_{j_0} - 1 \in J + p\Lambda$, so $\gamma^{a_{j_0}}$ acts trivially on $A/pA$. As $a_{j_0} \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ and the action is continuous, $\gamma$ acts trivially, so $\Lambda$ acts on the cyclic module $A/pA$ through the augmentation, and $A/pA$ is $\mathbb{F}_p \cdot 1$ or $0$. By the Nakayama property above, applied to $A/\mathbb{Z}_p \cdot 1$, $A = \mathbb{Z}_p \cdot 1$. So $A \cong \mathbb{Z}_p/p^a$ with $0 \le a \le \infty$, $\gamma$ acts on $A$ by some $g \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, and $g \equiv 1 \pmod p$ if $a \ge 1$.

*Step 5: the units of the layers.* For $m \ge 0$, $X_K/\omega_mX_K \cong U^1(K_m) = 1 + \mathfrak{m}_{K_m}$ as $\Lambda$-modules, with $\Lambda$ acting on $U^1(K_m)$ through $\mathrm{Gal}(K_m/K)$. Indeed, $\mathrm{Gal}(K(p)/K_m) = H \rtimes C$ with $C \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ generated by a lift of $\gamma^{p^m}$, so its abelianization $\mathrm{Gal}(K_m^{ab}(p)/K_m)$ is $X_K/\omega_mX_K \times C$, and the first factor is the image $\mathrm{Gal}(K_m^{ab}(p)/F)$ of $H$. The local Artin map gives $\widehat{K_m^\times} = U_{K_m} \times \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \cong \mathrm{Gal}(K_m^{ab}/K_m)$, and $U_{K_m}$ maps onto the subgroup fixing $K_m^{un}$ [@milnecft, I, 1.10–1.11, pp. 23–24]. The maximal pro-$p$ unramified extension of $K_m$ is $F$ [@milnecft, I, 1.8, p. 23], and the maximal pro-$p$ quotient of $U_{K_m}$ is $U^1(K_m)$, since $U_{K_m}/U^1(K_m)$ is the multiplicative group of the residue field and $U^1(K_m)$ is pro-$p$ [@milnecft, I, 1.9, p. 23]. So $\mathrm{Gal}(K_m^{ab}(p)/F) \cong U^1(K_m)$. This is $\mathrm{Gal}(K_m/K)$-equivariant: for $\rho$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(\bar K/K)$, $a \mapsto \rho^{-1}\phi_{K_m}(\rho a)\rho$ satisfies the two conditions characterizing the Artin map $\phi_{K_m}$, so equals it by uniqueness [@milnecft, I, Theorem 1.1, p. 20].

The torsion of the pro-$p$ group $U^1(K_m)$ is $\mu_{p^\infty}(K_m)$, as every $p$-power root of unity reduces to $1$ in the residue field. It contains $\mu_p$, lies in $\mu_{p^\infty}(K^{ur})$, and equals it for large $m$: $K(\mu_{p^\infty}(K^{ur}))/K$ is unramified of $p$-power degree, as its Galois group embeds in the kernel of $(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s'})^\times \to (\mathbb{Z}/p)^\times$, so it lies in $K_m$ for large $m$. Hence the torsion submodule of $X_K/\omega_mX_K$ is nonzero, of order at most $p^{s'}$, and isomorphic to $\mu_{p^\infty}(K^{ur})$ as a $\Lambda$-module for large $m$.

*Step 6: conclusion.* A vector $v \in \Lambda^k$ whose coordinates generate an ideal $J$ needing exactly $\nu \le k$ generators $h_1, \dots, h_\nu$ satisfies $\Lambda^k/\Lambda v \cong \Lambda^k/\Lambda(h_1, \dots, h_\nu, 0, \dots, 0)$. Indeed, as $\Lambda$ is local, after reordering $v_1, \dots, v_\nu$ map to a basis of $J/\mathfrak{m}J$ and so generate $J$. Subtracting $\Lambda$-combinations of them from the other coordinates, an invertible change of basis, makes those $0$. Finally $(v_1, \dots, v_\nu)$ and $(h_1, \dots, h_\nu)$ differ by a matrix that is invertible modulo $\mathfrak{m}$, hence invertible.

- $a = 0$ is impossible: then $J = \Lambda$, $X_K \cong \Lambda^n$, and $X_K/\omega_mX_K \cong (\Lambda/\omega_m)^n$ is torsion-free, contradicting step 5.
- $a = \infty$ is impossible. Since $\gamma - g$ kills $A = \Lambda/J$, $J \supseteq (\gamma - g)$. As $g \equiv 1 \pmod p$, $\Lambda/(p, \gamma - g) = \Lambda/\mathfrak{m} = \mathbb{F}_p$, so $\Lambda/(\gamma - g)$ is generated by $1$ as a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module, by the Nakayama property. Its surjection onto $A \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ is then an isomorphism, so $J = (\gamma - g)$. So $X_K \cong \Lambda^n \oplus \Lambda/(\gamma - g)$, and the torsion of $X_K/\omega_mX_K$ is that of $\mathbb{Z}_p/(g^{p^m} - 1)$, as $\Lambda/(\gamma - g) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ with $\gamma$ acting by $g$. If $g^{p^m} = 1$ for some $m$, this is $0$ for all larger $m$. Otherwise its order tends to infinity, since $g \equiv 1 \pmod p$ gives $g^{p^m} \to 1$. Both contradict step 5.

So $1 \le a < \infty$. As in the previous case, $J \supseteq (p^a, \gamma - g)$, and $\Lambda/(p^a, \gamma - g)$ is generated by $1$ as a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module and killed by $p^a$, so its surjection onto $A \cong \mathbb{Z}/p^a$ is an isomorphism and $J = (p^a, \gamma - g)$. This ideal is not principal, since $\Lambda/h\Lambda$ is infinite for every nonzero nonunit $h$, as $\Lambda$ is a local domain of dimension $2$. So $X_K \cong \Lambda^{n-1} \oplus \Lambda^2/\Lambda(p^a, \gamma - g)$. The map $(x, y) \mapsto x(\gamma - g) - yp^a$ identifies $\Lambda^2/\Lambda(p^a, \gamma - g)$ with $J$: if $x(\gamma - g) = yp^a$, then $p^a \mid x$, because $\Lambda/p \cong \mathbb{F}_p[[T]]$ is a domain and $\gamma - g \notin p\Lambda$, and then $(x, y) \in \Lambda(p^a, \gamma - g)$. Hence $X_K \cong \Lambda^{n-1} \oplus J$.

Tensoring $0 \to J \to \Lambda \to A \to 0$ with $\Lambda/\omega_m$ shows that the torsion of $J/\omega_mJ$ is $A[\omega_m]$, the kernel of $\omega_m$ on $A$, because $\omega_m$ is not a zero divisor in $\Lambda$ and $\Lambda/\omega_m$ is torsion-free. So the torsion of $X_K/\omega_mX_K$ is $A[\omega_m]$. For large $m$, $g^{p^m} \equiv 1 \pmod{p^a}$, so $A[\omega_m] = A$. By step 5, $A \cong \mu_{p^\infty}(K^{ur})$ as $\Lambda$-modules. So $a = s'$ and $g \equiv g_K \pmod{p^{s'}}$, and $J = J_K$. $\square$

*Proof of the corollary.* Since $P$ is characteristic in $G_K$ (step 1, applied to $G_K$), an isomorphism $G_K \to G_L$ induces one $E_K \to E_L$. Conversely, let $E_K \cong E_L$. Then $\mu_p \subset L$, and by part 2 the degrees, the residue fields, $s'$ and $g$ agree. The $p$-power roots of unity in $K_{tr}$ lie in $K^{ur}$: they generate an extension of $p$-power degree, since $\mu_p \subset K$, and a tamely ramified extension of $p$-power degree is unramified. So, in the notation of [the classification](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), $s_K = s'$, $H_K = 1$ and $\Phi_K = \{g_K\}$, and the same for $L$. For $p = 2$, $s' \ge 2$ for $K$, hence for $L$, so $L(\sqrt{-1})/L$ is unramified too. Steps 3 and 4 (odd $p$) and step 5 ($p = 2$) of the proof of part 2 there use only these data and the degree, and give $G_K \cong G_L$. $\square$
