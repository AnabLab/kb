**Setting.** Let $\Pi = \widehat{\Gamma}_{2,0}$ be the profinite surface group of genus $2$ and $A = \mathrm{Aut}(\Pi)$. As in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-profinite-surface-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), identify $\Pi$ with $\mathrm{Inn}(\Pi) \subseteq A$ through $x \mapsto c_x$, $c_x(z) = xzx^{-1}$, so that $\alpha c_x \alpha^{-1} = c_{\alpha(x)}$ for $\alpha \in A$.

Let $C$ be Poonen's curve of genus $2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$: the smooth projective model of $y^2 = f(x)$ with $f = x(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)(x-4)(x-t)$, for a suitable $t \in \mathbb{Q} \setminus \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$. Its automorphism group over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ is $\{1, \iota\}$ [@poonen2000hyperelliptic, Theorem 1, p. 1, and §2, Case II, p. 2]. Put $X = C_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}$. As $f$ has six distinct roots, it is not a square, and $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(X) = \bar{\mathbb{Q}}(x)[y]/(y^2 - f)$ has degree $2$ over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(x)$. Nonsingular projective curves over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ with nonconstant morphisms are equivalent to their function fields [@stacks2026, Theorem 53.2.6, Tag 0BY1]. So $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(x) \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}(X)$ gives a morphism $h : X \to \mathbb{P}^1$ of degree $2$, and the $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(x)$-automorphism $y \mapsto -y$ gives a nontrivial automorphism of $X$ over $\mathbb{P}^1$, defined over $\mathbb{Q}$. It is $\iota$, so $h\iota = h$.

*Fundamental groups.* For a closed point $v$ of $X$, let $F_v$ be the fibre functor at $v$ on finite étale covers of $X$, and $\pi_1(X, v) = \mathrm{Aut}(F_v)$. Then $F_v$ is an equivalence onto finite sets with continuous $\pi_1(X, v)$-action, connected covers correspond to transitive sets, and a connected cover with a point of $F_v$ corresponds to the stabilizer of that point. For closed points $v, v'$, the paths $\pi_1(X; v, v')$ are the isomorphisms $F_v \to F_{v'}$ [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. V, §7, pp. 115–116]. If $\iota(v) = v$, write $\iota^\bullet$ for pullback along $\iota$. Then $F_v(\iota^\bullet Y) = F_v(Y)$, since both are the points of $Y$ over $v$. This is the identification $F_v \cong F_v \circ \iota^\bullet$ that defines $u_v = \pi_1(\iota; v) \in \mathrm{Aut}(\pi_1(X, v))$, namely $u_v(\gamma)_Y = \gamma_{\iota^\bullet Y}$ [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. V, §7, p. 116]. As $\iota^2 = 1$, $u_v^2 = 1$.

Fix a closed point $w_1$ with $\iota(w_1) = w_1$, which exists by Step 1 below. Put $\Delta = \pi_1(X, w_1)$ and $u = u_{w_1}$. Then $\Delta \cong \Pi$ ([geometric fundamental groups of curves](geometric-fundamental-groups-of-curves.md), part 4). Fix such an isomorphism, and use it to identify $A$ with $\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)$. By functoriality of $\pi_1$ for the square formed by $\iota$ on $X$ and on $C$, the class of $u$ in $\mathrm{Out}(\Delta)$ is the class $\iota_*$ of [the slimness entry](outer-automorphism-groups-of-profinite-surface-groups-are-slim.md), and it is nontrivial by part 1 there. Let $E \subseteq A$ be the inverse image of the subgroup $\{1, \iota_*\}$ of $\mathrm{Out}(\Delta)$. So $E = \Delta \cup \Delta u$ and $(E : \Delta) = 2$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Weierstrass involutions.* $\iota$ has exactly six fixed points $w_1, \dots, w_6$ on $X$. For each $j$ there is $t_j \in E \setminus \Delta$ with $t_j^2 = 1$: the transport of $u_{w_j}$ to $w_1$ along a path. For $j = 1$, $t_1 = u$.
2. *Subgroups of index $2$.* Every closed subgroup $N$ of index $2$ in $E$ with $N \ne \Delta$ contains some $t_j$. So $\Delta$ is the only torsion-free closed subgroup of index $2$ in $E$.
3. *No open meeting.* If $N \ne \Pi$ is a closed normal subgroup of $A$ isomorphic to $\Pi$, then $N \cap \Pi$ has infinite index in both $\Pi$ and $N$ and is not topologically finitely generated. With part 3 of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-profinite-surface-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), this holds in every genus $g \ge 2$. So $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0}$ is the only closed normal subgroup of its automorphism group isomorphic to it exactly when no rival copy of type (b) exists.

No source stating these results was opened. Part 3 does not use, or decide, whether $\iota_*$ is central in $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi)$.

**Step 0: double covers.** Let $\varphi : Y \to Z$ be a morphism of degree $2$ of nonsingular projective curves over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, and $\sigma \ne 1$ an automorphism of $Y$ with $\varphi\sigma = \varphi$. Then a closed point $P$ of $Y$ has ramification index $e_P = 2$ if $\sigma(P) = P$, and $e_P = 1$ otherwise.

*Proof.* $\varphi$ is proper, as $Y$ is proper and $Z$ separated, and its fibres are finite, as $\varphi$ is nonconstant and $Y$ is an irreducible curve. So $\varphi$ is finite [@stacks2026, Lemma 30.21.1, Tag 02OG]. Let $Q = \varphi(P)$, $R = \mathcal{O}_{Z,Q}$, $K = \bar{\mathbb{Q}}(Z)$ and $L = \bar{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)$. The stalk $B$ of $\varphi_*\mathcal{O}_Y$ at $Q$ is finite over $R$, and normal with fraction field $L$, so it is the integral closure of $R$ in $L$. Its maximal ideals are the points of $\varphi^{-1}(Q)$, with localizations their local rings. Now $\sigma^*$ is a $K$-automorphism of $L$, nontrivial by Tag 0BY1, and $[L : K] = 2$. So $L/K$ is Galois with group $\{1, \sigma^*\}$. This group acts transitively on the maximal ideals of $B$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 15.114.1, Tag 09EA], and $2 = [L : K] = \sum_i e_i f_i$ over them [@stacks2026, Remark 15.113.6, Tag 09E8], where every $f_i = 1$ as the residue fields are $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$. So $\sigma$ acts transitively on $\varphi^{-1}(Q)$, which has one or two points. If $\sigma(P) = P$, then $\varphi^{-1}(Q) = \{P\}$ and $e_P = 2$. Otherwise $\varphi^{-1}(Q) = \{P, \sigma(P)\}$ and $e_P = 1$. $\square$

**Step 1: fixed points of $\iota$.** By Riemann–Hurwitz for $h$, $2 \cdot 2 - 2 = 2(0 - 2) + \sum_x d_x$, where $d_x = e_x - 1$ because every residue field is $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, of characteristic $0$, so $h$ is tamely ramified at $x$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 53.12.4, Tag 0C1F]. So exactly six points have $e_x = 2$, and by Step 0 they are the fixed points of $\iota$.

**Step 2: the involutions $t_j$.** For each $j$, choose a path $p_j \in \pi_1(X; w_1, w_j)$, with $p_1$ the identity. Define $\iota(p_j) \in \pi_1(X; w_1, w_j)$ by $\iota(p_j)_Y = (p_j)_{\iota^\bullet Y}$, using $F_v(\iota^\bullet Y) = F_v(Y)$ at $v = w_1, w_j$. Put
$$t_j(\alpha) = p_j^{-1}\, u_{w_j}(p_j \alpha p_j^{-1})\, p_j \qquad (\alpha \in \Delta).$$
Then $t_j$ is conjugate to $u_{w_j}$, so $t_j^2 = 1$. For every cover $Y$,
$$u_{w_j}(p_j\alpha p_j^{-1})_Y = (p_j)_{\iota^\bullet Y}\, \alpha_{\iota^\bullet Y}\, (p_j)_{\iota^\bullet Y}^{-1} = \iota(p_j)_Y\, u(\alpha)_Y\, \iota(p_j)_Y^{-1}.$$
So $t_j = c_{s_j} \circ u$ with $s_j = p_j^{-1}\iota(p_j) \in \Delta$. Hence $t_j \in \Delta u = E \setminus \Delta$, and $t_j \ne 1$. This proves part 1.

**Step 3: lifting $\iota$.** Let $M \subseteq \Delta$ be an open subgroup of index $2$, hence normal, with $u(M) = M$. Let $\varphi : Y \to X$ be the corresponding connected finite étale cover, $b \in F_{w_1}(Y)$ a point with stabilizer $M$, and $\delta$ the nontrivial automorphism of $Y$ over $X$. Its existence and uniqueness hold because the equivariant automorphisms of $F_{w_1}(Y) \cong \Delta/M$ form a group of order $2$, as $M$ is normal of index $2$.

(a) *There is an automorphism $\lambda \ne 1$ of $Y$ with $\varphi\lambda = \iota\varphi$, $\lambda(b) = b$, $\lambda^2 = 1$ and $\lambda\delta = \delta\lambda$.* By the definition of $u$, each $\gamma \in \Delta$ acts on $F_{w_1}(\iota^\bullet Y) = F_{w_1}(Y)$ as $u(\gamma)$ acts on $F_{w_1}(Y)$. As $u(M) \subseteq M$, the map $\gamma b \mapsto u(\gamma) b$ from $F_{w_1}(Y)$ to $F_{w_1}(\iota^\bullet Y)$ is well defined and equivariant. By the equivalence, it is $F_{w_1}(\Lambda)$ for a morphism $\Lambda : Y \to \iota^\bullet Y = Y \times_{X, \iota} X$ over $X$. Put $\lambda = \mathrm{pr}_1 \circ \Lambda$. Then $\varphi\lambda = \iota \circ \mathrm{pr}_2 \circ \Lambda = \iota\varphi$. At every fixed point $v$ of $\iota$, $\lambda$ restricted to $\varphi^{-1}(v)$ is $F_v(\Lambda)$, by the identification above, so $\lambda(b) = b$. Next, $\lambda^2$ is a morphism over $X$, as $\iota^2 = 1$, and it fixes $b$. On the transitive $\Delta$-set $F_{w_1}(Y)$ it induces an equivariant map fixing $b$, which is the identity, so $\lambda^2 = 1$ by the equivalence. Also $\lambda \ne 1$, since $\iota\varphi \ne \varphi$, as $\varphi$ is surjective and $\iota \ne 1$. Finally, $\lambda\delta\lambda^{-1}$ lies over $X$, because $\varphi\lambda^{-1} = \varphi\lambda = \iota\varphi$, and it is not $1$, as $\delta \ne 1$. So it is $\delta$.

(b) *For each $j$, $\lambda$ fixes both points of $\varphi^{-1}(w_j)$ if and only if $s_j \in M$.* Put $z_j = (p_j)_Y(b) \in \varphi^{-1}(w_j)$. Naturality of $p_j$ with respect to $\Lambda$ gives $F_{w_j}(\Lambda) \circ (p_j)_Y = (p_j)_{\iota^\bullet Y} \circ F_{w_1}(\Lambda)$. At $b$, this reads $\lambda(z_j) = \iota(p_j)_Y(b)$. So
$$s_j \cdot b = (p_j)_Y^{-1}\big(\iota(p_j)_Y(b)\big) = (p_j)_Y^{-1}(\lambda(z_j)),$$
and $s_j \in M$ exactly when $\lambda(z_j) = z_j$. The fibre $\varphi^{-1}(w_j)$ is $\{z_j, \delta z_j\}$, and $\lambda(\delta z_j) = \delta\lambda(z_j)$. So $\lambda$ fixes $z_j$ exactly when it fixes both points.

**Step 4: proof of part 2.** Let $N \ne \Delta$ be a closed subgroup of index $2$ in $E$, and $M = N \cap \Delta$. Both $N$ and $\Delta$ are normal in $E$, so $M$ is normal in $E$. As $N \ne \Delta$ have the same index, $N\Delta = E$, so $\Delta/M \cong E/N$ has order $2$, and $M$ is open in $\Delta$. In particular $u(M) = M$, as $u c_x u^{-1} = c_{u(x)}$. The group $E/M$ has order $4$, and its distinct subgroups $\Delta/M$ and $N/M$ of order $2$ cover three of its elements. Suppose that no $t_j$ lies in $N$. As $t_j \notin \Delta$, every $t_jM$ is the fourth element, so $t_1^{-1}t_j \in M$ and $c_{s_j} = t_jt_1^{-1} \in M$, that is $s_j \in M$. By Step 3, $\lambda$ fixes the $12$ points of $\varphi^{-1}(\{w_1, \dots, w_6\})$.

This is impossible. The curve $Y$ is nonsingular, projective and connected, and Riemann–Hurwitz for the étale map $\varphi$, where every $d_P$ is the length of $(\Omega_{Y/X})_P = 0$, gives $2g_Y - 2 = 2(2 \cdot 2 - 2)$, so $g_Y = 3$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 53.12.4, Tag 0C1F]. Let $L = \bar{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)$. By Tag 0BY1, $\lambda^*$ is an automorphism of $L$ of order $2$, so its fixed field $K$ has $[L : K] = 2$. Then $K$ has transcendence degree $1$, and it is finitely generated over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, since it lies between $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(s)$ and $L$ for any $s \in K \setminus \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, and $L$ is finite over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}(s)$. By Tag 0BY1 there is a nonsingular projective curve $Z$ with function field $K$, and a morphism $\psi : Y \to Z$ of degree $2$ with $\psi\lambda = \psi$. By Step 0, applied to $\psi$ and $\lambda$, the $12$ fixed points have $e_P = 2$. By Riemann–Hurwitz, with $d_P \ge e_P - 1$ [@stacks2026, Lemma 53.12.4, Tag 0C1F],
$$4 = 2g_Y - 2 \ge 2(2g_Z - 2) + 12,$$
so $g_Z \le -1$, a contradiction. Hence $N$ contains some $t_j$.

Each $t_j$ has order $2$. The geometric fundamental group of a hyperbolic curve over a field of characteristic $0$ is torsion-free [@hoshi2014polycurves, Proposition 2.4 (i) and (iii), pp. 169–170], as recorded in [hyperbolic polycurve](hyperbolic-polycurve.md), so $\Delta$ is torsion-free. This proves part 2. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Let $N$ be such a subgroup and $M = N \cap \Pi$. By part 2 of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-profinite-surface-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), either the claim holds or $M$ is open in both. In the second case, part 4 there gives $(\Pi : M) = (N : M) = 2$, with $N\Pi/\Pi$ generated by the image of $\iota_*$, for the curve $C$ and the isomorphism $\Delta \cong \Pi$ fixed above. So $N\Pi = E$, and $(E : N) = (\Pi : M) = 2$ with $N \ne \Pi$. By part 2, $N$ contains an element of order $2$. But $N \cong \Pi$ is torsion-free. So the second case does not occur. For $g \ge 3$, part 3 there gives the claim. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Heuristic picture, not used here.* Topologically, $E$ should be the profinite completion of the orbifold fundamental group of the sphere with six cone points of order $2$, and the $t_j$ the images of its standard generators. Step 3 replaces that description with a fixed-point count.
- *What remains.* Case (b) of the dichotomy is untouched in every genus. So [Tsujimura's question](profinite-surface-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md) stays open.
