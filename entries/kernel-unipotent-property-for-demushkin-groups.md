**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $G$ a pro-$p$ group, with $p$-Zassenhaus filtration $G_{(n)}$ as in [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md), and let $U_n(\mathbb F_p)$ be the upper unitriangular $n\times n$ matrices. $G$ has the *kernel $n$-unipotent property* if
$$G_{(n)}=\bigcap_{\rho:G\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)}\ker\rho,$$
over all continuous homomorphisms [@minactan2015kernel, Definition 1.2, p. 2]. The inclusion $\subseteq$ holds for every pro-$p$ group [@minactan2015kernel, Lemma 2.5(b), p. 6].

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $G$ be a [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $d\ge3$.

1. If $m\ge1$ and $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$, there is a continuous homomorphism $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ with $\rho(g)\ne1$. Its superdiagonal entries $a_j=\rho(\cdot)_{j,j+1}\in H^1(G,\mathbb F_p)$ can be taken with $a_j\cup a_{j+1}=0$ for all $j$.
2. $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property for every $n\ge1$.

**Corollary.** Every Demushkin group has the kernel $n$-unipotent property for every $n$: rank $2$ is due to Mináč and Tân, for every $p$ including $2$ [@minactan2015kernel, Proposition 5.4 and the paragraph opening its subsection, pp. 13–14], and the only Demushkin group of rank $1$ is $\mathbb Z/2$ [@minactan2015kernel, Remark 5.5, p. 14]. So the [kernel-unipotent conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md) holds, for every $n$, for every field $F$ containing a primitive $p$-th root of unity whose maximal pro-$p$ Galois group $G_F(p)$ is a Demushkin group. In particular it holds for every finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb Q_p$ with $\zeta_p\in K$: there $G_K(p)$ is a Demushkin group of rank $[K:\mathbb Q_p]+2\ge3$ ([maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md)), so part 2 applies directly.

**Proof.** Write $H^i(G)=H^i(G,\mathbb F_p)$, with trivial coefficients.

*Notation.* $G$ has a minimal presentation $G=S/R$, with $S$ free pro-$p$ on $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ and $R\subseteq S_{(2)}$ the closed normal subgroup generated by one element $r$, because the number of defining relations of a minimal presentation is $\dim H^2(G)=1$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, §4, (4.2) and the sentence after it, p. 9]. The Magnus map $x_i\mapsto1+X_i$ identifies $\mathbb F_p[[S]]$ with $\mathbb F_p\langle\langle X_1,\ldots,X_d\rangle\rangle$, and $S_{(n)}=\{s\in S:s-1\in I(X)^n\}$, where $I(X)^n$ consists of the series with no terms of degree below $n$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, (3.3) and the display after it, p. 8]. Let $T=\mathbb F_p\langle X_1,\ldots,X_d\rangle$, with homogeneous parts $T_m$, and $V=T_1$. Write $\bar s$ for the image in $G$ of $s\in S$. Identify $H^1(G)=\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb F_p)$ with $U:=V^*$ by $a(X_i)=a(\bar x_i)$; this is bijective because $H^1(G)\cong(G/G_{(2)})^*$ for a minimal presentation [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, (4.2), p. 9]. Fix an isomorphism $H^2(G)\cong\mathbb F_p$ and let $\omega(a,b)=a\cup b$. It is nondegenerate because $G$ is Demushkin, and $\omega(b,a)=-\omega(a,b)$ by graded commutativity [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, p. 10]. It satisfies hypothesis (H) of [the chain theorem](isotropic-chains-detect-the-quadratic-algebra-of-a-nondegenerate-form.md): for odd $p$ it is alternating, and for $p=2$ it is symmetric over $\mathbb F_2$ with $d\ge3$. Let $\xi\in T_2$ be its tensor, and use the notation of that entry: chains, $C_m$, and $(\xi)_m$. A chain is a sequence of classes with zero adjacent cup products.

*Step 1: chains give representations.* Let $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ be a chain. Then there is a continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ with $\rho(g)_{j,j+1}=a_j(g)$. Use the standard conventions $(\partial f)(g,h)=f(g)+f(h)-f(gh)$ and $(f\cup f')(g,h)=f(g)f'(h)$ for $1$-cochains. Put $c_j=-a_j$; adjacent cup products of the $c_j$ vanish. A *full system* is a family of $1$-cochains $c_{ij}$, $1\le i<j\le m+1$, with $c_{i,i+1}=c_i$ and $\partial c_{ij}=\sum_{i<l<j}c_{il}\cup c_{lj}$. For $m\ge3$ one exists by part 1 of [strong Massey vanishing for Demushkin groups](strong-massey-vanishing-for-demushkin-groups-and-p-adic-fields.md); its proof uses only the Leibniz rule $\partial(x\cup y)=\partial x\cup y-x\cup\partial y$ for $1$-cochains $x$, associativity, and that $1$-cocycles are homomorphisms, all valid with these conventions. For $m=2$ choose $c_{13}$ with $\partial c_{13}=c_1\cup c_2$, which is possible as the class is $0$; for $m=1$ there is nothing to choose. Set $\rho(g)_{ii}=1$ and $\rho(g)_{ij}=-c_{ij}(g)$ for $i<j$. Then for $i<j$
$$\rho(gh)_{ij}=-c_{ij}(g)-c_{ij}(h)+(\partial c_{ij})(g,h)=-c_{ij}(g)-c_{ij}(h)+\sum_{i<l<j}c_{il}(g)c_{lj}(h)=(\rho(g)\rho(h))_{ij},$$
so $\rho$ is a homomorphism, continuous as the cochains are, with $\rho(g)_{j,j+1}=-c_j(g)=a_j(g)$.

*Step 2: a finite quotient.* Fix $m\ge1$. A continuous surjection of compact groups maps the closed subgroup generated by a set onto the closed subgroup generated by its image, so, by induction on $n$, surjections of pro-$p$ groups map Zassenhaus filtrations onto Zassenhaus filtrations. Each $S_{(k)}/S_{(k+1)}$ embeds in $T_k$ by $s\mapsto$ the degree-$k$ part of $s-1$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, p. 8], so it is finite, and so is its image $G_{(k)}/G_{(k+1)}$. Hence $P=G/G_{(m+1)}$ is a finite $p$-group, with $P_{(k)}=G_{(k)}/G_{(m+1)}$ for $k\le m+1$; write $\bar g$ for the image of $g\in G$ in $P$, and $\bar x_i$ also for the images in $P$ of the generators. Every continuous homomorphism $G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ kills $G_{(m+1)}$ [@minactan2015kernel, Lemma 2.5(b), p. 6], so factors through $P$. Let $I$ be the augmentation ideal of the group algebra $\mathbb F_p[P]$. By Jennings' theorem, if $y\in P_{(k)}\setminus P_{(k+1)}$ then $y-1\in I^k\setminus I^{k+1}$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 3.4, p. 9].

Every element of the finite group $P$ is a product of the generators $\bar x_i$, without inverses, and $y\bar x_i-1=y(\bar x_i-1)+(y-1)$. By induction on the length of the product, $I=\sum_i\mathbb F_p[P](\bar x_i-1)$. As $\mathbb F_p[P]=\mathbb F_p+I$, this gives $I\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{\bar x_i-1\}+I^2$, and then $I^k\subseteq\mathrm{span}\{(\bar x_{i_1}-1)\cdots(\bar x_{i_k}-1)\}+I^{k+1}$. So the algebra homomorphism
$$\mu:T\to\bigoplus_{k\ge0}I^k/I^{k+1},\qquad X_i\mapsto(\bar x_i-1)+I^2,$$
is onto. Its kernel $K$ is a two-sided ideal; put $K_k=K\cap T_k$.

*Step 3: the corner entry.* Let $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ be continuous, with superdiagonal entries $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in U$, and let $\tilde\rho:\mathbb F_p[P]\to M_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$ be the linear extension of the induced homomorphism $P\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$. Then $\tilde\rho(I)$ lies in the strictly upper triangular matrices $\mathfrak n$, so $\tilde\rho(I^{m+1})=0$ and $\tilde\rho(I^m)\subseteq\mathfrak n^m$, the matrices supported on the entry $(1,m+1)$. The $(1,m+1)$ entry of a product of $m$ strictly upper triangular matrices of size $m+1$ is the product of their entries in places $(1,2),(2,3),\ldots,(m,m+1)$. Hence the $(1,m+1)$ entry of $\tilde\rho\bigl((\bar x_{i_1}-1)\cdots(\bar x_{i_m}-1)\bigr)$ is $\prod_ja_j(\bar x_{i_j})=\langle X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_m},a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m\rangle$. So the $(1,m+1)$ entry of $\tilde\rho$ defines a functional on $I^m/I^{m+1}$ whose composite with $\mu$ is $t\mapsto\langle t,a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m\rangle$. In particular this pairing vanishes on $K_m$, and by Step 1, $C_m\subseteq K_m^\perp$. The same holds with $m$ replaced by any $k\le m$, using $\rho:G\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F_p)$, which also factors through $P$.

*Step 4: $K_2=\mathbb F_p\xi$, for $m\ge2$.* By Step 3 with $k=2$, $C_2\subseteq K_2^\perp$. The cup product $H^1(G)\otimes H^1(G)\to H^2(G)$ is onto, being nonzero with $\dim H^2(G)=1$, so the initial form of $r$ has degree $2$ [@minacpasiniquadrellitan2022mild, Proposition 4.1, p. 10]: $r-1=\rho_r+f$ with $0\ne\rho_r\in T_2$ and $f\in I(X)^3$. The continuous algebra homomorphism $\mathbb F_p[[S]]\to\mathbb F_p[P]$ induced by $S\to P$ sends $X_i$ to $\bar x_i-1$ and $r-1$ to $0$, and it sends $I(X)^3=\sum_{|w|=3}w\,\mathbb F_p\langle\langle X\rangle\rangle$, the sum over words of length $3$, into $I^3$. So $\mu(\rho_r)=0$, and $K_2\ne0$. Thus $\dim K_2^\perp\le d^2-1$. By the chain theorem, part 1 with $m=2$, $C_2=\xi^\perp$, which has dimension $d^2-1$. Hence $K_2^\perp=\xi^\perp$ and $K_2=\mathbb F_p\xi$. As $K$ is an ideal, $(\xi)_k\subseteq K_k$ for all $k$.

*Step 5: conclusion.* Let $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$. Then $\bar g\in P_{(m)}\setminus P_{(m+1)}$, as $P_{(m+1)}=1$, so $\bar g-1\in I^m\setminus I^{m+1}$ by Step 2. As $\mu$ is onto, there is $t\in T_m$ with $\mu(t)=(\bar g-1)+I^{m+1}\ne0$. So $t\notin K_m$, and $t\notin(\xi)_m$ by Step 4; for $m=1$, $(\xi)_1=0$. By the chain theorem, part 1, some chain $(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ has $\langle t,a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m\rangle\ne0$. Let $\rho$ be the representation of Step 1. Since $\tilde\rho$ kills $I^{m+1}$, Step 3 shows that the $(1,m+1)$ entry of $\rho(g)-1=\tilde\rho(\bar g-1)$ is $\langle t,a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_m\rangle\ne0$. So $\rho(g)\ne1$, proving part 1.

For part 2, let $g\notin G_{(n)}$, and let $m$ be largest with $g\in G_{(m)}$; then $1\le m<n$. Compose the representation of part 1 with the block embedding $U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)\to U_n(\mathbb F_p)$ in the upper left corner. With the inclusion $\subseteq$ from the setting, this proves part 2. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The graded group algebra.* Steps 3 and 4 give $(\xi)_m\subseteq K_m\subseteq C_m^\perp$, and the chain theorem gives $C_m^\perp=(\xi)_m$. So $\mu$ induces $T_m/(\xi)_m\cong I^m/I^{m+1}$ for $\mathbb F_p[G/G_{(m+1)}]$. By Step 1 of the chain entry, its dimension is the coefficient of $t^m$ in $1/(1-dt+t^2)$. This matches the Hilbert series that Mináč, Rogelstad and Tân state for $\mathrm{gr}\,\mathbb F_p[[G]]$, citing Labute [@minacrogelstadtan2015dimensions, §5, p. 20].
- *Where rank $3$ is used.* Only in the chain theorem, which fails over $\mathbb F_p$ for rank $2$ (its entry, "Rank two fails over finite fields"). So this argument does not cover rank $2$. Mináč and Tân's proof in rank $2$ uses the explicit structure of $G/G_{(n)}$ for those groups [@minactan2015kernel, Lemma 5.3 and Proposition 5.4, p. 14].

**Sources and status.** Mináč and Tân proved the rank-$2$ case and announced the cases $n=3,4$ for every Demushkin group, for a paper then in preparation [@minactan2015kernel, §1, p. 3]. A 2025 preprint cites, for Demushkin groups, only the rank-$2$ case and the case $n=4$ for finitely generated Demushkin groups [@minactantra2025, §1]. The case $n=3$ for every field, due to Efrat, is recorded in [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md). The theorem above, for rank at least $3$ and every $n$, is proved here from the cited inputs; I found no source stating it, in a search through November 2026 that was not exhaustive. Strong Massey vanishing for Demushkin groups, used in Step 1, is due to Pál and Szabó, as recorded in its entry.
