**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md), with its lower central input, its multigrading (M1), (M2), its Johnson maps $\mathcal J_n$, and, for $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, the set $\mathcal S$ of its Lemma 5 and the reductions (i')–(iv'). So $p$ is odd, $G=G_K$, $P$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$ with augmentation $\epsilon$, $P_j$ is the closed lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$, $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$, $V=H/pH=L\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d$ with $L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$ and $V_k=\Lambda\bar x_k$, $\omega$ is the action on $\mu_p$, $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, $\gamma\in\Gamma$ has $\omega(\gamma)=1$ and generates a closed subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$, and $\lambda_0=\gamma-1$, so that $\lambda_0^*=\gamma^{-1}-1$.

Here $d=[K:\mathbb Q_p]\in\{3,4\}$, so $r=1$. The single surface pair $(a,b)$ is $(x_2,x_3)$ for $d=3$ and $(x_3,x_4)$ for $d=4$. Write $\bar a=\bar x_a$, $\bar b=\bar x_b$, $S=V_a\oplus V_b$, $C_V=V_1$ for $d=3$ and $V_1\oplus V_2$ for $d=4$, and $A=L\oplus C_V$, so $V=A\oplus S$.

A tensor in $V^{\hat\otimes n}$ has a component for each choice of summands $(W_1,\dots,W_n)$, $W_t\in\{L,V_1,\dots,V_d\}$, which we call its *pattern*. If $T$ is the set of slots $t$ with $W_t\ne L$, the component has a coordinate in $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^T]]=\Lambda^{\hat\otimes|T|}$. On it, $\rho\in\Gamma$ acts by $\omega(\rho)^{n-|T|}$ times left multiplication by $\rho$ in every coordinate. A $\Lambda$-linear map $B$ of $V$ with $B\bar x_0=\bar x_0$ and $B(\mu\bar x_l)=\sum_{j\ge1}\mu\beta_{jl}\bar x_j$ for $l\ge1$ sends the component with pattern $(W'_t)$ into the patterns obtained by replacing each $W'_t=V_l$ by some $V_j$ with $\beta_{jl}\ne0$, and it multiplies the coordinate on the right by $\beta_{jl}$ in that slot. Put
$$\mathrm{ad}_0^{n-1}(v)=[\cdots[[v,\bar x_0],\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]\qquad(n-1\text{ copies of }\bar x_0).$$

**Lemma A (translations; proved here).** Let $T$ be a finite nonempty set, $\gamma_1\in\Gamma$ generate an infinite pro-$p$ closed subgroup, $z\in\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^T]]$, $Q\subseteq T$ and $c\in T\setminus Q$. Suppose that left multiplication by $\gamma_1$ in every coordinate has the same effect on $z$ as right multiplication by $\gamma_1$ in every coordinate in $Q$. Then $z=0$. For $Q=\emptyset$: if $z$ is fixed by left multiplication by $\gamma_1$ in every coordinate, then $z=0$.

*Proof.* A homeomorphism $\varphi$ of $\Gamma^T$ that, together with its inverse, descends to $(\Gamma/U)^T$ for every open normal $U$ induces a continuous linear automorphism $\varphi_*$ of $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^T]]=\varprojlim\mathbb F_p[(\Gamma/U)^T]$. Left and right multiplications in coordinates are of this form. The hypothesis says $\varphi_*z=z$ for $\varphi(g)_t=\gamma_1g_t\gamma_1^{-1}$ ($t\in Q$) and $\varphi(g)_t=\gamma_1g_t$ ($t\notin Q$). Let $h(g)_c=g_c$, $h(g)_t=g_c^{-1}g_tg_c$ for $t\in Q$, and $h(g)_t=g_c^{-1}g_t$ for the other $t$. Its inverse is $g_c=h_c$, $g_t=h_ch_th_c^{-1}$, $g_t=h_ch_t$, and both descend. Then $h(\varphi(g))_c=\gamma_1g_c$, and for $t\in Q$, $h(\varphi(g))_t=(\gamma_1g_c)^{-1}\gamma_1g_t\gamma_1^{-1}(\gamma_1g_c)=g_c^{-1}g_tg_c$. For the other $t\ne c$, $h(\varphi(g))_t=(\gamma_1g_c)^{-1}\gamma_1g_t=g_c^{-1}g_t$. So $h\varphi=l_ch$, with $l_c$ left multiplication by $\gamma_1$ in the coordinate $c$, and $h_*z$ is fixed by $(l_c)_*$. Let $\iota$ invert the coordinate $c$; then $\iota l_c\iota$ is right multiplication by $\gamma_1^{-1}$ in that coordinate. So $\iota_*h_*z$ is killed by right multiplication by $\gamma_1^{-1}-1$ in one coordinate, and it is $0$ by Lemma 1 (a) of the higher Johnson theorem, applied to $\gamma_1^{-1}$. Hence $z=0$. $\square$

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\in\{3,4\}$, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $n\ge2$. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ and induce the identity on $G/P_n$. Then $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$ for every $x\in P$, that is, $\mathcal J_n(\alpha)=0$.

There is no condition on $e=[K(\mu_p):K]$. For degree at least $5$ the same conclusion is [the higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md) when $e\nmid n-1$, and Corollary 2 of [the Jannsen–Wingberg relator entry](the-jannsen-wingberg-relator-constrains-wild-cocycle-classes-and-johnson-maps.md) when $e\mid n-1$. So it holds for every finite $K/\mathbb Q_p$ of degree at least $3$ with $p$ odd and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, and every $n\ge2$. For $n=2$ it is the theorem of [the degree-one Johnson theorem in degrees three and four](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-in-degrees-three-and-four.md).

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$ and $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$. Every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha_2$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_2$ that induces the identity on $G/P_3$ and on $P/P_4$. Two such $\alpha_2$ differ by $c_k$ with $k\in P_3$.

**Proof of the theorem.** By the saturation argument at the start of the proof of the higher Johnson theorem, it suffices to show $\bar J=0$ for every $J\in\mathcal S$. Fix $J\in\mathcal S$. By (i'), every step below also applies to $\mathcal L_n(B)\bar JB^{-1}$ for every tame-trivial automorphism with action $B$ on $V$.

*1. The unit $\gamma$ on the pair.* Let $\theta_\gamma$ be the automorphism of part 2 of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) for the unit $\gamma$ and the pair $(a,b)$. As in step 1 of the higher Johnson theorem, it is tame-trivial, and by part 3 there it acts on $V$ by $B_\gamma$: $\mu\bar a\mapsto\mu\gamma\bar a$, $\mu\bar b\mapsto\mu(\gamma^*)^{-1}\bar b=\mu\gamma\bar b$, and the identity on $A$. By (ii') there is $N\ge1$ with $\mathcal L_n(B_\gamma^N)\bar J=\bar JB_\gamma^N$. Put $\gamma_1=\gamma^N$, which generates an infinite pro-$p$ closed subgroup. The map $\mathcal L_n(B_\gamma^N)$ preserves every component and multiplies its coordinate on the right by $\gamma_1$ in the slots in $S$.

- (1a) For $\bar x_c=\bar x_0$ and for every complementary $\bar x_c$, $\bar J(\bar x_c)\in\mathcal L_n(A)$. This is (1) of the higher Johnson theorem: $B_\gamma^N\bar x_c=\bar x_c$, so every component of $\bar J(\bar x_c)$ with a slot in $S$ is fixed by right multiplication by $\gamma_1$ in those coordinates, and vanishes by Lemma 1 (b) there; then use (M1).
- (1b) For $k\in\{a,b\}$, $\bar J(\bar x_k)\in\mathcal L_n(L\oplus S)$. Indeed $B_\gamma^N\bar x_k=\gamma_1\bar x_k$, so $\mathcal L_n(B_\gamma^N)\bar J(\bar x_k)=\bar J(\gamma_1\bar x_k)=\gamma_1\cdot\bar J(\bar x_k)$, as $\bar J$ is $\Lambda$-linear. Take a component of $\bar J(\bar x_k)$ with a slot $c$ in $C_V$. Let $T$ be its set of slots not in $L$, and $Q\subseteq T$ those in $S$. Then $\gamma_1$ acts on its coordinate by left multiplication in every coordinate, as $\omega(\gamma_1)=1$. So Lemma A, with $c\in T\setminus Q$, shows that the component vanishes. Then use (M1).

*2. No letter $\bar b$ in $\bar J(\bar a)$.* We show $\bar J(\bar a)\in\mathcal L_n(L\oplus V_a)$. Let $\theta$ be the automorphism of part 2 ($d=4$) or part 3 ($d=3$) of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), with $\lambda=\lambda_0$ or $\nu=\lambda_0$. It is tame-trivial with $\Lambda$-linear action $B$, which fixes $\bar x_0$ and $\bar a$ and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda_0\bar a$. Here $\bar a=\bar x_3$ for $d=4$ and $\bar x_2$ for $d=3$. So $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\lambda_0\bar a$. Put $\bar J'=\mathcal L_n(B)\bar JB^{-1}$ and $Y=\lambda_0\cdot\bar J(\bar a)$. Then
$$\bar J'(\bar x_1)=\mathcal L_n(B)\bar J(\bar x_1)-\mathcal L_n(B)Y,\tag{1}$$
and $\bar J'(\bar x_1)\in\mathcal L_n(A)$ by (1a) for $\bar J'$. By (1a), $\bar J(\bar x_1)\in\mathcal L_n(A)$, and by (1b), $Y\in\mathcal L_n(L\oplus S)$, as $\mathcal L_n(L\oplus S)$ is $\Gamma$-stable. For a pattern $\wp$, write $Y_\wp$ for the coordinate of the $\wp$-component of $Y$.

- *$d=4$.* $B$ maps $L,V_1,V_2,V_3$ into $L$, $V_1\oplus V_3$, $V_2$, $V_3$, and $\mu\bar x_4\mapsto\mu\bar x_4-\mu\lambda_0^*\bar x_2$. Let $\wp$ be a pattern with entries in $\{L,V_3,V_4\}$ and at least one $V_4$. No component of $\mathcal L_n(B)\bar J(\bar x_1)$ has a slot in $V_4$. A component of $Y$ reaches the pattern $\wp$ under $\mathcal L_n(B)$ only if its own pattern is $\wp$, and then with its coordinate unchanged, since the $V_4$-part of $B(\mu\bar x_4)$ is $\mu\bar x_4$. So the $\wp$-component of (1) reads $0=-Y_\wp$, as $\wp$ has a slot in $S$.
- *$d=3$.* $B$ maps $L,V_1,V_2$ into $L$, $V_1\oplus V_2$, $V_2$, and $\mu\bar x_3\mapsto\mu\bar x_3-\mu\lambda_0^*\delta\lambda_0\bar x_2-\mu\lambda_0^*\delta_0\bar x_1$, with $\delta,\delta_0\in\Lambda$ from part 1 there. Let $\wp$ be a pattern with entries in $\{L,V_2,V_3\}$, with $F\ne\emptyset$ its set of slots in $V_3$ and $E$ its set of slots in $V_2$. No component of $\mathcal L_n(B)\bar J(\bar x_1)$ has a slot in $V_3$. For $R\subseteq E$ let $\wp^R$ be $\wp$ with the slots in $R$ changed to $V_3$. The components of $Y$ that reach $\wp$ are those with pattern $\wp^R$, and the contribution is $Y_{\wp^R}$ multiplied on the right by $-\lambda_0^*\delta\lambda_0$ in the slots of $R$. So the $\wp$-component of (1) reads $0=-\sum_{R\subseteq E}r_R(Y_{\wp^R})$, with $r_R$ that right multiplication. By induction on $|E|$, $Y_\wp=0$. Each $\wp^R$ with $R\ne\emptyset$ has the nonempty set $F\cup R$ of slots in $V_3$ and fewer slots in $V_2$, so $Y_{\wp^R}=0$ by induction, and the sum reduces to $Y_\wp$.

In both cases, $Y$ has no component with a slot in $V_b$. Such a component of $Y$ has coordinate $(\gamma-1)$ applied to the coordinate of the same component of $\bar J(\bar a)$, where $\gamma$ acts by left multiplication in every coordinate. So that component of $\bar J(\bar a)$ vanishes by Lemma A with $Q=\emptyset$. With (1b) and (M1), $\bar J(\bar a)\in\mathcal L_n(L\oplus V_a)$.

*3. Kondo's automorphisms.* By step 1 of [the degree-one Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-on-wild-inertia.md), every unipotent integral symplectic matrix of the surface lattice $\mathbb Za\oplus\mathbb Zb$ is the action on the surface classes of an automorphism $\psi$ as in Lemma 5 (iii) of the higher Johnson theorem that fixes $A$. So (iii') holds for the $\Lambda$-linear maps $\bar u:\bar a\mapsto\bar a+\bar b$ and $\bar l:\bar b\mapsto\bar b+\bar a$, each fixing $A$ and the other surface generator. It also holds for $\bar s=\bar u\bar l^{-1}\bar u$, which maps $\bar a\mapsto\bar b$ and $\bar b\mapsto-\bar a$, and fixes $A$. Indeed $\bar u\bar a=\bar a+\bar b$, $\bar l^{-1}(\bar a+\bar b)=\bar b$ and $\bar u\bar b=\bar b$; and $\bar l^{-1}\bar b=\bar b-\bar a$ and $\bar u(\bar b-\bar a)=-\bar a$.

By (iii') for $\bar s$, $\bar J(\bar b)=\mathcal L_n(\bar s)\bar J(\bar a)$. As $\bar s$ maps $\mu\bar a$ to $\mu\bar b$ and fixes $L$, step 2 gives $\bar J(\bar b)\in\mathcal L_n(L\oplus V_b)$. By (iii') for $\bar u$ at $\bar a$,
$$\mathcal L_n(\bar u)\bar J(\bar a)=\bar J(\bar a)+\bar J(\bar b).$$
Take a component of $\bar J(\bar a)$, with set $Q$ of slots in $V_a$, all other slots in $L$, and coordinate $z$. Here $Q\ne\emptyset$, since the projection of $V$ onto $L$ maps $\mathcal L_n(V)$ into $\mathcal L_n(L)=0$, as $n\ge2$ and $\dim L=1$. The map $\mathcal L_n(\bar u)$ sends this component to the sum, over $R\subseteq Q$, of the components with $V_b$ at $R$, $V_a$ at $Q\setminus R$ and $L$ elsewhere, each with coordinate $z$. Different pairs $(Q,R)$ give different patterns. The right side has no component with slots in both $V_a$ and $V_b$. So if $|Q|\ge2$, choosing $\emptyset\ne R\ne Q$ gives $z=0$. Hence every component of $\bar J(\bar a)$ has exactly one slot in $V_a$.

Let $\mu\in\Lambda$ be the coordinate of the component of $\bar J(\bar a)$ with pattern $(V_a,L,\dots,L)$. Let $E=\Lambda\tilde e\oplus A$ and $e_k:E\to V$ be as in step 3 of the higher Johnson theorem, and put $P=\mathrm{ad}_0^{n-1}(\mu\tilde e)\in\mathcal L_n(E)$. It is linear in the sense of Lemma 3 there. By the last assertion of that lemma, its component in $\Lambda\tilde e\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes n-1}$ is $\mu\tilde e\otimes\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}$. The element $\bar J(\bar a)$ lies in the image $\mathcal L_n(V_a\oplus A)$ of the injective map $\mathcal L_n(e_a)$, and its preimage is linear with the same component in $\Lambda\tilde e\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes n-1}$, since all its other slots lie in $L$. So Lemma 3 gives $\bar J(\bar a)=\mathcal L_n(e_a)P=\mathrm{ad}_0^{n-1}(\mu\bar a)$, and then
$$\bar J(\bar x_k)=\mathcal L_n(e_k)P\quad(k\in\{a,b\}),\qquad\text{since }\bar J(\bar b)=\mathcal L_n(\bar s)\,\mathrm{ad}_0^{n-1}(\mu\bar a)=\mathrm{ad}_0^{n-1}(\mu\bar b).\tag{2}$$

*4. The boundary twist.* For $r=1$, (2) is (3) of the higher Johnson theorem. Its step 4 uses only (3), (iv'), the formula for $\delta$ and property (P) of abelianized wild inertia, so it applies verbatim. Its element $\varphi$, the coordinate of the component of $P$ in $\Lambda\tilde e\hat\otimes A^{\hat\otimes n-1}$, is $\mu\otimes\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}$, and its conclusion (4) reads $\mu\otimes\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}=1\otimes\epsilon(\mu)\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}$. So $\mu=c_0\cdot1$ with $c_0=\epsilon(\mu)\in\mathbb F_p$, and
$$\bar J(\bar x_k)=c_0\,\mathrm{ad}_0^{n-1}(\bar x_k)\qquad(k\in\{a,b\}).\tag{3}$$

*5. The constant.* Let $\theta$, $B$, $\bar J'$ and $Y$ be as in step 2, and $\wp_1=(V_a,L,\dots,L)$. The $\wp_1$-component of $\bar J'(\bar x_1)\in\mathcal L_n(A)$ is $0$. As $B$ fixes $\bar a$ and $\bar x_0$, (3) gives $\mathcal L_n(B)Y=Y=c_0\lambda_0\cdot\mathrm{ad}_0^{n-1}(\bar a)$. The $\wp_1$-component of $\mathrm{ad}_0^{n-1}(\bar a)$ is $\bar a\otimes\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}$, with coordinate $1$, and $\gamma$ acts on $\wp_1$-coordinates by left multiplication by $\gamma$. So $Y_{\wp_1}=c_0\lambda_0$. The components of $\bar J(\bar x_1)\in\mathcal L_n(A)$ reach $\wp_1$ under $\mathcal L_n(B)$ only from the pattern $(V_1,L,\dots,L)$. Indeed $B$ fixes $L$, maps $\mu\bar x_1$ to $\mu\bar x_1+\mu\lambda_0\bar a$, and for $d=4$ maps $V_2$ into itself. If $\mu_1$ is the coordinate of that component, the contribution is $\mu_1\lambda_0$. So (1) gives $\mu_1\lambda_0=c_0\lambda_0$. Hence $\mu_1=c_0$, by Lemma 2 of [the central-element construction](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md).

Let $\eta$ be the continuous linear functional on $\mathcal L_n(V)$ that takes the coordinate of the component with pattern $(V_1,L,\dots,L)$ and applies the ring homomorphism $\omega^{2-n}:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$, $\rho\mapsto\omega(\rho)^{2-n}$. As in Corollary 2 of the Jannsen–Wingberg relator entry, $\rho$ acts on that coordinate by $\omega(\rho)^{n-1}$ times left multiplication by $\rho$, so $\eta(\rho m)=\omega(\rho)\eta(m)$. The map $J$ is continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant, so part (a) of the theorem of that entry gives $\eta(\bar J(\bar x_1))=0$. But $\eta(\bar J(\bar x_1))=\omega^{2-n}(\mu_1)=c_0$. So $c_0=0$, and by (3) and $\Lambda$-linearity,
$$\bar J(S)=0.\tag{4}$$

*6. The complementary generators.* Step 6 of the higher Johnson theorem uses only its (1), for $\bar J$ and for the maps $\mathcal L_n(B)\bar JB^{-1}$, together with its (5), which is (4) here, the mixing automorphisms with parameter $\lambda_0$, and $T_1$ for even $d$. Its (1) at generators of $A$ is (1a) here. So step 6 applies verbatim and gives $\bar J(\bar x_c)=0$ for $c=0$ and every complementary $c$. So $\bar J$ vanishes on all generators, hence on $V$. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** For degree at least $5$ this is the corollary of the higher Johnson theorem. Let $d\in\{3,4\}$. By Corollary 2 of the degree-one theorem in degrees three and four, $\alpha=c_g\circ\alpha_2$ with $\alpha_2$ inducing the identity on $G/P_3$. It represents the class of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates. As $e\ge3$, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, and the theorem with $n=3$ gives $\mathcal J_3(\alpha_2)=0$. If $c_k\alpha_2$ is another such representative, then $c_k$ induces the identity on $G/P_3$, so $k\in P_3$ by Lemma 5 (o) of the higher Johnson theorem. Conversely, $c_k$ with $k\in P_3$ preserves both properties, since $[k,x]\in P_4$ for $x\in P$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* With one surface pair, step 1 of the higher Johnson theorem localizes nothing at the surface generators, and Kondo's matrices $M_{ii'}$ are not available. Two observations replace them. First, the unit $\gamma$ on the pair acts on $\bar J(\bar a)$ through the input, as left multiplication in every coordinate, and through the output, as right multiplication in the surface slots. On a component with a complementary slot these translations cannot agree (Lemma A), so $\bar J(\bar a)$ has letters only in $L$ and $S$. Second, conjugating by a mixing automorphism $B$ must keep the value at the complementary generator $\bar x_1$ inside $\mathcal L_n(A)$, and $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\lambda_0\bar a$. Every component of $\lambda_0\cdot\bar J(\bar a)$ with a letter $\bar b$ reaches a pattern that nothing else reaches, or, for $d=3$, only components with fewer letters $\bar a$. Then $\bar u$ leaves a single letter $\bar a$, and the boundary twist makes its coefficient a constant $c_0$. The same mixing automorphism ties $c_0$ to the coordinate of $\bar J(\bar x_1)$ on $(V_1,L,\dots,L)$, and the Jannsen–Wingberg relator kills that coordinate. No condition on $e$ is needed, because the relator replaces the units on a second pair used in step 5 of the higher Johnson theorem. For $n=2$ this is a second proof of the degree-one theorem in degrees three and four.
