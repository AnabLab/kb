**Definition.** Let $S$ be a scheme. Following Hoshi, a scheme $X$ over $S$ is a *hyperbolic curve of type $(g, r)$ over $S$* if there are a scheme $X^{\mathrm{cpt}}$, smooth, proper and geometrically connected of relative dimension one over $S$, and a possibly empty closed subscheme $D \subseteq X^{\mathrm{cpt}}$, finite étale over $S$, such that $2g - 2 + r > 0$, every geometric fibre of $X^{\mathrm{cpt}} \to S$ has genus $g$, $D \to S$ has degree $r$, and $X \cong X^{\mathrm{cpt}} \setminus D$ over $S$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Definition 2.1 (i)]. This is Mochizuki's family of hyperbolic curves, recorded in [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md). A scheme $X$ over $S$ is a *hyperbolic polycurve of relative dimension $n$* if its structure morphism factors as
$$X = X_n \to X_{n-1} \to \cdots \to X_1 \to X_0 = S$$
with each $X_i \to X_{i-1}$ a hyperbolic curve. Such a factorization is a *sequence of parametrizing morphisms*, and it need not be unique [@hoshi2014polycurves, Definition 2.1 (ii)]. If $S$ is a normal, respectively smooth, variety of dimension $m$ over a field of characteristic $0$, then $X$ is a normal, respectively smooth, variety of dimension $n + m$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Remark 2.1.1]. For $S = \mathrm{Spec}\, K$ one speaks of a hyperbolic polycurve of dimension $n$ over $K$.

Carlson uses the same definition [@carlson2026, Definitions 2.1 and 2.2]. He recalls that Grothendieck expected *elementary anabelian varieties*, built by successive smooth fibrations from hyperbolic curves, to be anabelian, and that the name hyperbolic polycurve was suggested by Hoshi [@carlson2026, Introduction, p. 1]. In dimension $2$ these are Mochizuki's *hyperbolically fibred surfaces*. Such a surface is a smooth surface $X$ over $K$ with a hyperbolic curve $X'$ over $K$ and a smooth, proper, connected morphism $\bar X \to X'$ of relative dimension one, such that $X$ is open in $\bar X$, the complement $\bar X \setminus X$ is a divisor étale over $X'$, and the geometric fibres of $X \to X'$ are hyperbolic curves [@mochizuki1999, Definition a2.1, p. 97]. The two notions agree, as checked here. The complement is proper and étale over $X'$, hence finite étale, and since $X'$ is connected, the genus of the fibres and the degree of the complement are constant. Hoshi likewise reads Mochizuki's theorem on these surfaces as the case of dimension $2$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Remark A.1 (iii)].

**Examples.** These are checked here, except where a source is cited.

- *Curves.* For $n = 1$ and $S = \mathrm{Spec}\, K$, the definition gives the hyperbolic curves over $K$ in Mochizuki's sense, which over a field of characteristic $0$ are the hyperbolic curves in the sense of their type.
- *Products.* If $C_1, C_2$ are hyperbolic curves over $K$, with compactifications $\bar C_i$ and cusps $D_i$, then the projection $C_1 \times_K C_2 \to C_1$ is a hyperbolic curve over $C_1$, with compactification $C_1 \times_K \bar C_2$ and divisor $C_1 \times_K D_2$. So $C_1 \times_K C_2$ is a hyperbolic polycurve of dimension $2$, with at least two sequences of parametrizing morphisms, one through each factor. The same holds for products of $n$ hyperbolic curves.
- *Configuration spaces.* Let $C = \bar C \setminus D$ be a hyperbolic curve of type $(g, r)$ over $K$, and let $C_n \subseteq C^n$ be the complement of all diagonals, with $C_0 = \mathrm{Spec}\, K$. The projection $C_n \to C_{n-1}$ forgetting the last point is a hyperbolic curve of type $(g, r + n - 1)$ over $C_{n-1}$. Indeed, take $C_{n-1} \times_K \bar C$ as compactification, and as divisor the union of $C_{n-1} \times_K D$, which is finite étale of degree $r$, and of the graphs $\Gamma_1, \dots, \Gamma_{n-1}$ of the $n - 1$ projections $C_{n-1} \to C \subseteq \bar C$, each a section. On $C_{n-1}$ the points are pairwise distinct and none is a cusp, so these $n$ closed subschemes are pairwise disjoint, and their union is finite étale of degree $r + n - 1$. Its complement consists of the points $(x_1, \dots, x_{n-1}, y)$ with $y \notin D$ and $y \ne x_i$ for all $i$, which is $C_n$. Finally $2g - 2 + r + n - 1 > 0$. So $C_n$ is a hyperbolic polycurve of dimension $n$. For $C = \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ these are the moduli spaces $\mathcal{M}_{0, n+3}$ of $n + 3$ ordered distinct points on the projective line, and Schmidt and Stix use the same tower for $\mathcal{M}_{0, m}$, $m \ge 4$ [@schmidtstix2016, §6, p. 18]. Configuration spaces have their own anabelian results, as in [the semi-absolute theorem over TKND-AVKF fields](grothendieck-conjecture-for-configuration-spaces-over-tknd-avkf-fields.md).
- *Kodaira fibrations.* A Kodaira fibration $X \to B$, as defined in [the entry on their sections](topological-sections-of-kodaira-fibrations.md), is a proper hyperbolic polycurve of dimension $2$ over $\mathbb{C}$. It is a flat morphism with smooth fibres, hence smooth, it is proper because $X$ is projective, its fibres are smooth projective curves of one genus $g \ge 3$, and the base $B$ is a proper curve of genus at least $2$, as recorded there.
- *Strongly hyperbolic Artin neighbourhoods.* Schmidt and Stix call a smooth $K$-variety $X$ a strongly hyperbolic Artin neighbourhood if it has a sequence $X = X_n \to \cdots \to X_0 = \mathrm{Spec}\, K$ in which each $X_i \to X_{i-1}$ is an elementary fibration into hyperbolic curves and each $X_i$ embeds into a product of hyperbolic curves [@schmidtstix2016, Definition 6.1]. Carlson describes them as a well-behaved class of hyperbolic polycurves [@carlson2026, Introduction, p. 3]. Every point of a smooth geometrically connected variety over an infinite perfect field has a fundamental system of Zariski open neighbourhoods of this kind [@schmidtstix2016, Lemma 6.3]. So hyperbolic polycurves are Zariski-locally everywhere on smooth varieties.

**Fundamental groups.** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$, let $S$ be a connected, noetherian, separated, normal $k$-scheme, and let $X$ be a hyperbolic polycurve of relative dimension $n$ over $S$ with a sequence of parametrizing morphisms as above. Write $\Pi_X$ for the étale fundamental group, and $\Delta_{X/X_m}$ for the kernel of $\Pi_X \to \Pi_{X_m}$.

- *Exactness.* For $0 \le m < n$ and a geometric point $\bar x_m$ of $X_m$, the sequence $1 \to \Pi_{X \times_{X_m} \bar x_m} \to \Pi_X \to \Pi_{X_m} \to 1$ is exact, so $\Delta_{X/X_m}$ is the fundamental group of a geometric fibre [@hoshi2014polycurves, Proposition 2.4 (i)].
- *Structure.* $\Delta_{X/X_m}$ is nontrivial, topologically finitely generated, slim and torsion-free, where *slim* means that every open subgroup has trivial centre [@hoshi2014polycurves, Proposition 2.4 (iii)]. If $X_{m+1} \to X_m$ has type $(g, r)$, then the abelianization of $\Delta_{X_{m+1}/X_m}$ is free over $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ of rank $2g + \max\{r - 1, 0\}$, and $\Delta_{X_{m+1}/X_m}$ is free profinite exactly when $r \ne 0$ [@hoshi2014polycurves, Proposition 2.4 (v)]. It is also *elastic*: a nontrivial topologically finitely generated closed subgroup that is normal in an open subgroup is open [@hoshi2014polycurves, Proposition 2.4 (iv)]. So for $S = \mathrm{Spec}\, k$, the geometric fundamental group $\Delta_{X/k} = \pi_1(X \otimes_k \bar k)$ is an iterated extension of geometric fundamental groups of hyperbolic curves.
- *Coverings.* A connected finite étale covering $Y \to X$ is again a hyperbolic polycurve of relative dimension $n$, over the normalization of $S$ in $Y$, which is finite étale over $S$, with a sequence of parametrizing morphisms lying over the given one [@hoshi2014polycurves, Proposition 2.3].
- *Large fundamental group.* A hyperbolic polycurve over $k$ is of *LFG-type*: for every normal variety $Z$ over $\bar k$ and every nonconstant morphism $Z \to X \otimes_k \bar k$, the image of $\Pi_Z$ in $\Pi_{X \otimes_k \bar k}$ is infinite [@hoshi2014polycurves, Definition 2.5 and Proposition 2.7].
- *$K(\pi, 1)$.* A hyperbolic polycurve over a field of characteristic $0$ is a $K(\pi, 1)$, so its étale cohomology with locally constant constructible coefficients is the continuous cohomology of its fundamental group [@carlson2026, Definition 2.6, Proposition 2.13 and the sentence after it].

The fundamental group need not see the dimension through its pro-$p$ quotients. Over $p$-adic fields with $p$ odd, Nagamachi constructs a proper hyperbolic polycurve of dimension $2$ whose geometrically pro-$p$ fundamental group is that of a proper hyperbolic curve. See [the Grothendieck conjecture for hyperbolic polycurves](grothendieck-conjecture-for-hyperbolic-polycurves.md), which records what the full fundamental group determines.
