**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $G = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, $V \subset G$ the wild inertia group, $\mathcal{G} = G/V$ with generators $\sigma, \tau$, $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^p$, and $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathcal{G}]]$ with its anti-automorphism $*$. Use the notation of [nonabelian finite subgroups](nonabelian-finite-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md): the element $\delta_0 = \delta - \delta^*$, the group $\mathcal{U}$ of unitary units and the injective anti-homomorphism $\Phi : \mathcal{U} \to \mathrm{Aut}(G/V^2)$ of its part 1. Fix an odd prime $\ell \ne p$ such that the order $f$ of $p$ modulo $\ell$ is odd, at least $3$ and prime to $p$, as in its part 2, and use the objects of steps (c) and (e) of its proof:

- a character $\xi$ of tame inertia of order $\ell$, its Frobenius orbit $O$, which has $f$ elements, the central idempotent $T_O \in \Lambda$ and the ring $R = T_O\Lambda$;
- the central idempotent $e_1 = T_Oe_S$ of $\Lambda$, and the units $a = e_1\tau$, $b = e_1\sigma_{p'}$ of $e_1\Lambda$, with $a^\ell = b^f = e_1$ and $bab^{-1} = a^p$;
- the group $\Pi = \langle \alpha, \beta \mid \alpha^\ell, \beta^f, \beta\alpha\beta^{-1}\alpha^{-p}\rangle$ of order $\ell f$, the surjection $\pi : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p[\Pi]$ with $\tau, \sigma \mapsto \alpha, \beta$, and the elements $\bar t_{\xi'} = \frac1\ell\sum_{i<\ell}\xi'(\tau)^{-i}\alpha^i$ of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p[\Pi]$ for $\xi' \in O$. These are nonzero orthogonal idempotents with $\alpha\bar t_{\xi'} = \xi'(\tau)\bar t_{\xi'}$, and $\bar T_O = \sum_{\xi' \in O}\bar t_{\xi'} = \pi(T_O) = \pi(e_1)$ is a central idempotent of $\mathbb{F}_p[\Pi]$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *A matrix algebra in the block.* The ring $e_1\Lambda$, whose identity is $e_1$, has a subring with identity $e_1$ isomorphic to $\mathrm{M}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$, and it contains $a$ and $b$. So $(e_1\Lambda)^\times$ has a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathrm{GL}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$.
2. *Groups of automorphisms.* For every subgroup $Q$ of $\mathrm{GL}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$ of order prime to $p$, $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has a subgroup isomorphic to $Q$ whose elements all induce the identity on $G/V$. It maps isomorphically onto a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G)$. For every finite tamely ramified $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ inside $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, restriction to $G_K$ embeds it in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$, and the image maps isomorphically onto a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$.
3. *Every finite group of order prime to $p$.* Let $H$ be a finite group of order prime to $p$, and $K$ a finite tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, for instance $\mathbb{Q}_p$ itself. Then $H$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ whose elements induce the identity on $G_K/V$, and to a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ no nontrivial element of which lies in a quasi-geometric subgroup.

**Examples.** For $p \ge 7$, the alternating group $A_5$, of order $60$, is simple and nonabelian, and it embeds in $\mathrm{GL}_5(\mathbb{F}_p)$ by permutation matrices. Part 2 with $f = 5$ applies for every $p \ge 7$, since step (e) of the proof gives a suitable $\ell$; for $p = 7$ one can take $\ell = 2801$, a prime with $7^5 = 6 \cdot 2801 + 1$. So $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_7})$ has a subgroup isomorphic to $A_5$. For $p = 5$, part 2 applies for instance to the simple group $\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)$, of order $168 = 2^3\cdot 3\cdot 7$, which permutes the $7$ nonzero vectors of $\mathbb{F}_2^3$ faithfully, so that $f = 7$ and $\ell = 19531$ qualify.

**Context.** Nishio asks whether every finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ is quasi-geometric. For odd $p$ his construction needs $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 3$, and it gives a given finite group $F$ of order prime to $p$ once $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 2n + 1$ for a faithful action of $F$ on $n$ points ([finite subgroups of outer automorphism groups](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), consequence 3). Part 3 removes the bound on the degree for tamely ramified $k$, in particular for $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and for the fields of degree $2$, where Nishio's construction gives nothing.

- Earlier entries found [abelian groups](prime-to-p-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) from central units of $\Lambda$, and [Frobenius groups](nonabelian-finite-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) $\mathbb{Z}/\ell \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/f$ from the block $e_1\Lambda$, on which every unit is unitary once paired with its adjoint. The new observation is that this block contains a full matrix algebra $\mathrm{M}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$ with the same identity, as the image of a block of the group algebra of $\Pi$, so all of $\mathrm{GL}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$ lies in its unit group.
- By consequence 2 of the Nishio entry, a quasi-geometric finite subgroup is solvable. The nonsolvable subgroups of part 3, such as $A_5$ for $p \ge 7$, fail to be quasi-geometric for that reason alone; consequence 1 there gives the stronger statement of part 3.
- *Not settled here.* Whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ has elements of order $p$: the splitting step cannot produce them, because the kernel it splits off is pro-$p$. Whether every finite group of order prime to $p$ embeds in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ for wildly ramified $K$, where Nishio's consequence 3 needs a degree bound. The case $p = 2$, where the construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg is not available.
- *Literature.* The statement was not found in the sources opened for this entry, which include Nishio's paper on finite subgroups.

**Proof.**

*(a) A block of the group algebra of $\Pi$.* Write $A = \mathbb{F}_p[\Pi]$ and $B = \bar T_OA$, a ring with identity $\bar T_O$, because $\bar T_O$ is a central idempotent. Let $q \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$ be the polynomial $q(x) = \sum_{i<\ell}\big(\frac1\ell\sum_{\xi' \in O}\xi'(\tau)^{-i}\big)x^i$; its coefficients are traces from $\mathbb{F}_{p^f}$ to $\mathbb{F}_p$, as $O$ is a Frobenius orbit, and $q(\alpha) = \bar T_O$. For $P \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$, the eigenvalue relations give $P(\alpha)\bar T_O = \sum_{\xi' \in O}P(\xi'(\tau))\bar t_{\xi'}$.

- *A field.* Put $E = \{P(\alpha)\bar T_O : P \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]\} \subseteq B$ and $z = \alpha\bar T_O$. The map $P \mapsto P(\alpha)\bar T_O$ is a surjective ring homomorphism $\mathbb{F}_p[x] \to E$. As the $\bar t_{\xi'}$ are nonzero orthogonal idempotents, they are linearly independent, so its kernel consists of the $P$ vanishing at every $\xi'(\tau)$, $\xi' \in O$. These values are the conjugates $\zeta^{p^k}$ of $\zeta = \xi(\tau)$, a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity, so the kernel is generated by the minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, of degree $f$. Hence $E$ is a field with $p^f$ elements, with identity $\bar T_O$, generated by $z$.
- *Conjugation by $\beta$.* It is a ring automorphism of $A$ that fixes the central element $\bar T_O$ and maps $\alpha$ to $\alpha^p$. So it maps $E$ onto $E$ and $z$ to $z^p$. The Frobenius $\mathrm{Fr}(e) = e^p$ is a ring automorphism of $E$ that also maps $z$ to $z^p$, and $z$ generates $E$, so $\beta e\beta^{-1} = \mathrm{Fr}(e)$ for $e \in E$.
- *The representation.* For $e \in E$ let $m_e \in \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_p}(E)$ be multiplication by $e$. Then $m_z^\ell = m_{z^\ell} = m_{\bar T_O} = 1$, $\mathrm{Fr}^f = 1$ because $|E| = p^f$, and $\mathrm{Fr}\,m_z\,\mathrm{Fr}^{-1} = m_{z^p} = m_z^p$. So $\alpha \mapsto m_z$, $\beta \mapsto \mathrm{Fr}$ defines a homomorphism $\Pi \to \mathrm{GL}_{\mathbb{F}_p}(E)$, which extends to an $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra homomorphism $\rho : A \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_p}(E)$. As $z^i = \alpha^i\bar T_O$ for $i \ge 0$, $\rho(P(\alpha)) = m_{P(\alpha)\bar T_O}$; in particular $\rho(\bar T_O) = \rho(q(\alpha)) = m_{\bar T_O} = 1$. So $\rho(1 - \bar T_O) = 0$, and $\rho$ restricts to a homomorphism of rings with identity $B \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_p}(E)$, with $\rho(e\,(\beta\bar T_O)^j) = m_e\mathrm{Fr}^j$ for $e \in E$.
- *Bijectivity.* $B$ is spanned by the elements $\alpha^i\beta^j\bar T_O = z^i(\beta\bar T_O)^j$, so $B = \sum_{j<f}E\,(\beta\bar T_O)^j$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}B \le f^2$. The automorphisms $\mathrm{Fr}^j$, $0 \le j < f$, of $E$ are distinct, because $\mathrm{Fr}^j = 1$ forces $E$ to lie in the field with $p^j$ elements. By Dedekind's lemma they are linearly independent over $E$: in a relation $\sum_{j \in J}e_j\mathrm{Fr}^j = 0$ with all $e_j \ne 0$ and $|J|$ minimal, $|J| = 1$ is impossible, since $\mathrm{Fr}^j(1) = 1$. If $j_1 \ne j_2$ lie in $J$, choose $x$ with $\mathrm{Fr}^{j_1}(x) \ne \mathrm{Fr}^{j_2}(x)$. Evaluating the relation at $xy$ and subtracting $\mathrm{Fr}^{j_1}(x)$ times its value at $y$ gives $\sum_{j \in J}e_j(\mathrm{Fr}^j(x) - \mathrm{Fr}^{j_1}(x))\mathrm{Fr}^j = 0$, a shorter nontrivial relation. So the maps $m_e\mathrm{Fr}^j$ span a subspace of dimension $f \cdot f = f^2 = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_p}(E)$. Hence $\rho|_B$ is surjective, and, as $\dim B \le f^2$, an isomorphism. So $B \cong \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}_p}(E) \cong \mathrm{M}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$, and $B^\times \cong \mathrm{GL}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$.

*(b) Proof of part 1.* The relations $a^\ell = b^f = e_1$ and $bab^{-1} = a^p$ give a homomorphism $\Pi \to (e_1\Lambda)^\times$ with $\alpha, \beta \mapsto a, b$, which extends to an $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra homomorphism $h : A \to e_1\Lambda$ with $h(1) = e_1$. By step (e) of the nonabelian entry, $\pi(h(g)) = \bar T_Og$ for $g \in \Pi$; both sides are $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear in $g$, so $\pi(h(x)) = \bar T_Ox$ for all $x \in A$.

- *$h(\bar T_O) = e_1$.* As $a^i = e_1\tau^i$ for $i \ge 0$, $h(\bar T_O) = q(a) = e_1q(\tau)$, so it suffices that $T_Oq(\tau) = T_O$. Check this at each level $K'$, with ramification index $e'$ and $\bar\tau$ the image of $\tau$. If $O$ does not consist of characters of $\langle\bar\tau\rangle$, the component of $T_O$ at $K'$ is $0$. Otherwise $T_O = \sum_{\xi' \in O}t_{\xi'}$ with $\bar\tau t_{\xi'} = \xi'(\tau)t_{\xi'}$ in $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(K'/\mathbb{Q}_p)]$, by step (a) of the [finite-abelian entry](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md), so $T_Oq(\bar\tau) = \sum_{\xi' \in O}q(\xi'(\tau))t_{\xi'}$. Here $q(\xi'(\tau)) = \sum_{\xi'' \in O}\frac1\ell\sum_{i<\ell}\big(\xi'(\tau)\xi''(\tau)^{-1}\big)^i = 1$: the term $\xi'' = \xi'$ gives $1$, and for $\xi'' \ne \xi'$ the number $\xi'(\tau)\xi''(\tau)^{-1}$ is an $\ell$-th root of unity other than $1$, since a character of tame inertia is determined by its value at $\tau$, so the inner sum is $0$. Hence $T_Oq(\bar\tau) = T_O$ at every level.
- *Injectivity.* So $h(1 - \bar T_O) = 0$, and $h$ restricts to a homomorphism of rings with identity $B \to e_1\Lambda$. It is injective, because $\pi(h(x)) = \bar T_Ox = x$ for $x \in B$.

So $h(B)$ is a subring of $e_1\Lambda$ with identity $e_1$, isomorphic to $\mathrm{M}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$ by (a). It contains $a = h(\alpha) = h(\alpha\bar T_O)$ and $b = h(\beta\bar T_O)$, and its units, which are units of $e_1\Lambda$, form a group $h(B^\times) \cong \mathrm{GL}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$. $\square$

*(c) Every unit of the block is unitary after pairing.* This is step (f) of the nonabelian entry, which uses only that $g$ is a unit of $e_1\Lambda$. There, $e_1^* = e_S^*T_{\iota(O)}$ is a central idempotent with $e_1e_1^* = 0$, and $e_1\delta_0$ is central in $R$ by steps (c) and (d). For $g \in (e_1\Lambda)^\times$, $g^*$ lies in $e_1^*\Lambda$ and is a unit there, with inverse $(g^{-1})^*$, because $g^*(g^{-1})^* = (g^{-1}g)^* = e_1^*$. Put
$$v_g = (1 - e_1 - e_1^*) + g + (g^*)^{-1}.$$
As $1 - e_1 - e_1^*$, $e_1$ and $e_1^*$ are orthogonal central idempotents with sum $1$, products can be computed componentwise. So $v_gv_{g'} = (1 - e_1 - e_1^*) + gg' + (g'^*g^*)^{-1} = v_{gg'}$, using $(gg')^* = g'^*g^*$, and $g \mapsto v_g$ is an injective homomorphism $(e_1\Lambda)^\times \to \Lambda^\times$, as $e_1v_g = g$. It takes values in $\mathcal{U}$:

- Here $v_g^* = (1 - e_1 - e_1^*) + g^* + g^{-1}$. The $e_1$-component of $v_g^*\delta_0v_g$ is $g^{-1}(e_1\delta_0)g = e_1\delta_0$, since $g \in e_1\Lambda \subseteq R$ and $e_1\delta_0$ is central in $R$. Its $e_1^*$-component is $g^*(e_1^*\delta_0)(g^*)^{-1}$; applying $*$ to $g(e_1\delta_0) = (e_1\delta_0)g$ and using $(e_1\delta_0)^* = \delta_0^*e_1^* = -e_1^*\delta_0$ shows that $g^*$ commutes with $e_1^*\delta_0$, so this is $e_1^*\delta_0$. The third component is that of $\delta_0$. So $v_g^*\delta_0v_g = \delta_0$.
- At every level $K'$, $\lambda_{K'} = T_{\{\omega\}}$, and $T_{\{\omega\}}T_O = T_{\{\omega\}}T_{\iota(O)} = 0$, as the orbits $O$, $\iota(O)$ and $\{\omega\}$ are distinct (step (c) there). So $\lambda_{K'}e_1 = \lambda_{K'}e_1^* = 0$ and $\lambda_{K'}v_g = \lambda_{K'}$.

*(d) Proof of part 2.* Let $Q \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$ have order prime to $p$, let $Q_1 \subseteq h(B^\times)$ correspond to it under the isomorphism of (b), and put $Y = \{v_g : g \in Q_1\}$. By (c), $Y$ is a subgroup of $\mathcal{U}$ isomorphic to $Q$. By part 1 of the nonabelian entry, $\Phi(Y)$ is a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G/V^2)$, anti-isomorphic and hence isomorphic to $Q$, and each of its elements is induced by an automorphism of $G$ that induces the identity on $G/V$. The rest is step (g) of the nonabelian entry, with $\Phi(Y)$ in place of the image of the Frobenius group. Every automorphism of $G$ preserves $V$, hence $V^2$; let $\Delta \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ be the subgroup of automorphisms inducing an element of $\Phi(Y)$ on $G/V^2$, which maps onto $\Phi(Y)$. Nishio's Corollary 1.4 holds for any finite group of order prime to $p$. Its proof of (ii) implies (i), with $(J, P, i, s) = (G, V, 2, 1)$, splits the sequence $1 \to \mathrm{Aut}_{G/V^2}(G) \to \Delta \to \Phi(Y) \to 1$, whose kernel is pro-$p$ by his Theorem 1.2 [@nishio2026finite, Theorem 1.2, p. 6; Corollary 1.4 and its proof, p. 7]. This gives a subgroup $\Gamma \subseteq \Delta$ mapping isomorphically onto $\Phi(Y)$, so $\Gamma \cong Q$, and every element of $\Gamma$ induces the identity on $G/V$. By consequence 1 of the [Nishio entry](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), $\Gamma$ maps isomorphically into $\mathrm{Out}(G)$. For tamely ramified $K$, step (h) of the nonabelian entry applies verbatim: $V \subseteq G_K$, so every $\gamma \in \Gamma$ maps $G_K$ onto itself; restriction is injective because $G$ is slim; and the finite image maps isomorphically into $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ by consequence 1 again. $\square$

*(e) Proof of part 3.* Let $n = |H|$, and choose a prime $f > \max(n, p)$. Then $f$ is odd, at least $3$, and prime to $p$. Put $N = (p^f - 1)/(p - 1) = 1 + p + \dots + p^{f-1}$, which is odd as a sum of $f$ odd numbers, satisfies $N > f$, and is $\equiv 1 \pmod p$. Step (i) of the nonabelian entry shows, using only that $f$ is an odd prime different from $p$, that $f^2 \nmid N$, so $N$ has a prime factor $\ell \ne f$, and that the order of $p$ modulo $\ell$ is $f$: it divides $f$, and if it were $1$ then $N \equiv f \pmod \ell$, so $\ell \mid f$. This $\ell$ is odd, as $N$ is, and different from $p$, as $N \equiv 1 \pmod p$. So $\ell$ satisfies the hypotheses of the Setting.

Left multiplication makes $H$ act faithfully on itself, which gives an injective homomorphism from $H$ to the permutation matrices in $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_p)$. Composing with $M \mapsto \mathrm{diag}(M, I_{f-n})$ embeds $H$ in $\mathrm{GL}_f(\mathbb{F}_p)$, with image $Q$ of order $n$, prime to $p$. Part 2 gives a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ isomorphic to $H$, whose elements induce the identity on $G_K/V$ because those of $\Gamma$ induce the identity on $G/V$, and its isomorphic image in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. By consequence 1 of the Nishio entry, no nontrivial element of that image lies in a quasi-geometric subgroup. $\square$

For the examples: $A_5 \subseteq \mathfrak{S}_5$ gives $A_5 \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_5(\mathbb{F}_p)$ directly, so part 2 applies with $f = 5$ whenever $p \ge 7$, and the argument of (e) with $f = 5$ produces $\ell$. For $p = 7$, $N = 2801$, which has no prime factor up to $\sqrt{2801} < 53$, and for $p = 5$ and $f = 7$, $N = 19531$, which has none up to $\sqrt{19531} < 140$; in both cases $\ell = N$.

**Taken from the sources without re-verification.** As in the nonabelian entry, whose part 1 and steps (c)–(f) this proof uses: the congruence for the Demuškin relations modulo the third term of the $p$-central series, the passage to an automorphism of $G/V^3$, the lifting theorem (7.5.15), the formula for $y_1$ and the freeness of $\Lambda\bar x_1$ in $V/V^2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 419–423]; and, in Nishio's proof of Corollary 1.4, a profinite Schur–Zassenhaus theorem from Ribes and Zalesskii's book, which was not opened. The simplicity of $A_5$ and of $\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)$ is standard, was not checked against a source here, and is used only in the examples. The primality of $2801$ and $19531$ was checked by trial division by computer.
