**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the degree-one Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-on-wild-inertia.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$ with augmentation $\epsilon$, $P_j$ is the closed lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$, $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$, and
$$V=H/pH=L\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d,\qquad L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0,\quad V_k=\Lambda\bar x_k\cong\Lambda\ (k\ge1),$$
with coordinates $\pi_k:V\to\Lambda$ for $k\ge1$. Also $\omega$, $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, $\xi_k\in H$, the surface pairs $(a_i,b_i)$ for $1\le i\le r=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor$, the element $\gamma$ with $\omega(\gamma)=1$, and $\lambda_0=\gamma-1$ are as there. The first pair $(a_1,b_1)$ is $(x_2,x_3)$ for odd $d$ and $(x_3,x_4)$ for even $d$. Indices of surface generators are *surface indices*; $0$, $1$ and, for even $d$, $2$ are *complementary*. We view $\mathcal G\cong\Gamma$ inside $G$, so $\sigma,\tau,\gamma\in G$. An automorphism is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$, and $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$.

From the lower central input there: each $\mathcal L_j$ is torsion-free, $\mathcal L_j/p\mathcal L_j$ is the completed free Lie space $\mathcal L_j(V)\subseteq V^{\hat\otimes j}$, functorially in automorphisms of $G$, and $\mathcal L_2(V)$ is the space of antisymmetric tensors, as $p$ is odd.

Let $\iota:\Lambda\to\Lambda$ be the continuous linear map with $\iota(\rho)=\rho^{-1}$; it reverses products. Let $\omega^{-1}:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ be the continuous ring homomorphism with $\rho\mapsto\omega(\rho)^{-1}$, and let $\Delta_3:\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\hat\otimes3}$ extend $\rho\mapsto\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\rho$. Let $e$ be the order of $\omega$, which is $[K(\mu_p):K]$. So $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ exactly when $e\ge2$, and $\omega^2\ne1$ exactly when $e\ge3$.

*Cohomology.* For a compact abelian pro-$p$ group $M$ with a continuous $\Gamma$-action, $H^1(\Gamma,M)$ is the group of continuous $1$-cocycles modulo the coboundaries $\delta h(\rho)=(\rho-1)h$. Let $I_\Gamma M$ be the closure of the subgroup generated by the $(\rho-1)m$, $M_\Gamma=M/I_\Gamma M$, and $\Phi_M:H^1(\Gamma,M)\to M_\Gamma$, $[c]\mapsto c(\sigma)+I_\Gamma M$. It is well defined and $\mathbb Z_p$-linear. A continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant homomorphism $f:M\to M'$ induces $f_*$ on $H^1$ and $f_\Gamma$ on coinvariants, with $\Phi_{M'}f_*=f_\Gamma\Phi_M$.

*The class.* Let $\mathcal A$ be the set of $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ that induce the identity on $G/P_2$ and on $P/P_3$. For $\alpha\in\mathcal A$ and $\rho\in\Gamma$ put $c_\alpha(\rho)=\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}P_3\in\mathcal L_2$, for $\tilde\rho\in G$ over $\rho$. For $x\in G$, $\alpha(\tilde\rho x)(\tilde\rho x)^{-1}=\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}\cdot\tilde\rho\,\alpha(x)x^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}$. For $x\in P$ the last factor lies in $P_3$, so $c_\alpha$ does not depend on $\tilde\rho$. For general $x$ the identity shows that $c_\alpha$ is a cocycle; it is continuous, taking $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$. Put $u(\alpha)=[c_\alpha]\in H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_2)$. For $v\in H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_2)$, its *reduction* $\bar v$ is the image of $\Phi_{\mathcal L_2}(v)$ in $(\mathcal L_2/p)_\Gamma=\mathcal V$, where $\mathcal V=\mathcal L_2(V)_\Gamma$. That is, $\bar v$ is the class of $v(\sigma)$ modulo $p\mathcal L_2+I_\Gamma\mathcal L_2$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many outer conjugates and induce the identity on $G/[P,P]$ and on $P/[P,[P,P]]$.

- (a) If $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$, then $u(\alpha)=0$. Equivalently, $c_h\circ\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/[P,[P,P]]$ for some $h\in[P,P]$.
- (b) If $[K(\mu_p):K]=2$, then $\overline{u(\alpha)}$ is a multiple of the class $E\in\mathcal V$ of $[\bar x_0,\bar x_1]=\bar x_0\otimes\bar x_1-\bar x_1\otimes\bar x_0$. Moreover, there is one $w\in(\mathcal L_2)_\Gamma$ with $\Phi_{\mathcal L_2}(u(\alpha'))\in\mathbb Z_pw$ for every $\alpha'$ satisfying the hypotheses.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$ and $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$. Every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha_1$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_1$ inducing the identity on $G/[P,[P,P]]$. Two such $\alpha_1$ differ by $c_k$ with $k\in[P,[P,P]]$. So every outer class with finitely many conjugates lies in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/[P,[P,P]])$.

**Lemma 1 (cohomology; proved here).** Let $M$ be a compact abelian pro-$p$ group with a continuous $\Gamma$-action, and $M^\gamma$ the set of points fixed by $\gamma$.

- (a) A continuous cocycle $c:\Gamma\to M$ with $c(\sigma)\in I_\Gamma M$ is a coboundary. So $\Phi_M$ is injective.
- (b) Let $M$ be torsion-free. If $\Phi_M(v)\in pM_\Gamma$, then $v\in pH^1(\Gamma,M)$. If also $(M/pM)^\gamma=0$, then $H^1(\Gamma,M)$ is torsion-free and $M^\Gamma=0$.
- (c) $\mathcal L_2(V)^\gamma=0$ and $\mathcal L_4(V)^\gamma=0$. Hence $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_2)$ and $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_4)$ are torsion-free, and $\mathcal L_2^\Gamma=0$.

*Proof.* (a) The proof of Lemma 2 of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md) uses about $H$ only that it is a compact abelian pro-$p$ group with a continuous $\Gamma$-action, so it applies to $M$.

(b) Write $v=[c]$ with $c(\sigma)\in pM+I_\Gamma M$. The reduction $\bar c:\Gamma\to M/pM$ is a continuous cocycle with $\bar c(\sigma)\in I_\Gamma(M/pM)$, since $I_\Gamma M$ maps into $I_\Gamma(M/pM)$. By (a) for $M/pM$, $\bar c=\delta\bar k$; lift $\bar k$ to $k\in M$. Then $c-\delta k$ takes values in $pM$. Multiplication by $p$ is a continuous bijection of the compact group $M$ onto $pM$, hence a homeomorphism, so $c'=p^{-1}(c-\delta k)$ is a continuous cocycle, and $v=p[c']$. Next, $H^1(\Gamma,M)$ is a $\mathbb Z_p$-module, so it suffices to exclude $p$-torsion; let $pc=\delta h$. Then $(\rho-1)h\in pM$ for all $\rho$, so the image of $h$ in $M/pM$ is fixed by $\gamma$, hence $0$. So $h=ph'$, and $p(c-\delta h')=0$ gives $c=\delta h'$. Finally let $h\in M^\Gamma$ be nonzero. As $\bigcap_kp^kM=0$, $h=p^kh'$ with $h'\notin pM$, and $h'\in M^\Gamma$ by torsion-freeness. Its image in $M/pM$ is nonzero and fixed by $\gamma$, a contradiction.

(c) $V^{\hat\otimes j}$ is the direct sum of the components $W_1\hat\otimes\cdots\hat\otimes W_j$, where each $W_i$ is $L$ or some $V_k$, and $\gamma$ preserves each component. Take a component with $m\ge1$ factors of the form $V_k$. Through the coordinates it is $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^m]]$, and $\gamma$ acts by left multiplication by $(\gamma,\dots,\gamma)$, as $\omega(\gamma)=1$. The homeomorphism $(g_1,\dots,g_m)\mapsto(g_1,g_1^{-1}g_2,\dots,g_1^{-1}g_m)$ of $\Gamma^m$ descends to $(\Gamma/U)^m$ for every open normal $U$, so it induces a continuous linear automorphism $h_*$ of $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^m]]=\Lambda\hat\otimes\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^{m-1}]]$, which turns the action of $\gamma$ into $\ell_\gamma\otimes\mathrm{id}$, with $\ell_\gamma$ left multiplication by $\gamma$. If $z$ is fixed, then for each continuous linear $f$ on $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^{m-1}]]$, $(\mathrm{id}\otimes f)(h_*z)\in\Lambda$ is killed by left multiplication by $\gamma-1$, so it is $0$ by Lemma 2 of [the central-element construction](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md). At each finite level a tensor killed by every $\mathrm{id}\otimes\bar f$ is $0$, so $h_*z=0$ and $z=0$. For the component $L^{\otimes j}$, let $q:V\to L$ be the projection along $\bigoplus_kV_k$. The $L^{\otimes j}$-component of $Y\in V^{\hat\otimes j}$ is $q^{\otimes j}(Y)$, and $q^{\otimes j}$ maps $\mathcal L_j(V)$ into the closed span of the $j$-fold brackets of elements of $L$, which is $0$ for $j\ge2$ as $\dim L=1$. So a $\gamma$-fixed element of $\mathcal L_j(V)$ with $j\in\{2,4\}$ has all its components $0$. The rest follows from (b) for $M=\mathcal L_2,\mathcal L_4$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (coordinates; proved here).**

- (a) For $k,l\ge1$, the map $\kappa_{kl}:V_k\hat\otimes V_l\to\Lambda$, $\mu\bar x_k\otimes\nu\bar x_l\mapsto\iota(\mu)\nu$, is continuous, linear and invariant under the diagonal action of $\Gamma$, and it induces an isomorphism $(V_k\hat\otimes V_l)_\Gamma\cong\Lambda$. For $l\ge1$, the maps $\kappa_{0l}:\bar x_0\otimes\nu\bar x_l\mapsto\omega^{-1}(\nu)$ on $L\hat\otimes V_l$ and $\kappa_{l0}:\nu\bar x_l\otimes\bar x_0\mapsto\omega^{-1}(\nu)$ on $V_l\hat\otimes L$ induce isomorphisms of the coinvariants with $\mathbb F_p$.
- (b) Let $j,k\ge1$, $\beta\in\Lambda$, and $f:V_k\to V_j$, $\mu\bar x_k\mapsto\mu\beta\bar x_j$. Then $\kappa_{jl}\circ(f\otimes1)=\iota(\beta)\,\kappa_{kl}$ and $\kappa_{lj}\circ(1\otimes f)=\kappa_{lk}\,\beta$ for $l\ge1$, while $\kappa_{j0}\circ(f\otimes1)=\omega^{-1}(\beta)\kappa_{k0}$ and $\kappa_{0j}\circ(1\otimes f)=\omega^{-1}(\beta)\kappa_{0k}$.
- (c) For $X\in\mathcal V$ and $k,l\ge0$ not both $0$, let $X_{kl}$ be the coordinate $\kappa_{kl}$ of the $V_k\hat\otimes V_l$-component of the image of $X$ in $(V\hat\otimes V)_\Gamma$, where $V_0=L$. Then $X_{lk}=-\iota(X_{kl})$ for $k,l\ge1$, $X_{l0}=-X_{0l}$, and $X$ is determined by its coordinates.
- (d) Let $\eta:V^{\hat\otimes4}\to\Lambda^{\hat\otimes3}$ be $\pi_{b_1}\otimes\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{a_1}\otimes\pi_{b_1}$ followed by the map induced by $(g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4)\mapsto(g_1^{-1}g_2,g_1^{-1}g_3,g_1^{-1}g_4)$. It is continuous, linear and $\Gamma$-invariant, so it factors through $(V^{\hat\otimes4})_\Gamma$.

*Proof.* (a) Through the coordinates, $V_k\hat\otimes V_l=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^2]]$, with $\rho$ acting by left multiplication by $(\rho,\rho)$. The continuous maps $m(g,h)=g^{-1}h$ and $s(h)=(1,h)$ satisfy $m\circ s=\mathrm{id}$ and $m(\rho g,\rho h)=m(g,h)$. They induce continuous linear maps with $m_*=\kappa_{kl}$ and $m_*s_*=\mathrm{id}$, so $\kappa_{kl}$ is onto and kills $I_\Gamma(V_k\hat\otimes V_l)$. For $z=(g,h)$, $z-s_*m_*z=z-(g^{-1},g^{-1})z$ lies in $I_\Gamma$. By linearity, continuity and density of the span of $\Gamma^2$, $z-s_*m_*z\in I_\Gamma$ for every $z$, so $\ker\kappa_{kl}=I_\Gamma$. For $L\hat\otimes V_l$, identify it with $\Lambda$ by $\bar x_0\otimes\nu\bar x_l\mapsto\nu$, with $\rho$ acting by $\nu\mapsto\omega(\rho)\rho\nu$. Then $\omega^{-1}$ is invariant and onto. For $g\in\Gamma$, $g-\omega^{-1}(g)\cdot1$ is $g$ minus the image of $g$ under the action of $g^{-1}$, so it lies in $I_\Gamma$, and the kernel is $I_\Gamma$ as before. $V_l\hat\otimes L$ is the same.

(b) Both sides are continuous and bilinear in the coefficients, and on $\mu\otimes\nu$ they give $\iota(\mu\beta)\nu=\iota(\beta)\iota(\mu)\nu$, $\iota(\mu)\nu\beta$, and $\omega^{-1}(\mu\beta)=\omega^{-1}(\beta)\omega^{-1}(\mu)$.

(c) $\mathcal L_2(V)$ is the image of the $\Gamma$-equivariant idempotent $\frac12(1-\mathrm{flip})$ on $V\hat\otimes V$, so $\mathcal V\to(V\hat\otimes V)_\Gamma$ is injective. By (a), $(V\hat\otimes V)_\Gamma$ is the direct sum of the coinvariants of the components, each identified with its coordinate, and the $L\hat\otimes L$-component of an antisymmetric tensor is $0$. The flip carries $V_k\hat\otimes V_l$ to $V_l\hat\otimes V_k$ with $\kappa_{lk}\circ\mathrm{flip}=\iota\circ\kappa_{kl}$, as $h^{-1}g=\iota(g^{-1}h)$, and $\kappa_{l0}\circ\mathrm{flip}=\kappa_{0l}$. Antisymmetry gives the relations.

(d) $(\rho g_1)^{-1}(\rho g_i)=g_1^{-1}g_i$. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (the conditions; proved here).** Let $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Put $\mathcal U=\{u(\alpha):\alpha\in\mathcal A\}$ and let $\mathcal S$ be the set of $v\in H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_2)$ with $p^mv\in\mathcal U$ for some $m\ge0$.

- (u1) For $\alpha\in\mathcal A$ and $g\in P_2$, $c_g\alpha\in\mathcal A$ and $c_{c_g\alpha}(\rho)=c_\alpha(\rho)+(1-\rho)(gP_3)$.
- (u2) For $\alpha\in\mathcal A$ and tame-trivial $\theta$, $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}\in\mathcal A$ and $c_{\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}}=\theta_2\circ c_\alpha$, where $\theta_2$ is the action of $\theta$ on $\mathcal L_2$.
- (u3) $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal S$ are subgroups, and $\mathcal S\cap pH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_2)=p\mathcal S$.
- (u4) $u(\alpha)=0$ if and only if $c_h\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/P_3$ for some $h\in P_2$.

Moreover, for every $v\in\mathcal S$:

- (i) $\theta_*v\in\mathcal S$ for every tame-trivial $\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$.
- (ii) If $\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is tame-trivial and $\psi_H-1$ is nilpotent, then $\psi_*v=v$.
- (iii) Let $T=T^{(1)}_1$ be the boundary twist of the first pair, in the notation of [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md), and $\partial^T_2:\mathcal L_2\to\mathcal L_4$ its map from Lemma 1 (a) of the degree-one Johnson theorem. Then $\partial^T_2$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant and $(\partial^T_2)_*v=0$ in $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_4)$.

*Proof.* (u1) $c_g\alpha$ represents the outer class of $\alpha$. For $x\in G$, $c_g(x)x^{-1}=[g,x]$ lies in $P_2$, and in $P_3$ if $x\in P$, so $c_g\alpha\in\mathcal A$. For $\tilde\rho$ over $\rho$, $c_g\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}=g\cdot\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}\cdot\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}$, a product of three elements of $P_2$ with classes $gP_3$, $c_\alpha(\rho)$ and $-\rho(gP_3)$.

(u2) $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}$ has finitely many outer conjugates, and $P_2,P_3$ are characteristic, as $P$ is ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)); so it lies in $\mathcal A$. As $\theta$ is tame-trivial, $\theta^{-1}(\tilde\rho)$ lies over $\rho$. Write $\alpha(\theta^{-1}(\tilde\rho))=y\,\theta^{-1}(\tilde\rho)$ with $y\in P_2$ of class $c_\alpha(\rho)$. Then $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}=\theta(y)$.

(u3) If $a$ and $b$ have finitely many conjugates in a group, so does $ab^{-1}$, since each conjugate of $ab^{-1}$ is a conjugate of $a$ times a conjugate of $b^{-1}$. So $\mathcal A$ is closed under products and inverses. For $\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A$ write $\beta(\tilde\rho)=j\tilde\rho$ with $j\in P_2$. Then $\alpha\beta(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}=\alpha(j)\cdot\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}$, and $\alpha(j)j^{-1}\in P_3$ as $j\in P$. So $c_{\alpha\beta}=c_\alpha+c_\beta$, and $c_{\alpha^{-1}}=-c_\alpha$. Hence $\mathcal U$ is a subgroup, and so is $\mathcal S$. If $v\in\mathcal S$ with $p^mv\in\mathcal U$ and $v=pv'$, then $p^{m+1}v'\in\mathcal U$, so $v'\in\mathcal S$.

(u4) If $c_\alpha=\delta\bar k$ with $\bar k\in\mathcal L_2$, choose $h\in P_2$ over $\bar k$. By (u1), $c_{c_h\alpha}=0$, so $c_h\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}\in P_3$ for $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$. As $c_h\alpha$ is trivial on $P/P_3$ and $c_h\alpha(y\tilde\rho)(y\tilde\rho)^{-1}=c_h\alpha(y)y^{-1}\cdot y\,c_h\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}y^{-1}$, it induces the identity on $G/P_3$. Conversely, if $c_h\alpha$ does, then $c_{c_h\alpha}=0$, and $u(\alpha)=u(c_h\alpha)=0$ by (u1).

(i) If $p^mv=u(\alpha)$ with $\alpha\in\mathcal A$, then $p^m\theta_*v=u(\theta\alpha\theta^{-1})$ by (u2).

(ii) Let $p^mv=u(\alpha)$ with $\alpha\in\mathcal A$. As $\alpha$ is tame-trivial with finitely many outer conjugates, Lemma (b) of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md) gives $\psi^n\alpha\psi^{-n}=c_g\alpha$ with $n\ge1$ and $g\in P$. Both sides induce the identity on $G/P_2$, hence so does $c_g$. So $(1-\rho)(gP_2)=0$ for all $\rho$, and $gP_2\in H^\Gamma=0$ by Lemma 3 (c) of the degree-one Johnson theorem. So $g\in P_2$, and (u1), (u2) give $\psi^n_*u(\alpha)=u(\alpha)$. Hence $p^m(\psi^n_*v-v)=0$, and $\psi^n_*v=v$ by Lemma 1 (c). As in the proof of Lemma 4 (ii) there, $\psi_2-1$ is nilpotent on $\mathcal L_2$, so $U=\psi_*$ has $U-1$ nilpotent on $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_2)$. If $(U-1)v\ne0$, let $q\ge1$ be largest with $(U-1)^qv\ne0$. Then $0=(U-1)^{q-1}(U^n-1)v=\sum_{t\ge1}\binom nt(U-1)^{q-1+t}v=n(U-1)^qv$, contradicting torsion-freeness. So $\psi_*v=v$.

(iii) Let $p^mv=u(\alpha)$ with $\alpha\in\mathcal A$. In the Setting of interval twists, $T$ fixes $\mathcal G$ pointwise, and by Lemma 2 (a) there it is a tame-trivial automorphism, trivial on $P/P_3$. For $z\in P_2$, $T(\tilde\rho z\tilde\rho^{-1})(\tilde\rho z\tilde\rho^{-1})^{-1}=\tilde\rho\,T(z)z^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}$ for $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$, so $\partial^T_2$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant. By Lemma (b) of abelianized wild inertia, $T^n\alpha T^{-n}=c_g\alpha$ with $n\ge1$ and $g\in P$.

*The element $g$ lies in $P_4$.* Put $\theta_1=T^n$ and $\theta_2=\alpha$. For $y\in P$, $e_i=\theta_i(y)y^{-1}$ lies in $P_3$, and by Lemma 1 (a) of the degree-one Johnson theorem, with $k=2$ and $m=3$, $\theta_1(e_2)\in e_2P_5$ and $\theta_2(e_1)\in e_1P_5$. So $\theta_1\theta_2(y)=\theta_1(e_2)e_1y$ and $\theta_2\theta_1(y)=\theta_2(e_1)e_2y$ differ by left multiplication by an element of $P_5$, as $[P_3,P_3]\subseteq P_6$. Now $c_g=\theta_1\theta_2\theta_1^{-1}\theta_2^{-1}$ maps $\theta_2\theta_1(y)$ to $\theta_1\theta_2(y)$, and $\theta_2\theta_1(P)=P$. So $[g,y]\in P_5$ for all $y\in P$. Injectivity of the bracket maps on $H$ and on $\mathcal L_2$ (lower central input) gives $g\in P_2$ and then $g\in P_3$. Lemma 2 (b) there, for every $k$, gives $gP_4\in\bigcap_kp^k\mathcal L_3=0$. So $g\in P_4$.

*The cocycle.* For $\rho\in\Gamma$ take $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$, which $T$ fixes, and write $\alpha(\tilde\rho)=j\tilde\rho$ with $j\in P_2$ of class $c_\alpha(\rho)$. Then $T^n\alpha T^{-n}(\tilde\rho)=T^n(j)\tilde\rho$, while $c_g\alpha(\tilde\rho)=gj\cdot\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}\cdot\tilde\rho$. So $T^n(j)j^{-1}=g\cdot j(\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1})j^{-1}$, which is $g\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}$ modulo $P_6$, as $[P_2,P_4]\subseteq P_6$. By Lemma 1 (a) there, $T^n(j)j^{-1}\in P_4$ has class $\partial^{T^n}_2(c_\alpha(\rho))$, and $\partial^{T^n}_2=n\partial^T_2$, as in the proof of Lemma 1 (b) there. Hence $n\,\partial^T_2(c_\alpha(\rho))=(1-\rho)(gP_5)$ for all $\rho$, so $n(\partial^T_2)_*u(\alpha)=0$. Then $np^m(\partial^T_2)_*v=0$, and $(\partial^T_2)_*v=0$ by Lemma 1 (c). $\square$

*Reductions.* Let $v\in\mathcal S$ and $X=\bar v$. If a tame-trivial $\theta$ acts on $V$ by $B$, then $\theta_2$ reduces to $\mathcal L_2(B)$, so the reduction of $\theta_*v$ is $\mathcal L_2(B)_\Gamma X$. By Lemma 1 (a) of the degree-one Johnson theorem, $\partial^T_2$ reduces to the restriction to $\mathcal L_2(V)$ of the map $\delta^{(2)}$ on $V\hat\otimes V$ that applies the reduction $\delta$ of $J(T)$ in one slot at a time and adds the results. So Lemma 3 gives:

- (i') $\mathcal L_2(B)_\Gamma X$ is the reduction of an element of $\mathcal S$, if $B$ is the action on $V$ of a tame-trivial automorphism;
- (ii') $\mathcal L_2(\bar\Psi)_\Gamma X=X$, if $\bar\Psi$ is the action on $V$ of an automorphism $\psi$ as in (ii);
- (iii') $\eta(\delta^{(2)}Y)=0$ for every $Y\in V\hat\otimes V$ whose class in $(V\hat\otimes V)_\Gamma$ is the image of $X$, since $\delta^{(2)}_\Gamma X=0$ in $\mathcal L_4(V)_\Gamma$ and $\delta^{(2)}$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant on $V\hat\otimes V$;
- (iv') if $X=0$, then $v\in p\mathcal S$, by Lemma 1 (b) and (u3).

*Coordinate rule.* Let $B$ be a $\Lambda$-linear automorphism of $V$ fixing $\bar x_0$, with $B(\mu\bar x_k)=\sum_j\mu\beta_{jk}\bar x_j$ for $k\ge1$, where $\beta_{jk}\in\Lambda$ and $j,k\ge1$. By Lemma 2 (b), applied in each slot, $Y=\mathcal L_2(B)_\Gamma X$ has coordinates
$$Y_{jl}=\sum_{k,m\ge1}\iota(\beta_{jk})\,X_{km}\,\beta_{lm}\quad(j,l\ge1),\qquad Y_{j0}=\sum_{k\ge1}\omega^{-1}(\beta_{jk})X_{k0}.\tag{R}$$

**Proof of the theorem.** Fix $v\in\mathcal S$, and let $X=\bar v$, with coordinates $X_{kl}$. By (i'), each step below also applies to the reduction of $\theta_*v$ for every tame-trivial $\theta$.

*1. Kondo's automorphisms.* As in step 1 of the degree-one Johnson theorem, every unipotent integral symplectic matrix $M$ of the surface lattice is induced by a tame-trivial $\psi$ that fixes the complementary generators, with $\psi_H-1$ nilpotent. Its action on $V$ is given by (R) with $\beta_{cc}=1$ for complementary $c$ and $\beta_{jk}=M_{jk}\in\mathbb Z$ for surface $j,k$, and the other $\beta_{jk}$ zero. By (ii') and (R), $X$ is fixed by $X\mapsto MXM^{\mathsf T}$ on the surface block, by $X_{kc}\mapsto\sum_jM_{kj}X_{jc}$ and $X_{ck}\mapsto\sum_jM_{kj}X_{cj}$ for surface $k$ and complementary $c$, and by the identity on the complementary block. Write $N$ for $M-1$.

- For $u_i:a_i\mapsto a_i+b_i$, $N$ sends $e_{a_i}$ to $e_{b_i}$, and invariance reads $NX+XN^{\mathsf T}+NXN^{\mathsf T}=0$. Its entries are: $X_{a_il}=0$ for $l\ne b_i$ (row $b_i$), $X_{ka_i}=0$ for $k\ne b_i$ (column $b_i$), and $X_{a_ib_i}+X_{b_ia_i}+X_{a_ia_i}=0$. Here $l$ and $k$ run over all indices, complementary ones included.
- For $l_i:b_i\mapsto b_i+a_i$, symmetrically $X_{b_il}=0$ for $l\ne a_i$ and $X_{kb_i}=0$ for $k\ne a_i$.
- For $i\ne i'$, $M_{ii'}:a_i\mapsto a_i+b_{i'}$, $a_{i'}\mapsto a_{i'}+b_i$. The $(b_{i'},b_i)$ entry of $NX+XN^{\mathsf T}+NXN^{\mathsf T}$ is $X_{a_ib_i}+X_{b_{i'}a_{i'}}+X_{a_ia_{i'}}$, so $X_{a_ib_i}=X_{a_{i'}b_{i'}}$.

So there is $y\in\Lambda$ with
$$X_{a_ib_i}=y,\quad X_{b_ia_i}=-y\ \ (1\le i\le r),\qquad\text{and all other coordinates with a surface index vanish.}\tag{1}$$

*2. The boundary twist.* Let $\bar a=\bar x_{a_1}$ and $\bar b=\bar x_{b_1}$. By step 3 of the degree-one Johnson theorem, $\delta$ is $\Lambda$-linear, kills $L$ and every $V_k$ with $k\notin\{a_1,b_1\}$, and $\delta(\bar a)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]=2\bar a\bar b\bar a-\bar b\bar a\bar a-\bar a\bar a\bar b$, $\delta(\bar b)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]$. By (1), Lemma 2 (a) and (c), the image of $X$ in $(V\hat\otimes V)_\Gamma$ is the class of
$$Y=Y_A+\sum_{i=1}^r\big(\bar x_{a_i}\otimes y\bar x_{b_i}-y\bar x_{b_i}\otimes\bar x_{a_i}\big),$$
with $Y_A$ in the sum of the components whose two factors are complementary. Indeed $\kappa_{a_ib_i}(\bar x_{a_i}\otimes y\bar x_{b_i})=y$, and $\kappa_{b_ia_i}(y\bar x_{b_i}\otimes\bar x_{a_i})=\iota(y)$, while $X_{b_ia_i}=-\iota(y)$ by Lemma 2 (c). Then $\delta^{(2)}Y=\delta^{(2)}(\bar a\otimes y\bar b-y\bar b\otimes\bar a)$. Apply $\eta$, which sees only words with letters in $V_{b_1},V_{a_1},V_{a_1},V_{b_1}$ in this order. In $\delta(\bar a)\otimes y\bar b$ only $-\bar b\bar a\bar a\otimes y\bar b$ qualifies, giving $-1\otimes1\otimes y$. In $y\bar b\otimes\delta(\bar a)$ only $-y\bar b\otimes\bar a\bar a\bar b$ qualifies, giving $-\Delta_3(\iota(y))$, as $(h,1,1,1)\mapsto(h^{-1},h^{-1},h^{-1})$. The terms $\bar a\otimes\delta(y\bar b)$ and $\delta(y\bar b)\otimes\bar a$ have a letter $\bar a$ in the first or last slot. As $y\bar b\otimes\bar a$ enters $Y$ with a minus sign, (iii') gives
$$0=\eta(\delta^{(2)}Y)=\Delta_3(\iota(y))-1\otimes1\otimes y.$$
Applying $\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon$ gives $\iota(y)=\epsilon(y)\cdot1$. So
$$y=c\cdot1\quad\text{for some }c\in\mathbb F_p.\tag{2}$$

*3. The transvection $T_1$ (even $d$).* By step 5 of abelianized wild inertia, $T_1$ fixes $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$, and is tame-trivial with $(T_1)_H-1$ of square $0$. Its action on $V$ is given by (R) with $\beta_{kk}=1$ and $\beta_{12}=1$. By (R), the image has coordinates $X_{1l}+X_{2l}$ in place of $X_{1l}$ for $l\ne1$, including $l=0$ and $l=2$. By (ii') they equal $X_{1l}$, so $X_{20}=0$ and $X_{22}=0$. Hence
$$X_{02}=X_{20}=0,\qquad X_{22}=0.\tag{3}$$

*4. The mixing automorphisms.* Let $\theta$ be tame-trivial, acting on $V$ by $B$. Step 1 applies to the reduction $X'=\mathcal L_2(B^{-1})_\Gamma X$ of $\theta^{-1}_*v$, so its coordinates $X'_{kc}$ vanish for surface $k$ and complementary $c$.

- *Even $d$.* Let $\lambda\in\ker\omega$, and let $\theta$ be given by part 2 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md). It is tame-trivial, and $B$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,4$, and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda\bar x_3$ and $B\bar x_4=\bar x_4-\lambda^*\bar x_2$. So $B^{-1}$ is given by (R) with $\beta_{kk}=1$, $\beta_{31}=-\lambda$, $\beta_{24}=\lambda^*$, and the rest zero. Here $(a_1,b_1)=(x_3,x_4)$, so (1) and (2) give $X_{34}=c$ and $X_{14}=X_{32}=X_{30}=0$. By (R):
$$X'_{30}=\omega^{-1}(\lambda)X_{01},\qquad X'_{31}=-\iota(\lambda)X_{11},\qquad X'_{32}=c\lambda^*-\iota(\lambda)X_{12}.$$
All three vanish. For $\lambda=\lambda_0$, $\lambda_0^*=\iota(\lambda_0)=\gamma^{-1}-1$, and left multiplication by it is injective, by Lemma 2 of the central-element construction applied to $\gamma^{-1}$. So $X_{11}=0$ and $X_{12}=c$. As $\omega\ne1$, choose $\rho$ with $\omega(\rho)\ne1$ and put $\lambda=\rho-\omega(\rho)\in\ker\omega$. Then $\lambda^*-\iota(\lambda)=(\omega(\rho)-1)\rho^{-1}$, so $0=c\lambda^*-\iota(\lambda)c=c(\omega(\rho)-1)\rho^{-1}$, and $c=0$. So $X_{12}=0$, and $X_{21}=-\iota(X_{12})=0$.
- *Odd $d$.* Let $\nu\in\ker\omega$, and let $\theta$ be given by part 3 of the mixing theorem: tame-trivial, with $B$ $\Lambda$-linear, fixing $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,3$, $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\nu\bar x_2$ and $B\bar x_3=\bar x_3-\nu^*\delta\nu\,\bar x_2-\nu^*\delta_0\,\bar x_1$. Then $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\nu\bar x_2$ and $B^{-1}\bar x_3=\bar x_3+\beta\bar x_2+\nu^*\delta_0\bar x_1$ with $\beta=\nu^*\delta\nu-\nu^*\delta_0\nu$, as $B\bar x_2=\bar x_2$. So (R) applies with $\beta_{kk}=1$, $\beta_{21}=-\nu$, $\beta_{23}=\beta$, $\beta_{13}=\nu^*\delta_0$. Here $(a_1,b_1)=(x_2,x_3)$, so $X_{23}=c$ and $X_{13}=X_{31}=X_{33}=X_{21}=X_{20}=X_{30}=0$. By (R):
$$X'_{20}=\omega^{-1}(\nu)X_{01},\qquad X'_{21}=c\,\nu^*\delta_0-\iota(\nu)X_{11}.$$
Both vanish. For $\nu=\lambda_0$ this gives $\iota(\lambda_0)(c\delta_0-X_{11})=0$, so $X_{11}=c\delta_0$. For $\nu=\rho-\omega(\rho)$ with $\omega(\rho)\ne1$ it gives $c(\nu^*-\iota(\nu))\delta_0=c(\omega(\rho)-1)\rho^{-1}\delta_0=0$. By part 1 of the mixing theorem, $\delta_0\ne0$, as its image under the unramified quadratic character is nonzero. So $c=0$ and $X_{11}=0$.

In both cases
$$c=0,\qquad\text{the complementary coordinates other than }X_{01},X_{10}\text{ vanish},\qquad\omega^{-1}(\nu)X_{01}=0\ \ (\nu\in\ker\omega).\tag{4}$$

*5. Conclusion.* By (1)–(4) and Lemma 2 (c), $X=X_{01}E$, since $E$ has coordinates $E_{01}=\kappa_{01}(\bar x_0\otimes\bar x_1)=1$, $E_{10}=-1$, and $0$ otherwise.

(a) Let $e\ge3$. Choose $\rho$ with $\omega(\rho)^2\ne1$ and $\nu=\rho-\omega(\rho)\in\ker\omega$. Then $\omega^{-1}(\nu)=\omega(\rho)^{-1}-\omega(\rho)\ne0$, so $X_{01}=0$ by (4), and $X=0$. Thus $\bar v=0$ for every $v\in\mathcal S$, and $v\in p\mathcal S$ by (iv'). For $u=u(\alpha)\in\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal S$ this gives $u\in p^k\mathcal S$ for every $k$, so $\Phi_{\mathcal L_2}(u)\in\bigcap_kp^k(\mathcal L_2)_\Gamma=0$, as $(\mathcal L_2)_\Gamma$ is a compact abelian pro-$p$ group. So $u=0$ by Lemma 1 (a), and (u4) gives $h$.

(b) Let $e=2$. Then $\bar v\in\mathbb F_pE$ for every $v\in\mathcal S$. If $\mathcal S\subseteq pH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_2)$, then $\mathcal S=p\mathcal S$ by (u3), so $\mathcal S=0$ as in (a); take $w=0$. Otherwise choose $v_0\in\mathcal S\setminus pH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_2)$. By Lemma 1 (b), $\bar v_0\ne0$, so $\bar v_0=c_0E$ with $c_0\ne0$. For $v\in\mathcal S$, $\bar v=a\bar v_0$ for some $a\in\mathbb Z$, so $v-av_0\in\mathcal S\cap pH^1=p\mathcal S$, by Lemma 1 (b) and (u3). Iterating, $v=s_kv_0+p^kv_k$ with $v_k\in\mathcal S$ and $s_k\in\mathbb Z$, $s_{k+1}\equiv s_k\pmod{p^k}$. Let $s=\lim s_k\in\mathbb Z_p$. Then $\Phi(v)-s\Phi(v_0)\in\bigcap_kp^k(\mathcal L_2)_\Gamma=0$, with $\Phi=\Phi_{\mathcal L_2}$. Take $w=\Phi(v_0)$; every $u(\alpha')$ lies in $\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal S$. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** As $e\ge3$, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. By the corollary of the degree-one Johnson theorem, $\alpha=c_g\circ\alpha_0$ with $\alpha_0$ inducing the identity on $G/P_2$ and on $P/P_3$. It represents the outer class of $\alpha$, so $\alpha_0\in\mathcal A$. By part (a), $\alpha_1=c_h\alpha_0$ induces the identity on $G/P_3$ for some $h\in P_2$, and $\alpha=c_{gh^{-1}}\alpha_1$. If $c_k\alpha_1$ also induces the identity on $G/P_3$, then so does $c_k$. So $c_k$ is tame-trivial, and $k\in P$ since $\Gamma$ has trivial centre (part 3 of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). Then $kP_2\in H^\Gamma=0$, so $k\in P_2$. Also $(1-\rho)(kP_3)=0$ for all $\rho$, so $kP_3\in\mathcal L_2^\Gamma=0$ by Lemma 1 (c), and $k\in P_3$. As $P_3$ is characteristic, $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_3)$ is defined, and it maps the class of $\alpha$ to the class of $\alpha_1$, which is trivial. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* The class $u(\alpha)$ lies in $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_2)$, which embeds in $(\mathcal L_2)_\Gamma$. Unlike $H_\Gamma$ in [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md), this is not finitely generated over $\mathbb Z_p$: by Lemma 2 (a), the coinvariants of each $V_k\hat\otimes V_l$ are a copy of $\Lambda$. Kondo's symplectic automorphisms must fix the class. They kill every mixed coordinate, and they force the surface block to be $y$ times the symplectic form, for one $y\in\Lambda$. The boundary twist, read in the tensor pattern $\bar b\bar a\bar a\bar b$, forces $y$ to be a constant. The mixing automorphisms are known only modulo the Frattini subgroup, so the $p$-adic layers are peeled off as in the degree-one Johnson theorem: every condition is linear, and $\mathcal S$ is stable under division by $p$. Conjugation by a mixing automorphism moves the constant and the complementary coordinates onto mixed ones. The parameter $\lambda_0$ ties $X_{12}$, or $X_{11}$ for odd $d$, to the constant. A parameter $\rho-\omega(\rho)$ with $\omega(\rho)\ne1$, for which $\lambda^*-\iota(\lambda)$ is a unit, then forces the constant to be $0$.
- *What the boundary twist cannot see.* For $w=[\bar a,\bar b]$, $\delta^{(2)}w=[[w,\bar a],\bar b]+[\bar a,[w,\bar b]]=[w,w]=0$ by the Jacobi identity, matching the fact that $T$ fixes $[a,b]$. So step 2 leaves the constant multiples of the symplectic form, and step 4 is needed for them.
- *The remaining coordinate.* The coordinate $X_{01}$ pairs $\bar x_0$ with the $\omega^{-1}$-part of the $\bar x_1$-coefficient. In step 4, conjugation by an automorphism acting by $\bar x_1\mapsto\bar x_1-\nu\bar x_k$, with $k$ a surface index, moves it onto the surface coordinate $X'_{k0}$ with the factor $\omega^{-1}(\nu)$, by (R). The mixing theorem supplies only $\nu\in\ker\omega$, and $\omega^{-1}=\omega$ when $e=2$. Whether $X_{01}$ can be nonzero when $e=2$, or whether another automorphism removes it, is not decided here. For $p=3$, $e\le2$, so only part (b) applies; part (a) needs $p\ge5$.
- *Related results.* For $p$ odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$ and $e\ge3$, the corollary places the normal lattices of [the normal-lattice theorem](finite-conjugacy-outer-classes-generate-normal-p-adic-lattices.md) in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/[P,[P,P]])$.
- *What remains.* (1) The coordinate $X_{01}$ when $e=2$. (2) The case $\mu_p\subseteq K$, where $V^\Gamma=L\ne0$, so the cited proof of $H^\Gamma=0$ no longer applies, and the normalization of $\alpha$ to $\mathcal A$ is known only under the conditions of [the case with roots of unity](finite-outer-conjugacy-modulo-wild-commutators-when-k-contains-mu-p.md). (3) Degrees $3$ and $4$: the theorem holds there, but whether a finite-conjugacy automorphism trivial on $G/P_2$ is automatically trivial on $P/P_3$ is not known, so the corollary needs degree at least $5$. (4) The next layers. For $\alpha_1$ as in the corollary, the degree-two Johnson map $H\to\mathcal L_3$, $xP_2\mapsto\alpha_1(x)x^{-1}P_4$, is $\Gamma$-equivariant, by the argument of the remark *What remains* in the cocycle theorem with $P_3$ in place of $P_2$. After it comes a cocycle $\Gamma\to\mathcal L_3$, and so on; innerness of $\alpha$ is equivalent to innerness on every $G/P_j$. This continues item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535).
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, every $s$, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ for the theorem, with $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$ for part (a), and $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$ with $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$ for the corollary. Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
