**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, let $K/\mathbb Q_p$ have degree $d\ge3$ and residue cardinality $q$, and put
$$G=G_K,\qquad P=P_K,\qquad \Gamma=G/P,\qquad
\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]],\qquad V=P/P^{(2)},$$
where $P^{(2)}=P^p[P,P]$. All groups, maps and group algebras are topological, and subgroups generated by commutators are closed. Use the presentation and notation of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md). In particular
$$V=L\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^d\Lambda\bar x_j,
\qquad L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0,\qquad
\rho\bar x_0=\omega(\rho)\bar x_0,$$
where $\omega$ is the character on $\mu_p$. Write $W=V/L$ and $e_j=\bar x_j+L$. The complementary free summand $C\subset W$ is $\Lambda e_1$ for odd $d$ and $\Lambda e_1\oplus\Lambda e_2$ for even $d$. The first surface pair $(e_s,e_t)$ is $(e_2,e_3)$ or $(e_3,e_4)$, respectively. An automorphism is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Suppose $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is tame-trivial and its class has finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. Then $\alpha$ preserves $L$, and there is a unique
$$z_\alpha\in Z(\Lambda)^\times$$
such that its action on $W$ is $w\mapsto z_\alpha w$. No finite-order assumption is needed, and no condition is imposed on the complementary generators.

**Corollary 1 (proved here).** Let $B$ be a finite subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ of order prime to $p$. Suppose every element is tame-trivial and has finitely many outer conjugates. If $\alpha|_L=c_\alpha$, then
$$\alpha\longmapsto(c_\alpha,z_\alpha)
\quad\text{embeds }B\text{ into }
\mathbb F_p^\times\times Z(\Lambda)^\times.$$
In particular $B$ is abelian.

**Corollary 2 (proved here).** Let $N\ne G$ be a closed normal subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ isomorphic to $G$, and suppose $M=N\cap G$ is open with $m=(G:M)$ prime to $p$. Write $M=G_{K'}$. Then $K'/K$ is tame and abelian, and
$$e(K'/K)\mid q-1.$$
For $p\ge5$, the index condition is automatic by [the rival-copy reduction](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-index-prime-to-p.md), so the conclusion holds for every rival copy meeting $G$ openly.

**Proof of the theorem.** Let $A$ be the induced automorphism of $V$. Tame triviality makes $A$ $\Lambda$-linear. The [separation lemma](finite-outer-conjugacy-detects-complementary-wild-generators.md) says that $A$ preserves $L$, that its induced action on $W$ preserves $C$, and that for every surface generator
$$A(e_j)=z_j e_j\qquad(z_j\in\Lambda^\times).\tag{1}$$
The coefficients are units: the same assertions for $\alpha^{-1}$ give an inverse on each surface summand. We use the lemma also for conjugates of $\alpha$ by tame-trivial automorphisms.

*1. The surface coefficients are central.* For the first pair, let $\Theta_\lambda$ be the action on $V$ of the unit-scaling automorphism from [the unit theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md), with $\lambda\in\Lambda^\times$. It sends $e_s$ to $\lambda e_s$, $e_t$ to $(\lambda^*)^{-1}e_t$, and fixes the other generators, including $\bar x_0$. The restrictions $\Theta_\lambda A\Theta_\lambda^{-1}$ take only finitely many values. Indeed, their lifts to $G$ have only finitely many outer classes. If two lifts differ by conjugation by $g\in G$, their identity action on $\Gamma$ implies that $gP\in Z(\Gamma)=1$. Thus $g\in P$, whose conjugation acts trivially on $V$.

Consequently
$$H=\{\lambda\in\Lambda^\times:\Theta_\lambda A=A\Theta_\lambda\}$$
is a subgroup of finite index. It is closed: the entries of $\Theta_\lambda$ and its inverse are continuous functions of $\lambda$, and the commutation equations are closed equations in the finite matrix of coefficients of $A$. The unit group is profinite, being the inverse limit of the unit groups of the finite group algebras. A closed subgroup of finite index is open, so $H$ contains a neighborhood of $1$.

Use $\varepsilon_a,\eta_a\in Z(\Lambda)$ from Lemma 1 of [the central-element construction](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md), for positive $a$ prime to $p$ and divisible by $p-1$. They satisfy
$$\varepsilon_a\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\eta_a^{p^k}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
\eta_a^u b=0\Longrightarrow\varepsilon_a b=0\quad(u\ge1).\tag{2}$$
Fix $\rho\in\Gamma$. The elements $\lambda_k=1+\eta_a^{p^k}\rho$ are units converging to $1$. To see invertibility, in each finite quotient $\eta_a$ is nilpotent, and its centrality makes $\eta_a^{p^k}\rho$ nilpotent there; the geometric-series inverses are compatible. For large $k$, $\lambda_k\in H$. Comparing the $e_s$ coefficients in the commutation equation gives $\lambda_k z_s=z_s\lambda_k$, whence
$$\eta_a^{p^k}(\rho z_s-z_s\rho)=0.$$
By (2), $\varepsilon_a(\rho z_s-z_s\rho)=0$. Letting $a$ tend along divisibility proves that $z_s$ commutes with every $\rho$, hence with $\Lambda$ by density and continuity. Integral symplectic changes of the surface generators exchange the two members of a pair and exchange pairs. Apply this argument to the corresponding conjugates of $\alpha$ to obtain $z_j\in Z(\Lambda)^\times$ for every surface generator.

*2. There is a common surface coefficient.* Kondo's tame-trivial automorphisms fix the complementary generators and realize every integral symplectic change of the surface basis. Such changes can put the sum of any two distinct surface basis vectors into the basis. Explicitly, within one pair this is a transvection. For vectors in different pairs, replacing $u_i$ by $u_i+u_j$ and the dual partner $v_j$ by $v_j-v_i$ preserves the symplectic form; exchanges and signed exchanges within pairs cover the other choices. Apply the separation lemma to the conjugate of $\alpha$ by such a change. It says that $A$ preserves the $\Lambda$-line generated by that sum in $W$. Equation (1), compared in its two coordinates, gives $z_i=z_j$. Denote the common central unit by $z$.

*3. Mixing determines the complementary block.* Put $\gamma=\sigma^{\pi_p}$, where $\pi_p\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ has $p$-component $1$ and all other components $0$, and let $\nu=\gamma^{-1}-1$. Then $\omega(\nu)=0$, $\nu^*=\gamma-1$, and both left and right multiplication by either $\nu$ or $\nu^*$ are injective on $\Lambda$, by Lemma 2 of the central-element construction. Write $A$ also for its action on $W$.

For odd $d$, write $A(e_1)=\beta e_1$. The mixing automorphism $\varphi$ of part 3 of the mixing theorem, with parameter $\nu$, sends $e_1$ to $e_1+\nu e_s$ and preserves $L$. Apply the separation lemma to $\varphi^{-1}\alpha\varphi$: it implies that $A$ preserves $\varphi(C)$. Comparing the two coordinates in
$$A(e_1+\nu e_s)=\beta e_1+\nu z e_s\in\Lambda(e_1+\nu e_s)$$
gives $\beta\nu=\nu z=z\nu$, hence $\beta=z$.

For even $d$, write
$$A(e_1)=\beta_{11}e_1+\beta_{12}e_2,\qquad
A(e_2)=\beta_{21}e_1+\beta_{22}e_2.$$
Part 2 of the mixing theorem gives $\varphi$ with
$$\varphi(e_1)=e_1+\nu e_s,\quad
\varphi(e_t)=e_t-\nu^*e_2,\quad
\varphi(e_2)=e_2,\quad\varphi(e_s)=e_s.$$
Again $A$ preserves $\varphi(C)$. Vectors in this submodule have $e_s$ coefficient equal to their $e_1$ coefficient multiplied on the right by $\nu$. Applying this to $A\varphi(e_1)$ and $A\varphi(e_2)$ gives $\beta_{11}\nu=\nu z$ and $\beta_{21}\nu=0$, so $\beta_{11}=z$ and $\beta_{21}=0$. The separation lemma for the same conjugate says that $A$ preserves $\Lambda\varphi(e_t)$. Comparison of its $e_t$ and $e_2$ coordinates gives
$$\nu^*\beta_{22}=z\nu^*=\nu^*z,$$
so $\beta_{22}=z$. At this point $A(e_1)=ze_1+be_2$, $A(e_2)=ze_2$, with $b=\beta_{12}$.

The exact transvection $T$ in the Setting of [the even-degree entry](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md) fixes the tame group, the surface generators and $x_1$, and sends $x_2$ to $x_2x_1$. Thus $T(e_2)=e_2+e_1$. The preceding calculation applies to $T^{-1}\alpha T$ as well. Its surface coefficient is still $z$, so its $e_1$ coefficient at $e_1$ must be $z$. But directly
$$T^{-1}AT(e_1)=(z-b)e_1+be_2.$$
Hence $b=0$. This proves $A|_W=zI$ in both parities. Its coefficient at $e_s$ proves uniqueness. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 1.** The actions on $L$ and $W$ make $\alpha\mapsto(c_\alpha,z_\alpha)$ a homomorphism; centrality of the $z_\alpha$ removes the order reversal from right multiplication on a left free module. If both coordinates are $1$, then $D=A-1$ sends $V$ into $L$ and kills $L$, so $D^2=0$. If $\alpha$ has order $r$ prime to $p$, then
$$1=A^r=(1+D)^r=1+rD,$$
which gives $D=0$. Part 2 of [finite-order detection](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-on-the-frattini-quotient-of-wild-inertia.md) now gives $\alpha=1$. The target is abelian, proving the assertion. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 2.** Use parts 1 to 3 of [the prime-to-$p$ rival-copy theorem](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-index-prime-to-p.md). They give common wild inertia $P\subset M$, the group $J=NG\subset\operatorname{Aut}(G)$, and
$$J/P=\Gamma\times Q,\qquad
Q\cong J/G\cong\operatorname{Gal}(K'/K),\qquad |Q|=m.$$
Let $J_0$ be the inverse image of $Q$ in $J$. The extension $1\to P\to J_0\to Q\to1$ has a finite complement $B\cong Q$, since $P$ is pro-$p$ and $|Q|$ is prime to $p$. Here is the splitting argument to make this input explicit.

In a finite extension with $p$-group kernel $R$, induct on $|R|$. If $R\ne1$, its characteristic subgroup $Z=\{x\in Z(R):x^p=1\}$ is nontrivial elementary abelian. Split the quotient by $Z$ by induction, and take the inverse image of that complement. Its kernel is $Z$. Such an abelian-kernel extension splits by averaging: for an additive factor set $f(a,b)\in Z$, its cocycle equation shows that
$$t(a)=|Q|^{-1}\sum_{x\in Q}f(a,x),\qquad
f(a,b)=a\,t(b)+t(a)-t(ab).$$
Changing the chosen section by $-t$ gives a homomorphism. For $J_0$, apply the finite result to each quotient by an open normal subgroup contained in $P$. Complements map to complements in smaller quotients. These finite nonempty sets have a nonempty inverse limit: for any finitely many quotients, a complement in a common refinement provides compatible choices. The inverse limit of the chosen subgroups maps isomorphically onto $Q$, giving $B$.

Every element of $B$ is tame-trivial, since its image in $J/P$ lies in $Q$, which centralizes $\Gamma$. Its outer class lies in the finite normal subgroup $J/G$ of $\operatorname{Out}(G)$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates. Corollary 1 makes $B$, and hence $Q$, abelian. Tameness of $K'/K$ was already given by $P\subset M$. In an abelian finite tame quotient of $\Gamma$, the relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^q$ gives $\tau^{q-1}=1$. The order of the inertia image is $e(K'/K)$, proving the divisibility. $\square$

**Scope and checks.** The theorem permits arbitrary action on the line $L$ and does not force the central unit $z_\alpha$ to be $1$. Thus it does not exclude rival copies, and says nothing about copies whose intersection with $G$ has infinite index. Finite outer conjugacy is used with the actual lifted unit and mixing automorphisms of $G$, not with the full automorphism group of a wild-abelian quotient. Degree $3$ has one surface pair and a single complementary free generator, so Steps 1 to 3 still apply. Even degree first occurs at $d=4$. Without the exact transvection, a nonzero shear $e_1\mapsto e_1+be_2$ survives the preceding module constraints; conjugation by $T$ is what eliminates it. In Corollary 1 the order condition is used to cancel $r$ in characteristic $p$: the calculation alone would allow a nonzero shear into $L$ at order $p$. No case at $p=2$ or $d\le2$ is asserted. The presentation and lifting theorems are used through the linked entries on main; their underlying sources are not reverified here.
