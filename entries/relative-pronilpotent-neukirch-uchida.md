**Setting.** Fix an algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, and let $K \subset \overline{\mathbb Q}$ be a number field. For a prime $\ell$, let $K(\ell)$ be the maximal pro-$\ell$ extension of $K$ and $G_K(\ell) = \mathrm{Gal}(K(\ell)/K)$. Let $K^{\mathrm{nil}}$ be the maximal pronilpotent extension and $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}} = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{nil}}/K)$. For number fields $K_1, K_2$, write $\mathrm{Iso}(K_2^{\mathrm{nil}}/K_2, K_1^{\mathrm{nil}}/K_1)$ for the field isomorphisms $\tau : K_2^{\mathrm{nil}} \to K_1^{\mathrm{nil}}$ with $\tau(K_2) = K_1$. Each one induces the isomorphism $g \mapsto \tau^{-1} g \tau$ from $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{nil}}$ to $G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{nil}}$, and similarly for $K_i(\ell)$. This follows the conventions of [Shimizu's theorem](pro-c-neukirch-uchida.md). Since $K\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}} \subset K^{\mathrm{nil}}$, restriction gives a map $r_K : G_K^{\mathrm{nil}} \to \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb Q)$.

**Theorem (proved here).**

1. *Product decomposition.* $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}} = \prod_\ell G_K(\ell)$, and each factor is characteristic. So the isomorphisms $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{nil}} \to G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{nil}}$ are exactly the products of families of isomorphisms $\sigma_\ell : G_{K_1}(\ell) \to G_{K_2}(\ell)$, and $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{nil}}) = \prod_\ell \mathrm{Out}(G_K(\ell))$.
2. *Relative reconstruction.* Let $\sigma : G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{nil}} \to G_{K_2}^{\mathrm{nil}}$ be an isomorphism with $r_{K_2} \circ \sigma = r_{K_1}$. Then:
   - $\sigma_2$ is induced by a unique element of $\mathrm{Iso}(K_2(2)/K_2, K_1(2)/K_1)$, so in particular $K_1 \cong K_2$;
   - if $K_1$ has a complex place, each $\sigma_\ell$ is induced by a unique element of $\mathrm{Iso}(K_2(\ell)/K_2, K_1(\ell)/K_1)$;
   - if, in addition, $\mathrm{Aut}(K_1) = 1$, then $\sigma$ is induced by a unique element of $\mathrm{Iso}(K_2^{\mathrm{nil}}/K_2, K_1^{\mathrm{nil}}/K_1)$.
3. *Non-functoriality.* For each $\ell$, let $\iota_\ell : \mathrm{Aut}(K) \to \mathrm{Out}(G_K(\ell))$ send $s$ to the class of $g \mapsto \tilde s g \tilde s^{-1}$, where $\tilde s$ is any extension of $s$ to $K(\ell)$. Then:
   - each $\iota_\ell$ is an injective homomorphism;
   - the classes in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{nil}})$ induced by field automorphisms of $K^{\mathrm{nil}}$ preserving $K$ are exactly the diagonal elements $(\iota_\ell(s))_\ell$;
   - every element of $\prod_\ell \iota_\ell(\mathrm{Aut}(K))$ has a representative $\alpha$ with $r_K \circ \alpha = r_K$.

   So if $\mathrm{Aut}(K) \ne 1$, the group $\prod_\ell \mathrm{Aut}(K)$ embeds in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{nil}})$. It is uncountable, and all but $|\mathrm{Aut}(K)|$ of its elements are not induced by any field automorphism.

**Consequences.**

- *No relative counterexamples.* Part 2 says that an isomorphism of pronilpotent quotients over $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb Q)$ cannot exist between non-isomorphic number fields. Only its pro-$2$ part is used, so compatibility with the maximal pro-$2$ quotient of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb Q)$ suffices. A counterexample to [the pronilpotent problem](pronilpotent-neukirch-uchida-problem.md) would therefore need an isomorphism that is not of this form.
- *Functoriality fails.* By part 3, the functorial analogue of Neukirch–Uchida fails for $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}}$ whenever $\mathrm{Aut}(K) \ne 1$, for example for every quadratic field. For $K = \mathbb Q(i)$, extend complex conjugation to some $\tilde c \in \mathrm{Aut}(K(2))$. The automorphism that conjugates the factor $G_K(2)$ by $\tilde c$ and fixes every other factor is not induced by any field automorphism of $K^{\mathrm{nil}}$, although it commutes with $r_K$. So the hypothesis $\mathrm{Aut}(K_1) = 1$ in part 2 cannot be dropped. This contrasts with [Shimizu's theorem](pro-c-neukirch-uchida.md), under which $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathcal C}) \cong \mathrm{Aut}(K)$ when $\Sigma(\mathcal C)$ has positive density [@shimizu2023proc, Corollary 7.6, p. 30].

**Proof.** Two inputs from Shimizu are used, for the full class of finite $\ell$-groups, whose maximal pro-$\mathcal C$ quotient of $G_K$ is $G_K(\ell)$:

- (S1) a field automorphism $u$ of $K(\ell)$ with $u(K) = K$ that commutes with every element of $G_K(\ell)$ is trivial [@shimizu2023proc, Proposition 1.5, p. 6];
- (S2) the relative theorem, Corollary 7.11, with its conditions (a) and (b) as recorded in [Shimizu's theorem](pro-c-neukirch-uchida.md) [@shimizu2023proc, Corollary 7.11, p. 34].

One standard fact is also used. If $s$ is an isomorphism between number fields $K \to K'$ in $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, extend it to an automorphism $\hat s$ of $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. Then $\hat s(K(\ell)) = K'(\ell)$ and $\hat s(K^{\mathrm{nil}}) = K'^{\mathrm{nil}}$, because these fields are defined by a maximality property over $K$ inside $\overline{\mathbb Q}$.

*Part 1.* A pronilpotent group is the direct product of its Sylow subgroups, as Karshon and Shusterman also use [@karshonshusterman2026, §1, p. 1]. So $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}} = \prod_\ell P_\ell$, where $P_\ell$ is normal and is therefore the unique Sylow pro-$\ell$ subgroup, so it is characteristic. A continuous homomorphism from a pro-$\ell'$ group to a pro-$\ell$ group is trivial for $\ell' \ne \ell$. Hence every pro-$\ell$ quotient of $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}}$ factors through the projection onto $P_\ell$, so $P_\ell$ is the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}}$. Every pro-$\ell$ quotient of $G_K$ is pronilpotent and so factors through $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}}$. So $P_\ell$ is identified, by restriction, with $G_K(\ell)$, and $K^{\mathrm{nil}}$ is the compositum of the $K(\ell)$. An isomorphism $\sigma$ carries $P_\ell$ onto the Sylow pro-$\ell$ subgroup of the target, which defines $\sigma_\ell$. Conversely, any family $(\sigma_\ell)$ defines the continuous isomorphism $\prod_\ell \sigma_\ell$. Conjugation by $g = (g_\ell)_\ell$ is $\prod_\ell$ (conjugation by $g_\ell$). So $\mathrm{Aut}$ and $\mathrm{Inn}$ both decompose as products, and so does $\mathrm{Out}$.

*Part 2.* Let $\pi_\ell$ be the projection of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb Q)$ onto its maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab},(\ell)}/\mathbb Q)$, and let $p_{i,\ell} : G_{K_i}^{\mathrm{nil}} \to G_{K_i}(\ell)$ be the projection. The extension $K_i\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab},(\ell)}/K_i$ is pro-$\ell$, so $\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab},(\ell)} \subset K_i(\ell)$, and restriction to $\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab},(\ell)}$ factors through restriction to $K_i(\ell)$. That is, $\pi_\ell \circ r_{K_i} = \mathrm{res}_{i,\ell} \circ p_{i,\ell}$. Here $\mathrm{res}_{i,\ell} : G_{K_i}(\ell) \to \mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab},(\ell)}/\mathbb Q)$ is restriction, the map in (S2) (b). Using $p_{2,\ell} \circ \sigma = \sigma_\ell \circ p_{1,\ell}$,
$$
\mathrm{res}_{2,\ell} \circ \sigma_\ell \circ p_{1,\ell} = \pi_\ell \circ r_{K_2} \circ \sigma = \pi_\ell \circ r_{K_1} = \mathrm{res}_{1,\ell} \circ p_{1,\ell}.
$$
As $p_{1,\ell}$ is surjective, $\mathrm{res}_{2,\ell} \circ \sigma_\ell = \mathrm{res}_{1,\ell}$, which is condition (b) of (S2) for $\Sigma = \{\ell\}$. Condition (a) holds when $\ell = 2$, and for every $\ell$ when $K_1$ has a complex place. So (S2) gives the unique $\tau_\ell \in \mathrm{Iso}(K_2(\ell)/K_2, K_1(\ell)/K_1)$ inducing $\sigma_\ell$. Restricting $\tau_2$ to $K_2$ gives $K_2 \cong K_1$.

Now let $K_1$ have a complex place and $\mathrm{Aut}(K_1) = 1$. Any two isomorphisms $K_2 \to K_1$ differ by an element of $\mathrm{Aut}(K_1)$, so there is exactly one, $\tau_0$, and $\tau_\ell|_{K_2} = \tau_0$ for every $\ell$. Extend $\tau_0$ to an automorphism $T$ of $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, so that $T(K_2(\ell)) = K_1(\ell)$ and $T(K_2^{\mathrm{nil}}) = K_1^{\mathrm{nil}}$. Then $h_\ell = \tau_\ell \circ T^{-1}|_{K_1(\ell)}$ fixes $K_1$, so $h_\ell \in G_{K_1}(\ell)$. By part 1 there is $h \in G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{nil}}$ with $h|_{K_1(\ell)} = h_\ell$ for all $\ell$. Put $\tau = h \circ T|_{K_2^{\mathrm{nil}}}$. Then $\tau(K_2) = K_1$ and $\tau|_{K_2(\ell)} = \tau_\ell$, so the isomorphism induced by $\tau$ has components $\sigma_\ell$. By part 1 it equals $\sigma$.

For uniqueness, suppose $\tau, \tau'$ both induce $\sigma$. Then $u = \tau' \tau^{-1}$ is a field automorphism of $K_1^{\mathrm{nil}}$ with $u(K_1) = K_1$ that commutes with every element of $G_{K_1}^{\mathrm{nil}}$. Its restriction to each $K_1(\ell)$ is trivial by (S1), so $u = 1$, since $K_1^{\mathrm{nil}}$ is the compositum of the $K_1(\ell)$.

*Part 3.* By the standard fact, $s \in \mathrm{Aut}(K)$ extends to some $\tilde s \in \mathrm{Aut}(K(\ell))$, and $g \mapsto \tilde s g \tilde s^{-1}$ maps $G_K(\ell)$ to itself, because it fixes $\tilde s(K) = K$. Two extensions differ by an element of $G_K(\ell)$, so the class $\iota_\ell(s)$ is well defined. If $\tilde s, \tilde t$ extend $s, t$, then $\tilde s \tilde t$ extends $st$, so $\iota_\ell$ is a homomorphism.

- *Injectivity.* Suppose conjugation by $\tilde s$ equals conjugation by some $g \in G_K(\ell)$. Then $u = g^{-1} \tilde s$ satisfies $u(K) = K$ and commutes with every element of $G_K(\ell)$, so $u = 1$ by (S1). Thus $\tilde s = g$ fixes $K$, and $s = 1$.
- *Field-induced classes.* A field automorphism $\tau$ of $K^{\mathrm{nil}}$ with $\tau G_K^{\mathrm{nil}} \tau^{-1} = G_K^{\mathrm{nil}}$ preserves the fixed field $K$ of $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}}$, so only automorphisms preserving $K$ can induce automorphisms of $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}}$. Restriction maps the group of field automorphisms of $K^{\mathrm{nil}}$ preserving $K$ onto $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$, by the standard fact, with kernel $G_K^{\mathrm{nil}}$. If $\tau$ is such an automorphism with $\tau|_K = s$, then conjugation by $\tau$ has $\ell$-component conjugation by $\tau|_{K(\ell)}$, which extends $s$. So its class is $(\iota_\ell(s))_\ell$, and every diagonal element arises this way. The induced classes thus form the diagonal image of $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$, of order $|\mathrm{Aut}(K)|$, by injectivity.
- *The embedding.* By part 1 and injectivity, $(s_\ell)_\ell \mapsto (\iota_\ell(s_\ell))_\ell$ embeds $\prod_\ell \mathrm{Aut}(K)$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K^{\mathrm{nil}})$. If $\mathrm{Aut}(K) \ne 1$ this product over infinitely many primes is uncountable, while the induced classes are finite in number.
- *Compatibility with $r_K$.* Given $(s_\ell)_\ell$, choose extensions $\tilde s_\ell \in \mathrm{Aut}(K(\ell))$, and let $\alpha$ act on the factor $G_K(\ell)$ by conjugation by $\tilde s_\ell$. The field $\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab},(\ell)}$ is Galois over $\mathbb Q$, so $\tilde s_\ell$ restricts to an element of the abelian group $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab},(\ell)}/\mathbb Q)$. Hence $\mathrm{res}_\ell(\tilde s_\ell g \tilde s_\ell^{-1}) = \mathrm{res}_\ell(g)$ for $g \in G_K(\ell)$. The abelian profinite group $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathbb Q)$ is the product of its maximal pro-$\ell$ quotients, and $\pi_\ell \circ r_K = \mathrm{res}_\ell \circ p_\ell$ as in part 2. So $r_K \circ \alpha = r_K$. $\square$

**Checks.** For $K = \mathbb Q$, part 3 is empty and part 2 gives only that $\sigma_2$ is induced by a unique automorphism of $\mathbb Q(2)$, which is then an element of $G_{\mathbb Q}(2)$. For a totally real $K$ and odd $\ell$, part 2 says nothing about $\sigma_\ell$, matching condition (a) of (S2).

**Status.** Parts 2 and 3 rest on Shimizu's results, which are cited from an arXiv preprint with no journal reference as of November 2026.
