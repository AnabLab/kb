**Statement.** Let $k$ be a number field and $D \in k^\times$. Use the notation of [Hoshi's question on moderate points](l-moderate-points-and-pro-l-kernel-fields.md): for a hyperbolic curve $C$ over $k$, $\Omega_C$ is the field cut out by the pro-$\ell$ outer Galois representation, $k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}\ell}$ is the field generated by the $\ell$-moderate closed points, and $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ is $\Omega_C$ for the tripod $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$.

1. *($\ell = 2$.)* Let $E$ be $y^2 = x^3 - Dx$ and $C = E \setminus \{O\}$. Then $\Omega_C = k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}2}$, and
$$\Omega_C = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot k(E[4]) = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(D^{1/4}).$$
2. *($\ell = 3$.)* Let $E$ be $y^2 = x^3 + D$ and $C = E \setminus \{O\}$. Then $\Omega_C = k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}3}$, and
$$\Omega_C = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot k(E[3]) = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(\sqrt{D}, \sqrt[3]{4D}).$$
3. *Different fields.* In either case, if $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(D)$ is odd for some prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $k$ not above $\ell$, then $\Omega_C \ne \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$. For example, this holds for $y^2 = x^3 - 3x$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ at $\ell = 2$ and for $y^2 = x^3 + 5$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ at $\ell = 3$.

These curves have $j$-invariant $1728$ and $0$, and the proofs use their automorphisms of order $4$ and $3$ that fix $O$. The whole statement is checked here. It combines results of Hoshi's paper on kernels and moderate points through the [transfer principles](moderate-point-equality-transfer-principles.md), cited below as parts 1, 2 and 3 of that entry. No source opened for this entry states it, and a search in November 2026 found none, though it was not exhaustive.

**Step 0: removing torsion does not change the kernel field.** This is checked here, for any elliptic curve $E$ over $k$, any prime $\ell$ and any $n \ge 1$: $\Omega_{E \setminus E[\ell^n]} = \Omega_{E \setminus \{O\}}$. Multiplication by $\ell^n$ maps $E \setminus E[\ell^n]$, the full preimage of $E \setminus \{O\}$, finite étale onto $E \setminus \{O\}$. Over $\bar k$ it is Galois with group $E[\ell^n](\bar k)$, acting by translation, and $E \setminus E[\ell^n]$ is geometrically connected. So it arises from an open subgroup of $\Pi_{E \setminus \{O\}}$, by the proof of part 1. Let $Q \in E(\bar k)$ have order $\ell^{2n}$, and let $q$ be the closed point of $E \setminus E[\ell^n]$ under $Q$. Its image is the closed point under $\ell^n Q$, a nontrivial point of $\ell$-power order, which is $\ell$-moderate on $E \setminus \{O\}$ [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.7, p. 662]. So $q$ is $\ell$-moderate, and $\Omega_{E \setminus E[\ell^n]} = \Omega_{E \setminus \{O\}} \cdot \kappa(q)$ [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.2 (i), p. 665]. But $Q$ itself is a nontrivial point of $\ell$-power order, so the closed point of $E \setminus \{O\}$ under $Q$ is $\ell$-moderate, and $\kappa(q) \subseteq \Omega_{E \setminus \{O\}}$ [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.7, p. 662, and Theorem 3.1, p. 664].

**Two duplication formulas.** These follow from the tangent construction, as checked here. On $y^2 = x^3 + ax$, the tangent slope $(3x^2 + a)/2y$ gives
$$x(2P) = \frac{(x^2 - a)^2}{4y^2}.$$
On $y^2 = x^3 + D$, the slope $3x^2/2y$ gives $x(2P) = (x^4 - 8Dx)/(4(x^3 + D))$. A point $P \ne O$ with $y(P) \ne 0$ has order $3$ exactly when $x(2P) = x(P)$, that is, when $3x^4 + 12Dx = 0$. So the points of order $3$ are $(0, \pm\sqrt{D})$ and the points with $x^3 = -4D$ and $y^2 = -3D$.

**Proof of 1.** Here $E[2] = \{O, (0,0), (\pm\sqrt{D}, 0)\}$, and $U = E \setminus E[2]$ is defined over $k$.

*The cover.* Put $u = x^2/D$. Then $u^{-1}(0) = \{(0,0)\}$, $u^{-1}(1) = \{(\pm\sqrt{D}, 0)\}$ and $u^{-1}(\infty) = \{O\}$, so $u$ restricts to a finite morphism of degree $4$ from $U$ onto $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$. Over $\bar k$, the automorphism $[i] : (x, y) \mapsto (-x, iy)$ of $E$ has order $4$ and preserves $u$. Its powers other than the identity have their fixed points in $E[2]$: those of $[i]^2 = [-1]$ are $E[2]$, and those of $[\pm i]$ are among them. So $\langle [i] \rangle$ acts freely on $U$, each fibre of $u$ over the tripod contains a free orbit of size $4$, and $u$ is étale and Galois over $\bar k$ with group $\mathbb{Z}/4$. As $U$ is geometrically connected, part 1 applies with $\ell = 2$. The tripod satisfies equality [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.3, p. 666], so $U$ satisfies equality, and $\Omega_U = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot \kappa(z)$ for every $2$-moderate closed point $z$ of $U$.

*Back to $C$.* By Step 0 with $n = 1$, $\Omega_U = \Omega_C$. By part 2, $C$ satisfies equality.

*The field.* Tripod $2$-units lie in the sets reached from $\{0, 1, \infty\}$ by two steps: replacing a set by the square roots of its elements, and moving three of its elements to $0, 1, \infty$ by a Möbius transformation [@hoshi2015, Definitions 1.4 and 1.6, pp. 655–656]. The chain $\{0,1,\infty\} \to \{0, \pm 1, \infty\} \to \{0, \pm 1, \pm i, \infty\} \to \cdots$ of root steps shows that $-1$, $i$ and all $2$-power roots of unity are tripod $2$-units. The Möbius step $w \mapsto w + 1$, which sends $-1, 0, \infty$ to $0, 1, \infty$, takes $\{0, \pm 1, \infty\}$ to $\{0, 1, 2, \infty\}$, and a root step then gives $\sqrt{2}$. So $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$, which the tripod $2$-units generate [@hoshi2015, Definition 1.6, p. 656], contains $\mu_{2^\infty}$ and $\sqrt{2}$.

The $k$-rational point $-1$ of the tripod is $2$-moderate [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.8, p. 662], so a closed point $z$ of $U$ over it is $2$-moderate [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.2 (i), p. 665]. Its geometric points $P$ have $x^2 = -D$, so $x(2P) = 0$ by the first duplication formula with $a = -D$; thus $2P = (0,0)$ and $P \in E[4]$. All points of $E[4] \setminus \{O\}$ are $2$-moderate on $C$, so $k(E[4]) \subseteq \Omega_C$ [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.7, p. 662, and Theorem 3.1, p. 664], and $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \subseteq \Omega_C$ [@hoshi2015, Proposition 1.2 (vi), p. 654]. Hence
$$\Omega_C = \Omega_U = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot \kappa(z) \subseteq \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot k(E[4]) \subseteq \Omega_C.$$
For the last form, fix $\delta$ with $\delta^2 = -D$, and a square root $\sqrt{\delta}$, so that $(\sqrt\delta)^4 = -D$. The field $\kappa(z)$ is generated by the coordinates of the geometric points over $z$. Each of them is $(\delta, y)$ with $y^2 = -2D\delta = 2\delta^3$, or $(-\delta, y)$ with $y^2 = -2\delta^3$. So $y = \pm\delta\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\delta}$ or $y = \pm i\delta\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\delta}$, and since $i, \sqrt 2 \in \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$, each of these points generates $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(\sqrt{\delta})$ over $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$. Hence $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot \kappa(z) = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(\sqrt{\delta})$. As $\zeta_8 \in \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ and $\zeta_8^4 = -1$, this is $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(D^{1/4})$, for any fourth root.

**Proof of 2.** Fix $d$ with $d^2 = D$ and put $k' = k(d)$. The points $(0, \pm d)$ have order $3$.

*The cover over $k'$.* Let $U' = E_{k'} \setminus \{O, (0, d), (0, -d)\}$, and $\varphi = (y + d)/2d$. The function $y$ has degree $3$, with a triple pole at $O$, and $y = \pm d$ forces $x = 0$. So $\varphi$ restricts to a finite morphism of degree $3$ from $U'$ onto $\mathbb{P}^1_{k'} \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$. Over $\bar k$, the automorphism $(x, y) \mapsto (\zeta_3 x, y)$ has order $3$, preserves $\varphi$, and has fixed points only at $O$ and $(0, \pm d)$. As in the proof of 1, $\varphi$ is étale and Galois over $\bar k$ with group $\mathbb{Z}/3$. Part 1 with $\ell = 3$ and Hoshi's Corollary 3.3 over $k'$ [@hoshi2015, p. 666] show that $U'$ satisfies equality over $k'$.

*Back to $C_{k'}$.* We have $E_{k'} \setminus E[3] \subseteq U' \subseteq C_{k'}$, so Hoshi's Remark 1.1.2 [@hoshi2015, p. 653], applied to both inclusions, and Step 0 over $k'$ give $\Omega_{C_{k'}} \subseteq \Omega_{U'} \subseteq \Omega_{E_{k'} \setminus E[3]} = \Omega_{C_{k'}}$. By part 2, $C_{k'}$ satisfies equality over $k'$.

*Descent.* The closed point of $C$ under $(0, d)$ is a nontrivial point of order $3$, so it is $3$-moderate [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.7, p. 662], and its residue field contains $d$. So $k' \subseteq k_C^{\mathrm{mdr}\text{-}3}$, and part 3 shows that $C$ satisfies equality, with $\Omega_C = \Omega_{C_{k'}} = \Omega_{U'}$.

*The field.* Let $\zeta = \zeta_3$. The root step takes $\{0, 1, \infty\}$ to $\{0, 1, \zeta, \zeta^2, \infty\}$. The Möbius map $w \mapsto (w - 1)/(\zeta - 1)$ sends $1, \zeta, \infty$ to $0, 1, \infty$ and $\zeta^2$ to $\zeta + 1 = -\zeta^2$. So $\zeta$ and $a = -\zeta^2$ are tripod $3$-units, and the closed point of $\mathbb{P}^1_{k'} \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ under $a$ is $3$-moderate [@hoshi2015, Proposition 2.8, p. 662]. Let $z$ be a closed point of $U'$ over it, so $z$ is $3$-moderate [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.2 (i), p. 665] and $\Omega_{U'} = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k'} \cdot \kappa_{k'}(z)$, with $\kappa_{k'}$ the Galois closure over $k'$. A geometric point $P$ over $z$ has $\varphi(P) \in \{-\zeta^2, -\zeta\}$, the conjugates of $a$. Then $y(P) = d(2\varphi(P) - 1) = \pm d\sqrt{-3}$, so $y^2 = -3D$ and $x^3 = -4D$, and $P$ has order $3$. Using $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k'} = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot k'$ and $k' \subseteq k(E[3])$,
$$\Omega_C = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot k' \cdot \kappa_{k'}(z) \subseteq \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k} \cdot k(E[3]) \subseteq \Omega_C,$$
the last inclusion as in the proof of 1. By the second duplication formula, $k(E[3])$ is generated by $\sqrt{D}$, $\sqrt{-3D}$ and the three cube roots of $-4D$, so it contains $\zeta_3$. As $\zeta_3$ and $\sqrt{-3} = 2\zeta_3 + 1$ lie in $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$, this gives $\Omega_C = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}(\sqrt{D}, \sqrt[3]{4D})$, for any cube root.

**Proof of 3.** In both cases $\sqrt{D} \in \Omega_C$, as a coordinate of the point $(\sqrt{D}, 0)$ of $E[4]$, respectively $(0, \sqrt{D})$ of $E[3]$. If $\mathfrak{P}$ lies over $\mathfrak{p}$ in $k(\sqrt{D})$ with ramification index $e$, then $e \, v_{\mathfrak{p}}(D) = 2 \, v_{\mathfrak{P}}(\sqrt{D})$, so $e = 2$ when $v_{\mathfrak{p}}(D)$ is odd, and $\Omega_C$ is ramified at $\mathfrak{p}$. The tripod has good reduction at every prime, because $0$, $1$ and $\infty$ stay distinct modulo every prime, so $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$ is unramified at $\mathfrak{p}$ when $\mathfrak{p}$ is not above $\ell$ [@hoshi2015, Proposition 1.2 (ii), pp. 653–654].

**Remarks.**

- *Same kernel as the tripod.* Over $\mathbb{Q}$, part 1 gives $\Omega_C = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/\mathbb{Q}}$ for both $y^2 = x^3 - x$ and $y^2 = x^3 + x$, since $(-1)^{1/4} = \zeta_8$; part 2 gives $\Omega_C = \Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/\mathbb{Q}}$ for $y^2 = x^3 + 16$, since $\sqrt[3]{64} = 4$. So these once-punctured curves have the same pro-$\ell$ kernel as the tripod, at $\ell = 2$ and $\ell = 3$ respectively. Ishii notes that higher-genus curves with the tripod's kernel can be constructed, the Fermat curve of degree $p$ being one [@ishii2026, §1, pp. 2–3]; these are examples of genus one.
- *The kernel does not determine the curve.* The curves $y^2 = x^3 - x$ and $y^2 = x^3 + x$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ have $8$ and $4$ points over $\mathbb{F}_5$, as a direct count shows. Since curves isogenous over $\mathbb{Q}$ have the same number of points over $\mathbb{F}_p$ at common primes of good reduction, a standard fact not checked against a source here, they are not isogenous, yet their once-punctured curves have the same pro-$2$ kernel. Both have complex multiplication, so this is consistent with [Mochizuki's theorem on non-CM curves](torsion-fields-determine-non-cm-elliptic-curves.md), and it parallels the failure there of $K(E[\ell^\infty])$ alone to determine $E$.
- *Relation to Ishii's theorem.* Ishii describes the kernel field of a once-punctured elliptic curve with complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic field $K$ of class number one, for primes $p \ge 5$ that split in $K$, under further assumptions including a working conjecture; see the [Anderson–Ihara question](anderson-ihara-question.md). Here $\ell$ ramifies in the CM field, $2$ in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ and $3$ in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$, and the description is unconditional but relative to $\Omega_{\mathrm{tpd}/k}$. Ishii presents his result as the first in which the kernel field is determined independently of the tripod [@ishii2026, §1, p. 2]; the description here depends on it.
- *Why these curves.* The method needs an open subcurve of $E \setminus \{O\}$ with the same kernel field that maps finite étale, with $\ell$-power geometric degree, onto a curve already known to satisfy equality. Here the quotient by the automorphisms of order $4$, respectively $3$, provides one. For other curves of genus one, part 1 still gives equality after removing more points. For example, let $i \in k$ and $E : y^2 = x(x-1)(x-i)$, with Legendre parameter $\lambda = i$ and so, by the standard formula $j = 2^8(\lambda^2 - \lambda + 1)^3/(\lambda^2(\lambda - 1)^2)$, of $j$-invariant $128$. Let $S = \{0, \pm 1, \pm i, \infty\}$, which two root steps reach from $\{0, 1, \infty\}$. The map $x$ is branched only over $0, 1, i, \infty \in S$, so it maps $E \setminus x^{-1}(S)$ finite étale of degree $2$ onto $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus S$. That curve satisfies equality [@hoshi2015, Corollary 3.3, p. 666], so $E \setminus x^{-1}(S)$ does too. This entry does not compare its kernel field with that of $E \setminus \{O\}$.
