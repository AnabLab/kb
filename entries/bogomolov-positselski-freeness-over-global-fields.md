**Setting.** Let $p$ be a prime and $K$ a global field, that is, a number field or a function field of one variable over a finite field, with $\operatorname{char}K\ne p$. Fix a separable closure $K_s$, and let
$$
L_K=K\bigl(\beta\in K_s:\ \beta^{p^m}\in K^\times\text{ for some }m\ge1\bigr)
$$
be the field generated by all $p$-power roots of all elements of $K^\times$. It contains every $p$-power root of unity. For a field $E\subseteq K_s$ write $G_E=\mathrm{Gal}(K_s/E)$ and $G_E(p)$ for its maximal pro-$p$ quotient. For a field $F$, $\mathrm{Br}(F)$ is the group of classes of central simple $F$-algebras and $\mathrm{Br}(F)[p]$ its $p$-torsion. For $F\subseteq F'$, restriction $\mathrm{res}_{F'/F}$ sends $[A]$ to $[A\otimes_FF']$.

For a field $F$ of characteristic different from $p$, let $L$ be obtained by adjoining to $F$ all roots of degree $p^N$, $N>0$, of all elements of $F$. Positselski conjectures that a Sylow pro-$p$ subgroup of $G_L$ is free pro-$p$ (his Conjecture 1), and, when $F$ contains a primitive $p$th root of unity and also $\sqrt{-1}$ if $p=2$, that $G_L(p)$ is free pro-$p$ (his Conjecture 2) [@positselski2005, §1.2–1.3, Conjectures 1 and 2, p. 2]. The second is [the Bogomolov–Positselski conjecture](bogomolov-positselski-freeness-conjecture.md).

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $E\subseteq K_s$ be a field containing $L_K$.

1. $\mathrm{Br}(E)[p]=0$ and $H^2(G_E,\mathbb F_p)=0$.
2. Every closed pro-$p$ subgroup of $G_E$ is a free pro-$p$ group. In particular, the Sylow pro-$p$ subgroups of $G_E$ are free.
3. $G_E(p)$ is a free pro-$p$ group.

**Corollary.** Let $F$ be a field with $K\subseteq F\subseteq K_s$. Then both of Positselski's conjectures hold for $F$, and the conclusion of the second holds even without its hypothesis on roots of unity. Indeed, the field $L$ attached to $F$ contains $L_K$, so parts 2 and 3 apply to $E=L$. In characteristic $0$ this covers every algebraic extension of $\mathbb Q$.

In the group formulation of [the problem](bogomolov-positselski-freeness-conjecture.md), when $\zeta_p\in K$, and $\sqrt{-1}\in K$ if $p=2$, the corollary says that the subgroup $K_\theta(G)$ of $G=G_K(p)$ is free. This lies outside the elementary-type case recorded there: $K^\times/K^{\times p}$ is infinite, since for distinct finite places $v_1,\dots,v_r$ weak approximation gives $a_i\in K^\times$ with $\mathrm{ord}_{v_j}(a_i)=\delta_{ij}$, and their classes are independent. Quadrelli and Weigel's unconditional cases all assume $F^\times/F^{\times p}$ finite [@quadrelliweigel2022bp, Corollary 1.3, p. 3].

**Attribution.** Positselski states the corollary for algebraic extensions of $\mathbb Q$ and of $\mathbb F_q(x)$, in the stronger form in which only the $p$-power roots of unity are adjoined. He sketches a proof through Brauer groups and class field theory, and calls the assertion essentially a particular case of a result in Serre's *Cohomologie galoisienne* [@positselski2005, Appendix, Proposition A.1 and its proof, p. 26]. His introduction says the same for number fields and their function-field analogues [@positselski2005, §1.2, p. 2]. The proof below follows his outline, but adjoins roots of elements of $K$ of valuation $1$ instead of roots of unity, which reduces the local degree computation to a norm identity. The stronger form with roots of unity only is not proved here. No novelty is claimed.

**Inputs.**

- (a) *Kummer theory.* For every field $E\subseteq K_s$, $H^2(G_E,\mu_p)\cong\mathrm{Br}(E)[p]$. Indeed, $1\to\mu_p\to K_s^\times\xrightarrow{p}K_s^\times\to1$ is an exact sequence of discrete $G_E$-modules, as $x^p-a$ is separable for $a\ne0$. Its cohomology sequence gives $H^1(G_E,K_s^\times)\to H^2(G_E,\mu_p)\to H^2(G_E,K_s^\times)\xrightarrow{p}H^2(G_E,K_s^\times)$. The first group is the direct limit of the groups $H^1(\mathrm{Gal}(E'/E),E'^\times)$ over finite Galois $E'/E$ [@milnecft, Chapter II, Proposition 4.2, p. 87], which vanish by Hilbert's Theorem 90 [@milnecft, Chapter II, Proposition 1.22, p. 67]. And $\mathrm{Br}(E)\cong H^2(G_E,K_s^\times)$ [@milnecft, Chapter IV, Corollary 3.16, p. 138]. Also $\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb C)=0$, as $\mathbb C$ is algebraically closed [@milnecft, Chapter IV, Example 2.14(a), p. 130].
- (b) *Local invariants.* For a nonarchimedean completion $F_v$ of a global field there is an isomorphism $\mathrm{inv}_v:\mathrm{Br}(F_v)\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$, and for a finite separable extension $M/F_v$, $\mathrm{inv}_M(\mathrm{res}_{M/F_v}\beta)=[M:F_v]\,\mathrm{inv}_v(\beta)$ [@milnecft, Chapter IV, Proposition 4.3 and Remark 4.4(c), p. 141] [@milne2006adt, Chapter I, Appendix A, Proposition A.1 and the paragraph after Lemma A.2, p. 128]. For a real place, $\mathrm{inv}_v$ is an isomorphism of $\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb R)$ onto $\tfrac12\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z$ [@milnecft, Chapter VIII, §4, p. 240].
- (c) *Local-global principle.* For a global field $F$, the map $\mathrm{Br}(F)\to\bigoplus_v\mathrm{Br}(F_v)$, $[A]\mapsto([A\otimes_FF_v])_v$, is injective, and each class has only finitely many nonzero components. For number fields this is [the Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem](brauer-hasse-noether-theorem.md) [@milnecft, Chapter VIII, Theorem 4.2, p. 241]. For a function field with finite constant field $k$ and smooth projective model $X$, the kernel is $H^1(G_k,\mathrm{Jac}_X(k_s))$ [@milne2006adt, Chapter I, Appendix A, Theorem A.7, p. 131], which vanishes by Lang's lemma [@milne2006adt, Chapter I, Appendix A, proof of Corollary A.9, p. 133].
- (d) *Freeness.* A pro-$p$ group $P$ with $H^2(P,\mathbb F_p)=0$ is free pro-$p$. Quadrelli and Weigel use this, citing Neukirch–Schmidt–Wingberg, Proposition 3.5.17, for a closed normal subgroup of a pro-$\ell$ group, with no finite generation assumption [@quadrelliweigel2022bp, proof of Theorem 4.5, p. 13].
- (e) *Maximal pro-$p$ quotients.* For a profinite group $G$, inflation $H^2(G(p),\mathbb F_p)\to H^2(G,\mathbb F_p)$ is injective [@positselski2005, §1.3, p. 2]. Positselski calls this easily seen. By the five-term sequence of $1\to N\to G\to G(p)\to1$, it reduces to $H^1(N,\mathbb F_p)^G=0$, which holds because a $G$-invariant homomorphism $\varphi:N\to\mathbb F_p$ has kernel normal in $G$ with $G/\ker\varphi$ pro-$p$, so $\ker\varphi\supseteq N$.

**Lemma (descent).** Let $E$ be the union of a directed family of subfields $F$.

1. Every class in $\mathrm{Br}(E)$ is $\mathrm{res}_{E/F}\beta$ for some $F$ in the family and some $\beta\in\mathrm{Br}(F)$.
2. If $\beta\in\mathrm{Br}(F)$ and $\mathrm{res}_{E/F}\beta=0$, then $\mathrm{res}_{F'/F}\beta=0$ for some $F'\supseteq F$ in the family.

*Proof.* (1) Let $A$ be a central simple $E$-algebra with basis $e_1,\dots,e_r$, structure constants $e_ie_j=\sum_kc_{ijk}e_k$ and unit $\sum_iu_ie_i$. As the family is directed, some $F$ in it contains all $c_{ijk}$ and $u_i$. Let $A_0=\bigoplus_iFe_i$ with the same multiplication. Associativity and the unit laws are identities among the $c_{ijk}$ and $u_i$, so $A_0$ is an $F$-algebra with $A_0\otimes_FE\cong A$. A two-sided ideal $I$ of $A_0$ gives the two-sided ideal $I\otimes_FE$ of the simple algebra $A$, so $I=0$ or $I=A_0$. The centre of $A_0$ is the solution space of the $F$-linear system $xe_j=e_jx$, $1\le j\le r$. Over $E$ the same system has solution space the centre $E$ of $A$, of dimension $1$, and solution spaces of linear systems keep their dimension under field extension. So $A_0$ is central simple over $F$, and $[A]=\mathrm{res}_{E/F}[A_0]$.

(2) Let $\beta=[A_0]$. As $\mathrm{res}_{E/F}\beta=0$, the $E$-algebra $A_0\otimes_FE$ is a matrix algebra $M_n(E)$ [@milnecft, Chapter IV, §2, p. 131]. The matrices of such an isomorphism and of its inverse, with respect to the bases $(e_i)$ and $(E_{jk})$, have finitely many entries, all in some $F'\supseteq F$ in the family. Over $F'$ they define mutually inverse linear maps between $A_0\otimes_FF'$ and $M_n(F')$, which respect products and units because these are polynomial identities in the entries that hold in $E$. So $A_0\otimes_FF'\cong M_n(F')$. Milne uses the same argument to show that every central simple algebra is split by a finite extension [@milnecft, Chapter IV, proof of Proposition 2.17, p. 131]. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** Let $\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(E)[p]$. The field $E$ is the directed union of its subfields finite over $K$. By the lemma, $\alpha=\mathrm{res}_{E/F_0}\beta_0$ for some finite $F_0/K$ inside $E$, and since $\mathrm{res}_{E/F_0}(p\beta_0)=p\alpha=0$, some finite $F\supseteq F_0$ inside $E$ has $p\,\mathrm{res}_{F/F_0}\beta_0=0$. Put $\beta=\mathrm{res}_{F/F_0}\beta_0$. Then $\beta\in\mathrm{Br}(F)[p]$ and $\alpha=\mathrm{res}_{E/F}\beta$.

Write $\beta_v=[A\otimes_FF_v]$ for the components of $\beta=[A]$, and let $T$ be the finite set of places $v$ of $F$ with $\beta_v\ne0$, by (c). As $p\beta_v=0$, the invariant of $\beta_v$ is killed by $p$. So $T$ contains no complex place, by (a). It contains a real place only if $p=2$, since $\tfrac12\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z$ has no nonzero element killed by an odd prime, by (b). And $\mathrm{inv}_v(\beta_v)\in\tfrac1p\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z$ for nonarchimedean $v\in T$.

For each nonarchimedean $v\in T$, let $u$ be the place of $K$ below $v$, with normalized valuation $\mathrm{ord}_u:K^\times\to\mathbb Z$. It is surjective, so choose $a_v\in K^\times$ with $\mathrm{ord}_u(a_v)=1$. Let $e_v$ be the ramification index of $F_v$ over $K_u$, so that the normalized valuation of $F_v$ satisfies $\mathrm{ord}_v(a_v)=e_v$. Choose $m\ge1$ with $p^m>e_v$ for every such $v$, and choose $b_v\in L_K$ with $b_v^{p^m}=a_v$. Let $F'\subseteq E$ be the finite extension of $F$ generated by the $b_v$, and also by a root $i\in L_K$ of $x^2+1$ if $p=2$.

We show that $\mathrm{res}_{F'/F}\beta=0$. Let $w$ be a place of $F'$ over a place $v$ of $F$. Since the embedding $F\to F'_w$ factors through both $F'$ and $F_v$, the $w$-component of $\mathrm{res}_{F'/F}\beta$ is $\mathrm{res}_{F'_w/F_v}\beta_v$.

- If $v\notin T$, then $\beta_v=0$, so this component is $0$.
- If $v\in T$ is real, then $p=2$ and $i\in F'$, so $F'$ has no real embedding, $w$ is complex, and $\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb C)=0$.
- If $v\in T$ is nonarchimedean, put $n=[F'_w:F_v]$ and let $N:F_w'^\times\to F_v^\times$ be the norm, which is multiplicative with $N(x)=x^n$ for $x\in F_v^\times$. From $b_v^{p^m}=a_v$ we get $N(b_v)^{p^m}=a_v^n$, so $p^m\,\mathrm{ord}_v(N(b_v))=n\,e_v$. As $\mathrm{ord}_v(N(b_v))$ is an integer and $p^m>e_v$, the $p$-adic valuation of $e_v$ is less than $m$, so $p\mid n$. By (b), $\mathrm{inv}_w(\mathrm{res}_{F'_w/F_v}\beta_v)=n\,\mathrm{inv}_v(\beta_v)=0$, and $\mathrm{inv}_w$ is injective. Here $F'_w/F_v$ is separable, as it is generated over $F_v$ by a primitive element of the separable extension $F'/F$.

So every component of $\mathrm{res}_{F'/F}\beta$ vanishes, and (c) for the global field $F'$ gives $\mathrm{res}_{F'/F}\beta=0$. Hence $\alpha=\mathrm{res}_{E/F'}(\mathrm{res}_{F'/F}\beta)=0$. Finally $\zeta_p\in L_K\subseteq E$, so $\mu_p\cong\mathbb F_p$ as $G_E$-modules, and (a) gives $H^2(G_E,\mathbb F_p)\cong\mathrm{Br}(E)[p]=0$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $P\subseteq G_E$ be a closed pro-$p$ subgroup, with fixed field $E_P$. By Galois theory $P=G_{E_P}$, and $E_P\supseteq E\supseteq L_K$, so $H^2(P,\mathbb F_p)=0$ by part 1 applied to $E_P$. By (d), $P$ is free pro-$p$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** By (e) and part 1, $H^2(G_E(p),\mathbb F_p)$ injects into $H^2(G_E,\mathbb F_p)=0$, so $G_E(p)$ is free pro-$p$ by (d). $\square$

**Scope.**

- *What is used.* The proof uses only the $p$-power roots of elements of $K$ with valuation $1$ at the finitely many places in $T$, together with $\zeta_p$, and $\sqrt{-1}$ when $p=2$. Positselski's stronger form needs instead that adjoining $p$-power roots of unity to a completion $F_v$ gives local degrees divisible by arbitrarily high powers of $p$ [@positselski2005, proof of Proposition A.1, p. 26]. That computation is not done here.
- *Other fields.* Nothing is proved here for fields that are not algebraic over a global field, such as function fields of curves over number fields. Positselski also shows that a Henselian discretely valued field satisfies his Conjecture 1 whenever its residue field does, and in particular when the residue field has characteristic $p$ [@positselski2005, Appendix, Proposition A.2, p. 26].
