**Setting.** Fix a prime $p$ and a type $(g,n)$ with $2g - 2 + n > 0$, so that the curves below are [hyperbolic](hyperbolic-curve.md). Let $M_{g,n,\mathbb{F}_p}$ be the coarse moduli space over $\mathbb{F}_p$ of smooth curves of genus $g$ with $n$ unordered marked points. For a point $q$, let $k(q)$ be its residue field and $V_q$ its closure. An algebraically closed field $k_q$ containing $k(q)$ gives a morphism $\operatorname{Spec} k_q \to M_{g,n,\mathbb{F}_p}$, which determines a curve over $k_q$; let $U_{X_{k_q}}$ be this curve minus its marked points. When $k_q$ is an algebraic closure of $k(q)$, write $U_{X_q}$ for $U_{X_{k_q}}$. The isomorphism class of the tame fundamental group of $U_{X_{k_q}}$ depends only on $q$, not on $k_q$, and is written $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(q)$ [@huyangzong2024, §1.2.1, p. 2].

The scheme $U_{X_{k_q}}$ does depend on $k_q$, which is why the conjecture compares the models $U_{X_q}$. For example, take the unique point of $M_{0,3,\mathbb{F}_p}$ with $k_q = \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ and with $k_q$ an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$. The two thrice-punctured lines have isomorphic tame fundamental groups, but their function fields have transcendence degrees $1$ and $2$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, so they are not isomorphic as schemes.

**Conjecture (Tamagawa).** For points $q_1, q_2$ of $M_{g,n,\mathbb{F}_p}$,
$$\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(q_1) \cong \pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(q_2) \iff V_{q_1} = V_{q_2},$$
that is, if and only if $U_{X_{q_1}} \cong U_{X_{q_2}}$ as schemes [@huyangzong2024, §1.2.2, p. 2].

This is Hu, Yang and Zong's reformulation. They attribute the conjecture to Tamagawa [T3, Conjecture 2.2], in the form that curves are isomorphic if and only if their tame, or étale, fundamental groups are isomorphic [@huyangzong2024, §1.1.2, p. 2]. By the example above, "curves" must there be read as the models $U_{X_q}$. Their [T3] is Tamagawa's paper in Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 70 (2002), pp. 297–333 [@huyangzong2024, References, p. 36], which was not opened for this entry. The conjecture asks only that isomorphic groups come from isomorphic curves, not that every isomorphism of groups come from one of curves.

**Remarks.**

- *Scheme isomorphism.* Two operations can change a point of the moduli space of curves over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ with ordered marked points without changing the scheme $U$. One is relabelling the marked points. The other is Frobenius twisting: the projection $X \times_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p, \mathrm{Frob}} \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p \to X$ is the base change of the isomorphism of $\operatorname{Spec} \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ given by Frobenius, so it is an isomorphism of schemes, though in general not of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$-schemes. Twisting alone does not account for all scheme isomorphisms. In characteristic $11$, the ordered cross-ratios $3$ and $9 = 1 - 3$ are distinct and both fixed by Frobenius, but $x \mapsto 1 - x$ maps $\mathbb{P}^1 - \{0, 1, \infty, 3\}$ onto $\mathbb{P}^1 - \{1, 0, \infty, 9\}$. Let $M^{\mathrm{ord}}_{g,n}$ be the coarse moduli space over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ of curves with ordered marked points. Hu, Yang and Zong define a Frobenius equivalence on its closed points, comparing Frobenius twists after forgetting the order of the marked points, and extend it to arbitrary points through the closed points of their closures. They show that two points of $M^{\mathrm{ord}}_{g,n}$ are Frobenius equivalent exactly when their images under the natural morphism $M^{\mathrm{ord}}_{g,n} \to M_{g,n,\mathbb{F}_p}$, which forgets the order of the marked points, coincide, that is, exactly when their curves $U_{X_q}$ are isomorphic as schemes [@huyangzong2024, Definition 2.1 and Proposition 2.2, p. 7]. So the conjecture says that the moduli space can be recovered as a set from the isomorphism classes of tame fundamental groups [@huyangzong2024, §1.2.2, p. 3].
- *Finite quotients.* The tame fundamental group is topologically finitely generated, so $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(q_1) \cong \pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(q_2)$ exactly when the two groups have the same finite quotients [@huyangzong2024, §2.1.2, p. 6]. So the conjecture says that the model $U_{X_q}$ is determined, up to scheme isomorphism, by the finite groups that occur as Galois groups of its connected tame coverings.
- *Why the tame group.* For an affine curve, the set of finite quotients of the étale fundamental group depends only on $(g,n)$, by Abhyankar's conjecture as proved by Raynaud for the affine line and by Harbater in general. The étale group itself is not topologically finitely generated, so these quotients do not determine it [@huyangzong2024, §1.4.2, p. 5].
- *Characteristic $0$.* There the tame fundamental group depends only on $(g,n)$ ([Tamagawa's theorems](tame-fundamental-groups-in-positive-characteristic.md)). So the analogue fails for every type except $(0,3)$, whose moduli space is a point. This is checked here: $\dim M_{g,n} = 3g - 3 + n$ vanishes for a hyperbolic type only when $(g,n) = (0,3)$.

**Known cases.**

- *Genus $0$.* The conjecture holds when $g = 0$ and one of $q_1, q_2$ is a closed point, that is, one of the curves $U_{X_{q_i}}$ is defined over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ [@huyangzong2024, §1.2.2, p. 3], and for all points of $M_{0,4,\mathbb{F}_p}$, by [Tamagawa's theorems](tame-fundamental-groups-in-positive-characteristic.md). In moduli terms, Hu, Yang and Zong note that Tamagawa's theorem makes the natural map from $M_{0,4,\mathbb{F}_p}$ to the set of isomorphism classes of fundamental groups a bijection [@huyangzong2024, §1.4.1, p. 5]. They say that the conjecture had been confirmed only in genus $0$ [@huyangzong2024, §1.2.2, p. 3], and that no published results were known for nonclosed points. The difficulty there is that nobody knows how to recover the tame fundamental groups of the closed points of $V_q$ from $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(q)$ [@huyangzong2024, §2.2.3, p. 8]. On the same page they use a theorem of Tamagawa that separates a nonclosed point from the closed points of its closure, recorded in the next item. This does not conflict with the type $(0,4)$: the curve $M_{0,4,\mathbb{F}_p}$ has only one nonclosed point, its generic point, and there the conjecture follows from the closed case.
- *A closed point and the points specializing to it.* Work in the moduli space $M^{\mathrm{ord}}_{g,n}$ over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ of the remarks above. Let $q$ be a nonclosed point and $t$ a closed point of its closure. Hu, Yang and Zong note that a theorem of Tamagawa implies that every finite quotient of the tame fundamental group of the curve at $t$ is a finite quotient of that at $q$, but not conversely [@huyangzong2024, §2.2.2, p. 8]. So the two tame fundamental groups are not isomorphic, as the conjecture predicts. For proper curves of genus at least $2$ this is Tamagawa's specialization theorem, which answers the question of Pop and Saïdi in the next item. Saïdi and Tamagawa also show that in a nonisotrivial family of hyperbolic curves over any field of characteristic $p$, the tame fundamental groups of the geometric fibres are not all isomorphic. See [variation of fundamental groups in characteristic $p$](variation-of-fundamental-groups-of-curves-in-positive-characteristic.md) for both results. Over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$, only finitely many isomorphism classes of hyperbolic curves with their marked points have a given tame fundamental group [@yang2018admissible, §1, p. 651].
- *Proper curves.* Let $g \ge 2$, let $M_g$ be the coarse moduli space of proper smooth curves of genus $g$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$, $\eta$ its generic point, and $\pi_1(x)$ the étale fundamental group of the curve at a point $x$. If $x$ is a specialization of $y$, Grothendieck's specialization theorem gives a surjection $\pi_1(y) \to \pi_1(x)$. Let $S^{\mathrm{a.s.}}$ be the set of closed points whose curves have absolutely simple Jacobians. By a theorem of Chai and Oort it has positive Dirichlet density, so it is Zariski dense [@popsaidi2003, Introduction, pp. 108–109]. Pop and Saïdi prove the following [@popsaidi2003, Introduction, Theorems A and B and Corollary, p. 109].
  1. For every $s \in S^{\mathrm{a.s.}}$, the specialization map $\pi_1(\eta) \to \pi_1(s)$ is not an isomorphism. Every cyclic étale covering of the generic curve of degree prime to $p$ is ordinary, but the curve at $s$ has such coverings that are not.
  2. Let $s \in S^{\mathrm{a.s.}}$ have $p$-rank $g$ or $g-1$. Then no specialization map to $s$ from a point other than $s$ is an isomorphism, and only finitely many other such points $s'$ have $\pi_1(s') \cong \pi_1(s)$.
  3. No nonempty open subset of $M_g$ has constant fundamental group, which answers a question of Harbater.

  *Check, done here.* By part 1, $\pi_1(\eta) \not\cong \pi_1(s)$ even as abstract profinite groups, as the conjecture predicts. Both groups are topologically finitely generated, and a surjection between two isomorphic topologically finitely generated profinite groups is an isomorphism, a fact that Pop and Saïdi use in the proof of part 2 [@popsaidi2003, §4, pp. 116–117]. So in every genus $g \ge 2$ the generic curve and every curve over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ with absolutely simple Jacobian have nonisomorphic fundamental groups. Pop and Saïdi ask whether a specialization map $\pi_1(y) \to \pi_1(x)$ to a closed point $x \ne y$ is never an isomorphism. If so, then for every closed point $x$ of $M_g$ only finitely many closed points $x'$ would have $\pi_1(x') \cong \pi_1(x)$ [@popsaidi2003, Question, p. 110]. Tamagawa's specialization theorem answers this affirmatively, and Saïdi and Tamagawa extend part 3 to every subvariety of $M_g$ of positive dimension ([variation of fundamental groups in characteristic $p$](variation-of-fundamental-groups-of-curves-in-positive-characteristic.md)).
- *Type $(1,1)$, étale form.* Sarashina proves the following for the full étale fundamental group, in a preprint. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p \ne 2$, $F$ the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{F}_p$ in $k$, $U$ a curve over $k$, and $U_0$ a curve over $F$ whose smooth compactification has genus $1$ and exactly one point outside $U_0$. Then $\pi_1(U) \cong \pi_1(U_0)$ if and only if $U \cong U_0 \times_F k$ as schemes [@sarashina2017, Theorem 1.2, pp. 1–2]. The tame fundamental group of an affine curve can be recovered from its étale fundamental group, so a statement about tame groups is the stronger one [@tamagawa2003tame, Introduction, pp. 47–48]. So this does not give the conjecture for type $(1,1)$, and Hu, Yang and Zong, writing about tame groups in 2024, still list only genus $0$ as confirmed. Yang wrote in 2018 that tame fundamental groups distinguish curves of type $(1,1)$ over algebraic closures of finite fields of characteristic $\ne 2$ up to isomorphism of schemes, citing Tamagawa's 2015 lecture slides, but the slides list that theorem as in progress and state it only when the curve and its $2$-torsion points are defined over $\mathbb{F}_p$ ([admissible fundamental groups of stable curves](admissible-fundamental-groups-of-stable-curves-in-positive-characteristic.md)).

**Stronger conjectures.**

- *Weak Hom-version.* Hu, Yang and Zong conjecture that an open continuous homomorphism $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(q_1) \to \pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(q_2)$ exists if and only if $V_{q_1} \supseteq V_{q_2}$, that is, $q_2$ is a specialization of $q_1$. This implies the conjecture above, and would recover the moduli space as a topological space [@huyangzong2024, §1.2.2, p. 3, and §2.1.4, p. 7]. They prove it when $q_1$ is a closed point of $M_{0,n,\mathbb{F}_p}$ [@huyangzong2024, Theorem 1.1, p. 3]. In its version for ordered marked points over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$, they prove it more generally when the closure of $q_1$ is an intersection of closures of coordinated points [@huyangzong2024, Theorem 4.4 (iv), p. 34]. Normalize the first three marked points to $1, 0, \infty$. Then the coordinated points are the generic point and the points of codimension $1$ at which another marked point lies in $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ [@huyangzong2024, §4.2.2, p. 33].
- *Homeomorphism conjecture.* Yang defined a topological space of isomorphism classes of fundamental groups of curves of type $(g,n)$, and conjectured that $M_{g,n}$, after dividing by an equivalence relation induced by Frobenius, is homeomorphic to it [@huyangzong2024, §1.4.1, p. 4]. Hu, Yang and Zong write that a preprint of Yang and a paper of his then in preparation prove this for one-dimensional moduli spaces [@huyangzong2024, §1.4.1, p. 5]. Neither was checked here, so the type $(1,1)$ is not recorded here as settled.

**Status.** Hu, Yang and Zong, in a paper published in 2024, call the conjecture "still wide open" [@huyangzong2024, §1.1.2, p. 2]. A search in November 2026 found no proof, but it was not exhaustive.
