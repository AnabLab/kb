**Setting.** Fix a prime $\ell$ and put $\Lambda=\mathbb F_\ell$. For any field $F$ put $A_F=\mathrm{Hom}(F^\times,\Lambda)$, the dual of $V_F=F^\times/F^{\times\ell}$, with the topology of pointwise convergence. It is compact, and $V_F$ is its space of continuous linear forms. A pair $f,g\in A_F$ is *alternating* if
$$f(x)g(1-x)=f(1-x)g(x)\qquad(x\in F\setminus\{0,1\}).$$
For a valuation $v$ of $F$, with units $U_v$ and principal units $U_v^1$, put $I_v=U_v^\perp\subseteq D_v=(U_v^1)^\perp$. Restriction to units gives $\rho_v:D_v\to A_{Fv}$ with kernel $I_v$. A subset of $A_F$ is *valuative* if it lies in some $I_v$ [@openai2026modl, §2, p. 5].

Let $F/\kappa$ be a function field over an algebraically closed field of any characteristic, and $s=\mathrm{trdeg}(F/\kappa)$. Every character kills $\kappa^\times$, which is $\ell$-divisible, so every character kills $-1$. A *quasi-prime divisor* is a valuation minimal under coarsening among those with $vF/v\kappa\cong\mathbb Z$ and $\mathrm{trdeg}(Fv/\kappa v)=s-1$. A *quasi-prime $r$-divisor* is a composition of $r$ successive quasi-prime divisors of the residue fields [@openai2026modl, Definition 2.3, p. 7]. This agrees with [quasi prime divisors](quasi-prime-divisor.md).

**Milnor input.** Let $R_F\subseteq V_F\otimes V_F$ be spanned by the $[x]\otimes[1-x]$. The functional $[x]\otimes[y]\mapsto f(x)g(y)-f(y)g(x)$ kills $R_F$ exactly when $f,g$ alternate. So if $\Theta:V_K\to V_L$ is a linear isomorphism with $(\Theta\otimes\Theta)(R_K)=R_L$, its dual $\Phi=\Theta^*:A_L\to A_K$ is a continuous linear isomorphism that preserves alternating pairs in both directions [@openai2026modl, §2, p. 5]. Mod $\ell$ the alternating relation is read off the product directly, so the Merkurjev–Suslin input of the pro-$\ell$ case is not needed.

**Theorem.** Let $F/\kappa$ be as above.

1. $s$ is the largest dimension of an alternating subspace of $A_F$. If $S$ is alternating of dimension $s$ and $S_0$ is its subspace of valuative elements, with associated valuation $v$, then $S_0=I_v$ and $v$ has no transcendence defect [@openai2026modl, Proposition 2.6, p. 8].
2. Let $s\ge2$. A line $H\subseteq A_F$ is $I_v$ for a quasi-prime divisor $v$ if and only if $H=S_1\cap S_2$ for alternating subspaces $S_1,S_2$ of dimension $s$. Then $v$ is unique, and $D_v=\{g:(h,g)\text{ alternating}\}$ for any $0\ne h\in H$ [@openai2026modl, Proposition 2.7, p. 9].
3. If $v$ is a quasi-prime $r$-divisor with $r\ge1$, then $vF$ has no nonzero $\ell$-divisible convex subgroup, and $v$ is the coarsest valuation whose inertia contains $I_v$. In particular $I_v$ determines $v$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 2.8, pp. 9–10].
4. Let $K/k$ and $L/l$ be function fields over algebraically closed fields of any characteristics, of transcendence degree at least $2$, and let $\Phi:A_L\to A_K$ be a continuous linear isomorphism that preserves alternating pairs in both directions. Then both transcendence degrees equal some $d$. For each $1\le r\le d-1$ there is a bijection $w\mapsto v$ from the quasi-prime $r$-divisors of $L/l$ to those of $K/k$ with $\Phi(I_w)=I_v$ and $\Phi(D_w)=D_v$. The induced map $A_{Lw}=D_w/I_w\to D_v/I_v=A_{Kv}$ is a topological linear isomorphism that preserves alternating pairs in both directions [@openai2026modl, Corollary 2.9, p. 10]. The manuscript states this for characteristic different from $\ell$, as in its main theorem; the proof below does not use that restriction.

Residue fields can have characteristic $\ell$ even when $K$ does not. There $A_{Fv}$ is still the dual of $(Fv)^\times/\ell$, with no Galois interpretation, and the argument is unchanged.

**Local input.** Topaz's local theory is stated for arbitrary fields with coefficient ring $\mathbb Z_\ell$ or $\mathbb Z/\ell$; for $\mathbb Z/\ell$ his character space $\mathcal G(F,\Lambda)$ is $A_F$ [@topaz2019galois, §2.1, p. 4]. Its restriction to characteristic different from $\ell$ begins only in §3 [@topaz2019galois, §3, p. 7]. Since every character here kills $-1$, his sign condition is automatic. The prime $\ell=2$ is included; for $\mathbb Z/2$ his proof of the underlying Theorem 2.6 refers to the theory of rigid elements instead of the argument given for odd $\ell$ [@topaz2019galois, proof of Theorem 2.6, p. 6]. The proof cites:

- (T1) A valuative set $\Sigma$ has a unique coarsest valuation $v_\Sigma$ with $\Sigma\subseteq I_{v_\Sigma}$. If $\Sigma\subseteq I_w$, then $v_\Sigma$ is the coarsening of $w$ by the largest convex subgroup of $w(\{x:f(x)=0\ \forall f\in\Sigma\})$ [@topaz2019galois, Fact 2.1, p. 4].
- (T2) If $\Sigma\cup\{g\}$ is alternating and every element of $\Sigma$ is valuative, then $\Sigma$ is valuative and $g\in D_{v_\Sigma}$ [@topaz2019galois, Lemma 2.5, pp. 5–6].
- (T3) The valuative elements of an alternating subspace $S$ form a valuative subspace $S_0$ of codimension at most $1$, and $S\subseteq D_{v_{S_0}}$ [@topaz2019galois, Corollary 2.7, p. 7].
- (T4) For finitely many nontrivial pairwise independent valuations, that is valuations with no nontrivial common coarsening, $v_i(x-x_i)>v_i(z_i)$ can be solved simultaneously [@anscombedittmannfehm2020, Theorem 1.2 and footnote 4, p. 1]. Incomparable valuations induce independent valuations on the residue field of their finest common coarsening [@anscombedittmannfehm2020, Remark 2.7, p. 5].

**Proof.** This rebuilds the manuscript's argument, which adapts Topaz's Fact 3.2 and Theorem 3.3 [@topaz2019galois, pp. 8–9].

*Step 1: valuation facts.* For any valuation $v$ of $F$, $\kappa v$ is algebraically closed and $v\kappa$ is divisible. If $n\gamma\in v\kappa$, then $n\gamma=n\delta$ with $\delta\in v\kappa$, so $\gamma=\delta$; thus $vF/v\kappa$ is torsion free. Abhyankar's inequality reads
$$\mathrm{rrank}(vF/v\kappa)+\mathrm{trdeg}(Fv/\kappa v)\le s,$$
where $\mathrm{rrank}$ is $\dim_{\mathbb Q}(-\otimes\mathbb Q)$. To see it, take $x_i$ with values independent modulo $v\kappa$ and units $y_j$ with residues algebraically independent over $\kappa v$. In a polynomial relation over $\kappa$, terms with distinct monomials in the $x_i$ have distinct values modulo $v\kappa$. Within one such monomial the least-value terms in the $y_j$ cannot cancel, by independence of the residues, so no relation exists. At equality, $F$ is finite over $F'=\kappa(x,y)$, where $vF'/v\kappa\cong\mathbb Z^a$ and $F'v=\kappa v(\bar y)$. The fundamental inequality then makes $vF/v\kappa$ finitely generated and torsion free, hence free, and $Fv/\kappa v$ finitely generated. For $v=w\circ u$ the sequence $0\to w(Fu)/w(\kappa u)\to vF/v\kappa\to uF/u\kappa\to0$ is exact. Adding the inequalities for $u$ and for $w$ on $Fu/\kappa u$ gives the one for $v$. So equality for $v$ is equivalent to equality for both $u$ and $w$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 2.2, pp. 6–7].

*Step 2: residues and approximation.* Let $vF/v\kappa$ be free of finite rank. The sequence
$$1\to(Fv)^\times/(\kappa v)^\times\to F^\times/(\kappa^\times U_v^1)\to vF/v\kappa\to0$$
is exact: an element with value in $v\kappa$ becomes a unit after rescaling by a constant. The sequence splits, and characters kill both constant groups, so dualizing gives a surjection $\rho_v:D_v\to A_{Fv}$ with kernel $I_v$. The induced bijection $D_v/I_v\to A_{Fv}$ is a homeomorphism, being a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff one.

For $f,g\in D_v$, alternation is equivalent to alternation of $\rho_vf,\rho_vg$. If $v(x)>0$ then $1-x\in U_v^1$, and if $v(1-x)>0$ then $x\in U_v^1$; either way both sides vanish. If $v(x)<0$ then $(1-x)/(-x)\in U_v^1$, so $f(1-x)=f(x)$ and $g(1-x)=g(x)$, and both sides agree. The remaining case $v(x)=v(1-x)=0$ is the residual identity, and every residue other than $0,1$ lifts to it. If $w$ is a valuation of $Fv$, then $U_{w\circ v}$ and $U^1_{w\circ v}$ contain $U_v^1$ and reduce onto $U_w$ and $U^1_w$. Hence
$$I_{w\circ v}=\rho_v^{-1}(I_w),\qquad D_{w\circ v}=\rho_v^{-1}(D_w)\qquad\text{inside }D_v$$
[@openai2026modl, Lemma 2.4, p. 7].

Let $v_1,v_2$ be incomparable, with finest common coarsening $u$. By (T4) their induced valuations $w_1,w_2$ on $Fu$ are nontrivial and independent. For $a\in(Fu)^\times$ choose $b$ with $w_1(b-1)>0$ and $w_2(b-a)>w_2(a)$. Then $b\in U^1_{w_1}$ and $a/b\in U^1_{w_2}$. Now let $a\in U_u$, apply this to its residue, and lift $b$ to $\beta\in U^1_{v_1}$. Then $a/\beta\in U_u$ has residue in $U^1_{w_2}$, so $a/\beta\in U^1_{v_2}$. This gives $U_u=U^1_{v_1}U^1_{v_2}$; the reverse inclusion holds because $u$ coarsens both. Taking annihilators,
$$D_{v_1}\cap D_{v_2}=I_u$$
[@openai2026modl, Lemma 2.5, pp. 7–8].

*Step 3: dimension.* For a torsion-free group $G$ of finite rational rank, $\dim_\Lambda G/\ell G\le\mathrm{rrank}\,G$. Indeed, if lifts of independent classes satisfied a nontrivial integral relation, dividing it by the gcd of its coefficients, which is allowed by torsion-freeness, would leave a coefficient nonzero modulo $\ell$. Characters in $I_v$ factor through $vF$ and kill the divisible group $v\kappa$, so $\dim I_v\le\mathrm{rrank}(vF/v\kappa)$.

Let $S$ be alternating, with valuative part $S_0$ and $v=v_{S_0}$, so that $S_0\subseteq I_v$ and $S\subseteq D_v$ by (T3). Every element of $I_v$ is valuative, so $S\cap I_v=S_0$. If $S=S_0$, then $\dim S\le\dim I_v\le\mathrm{rrank}(vF/v\kappa)\le s$. Otherwise an element of $S\setminus S_0$ has nonzero image under $\rho_v$, so $(Fv)^\times$ is not $\ell$-divisible. Then $Fv\ne\kappa v$, and as $\kappa v$ is algebraically closed, $\mathrm{trdeg}(Fv/\kappa v)\ge1$. So
$$\dim S=\dim S_0+1\le\mathrm{rrank}(vF/v\kappa)+\mathrm{trdeg}(Fv/\kappa v)\le s.$$
This applies to every finite-dimensional subspace of $S$, so it bounds $\dim S$. If $\dim S=s$, every inequality in the relevant chain is an equality, giving $S_0=I_v$ and equality in Step 1. For the lower bound, prolong a full coordinate flag valuation of a rational subfield $\kappa(t_1,\dots,t_s)$ to $F$. Its value group contains $\mathbb Z^s$ with finite index and is torsion free, hence is $\cong\mathbb Z^s$. So $I_v$ has dimension $s$, and it is alternating by Step 2, as its residual images are $0$ [@openai2026modl, Proposition 2.6, p. 8]. This proves part 1.

*Step 4: rank one.* Let $v$ be a quasi-prime divisor. On $Fv/\kappa v$, which has transcendence degree $s-1\ge1$, choose full discrete flags $w_1,w_2$ trivial on $\kappa v$. Take coordinate flags with distinct first prime divisors on a rational subfield, and prolong them. The flags on the subfield have no nontrivial common coarsening, because their coarsest nontrivial coarsenings are their distinct first prime divisors. A common coarsening of the prolongations would restrict to the trivial valuation of the subfield. A valuation of a finite extension that is trivial on the base has finite, hence zero, value group. So the prolongations are independent too, and Step 2 gives $I_{w_1}\cap I_{w_2}\subseteq D_{w_1}\cap D_{w_2}=0$. Put $v_i=w_i\circ v$. By Step 1, $v_iF/v_i\kappa\cong\mathbb Z^s$, so $S_i=I_{v_i}$ is alternating of dimension $s$, and $S_1\cap S_2=\rho_v^{-1}(I_{w_1}\cap I_{w_2})=I_v$. This is a line, since $vF/v\kappa\cong\mathbb Z$.

Conversely, let $H=S_1\cap S_2$ be a line. By Step 3 the valuative part of $S_i$ is $I_{v_i}$, of dimension at least $s-1\ge1$, with $S_i\subseteq D_{v_i}$ and $v_i$ without defect. If $v_1$ coarsens $v_2$, then $0\ne I_{v_1}\subseteq I_{v_2}$, so $I_{v_1}\subseteq S_1\cap S_2$ and $H=I_{v_1}$. The case where $v_2$ coarsens $v_1$ is symmetric. If they are incomparable, with finest common coarsening $a$, then $H\subseteq D_{v_1}\cap D_{v_2}=I_a$ by Step 2. Either way every element of $H$ is valuative, so $H\subseteq I_{v_1}\cap I_{v_2}\subseteq S_1\cap S_2=H$. Let $u=v_H$. By (T1) it coarsens both $v_i$, so $H\subseteq I_u\subseteq I_{v_1}\cap I_{v_2}=H$. Equality in Step 1 passes to $u$, so $uF/u\kappa$ is free, and $\dim I_u=1$ forces $uF/u\kappa\cong\mathbb Z$ and $\mathrm{trdeg}(Fu/\kappa u)=s-1$. A coarsening $u'$ of $u$ with the same two properties has $I_{u'}\subseteq I_u$ of dimension $1$. Then $I_{u'}=H$, so $u$ coarsens $u'$ by (T1), and $u'=u$. Thus $u$ is a quasi-prime divisor. If $v$ is any quasi-prime divisor with $I_v=H$, then $v_H$ coarsens $v$, and $H\subseteq I_{v_H}\subseteq I_v=H$. By Step 1, $v_H$ has free relative value group, hence $\cong\mathbb Z$ as its inertia is a line, and residue transcendence degree $s-1$. Minimality gives $v=v_H$.

Finally, if $0\ne h\in I_v$, an element of $D_v$ alternates with $h$ by Step 2, since $\rho_vh=0$. Conversely $g$ alternating with $h$ lies in $D_{v_H}=D_v$ by (T2), since $H$ is alternating, lying in $I_v$ [@openai2026modl, Proposition 2.7, p. 9]. This proves part 2.

*Step 5: inertia determines the valuation.* Let $v$ be a quasi-prime divisor and $C\subseteq vF$ an $\ell$-divisible convex subgroup. Its image in $vF/v\kappa\cong\mathbb Z$ is $\ell$-divisible, hence $0$, so $C\subseteq v\kappa$. Coarsening $v$ by $C$ does not change the relative value group, and by Step 1 the residue transcendence degree stays $s-1$. Minimality of $v$ forces $C=0$. For a quasi-prime $r$-divisor, the value group $B$ of its last step is a nonzero convex subgroup of $vF$. That step is a quasi-prime divisor of a residue function field, so by the case just proved $B$ has no nonzero $\ell$-divisible convex subgroup. Convex subgroups are totally ordered by inclusion. A nonzero $\ell$-divisible convex subgroup $C\subseteq B$ is impossible. If $B\subseteq C$, then $B$ is $\ell$-divisible: if $b=\ell c$ with $c\in C$, then $|c|\le|b|$, so $c\in B$ by convexity. That is also impossible.

Now put $\Gamma=vF$. Characters in $I_v$ are exactly the homomorphisms $\Gamma\to\Lambda$, as these kill the divisible group $v\kappa$. Their common kernel is $\ell\Gamma$, which is the image under $v$ of the common kernel in $F^\times$. A convex subgroup $C\subseteq\ell\Gamma$ is $\ell$-divisible: for $0\le c=\ell\gamma\in C$ we have $0\le\gamma\le c$. So $C=0$, and (T1) gives $v_{I_v}=v$ [@openai2026modl, Lemma 2.8, pp. 9–10]. This proves part 3.

*Step 6: iteration.* $\Phi$ and $\Phi^{-1}$ preserve alternating subspaces and their dimensions, so part 1 gives equal transcendence degrees. Part 2 is stated purely in terms of alternating subspaces, so $\Phi$ permutes the quasi-prime inertia lines, and their decomposition spaces with them. By Step 2, $\Phi$ induces a topological isomorphism $A_{Lw}\to A_{Kv}$ that preserves alternating pairs and intertwines $\rho_w$ and $\rho_v$. By Step 1 the residue fields of quasi-prime $r$-divisors are function fields of transcendence degree $d-r$ over algebraically closed fields, possibly of characteristic $\ell$, so the argument repeats while $d-r\ge2$. The displayed inverse-image identities carry each residual match to a match of composites on $L$ and $K$. Every quasi-prime $r$-divisor arises in this way from a quasi-prime $(r-1)$-divisor. Part 3 makes the matching of valuations well defined, whatever presentation as a composite is used. The iteration stops at $r=d-1$, with residual curves [@openai2026modl, Corollary 2.9, p. 10]. $\square$

**Differences from the pro-$\ell$ case.** In the [$\mathbb Z_\ell$-character version](characteristic-free-quasi-divisor-recovery.md), dimensions are counted over $\mathbb Q_\ell$, and the inertia of a quasi-prime divisor determines it directly. Mod $\ell$, $I_v=\mathrm{Hom}(vF,\Lambda)$ depends only on $vF/\ell vF$. So Step 3 needs the bound $\dim G/\ell G\le\mathrm{rrank}\,G$, and Step 5 needs the absence of $\ell$-divisible convex subgroups. No integral or saturation step is needed.

**Scope.** This checks §2 of the [October 2026 mod-$\ell$ manuscript](manuscripts-claiming-bogomolov-pop.md); no gap was found. Topaz's local theorems and the approximation theorem are cited inputs, not re-proved. It does not recognize finite residual constants or point lines on the residual curves, which is the manuscript's §3, and it does not separate true prime divisors from quasi-prime ones.
