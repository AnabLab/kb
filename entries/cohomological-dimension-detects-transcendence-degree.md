**Statement.** Let $k$ be algebraically closed, let $K/k$ be finitely generated of transcendence degree $d$, and let $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$ be prime. Write $G_K(\ell)$ for the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of $G_K$. Then
$$
d=\operatorname{cd}_\ell(G_K)
=\operatorname{cd}_\ell(G_K(\ell))
=\max\{n\ge0:H^n(G_K,\mathbb F_\ell)\ne0\}
=\max\{n\ge0:K_n^M(K)/\ell\ne0\}.
$$
Cohomology is continuous with trivial discrete coefficients. Here $\operatorname{cd}_\ell(G)$ is the least $r$ such that $H^q(G,M)=0$ for every discrete $\ell$-primary torsion $G$-module $M$ and every $q>r$ [@poonen2017, Definition 1.4.1, p. 12]. The equalities are deduced below from standard results; top-degree nonvanishing is checked explicitly.

**Dimension and vanishing.** Serre's transcendence-degree formula, as stated by Poonen, gives
$$
\operatorname{cd}_\ell(G_K)=\operatorname{cd}_\ell(G_k)+d=d
$$
because $G_k=1$ [@poonen2017, Proposition 1.4.11 and §1.4.3(1), p. 15]. Finite generation and $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$ are hypotheses of the equality. A choice of primitive $\ell$th root in $k$ trivializes $\mu_\ell$ and its tensor powers compatibly with cup products. The norm residue theorem therefore identifies $K_n^M(K)/\ell$ with $H^n(G_K,\mathbb F_\ell)$ [@voevodsky2011, Theorem 6.16, p. 41]. Both vanish for $n>d$.

**A nonzero top symbol, checked here.** Suppose $d>0$. Since $k$ is perfect, choose a smooth integral $k$-model of $K$ and a closed point on it [@stacks2026, Lemma 33.25.8, Tag 0B8X]. Its residue field is $k$. Its local ring $A$ is regular of dimension $d$; choose regular parameters $t_1,\ldots,t_d$. Put
$$
A_i=A/(t_1,\ldots,t_i),\qquad F_i=\operatorname{Frac}(A_i)
\quad(0\le i\le d).
$$
Each $A_i$ is a regular local domain, $F_0=K$, and $F_d=k$ [@stacks2026, Lemmas 10.106.2–10.106.3, Section 10.106, Tag 00NN]. For $1\le i\le d$, localizing $A_{i-1}$ at the prime generated by the image of $t_i$ gives a Noetherian local domain with maximal ideal generated by that nonzero image, hence a discrete valuation ring [@stacks2026, Lemma 10.119.7(4), Tag 00PD]. Its fraction field is $F_{i-1}$ and its residue field is $F_i$. The image of $t_i$ is a uniformizer and those of the remaining parameters are units there. The Milnor residue homomorphism sends a symbol beginning with a uniformizer to the symbol of the remaining unit residues [@gilleszamuely2006, Proposition 7.1.4, p. 185]. Iterating gives
$$
K_d^M(K)\longrightarrow K_0^M(k)=\mathbb Z,
\qquad\{t_1,\ldots,t_d\}\longmapsto1.
$$
After reduction modulo $\ell$, the image is still nonzero, so $K_d^M(K)/\ell\ne0$. For $d=0$, $K=k$ and degree zero is $\mathbb F_\ell$.

**The pro-l quotient and smaller input.** Inflation gives
$$
H^*(G_K(\ell),\mathbb F_\ell)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^*(G_K,\mathbb F_\ell)
$$
[@cheboluefratminac2012, Remark 8.2]. For a pro-$\ell$ group, $\operatorname{cd}_\ell(G)\le r$ exactly when $H^{r+1}(G,\mathbb F_\ell)=0$ [@gilleszamuely2006, Proposition 6.1.5, pp. 137–138]. Together with the nonzero top class, this proves the remaining dimension equality.

Even $V=H^1(G_K,\mathbb F_\ell)$ and the kernel $R$ of $V\otimes V\to H^2(G_K,\mathbb F_\ell)$ suffice: the [quadraticity consequence of norm residue](norm-residue-isomorphism-theorem.md) identifies the full ring with $T_{\mathbb F_\ell}(V)/(R)$. The largest nonzero degree is $d$. In particular, the [small abelian-by-central quotient](abelian-by-central-galois-cohomology.md) determining this cohomology ring already determines $d$ [@cheboluefratminac2012, Theorem A]. This recovers a dimension invariant before the stronger [birational field reconstruction](pop-pro-l-birational-theorem.md).

**At the characteristic prime.** If $\operatorname{char}K=p>0$, then $\operatorname{cd}_p(G_K)\le1$ regardless of $d$ [@gilleszamuely2006, Proposition 6.1.9, pp. 139–140]. Over finite constants, the [Artin–Schreier example](artin-schreier-and-pro-p-galois-groups.md) even gives function fields of different transcendence degrees with isomorphic maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups. Thus the prime restriction in the statement is essential.
