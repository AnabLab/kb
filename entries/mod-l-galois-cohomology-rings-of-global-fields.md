**Setting.** Let $\ell$ be an odd prime and $K$ a field containing a primitive $\ell$th root of unity, so that $\mathrm{char}\, K \ne \ell$. Write $H^n(G_K)$ for $H^n(G_K, \mathbb{Z}/\ell)$ with trivial action. For a profinite group $G$, let $G_{(3)}$ be the closed subgroup generated by the $\ell$th powers and by $[[G, G], G]$, and $G_{[3]} = G/G_{(3)}$. This is the [minimal quotient](minimal-galois-cohomology-quotient.md) of Efrat and Mináč for $q = \ell$, who define $G_{(3)} = G^\ell[G^{(2)}, G]$ with $G^{(2)} = G^\ell[G, G]$ [@efratminac2017functors, §2, equation (2.1)]. The two descriptions agree, as checked here: $[G, G] \subseteq G^{(2)}$ gives one inclusion, and the identity $[ab, g] = [a, g]^b [b, g]$, with $[G^\ell, G] \subseteq G^\ell$ since $G^\ell$ is normal, gives the other. So $G_{[3]}$ is the maximal quotient of $G$ of exponent $\ell$ and nilpotency class at most $2$.

For a number field $K$ and a prime $P$ of $K$ above $\ell$, write $m_P = [K_P : \mathbb{Q}_\ell]$. Two global fields $K, L$ containing primitive $\ell$th roots of unity are *Hilbert-symbol equivalent of degree $\ell$* if there are an isomorphism $f : \mu_\ell(K) \to \mu_\ell(L)$, a group isomorphism $t : K^\times/K^{\times \ell} \to L^\times/L^{\times \ell}$ and a bijection $T$ from the primes of $K$ to those of $L$ with $(a, b)_P^f = (ta, tb)_{TP}$ for all $a, b$ and every prime $P$, where $(a, b)_P$ is the Hilbert symbol of degree $\ell$ [@czogala2001, §1, p. 393].

**Theorem (number fields).** Let $K, L$ be number fields containing primitive $\ell$th roots of unity, $\ell$ odd. The following are equivalent.

1. $H^*(G_K) \cong H^*(G_L)$ as graded rings.
2. There are group isomorphisms $\varphi_i : H^i(G_K) \to H^i(G_L)$, $i = 1, 2$, with $\varphi_2(x \cup y) = \varphi_1(x) \cup \varphi_1(y)$.
3. $(G_K)_{[3]} \cong (G_L)_{[3]}$ as profinite groups.
4. $K^M_*(K)/\ell \cong K^M_*(L)/\ell$ as graded rings.
5. $K$ and $L$ are Hilbert-symbol equivalent of degree $\ell$.
6. There is a bijection from the primes of $K$ above $\ell$ to those of $L$ above $\ell$ that preserves the local degrees $m_P$.

As the local degrees at $\ell$ add up to $[K : \mathbb{Q}]$, a standard fact, these conditions force $[K : \mathbb{Q}] = [L : \mathbb{Q}]$. The equivalences 4 $\Leftrightarrow$ 5 $\Leftrightarrow$ 6 are due to Czogała and Sładek, as cited below; the translation to 1, 2 and 3 is made here.

**Theorem (function fields).** Any two global function fields containing primitive $\ell$th roots of unity, $\ell$ odd, are Hilbert-symbol equivalent of degree $\ell$ [@czogala2001, Theorem 2.4], so any two of them satisfy conditions 1–4 above. For example, $\mathbb{F}_4(t)$, $\mathbb{F}_7(t)$ and the function field of any curve over $\mathbb{F}_{13}$ all contain a primitive cube root of unity, so their mod-$3$ cohomology rings are isomorphic, and so are their maximal quotients of exponent $3$ and class at most $2$, though their characteristics are $2$, $7$ and $13$.

**Proof.**

- *1 $\Leftrightarrow$ 4.* A primitive $\ell$th root of unity in each field turns the norm residue isomorphism into an isomorphism of graded rings $K^M_*(K)/\ell \cong H^*(G_K)$, by part 1 of the consequences in [the norm residue isomorphism theorem](norm-residue-isomorphism-theorem.md).
- *1 $\Leftrightarrow$ 2.* One direction is clear. Conversely, by the same part 1, $H^*(G_K)$ is the tensor algebra on $H^1(G_K)$ modulo the ideal generated by the kernel $R_K$ of the cup product $H^1(G_K) \otimes H^1(G_K) \to H^2(G_K)$, and likewise for $L$. If $x \in R_K$, the cup product of $(\varphi_1 \otimes \varphi_1)(x)$ is $\varphi_2(0) = 0$, so $(\varphi_1 \otimes \varphi_1)(R_K) \subseteq R_L$. The inverses of $\varphi_1, \varphi_2$ give the reverse inclusion, so $\varphi_1$ induces an isomorphism of graded rings.
- *3 $\Rightarrow$ 1.* By Efrat and Mináč, inflation from $G_{[3]}$ to $G = G_K$ is an isomorphism from the subring of $H^*((G_K)_{[3]})$ generated by degree $1$ onto $H^*(G_K)$ [@efratminac2017functors, Theorem A]. The source of this map depends only on the profinite group $(G_K)_{[3]}$.
- *2 $\Rightarrow$ 3.* Efrat and Mináč prove that the part of $H^*(G_K)$ in degrees at most $2$, with its cup product, determines $(G_K)_{[3]}$ [@efratminac2017functors, Theorem B], by applying their Theorem 7.2 through Corollary 7.3(1). That theorem says the following [@efratminac2017functors, Theorem 7.2 and Examples 4.2(1)]. Let $\pi_i : S \to G_i$, $i = 1, 2$, be *covers*: epimorphisms from one profinite group $S$ with $H^2(S, \mathbb{Z}/\ell) = 0$ that induce isomorphisms $S/S^{(2)} \to G_i/G_i^{(2)}$ [@efratminac2017functors, §4, p. 10]. Let $\sigma : H^1(G_1) \to H^1(G_2)$ be the induced isomorphism, and suppose some injective $\tau : H^2(G_1) \to H^2(G_2)$ satisfies $\tau(x \cup y) = \sigma(x) \cup \sigma(y)$. Then $(G_1)_{[3]} \cong (G_2)_{[3]}$. To apply it, take $G_1 = G_K(\ell)$ and $G_2 = G_L(\ell)$, the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotients. Inflation identifies their cohomology rings with those of $G_K$ and $G_L$, by part 3 of the consequences in [the norm residue isomorphism theorem](norm-residue-isomorphism-theorem.md), and $(G_K(\ell))_{[3]} = (G_K)_{[3]}$ because the latter is a pro-$\ell$ group. The following construction of covers inducing $\sigma = \varphi_1$ uses standard facts on pro-$\ell$ groups, not checked in a source here: a closed subgroup of a pro-$\ell$ group $G$ that maps onto $G/G^{(2)}$ is $G$, and a free pro-$\ell$ group on a sequence converging to $1$ has $H^2 = 0$. As $K$ is countable, $G_1$ has countably many open subgroups. Choose a basis $\chi_1, \chi_2, \dots$ of $H^1(G_1)$, which is countable by Kummer theory, let $e_n \in G_1/G_1^{(2)}$ be the dual elements, with $\chi_m(e_n) = \delta_{mn}$, and lift them to $x_n \in G_1$ with $x_n \to 1$, which is possible because $e_n \to 1$ and $G_1$ has a countable basis of open normal subgroups. Do the same in $G_2$ with the basis $\varphi_1(\chi_n)$, giving $x'_n$. Let $S$ be free pro-$\ell$ on $s_1, s_2, \dots$ converging to $1$, and define $\pi_1, \pi_2$ by $s_n \mapsto x_n$ and $s_n \mapsto x'_n$. They are onto by the first fact, since the $e_n$ generate $G_1/G_1^{(2)}$ topologically, their annihilator in $H^1(G_1)$ being $0$, and likewise in $G_2$. As $\varphi_1(\chi_m)(x'_n) = \delta_{mn} = \chi_m(x_n)$, the maps $\pi_2^*$ and $\pi_1^*$ send $\varphi_1(\chi_m)$ and $\chi_m$ to the same character of $S$, the one that is $1$ at $s_m$ and $0$ at the other $s_n$. These characters form a basis of $H^1(S)$, whose elements are the characters vanishing at almost all $s_n$, so $\pi_1, \pi_2$ are isomorphisms on $H^1$, hence on $S/S^{(2)}$ by duality, and $\sigma = (\pi_2^*)^{-1}\pi_1^* = \varphi_1$. Take $\tau = \varphi_2$.
- *4 $\Leftrightarrow$ 6.* Sładek calls fields containing a primitive $n$th root of unity Milnor $n$-equivalent if there are a group isomorphism $t : K^\times/K^{\times n} \to L^\times/L^{\times n}$ with $t(-1) = -1$ and a group isomorphism $K^M_2(K)/n \to K^M_2(L)/n$ sending each symbol $\{a, b\}$ to $\{ta, tb\}$ [@sladek1998, §2, p. 185]. For odd $n$ the condition on $-1$ is automatic, as $-1 = (-1)^n$ [@sladek1998, Remark 2.1]. Milnor $n$-equivalence holds exactly when there is an isomorphism of graded rings $K^M_*(K)/n \to K^M_*(L)/n$ sending $\{-1\}$ to $\{-1\}$ [@sladek1998, Proposition 2.4], and for number fields and $n = \ell$ this happens exactly when the invariants $(m, g; m_1, \dots, m_g)$ of $K$ and $L$ agree [@sladek1998, Theorem 3.3]. Here $m$ is the degree over $\mathbb{Q}$, $g$ the number of primes above $\ell$, and $m_1 \le \dots \le m_g$ their local degrees [@sladek1998, Remark 3.2]. Since $m$ is the sum of the $m_i$, equality of the invariants is condition 6.
- *5 $\Leftrightarrow$ 6 and 4 $\Leftrightarrow$ 5.* For global fields of characteristic prime to $\ell$ containing a primitive $\ell$th root of unity, Czogała states both: Hilbert-symbol equivalence of degree $\ell$ is the same as an isomorphism of graded rings $K^M_*(K)/\ell \cong K^M_*(L)/\ell$ sending $\{-1\}$ to $\{-1\}$, crediting Sładek, and for number fields and odd $\ell$ it is the same as condition 6, crediting Czogała and Sładek [@czogala2001, §1 and §2, statement 2.3, p. 394]. Sładek's own paper compares the two equivalences only for number fields [@sladek1998, §3]. For function fields, the first statement gives 4, and then 1–3 follow as above. $\square$

**Examples.** These are checked here. Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)$. It has degree $\ell - 1$, the prime $\ell$ is totally ramified in it, so $F$ has one prime $\lambda$ above $\ell$, with residue field $\mathbb{F}_\ell$, and its only quadratic subfield is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell^*})$, $\ell^* = (-1)^{(\ell - 1)/2}\ell$. These are standard facts, not checked in a source here.

- *$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)$ is determined.* If $L$ contains a primitive $\ell$th root of unity and $(G_L)_{[3]} \cong (G_F)_{[3]}$, then $[L : \mathbb{Q}] = \ell - 1$ by the theorem, and $L \supseteq F$, so $L \cong F$. So among number fields containing $\mu_\ell$, the maximal quotient of exponent $\ell$ and class at most $2$ determines $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_\ell)$, as the W-group determines $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ when $\ell = 2$ ([Witt equivalence](witt-equivalence-of-number-fields.md)).
- *Degree $2(\ell - 1)$: two classes, each infinite.* A number field $K$ of degree $2(\ell - 1)$ containing $\mu_\ell$ is a quadratic extension of $F$, so either $\lambda$ splits in $K$, giving local degrees $(\ell - 1, \ell - 1)$, or it does not, giving one prime of local degree $2(\ell - 1)$. By the theorem, these two cases are exactly the isomorphism classes of rings $H^*(G_K)$, and of quotients $(G_K)_{[3]}$, in this degree, and both occur, as follows. For an integer $d$ prime to $\ell$ with $\sqrt{d} \notin F$, the field $F(\sqrt{d})$ is in the first class if $d$ is a square modulo $\ell$ and in the second otherwise. Indeed, $\lambda$ splits exactly when $d$ is a square in $F_\lambda$, and since $\ell$ is odd and $d$ is a unit at $\lambda$ with residue in $\mathbb{F}_\ell$, Hensel's lemma makes this equivalent to $d$ being a square modulo $\ell$. For distinct primes $p, p' \ne \ell$, the fields $F(\sqrt{p})$ and $F(\sqrt{p'})$ are not isomorphic. They are Galois over $\mathbb{Q}$, so an isomorphism would make them equal inside $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, and then $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{pp'})$ would be a quadratic subfield of $F$, that is, $pp' = \ell^*$ as both are squarefree, which is impossible. In the same way $\sqrt{p} \notin F$, so $F(\sqrt{p})$ has degree $2(\ell - 1)$. By Dirichlet's theorem there are infinitely many primes that are squares modulo $\ell$ and infinitely many that are not, so each class contains infinitely many fields.
- *For $\ell = 3$.* Here $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$. As $-1$ and $2$ are not squares modulo $3$, the fields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}, i) = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}, \sqrt{2})$ have one prime above $3$, of local degree $4$. Their mod-$3$ cohomology rings and their maximal quotients of exponent $3$ and class at most $2$ are isomorphic, but the fields are not, since their quadratic subfields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}), \mathbb{Q}(i), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}), \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-6})$ differ. As $7 \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, the field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}, \sqrt{7})$ is in the other class.

**Role in the graph.** By the [Neukirch–Uchida theorem](neukirch-uchida-theorem.md) and [Uchida's theorem](uchida-global-function-fields.md), $G_K$ determines a global field $K$. For odd $\ell$ and $\mu_\ell \subset K$, its quotient $(G_K)_{[3]}$, and with it the whole mod-$\ell$ cohomology ring, retains only the local degrees at $\ell$, and it is the same for all global function fields containing $\mu_\ell$. For $\ell = 2$ the corresponding quotient is the W-group, which for number fields also records the number of real places, the level, and the dyadic local degrees and levels ([Witt equivalence](witt-equivalence-of-number-fields.md)). [Koymans and Pagano](two-step-nilpotent-not-anabelian.md) show that the larger quotient of nilpotency class $2$, without the exponent condition, does not determine a number field either.

They have not been opened for this entry, and neither has the paper of Szymiczek that Sładek cites for the case $\ell = 2$.
