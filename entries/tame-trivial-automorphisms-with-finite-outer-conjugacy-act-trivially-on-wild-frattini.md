**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P$ its wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$ and $V=P/P^{(2)}$ with $P^{(2)}=P^p[P,P]$. Use the presentation $G=F/(\varrho)$ and the notation of [the tame-group-algebra theorem](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). So $\mathcal G\cong\Gamma$ is generated by $\sigma,\tau$; $p^s$ is the number of $p$-power roots of unity in $K^{\mathrm{tr}}$; $\omega:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ is the character on $\mu_p$; $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$; and
$$\varrho=(x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0,\tau)^\kappa\,x_1^{p^s}\,C .$$
Here $C=[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d]$ for even $d$, and $C=[x_1,y_1][x_2,x_3]\cdots[x_{d-1},x_d]$ for odd $d$, where $y_1$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by $x_1$ and $\mathcal G$. By part 1 there,
$$V=L\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^d\Lambda\bar x_i,\qquad L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0,\qquad \rho\bar x_0=\omega(\rho)\bar x_0,$$
with each $\Lambda\bar x_i$ free of rank one. The *surface pairs* are $(x_{2k},x_{2k+1})$ for odd $d$ and $(x_{2k+1},x_{2k+2})$ for even $d$, for $k\ge1$ with both indices at most $d$. Each generator of a surface pair $(a,b)$ occurs in $\varrho$ only in its factor $[a,b]$. An automorphism of $G$ is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$.

As in [the boundary-twist theorem](boundary-twists-exclude-finite-outer-conjugacy-of-wild-scalars.md), let $P_j$ be the ordinary lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$ and $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$. Its lower central input gives the following. The $\mathcal L_j$ are torsion-free. $\mathcal L_j/p$ is the degree-$j$ completed free Lie space $\mathcal L_j(V)$ on $H/p=V$, functorially in $V$. The map $V\to\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathcal L_2(V))$, $h\mapsto[h,\cdot\,]$, is injective.

*Coordinates.* For distinct indices $i_1,i_2,i_3\in\{0,\dots,d\}$, let $\pi:V\to\mathbb F_p^3$ be the continuous linear map sending $\bar x_{i_k}$ to the $k$-th basis vector $X_{i_k}$, sending $\lambda\bar x_{i_k}$ to $\operatorname{aug}(\lambda)X_{i_k}$ when $i_k\ne0$, and killing the other summands. By functoriality it induces maps $\mathcal L_j(V)\to\mathcal L_j(\mathbb F_p^3)$ compatible with brackets. The target lies in the free associative algebra on $X_{i_1},X_{i_2},X_{i_3}$, where coefficients of words can be compared.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ be tame-trivial with finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$. Then $\alpha$ acts trivially on $V$; that is, the shear $E$ of the boundary-twist theorem vanishes. Consequently, if $\alpha$ has finite order, then $\alpha=1$. This includes $p=3$, where the boundary-twist theorem left finite order divisible by $3$ open.

**Proof.**

*0. The shear.* By the boundary-twist theorem, the action $A$ of $\alpha$ on $V$ is $1+E$, with $E(V)\subseteq L$ and $E(L)=0$; so $A^{-1}=1-E$. Since $\alpha(\rho)\in\rho P$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$ and $P$ acts trivially on $V$, $A$ and $E$ are $\Lambda$-linear. Write $E(\bar x_i)=t_i\bar x_0$ with $t_i\in\mathbb F_p$ for $1\le i\le d$. Then
$$E\Big(a\bar x_0+\sum_{i=1}^d\lambda_i\bar x_i\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^d\omega(\lambda_i)\,t_i\,\bar x_0,$$
so it suffices to show that every $t_i$ vanishes. Every conjugate $\beta^{-1}\alpha\beta$ with $\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ is again tame-trivial and has the same finite set of outer conjugates. So everything proved below for $\alpha$ also holds for it.

*1. Surface pairs.* Let $(a,b)$ be a surface pair. We show $t_a=t_b=0$.

Steps 1 and 2 of the proof of the boundary-twist theorem are written for the first surface pair. The only property of the pair that they use is that $a$ and $b$ occur in $\varrho$ only in $[a,b]$, so they apply verbatim to $(a,b)$. Let $T$ be the boundary twist, with $T(a)=waw^{-1}$ and $T(b)=wbw^{-1}$ for $w=[a,b]$, fixing $\mathcal G$ and the other $x_i$. Let $D=J(T):H\to\mathcal L_3$ be its Johnson map. Those steps give $h'\in\mathcal L_2$ with $A_3DA_H^{-1}-D=\operatorname{ad}(h')$. Reduce modulo $p$, and write $\bar D:V\to\mathcal L_3(V)$ for the reduction of $D$:
$$A_3\bar DA^{-1}-\bar D=\operatorname{ad}(\bar h),\qquad \bar h\in\mathcal L_2(V).\tag{1}$$
Here $A_3$ is the action induced by $A$ on $\mathcal L_3(V)$. Since $T$ fixes $x_0$, $x_1$ and $\mathcal G$, $\bar D$ vanishes on $L$ and on $\Lambda\bar x_1$. Also $\bar D(\bar a)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]$ and $\bar D(\bar b)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]$, since $T(b)b^{-1}=[w,b]$, and similarly for $a$.

Evaluate (1) at $\bar x_1$. Both $\bar x_1$ and $A^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-t_1\bar x_0$ lie in the kernel of $\bar D$, so $[\bar h,\bar x_1]=0$. Step 3 there shows, by the single-generator tensor argument, that a degree-two Lie element commuting with a nonzero vector of $V$ vanishes; that argument does not use the quotient by $L$. Hence $\bar h=0$, and
$$A_3\bar D=\bar DA.\tag{2}$$
Evaluate (2) at $\bar b$. The right side is $\bar D(\bar b+t_b\bar x_0)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]$. The left side is $[[\bar a+t_a\bar x_0,\bar b+t_b\bar x_0],\bar b+t_b\bar x_0]$. Apply the coordinates for the indices of $x_0,a,b$ and take the coefficient of the word $X_0X_bX_b$. The right side has none, because each of its words contains $X_a$. On the left, a term with one $X_0$, two $X_b$ and no $X_a$ must take $t_aX_0$ from the first slot and $X_b$ from the other two. So the only contribution is $t_a[[X_0,X_b],X_b]=t_a(X_0X_bX_b-2X_bX_0X_b+X_bX_bX_0)$, with coefficient $t_a$. Hence $t_a=0$.

Evaluate (2) at $\bar a$ in the same way, now with the word $X_0X_aX_a$. A term without $X_b$ must take $t_bX_0$ from the middle slot, and then $X_a$ from the other two. So the only contribution is $t_b[[X_a,X_0],X_a]=t_b(2X_aX_0X_a-X_0X_aX_a-X_aX_aX_0)$, with coefficient $-t_b$. The right side $[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]$ contributes nothing. Hence $t_b=0$.

*2. Even $d$: the complementary pair.* Let $d$ be even, and let $w'=x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2]$, which is a subword of $\varrho$. Let $T'$ fix $\mathcal G$ and the $x_i$ with $i\notin\{1,2\}$, and put $T'(x_i)=w'x_iw'^{-1}$ for $i=1,2$. Then $T'(w')=w'w'w'^{-1}=w'$, so $T'(\varrho)=\varrho$. Substituting $w'^{-1}$ for $w'$ in the same way gives the inverse, so $T'$ induces an automorphism of $G$.

*A degree-one Johnson map.* For a continuous automorphism $\theta$ of $P$ that acts trivially on $H$, put
$$J_1(\theta)(xP_2)=\theta(x)x^{-1}P_3\in\mathcal L_2 .$$
Such a $\theta$ acts trivially on $\mathcal L_2$. Indeed $\mathcal L_2$ is topologically generated by classes of commutators, and $[y',z']\equiv[y,z]$ modulo $P_3$ when $y'\in yP_2$ and $z'\in zP_2$. From $\theta(xy)(xy)^{-1}=\theta(x)x^{-1}\cdot x\,\theta(y)y^{-1}x^{-1}$ it follows that $J_1(\theta)$ is a well-defined continuous homomorphism $H\to\mathcal L_2$. From $\theta\theta'(x)x^{-1}=\theta(\theta'(x)x^{-1})\,\theta(x)x^{-1}$ it follows that $J_1(\theta\theta')=J_1(\theta)+J_1(\theta')$. Substituting $x=\beta(y)$ gives $J_1(\beta\theta\beta^{-1})=\beta_2J_1(\theta)\beta_H^{-1}$ for every automorphism $\beta$ of $P$, where $\beta_H$ and $\beta_2$ are the induced actions on $H$ and $\mathcal L_2$. Finally, $J_1(c_g)(v)=[gP_2,v]$ for $g\in P$, since $c_g(x)x^{-1}=[g,x]$.

The automorphism $T'$ preserves $P$ and acts trivially on $H$. It fixes $\mathcal G$, so it commutes with the action of $\Gamma$ on $H$. It fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,2$, and $T'(x_i)x_i^{-1}=[w',x_i]\in P_2$ for $i=1,2$. The $\bar x_i$ generate $H$ as a topological $\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$-module. Put $z=[x_1,x_2]\in P_2$. Using $[yz,x]=y[z,x]y^{-1}[y,x]$,
$$[w',x_1]=x_1^{p^s}[z,x_1]x_1^{-p^s}\in P_3,\qquad [w',x_2]=x_1^{p^s}[z,x_2]x_1^{-p^s}\,[x_1^{p^s},x_2]\equiv[x_1^{p^s},x_2]\pmod{P_3}.$$
So $J=J_1(T')$ vanishes at $\bar x_0$ and $\bar x_1$ and has $J(\bar x_2)=p^s[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]$. As $J$ is continuous and $\Gamma$-equivariant, its image lies in the closed subgroup $p^s\mathcal L_2$. Multiplication by $p^s$ is a homeomorphism of the torsion-free compact group $\mathcal L_2$ onto $p^s\mathcal L_2$. So $J=p^sj$ for a continuous homomorphism $j:H\to\mathcal L_2$ with
$$j(\bar x_0)=j(\bar x_1)=0,\qquad j(\bar x_2)=[\bar x_1,\bar x_2].$$

Finite outer conjugacy gives $n>0$ and $g\in G$ with $\alpha T'^n\alpha^{-1}T'^{-n}=c_g$, as in step 2 of the boundary-twist proof. The left side is tame-trivial, so $gP\in Z(\Gamma)=1$ and $g\in P$. Both sides preserve $P$ and act trivially on $H$, so the rules above give
$$np^s\big(A_2jA_H^{-1}-j\big)=\operatorname{ad}(\bar g),\qquad \bar g=gP_2\in H.$$
Write $np^s=p^ku$ with $u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$. If $k\ge1$, then $[\bar g,H]\subseteq p\mathcal L_2$, and injectivity of $h\mapsto[h,\cdot\,]$ on $V$ gives $\bar g\in pH$. Since $H$ and $\mathcal L_2$ are torsion-free, we may divide by $p$ and repeat, until $\bar g=p^kh_1$ with $h_1\in H$. Cancelling $p^k$ in the torsion-free group $\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)$ gives $A_2jA_H^{-1}-j=\operatorname{ad}(u^{-1}h_1)$. Reducing modulo $p$, with $\bar\jmath$ the reduction of $j$,
$$A_2\bar\jmath A^{-1}-\bar\jmath=\operatorname{ad}(\gamma),\qquad \gamma\in V.\tag{3}$$
At $\bar x_1$, the left side of (3) is $A_2\bar\jmath(\bar x_1-t_1\bar x_0)-\bar\jmath(\bar x_1)=0$, so $[\gamma,\bar x_1]=0$. At $\bar x_2$, we have $\bar\jmath(A^{-1}\bar x_2)=\bar\jmath(\bar x_2-t_2\bar x_0)=[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]$. So the left side is
$$[\bar x_1+t_1\bar x_0,\bar x_2+t_2\bar x_0]-[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]=t_1[\bar x_0,\bar x_2]+t_2[\bar x_1,\bar x_0],$$
which equals $[\gamma,\bar x_2]$. Apply the coordinates for the indices $0,1,2$, and write $\pi(\gamma)=r_0X_0+r_1X_1+r_2X_2$. The brackets $[X_0,X_1]$, $[X_0,X_2]$, $[X_1,X_2]$ are linearly independent. The first identity becomes $r_0[X_0,X_1]-r_2[X_1,X_2]=0$, so $r_0=r_2=0$. The second becomes $t_1[X_0,X_2]-t_2[X_0,X_1]=r_0[X_0,X_2]+r_1[X_1,X_2]$, so $t_2=0$ and $t_1=r_0=0$.

With step 1, all $t_i$ vanish for even $d$.

*3. Odd $d$: the complementary generator.* Let $d$ be odd. If $\omega$ were trivial, then $\mu_p\subseteq K$, so $\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p)\subseteq K$ and $p-1$ would divide $d$, making $d$ even. So $\omega\ne1$. As $\sigma$ and $\tau$ topologically generate $\Gamma$, there is $\eta\in\{\sigma,\tau\}$ with $\omega(\eta)\ne1$. Put $\nu=\eta-\omega(\eta)$. Then $\nu\in\ker\omega$ and $\operatorname{aug}(\nu)=1-\omega(\eta)\ne0$.

Part 3 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md) gives an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\theta(\rho)\in\rho P^{(2)}$ for $\rho\in\mathcal G$ and $\theta(x_0)\in x_0P^{(2)}$, whose action $B$ on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear and satisfies
$$B\bar x_3=\bar x_3-\nu^*\delta\nu\,\bar x_2-\nu^*\delta_0\,\bar x_1 .$$
Here $\bar y_1=\delta\bar x_1$ and $\delta_0=\delta-\delta^*$, as in part 1 there. The conjugate $\theta^{-1}\alpha\theta$ acts on $V$ by $B^{-1}AB=1+B^{-1}EB$. Step 1, applied to $\theta^{-1}\alpha\theta$ and the surface pair $(x_2,x_3)$, gives $B^{-1}EB\bar x_3=0$. Step 1 applied to $\alpha$ gives $t_2=t_3=0$. So
$$0=E(B\bar x_3)=\big(t_3-\omega(\nu^*\delta\nu)t_2-\omega(\nu^*)\,\omega(\delta_0)\,t_1\big)\bar x_0=-\omega(\nu^*)\,\omega(\delta_0)\,t_1\,\bar x_0 .$$

For $\rho\in\mathcal G$, $\omega(\rho^*)=\omega(\rho)\omega(\rho)^{-1}=1=\operatorname{aug}(\rho)$. By linearity and continuity $\omega(\lambda^*)=\operatorname{aug}(\lambda)$ for all $\lambda\in\Lambda$. So $\omega(\nu^*)=\operatorname{aug}(\nu)\ne0$, and $\omega(\delta_0)=\omega(\delta)-\operatorname{aug}(\delta)$.

Formula (2) in the proof of part 1 of the mixing theorem holds for every continuous character $\chi:\mathcal G\to\mathbb F_p^\times$. Its indicators, written $c_1,c_2$ there and $\iota_1,\iota_2$ here, are $1$ exactly when $\omega(\rho_k)\chi(\rho_k)^2=1$, where $\rho_1=\tau_2^{p+1}$ and $\rho_2=\sigma_2\tau_2^{a_0}$. The elements $\rho_k$ lie in a pro-$2$ subgroup, so $\omega(\rho_k)$ has $2$-power order. For $\chi=1$ the condition reads $\omega(\rho_k)=1$. For $\chi=\omega$ it reads $\omega(\rho_k)^3=1$, which is equivalent. With $w_\sigma=\omega(\sigma_2)$ and $w_\tau=\omega(\tau_2)$, both characters therefore give the indicators of step (e) there: $\iota_1=1$ exactly when $w_\tau^{p+1}=1$, and $\iota_2=1$ exactly when $w_\sigma w_\tau^{a_0}=1$. Step (e) also shows that $\iota_1=\iota_2=1$ does not occur. Formula (2) then gives
$$\operatorname{aug}(\delta)=1+\iota_1,\qquad \omega(\delta)=w_\tau^{p+1}+w_\sigma w_\tau^{a_0}\iota_1 .$$
If $\iota_1=0$, then $\omega(\delta_0)=w_\tau^{p+1}-1\ne0$. If $\iota_1=1$, then $\iota_2=0$ and $\omega(\delta_0)=w_\sigma w_\tau^{a_0}-1\ne0$. In both cases $t_1=0$, and with step 1 all $t_i$ vanish for odd $d$.

*4. Conclusion.* So $E=0$ and $A=1$ on $V$. If $\alpha$ has finite order, part 2 of [finite-order detection](finite-order-local-galois-automorphisms-detected-on-the-frattini-quotient-of-wild-inertia.md) gives $\alpha=1$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *What changes.* The boundary-twist theorem left a shear $E$ into $L$, and excluded finite order only when it is prime to $p$ or when $p\ge5$. Since $E=0$, its conclusion 2 now holds for $p=3$ as well. Step 1 is the first step of the sketch in [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535).
- *Why three tools.* Boundary twists act trivially on $P/P_3$, and their Johnson maps involve only surface generators, so they see only the surface coefficients. The exact twist $T'$ along $x_1^{p^s}[x_1,x_2]$ acts trivially on $H$ but not on $P/P_3$. Its degree-one Johnson map lies in $p^s\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)$, and the same saturation that cancels $n$ also cancels $p^s$. For odd $d$ no such exact twist is available, because $y_1$ involves tame conjugates of $x_1$. There the mixing automorphism moves $t_1$ onto the surface generator $x_3$ with coefficient $\operatorname{aug}(\nu)\,\omega(\delta_0)$, which is nonzero because $\omega\ne1$ in odd degree.
- *Inner or not.* Not decided here: whether a tame-trivial automorphism that has finite outer conjugacy and acts trivially on $V$ must be inner. Triviality on $\Gamma$ and $V$ alone does not force this. Indeed, a boundary twist $T$ is tame-trivial and trivial on $V$, but not inner. Suppose $T=c_g$. Then $gP\in Z(\Gamma)=1$. Since $T$ is trivial on $P/P_3$, injectivity of the bracket map gives $g\in P_2$, as in step 2 of the boundary-twist proof, so $D=\operatorname{ad}(gP_3)$. Its reduction $\bar D=\operatorname{ad}(\bar h)$ vanishes at $\bar x_1$, so $\bar h=0$ as in step 1, and $\bar D=0$. But $\bar D(\bar a)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]$ is nonzero, since under the coordinates its word $X_aX_aX_b$ has coefficient $-1$. For the first surface pair, $(a,b)=(x_2,x_3)$ for odd $d$ and $(x_3,x_4)$ for even $d$, every nonzero $p$-adic power of $T$ has [infinitely many outer conjugates](first-surface-pair-boundary-twists-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md). Twists along the other surface pairs, when $d\ge5$, are not covered by that result. Whether some other outer automorphism trivial on $V$ has finitely many outer conjugates remains open.
- *Scope.* The theorem concerns tame-trivial automorphisms only. For $p=3$ it gives no new case of [Tsujimura's question](p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md) by itself: the rival-copy reduction for $p=3$ also needs the index condition and tame triviality. Degree at most two and $p=2$ are not covered. All of this is proved here from the linked entries on main, with no novelty claim. The sources behind those entries were not reopened.
