**Setting.** Let $k/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite with residue field of cardinality $Q$, and let $\pi:G_k\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma$ be its tame quotient. Put $A=\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell$. Use the [tame presentation](tame-automorphisms-lift-modulo-wild-commutators.md)
$$
\Gamma=I\rtimes\widehat{\mathbb Z},\qquad
I=\langle\tau\rangle\cong A,\qquad
\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}=\tau^Q.
$$
All maps are continuous. Every automorphism of $\Gamma$ is uniquely
$$
\beta_{u,h}(\tau)=\tau^u,\qquad
\beta_{u,h}(\sigma)=\sigma\tau^h,
\qquad u\in A^\times,\ h\in A,
\tag{1}
$$
and every such pair occurs, as recalled below. Fix an epimorphism $r:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow D$ to a finite group. Write
$$
a=r(\tau),\quad b=r(\sigma),\quad m=|a|,\quad
g=\gcd(m,Q-1).
$$
Thus $p\nmid m$ and $\langle a\rangle=r(I)$. When $m=1$, put $\operatorname{ord}_1(Q)=1$ and regard $(\mathbb Z/1)^\times$ as the one-element group.

**Theorem (proved here).**

1. The maps $r\beta_{u,h}$ and $r\beta_{u',h'}$ are conjugate by an element of $D$ if and only if
   $$
   u'u^{-1}\bmod m\ \in\langle Q\bmod m\rangle,
   \qquad h'\equiv h\pmod g.
   \tag{2}
   $$
   Consequently their conjugacy classes are parametrized, as a set, by
   $$
   (\mathbb Z/m)^\times/\langle Q\rangle\ \times\ \mathbb Z/g,
   \tag{3}
   $$
   and there are exactly $g\varphi(m)/\operatorname{ord}_m(Q)$ classes. This depends on the inertia order and $Q$, not on the order of $D/r(I)$ or on a choice of splitting of $D$.
2. In particular, $r\beta_{u,h}$ is conjugate to $r$ exactly when $u\bmod m\in\langle Q\rangle$ and $g\mid h$. Under these conditions, for every epimorphism $s:H\twoheadrightarrow D$ from a finite group,
   $$
   \#\{F:G_k\twoheadrightarrow H:sF=r\pi\}
   =\#\{F:G_k\twoheadrightarrow H:sF=r\beta_{u,h}\pi\}.
   \tag{4}
   $$
   These count actual maps, including conjugate ones.

**Proof.** First recall (1). The unramified quotient $\Gamma/I\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ is torsion-free. Applying an automorphism $\beta$ to the tame relation and then projecting to this quotient shows that the projection $x$ of $\beta(\tau)$ satisfies $(Q-1)x=0$, so $x=0$. Applying the same argument to $\beta^{-1}$ gives $\beta(I)=I$. Therefore $\beta(\tau)=\tau^u$ for a unit $u$, and $\beta(\sigma)=\sigma^v\tau^h$ with $v\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}^{\times}$. The relation then gives $Q^{v-1}=1$ in $A^\times$.

For each integer $n\ge2$, $Q^n-1$ is prime to $p$, and $Q$ has exact order $n$ modulo $Q^n-1$: no positive smaller power can be congruent to one, by size. Thus $v-1\in n\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ for every $n\ge2$, whence $v=1$. Conversely, the substitutions in (1) preserve the tame relation and define homomorphisms by the presentation. They satisfy
$$
\beta_{u,h}\beta_{u',h'}=\beta_{uu',\,h+uh'},
$$
so $\beta_{u^{-1},-u^{-1}h}$ is an inverse. This proves (1), including existence and uniqueness.

The map $r\beta_{u,h}$ is specified by the pair $(a^u,ba^h)$. Only $u$ and $h$ modulo $m$ matter. Every unit modulo $m$ lifts to $A^\times$, by lifting separately at the primes dividing $m$ and taking one at the other primes. Every residue modulo $m$ lifts to $A$. Thus all $\varphi(m)m$ such pairs occur, and they are distinct.

Every element of $D$ has the form $b^j a^c$ for integers $j,c$, because $\langle a\rangle$ is normal and the quotient is generated by the image of $b$. Since $bab^{-1}=a^Q$, conjugation by $b^j a^c$ sends the pair $(a^u,ba^h)$ to
$$
\left(a^{uQ^j},\ b a^{Q^j h+Q^{j-1}(1-Q)c}\right).
\tag{5}
$$
All exponents in (5) may be read modulo $m$; negative powers of $Q$ exist there. As $c$ varies, $Q^{j-1}(1-Q)c$ runs through precisely the multiples of $g$ modulo $m$. Moreover $Q^j\equiv1\pmod g$. Therefore (5) can give $(a^{u'},ba^{h'})$ exactly under (2). This proves necessity and sufficiency without assuming that $\ker r$ is preserved by any $\beta_{u,h}$. It also proves (3) and its cardinality, since the cyclic subgroup generated by $Q$ has order $\operatorname{ord}_m(Q)$.

Under the conditions of part 2 choose $d\in D$ with $r\beta_{u,h}=c_d r$, where $c_d$ denotes conjugation by $d$. Lift $d$ to $z\in H$ through $s$. Then $F\mapsto c_zF$ is a bijection between the sets in (4), with inverse $F\mapsto c_{z^{-1}}F$. It preserves continuity and surjectivity. The counts are finite because $G_k$ has $[k:\mathbb Q_p]+2$ generators [@jardenshusterman2023, Theorem 2.1, p. 2]: a map to $H$ is determined by the images of these generators. This proves (4). $\square$

**Consequences for the obstruction search (proved here).**

- For a pure Frobenius shift $\beta_{1,h}$, only $h\bmod g$ can affect the marked count for any fixed $H,s,r$. Indeed, part 1 and the same conjugation bijection compare any two shifts congruent modulo $g$. If $g=1$, every shift preserves the count, for every finite target $H$.
- For the unit shift over $U_f/\mathbb Q_2$, an unequal-count witness must satisfy $\gcd(m,2^f-1)>1$. In particular its tame inertia image, and hence its finite target, must have order divisible by a prime factor of $2^f-1$. For $f=2$ that prime is $3$; for $f=3$ it is $7$.
- Every tame automorphism preserves $r$ up to conjugacy exactly when $g=1$ and $Q$ generates $(\mathbb Z/m)^\times$. The symmetric cubic case is $m=3$, $Q\equiv-1\pmod3$. Another family is $m=5$, $Q=2^f$ with $f$ odd: then $Q$ has order four modulo five and $g=1$. It includes markings onto $C_5\rtimes C_4$ with faithful action, whenever that action is the $Q$-power action. All finite targets above each such marking are excluded from count obstructions.

**A nonsplit check.** Take $Q=4$, $D=C_9=\langle b\rangle$, and $a=b^3$. The tame relation holds, so these images define an epimorphism $r$. Its inertia image has order three and has no complement in $D$. Formula (3) gives six conjugacy classes: the six pairs $(a^u,ba^h)$ with $u\in\{1,2\}$ and $h\in\mathbb Z/3$ are distinct, and $D$ is abelian. Moreover $\sigma^3\tau^{-1}\in\ker r$ but
$$
r\beta_{2,0}(\sigma^3\tau^{-1})=a^{-1}\ne1.
$$
Here $2\in A^\times$ because $p=2$. Thus the classification applies even when the automorphism does not descend to $D$.

**Scope.** This classifies conjugacy of the markings obtained from a fixed $r$ by tame automorphisms. Failure of (2) does not imply unequal counts: for instance, $H=D$ and $s=\mathrm{id}$ give both counts equal to one for every automorphism. Neither a nonliftable dyadic automorphism nor a full lift is obtained here.
