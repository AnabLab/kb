**Setting.** Let $K$ be a number field and $C/K$ a smooth projective geometrically connected curve of genus at least $2$. Write $\mathrm{Sec}(C/K)$ for the sections of $\pi_1(C) \to G_K$ up to conjugation by $\pi_1(C_{\bar K})$, and $\kappa_C : C(K) \to \mathrm{Sec}(C/K)$ for the map taking a point to its section. A section $s$ restricts to a section $s \otimes K_v$ over each completion [@stix2015birational, §2.2, p. 3]. Use Stoll's modified adelic points
$$C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet = \prod_{v \nmid \infty} C(K_v) \times \prod_{v \text{ real}} \pi_0(C(K_v)),$$
with the complex places left out [@stoll2017descenterrata, §1, p. 1]. A torsor $Y \to C$ under a finite étale $K$-group scheme $G$ gives an evaluation map $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet \to \prod_v H^1(K_v, G)$. A point $P$ *survives* the torsor if it lifts to a twist of it, which happens exactly when its image lies in the image of $H^1(K, G)$ [@stoll2007descent, Definition 5.2 and the paragraph after it, p. 362]. Write $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}}$ for the points that survive every such torsor, and $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}ab}}$ for those that survive every torsor with $G$ abelian. Then
$$C(K) \subseteq C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}} \subseteq C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}ab}}$$
[@stoll2007descent, Definition 5.4, p. 364]. Stoll calls $C$ *excellent with respect to abelian coverings* if $C(K) = C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}ab}}$ [@stoll2007descent, Definition 6.1 (6), p. 373].

Consider the hypothesis:

- **(LSC$_C$)** For every finite place $v$, every section of $\pi_1(C_{K_v}) \to G_{K_v}$ comes from a point of $C(K_v)$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here from the cited results.

1. *Localization.* Assume (LSC$_C$). For $s \in \mathrm{Sec}(C/K)$ there is a unique $x(s) \in C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet$ whose component at each finite $v$ is a point with section $s \otimes K_v$, and whose component at each real $v$ is a connected component whose points have section $s \otimes K_v$. The image of $s \mapsto x(s)$ is exactly $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}}$.
2. *Criterion.* Assume (LSC$_C$). Then $\kappa_C$ is bijective if and only if $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}} = C(K)$. The implication from left to right holds without (LSC$_C$).
3. *Cases.* In each of the following cases, $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}ab}} = C(K)$, so $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}} = C(K)$, and part 2 applies.
   - (a) There is a nonconstant $K$-morphism $C \to A$ to an abelian variety with $A(K)$ finite and with trivial maximal divisible subgroup of the Tate–Shafarevich group $Ш(K, A)$ [@stoll2007descent, Theorem 8.6, p. 385]. Over $\mathbb{Q}$ this holds when $A$ is modular of analytic rank zero [@stoll2007descent, Theorem 8.6, p. 385]. Stoll takes the finiteness of $A(\mathbb{Q})$ and $Ш(\mathbb{Q}, A)$ for such $A$ from Kolyvagin and Logachev [@stoll2007descent, proof of Corollary 6.3, p. 374].
   - (b) The Jacobian $J$ of $C$ has $J(K)$ finite and the divisible part of $Ш(K, J)$ trivial [@stoll2007descent, Corollary 8.1, p. 383].
   - (c) $K = \mathbb{Q}$ and $C$ is $X_0(N)$ or $X_1(N)$ [@stoll2007descent, Corollary 8.8, p. 385].
   - (d) $K = \mathbb{Q}$ and $C$ is $y^2 = x^6 + a$, where $a$ is a nonzero integer with $|a| \le 20$, other than $-15, -13, -11, 3, 10, 11, 15$ and $17$ [@stoll2007descent, Examples 8.7, p. 385].

**Unconditional form.** A manuscript dated 6 October 2026 proves (LSC$_C$) for every smooth proper geometrically connected curve of genus at least $2$ over a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, for every $p$ [@openai2026padicsections, Theorem 1.1, p. 2]. The lab has checked its proof, but the manuscript is not peer reviewed; see [the $p$-adic section conjecture](p-adic-section-conjecture.md). Given that theorem, parts 1 and 2 hold for every $C$ as above, and so the section conjecture holds in each case of part 3. For instance, for every $N$ such that $X_0(N)$ has genus at least $2$, every section of $\pi_1(X_0(N)) \to G_{\mathbb{Q}}$ comes, up to conjugacy, from exactly one point of $X_0(N)(\mathbb{Q})$, and the same holds for $X_1(N)$.

For curves of genus at least $2$, Stoll conjectures that every such curve over a number field is excellent with respect to abelian coverings [@stoll2007descent, Conjecture 9.1 and the paragraph after it, p. 386]. So, given the $p$-adic theorem, his conjecture implies the section conjecture over number fields for these curves. In fact the weaker equality $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}} = C(K)$ is enough, and by part 2 it is also necessary. Stoll states this necessity without proof, as the remark that the section property implies excellence with respect to all coverings [@stoll2007descent, Remark 8.9, p. 386]. For the analogous reduction to the Brauer–Manin obstruction, whose proof applies its hypothesis to every finite étale neighbourhood of a section, see Stix's theorem in [descent obstructions for Galois sections](descent-obstructions-for-galois-sections.md).

**Proof.**

*Uniqueness of local data.* At a finite place $v$, the field $K_v$ is sub-$p$-adic, so distinct points of $C(K_v)$ give distinct sections, by [injectivity](section-conjecture-injectivity.md). At a real place, the sections of two points agree exactly when the points lie in the same connected component, by the [real section theorem](real-section-conjecture.md). At a complex place $G_{K_v}$ is trivial.

*Part 1.* Let $s \in \mathrm{Sec}(C/K)$. Then (LSC$_C$) gives a point $P_v \in C(K_v)$ with $s \otimes K_v = s_{P_v}$ at each finite $v$. At each real $v$, the real section theorem gives a component whose points have section $s \otimes K_v$. These are unique, by the previous paragraph, which defines $x(s)$. In Stix's terms, $s$ is a Selmer section and $x(s)$ is its adelic point [@stix2015birational, §2.2.1, p. 3].

To see that $x(s)$ survives every torsor, choose a point in the given component at each real place and any point at each complex place. This gives an ordinary adelic point $(P_v)_v$ with $s \otimes K_v = s_{P_v}$ at every place. Implication (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) of Harari and Stix's theorem, with $S$ empty and $\bar U$ trivial, shows that for every finite $K$-group scheme $G$ and every $\alpha \in H^1(C, G)$, the family $(\alpha(P_v))_v$ lies in the diagonal image of $H^1(K, G)$ [@hararistix2012, Theorem 2.1, p. 11]. That is the condition in Stoll's description of survival, as Harari and Stix note [@hararistix2012, Remark (1), p. 12], so $x(s) \in C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}}$.

Conversely, let $x \in C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}}$, and choose an ordinary representative $(P_v)_v$ as before. It satisfies condition (i), so implication (i) $\Rightarrow$ (iii) gives a section $s$ with $s \otimes K_v = s_{P_v}$ at every place [@hararistix2012, Theorem 2.1, p. 11]. By uniqueness, $x(s) = x$. Stix states the same surjectivity onto the finite descent locus, citing Harari and Stix [@stix2015birational, §2.2.1, p. 4].

*Part 2, from right to left.* Assume $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}} = C(K)$, and let $s \in \mathrm{Sec}(C/K)$. By part 1, $x(s)$ is the image of some $a \in C(K)$. In particular the component of $x(s)$ at every finite place is $a$, so the support of $s$, the Zariski closure of these components, is the single point $a$ and is finite over $K$. Since $C$ is proper, $s$ is not cuspidal, so by Stix's Corollary 5 it is the section of a rational point [@stix2015birational, Corollary 5, p. 5]. (The convention that only finite places enter the support, and why Stix's proof works with it, are explained in [birational sections with local conditions](birational-sections-with-local-conditions.md).) So $\kappa_C$ is surjective. It is injective by [injectivity](section-conjecture-injectivity.md).

*Part 2, from left to right.* Assume that $\kappa_C$ is surjective; (LSC$_C$) is not used. Let $x \in C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}}$ with ordinary representative $(P_v)_v$. As in part 1, Harari and Stix's theorem gives a section $s$ with $s \otimes K_v = s_{P_v}$ at every place. By surjectivity, $s = s_a$ for some $a \in C(K)$. At each place, $s_a \otimes K_v$ is the section of $a$ viewed as a point of $C(K_v)$. So $P_v = a$ at every finite place, and $P_v$ lies in the component of $a$ at every real place, by the uniqueness of local data. Hence $x$ is the image of $a$. Together with $C(K) \subseteq C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}}$, this gives equality.

*Part 3.* Each cited result asserts that $C$ is excellent with respect to abelian coverings, that is, $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}ab}} = C(K)$. The inclusions in the setting then give $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}cov}} = C(K)$. Two points about the sources:

- Stoll's proofs of (a), (c) and (d) go through his Proposition 8.5. In its proof, membership of a fibre can fail at the real places, but his Theorem 8.2 needs it only at a set of places of density $1$ [@stoll2017descenterrata, §3, p. 2]. Section 2 of the errata corrects an argument about components of torsors and weakens some statements about abelian coverings. It notes that the equalities for abelian varieties on p. 373 survive, because $A(K) \neq \emptyset$, and that the statements on p. 374 remain valid, together with the statements about $C(\mathbb{A}_K)_\bullet^{\mathrm{f\text{-}ab}}$ for curves that rely on them [@stoll2017descenterrata, §2, items (5) and (6), p. 2]. The proofs used here were traced back, through Theorem 8.2, Corollary 6.6 and Theorem 6.4, to Corollary 6.2 and those pages, and to Proposition 5.9. The errata says that Proposition 5.9 depends only on basic properties [@stoll2017descenterrata, §2, proof of Lemma 5.5′, p. 2].
- For (c), Stoll uses Mazur's theorem that $J_0(p)$ has a nontrivial factor of analytic rank zero when $p = 11$ or $p \ge 17$ is prime. He then uses the nonconstant maps $X_1(N) \to X_0(N) \to X_0(M)$ for $M \mid N$, and William Stein's tables for the remaining minimal levels [@stoll2007descent, proof of Corollary 8.8, p. 385]. Mazur's paper and Stein's tables were not consulted here.

$\square$

**The manuscript's §8.** This entry checks the global part of the manuscript, which was left unchecked in [the note on it](manuscript-claiming-p-adic-section-conjecture.md). Its Proposition 8.1 is part 1 [@openai2026padicsections, Proposition 8.1, p. 19]. Its Theorem 8.2 is part 2 from right to left [@openai2026padicsections, Theorem 8.2, p. 20]. Its Corollary 8.3 is cases (a) and (b) [@openai2026padicsections, Corollary 8.3, p. 20], and its Corollary 8.5 is case (c) [@openai2026padicsections, Corollary 8.5, p. 21]. Each step was compared with the cited source, and no gap was found. The manuscript cites Harari and Stix's author preprint of 11 February 2011 as Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.2 (1). That version was not opened here. In arXiv:1005.1302v2, used here, the corresponding statements are Theorem 2.1 and Remark (1), pp. 11–12.

Two parts were not checked. Corollary 8.4 treats a nonconstant map to an elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ whose $p^\infty$-Selmer group has corank $0$ for some prime $p$ [@openai2026padicsections, Corollary 8.4, p. 21]. Its proof needs the finiteness of $Ш(\mathbb{Q}, E)$, which it takes from a companion manuscript on the Selmer converse that was not opened here. The further restrictions on localization in §8.3 rest on results of Harari–Stix and Betts–Stix that were not compared with their sources.
