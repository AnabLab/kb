**Setting.** Fix a prime $p$ and a finite field $\mathbb F$ of characteristic $p$. For a finite simple graph $\Gamma=(V,E)$ use the notation of [induced subgraphs decide the property](small-induced-subgraphs-decide-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md): the right-angled Artin pro-$p$ group $G=G_\Gamma$, the groups $G_S$ and the maps $\sigma_S:G_S\to G$ and $\tau_S:G\to G_S$ for $S\subseteq V$, the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$, the graded algebra $A(H)=\bigoplus_kI_H^k/I_H^{k+1}$ of the abstract group algebra $\mathbb F_p[H]$, the initial forms $\mathrm{in}_k$, Jennings' theorem, and the surjection $\mu:T\to A(G)$ from the free algebra $T=\mathbb F_p\langle X_v:v\in V\rangle$ with $\mu(X_v)=\mathrm{in}_1(v)$. Write $T_k$ for the span of the words of length $k$. Two facts from that entry are used:
- the commutator formula from the proof of its Lemma 2(b): for a pro-$p$ group $H$, $x\in H_{(i)}$ and $y\in H_{(j)}$, $[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ lies in $H_{(i+j)}$ and $\mathrm{in}_{i+j}([x,y])=\mathrm{in}_i(x)\mathrm{in}_j(y)-\mathrm{in}_j(y)\mathrm{in}_i(x)$; the proof there uses nothing about $H$;
- its Lemma 4(b): $\mu(X_u)$ and $\mu(X_v)$ commute for adjacent $u,v$.

Let $J\subseteq T$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the $X_uX_v-X_vX_u$ with $u,v$ adjacent. Lemma 4(b) gives $J\subseteq\ker\mu$. As in the Setting of [the kernel $6$-unipotent property](kernel-6-unipotent-property-for-right-angled-artin-pro-p-groups.md), two words are *equivalent* if one is obtained from the other by repeatedly exchanging two consecutive letters that are distinct adjacent vertices, the equivalence classes are *classes*, $c_t(Q)=\sum_{w\in t}\langle Q,X_w\rangle$ for a class $t$ and $Q\in T$, where $\langle Q,X_w\rangle$ is the coefficient of $X_w$, and the *pair invariant* says that for distinct non-adjacent vertices $a,b$ all words of a class have the same subsequence of letters equal to $a$ or $b$. For a class $t$ and a vertex $v$, $vt$ denotes the class of $vw$ for $w\in t$, which does not depend on $w$. Let $\mathrm{alph}(t)$ be the set of vertices occurring in the words of $t$, and $F(t)$ the set of vertices $v$ such that some word of $t$ ends with $v$. The *complement* of $\Gamma$ is the graph on $V$ in which two distinct vertices are joined exactly when they are not adjacent in $\Gamma$.

From [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md), for a finitely generated pro-$p$ group $H$, take the corner functionals $\kappa_\rho$ and formula (1), $\rho(h)=1+\kappa_\rho(\mathrm{in}_k(h))E_{1,k+1}$ for continuous $\rho:H\to U_{k+1}(\mathbb F)$ and $h\in H_{(k)}$; the invisible part $\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)\subseteq A_k(H)^{\mathbb F}$, the common kernel of these $\kappa_\rho$; and detection: $H$ *detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F$* if every $h\in H_{(m)}\setminus H_{(m+1)}$ has $\rho(h)\ne1$ for some continuous $\rho:H\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$. By formula (1), an element $h\in H_{(m)}$ is killed by every continuous $\rho:H\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ exactly when $\mathrm{in}_m(h)\in\mathrm{Inv}_m(H,\mathbb F)$. Two lemmas from that entry are used: its Lemma 1, that detection of every degree $m\le n-1$ gives the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$, and that the kernel $(m+1)$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ gives detection of degree $m$; and its Lemma 2, that $\bigoplus_k\mathrm{Inv}_k(H,\mathbb F)$ is a two-sided ideal of $A(H)^{\mathbb F}$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $\Gamma$ be a finite simple graph and $\mathbb F$ a finite field of characteristic $p$.

1. *Failures persist.* If $G_\Gamma$ does not detect degree $m$ over $\mathbb F$, it does not detect degree $m+1$. Hence, for $n\ge2$, $G_\Gamma$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ exactly when it detects degree $n-1$, and if it fails the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$, it fails the kernel $(n+1)$-unipotent property. So if $\Gamma$ is complete multipartite, $G_\Gamma$ has the property over $\mathbb F$ for every $n$, and otherwise there is $n_\Gamma(\mathbb F)$ such that $G_\Gamma$ has the property over $\mathbb F$ exactly for $n<n_\Gamma(\mathbb F)$. In the second case, for $\mathbb F=\mathbb F_q$, $7\le n_\Gamma(\mathbb F_q)\le n_0(q)$, with $n_0(q)$ as in [no fixed finite field detects every Zassenhaus degree](no-fixed-finite-field-detects-every-zassenhaus-degree-of-zp2-free-product-zp.md).
2. *Witnesses.* Suppose that the complement of $\Gamma$ is connected and $|V|\ge2$. Let $m\ge1$ and let $h\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$ be killed by every continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$. Then some vertex $v$ has $[h,v]\in G_{(m+1)}\setminus G_{(m+2)}$, and $[h,v]$ is killed by every continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+2}(\mathbb F)$.
3. *Spider complements.* Let $s\ge0$ and $t\ge2$ with $s+t\ge3$, and let $\Gamma_{s,t}$, $a$, $d=1+s+2t$ and $g_{s,t}$ be as in [spider complements fail the property](spider-complements-fail-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md). Let $z$ be a vertex other than $a$, and put $h_0=g_{s,t}$ and $h_{r+1}=[h_r,z]$. Then for every $r\ge0$, $h_r\in G_{(d+r)}\setminus G_{(d+r+1)}$, and every continuous $\rho:G_{\Gamma_{s,t}}\to U_{d+1+r}(\mathbb F)$ has $\rho(h_r)=1$. So $G_{\Gamma_{s,t}}$, and $G_{\Gamma'}$ for every finite graph $\Gamma'$ with an induced subgraph isomorphic to $\Gamma_{s,t}$, fail the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ for every $n\ge d+1$.

**Corollaries.** These are proved here.

- (a) *The six-vertex graph.* For every prime $p$ and every finite field $\mathbb F$ of characteristic $p$, $G_{\Gamma_{1,2}}$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ exactly for $n\le6$, and so does $G_{\Gamma'}$ for every finite graph $\Gamma'$ with an induced subgraph isomorphic to $\Gamma_{1,2}$. In size $7+r$ a witness is $[\cdots[g_{1,2},y_1],\ldots,y_1]$ with $r$ letters $y_1$. So $n_{\Gamma_{1,2}}(\mathbb F)=7$ for every $\mathbb F$, and a single graph on six vertices fails the property in every size $n\ge7$ over every finite field of characteristic $p$. In particular $n_{\min}(p)=7$, in the notation of corollary (c) of [induced subgraphs decide the property](small-induced-subgraphs-decide-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md). This replaces the graphs $\Gamma_{s,2}$ with large $s$ in corollary (c) of [the spider-complement theorem](spider-complements-fail-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md).
- (b) *$\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$.* For every power $q$ of $p$ there is $n_1(q)$ such that $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_q$ exactly for $n<n_1(q)$, and detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F_q$ exactly for $m\le n_1(q)-2$. Moreover $2q+3\le n_1(q)\le n_0(q)$, $n_1(q)\ge2p+5$, and $n_1(q)\le n_1(q')$ when $\mathbb F_q\subseteq\mathbb F_{q'}$. For $q=p=2$ this gives $9\le n_1(2)\le18$.
- (c) *Galois groups.* Let $K$ be a field containing a primitive $p$th root of unity with $G_K(p)\cong G_\Gamma$ for a finite graph $\Gamma$. Then the equality of the [kernel-unipotent conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md) holds for $G_K(p)$ exactly for $n<n_\Gamma(\mathbb F_p)$, where $n_\Gamma(\mathbb F_p)=\infty$ if $\Gamma$ is complete multipartite.

**Lemma A (the graded algebra).** For $k\ge0$ and $P\in T_k$, the following are equivalent: $P\in J$; $c_t(P)=0$ for every class $t$; $\mu(P)=0$. So $\ker\mu=J$, and $\mu$ induces an isomorphism of graded algebras $T/J\to A(G_\Gamma)$.

*Proof.* As $J$ is generated by homogeneous elements of degree $2$, $J\cap T_k$ is spanned by the elements $X_w(X_uX_v-X_vX_u)X_{w'}$ with $u,v$ adjacent and $w,w'$ words of total length $k-2$. The words $wuvw'$ and $wvuw'$ are equivalent, so every $c_t$ vanishes on $J\cap T_k$. Conversely, suppose that $c_t(P)=0$ for every class $t$, and choose a word $w_t$ in each class. For $w\in t$, $X_w-X_{w_t}\in J$, since each exchange changes $X_w$ by an element of $J$. So $P\equiv\sum_tc_t(P)X_{w_t}=0$ modulo $J$. This proves the first equivalence, and $J\subseteq\ker\mu$ was noted in the Setting.

Now let $P\in T_k$ with $\mu(P)=0$; we show $P\in J$. The case $k=0$ is clear, as $\mu$ is the identity on $T_0=\mathbb F_p=A_0(G)$, so let $k\ge1$. We use the finite target of the proof of part 1 of [the spider-complement theorem](spider-complements-fail-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md), whose construction uses nothing about the graph. Let $T_{\ge k+1}$ be the span of the words of length at least $k+1$ and $\bar A=T/(J+T_{\ge k+1})$. It is graded, with $\bar A_j=T_j/(J\cap T_j)$ for $j\le k$. As $(\bar A_{\ge1})^{k+1}=0$, $U=1+\bar A_{\ge1}$ is a finite $p$-group under multiplication. For adjacent $u,v$ the images $\bar X_u,\bar X_v$ commute, so there is a continuous homomorphism $\bar\rho:G\to U$ with $\bar\rho(v)=1+\bar X_v$. Its linear extension $\tilde{\bar\rho}:\mathbb F_p[G]\to\bar A$ is an algebra homomorphism, and it maps each $x-1$ with $x\in G$ into $\bar A_{\ge1}$. These elements span $I_G$, so $\tilde{\bar\rho}(I_G^{k+1})\subseteq\bar A_{\ge k+1}=0$. Let $P(v-1)\in I_G^k$ be the result of substituting $v-1$ for each $X_v$ in $P$. Since $\mu(X_v)=(v-1)+I_G^2$ and the product of $A(G)$ comes from that of $\mathbb F_p[G]$, $\mu(P)=P(v-1)+I_G^{k+1}$. So $\mu(P)=0$ gives $P(v-1)\in I_G^{k+1}$, and applying $\tilde{\bar\rho}$ gives $0=P(\bar X_v)$, which is the image of $P$ in $\bar A_k=T_k/(J\cap T_k)$. Hence $P\in J$.

Finally $\mu$ maps $T_k$ into $A_k(G)$, so $\ker\mu$ is the sum of the $\ker\mu\cap T_k=J\cap T_k$, which is $J$. As $\mu$ is onto, it induces the stated isomorphism. $\square$

**Lemma B (no central elements).** Suppose that the complement of $\Gamma$ is connected and $|V|\ge2$. Let $k\ge1$ and $P\in T_k$ with $P\notin J$. Then $PX_v-X_vP\notin J$ for some $v\in V$.

*Proof.* (i) *Cancellation.* If $vw$ and $vw'$ are equivalent words, so are $w$ and $w'$. Indeed, take a sequence of exchanges from $vw$ to $vw'$ and follow the first letter of $vw$, an occurrence of $v$. Exchanges involve two distinct letters, so they never change the relative order of the occurrences of $v$, and the followed occurrence stays the first occurrence of $v$ in every word of the sequence. In $vw'$ it is therefore the first letter. Deleting it from every word of the sequence gives a sequence from $w$ to $w'$ in which consecutive words are equal, if the exchange moved the followed occurrence, or differ by the same exchange, whose two letters stay consecutive after the deletion.

(ii) *Final letters.* Let $t$ be a class and $v$ a vertex such that $vt$ contains a word ending with $v$. Then either $v\in F(t)$, or $v\notin\mathrm{alph}(t)$ and every vertex of $\mathrm{alph}(t)$ is adjacent to $v$. Indeed, let $w\in t$ and $vw$ be equivalent to $w''v$. If $v\in\mathrm{alph}(t)$, follow the first occurrence of $v$ through a sequence of exchanges from $vw$ to $w''v$ and delete it, as in (i). This gives an equivalence between $w$ and the word obtained from $w''v$ by deleting its first occurrence of $v$. That occurrence is not the last letter, since $w''v$, like $vw$, contains $v$ at least twice. So $w$ is equivalent to a word ending with $v$, and $v\in F(t)$. If $v\notin\mathrm{alph}(t)$, let $b\in\mathrm{alph}(t)$, so $b\ne v$. The subsequence of letters in $\{b,v\}$ is $v$ followed by a nonempty run of $b$ in $vw$, but ends with its only $v$ in $w''v$. By the pair invariant, $b$ and $v$ are adjacent.

(iii) *$F(t)$ is a clique.* Two distinct vertices $u,v\in F(t)$ are adjacent. Otherwise, by the pair invariant, all words of $t$ have the same subsequence of letters in $\{u,v\}$, but this subsequence ends with $u$ in a word ending with $u$, and with $v$ in a word ending with $v$.

(iv) Suppose, for a contradiction, that $PX_v-X_vP\in J$ for every $v$. By Lemma A there is a class $t$ with $c_t(P)\ne0$. Fix a vertex $v$. By (i), the words $w$ with $vw\in vt$ are exactly the words of $t$, so $c_{vt}(X_vP)=c_t(P)\ne0$. By Lemma A, $c_{vt}(PX_v)=c_{vt}(X_vP)\ne0$. Every word occurring in $PX_v$ ends with $v$, so $vt$ contains a word ending with $v$. By (ii), every $v\notin F(t)$ lies outside $\mathrm{alph}(t)$ and is adjacent to every vertex of $\mathrm{alph}(t)$. As $F(t)\subseteq\mathrm{alph}(t)$, this gives $\mathrm{alph}(t)=F(t)$, and no edge of the complement joins $\mathrm{alph}(t)$ to its complement in $V$. The set $\mathrm{alph}(t)$ is nonempty, as $k\ge1$, and the complement of $\Gamma$ is connected, so $\mathrm{alph}(t)=V$. Then $F(t)=V$, which is a clique of $\Gamma$ by (iii). So the complement of $\Gamma$ has no edges, and it is disconnected because $|V|\ge2$. This is a contradiction. $\square$

**Lemma C (components of the complement).** Let $S_1,\ldots,S_l$ be the vertex sets of the connected components of the complement of $\Gamma$, and $m\ge1$. Then $G_\Gamma$ detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F$ if and only if every $G_{S_i}$ does.

*Proof.* *Only if.* Let $h\in(G_{S_i})_{(m)}\setminus(G_{S_i})_{(m+1)}$. By item 1 of [the filtration entry](p-zassenhaus-filtration.md), $\sigma_{S_i}(h)\in G_{(m)}$, and $\sigma_{S_i}(h)\notin G_{(m+1)}$, since otherwise $h=\tau_{S_i}(\sigma_{S_i}(h))\in(G_{S_i})_{(m+1)}$. If $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ has $\rho(\sigma_{S_i}(h))\ne1$, then $\rho\sigma_{S_i}$ does not kill $h$.

*If.* For $i\ne j$, every vertex of $S_i$ is adjacent in $\Gamma$ to every vertex of $S_j$, since no edge of the complement joins two components. Centralizers in $G$ are closed subgroups. For $w\in S_j$, the preimage under $\sigma_{S_i}$ of the centralizer of $w$ is a closed subgroup of $G_{S_i}$ containing the topological generators $S_i$, so it is $G_{S_i}$. So every element of $\sigma_{S_i}(G_{S_i})$ commutes with $S_j$, and the same argument with $\sigma_{S_j}$ shows that it commutes with $\sigma_{S_j}(G_{S_j})$. This is the argument of 2 implies 3 in [the classification](kernel-unipotent-property-for-right-angled-artin-pro-p-groups.md). Hence $\psi(x_1,\ldots,x_l)=\sigma_{S_1}(x_1)\cdots\sigma_{S_l}(x_l)$ is a continuous homomorphism $G_{S_1}\times\cdots\times G_{S_l}\to G$. The continuous endomorphism $g\mapsto\psi(\tau_{S_1}(g),\ldots,\tau_{S_l}(g))$ of $G$ fixes every vertex, as $\tau_{S_j}$ kills the vertices outside $S_j$, so it is the identity. Now let $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$. Each $\tau_{S_i}(g)$ lies in $(G_{S_i})_{(m)}$, by item 1 of the filtration entry. If each lay in $(G_{S_i})_{(m+1)}$, then $g=\prod_i\sigma_{S_i}(\tau_{S_i}(g))$ would lie in $G_{(m+1)}$, by the same item. So some $\tau_{S_i}(g)$ lies in $(G_{S_i})_{(m)}\setminus(G_{S_i})_{(m+1)}$. If $G_{S_i}$ detects degree $m$, some continuous $\rho:G_{S_i}\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ has $\rho(\tau_{S_i}(g))\ne1$, and then $\rho\tau_{S_i}$ does not kill $g$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** By Jennings' theorem $\mathrm{in}_m(h)\ne0$, and $\mathrm{in}_m(h)\in\mathrm{Inv}_m(G,\mathbb F)$ by formula (1), as $h$ is killed by every continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$. Since $\mu$ is onto, $\mathrm{in}_m(h)=\mu(P)$ for some $P\in T_m$, and $P\notin J$ because $J\subseteq\ker\mu$. By Lemma B some vertex $v$ has $PX_v-X_vP\notin J$, so $\mu(PX_v-X_vP)\ne0$ by Lemma A. By the commutator formula, $[h,v]\in G_{(m+1)}$ and
$$\mathrm{in}_{m+1}([h,v])=\mathrm{in}_m(h)\,\mathrm{in}_1(v)-\mathrm{in}_1(v)\,\mathrm{in}_m(h)=\mu(PX_v-X_vP)\ne0.$$
Jennings' theorem puts $x-1$ in $I_G^{m+2}$ for every $x\in G_{(m+2)}$, so $[h,v]\notin G_{(m+2)}$. The displayed element lies in $\mathrm{Inv}_{m+1}(G,\mathbb F)$, by Lemma 2 of the invisible-ideal theorem, since $\mathrm{in}_m(h)$ does. So formula (1) shows that every continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+2}(\mathbb F)$ kills $[h,v]$. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** Suppose that $G=G_\Gamma$ does not detect degree $m$ over $\mathbb F$. By Lemma C, some $G_{S_i}$ does not detect degree $m$. Here $|S_i|\ge2$: a one-vertex graph is complete multipartite, so $G_{S_i}\cong\mathbb Z_p$ would have the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_p$ for every $n$, by item 2 implies item 4 of the theorem of [the classification](kernel-unipotent-property-for-right-angled-artin-pro-p-groups.md), hence over $\mathbb F$, and so would detect every degree by Lemma 1 of the invisible-ideal theorem. The graph $\Gamma[S_i]$ has as its complement the component on $S_i$, which is connected. So there is $h\in(G_{S_i})_{(m)}\setminus(G_{S_i})_{(m+1)}$ killed by every continuous $\rho:G_{S_i}\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$, and part 2, applied to $\Gamma[S_i]$, shows that $G_{S_i}$ does not detect degree $m+1$. By Lemma C, neither does $G$.

Let $n\ge2$. If $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$, it detects degree $n-1$, by Lemma 1 of the invisible-ideal theorem. Conversely, if $G$ detects degree $n-1$, then by the first assertion it detects every degree $m\le n-1$, for a degree $m<n-1$ that is not detected would make each of $m+1,\ldots,n-1$ undetected. So $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$ by the same lemma. Now if $G$ fails the kernel $n$-unipotent property, then $n\ge2$, as $G_{(1)}=G$, so $G$ does not detect degree $n-1$, hence not degree $n$, and it fails the kernel $(n+1)$-unipotent property.

So the set of $n$ for which $G$ has the property over $\mathbb F$ is either every $n\ge1$ or the set of $n$ below its least element of failure. By Corollary 2(ii) of [no fixed finite field detects every Zassenhaus degree](no-fixed-finite-field-detects-every-zassenhaus-degree-of-zp2-free-product-zp.md), the first happens if $\Gamma$ is complete multipartite, and otherwise $G$ fails the property over $\mathbb F_q$ for every $n\ge n_0(q)$. Every finite graph gives the property for $n\le6$, by part 1 of [the kernel $6$-unipotent theorem](kernel-6-unipotent-property-for-right-angled-artin-pro-p-groups.md). This gives $7\le n_\Gamma(\mathbb F_q)\le n_0(q)$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Write $G=G_{\Gamma_{s,t}}$, and use the notation of [the spider-complement theorem](spider-complements-fail-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md): the legs, the word $w^*=a\,y_1\cdots y_s\,u_1v_1\cdots u_tv_t$, and $L_{s,t}\in T_d$. By its part 1, $g_{s,t}\in G_{(d)}$ and $\mathrm{in}_d(g_{s,t})=\mu(L_{s,t})$. Put $L^{(0)}=L_{s,t}$ and $L^{(r+1)}=L^{(r)}X_z-X_zL^{(r)}$. As $z\in G_{(1)}$ and $\mathrm{in}_1(z)=\mu(X_z)$, the commutator formula and induction on $r$ give $h_r\in G_{(d+r)}$ and $\mathrm{in}_{d+r}(h_r)=\mu(L^{(r)})$.

*Invisibility.* By part 2 of the spider-complement theorem, every continuous $\rho:G\to U_{d+1}(\mathbb F)$ kills $h_0$, so $\mu(L^{(0)})\in\mathrm{Inv}_d(G,\mathbb F)$ by formula (1). By Lemma 2 of the invisible-ideal theorem and induction, $\mu(L^{(r)})=\mu(L^{(r-1)})\mu(X_z)-\mu(X_z)\mu(L^{(r-1)})$ lies in $\mathrm{Inv}_{d+r}(G,\mathbb F)$. By formula (1) again, every continuous $\rho:G\to U_{d+1+r}(\mathbb F)$ kills $h_r$.

*Nonvanishing.* By Jennings' theorem it remains to show $\mu(L^{(r)})\ne0$, and by Lemma A it suffices to find a class $t_r$ with $c_{t_r}(L^{(r)})\ne0$. Let $t_r$ be the class of $w^*z^r$, the word $w^*$ followed by $r$ letters $z$. Every word of $t_r$ begins with $a$. Indeed, $a$ occurs once in $w^*z^r$, as $z\ne a$. Each $y_i$ and each $u_j$ is joined to $a$ in the spider, so it is not adjacent to $a$ in $\Gamma_{s,t}$, and the subsequence of $w^*z^r$ of letters in $\{a,y_i\}$, or in $\{a,u_j\}$, begins with $a$. The subsequence of letters in $\{u_j,v_j\}$ begins with $u_j$. By the pair invariant, no word of $t_r$ begins with a $y_i$, a $u_j$ or a $v_j$.

By induction on $r$, $L^{(r)}-L_{s,t}X_z^r$ is a combination of words beginning with $z$, which contribute nothing to $c_{t_r}$. In the expansion (2) of $L_{s,t}$ in the spider-complement theorem, every word of a term with $E\ne\emptyset$ begins with a letter of a leg, not with $a$, and this remains so after multiplying on the right by $X_z^r$. The remaining term is $X_aX_{y_1}\cdots X_{y_s}B_1\cdots B_tX_z^r$, with $B_j=X_{u_j}X_{v_j}-X_{v_j}X_{u_j}$. It is the sum of the $2^t$ distinct words $a\,y_1\cdots y_s\,\varepsilon_1\cdots\varepsilon_t\,z^r$ with $\varepsilon_j\in\{u_jv_j,v_ju_j\}$, each with coefficient $\pm1$, and the word with every $\varepsilon_j=u_jv_j$ is $w^*z^r$, with coefficient $1$. If $\varepsilon_j=v_ju_j$ for some $j$, the subsequence of letters in $\{u_j,v_j\}$ begins with $v_j$, so the word is not in $t_r$. Hence $c_{t_r}(L^{(r)})=1$.

So $h_r\in G_{(d+r)}\setminus G_{(d+r+1)}$ is killed by every continuous homomorphism to $U_{d+1+r}(\mathbb F)$, and $G$ fails the kernel $(d+1+r)$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F$. For $\Gamma'$, the only-if direction of part 2 of [induced subgraphs decide the property](small-induced-subgraphs-decide-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md) applies, as its proof works for any induced subgraph. $\square$

**Proof of the corollaries.** (a) Part 1 of the kernel $6$-unipotent theorem gives the property for $n\le6$ for every finite graph, and part 3 with $(s,t)=(1,2)$, $d=6$ and $z=y_1$ gives the failure and the witnesses for $n\ge7$. Then $n_{\min}(p)\le7$, and $n_{\min}(p)\ge7$ by the same part 1.

(b) $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p=\langle x,y,z\mid[x,y]=1\rangle$ is $G_\Delta$ for the graph $\Delta$ with one edge $\{x,y\}$ and the vertex $z$. Its complement is the path $x,z,y$, which is connected, and $\Delta$ is not complete multipartite. Part 1 gives $n_1(q)=n_\Delta(\mathbb F_q)$, and the characterization by detection. By Corollary 1 of the fixed-field theorem, the property holds over $\mathbb F_q$ for $n\le2q+2$ and fails for $n\ge n_0(q)$. By the theorem of [the prime-field bound](zp2-free-product-zp-has-the-kernel-unipotent-property-up-to-size-2p-plus-4.md), it holds over every finite field of characteristic $p$ for $n\le2p+4$. If $\mathbb F_q\subseteq\mathbb F_{q'}$, then $U_n(\mathbb F_q)\subseteq U_n(\mathbb F_{q'})$, so the property over $\mathbb F_q$ implies the property over $\mathbb F_{q'}$. For $p=2$, $n_0(2)=18$.

(c) Over $\mathbb F_p$ the property over $\mathbb F$ is the equality of the conjecture, so this is part 1 with $\mathbb F=\mathbb F_p$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Beyond right-angled Artin groups.* The proof of part 2 uses only that $[\mathrm{in}_m(h),\mathrm{in}_1(v)]\ne0$. So for any finitely generated pro-$p$ group $H$, if $h\in H_{(m)}\setminus H_{(m+1)}$ is killed by every continuous $\rho:H\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F)$ and $g\in H$ has $[\mathrm{in}_m(h),\mathrm{in}_1(g)]\ne0$ in $A(H)$, then $[h,g]\in H_{(m+1)}\setminus H_{(m+2)}$ is killed by every continuous $\rho:H\to U_{m+2}(\mathbb F)$. Lemma B supplies $g$ when $H$ is a right-angled Artin pro-$p$ group whose graph has connected complement.
- *Why the components are needed.* If the complement of $\Gamma$ is disconnected, $A(G_\Gamma)$ can have central elements of positive degree; for instance $\mu(X_v)$ is central when $v$ is adjacent to every other vertex. Lemma C avoids this by passing to the components, where Lemma B applies.
- *Dependence on the field.* The threshold $n_\Gamma(\mathbb F)$ does not decrease when $\mathbb F$ grows. It equals $7$ for every $\mathbb F$ when $\Gamma=\Gamma_{1,2}$, while for $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$ it is at least $2q+3$ over $\mathbb F_q$, so it is unbounded as $q$ grows.
- *Open.* The value of $n_1(q)$ for $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$, between the bounds of corollary (b), is not decided here. For a graph that is not complete multipartite, $n_\Gamma(\mathbb F)$ is known only when it has an induced subgraph isomorphic to $\Gamma_{1,2}$, where it is $7$; an induced $\Gamma_{s,t}$ gives only the upper bound $2+s+2t$.
