**Fields.** Let $K$ be a field. It is *AVKF* (abelian variety Kummer-faithful) if, for every finite extension $L/K$ and abelian variety $A/L$, the group $A(L)$ has no nonzero divisible elements [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 6.1 (iii)]. Let $K_{\mathrm{prm}}$ be its prime field and
$$K_{\mathrm{div}} = \bigcup_{L/K \text{ finite}} K_{\mathrm{prm}}\bigl(L^{\times\infty}\bigr), \qquad L^{\times\infty} = \bigcap_{m \ge 1} (L^\times)^m.$$
Then $K$ is *TKND* (torally Kummer-nondegenerate) if an algebraic closure of $K$ is an infinite extension of $K_{\mathrm{div}}$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 6.6]. Both properties pass from an algebraic extension to $K$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remarks 6.1.2 and 6.6.1]. In characteristic $0$, Kummer-faithful fields, as in [Mochizuki's reconstruction](semi-absolute-reconstruction-over-kummer-faithful-fields.md), are exactly the fields that are torally Kummer-faithful and AVKF, and they are TKND-AVKF [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remarks 6.1.3 and 6.6.2].

*Examples.*

- Sub-$p$-adic subfields of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, in particular number fields, are Kummer-faithful, hence TKND-AVKF [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Introduction, p. 11].
- Let $F$ be a number field. Call a profinite group *$\mathfrak{Primes}$-sparse* if, for every prime $\ell$, the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of each of its open subgroups is finite [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Definition 6.1 (ii)]. Every Galois extension $L$ of $F \cdot \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ with $\mathrm{Gal}(L/F\cdot\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}})$ $\mathfrak{Primes}$-sparse is TKND-AVKF [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Proposition 6.3 (i) and Remark 6.6.3]. This includes $\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and its finite extensions. Following Tamagawa, a countable product of copies of a fixed alternating group $A_n$ with $n \ge 5$ is $\mathfrak{Primes}$-sparse and is the Galois group of some extension of $F$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 6.3.1].
- These fields $L$ contain all roots of unity, have infinite ramification index at every nonarchimedean prime, and have algebraically closed residue fields. So they are not generalized sub-$p$-adic for any $p$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remarks 6.3.4 and 6.6.4].
- At the other extreme with respect to roots of unity, Proposition 6.3 (ii) gives Kummer-faithful fields [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Proposition 6.3 (ii) and Remark 6.6.4]. Among them are abelian extensions of number fields containing $\sqrt{-1}$ with infinite ramification index at every nonarchimedean prime, which are not generalized sub-$p$-adic for any $p$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 6.3.3].

**Theorem G** [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Theorem 6.10]. Let $m, n \ge 1$, let $K, L \subseteq \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ be TKND-AVKF, and let $X_K$ and $Y_L$ be hyperbolic curves over $K$ and $L$, of types $(g_X, r_X)$ and $(g_Y, r_Y)$, meaning genus and number of cusps. Let $(X_K)_m$ and $(Y_L)_n$ be their $m$-th and $n$-th configuration spaces, and let $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi_{(X_K)_m}/G_K, \Pi_{(Y_L)_n}/G_L)$ be the set of outer isomorphisms $\Pi_{(X_K)_m} \to \Pi_{(Y_L)_n}$ of étale fundamental groups that induce outer isomorphisms $G_K \to G_L$.

1. Suppose that $m \ge 4$ or $n \ge 4$ when $r_X = 0$ or $r_Y = 0$, and that $m \ge 3$ or $n \ge 3$ when $r_X \ne 0$ or $r_Y \ne 0$. Then the outer isomorphism $G_K \to G_L$ induced by any element of $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi_{(X_K)_m}/G_K, \Pi_{(Y_L)_n}/G_L)$ comes from a field isomorphism $K \to L$.
2. Suppose that $m \ge 2$ or $n \ge 2$, and that $g_X = 0$ or $g_Y = 0$. Then the natural map
$$\mathrm{Isom}\bigl((X_K)_m, (Y_L)_n\bigr) \to \mathrm{Out}\bigl(\Pi_{(X_K)_m}/G_K, \Pi_{(Y_L)_n}/G_L\bigr)$$
from isomorphisms of schemes is bijective.

If the set of outer isomorphisms is nonempty, then $m = n \ge 2$, $g_X = g_Y$ and $r_X = r_Y$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, proof of Theorem 6.10, p. 118]. The case of $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ over $K$ and over $L$, for every $n \ge 2$, is Corollary 6.9 [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Corollary 6.9, pp. 114–115].

**Proof outline.** For Corollary 6.9, an outer isomorphism over $G_K \to G_L$ conjugates $G_K$ to $G_L$ inside $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}}_n)$. The group-theoretic construction of the fields $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{G_K}$ and $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{G_L}$ from the [combinatorial construction of $G_{\mathbb{Q}}$](combinatorial-construction-of-the-absolute-galois-group-of-q.md) then gives an automorphism of $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ carrying $K$ to $L$ and inducing $G_K \to G_L$. For $K = L$, Theorem F there gives $N_{GT}(G_K) \subseteq C_{GT}(G_K) \subseteq G_{\mathbb{Q}}$, so $N_{GT}(G_K)/G_K = N_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(G_K)/G_K \cong \mathrm{Aut}(K)$. With the decomposition $\mathrm{Out}(\Pi^{\mathrm{tpd}}_n) = GT \times \mathfrak{S}_{n+3}$ of Hoshi, Minamide and Mochizuki, this yields surjectivity. Injectivity comes from the fiber subgroups and cuspidal inertia subgroups [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, proof of Corollary 6.9, pp. 115–117]. Theorem 6.10 (i) reduces to Corollary 6.9 through tripods inside the configuration space groups, using results of Hoshi and Mochizuki on tripods [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, proof of Theorem 6.10, pp. 119–120]. For (ii), the ISC-property of AVKF fields gives an isomorphism of the curves [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Proposition 6.4 and Claim 6.10.A]. A faithfulness result for outer actions on configuration space groups then shows that the remaining automorphism is inner [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Theorem 3.6 and proof of Theorem 6.10, pp. 121–122].

**Absolute version.** If $G_K$ and $G_L$ are very elastic, a group-theoretic condition of Mochizuki, then every outer isomorphism $\Pi_{(X_K)_m} \to \Pi_{(Y_L)_n}$ already induces one $G_K \to G_L$, so Theorem G holds in absolute form [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 6.10.1]. This applies when $K$ and $L$ are abelian extensions of number fields of the kinds above, such as $F \cdot \mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}}$ [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 6.10.2].

**Independence from $p$-adic Hodge theory.** Over fields such as those of the second to fourth examples above, anabelian geometry cannot be treated by the known techniques that need $p$-adic Hodge theory or Frobenius elements of Galois groups of finite fields, such as those behind Tamagawa's theorem and Mochizuki's Hom theorem [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Remark 6.3.5]. Even for sub-$p$-adic $K$ and $L$, the proof of Theorem G uses only combinatorial anabelian geometry. It is independent of the Grothendieck conjecture for hyperbolic curves over sub-$p$-adic fields, that is, of [Mochizuki's Hom theorem](mochizuki-hom-theorem-for-hyperbolic-curves.md) and Tamagawa's theorem. For sub-$p$-adic local fields it uses no theory of weights, neither $p$-adic Hodge theory nor the Weil conjectures [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Introduction, pp. 11–12, and Remark 3.3.2].

**Earlier results.** Tsujimura proved a weaker version for $m = n = 1$ over the stably $p$-$\times\mu$-indivisible fields of characteristic $0$ in his sense, which need not be TKND-AVKF. Hoshi proved a version for $m = n = 1$ over generalized sub-$p$-adic fields. Both are as reported by the authors [@hoshimochizukitsujimura2025, Introduction, p. 12]; neither paper has been opened for this entry.
