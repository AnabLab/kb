**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md), with its lower central input and its multigrading (M1), (M2). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite of degree $d\ge3$, $G=G_K$, $P$ is wild inertia, $\Gamma=G/P$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$ with augmentation $\epsilon$, $P_j$ is the closed lower central series of $P$, $H=P/P_2$, $\mathcal L_j=P_j/P_{j+1}$, and $V=H/pH=L\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d$ with $L=\mathbb F_p\bar x_0$ and $V_k=\Lambda\bar x_k\cong\Lambda$, with coordinates $\pi_k$. Also $\omega$, $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, the surface pairs $(a_i,b_i)$ for $1\le i\le r$, $C_V$, $A=L\oplus C_V$, the element $\gamma\in\mathcal G$ with $\omega(\gamma)=1$, and $\lambda_0=\gamma-1$ are as there, and $e=[K(\mu_p):K]$ is the order of $\omega$. As $\omega$ takes values in $\mathbb F_p^\times$, $e\mid p-1$. The first pair $(a,b)=(a_1,b_1)$ is $(x_2,x_3)$ for odd $d$ and $(x_3,x_4)$ for even $d$, and $\bar a=\bar x_a$, $\bar b=\bar x_b$. The indices $1$ and, for even $d$, $2$ are *complementary*. An automorphism is *tame-trivial* if it induces the identity on $\Gamma$; it then acts $\Lambda$-linearly on $V$. Write $c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}$.

*Cohomology.* For a compact abelian pro-$p$ group $M$ with a continuous $\Gamma$-action, let $I_\Gamma M$, $M_\Gamma$ and $\Phi_M:H^1(\Gamma,M)\to M_\Gamma$, $[c]\mapsto c(\sigma)+I_\Gamma M$, be as in the Cohomology paragraph of [the theorem modulo the third lower central term](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-the-third-lower-central-term-of-wild-inertia.md). By Lemma 1 (a) there $\Phi_M$ is injective. By Lemma 1 (b) there, if $M$ is torsion-free, then $\Phi_M(v)\in pM_\Gamma$ implies $v\in pH^1(\Gamma,M)$, and $H^1(\Gamma,M)$ is torsion-free if also $(M/pM)^\gamma=0$. For $j\ge2$ this applies to $M=\mathcal L_j$, as $\mathcal L_j(V)^\gamma=0$ by Lemma 1 (c) of the higher Johnson theorem.

*The class.* Let $n\ge2$, and let $\mathcal A_n$ be the set of $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ that induce the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$. For $\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$ and $\rho\in\Gamma$ put $c_\alpha(\rho)=\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}P_{n+1}\in\mathcal L_n$, for $\tilde\rho\in G$ over $\rho$. For $x\in G$, $\alpha(\tilde\rho x)(\tilde\rho x)^{-1}=\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}\cdot\tilde\rho\,\alpha(x)x^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}$. For $x\in P$ the last factor lies in $P_{n+1}$, so $c_\alpha$ does not depend on $\tilde\rho$, and for general $x$ the identity shows that $c_\alpha$ is a cocycle for the conjugation action of $\Gamma$ on $\mathcal L_n$. It is continuous, taking $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$. Put $u(\alpha)=[c_\alpha]\in H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$. For $v\in H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$, its *reduction* $\bar v$ is the image of $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}(v)$ in $(\mathcal L_n/p)_\Gamma=\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma$. Let $E_n\in\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma$ be the class of $[\cdots[[\bar x_1,\bar x_0],\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]$, with $n-1$ copies of $\bar x_0$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$, $e=[K(\mu_p):K]\ge2$, and $n\ge2$ with $p\nmid n$ and $e\nmid n-1$. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many outer conjugates and induce the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$.

- (a) If $e\nmid n$, then $u(\alpha)=0$. Equivalently, $c_h\circ\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$ for some $h\in P_n$.
- (b) If $e\mid n$, then $\overline{u(\alpha)}\in\mathbb F_pE_n$. Moreover, there is one $w\in(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma$ with $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}(u(\alpha'))\in\mathbb Z_pw$ for every $\alpha'$ satisfying the hypotheses.

**Corollary (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$ and $e=[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$. Every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha_e$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_e$ that induces the identity on $G/P_e$ and on $P/P_{e+1}$. Two such $\alpha_e$ differ by $c_k$ with $k\in P_e$. So every outer class with finitely many conjugates lies in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_e)$. The classes $u(\alpha_e)$ of all such $\alpha_e$ satisfy $\Phi_{\mathcal L_e}(u(\alpha_e))\in\mathbb Z_pw$ for one $w\in(\mathcal L_e)_\Gamma$.

For example, if $p\ge5$ and $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is unramified of degree at least $5$, then $e=p-1$: the polynomial $\Phi_p(x+1)$ is Eisenstein at $p$, so $\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p)/\mathbb Q_p$ is Galois and totally ramified of degree $p-1$, and it meets the unramified field $K$ only in $\mathbb Q_p$. So every finite-conjugacy outer class of $G_K$ is trivial in $\operatorname{Out}(G_K/P_{p-1})$.

**Lemma 1 (Lie projection; proved here).** Let $n\ge1$. Let $\theta_n$ be the continuous linear map of $V^{\hat\otimes n}$ given level by level by $v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n\mapsto[\cdots[[v_1,v_2],v_3],\dots,v_n]$. Then $\theta_n(V^{\hat\otimes n})\subseteq\mathcal L_n(V)$, $\theta_n$ is multiplication by $n$ on $\mathcal L_n(V)$, and $\theta_n$ commutes with $\Gamma$. So if $p\nmid n$, then $\mathcal L_n(V)$ is a $\Gamma$-stable direct summand of $V^{\hat\otimes n}$ and $\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma\to(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma$ is injective.

*Proof.* The image of an elementary tensor is a bracket in $\mathcal L_n(V)$, which is closed, so $\theta_n$ maps into it. For a continuous linear $f:V\to V$, $f^{\otimes n}$ preserves products and brackets of tensors, so $f^{\otimes n}\theta_n=\theta_nf^{\otimes n}$; take $f=\rho$. By linearity and continuity, $\theta_{m+1}(uv)=[\theta_m(u),v]$ for $u\in V^{\hat\otimes m}$, $v\in V$, $m\ge1$. *Claim:* $\theta_{m+l}(uQ)=[\theta_m(u),Q]$ for $u\in V^{\hat\otimes m}$ and $Q\in\mathcal L_l(V)$, $m,l\ge1$. Both sides are continuous and bilinear, and $\mathcal L_l(V)$ is the closed span of $V$ for $l=1$ and of the $[Q',v]$ with $Q'\in\mathcal L_{l-1}(V)$, $v\in V$ for $l\ge2$ (as in the proof of (M2) of the higher Johnson theorem). For $Q=v$ this is the rule above. For $Q=[Q',v]$, by induction on $l$ and the Jacobi identity,
$$\theta(uQ'v)-\theta(uvQ')=[\theta(uQ'),v]-[\theta(uv),Q']=[[\theta(u),Q'],v]-[[\theta(u),v],Q']=[\theta(u),[Q',v]].$$
Now let $Q=[Q',v]$ with $Q'\in\mathcal L_{n-1}(V)$, $n\ge2$. By induction, $\theta_n(Q'v)=[\theta_{n-1}(Q'),v]=(n-1)[Q',v]$, and by the claim $\theta_n(vQ')=[\theta_1(v),Q']=[v,Q']$. So $\theta_n(Q)=n[Q',v]=nQ$, and $\theta_n=n$ on $\mathcal L_n(V)$ by continuity; $\theta_1$ is the identity. If $p\nmid n$, $n^{-1}\theta_n$ is a continuous $\Gamma$-equivariant retraction of $V^{\hat\otimes n}$ onto $\mathcal L_n(V)$, and it induces a left inverse of $\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma\to(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma$. $\square$

*Words.* A *word* is $w=(w_1,\dots,w_n)$ with $w_t\in\{0,1,\dots,d\}$. Put $W_0=L$, $W_k=V_k$ for $k\ge1$, and $V_w=W_{w_1}\hat\otimes\cdots\hat\otimes W_{w_n}$, so that $V^{\hat\otimes n}=\bigoplus_wV_w$, a finite sum of $\Gamma$-stable summands, and $(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma=\bigoplus_w(V_w)_\Gamma$. The *letters* of $w$ are the $w_t\ne0$, at the slots $T(w)=\{t:w_t\ne0\}$. For $q\ne q'$ and $k,l\ge1$, $(k@q,l@q')$ is the word with $w_q=k$, $w_{q'}=l$ and $w_t=0$ otherwise, and $(k@q)$ is defined in the same way. Through $\pi_{w_t}$ on the slots in $T(w)$ and $\bar x_0\mapsto1$ on the others, $V_w$ is $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^{T(w)}]]=\Lambda^{\hat\otimes m}$, $m=|T(w)|$, with $\rho$ acting by $\omega(\rho)^{n-m}$ times left multiplication by $\rho$ in every coordinate. For $\beta\in\Lambda$ and $t\in T(w)$ let $r_{t,\beta}$ be right multiplication by $\beta$ in the coordinate $t$.

**Lemma 2 (coordinates; proved here).** Let $w$ be a word with $m=|T(w)|\ge1$, $k=n-m$ and $t_0\in T(w)$.

- (a) The continuous linear map $\kappa^{t_0}_w:\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^{T(w)}]]\to\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^{T(w)\setminus\{t_0\}}]]$, $g\mapsto\omega(g_{t_0})^{-k}(g_{t_0}^{-1}g_t)_{t\ne t_0}$, induces an isomorphism $(V_w)_\Gamma\cong\Lambda^{\hat\otimes(m-1)}$, read as $\mathbb F_p$ if $m=1$. Write $\kappa_w$ for $\kappa^{t_0}_w$ with $t_0=\min T(w)$.
- (b) For $t\in T(w)\setminus\{t_0\}$, $\kappa^{t_0}_wr_{t,\beta}=r_{t,\beta}\kappa^{t_0}_w$. If $m=1$, $\kappa_wr_{t_0,\beta}=\omega^{-k}(\beta)\kappa_w$, where $\omega^{-k}:\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ is the ring homomorphism with $\rho\mapsto\omega(\rho)^{-k}$. If $m=2$, $\kappa^{t_0}_wr_{t_0,\beta}=\iota_k(\beta)\kappa^{t_0}_w$, a left multiplication, where $\iota_k:\Lambda\to\Lambda$ is the continuous linear map with $\iota_k(\rho)=\omega(\rho)^{-k}\rho^{-1}$.
- (c) The image in $(V_w)_\Gamma$, $w=(0,\dots,0)$, of an element of $\mathcal L_n(V)$ is $0$.

*Proof.* (a) For an open normal $U\subseteq\ker\omega$ the map descends to $(\Gamma/U)^{T(w)}$, so it is continuous. It kills $I_\Gamma V_w$: on a basis element, the action of $\rho$ multiplies by $\omega(\rho)^k$ and replaces every $g_t$ by $\rho g_t$, which multiplies $\omega(g_{t_0})^{-k}$ by $\omega(\rho)^{-k}$ and leaves each $g_{t_0}^{-1}g_t$ unchanged. The continuous map $s$ inserting $1$ in the coordinate $t_0$ satisfies $\kappa^{t_0}_ws=\mathrm{id}$, and for a basis element $g$, $g-s\kappa^{t_0}_wg$ is $g$ minus its image under the action of $g_{t_0}^{-1}$, which lies in $I_\Gamma V_w$. By linearity, continuity and density of the span of $\Gamma^{T(w)}$, $\ker\kappa^{t_0}_w=I_\Gamma V_w$, as in Lemma 2 (a) of the theorem modulo the third lower central term. (b) For $\beta=h\in\Gamma$ and a basis element $g$: replacing $g_t$ by $g_th$ with $t\ne t_0$ replaces $g_{t_0}^{-1}g_t$ by $g_{t_0}^{-1}g_th$; replacing $g_{t_0}$ by $g_{t_0}h$ multiplies by $\omega(h)^{-k}$ and replaces each $g_{t_0}^{-1}g_t$ by $h^{-1}g_{t_0}^{-1}g_t$. Extend linearly and continuously in $\beta$. (c) The projection $q:V\to L$ along $\bigoplus_kV_k$ gives the component $q^{\otimes n}(Y)$ of $Y$ in $V_{(0,\dots,0)}$, and $q^{\otimes n}$ maps $\mathcal L_n(V)$ into $\mathcal L_n(L)=0$, as $n\ge2$ and $\dim L=1$. $\square$

For $X\in(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma$ and a word $w$ with $T(w)\ne\emptyset$, let $X_w\in\Lambda^{\hat\otimes(|T(w)|-1)}$ be $\kappa_w$ of the $w$-part of $X$. Whether $X_w=0$ does not depend on the choice of $t_0$.

*The rule (R).* Let $B$ be a $\Lambda$-linear automorphism of $V$ fixing $\bar x_0$, with $B(\mu\bar x_l)=\sum_{j\ge1}\mu\beta_{jl}\bar x_j$ for $l\ge1$, $\beta_{jl}\in\Lambda$. Then $B^{\otimes n}$ maps $V_{w'}$ into $\bigoplus_wV_w$ over the words $w$ with $T(w)=T(w')$, and its $w$-component is the product of the $r_{t,\beta_{w_tw'_t}}$, $t\in T(w)$. Call $w'$ a *source* of $w$ for $B$ if $T(w')=T(w)$ and $\beta_{w_tw'_t}\ne0$ for all $t\in T(w)$. So the $w$-part of $B^{\otimes n}_\Gamma X$ is the sum over the sources $w'$ of $w$ of the images of the $w'$-parts of $X$. If every $\beta_{jl}$ lies in $\mathbb Z$, then $(B^{\otimes n}_\Gamma X)_w=\sum_{w'}\big(\prod_{t\in T(w)}\beta_{w_tw'_t}\big)X_{w'}$.

**Lemma 3 (the conditions; proved here).** Let $n\ge2$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Put $\mathcal U=\{u(\alpha):\alpha\in\mathcal A_n\}$, and let $\mathcal S$ be the set of $v\in H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$ with $p^mv\in\mathcal U$ for some $m\ge0$.

- (u1) For $\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$ and $g\in P_n$, $c_g\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$ and $c_{c_g\alpha}(\rho)=c_\alpha(\rho)+(1-\rho)(gP_{n+1})$.
- (u2) For $\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$ and tame-trivial $\theta$, $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}\in\mathcal A_n$ and $c_{\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}}=\theta_n\circ c_\alpha$, with $\theta_n$ the action of $\theta$ on $\mathcal L_n$.
- (u3) $\mathcal U$ and $\mathcal S$ are subgroups, and $\mathcal S\cap pH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)=p\mathcal S$.
- (u4) $u(\alpha)=0$ if and only if $c_h\alpha$ induces the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$ for some $h\in P_n$.

Moreover, for every $v\in\mathcal S$:

- (i) $\theta_*v\in\mathcal S$ for every tame-trivial $\theta$.
- (ii) For every tame-trivial $\beta$ there is $N\ge1$ with $\beta^N_*v=v$.
- (iii) If $\psi$ is tame-trivial and $\psi_H-1$ is nilpotent, then $\psi_*v=v$.
- (iv) Let $T=T^{(1)}_1$ be the boundary twist of the first pair in the notation of [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md), and $\partial^T_n:\mathcal L_n\to\mathcal L_{n+2}$ its map from Lemma 1 (a) of [the degree-one Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-on-wild-inertia.md). Then $\partial^T_n$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant and $(\partial^T_n)_*v=0$ in $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_{n+2})$.

*Proof.* (u1) $P_n$ is normal in $G$, as it is characteristic in $P$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). For $x\in G$, $c_g(x)x^{-1}=[g,x]\in P_n$, and $[g,x]\in P_{n+1}$ if $x\in P$; and $c_g\alpha$ represents the class of $\alpha$. So $c_g\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$. Also $c_g\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}=g\cdot\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}\cdot\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}$, three elements of $P_n$ with classes $gP_{n+1}$, $c_\alpha(\rho)$ and $-\rho(gP_{n+1})$.

(u2) $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}$ has finitely many outer conjugates and lies in $\mathcal A_n$, as $P_n$, $P_{n+1}$ are characteristic. Since $\theta^{-1}(\tilde\rho)$ lies over $\rho$, $\alpha(\theta^{-1}(\tilde\rho))=y\,\theta^{-1}(\tilde\rho)$ with $y\in P_n$ of class $c_\alpha(\rho)$, and $\theta\alpha\theta^{-1}(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}=\theta(y)$.

(u3) Elements with finitely many conjugates form a subgroup, so $\mathcal A_n$ is closed under products and inverses. For $\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal A_n$ write $\beta(\tilde\rho)=j\tilde\rho$ with $j\in P_n$; then $\alpha\beta(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}=\alpha(j)\cdot\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}$ and $\alpha(j)j^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$. So $c_{\alpha\beta}=c_\alpha+c_\beta$ and $c_{\alpha^{-1}}=-c_\alpha$. If $v\in\mathcal S$, $p^mv\in\mathcal U$ and $v=pv'$, then $p^{m+1}v'\in\mathcal U$, so $v'\in\mathcal S$.

(u4) If $c_\alpha=\delta\bar k$, $\bar k\in\mathcal L_n$, choose $h\in P_n$ over $\bar k$. By (u1), $c_h\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$, and $c_h\alpha$ is trivial on $P/P_{n+1}$; as $c_h\alpha(y\tilde\rho)(y\tilde\rho)^{-1}=c_h\alpha(y)y^{-1}\cdot y\,c_h\alpha(\tilde\rho)\tilde\rho^{-1}y^{-1}$, it induces the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$. Conversely, if $c_h\alpha$ does, then $c_{c_h\alpha}=0$ and $u(\alpha)=u(c_h\alpha)=0$ by (u1).

(i) If $p^mv=u(\alpha)$, then $p^m\theta_*v=u(\theta\alpha\theta^{-1})$ by (u2).

(ii) Let $p^mv=u(\alpha)$, $\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$. By Lemma (b) of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), $\beta^N\alpha\beta^{-N}=c_g\alpha$ with $N\ge1$ and $g\in P$. Both sides induce the identity on $G/P_n$, hence so does $c_g$, and $g\in P_n$ by Lemma 5 (o) of the higher Johnson theorem. By (u1), (u2), $\beta^N_*u(\alpha)=u(\alpha)$, so $p^m(\beta^N_*v-v)=0$, and $\beta^N_*v=v$ as $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$ is torsion-free.

(iii) By (ii), $U^Nv=v$ for $U=\psi_*$ and some $N\ge1$. As in the proof of Lemma 5 (iii) of the higher Johnson theorem, $\psi_n-1$ is nilpotent on $\mathcal L_n$, hence $U-1$ is nilpotent on $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$. If $(U-1)v\ne0$, let $q\ge1$ be largest with $(U-1)^qv\ne0$. Then $0=(U-1)^{q-1}(U^N-1)v=N(U-1)^qv$, contradicting torsion-freeness.

(iv) By Lemma 2 (a) of interval twists, $T$ fixes $\mathcal G$ pointwise, is tame-trivial and is trivial on $P/P_3$. For $z\in P_n$ and $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$, $T(\tilde\rho z\tilde\rho^{-1})(\tilde\rho z\tilde\rho^{-1})^{-1}=\tilde\rho\,T(z)z^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}$, so $\partial^T_n$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant. Let $p^mv=u(\alpha)$, $\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$. By Lemma (b) of abelianized wild inertia, $T^N\alpha T^{-N}=c_g\alpha$ with $N\ge1$ and $g\in P$.

*The element $g$ lies in $P_{n+2}$.* Put $\theta_1=T^N$, $\theta_2=\alpha$. For $y\in P$, $e_1=\theta_1(y)y^{-1}\in P_3$ and $e_2=\theta_2(y)y^{-1}\in P_{n+1}$. By Lemma 1 (a) of the degree-one theorem (with $k=2$ for $\theta_1$ and $k=n$ for $\theta_2$), $\theta_1(e_2)\in e_2P_{n+3}$ and $\theta_2(e_1)\in e_1P_{n+3}$. So $\theta_1\theta_2(y)=\theta_1(e_2)e_1y$ and $\theta_2\theta_1(y)=\theta_2(e_1)e_2y$ differ by left multiplication by an element of $P_{n+3}$, as $[P_{n+1},P_3]\subseteq P_{n+4}$. Now $c_g=\theta_1\theta_2\theta_1^{-1}\theta_2^{-1}$ maps $\theta_2\theta_1(y)$ to $\theta_1\theta_2(y)$, and $\theta_2\theta_1(P)=P$, so $[g,P]\subseteq P_{n+3}$, and $g\in P_{n+2}$ by Lemma 2 (c) of the higher Johnson theorem.

*The cocycle.* For $\tilde\rho\in\mathcal G$, which $T$ fixes, write $\alpha(\tilde\rho)=j\tilde\rho$ with $j\in P_n$ of class $c_\alpha(\rho)$. Then $T^N\alpha T^{-N}(\tilde\rho)=T^N(j)\tilde\rho$ and $c_g\alpha(\tilde\rho)=gj\cdot\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}\cdot\tilde\rho$. So $T^N(j)j^{-1}=g\cdot j(\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1})j^{-1}$, which is $g\tilde\rho g^{-1}\tilde\rho^{-1}$ modulo $[P_n,P_{n+2}]\subseteq P_{n+3}$. By Lemma 1 (a) and (b) of the degree-one theorem, $T^N(j)j^{-1}\in P_{n+2}$ has class $\partial^{T^N}_n(c_\alpha(\rho))=N\partial^T_n(c_\alpha(\rho))$. Hence $N\partial^T_n(c_\alpha(\rho))=(1-\rho)(gP_{n+3})$ for all $\rho$, so $N(\partial^T_n)_*u(\alpha)=0$, and $Np^m(\partial^T_n)_*v=0$. As $H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_{n+2})$ is torsion-free, $(\partial^T_n)_*v=0$. $\square$

*Reductions.* Let $n\ge2$ with $p\nmid n$, and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. For $v\in\mathcal S$, let $X\in(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma$ be the image of $\bar v\in\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma$; by Lemma 1, $X$ determines $\bar v$. If a tame-trivial $\theta$ acts on $V$ by $B$, then $\theta_n$ reduces to $\mathcal L_n(B)$, the restriction of $B^{\otimes n}$. By Lemma 1 (a) of the degree-one theorem, $\partial^T_n$ reduces to the restriction to $\mathcal L_n(V)$ of the map $\delta^{(n)}$ of $V^{\hat\otimes n}$ that applies the reduction $\delta$ of $\mathcal J_3(T)$ in one slot at a time and adds the results. So Lemma 3 gives, for every $v\in\mathcal S$:

- (i') if a tame-trivial $\theta$ acts on $V$ by $B$, then $B^{\otimes n}_\Gamma X$ is the image of the reduction of $\theta_*v\in\mathcal S$;
- (ii') if a tame-trivial $\beta$ acts on $V$ by $B$, then $(B^N)^{\otimes n}_\Gamma X=X$ for some $N\ge1$;
- (iii') $\bar\Psi^{\otimes n}_\Gamma X=X$ for every $\bar\Psi$ in the group generated by the actions on $V$ of automorphisms $\psi$ as in (iii);
- (iv') $\eta(\delta^{(n)}Y)=0$ for every $Y\in V^{\hat\otimes n}$ with class $X$ and every continuous, linear, $\Gamma$-invariant $\eta$ on $V^{\hat\otimes n+2}$, since $\delta^{(n)}$ is $\Gamma$-equivariant and the image of $\delta^{(n)}_\Gamma\bar v=0$ in $(V^{\hat\otimes n+2})_\Gamma$ is the class of $\delta^{(n)}Y$;
- (v') if $X=0$, then $\bar v=0$, so $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}(v)\in p(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma$, and $v\in p\mathcal S$ by the Cohomology paragraph and (u3).

**Proof of the theorem.** Here $r\ge2$, as $d\ge5$, and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, as $e\ge2$. Fix $v\in\mathcal S$ with image $X$. By (i'), steps 1 to 3 also apply to $B^{\otimes n}_\Gamma X$ for every tame-trivial automorphism with action $B$. By Lemma 2 (c), $X_w$ is only needed for $T(w)\ne\emptyset$. Call a word $w$ with $T(w)\ne\emptyset$ *pure* if its letters all lie in one pair $\{a_i,b_i\}$ (*pure in pair $i$*) or are all complementary (*complementary*).

*1. Localization.* $X_w=0$ for every word $w$ with $T(w)\ne\emptyset$ that is not pure. Indeed, such a $w$ has a letter in some pair $\{a_i,b_i\}$ and a letter outside it. As in step 1 of the higher Johnson theorem, $\beta_i=\psi_i\theta_\gamma\psi_i^{-1}$ is tame-trivial and acts on $V$ by right multiplication by $\gamma$ on the coordinates of $V_{a_i}$ and $V_{b_i}$, and as the identity on the other summands. By (ii'), $(B_i^N)^{\otimes n}$ fixes the $w$-part of $X$ for some $N\ge1$, and it acts on $V_w$ by $r_{t,\gamma_1}$ for $t\in Q=\{t:w_t\in\{a_i,b_i\}\}$, with $\gamma_1=\gamma^N$. Choose $t_0\in T(w)\setminus Q$. By Lemma 2 (b), $\kappa^{t_0}_w$ of the $w$-part is fixed by right multiplication by $\gamma_1$ in every coordinate in $Q\ne\emptyset$, so it is $0$ by Lemma 1 (b) of the higher Johnson theorem, as $\gamma_1$ generates an infinite pro-$p$ closed subgroup.

*2. Kondo's automorphisms.* As in step 1 of the degree-one Johnson theorem, every unipotent integral symplectic matrix $M$ of the surface lattice is the action on the surface classes of an automorphism $\psi$ as in (iii) that fixes the complementary generators. So (iii') holds for the $\Lambda$-linear $\bar M$ given by $M$ on the $\bar x_{a_i},\bar x_{b_i}$ and the identity on $A$, and (R) applies with integer coefficients. We use $u_i:a_i\mapsto a_i+b_i$, $l_i:b_i\mapsto b_i+a_i$ and, for $i\ne i'$, $M_{ii'}:a_i\mapsto a_i+b_{i'}$, $a_{i'}\mapsto a_{i'}+b_i$ and $M'_{ii'}:b_i\mapsto b_i+a_{i'}$, $b_{i'}\mapsto b_{i'}+a_i$, the other basis vectors being fixed; as in step 2 of the higher Johnson theorem these are unipotent and symplectic. Let $w$ be pure in pair $i$, and fix $i'\ne i$.

- *Two letters $a_i$.* Let $w_q=w_{q''}=a_i$ with $q\ne q''$, and let $w^*$ be $w$ with $w_q$ replaced by $b_{i'}$. It is not pure, so $X_{w^*}=0$ by step 1. A source $w'$ of $w^*$ for $\bar M_{ii'}$ has $w'_q\in\{b_{i'},a_i\}$, $w'_t=a_i$ where $w^*_t=a_i$, and $w'_t\in\{b_i,a_{i'}\}$ where $w^*_t=b_i$. The only pure source is $w$, since $w'_{q''}=a_i$ forces every letter into pair $i$. So $0=X_{w^*}=X_w$.
- *Two letters $b_i$.* The same argument with $\bar M'_{ii'}$ gives $X_w=0$.
- *One letter.* For $w=(a_i@q)$, the sources of $(b_i@q)$ for $\bar u_i$ are $(b_i@q)$ and $(a_i@q)$, so $X_{(b_i@q)}=X_{(b_i@q)}+X_{(a_i@q)}$ and $X_{(a_i@q)}=0$. Likewise $\bar l_i$ gives $X_{(b_i@q)}=0$.

So $X_w\ne0$ only if $w$ has exactly one letter $a_i$ and one letter $b_i$. Let $q<q'$. The sources of $(b_i@q,b_i@q')$ for $\bar u_i$ are the four words with letters in $\{a_i,b_i\}$ at $q,q'$, so $X_{(a_i@q,b_i@q')}+X_{(b_i@q,a_i@q')}=0$. The sources of $(b_{i'}@q,b_i@q')$ for $\bar M_{ii'}$ are the words with $w'_q\in\{b_{i'},a_i\}$ and $w'_{q'}\in\{b_i,a_{i'}\}$; the pure ones are $(a_i@q,b_i@q')$ and $(b_{i'}@q,a_{i'}@q')$, and $(b_{i'}@q,b_i@q')$ is not pure. So $X_{(a_i@q,b_i@q')}=-X_{(b_{i'}@q,a_{i'}@q')}=X_{(a_{i'}@q,b_{i'}@q')}$. Hence there are $A_{qq'}\in\Lambda$, $1\le q<q'\le n$, with
$$X_{(a_i@q,b_i@q')}=A_{qq'},\quad X_{(b_i@q,a_i@q')}=-A_{qq'}\ \ (1\le i\le r),\qquad X_w=0\text{ for every other }w\text{ pure in a pair}.\tag{1}$$

*3. The boundary twist.* By step 1 of abelianized wild inertia, $\delta$ is $\Lambda$-linear, kills $L$ and every $V_l$ with $l\notin\{a,b\}$, and $\delta(\bar a)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]=2\bar a\bar b\bar a-\bar b\bar a\bar a-\bar a\bar a\bar b$, $\delta(\bar b)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]=\bar a\bar b\bar b-2\bar b\bar a\bar b+\bar b\bar b\bar a$. For $q<q'$ and $u,v\in V$ let $e_{qq'}(u,v)\in V^{\hat\otimes n}$ have $u$ in slot $q$, $v$ in slot $q'$ and $\bar x_0$ elsewhere. By steps 1, 2 and Lemma 2 (a), $X$ is the class of
$$Y=Y'+\sum_{q<q'}\big(e_{qq'}(\bar a,A_{qq'}\bar b)-e_{qq'}(\bar b,A_{qq'}\bar a)\big),$$
where $Y'$ is a sum of elements of the $V_w$ with $w$ complementary or pure in a pair other than the first. Every slot of such an element lies in $A$ or in some $V_{(i)}$ with $i\ge2$, so $\delta^{(n)}Y'=0$.

For $1\le t$ and $t+3\le t'\le n+2$, let $w^\dagger$ be the word of length $n+2$ with $b$ in slots $t,t'$, $a$ in slots $t+1,t+2$ and $0$ elsewhere, and let $\eta_{tt'}$ be the projection onto $V_{w^\dagger}$ followed by $\kappa_{w^\dagger}$, with values in $\Lambda^{\hat\otimes3}$; it is $\Gamma$-invariant by Lemma 2 (a). The map $\delta^{(n)}$ replaces the letter in slot $q$ or $q'$ of $e_{qq'}(u,v)$ by three letters, and keeps $\bar x_0$ in the other slots. A term reaching $V_{w^\dagger}$ has its four pair letters in the slots $t,t+1,t+2,t'$, in the pattern $\bar b\bar a\bar a\bar b$. If $\delta$ acts in slot $q$, its three letters fill $t,t+1,t+2$ and $q'+2=t'$; the last letter is $\bar b$, so the term is $-\bar b\bar a\bar a$ from $e_{qq'}(\bar a,A_{qq'}\bar b)$ with $q=t$, $q'=t'-2$. Its coordinate is $-(1,1,1,A_{t,t'-2})$, with image $-1\otimes1\otimes A_{t,t'-2}$. If $\delta$ acts in slot $q'$, its three letters fill $q'=t+1,t+2,t'$, which are consecutive only if $t'=t+3$; the first letter is $\bar b$, so the term is $-\bar b\otimes A_{t,t+1}\cdot(-\bar a\bar a\bar b)$ from $-e_{t,t+1}(\bar b,A_{t,t+1}\bar a)$. By property (P) of abelianized wild inertia, its image is $\Delta_3(A_{t,t+1})$. By (iv'):
$$1\otimes1\otimes A_{t,t'-2}=0\ \ (t'\ge t+4),\qquad\Delta_3(A_{t,t+1})=1\otimes1\otimes A_{t,t+1}.$$
Applying $\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}$ to the first equation, and $\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon$ to the second, with $(\mathrm{id}\otimes\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)\Delta_3=\mathrm{id}$,
$$A_{qq'}=0\ \ (q'\ge q+2),\qquad A_{q,q+1}=\epsilon(A_{q,q+1})\cdot1\in\mathbb F_p.\tag{2}$$

*4. The transvection $T_1$ (even $d$).* By step 5 of abelianized wild inertia, $T_1$ fixes $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$, and is tame-trivial with $(T_1)_H-1$ of square $0$. Its action $\bar T_1$ on $V$ maps $\mu\bar x_2$ to $\mu\bar x_2+\mu\bar x_1$ and fixes the other summands. Let $w$ be a complementary word whose letters all equal $2$, choose $q\in T(w)$, and let $w^*$ be $w$ with $w_q$ replaced by $1$. The sources of $w^*$ for $\bar T_1$ are $w^*$ and $w$, so (iii') gives $X_{w^*}=X_{w^*}+X_w$, and
$$X_w=0\quad\text{for every complementary }w\text{ without the letter }1.\tag{3}$$

*5. The mixing automorphisms.* Let $\theta$ be tame-trivial with action $B$ on $V$, and $X'=(B^{-1})^{\otimes n}_\Gamma X$. By (i'), steps 1 and 2 apply to $X'$: $X'_w=0$ if $w$ is not pure, or if $w$ is pure in a pair with a single letter.

*Even $d$.* For $\lambda\in\ker\omega$, part 2 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md) gives a tame-trivial $\theta$ whose action $B$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,4$, and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda\bar x_3$, $B\bar x_4=\bar x_4-\lambda^*\bar x_2$. So $B^{-1}$ maps $\mu\bar x_1\mapsto\mu\bar x_1-\mu\lambda\bar x_3$, $\mu\bar x_4\mapsto\mu\bar x_4+\mu\lambda^*\bar x_2$, and fixes the other summands; here $(a,b)=(3,4)$. Let $w$ be complementary with $|T(w)|\ge2$ and $w_q=1$, and let $w^*$ be $w$ with $w_q$ replaced by $3$. It is not pure, so $X'_{w^*}=0$. A source $w'$ of $w^*$ has $w'_q\in\{3,1\}$, $w'_t=1$ where $w^*_t=1$, $w'_t\in\{2,4\}$ where $w^*_t=2$, and $w'_t=0$ elsewhere. It is pure only if $w'=w$, or if $w'_q=3$ and every other letter of $w'$ is $4$; by (1) the latter contributes only if $w=(1@q,2@q')$ and $w'=(3@q,4@q')$. So for every other $w$, $r_{q,-\lambda}$ kills the $w$-part of $X$. For $\lambda=\lambda_0$, choose $t_0\in T(w)\setminus\{q\}$; by Lemma 2 (b) and Lemma 1 (a) of the higher Johnson theorem (for $\gamma_1=\gamma$), $X_w=0$. For $q<q'$ put $U_{qq'}=X_{(1@q,2@q')}$ and $U'_{qq'}=X_{(2@q,1@q')}$. By (R), (1) and Lemma 2 (b), the words $(3@q,2@q')$ and $(2@q,3@q')$ give
$$A_{qq'}\lambda^*-\iota_{n-2}(\lambda)U_{qq'}=0,\qquad -\iota_{n-2}(\lambda^*)A_{qq'}-U'_{qq'}\lambda=0.\tag{4e}$$
For $\lambda=\lambda_0$, $\lambda_0^*=\iota_{n-2}(\lambda_0)=\gamma^{-1}-1$ and $\iota_{n-2}(\lambda_0^*)=\gamma-1$. As $A_{qq'}\in\mathbb F_p$ by (2), (4e) reads $(\gamma^{-1}-1)(A_{qq'}-U_{qq'})=0$ and $(A_{qq'}+U'_{qq'})(\gamma-1)=0$, so $U_{qq'}=A_{qq'}$ and $U'_{qq'}=-A_{qq'}$, by Lemma 2 of [the central-element construction](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md).

*Odd $d$.* For $\nu\in\ker\omega$, part 3 of the mixing theorem gives a tame-trivial $\theta$ whose action $B$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_i$ for $i\ne1,3$, and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\nu\bar x_2$ and $B\bar x_3=\bar x_3-\nu^*\delta\nu\,\bar x_2-\nu^*\delta_0\bar x_1$. As $B\bar x_2=\bar x_2$, $B^{-1}$ maps $\mu\bar x_1\mapsto\mu\bar x_1-\mu\nu\bar x_2$ and $\mu\bar x_3\mapsto\mu\bar x_3+\mu\beta\bar x_2+\mu\nu^*\delta_0\bar x_1$, with $\beta=\nu^*\delta\nu-\nu^*\delta_0\nu$; here $(a,b)=(2,3)$. Every letter of a complementary word is $1$. Let $w$ be complementary with $|T(w)|\ge2$, $q\in T(w)$, and $w^*$ be $w$ with $w_q$ replaced by $2$; it is not pure. A source $w'$ of $w^*$ has $w'_q\in\{2,1,3\}$ and $w'_t\in\{1,3\}$ for the other $t\in T(w)$. It is pure only if $w'=w$, or if all its letters lie in $\{2,3\}$; by (1) the latter contributes only if $|T(w)|=2$ and $w'=(2@q,3@q')$ with $T(w)=\{q,q'\}$. So for $|T(w)|\ge3$, $X_w=0$ as in the even case, with $\nu=\lambda_0$. For $q<q'$ put $U_{qq'}=X_{(1@q,1@q')}$. The word $(2@q,1@q')$ gives
$$A_{qq'}\nu^*\delta_0-\iota_{n-2}(\nu)U_{qq'}=0.\tag{4o}$$
For $\nu=\lambda_0$ this reads $(\gamma^{-1}-1)(A_{qq'}\delta_0-U_{qq'})=0$, so $U_{qq'}=A_{qq'}\delta_0$.

In both cases, by steps 1 to 5, $X_w=0$ for every word except possibly the words $(1@q)$ and those in (1), (4e) and (4o), and these are determined by the constants $A_{qq'}$ of (2).

*6. The parameter $\rho-\omega(\rho)$.* Let $\rho\in\Gamma$ and $\lambda=\rho-\omega(\rho)\in\ker\omega$. Then $\lambda^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}-\omega(\rho)$ and $\iota_{n-2}(\lambda)=\omega(\rho)^{2-n}\rho^{-1}-\omega(\rho)$, so
$$\lambda^*-\iota_{n-2}(\lambda)=\big(\omega(\rho)-\omega(\rho)^{2-n}\big)\rho^{-1}=\omega(\rho)^{2-n}\big(\omega(\rho)^{n-1}-1\big)\rho^{-1}.$$
As $e\nmid n-1$, choose $\rho$ with $\omega(\rho)^{n-1}\ne1$. For even $d$, the first equation of (4e) with $U_{qq'}=A_{qq'}\in\mathbb F_p$ gives $A_{qq'}(\lambda^*-\iota_{n-2}(\lambda))=0$, so $A_{qq'}=0$. For odd $d$, (4o) with $U_{qq'}=A_{qq'}\delta_0$ gives $A_{qq'}(\lambda^*-\iota_{n-2}(\lambda))\delta_0=0$; as $\rho^{-1}$ is a unit and $\delta_0\ne0$ by part 1 of the mixing theorem, $A_{qq'}=0$. So all $A_{qq'}$, $U_{qq'}$, $U'_{qq'}$ vanish, and
$$X_w=0\quad\text{for every word }w\text{ other than the words }(1@q),\ 1\le q\le n.\tag{5}$$

*7. The words $(1@q)$.* Let $\rho\in\Gamma$, $\lambda=\rho-\omega(\rho)$, and $\theta$ as in step 5 for $\lambda$ (or $\nu=\lambda$), with $X'$ as there. The sources of $(a@q)$ for $B^{-1}$ are $(a@q)$, $(1@q)$, and for odd $d$ also $(b@q)$. By step 2 for $X$ and for $X'$, $X_{(a@q)}=X_{(b@q)}=X'_{(a@q)}=0$. By Lemma 2 (b) with $m=1$, $0=X'_{(a@q)}=-\omega^{1-n}(\lambda)X_{(1@q)}$, and $\omega^{1-n}(\lambda)=\omega(\rho)^{1-n}-\omega(\rho)=\omega(\rho)^{1-n}\big(1-\omega(\rho)^n\big)$.

(a) Let $e\nmid n$, and choose $\rho$ with $\omega(\rho)^n\ne1$ in step 7. Then $X_{(1@q)}=0$, so $X=0$ by (5), and $v\in p\mathcal S$ by (v'). For $u=u(\alpha)\in\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal S$ this gives $u\in p^k\mathcal S$ for every $k$, so $\Phi_{\mathcal L_n}(u)\in\bigcap_kp^k(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma=0$, as $(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma$ is a compact abelian pro-$p$ group. So $u=0$ by injectivity of $\Phi$, and (u4) gives $h$.

(b) Let $e\mid n$. By (5), the image $X$ of $\bar v$ is the sum of its parts in the $V_{(1@q)}$. Let $Z\in\mathcal L_n(V)$ lift $\bar v$, and let $Z'$ be its part with $n-1$ slots in $L$ and one in $V_1$, for $V=L\oplus V_1\oplus V''$, $V''$ the sum of the other summands. By (M2) and (M1) of the higher Johnson theorem, $Z'\in\mathcal L_n(L\oplus V_1)$, and $Z-Z'$ has image $0$ in $(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma$. So $\bar v$ is the class of $Z'$, by Lemma 1. Let $\mu\bar x_1\otimes\bar x_0^{\otimes n-1}$ be the component of $Z'$ in $V_1\hat\otimes L^{\hat\otimes n-1}$. Apply Lemma 3 of the higher Johnson theorem to the image of $Z'$ under the $\Lambda$-linear isomorphism of $L\oplus V_1$ onto $L\oplus\Lambda\tilde e\subseteq E$ with $\bar x_0\mapsto\bar x_0$, $\bar x_1\mapsto\tilde e$, which is linear in the sense there; it gives $Z'=[\cdots[\mu\bar x_1,\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]$. For $g\in\Gamma$, $[\cdots[g\bar x_1,\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]=\omega(g)^{1-n}\,g\cdot[\cdots[\bar x_1,\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]$, which is congruent to $\omega(g)^{1-n}[\cdots[\bar x_1,\bar x_0],\dots,\bar x_0]$ modulo $I_\Gamma\mathcal L_n(V)$. By linearity and continuity, $\bar v=\omega^{1-n}(\mu)E_n$. If $\mathcal S\subseteq pH^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$, then $\mathcal S=p\mathcal S$ by (u3), so $\mathcal S=0$ as in (a); take $w=0$. Otherwise choose $v_0\in\mathcal S\setminus pH^1$. Then $\bar v_0=c_0E_n\ne0$, by the Cohomology paragraph. For $v\in\mathcal S$, $\bar v=a\bar v_0$ with $a\in\mathbb Z$, so $v-av_0\in\mathcal S\cap pH^1=p\mathcal S$ by the Cohomology paragraph and (u3). Iterating, $v=s_kv_0+p^kv_k$ with $v_k\in\mathcal S$, $s_k\in\mathbb Z$ and $s_{k+1}\equiv s_k\pmod{p^k}$. With $s=\lim s_k\in\mathbb Z_p$, $\Phi(v)-s\Phi(v_0)\in\bigcap_kp^k(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma=0$. Take $w=\Phi(v_0)$. $\square$

**Proof of the corollary.** For $3\le n\le e$ we show that $\alpha=c_g\circ\alpha_n$ with $\alpha_n$ inducing the identity on $G/P_n$ and on $P/P_{n+1}$. For $n=3$ this is the corollary of the higher Johnson theorem, as $e\ge3$. Let $3\le n\le e-1$. Then $e\nmid n-1$ and $e\nmid n$, as $1\le n-1<n<e$, and $p\nmid n$, as $n<e\le p-1$. The element $\alpha_n$ represents the class of $\alpha$, so part (a) applies: $c_h\alpha_n$ induces the identity on $G/P_{n+1}$ for some $h\in P_n$. As $e\nmid n$, [the higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md) with $n+1$ in place of $n$ shows that $c_h\alpha_n$ is trivial on $P/P_{n+2}$; take $\alpha_{n+1}=c_h\alpha_n$. If $c_k\alpha_e$ also induces the identity on $G/P_e$, then so does $c_k$, and $k\in P_e$ by Lemma 5 (o) of the higher Johnson theorem. Conversely, for $k\in P_e$, $c_k$ preserves both properties, as $[k,x]\in P_e$ for $x\in G$ and $[k,x]\in P_{e+1}$ for $x\in P$. As $P_e$ is characteristic, $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/P_e)$ is defined, and it maps the class of $\alpha$ to the trivial class. Finally $e\ge2$, $e\mid e$, $e\nmid e-1$ and $p\nmid e$, as $e\mid p-1$, so part (b) applies to every $\alpha_e$ with $n=e$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* The class $u(\alpha)\in H^1(\Gamma,\mathcal L_n)$ embeds in $(\mathcal L_n)_\Gamma$, and modulo $p$ in $\mathcal L_n(V)_\Gamma$, which by Lemma 1 embeds in $(V^{\hat\otimes n})_\Gamma$ when $p\nmid n$. There each word contributes a copy of $\Lambda^{\hat\otimes(m-1)}$, with $m$ its number of letters. The units $\gamma$ on one surface pair kill every word mixing that pair with other letters, as in the localization of the higher Johnson theorem, but here a word whose letters all lie in one pair survives, since right multiplication by $\gamma$ in all coordinates is conjugation after normalizing. Kondo's matrices then leave, on each pair, only the words with one letter $a_i$ and one letter $b_i$, with coefficients $A_{qq'}$ common to all pairs. The boundary twist, read in the pattern $\bar b\bar a\bar a\bar b$ with $\bar x_0$ inserted, kills $A_{qq'}$ unless the two letters are adjacent, and makes the adjacent ones constant. Conjugating by the mixing automorphisms ties each constant to a complementary coordinate, and the parameter $\rho-\omega(\rho)$ then kills both unless $e\mid n-1$. The words $(1@q)$, with coinvariants $\mathbb F_p$ through $\omega^{1-n}$, are killed by the same parameter unless $e\mid n$. The $p$-adic layers are peeled off as in the earlier layers.
- *The case $n=2$.* Then part (a), for $e\ge3$, is part (a) of [the theorem modulo the third lower central term](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-the-third-lower-central-term-of-wild-inertia.md), and part (b), for $e=2$, is its part (b), as $E_2$ is the class of $[\bar x_1,\bar x_0]=-[\bar x_0,\bar x_1]$; that entry needs only degree at least $3$.
- *How the layers alternate.* The higher Johnson theorem passes from $G/P_n$ to $P/P_{n+1}$ when $e\nmid n-1$, and part (a) passes from there to $G/P_{n+1}$ when also $e\nmid n$. Starting from the abelian level, these conditions hold for every $n<e$ and fail first at $n=e$, where part (b) leaves the single coordinate $E_e$. The chain cannot be continued past $G/P_e$ and $P/P_{e+1}$ by these theorems.
- *When $e$ divides $n-1$.* Steps 1 to 5 use only $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $p\nmid n$. When $e\mid n-1$, step 6 gives nothing, and the proof leaves the constants $A_{q,q+1}\in\mathbb F_p$ of (2) and the matching complementary coordinates of (4e) or (4o), while step 7 still kills the words $(1@q)$. Whether these constants vanish is not decided here.
- *What remains.* (1) Whether the coordinate $E_e$ in part (b) can be nonzero for a finite-conjugacy automorphism trivial on $G/P_e$ and $P/P_{e+1}$; for $e=2$ this is the open coordinate $X_{01}$ of the theorem modulo the third lower central term. (2) The Johnson map $H\to\mathcal L_{e+1}$ and the later layers. (3) Degrees $3$ and $4$, where Kondo's matrices $M_{ii'}$ are not available, and $p\mid n$, where Lemma 1 fails. (4) The case $\mu_p\subseteq K$. Innerness of a finite-conjugacy automorphism is equivalent to innerness on every $G/P_j$. This continues item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535).
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, every $s$, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$, $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge2$, $n\ge2$ with $p\nmid n$ and $[K(\mu_p):K]\nmid n-1$ for the theorem, and $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$ for the corollary. Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
