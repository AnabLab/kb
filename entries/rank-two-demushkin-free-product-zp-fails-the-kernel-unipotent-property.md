**Setting.** Fix a prime $p$ and a power $Q\ge3$ of $p$. Let
$$D_Q=\langle x,y\mid x^Q[x,y]=1\rangle_{\text{pro-}p},\qquad G_Q=D_Q*\mathbb Z_p=\langle x,y,z\mid x^Q[x,y]=1\rangle_{\text{pro-}p},$$
with $[u,v]=u^{-1}v^{-1}uv$, and write $G_{Q,(n)}$ for the $p$-Zassenhaus filtration of $G_Q$. By item 1 of [the classification of Demushkin groups](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), with rank $2$, $D_Q$ is the rank-two Demushkin group with invariant $q(D_Q)=Q$. Every rank-two Demushkin group $D$ with $q(D)\ne2$ is isomorphic to $D_{q(D)}$ with $q(D)\ge3$, or to $\mathbb Z_p^2$ if $q(D)=0$. The letter $q$ is used below for the size of a coefficient field, so the Demushkin invariant is always written $q(D)$ or $Q$.

As in [no fixed finite field detects every Zassenhaus degree](no-fixed-finite-field-detects-every-zassenhaus-degree-of-zp2-free-product-zp.md), for a power $q$ of $p$ a pro-$p$ group $H$ has the *kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_q$* if $H_{(n)}=\bigcap_\rho\ker\rho$ over all continuous $\rho:H\to U_n(\mathbb F_q)$. For $q=p$ this is the property in Mináč and Tân's [conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md). Put
$$N(q)=(q+1)(q^2-q+3),\qquad n_0(q)=N(q)+3\ \text{if }p=2,\qquad n_0(q)=N(q)+4\ \text{if }p\text{ is odd}.$$
So $n_0(2)=18$ and $n_0(3)=40$, and $n_0(p)$ is the bound of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md) for $\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. For every power $q$ of $p$ and every $n\ge n_0(q)$ there is $g\in G_{Q,(n-1)}$ with $g\notin G_{Q,(n)}$ and $\rho(g)=1$ for every continuous $\rho:G_Q\to U_n(\mathbb F_q)$. So $G_Q$ fails the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_q$ for every $n\ge n_0(q)$, and over $\mathbb F_p$ for every $n\ge n_0(p)$.

**Corollary 1 (every rank-two factor with $q(D)\ne2$).** This is proved here. Let $D$ be a rank-two Demushkin group with $q(D)\ne2$, and let $G'$ be a pro-$p$ group with continuous homomorphisms $\sigma:D*\mathbb Z_p\to G'$ and $\tau:G'\to D*\mathbb Z_p$ such that $\tau\sigma=\mathrm{id}$. Then, for every power $q$ of $p$, $G'$ fails the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_q$ for every $n\ge n_0(q)$.

This applies to every finite free pro-$p$ product $G'=G_1*\cdots*G_r$ with $r\ge2$, $G_1\cong D$, and some other factor $G_i$ that has a continuous surjection onto $\mathbb Z_p$. Examples of such $G_i$ are nontrivial free pro-$p$ groups of finite rank, $\mathbb Z_p^d$ with $d\ge1$, and pro-$p$ groups with a presentation by $d\ge2$ generators and one relation, such as Demushkin groups of rank at least $2$. If moreover every factor is of one of the types in [the polynomial factor theorem](polynomial-factors-double-the-unipotent-detection-range.md), which include $D$, then $G'$ has the property over $\mathbb F_q$ for every $n\le2q+2$, by that theorem.

*Proof.* If $q(D)=0$, then $D*\mathbb Z_p\cong\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p$, and the theorem of the fixed-field entry gives an element $g$ as in the theorem above. Otherwise $D\cong D_Q$ with $Q=q(D)\ge3$, and the theorem above gives $g$. Continuous homomorphisms map each term of the $p$-Zassenhaus filtration into the corresponding term. So $\sigma(g)\in G'_{(n-1)}$, and $\sigma(g)\notin G'_{(n)}$, since otherwise $g=\tau\sigma(g)$ would lie in $(D*\mathbb Z_p)_{(n)}$. For every continuous $\rho:G'\to U_n(\mathbb F_q)$, the composite $\rho\sigma$ is a continuous homomorphism $D*\mathbb Z_p\to U_n(\mathbb F_q)$, so $\rho(\sigma(g))=1$.

For the free product, let $\pi:G_i\to\mathbb Z_p=\langle z\rangle$ be a continuous surjection, choose $t\in G_i$ with $\pi(t)=z$, and let $s:\langle z\rangle\to G_i$ be the continuous homomorphism with $s(z)=t$, which exists as $\mathbb Z_p$ is free pro-$p$ on $z$. By the universal property of free pro-$p$ products there are continuous $\sigma:D*\langle z\rangle\to G'$, the identification $D\cong G_1$ on $D$ and $s$ on $\langle z\rangle$, and $\tau:G'\to D*\langle z\rangle$, the inverse identification on $G_1$, $\pi$ on $G_i$, and trivial on the other factors. Then $\tau\sigma$ is the identity on $D$ and on $z$, so $\tau\sigma=\mathrm{id}$. For the examples: a nontrivial free group or $\mathbb Z_p^d$ maps onto $\mathbb Z_p$ by sending one basis element to $z$ and the others to $1$. If $H=F_d/R$ with $F_d$ free pro-$p$ on $d\ge2$ generators and $R$ the closed normal subgroup generated by one element $r$, then $H^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $\mathbb Z_p^d$ modulo the closed subgroup generated by the image of $r$, a quotient of $\mathbb Z_p^d$ by a procyclic subgroup, which surjects onto $\mathbb Z_p$ as $d\ge2$. $\square$

**Corollary 2 (Galois groups).** This is proved here. For every prime $p$, every power $Q\ge3$ of $p$ and every nontrivial free pro-$p$ group $F$ of finite rank $r$, there is a field $K$ containing a root of unity of order $p$ with $G_K(p)\cong D_Q*F$. For such $K$, $G_K(p)$ fails the equality of the [kernel-unipotent conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md) for every $n\ge n_0(p)$. Its abelianization is $\mathbb Z/Q\times\mathbb Z_p^{r+1}$, which has torsion, so $G_K(p)$ is not a right-angled Artin pro-$p$ group, whose abelianization is $\mathbb Z_p^V$ (see [the classification for these groups](kernel-unipotent-property-for-right-angled-artin-pro-p-groups.md), remark "Other free products"). In particular it is not isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p^2*F$ or to the other Galois groups realized in [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md).

*Proof.* Let $\theta:\mathbb Z_p=\langle t\rangle\to1+p\mathbb Z_p$ be the continuous homomorphism with $\theta(t)=1-Q$, which exists because $1-Q\in1+p\mathbb Z_p$ and $1+p\mathbb Z_p$ is a pro-$p$ group (for $p=2$ it is $\mathbb Z_2^\times$). Quadrelli's family of oriented pro-$p$ groups of elementary type contains every finitely generated free pro-$p$ group with an arbitrary orientation, and is closed under the semidirect product $(\mathbb Z_p\rtimes_\theta G_0,\theta\circ\pi)$, in which $gag^{-1}=a^{\theta(g)}$, and under free products [@quadrelli2024, §3.2 (a) and (b), pp. 12–13, and Definition 3.4, p. 13]. Only these items enter the construction of $(\mathbb Z_p\rtimes_\theta\langle t\rangle)*F$, with $F$ trivially oriented, and every oriented pro-$p$ group constructed in this way occurs as a maximal pro-$p$ Galois group with its $p$-cyclotomic character, that is, as $(G_K(p),\theta_K)$ for a field $K$ containing a root of unity of order $p$, the setting of that conjecture [@quadrelli2024, Conjecture 3.6 and the paragraph following it, p. 13]; see also [the elementary type conjecture](elementary-type-conjecture.md). So it remains to show $D_Q\cong\mathbb Z_p\rtimes_\theta\langle t\rangle$.

Write $a$ for the generator of the normal factor $\mathbb Z_p$. In the semidirect product, $tat^{-1}=a^{1-Q}$. Send $x\mapsto a$ and $y\mapsto t^{-1}$. Then $y^{-1}xy\mapsto tat^{-1}=a^{1-Q}$, so $x^Q[x,y]=x^{Q-1}\,y^{-1}xy\mapsto a^{Q-1}a^{1-Q}=1$, and this defines a continuous homomorphism $\varphi:D_Q\to\mathbb Z_p\rtimes_\theta\langle t\rangle$, surjective because its closed image contains $a$ and $t$. Let $M$ be the closed subgroup of $D_Q$ generated by $x$. The relation gives $y^{-1}xy=x^{1-Q}\in M$. Also $x=y\,x^{1-Q}y^{-1}=(yxy^{-1})^{1-Q}$, and raising to the power $(1-Q)^{-1}\in\mathbb Z_p$ gives $yxy^{-1}=x^{(1-Q)^{-1}}\in M$. So $M$ is normal, $M\cdot\overline{\langle y\rangle}$ is a closed subgroup containing $x$ and $y$, hence all of $D_Q$, and every element of $D_Q$ has the form $x^\alpha y^\beta$ with $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb Z_p$. If $\varphi(x^\alpha y^\beta)=a^\alpha t^{-\beta}=1$, the image in the quotient $\langle t\rangle$ gives $\beta=0$, and then $a^\alpha=1$ gives $\alpha=0$. So $\varphi$ is injective.

The failure of the equality follows from Corollary 1 with $q=p$, since $D_Q*F$ is such a free product. The abelianization of a free pro-$p$ product is the product of the abelianizations of the factors. That of $D_Q$ is $\mathbb Z_p^2$ modulo the image $Q\bar x$ of the relation, which is $\mathbb Z/Q\times\mathbb Z_p$, and that of $F$ is $\mathbb Z_p^r$. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** Throughout, $k$ is a finite field of characteristic $p$, $n\ge3$, $m=n-1$, matrices are $(m+1)\times(m+1)$ over $k$, and $\mathfrak n^r$ is as in the proof of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md), the strictly upper triangular matrices supported on entries $(i,j)$ with $j-i\ge r$. For a continuous $\rho:G_Q\to U_{m+1}(k)$ with $\rho(x)=1+A$, $\rho(y)=1+B$, $\rho(z)=1+C$, use the superdiagonal vectors $u_j=(\alpha_j,\beta_j,\gamma_j)$ and $\pi_j=(\alpha_j,\beta_j)$ of Step 2 there. From the counterexample also take the elements $w_\mu$ and $\ell_\mu$, the free algebra $R=\mathbb F_p\langle X,Y,Z\rangle$, the pairing $\langle h,u\rangle$, formula (1), the maps $\kappa$ and $\kappa_{p_1,p_2}$, formula (2) and Step 4(a); and from the fixed-field entry the sums $S_{p_1,p_2}$, with $\langle h,u\rangle=\sum_{p_1<p_2}\gamma_{p_1}\gamma_{p_2}S_{p_1,p_2}$, and the polynomial of degree $m-2$ from its Step B, written $\Phi_{m-2}$ here because $D$ denotes a Demushkin group.

The proofs there use the relation $[x,y]=1$ in two places only. Step 2 of the counterexample derives from it that consecutive $\pi_j$ are linearly dependent, and Step A of the fixed-field entry uses this. The last paragraph of Step 6 of the counterexample uses the decoupled representations of Step 2, whose matrices for $x$ and $y$ commute. Steps 1 and 2 below replace these two inputs for $G_Q$.

*Step 1: consecutive superdiagonal vectors are dependent.* Let $\rho:G_Q\to U_{m+1}(k)$ be continuous. Then $\alpha_j\beta_{j+1}=\beta_j\alpha_{j+1}$ for $1\le j\le m-1$.

Indeed, $(1+A)^Q=1+A^Q$, as $1$ and $A$ commute and $Q$ is a power of the characteristic, and $A^Q\in\mathfrak n^Q\subseteq\mathfrak n^3$ as $Q\ge3$. The relation gives $[1+A,1+B]=(1+A)^{-Q}$, which lies in $1+\mathfrak n^3$, since $1+\mathfrak n^3$ is a subgroup. By Step 1 of the counterexample, $[1+A,1+B]-1\equiv AB-BA$ modulo $\mathfrak n^3$. So $AB-BA\in\mathfrak n^3$. The entry $(j,j+2)$ of a product of two strictly upper triangular matrices $PQ'$ is $P_{j,j+1}Q'_{j+1,j+2}$, so the entries $(j,j+2)$ of $AB$ and $BA$ are $\alpha_j\beta_{j+1}$ and $\beta_j\alpha_{j+1}$, and they are equal.

*Step 2: representations with decoupled superdiagonals.* Let $1\le p_1<p_2\le m$ and $v_0,v_1,v_2\in k^2$, and let $J_0=[1,p_1-1]$, $J_1=[p_1+1,p_2-1]$ and $J_2=[p_2+1,m]$, as in Step 2 of the counterexample. Then there is a continuous $\rho:G_Q\to U_{m+1}(k)$ with $\pi_j=v_l$ for $j\in J_l$, $\pi_{p_1}=\pi_{p_2}=0$, and $\gamma_j=1$ for $j\in\{p_1,p_2\}$ and $\gamma_j=0$ otherwise. These are the superdiagonal vectors of the decoupled representation there.

Indeed, the intervals $I_0=[1,p_1]$, $I_1=[p_1+1,p_2]$ and $I_2=[p_2+1,m+1]$ partition $[1,m+1]$, each is nonempty, and $J_l$ is the set of $j$ with $j,j+1\in I_l$. Write $v_l=(s_l,t_l)$. If $|I_l|\ge2$, the paragraph *Superdiagonals* in the proof of case (e) of Corollary 2 of [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md), applied with its $k+1$ equal to $|I_l|$, its field equal to $k$, and $(\lambda,\beta)=(s_l,-t_l)$, gives a continuous homomorphism $\rho_l:D_Q\to U_{|I_l|}(k)$ such that every superdiagonal entry of $\rho_l(x)$ is $s_l$ and every superdiagonal entry of $\rho_l(y)$ is $t_l$. That construction uses exactly the relation $x^Q[x,y]=1$ with $Q\ge3$, in the commutator convention used here. If $|I_l|=1$, let $\rho_l$ be trivial. Define $\rho$ on $D_Q$ as the block-diagonal homomorphism $d\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(\rho_0(d),\rho_1(d),\rho_2(d))$, with blocks on $I_0,I_1,I_2$, and put $\rho(z)=1+E_{p_1,p_1+1}+E_{p_2,p_2+1}$. By the universal property of the free pro-$p$ product, this is a continuous homomorphism $G_Q\to U_{m+1}(k)$. For $j\in J_l$, the entry $(j,j+1)$ lies in the block on $I_l$, so $\pi_j=v_l$. The entries $(p_1,p_1+1)$ and $(p_2,p_2+1)$ join consecutive blocks, so they vanish for block-diagonal matrices and $\pi_{p_1}=\pi_{p_2}=0$. The superdiagonal of $\rho(z)$ is as stated.

*Step 3: the element, and its vanishing over $\mathbb F_q$.* Let $q$ be a power of $p$, and let $n\ge n_0(q)$ and $m=n-1$, so that $\Phi_{m-2}$ is defined. Define $g\in G_Q$ from the coefficients of $\Phi_{m-2}$ by the recipe of Step 6 of the counterexample, with the same iterated commutators $w_\mu$ of $x,y,z$. Step 3 of the counterexample uses only the matrix identities of its Step 1 and the facts that commutators of elements of the $i$th and $j$th terms lie in the $(i+j)$th term and, for $p=2$, that squares of elements of the $\lceil m/2\rceil$th term lie in the $m$th term. These hold in every pro-$p$ group, and the relation $[x,y]=1$ is not used. So $g\in G_{Q,(m)}=G_{Q,(n-1)}$, and formula (1), $\rho(g)=1+\langle h,u\rangle E_{1,m+1}$, holds for every finite field $k$ of characteristic $p$ and every continuous $\rho:G_Q\to U_{m+1}(k)$, with the same $h\in R_m$ as there. By (2), $\kappa(h)=\Phi_{m-2}(a_0-a_1,a_1-a_2)$, a statement about $h$ alone.

Now let $\rho:G_Q\to U_n(\mathbb F_q)$ be continuous. Step A of the fixed-field entry uses about $\rho$ only that consecutive $\pi_j$ are linearly dependent, which holds by Step 1. So each $S_{p_1,p_2}$ is $0$ or a multiple of $\kappa_{p_1,p_2}(h)(v_0,v_1,v_2)$ for some $v_0,v_1,v_2\in\mathbb F_q^2$. Step C there shows, from $\kappa(h)$ alone, that every $\kappa_{p_1,p_2}(h)$ vanishes on $(\mathbb F_q^2)^3$. Hence $\langle h,u\rangle=0$, and $\rho(g)=1$ by (1).

*Step 4: $g\notin G_{Q,(n)}$.* As $\Phi_{m-2}\ne0$, the last paragraph of Step 6 of the counterexample gives $p_1<p_2$, a finite field $k$ of characteristic $p$ with more than $m-2$ elements, and $v_0,v_1,v_2\in k^2$ with $\kappa_{p_1,p_2}(h)(v_0,v_1,v_2)\ne0$. Let $\rho$ be the representation of Step 2 for these data. Then only the pair $(p_1,p_2)$ has $\gamma_{p_1}\gamma_{p_2}\ne0$, and Step 4(a) of the counterexample, with all $\lambda_j=1$, gives $\langle h,u\rangle=S_{p_1,p_2}=\kappa_{p_1,p_2}(h)(v_0,v_1,v_2)\ne0$. By (1), $\rho(g)\ne1$. By Step 1 of the counterexample, every continuous homomorphism $G_Q\to U_{m+1}(k)$ kills $G_{Q,(m+1)}$, so $g\notin G_{Q,(n)}$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The mechanism.* Step 1 holds more generally: if $\rho(x)$ and $\rho(y)$ satisfy any relation $r$ that is congruent to $[x,y]$ modulo the third term of the Zassenhaus filtration of the free pro-$p$ group on $x,y$, then $AB-BA\in\mathfrak n^3$, because Step 1 of the counterexample maps that third term into $1+\mathfrak n^3$. What the proof needs beyond this is a supply of representations with prescribed constant superdiagonals, which the invisible-ideal theorem provides for $D_Q$.
- *What remains open.* For $D*\mathbb Z_p$ with $q(D)\ne2$, the property over $\mathbb F_q$ holds for $n\le2q+2$ by Corollary 1 and fails for $n\ge n_0(q)$; the sizes in between are not decided here. At $p=2$, the rank-two Demushkin groups with $q(D)=2$ have the relation $x^{2+2^f}[x,y]$ with $f\ge2$ or $f=\infty$, by item 3 of [the classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md). For them, the entries $(j,j+2)$ of the relation give $\alpha_j\alpha_{j+1}+\alpha_j\beta_{j+1}+\beta_j\alpha_{j+1}=0$ instead of Step 1. A run of nonzero superdiagonal vectors then need not lie on one line. Over a field of characteristic $2$, if $\pi_j=(s,t)\ne0$, the condition on $\pi_{j+1}=(b',b'')$ is $(s+t)b'+sb''=0$, whose solutions are the multiples of $(s,s+t)$. So a run either stays on the line through $(0,1)$, or alternates between the distinct lines through $(s,t)$ and $(s,s+t)$ with $s\ne0$. So Step A does not apply, and whether $D*\mathbb Z_2$ has the property for all $n$ is not decided here.
- *Computer check.* This is not part of the proof. For $p=2$, $n=18$ and $Q\in\{4,8,16\}$, a direct matrix computation of $\rho(g)$ from the commutators gave $\rho(g)=1$ for several hundred continuous $\rho:G_Q\to U_{18}(\mathbb F_2)$, with $\rho(x)$ and $\rho(z)$ random unitriangular, or with prescribed superdiagonals as in Step 2, and $\rho(y)$ a random unitriangular solution of $\rho(y)^{-1}\rho(x)\rho(y)=\rho(x)^{1-Q}$. Over $\mathbb F_4$ and $\mathbb F_{16}$ some such representations gave $\rho(g)\ne1$, as expected since $18<n_0(4)$. The same computation for the group $\langle x,y\mid x^2[x,y]\rangle*\mathbb Z_2$, the case $q(D)=2$, $f=\infty$, also gave $\rho(g)=1$ for every sampled representation into $U_{18}(\mathbb F_2)$, which suggests that this case may fail too; no proof is offered.

**Status.** The theorem and its corollaries are proved here, from the linked results on `main` and the cited location in Quadrelli's paper. No statement of them was found in the sources opened for [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md), and no separate literature search was made.
