**Data.** As in the [Caruso–Liu bounds](ramification-bounds-for-torsion-semistable-representations.md): $p>2$, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ finite, $v_K(K^\times)=\mathbb Z$, $e=v_K(p)$, integers $n,r\ge1$, and $T=L/L'$ killed by $p^n$ for $G_K$-stable lattices $L'\subseteq L$ in a semistable representation with Hodge–Tate weights in $\{0,\ldots,r\}$. Let $F/K$ be the field cut out by $T$, and write $G_K^{(\mu)}=G_K^{\mu-1}$ for Fontaine's numbering of the [upper ramification groups](ramification-filtration.md).

**Conjecture (Caruso–Liu).** Write
$$
\frac{r}{p-1}=p^{\alpha'}\beta',\qquad \alpha'\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\qquad \frac1p<\beta'\le1.
$$
Then:

1. $G_K^{(\mu)}$ acts trivially on $T$ for every $\mu>1+e(n+\alpha')+\max\{e\beta'-p^{-(n+\alpha')},\ e/(p-1)\}$;
2. $v_K(\mathfrak D_{F/K})<1+e(n+\alpha'+\beta')-p^{-(n+\alpha')}$

[@carusoliu2011ramification, Conjecture 1.2, p. 2]. Their theorem is the same statement with $nr/(p-1)$ in place of $r/(p-1)$ [@carusoliu2011ramification, Theorem 1.1, p. 2], so for $n=1$ the conjecture is their theorem, apart from a boundary case that their written proof does not cover (see that entry). The arXiv version states a weaker form, with cutoff $1+e(n+\alpha'+\max\{\beta',1/(p-1)\})$ in (1) and bound $1+e(n+\alpha'+\beta')$ in (2) [@carusoliu2008ramification, Conjecture 1.4, p. 2].

**Motivation.** For $r<p-1$ one has $\alpha'=0$ and $\beta'=r/(p-1)$, so the conjectured bounds take the form $e(n+r/(p-1))$ plus a term between $0$ and $1$. Earlier bounds, all under the hypothesis $r<p-1$, have this form [@carusoliu2011ramification, Introduction, display (1.0.1), p. 2].

**Status.**

- *Part (1)* is [Caruso's theorem](caruso-ramification-bound-for-torsion-semistable-representations.md) [@caruso2013phitau, Théorème 3.28, p. 48]. The source gives only a sketch of its proof.
- *Hattori's case.* For $r<p-1$, part (1), and part (2) when $r\ge2$, are Hattori's bounds [@hattori2009ramification, Theorem 1.1, Corollaries 1.2 and 1.3(1), pp. 2–3].
- *Part (2) from part (1).* Let $c$ be the cutoff in (1), and suppose $e\beta'-p^{-(n+\alpha')}\ge e/(p-1)$. Then $c$ is exactly the bound in (2), and (1) implies (2). The proof is below.
- *Not established.* Part (2) for $n\ge2$ and $\beta'<1/(p-1)+1/(ep^{n+\alpha'})$, which includes $r=1$, is not proved in the sources opened here. For $r=1$, Hattori's corollary gives $v_K(\mathfrak D_{F/K})<1+e(n+1/(p-1))$, which is larger than the conjectured bound by $p^{-n}$.

**Proof that (1) implies (2) in that range.** By compatibility of upper numbering with quotients, (1) gives $\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)^{(\mu)}=1$ for $\mu>c$. Fontaine's $u_{F/K}$, the largest $u$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)^{(u)}\ne1$, is therefore at most $c$. Fontaine shows $v_K(\mathfrak D_{F/K})=u_{F/K}-i_{F/K}$, where $i_{F/K}$ is the largest of the numbers $v_K(\mathfrak a_g)$ for $g\ne1$, with $\mathfrak a_g$ the ideal generated by all $g(x)-x$ for integral $x$ [@fontaine1985, §1.1, pp. 517–518; Proposition 1.3, p. 518]. If $F/K$ is ramified, a nontrivial inertia element $g$ acts trivially on the residue field, so $\mathfrak a_g$ lies in the maximal ideal and $i_{F/K}>0$. Then $v_K(\mathfrak D_{F/K})<u_{F/K}\le c$. If $F/K$ is unramified, then $v_K(\mathfrak D_{F/K})=0<c$. Under the assumed inequality, the maximum in $c$ is $e\beta'-p^{-(n+\alpha')}$, and $c=1+e(n+\alpha'+\beta')-p^{-(n+\alpha')}$. $\square$
