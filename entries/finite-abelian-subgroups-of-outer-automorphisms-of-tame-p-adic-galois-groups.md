**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $G = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, $V \subset G$ the wild inertia group, $V^2 = V^p[V,V]$, and $\mathcal{G} = G/V$. As in [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on norm-one units](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md), which follows Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, pp. 419–423], fix a splitting $G = V \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\mathcal{G}$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^p$, and a surjection $F = P_2 \rtimes \mathcal{G} \to G$, where $P_2$ is the free pro-$p$ $\mathcal{G}$-operator group on $x_0, x_1$. Let $\omega : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ give the action on $\mu_p$, so $\omega(\sigma) = 1$ and $\omega(\tau)$ has order $p - 1$. Let $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathcal{G}]]$, with the anti-involution $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, and let $\chi : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$ be the ring homomorphism with $\chi(\sigma) = -1$ and $\chi(\tau) = 1$. For $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$, $b(\theta) \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ is the exponent by which $\theta$ acts, through reciprocity, on the norm-one principal units of the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (that entry, part 1). Write $\mu_{p-1}$ for the group of $(p-1)$-th roots of unity in $\mathbb{Z}_p$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here, from a construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg and a splitting theorem of Nishio.

1. *Abelian subgroups.* For every $n \ge 1$, $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has a subgroup $\Gamma \cong (\mathbb{Z}/(p-1))^n$ whose elements all induce the identity on $G/V$. It maps isomorphically onto a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G)$, and $b$ maps it onto $\mu_{p-1}$.
2. *Tame fields.* Let $K \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ be a finite tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Then every element of $\Gamma$ maps $G_K$ onto itself, and restriction embeds $\Gamma$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$; the image maps isomorphically onto a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. Hence every finite abelian group of exponent dividing $p - 1$, for instance $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^n$ for every $n$, is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$, and to a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ acting trivially on the quotient by wild inertia.
3. *Not absolute Galois groups.* For $K$ as in part 2, neither $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ nor $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ is isomorphic, even as an abstract group, to the absolute Galois group of a field.
4. *Hodge–Tate.* If $p \ge 5$, some $\gamma \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ of order $p - 1$ has $b(\gamma)$ a primitive $(p-1)$-th root of unity, which is not a rational integer. So for the irreducible $2$-dimensional Hodge–Tate representation $W$ of [that entry](two-dimensional-representation-of-g-qp-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), $W \circ \gamma$ is not Hodge–Tate.

**Context.** Nishio asks whether every finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ is quasi-geometric, and for $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$ this asks whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ is torsion-free ([finite subgroups of outer automorphism groups](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), consequence 2). For odd $p$ his own finite subgroups need $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 3$, and for $\mathbb{Q}_p$ only an involution was known ([outer automorphisms of order two](involutive-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). The involution comes from Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's central unit $\varepsilon$ of $\Lambda$, and the automorphisms of the companion entry from units $\eta_c$; each gives one cyclic group. The new input here is a supply of independent central units, from Frobenius orbits of characters of tame inertia.

- By consequence 1 of the Nishio entry, no nontrivial element of the image of $\Gamma$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ lies in a quasi-geometric subgroup, in particular in any conjugate of $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$.
- Part 3 for $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ is not covered by the other entries when $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p] \le 4$. For degree at least $5$, Nishio's subgroups $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$ already show it (consequence 5 of that entry). For $\mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$, every $k$ with $p$ odd is covered by [centralizers of involutions](centralizers-of-involutions-in-p-adic-galois-automorphism-groups.md).
- In part 4, the companion argument only gave some $\theta$ with $W \circ \theta$ or $W \circ \theta^{-1}$ not Hodge–Tate. Here $\gamma$ itself has finite order. For $p = 3$, $\mu_2 = \{\pm 1\}$ and part 4 says nothing.
- Whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ has finite subgroups of other kinds, such as elements of order $p$, elements of prime order not dividing $p - 1$, or nonabelian finite subgroups, is not settled here. The case $p = 2$ is not covered, because the construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg assumes $p$ odd.

**Proof.**

*(a) Idempotents from inertia characters.* Let $\mathcal{T} \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ be the closed subgroup generated by $\tau$, the tame inertia group, and let $X$ be the group of continuous homomorphisms $\xi : \mathcal{T} \to \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$. Each has finite image, of order prime to $p$, so $\xi(\tau)$ lies in a finite field and the Frobenius orbit $O(\xi) = \{\xi^{p^k} : k \ge 0\}$ is finite. Write $\omega$ also for the restriction of $\omega$ to $\mathcal{T}$. It takes values in $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, so $O(\omega) = \{\omega\}$, and $O(1) = \{1\}$. For an orbit $O$ put $\iota(O) = \{\omega\xi^{-1} : \xi \in O\}$. As $(\omega\xi^{-1})^p = \omega\xi^{-p}$, this is again an orbit, and $\iota$ is an involution on the set of orbits that swaps $\{1\}$ and $\{\omega\}$.

Call a finite Galois extension $K'$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ a *level* if it is tamely ramified and contains $\zeta_p$ and the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Levels are cofinal among the finite tamely ramified Galois extensions, so $\Lambda$ is the inverse limit of the $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$, $\Gamma' = \mathrm{Gal}(K'/\mathbb{Q}_p)$, over levels $K'$; and $\omega$ and $\chi$ factor through each such $\Gamma'$. Let $K'$ be a level, $e'$ its ramification index and $\bar\sigma, \bar\tau$ the images of $\sigma, \tau$, which generate $\Gamma'$. Then $\bar\tau$ generates the inertia subgroup, of order $e'$, which is prime to $p$ and divisible by $p - 1$. The characters of $\langle\bar\tau\rangle$ are the $\xi \in X$ with $\xi(\tau)^{e'} = 1$; they form a set $X_{e'}$ that is a union of orbits. For $\xi \in X_{e'}$ and an orbit $O \subseteq X_{e'}$ put
$$t_\xi = \frac{1}{e'}\sum_{i=0}^{e'-1}\xi(\tau)^{-i}\,\bar\tau^{\,i} \in \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p[\Gamma'],\qquad T_O = \sum_{\xi \in O} t_\xi .$$
Since $e'$ is prime to $p$, the $t_\xi$ are orthogonal idempotents with sum $1$, and $\bar\tau t_\xi = \xi(\tau)t_\xi$. Each $t_\xi$ is nonzero, its coefficient of $1$ being $1/e'$. If $O = O(\xi)$ has $f$ elements, then $\xi(\tau)$ generates $\mathbb{F}_{p^f}$, and the coefficient of $\bar\tau^{\,i}$ in $T_O$ is $\frac{1}{e'}\sum_{k<f}\big(\xi(\tau)^{-i}\big)^{p^k}$, a trace from $\mathbb{F}_{p^f}$ to $\mathbb{F}_p$. So:

1. The $T_O$ with $O \subseteq X_{e'}$ are nonzero orthogonal idempotents of $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$ with sum $1$.
2. They are central. They commute with $\bar\tau$. Let $p'$ be an integer with $pp' \equiv 1 \pmod{e'}$; then $p' \equiv p^{m-1}$ for the order $m$ of $p$ modulo $e'$. Substituting $j = pi$ gives $\bar\sigma t_\xi\bar\sigma^{-1} = \frac{1}{e'}\sum_i \xi(\tau)^{-i}\bar\tau^{\,pi} = t_{\xi^{p'}}$, and $\xi^{p'} = \xi^{p^{m-1}} \in O(\xi)$. So conjugation by $\bar\sigma$ permutes the $t_\xi$ within each orbit and fixes $T_O$.
3. $T_O^* = T_{\iota(O)}$. Indeed $*$ is an $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear anti-automorphism, which we extend $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$-linearly, with $(\bar\tau^{\,i})^* = \omega(\tau)^i\bar\tau^{\,-i}$. Substituting $j = -i$, $t_\xi^* = \frac{1}{e'}\sum_j \big(\omega\xi^{-1}\big)(\tau)^{-j}\bar\tau^{\,j} = t_{\omega\xi^{-1}}$.
4. $\lambda_{K'} = t_\omega = T_{\{\omega\}}$, where $\lambda_{K'} = \frac{1}{e'}\sum_i \omega(\tau)^{-i}\bar\tau^{\,i}$ is the element of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422]. The idempotents $T_1, T_\omega$ of the companion entry, proof of part 2 (c), are $T_{\{1\}}$ and $T_{\{\omega\}}$.
5. *Compatibility.* Let $K' \subseteq K''$ be levels, with ramification indices $e' \mid e''$. Under $\mathbb{F}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(K''/\mathbb{Q}_p)] \to \mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$, grouping $i = r + e'k$ shows that $t_\xi$, for $\xi \in X_{e''}$, maps to $t_\xi$ if $\xi(\tau)^{e'} = 1$, and to $0$ otherwise, because then $\xi(\tau)^{-e'}$ is a nontrivial $(e''/e')$-th root of unity and $\sum_k \xi(\tau)^{-e'k} = 0$. So $T_O$ maps to $T_O$ if $O \subseteq X_{e'}$, and to $0$ otherwise.
6. $\chi(T_O) = 1$ if $O = \{1\}$, and $\chi(T_O) = 0$ otherwise, because $\chi(t_\xi) = \frac{1}{e'}\sum_i \xi(\tau)^{-i}$.

*(b) Central units.* Call a function $c$ from the set of orbits to $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ *admissible* if $c(O)\,c(\iota(O)) = 1$ for every $O$ and $c(\{\omega\}) = 1$. For such $c$, the elements $\sum_{O \subseteq X_{e'}} c(O)\,T_O$ of $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$ are compatible by 5, so they define $u_c \in \Lambda$. By 1 and 2, $u_c$ is a central unit and $u_c u_{c'} = u_{cc'}$. By 3, $u_c^* = u_{c \circ \iota}$, so by centrality $u_c u_c^* = u_{c \cdot (c \circ \iota)} = 1$. By 4 and centrality, $u_c\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}u_c\lambda_{K'} = c(\{\omega\})\,\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$ for Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's $\kappa_{K'}$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422]. By 6, $\chi(u_c) = c(\{1\}) = c(\{\omega\})^{-1} = 1$.

So $u_c$ has the three properties that the companion entry's lifting argument uses (proof of part 2 (d)): it is a central unit, $u_cu_c^* = 1$, and $u_c\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$ at every level. The companion entry's units $\eta_h = 1 + (h-1)P + (h^{-1}-1)Q$, for $h \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times$, also have them, with $\chi(\eta_h) = h$, where $P = T_{\{1\}}S_-$ and $Q = T_{\{\omega\}}S_-$ are orthogonal central idempotents (its proof of part 2 (c)). Multiplying out with $P^2 = P$, $Q^2 = Q$ and $PQ = 0$ gives $\eta_h\eta_{h'} = \eta_{hh'}$. The three properties are preserved by products of central units: $(uv)(uv)^* = uvv^*u^* = 1$, and $uv\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = u\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$.

*Explicit units.* Let $\ell_1, \ldots, \ell_{n-1}$ be distinct primes not dividing $p(p-1)$, choose $\xi_j \in X$ of order $\ell_j$, and put $O_j = O(\xi_j)$. The elements of $O_j$ have order $\ell_j$, and those of $\iota(O_j)$ have order $\ell_j(p-1)$, since $\omega$ has order $p - 1$, prime to $\ell_j$. So the orbits $O_1, \iota(O_1), \ldots, O_{n-1}, \iota(O_{n-1}), \{1\}, \{\omega\}$ are distinct. For $a = (a_1, \ldots, a_{n-1}) \in (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^{n-1}$, let $c_a(O_j) = a_j$, $c_a(\iota(O_j)) = a_j^{-1}$, and $c_a(O) = 1$ for all other $O$. Then $c_a$ is admissible and $c_ac_{a'} = c_{aa'}$. Put
$$U = \{\eta_h u_{c_a} : h \in \mathbb{F}_p^\times,\ a \in (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^{n-1}\}.$$
The map $(h, a) \mapsto \eta_hu_{c_a}$ is a homomorphism, as all these units are central. It is injective. Suppose $\eta_hu_{c_a} = 1$, and fix $j$ and a level whose ramification index is divisible by $\ell_j$, for instance one containing $p^{1/\ell_j}$, so that $O_j \subseteq X_{e'}$. By 1, $T_{O_j}P = T_{O_j}T_{\{1\}}S_- = 0$ and $T_{O_j}Q = 0$, so $T_{O_j}\eta_h = T_{O_j}$, while $T_{O_j}u_{c_a} = a_jT_{O_j}$. Hence $a_jT_{O_j} = T_{O_j}$, and $a_j = 1$ because $T_{O_j} \ne 0$. Then $\eta_h = 1$, and applying $\chi$ gives $h = 1$. So $U \cong (\mathbb{F}_p^\times)^n \cong (\mathbb{Z}/(p-1))^n$, every element of $U$ has the three properties, and $\chi(\eta_hu_{c_a}) = h$.

*(c) Automorphisms of $G/V^2$.* Let $u \in U$. The companion entry's proof of part 2 (d), with $u$ in place of $\eta_c$, gives $\psi_u : F \to F$ fixing $\sigma, \tau, x_0$ and sending $x_1$ to an element whose image in the Frattini quotient $\Lambda\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1$ of $P_2$ is $u\bar x_1$, and $\theta_u \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ agreeing with $\psi_u$ modulo $V^2$. Let $\Phi(u)$ be the automorphism of $G/V^2 = (V/V^2) \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ induced by $\theta_u$.

- $\Phi(u)$ fixes the images of $\sigma$ and $\tau$, which topologically generate the image of $\mathcal{G}$. So its restriction $L_u$ to $V/V^2$ commutes with the action of $\mathcal{G}$, and being continuous it is $\Lambda$-linear.
- The map $F \to G$ induces a $\Lambda$-linear surjection from $\Lambda\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1$ onto $V/V^2$, since $P_2$ maps onto $V$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422]. Write $\bar x_0, \bar x_1$ also for the images in $V/V^2$. Then $V/V^2 = \Lambda\bar x_0 + \Lambda\bar x_1$, $L_u(\bar x_0) = \bar x_0$ and $L_u(\bar x_1) = u\bar x_1$.
- As $G/V^2$ is generated by $V/V^2$ and the image of $\mathcal{G}$, $\Phi(u)$ is determined by $u$. For $u, u' \in U$, $L_uL_{u'}$ and $L_{uu'}$ are $\Lambda$-linear and agree on $\bar x_0$ and on $\bar x_1$, where $L_u(u'\bar x_1) = u'u\bar x_1 = uu'\bar x_1$. So $\Phi(u)\Phi(u') = \Phi(uu')$.
- If $\Phi(u) = 1$, then $(u-1)\bar x_1 = 0$, so $u = 1$, because $\bar x_1$ generates a free $\Lambda$-module in $V/V^2$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423].

So $\Phi : U \to \mathrm{Aut}(G/V^2)$ is an injective homomorphism, and each $\Phi(u)$ is induced by an automorphism of $G$.

*(d) Splitting.* Every automorphism of $G$ preserves $V$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)), hence $V^2$. Let $\Delta \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ be the set of automorphisms that induce an element of $\Phi(U)$ on $G/V^2$. It is a subgroup, and by (c) its image in $\mathrm{Aut}(G/V^2)$ is $\Phi(U) \cong U$, of order $(p-1)^n$, prime to $p$. Apply Nishio's Corollary 1.4, (ii) implies (i), with $(J, P, i, s) = (G, V, 2, 1)$, so that $P^{p,2,1} = V^2$ and the group of automorphisms preserving $V$ is all of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$. Its proof splits the exact sequence $1 \to \mathrm{Aut}_{G/V^2}(G) \to \Delta \to \Phi(U) \to 1$, whose kernel, the automorphisms inducing the identity on $G/V^2$, is pro-$p$ by his Theorem 1.2 [@nishio2026finite, Theorem 1.2, p. 6; Corollary 1.4 and its proof, p. 7]. This gives a subgroup $\Gamma \subseteq \Delta$ mapping isomorphically onto $\Phi(U)$. So $\Gamma \cong (\mathbb{Z}/(p-1))^n$. Each $\gamma \in \Gamma$ induces $\Phi(u)$ on $G/V^2$ for a unique $u = u(\gamma) \in U$, and in particular the identity on $G/V$.

*(e) Part 1.* $\Gamma$ is finite, so it maps isomorphically into $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ by consequence 1 of the [Nishio entry](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md). Let $\gamma \in \Gamma$ with $u(\gamma) = \eta_hu_{c_a}$. Then $\gamma$ agrees with $\psi_{u(\gamma)}$ modulo $V^2$. The companion entry's proof of part 2 (e) uses only this, and gives $b(\gamma) \equiv \chi(u(\gamma)) = h \pmod p$. As $b$ is a homomorphism and $\gamma^{p-1} = 1$, $b(\gamma) \in \mu_{p-1}$. Reduction modulo $p$ is a bijection from $\mu_{p-1}$ onto $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, so $b(\gamma)$ is the root of unity lifting $h$. As $h$ ranges over $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, $b(\Gamma) = \mu_{p-1}$.

*(f) Part 2.* As $K$ is tamely ramified, $V \subseteq G_K$. For $g \in G_K$ and $\gamma \in \Gamma$, $\gamma(g) \in gV \subseteq G_K$, and the same holds for $\gamma^{-1} \in \Gamma$, so $\gamma(G_K) = G_K$. Restriction is a homomorphism $\Gamma \to \mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$. It is injective by the argument of part 2 of the [involution entry](involutive-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md): if $\gamma$ is the identity on $G_K$, then for $g \in G$ the element $g^{-1}\gamma(g)$ centralizes the open subgroup $G_K \cap g^{-1}G_Kg$, so it is $1$ because $G$ is slim, and $\gamma = 1$. The image is a finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$, so it maps isomorphically into $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ by consequence 1 of the Nishio entry, which holds for every finite $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$. Its elements act trivially on $G_K/V$, and $V$ is the wild inertia group of $K$. Finally, a finite abelian group of exponent dividing $p - 1$ is a product of $n$ cyclic groups of orders dividing $p - 1$, for some $n$, so it is isomorphic to a subgroup of $(\mathbb{Z}/(p-1))^n$.

*(g) Part 3.* Since $p - 1$ is even, part 2 gives subgroups of order $4$, isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/2 \times \mathbb{Z}/2$, in $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ and in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. An isomorphism of abstract groups carries them to finite subgroups of order $4$. By the [Artin–Schreier theorem](artin-schreier-theorem.md), a nontrivial finite subgroup of the absolute Galois group of a field has order $2$, as in consequence 5 of the Nishio entry.

*(h) Part 4.* Let $g$ generate $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, and let $\gamma \in \Gamma$ with $u(\gamma) = \eta_g$. It has order $p - 1$, the order of $\eta_g$ in $U$, and $b(\gamma)$ is the root of unity lifting $g$, which has order $p - 1 \ge 4$. A rational integer $m$ with $m^{p-1} = 1$ is $\pm 1$, of order at most $2$. So $b(\gamma)$ is not a rational integer, and by part 2 of the [two-dimensional entry](two-dimensional-representation-of-g-qp-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), $W \circ \gamma$ is not Hodge–Tate. $\square$

**Remark.** The subgroup $\Gamma$ is not canonical: its elements are determined only modulo the pro-$p$ group of automorphisms inducing the identity on $G/V^2$, and the splitting chooses among them.
