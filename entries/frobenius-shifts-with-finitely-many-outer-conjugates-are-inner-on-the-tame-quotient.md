**Setting.** Use the Setting and notation of [surface generators modulo the Frattini subgroup](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md), called the surface-generator entry below, which is that of [units of the tame group algebra](tame-group-algebra-units-give-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md). So $p$ is odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ has degree $d$ and residue field of order $q = p^f$, $G = G_K = F/(\varrho)$ with wild inertia $P_K$, and $\Gamma = G/P_K = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$ is identified with the group generated by $\sigma, \tau$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$. Tame inertia $I$ is the closed subgroup generated by $\tau$, and $x \mapsto \tau^x$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell \to I$. Every element of $\Gamma$ is uniquely $\tau^x\sigma^y$ with $x \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, and $\tau^x\sigma^y\tau^{x'}\sigma^{y'} = \tau^{x + q^yx'}\sigma^{y + y'}$, where $q^y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ is defined by continuity. Also $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\Gamma]]$, $\omega : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p$ is the ring homomorphism given by the action on $\mu_p$, $\lambda \mapsto \lambda^*$ is the anti-automorphism with $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, and
$$\bar V = P_K/P_K^{(2)} = \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus \Lambda\bar x_d,$$
with $\Lambda\bar x_i \cong \Lambda$ for $i \ge 1$ and $\lambda\bar x_0 = \omega(\lambda)\bar x_0$ (part 1 there). For $d \ge 3$ let $(x_s, x_t)$ be the surface pair, and for $\lambda \in \Lambda^\times$ let $\Theta_\lambda$ be the action on $\bar V$ of the automorphism $\theta_\lambda$ of parts 2 and 3 there: it is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_0$ and the $\bar x_i$ with $i \notin \{s, t\}$, and maps $\bar x_s \mapsto \lambda\bar x_s$ and $\bar x_t \mapsto \lambda'\bar x_t$, where $\lambda' = (\lambda^*)^{-1}$. For $m \ge 1$ prime to $p$ let $I_m$, $\varepsilon_m$ and $\eta_m = \varepsilon_m(\gamma_m - 1)$, with $\gamma_m = \sigma^{k_m\pi_p}$, be as in the Setting and Lemma 1 of the surface-generator entry.

*Tame twists.* As in the Setting of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), every $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ preserves $P_K$ and $P_K^{(2)}$ and induces an automorphism $\beta$ of $\Gamma$ and an additive automorphism $\bar\theta$ of $\bar V$ with $\bar\theta(\lambda v) = \beta(\lambda)\bar\theta(v)$, where $\beta$ also denotes the continuous ring automorphism of $\Lambda$ extending $\beta$. By the Setting of [tame levels](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-on-tame-levels.md), $\beta = \beta_{u,a}$ with $\beta_{u,a}(\tau) = \tau^u$ and $\beta_{u,a}(\sigma) = \sigma\tau^a$ for unique $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$, the *tame twist* of $\theta$, and $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and $\beta_{u,a}\beta_{u',a'} = \beta_{uu',\,a + ua'}$. The map $\nu \mapsto p^\nu$, $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$, is injective (Setting of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md)). Write $\overline{\langle q\rangle} = \{q^y : y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\}$.

*Inner automorphisms.* The automorphism $\mathrm{inn}(g)$ induces on $\Gamma$ conjugation by the image $\bar g$ of $g$. If $\bar g = \tau^x\sigma^y$, its tame twist is $q^y$. From $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$ we get $\sigma^{-1}\tau\sigma = \tau^{q^{-1}}$, so conjugation by $\tau^c$ maps $\sigma$ to $\tau^c\sigma\tau^{-c} = \sigma\tau^{cq^{-1}}\tau^{-c}$; that is, it is $\beta_{1,\,c(q^{-1} - 1)}$. As $q^{-1} - 1 = -q^{-1}(q - 1)$, the automorphisms $\beta_{1,a}$ with $a \in (q - 1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ are exactly the conjugations by elements of $I$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $p$ be odd and $d \ge 3$, and let $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ have only finitely many conjugates of its class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. Then its tame twist belongs to $\overline{\langle q\rangle}$, and $\theta$ induces an inner automorphism of $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{tr}}/K)$: there is $g \in G_K$ such that $\mathrm{inn}(g) \circ \theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$.

In particular, if $u = 1$, then $\theta$ induces $\beta_{1,a}$ with $a \in (q - 1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here. Let $p$ be odd and $d \ge 3$.

1. Every nonidentity finite-order element of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has infinitely many conjugates.
2. Every finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ is trivial.

This concerns the order of the outer class, even when its initial automorphism representative has infinite order.

**Lemma.** This is proved here. Let $w, v \in \Lambda^\times$ satisfy $w^{p^j} \to 1$ and $v^{p^j} \to 1$ as $j \to \infty$, and $w^{e'} = v^{e'}$ for an integer $e'$ prime to $p$. Then $w = v$.

*Proof.* $\Lambda^\times$ is a profinite group (surface-generator entry, proof of Lemma 4 (b)). For $x \in \Lambda^\times$ with $x^{p^j} \to 1$, the map $n \mapsto x^n$ is continuous for the $p$-adic topology on $\mathbb{Z}$: for an open normal subgroup $W$ of $\Lambda^\times$, $x^{p^j} \in W$ for some $j$, and then $x^n \in x^{n'}W$ whenever $n \equiv n' \pmod{p^j}$. So it extends to a continuous homomorphism $\mathbb{Z}_p \to \Lambda^\times$, $\alpha \mapsto x^\alpha$, with $(x^{n})^{\alpha} = x^{n\alpha}$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, as both sides are continuous in $\alpha$ and agree on $\mathbb{Z}$. Let $\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}_p$ with $e'\alpha = 1$. Then $w = (w^{e'})^\alpha = (v^{e'})^\alpha = v$. $\square$

The Lemma plays the role of Lemma 4 (b) of the surface-generator entry, with conjugation by $\mu$ in place of commutation with $\mu$.

**Proof of the Theorem.**

*Step 0: three properties for an arbitrary tame twist.* Write $\beta=\beta_{u,a}$. We do not assume $u\in\overline{\langle q\rangle}$ or normalize it to one.

- (i) $\beta(I_m)=I_m$, since $\beta(\tau)=\tau^u$ and $u$ is a unit. The element $\varepsilon_m$ is the normalized Haar measure of $I_m$, so $\beta(\varepsilon_m)=\varepsilon_m$. Explicitly, $\beta$ permutes the elements in each finite image of $I_m$ and preserves its normalized sum, compatibly between finite quotients.
- (ii) $\omega \circ \beta = \omega$ and $\beta(\lambda^*) = \beta(\lambda)^*$. Indeed, $\theta$ preserves the cyclotomic character ([invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md)), hence the action of $G$ on $\mu_p$, which factors through $\Gamma$ as $\omega$; so $\omega(\beta(\rho)) = \omega(\rho)$ for $\rho \in \Gamma$, and then $\beta(\rho^*) = \omega(\rho)\beta(\rho)^{-1} = \omega(\beta(\rho))\beta(\rho)^{-1} = \beta(\rho)^*$. Both identities extend to $\Lambda$ by linearity and continuity.
- (iii) $\beta(\eta_m) = \eta_m$ for every $m$ prime to $p$. Put $k = k_m$, so $m \mid q^k - 1$. By induction on $n$, $(\sigma\tau^a)^n = \sigma^n\tau^{as_n}$ with $s_n = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}q^{-i}$, since $\sigma^n\tau^{as_n}\sigma\tau^a = \sigma^{n+1}(\sigma^{-1}\tau^{as_n}\sigma)\tau^a = \sigma^{n+1}\tau^{a(q^{-1}s_n + 1)}$. So $\beta(\sigma^k) = \sigma^k\tau^{b}$ with $b = as_k$. In $\Gamma/I_m$ the images of $\sigma^k$ and $\tau$ commute, as $\sigma^k\tau\sigma^{-k} = \tau^{q^k} \in \tau I_m$. Hence $(\sigma^k\tau^b)^z \equiv \sigma^{kz}\tau^{b\bar z}$ modulo $I_m$ for all $z \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, where $\bar z$ is the image of $z$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$: both sides are continuous in $z$ and agree on $\mathbb{Z}$. The image of $\pi_p$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ is $0$, so $\beta(\gamma_m) = \beta(\sigma^k)^{\pi_p} \in \sigma^{k\pi_p}I_m = \gamma_mI_m$. Since $\varepsilon_m$ is central and $w\varepsilon_m = \varepsilon_m$ for $w \in I_m$ (Lemma 1 (a)), $\varepsilon_m\beta(\gamma_m) = \varepsilon_m\gamma_m$. With (i), $\beta(\eta_m) = \varepsilon_m(\beta(\gamma_m) - 1) = \eta_m$.

*Step 1: commuting with the $\Theta_\nu$.* By Step 1 of the proof of part 1 of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), which uses only the Setting there (odd $p$, $d \ge 3$) and no condition on the tame twist, there is a subgroup $L_0$ of finite index of $\Lambda^\times$ with $\Theta_\lambda\bar\theta = \bar\theta\Theta_\lambda$ for all $\lambda \in L_0$. Its normal core has some finite index $e$; write $e = p^be'$ with $p \nmid e'$. Then $\lambda^e$ lies in the normal core, hence in $L_0$, for every $\lambda \in \Lambda^\times$, so
$$\Theta_\nu\bar\theta = \bar\theta\,\Theta_\nu \qquad (\nu \in (\Lambda^\times)^e). \tag{C}$$

By Step 0 of the proof of the Theorem of the surface-generator entry, which uses only $\gamma = \sigma^{\pi_p}$ and its Lemma 2, $\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$ is the set of $v \in \bar V$ with $\rho v = \omega(\rho)v$ for all $\rho \in \Gamma$. If $v$ lies in it, then $\rho\,\bar\theta(v) = \bar\theta(\beta^{-1}(\rho)v) = \omega(\rho)\bar\theta(v)$ by (ii), so $\bar\theta(\mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0) = \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$. For $j \ge 1$ write $\bar\theta(\bar x_j) = \mu_{j0}\bar x_0 + \sum_{i \ge 1}\mu_{ji}\bar x_i$ with $\mu_{j0} \in \mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mu_{ji} \in \Lambda$. As $\beta(\lambda)\bar x_0 = \omega(\lambda)\bar x_0$ by (ii),
$$\bar\theta(\lambda\bar x_j) = \beta(\lambda)\bar\theta(\bar x_j) = \omega(\lambda)\mu_{j0}\bar x_0 + \sum_{i \ge 1}\beta(\lambda)\mu_{ji}\bar x_i.$$
Compare the $\bar x_s$-coordinates of the two sides of (C), evaluated at $\bar x_j$, for $\nu \in (\Lambda^\times)^e$. Since $\Theta_\nu(\sum_i\lambda_i\bar x_i)$ has $\bar x_s$-coordinate $\lambda_s\nu$:

- (R1) for $j \ge 1$, $j \notin \{s, t\}$: $\mu_{js}\nu = \mu_{js}$, as $\Theta_\nu$ fixes $\bar x_j$;
- (R2) $\mu_{ss}\nu = \beta(\nu)\mu_{ss}$, as $\bar\theta(\Theta_\nu\bar x_s) = \bar\theta(\nu\bar x_s)$;
- (R3) $\mu_{ts}\nu = \beta(\nu')\mu_{ts}$, as $\bar\theta(\Theta_\nu\bar x_t) = \bar\theta(\nu'\bar x_t)$.

*Step 2: $\mu_{ss}$ is a unit.* Fix $m$ prime to $p$ with $(p - 1) \mid m$ and $c \in \mathbb{F}_p \setminus \{0, 1\}$, which exists as $p \ge 3$. The element $1 + c\eta_m$ is a central unit (surface-generator entry, Step 2), so $\nu = (1 + c\eta_m)^e \in (\Lambda^\times)^e$. By (iii), $\beta(\nu) = \nu$, and by (ii), $\beta(\nu') = (\beta(\nu)^*)^{-1} = \nu'$. By Step 2 of the proof of the Theorem of the surface-generator entry, $\nu - 1$ is $\eta_m^{p^b}$ times a central unit, $\nu'$ is central, and $\nu - \nu'$ is $\eta_m^{2p^b}$ times a central unit. So (R1) gives $\eta_m^{p^b}\mu_{js} = 0$ and (R3), which reads $\mu_{ts}\nu = \mu_{ts}\nu'$, gives $\eta_m^{2p^b}\mu_{ts} = 0$. By Lemma 1 (c) there, $\varepsilon_m\mu_{js} = \varepsilon_m\mu_{ts} = 0$, and letting $m \to \infty$ along multiples of $p - 1$, $\mu_{js} = \lim\varepsilon_m\mu_{js} = 0$ and $\mu_{ts} = 0$ by Lemma 1 (a). So the $\bar x_s$-coordinate of $\bar\theta(\bar x_j)$ is $0$ for every $j \ge 1$ with $j \ne s$, and also for $\bar\theta(\bar x_0) \in \mathbb{F}_p\bar x_0$.

As $\bar\theta$ is surjective, $\bar x_s = \bar\theta(w)$ for some $w = c_0\bar x_0 + \sum_{j \ge 1}\lambda_j\bar x_j$. The $\bar x_s$-coordinate of $\bar\theta(w) = c_0\bar\theta(\bar x_0) + \sum_j\beta(\lambda_j)\bar\theta(\bar x_j)$ is $\beta(\lambda_s)\mu_{ss}$, so $\beta(\lambda_s)\mu_{ss} = 1$. In a finite ring a one-sided inverse is two-sided: if $xy = 1$, then $r \mapsto yr$ is injective, hence bijective, so $yr = 1$ for some $r$, and $r = xyr = x$. Applying this in every $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma/U]$, whose kernels intersect in $0$, gives $\mu_{ss}\beta(\lambda_s) = 1$. So $\mu_{ss}$ is a unit.

*Step 3: twisted commutation.* Let $z \in \Lambda$ and $m$ prime to $p$. Then $1 + \eta_mz$ is a unit (surface-generator entry, Step 3), so $\nu_z = (1 + \eta_mz)^e \in (\Lambda^\times)^e$, and $\beta(\nu_z) = (1 + \eta_m\beta(z))^e$ by (iii). Since $\eta_m$ is central and $\Lambda$ has characteristic $p$, $(1 + \eta_mz)^{p^b} = 1 + \eta_m^{p^b}z^{p^b} =: u_z$, and $\nu_z = u_z^{e'}$. So (R2) says $(\mu_{ss}u_z\mu_{ss}^{-1})^{e'} = u_{\beta(z)}^{e'}$. Both $\mu_{ss}u_z\mu_{ss}^{-1}$ and $u_{\beta(z)}$ have $p^j$-th powers tending to $1$: $u_z^{p^j} = 1 + \eta_m^{p^{b+j}}z^{p^{b+j}}$, and $\eta_m^nz^n$ lies in any given open two-sided ideal of $\Lambda$ for large $n$, as $\eta_m^n$ does (Lemma 1 (b)). By the Lemma, $\mu_{ss}u_z\mu_{ss}^{-1} = u_{\beta(z)}$, that is,
$$\eta_m^{p^b}\big(\mu_{ss}z^{p^b} - \beta(z)^{p^b}\mu_{ss}\big) = 0.$$
Lemma 1 (c) gives $\varepsilon_m(\mu_{ss}z^{p^b} - \beta(z)^{p^b}\mu_{ss}) = 0$, and letting $m \to \infty$,
$$\mu_{ss}\,z^{p^b} = \beta(z)^{p^b}\,\mu_{ss} \qquad (z \in \Lambda). \tag{T}$$

*Step 4: a commutative block.* Let $\psi : I \to \bar{\mathbb{F}}_p^\times$ be a continuous character of order exactly $q - 1$, which exists as $I/I_{q-1}$ is cyclic of order $q - 1$, and let $O = \{\psi^{p^i} : i \ge 0\}$. Let $h = h_O$ be the idempotent of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), section "Idempotents". It is central in $\Lambda$ by (ii) there, nonzero by (v) there, and every $\chi \in O$ has order $q - 1$.

(H) *If $X, X' \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ and $X \equiv X' \pmod{(q - 1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'}$, then $h\tau^X = h\tau^{X'}$.* Indeed, at each level $m'$ used to define $h_O$, the element $h_{O,(m')}(\tau^X - \tau^{X'})$ of $\mathbb{F}_p[I/I_{m'}]$ has value $\chi(\tau)^X - \chi(\tau)^{X'} = 0$ at every character $\chi \in O$, as $\chi^{q-1} = 1$, and value $0$ at every other character, as $\chi(h_{O,(m')}) = 0$ there. Since $p \nmid m'$, $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p[I/I_{m'}]$ is the product of copies of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ indexed by the characters, so this element is $0$, and hence $h(\tau^X - \tau^{X'}) = 0$.

*$h\Lambda$ is commutative.* For $\rho = \tau^x\sigma^y$ and $\rho' = \tau^{x'}\sigma^{y'}$ in $\Gamma$, $h\rho\rho' = h\tau^{x + q^yx'}\sigma^{y+y'}$. As $q^y \equiv 1 \pmod{(q - 1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'}$ (true for $y \in \mathbb{Z}$, and the set of such units is closed), (H) gives $h\rho\rho' = h\tau^{x + x'}\sigma^{y + y'}$, which is symmetric in $\rho$ and $\rho'$. As $h$ is central, $(h\rho)(h\rho') = h\rho\rho'$. The ring $h\Lambda$ is the closure of the span of the $h\rho$, so it is commutative.

*Step 5: detecting the inertia multiplier.* Put $n=p^b$. Apply (T) first with $z=\tau$, so
$$\mu_{ss}\tau^n=\tau^{un}\mu_{ss}.$$
Multiplying by $h$ and cancelling the unit $h\mu_{ss}$ in the commutative ring $h\Lambda$ gives $h\tau^n=h\tau^{un}$. Property (v) of [tame twists](tame-twists-of-automorphisms-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), with $m=q-1$, realizes the image of $h$ as $h_{O,(q-1)}$ in an embedded copy of $\mathbb F_p[I/I_{q-1}]$ in a finite quotient group algebra. Evaluate the equality there at $\psi$. Its value on $h$ is one, so
$$\psi(\tau)^{n(u-1)}=1.$$
Since $\psi(\tau)$ has order $q-1$ and $n$ is prime to $q-1$, this gives $u\equiv1\pmod{(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'}$.

The [odd-prime tame-twist theorem](odd-prime-tame-twists-and-finite-outer-conjugacy.md) gives $u=p^\nu$ for some $\nu\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, including at $p=3$. The order of $p$ modulo $p^f-1=q-1$ is exactly $f$: $p^f\equiv1$, while $0<p^j-1<p^f-1$ for $0<j<f$. Thus $\nu\in f\widehat{\mathbb Z}$, and $u=q^y$ for some $y\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$.

*Step 6: detecting the Frobenius shift.* Apply (T) with $z = \sigma$. As $\beta(\sigma)^n = (\sigma\tau^a)^n = \sigma^n\tau^{as_n}$ (proof of (iii)),
$$\mu_{ss}\sigma^n = \sigma^n\tau^{as_n}\mu_{ss}.$$
Multiply by the central idempotent $h$: $(h\mu_{ss})(h\sigma^n) = (h\sigma^n)(h\tau^{as_n})(h\mu_{ss})$ in the commutative ring $h\Lambda$, in which $h\mu_{ss}$ and $h\sigma^n$ are units, with inverses $h\mu_{ss}^{-1}$ and $h\sigma^{-n}$. So $h = h\tau^{as_n}$, that is, $h(\tau^{as_n} - 1) = 0$ in $\Lambda$.

Evaluate in the same finite inertia group algebra as in Step 5. This gives $\psi(\tau)^{as_n}=1$, so $as_n\in(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$. Finally $q^{-1}\equiv1\pmod{(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'}$, hence $s_n\equiv n=p^b$, a unit modulo $q-1$. Therefore $a\in(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$.

Choose $g_1\in G$ mapping to $\sigma^{-y}$. Then $\mathrm{inn}(g_1)\circ\theta$ induces $\beta_{1,q^{-y}a}$. Since $q^{-y}a\in(q-1)\widehat{\mathbb Z}'$, this is conjugation by some $\tau^c$, by the Setting. For $g_2\in G$ mapping to $\tau^{-c}$, the automorphism $\mathrm{inn}(g_2g_1)\circ\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** Let $x\in\mathrm{Out}(G)$ have finite order $r$ and finitely many conjugates. If $p\mid r$, then $x^{r/p}$ has order $p$ and finitely many conjugates, contrary to conclusion 2 of the [odd-prime theorem](odd-prime-tame-twists-and-finite-outer-conjugacy.md). Hence $p\nmid r$.

Choose an automorphism representing $x$, and apply the Theorem to adjust it by an inner automorphism to a representative $\alpha$ inducing the identity on $\Gamma$. Since $x^r=1$, write $\alpha^r=\mathrm{inn}(g)$ with $g\in G$. Its induced action on $\Gamma$ is the identity, so the image of $g$ lies in $Z(\Gamma)=1$, and $g\in P_K$.

Let $\pi'\in\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ have component zero in $\mathbb Z_p$ and one in every $\mathbb Z_\ell$ for $\ell\ne p$. Take the power $\alpha'=\alpha^{\pi'}$ in the procyclic closed subgroup generated by $\alpha$ in the profinite group $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$. The closure of $\langle\alpha^r\rangle$ lies in $\mathrm{Inn}(P_K)$, which is pro-$p$, so
$$ (\alpha')^r=(\alpha^r)^{\pi'}=1.$$
As $p\nmid r$, $\pi'\equiv1\pmod r$; hence the outer class of $\alpha'$ is still $x$. Conclusion 1 of the odd-prime theorem applies to this finite-order automorphism with finitely many outer conjugates and gives $\alpha'=1$. Thus $x=1$, proving part 1. Every element of a finite normal subgroup has finite order and finitely many conjugates, so part 2 follows. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The mechanism.* The idempotents $\varepsilon_m$ and the central elements $\eta_m$ are fixed for every $\beta_{u,a}$ induced from $G_K$, even when inertia is not fixed pointwise. This permits (T) before normalizing $u$. The commutative block for characters of order $q-1$ detects both $u\bmod(q-1)$ and $a\bmod(q-1)$; the order of $p$ modulo $q-1$ then upgrades the odd-prime restriction $u\in\overline{\langle p\rangle}$ to $u\in\overline{\langle q\rangle}$. The argument works when $p\mid f$ as well as when $p\nmid f$.
- *Finite-order representatives.* The corollary only constructs a finite-order representative after finite outer conjugacy has forced the tame action to be inner. It does not assert that arbitrary prime-to-$p$ torsion in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has such representatives.
- *Dependencies.* From the odd-prime theorem we use its tame-twist restriction and conclusions 1 and 2 only. Their proofs do not use the rival-copy reduction or this entry. Its conclusion 3, which invokes the rival-copy reduction and hence this entry, is not used here.
- *Scope.* The theorem and corollary include $p=3$ and every residue degree. They leave open whether every infinite-order class with finitely many conjugates is trivial, and the infinite-index rival-copy case of [Tsujimura's question](p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md). Degree at most two and $p=2$ are outside the statement. These are proofs from the linked entries; no novelty is claimed.
