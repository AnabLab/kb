**Setting.** Let $K$ be a field with algebraic closure $\bar K$, and let $X$ be a curve of type $(g, n)$ over $K$, as in [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md). So $X_{\bar K} = X \otimes_K \bar K$ is a proper smooth connected curve of genus $g$ over $\bar K$ with $n$ distinct points removed. The *geometric fundamental group* of $X$ is $\Delta_X = \pi_1(X_{\bar K})$; base points are omitted throughout.

For integers $g, n \ge 0$, let $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n}$ be the profinite group with generators $x_1, y_1, \ldots, x_g, y_g, \sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_n$ and the single relation
$$[x_1, y_1] \cdots [x_g, y_g] \, \sigma_1 \cdots \sigma_n = 1, \qquad [x, y] = x y x^{-1} y^{-1}.$$
That is, $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n}$ is the quotient of the free profinite group on these $2g + n$ letters by the closed normal subgroup generated by the left-hand side. For a set $\mathbb{L}$ of primes, the pro-$\mathbb{L}$ group with this presentation is defined in the same way, starting from the free pro-$\mathbb{L}$ group. Write $\widehat{F}_r$ for the free profinite group of rank $r$.

**Theorem (SGA 1).**

1. *The fundamental exact sequence.* Let $X$ be a quasi-compact $K$-scheme that is geometrically connected over $K$. Then the sequence
$$1 \to \pi_1(X_{\bar K}) \to \pi_1(X) \to G_K \to 1$$
induced by $X_{\bar K} \to X \to \mathrm{Spec}\, K$ is exact [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. IX, Theorem 6.1, p. 195]. If $X$ has a point whose residue field is purely inseparable over $K$, for instance a $K$-rational point, the sequence splits [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. IX, Corollary 6.4, p. 196]. These splittings are the sections that the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) is about.
2. *Riemann existence theorem.* Let $X$ be a connected scheme locally of finite type over $\mathbb{C}$. Then $X' \mapsto X'^{\mathrm{an}}$ is an equivalence from the finite étale coverings of $X$ to those of the analytic space $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. XII, Theorem 5.1, p. 251]. Hence $\pi_1(X)$ is canonically isomorphic to the profinite completion of the topological fundamental group of $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. XII, Corollary 5.2, p. 253].
3. *Generators and relation.* Let $k$ be a separably closed field of characteristic $p \ge 0$, let $Y$ be a proper smooth connected curve of genus $g$ over $k$, and let $U$ be $Y$ with $n$ distinct closed points $a_1, \ldots, a_n$ removed. The tame fundamental group $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)$ classifies the coverings of $U$ that are tamely ramified at the $a_j$. It is generated by $2g + n$ elements $x_i, y_i, \sigma_j$ that satisfy the relation above, where $\sigma_j$ generates an inertia group at $a_j$. Let $p'$ be the set of primes other than $p$. Then the maximal pro-$p'$ quotient $\pi_1^{p'}(U)$ is the pro-$p'$ group with this presentation [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. XIII, Corollary 2.12, p. 290]. For proper $Y$ over an algebraically closed field, the prime-to-$p$ statement was proved earlier, by specialization from characteristic $0$ [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. X, Theorem 3.8 and Corollary 3.10, p. 217].
4. *Characteristic $0$.* If $p = 0$, every finite extension is tamely ramified at a discrete valuation whose residue field has characteristic $0$ [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. X, discussion before Lemma 3.6, p. 214]. So $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U) = \pi_1(U)$, and $p'$ is the set of all primes. Part 3 then gives $\pi_1(U) \cong \widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n}$. Its proof reduces to $k = \mathbb{C}$, where part 2 identifies $\pi_1(U)$ with the profinite completion of the fundamental group of a compact Riemann surface of genus $g$ with $n$ points removed, computed by topology [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. XIII, proof of Corollary 2.12, p. 291]. In particular, $\Delta_X \cong \widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n}$ for every curve $X$ of type $(g, n)$ over a field of characteristic $0$.
5. *Changing the algebraically closed base field.* Let $Y$ be proper and connected over an algebraically closed field $k$, and let $k'$ be an algebraically closed extension of $k$. Then $\pi_1(Y \otimes_k k') \to \pi_1(Y)$ is an isomorphism [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. X, Corollary 1.8, p. 204]. Without properness this fails in characteristic $p > 0$. The Artin–Schreier coverings $x^p - x = st$ of the affine line, with $s$ transcendental over $k$, give an étale covering of $\mathbb{A}^1_{k'}$ that does not come from $\mathbb{A}^1_k$ [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. X, Remark 1.10, p. 205]. So $\bar K$ in part 1 cannot be replaced by an arbitrary algebraically closed extension of $K$ [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. IX, Remark 6.3, p. 196]. A remark added by Raynaud in 2003 notes that $\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1_k)$ is not topologically finitely generated when $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $p > 0$. By the theorems of Raynaud and Harbater on Abhyankar's conjecture, a finite group is the Galois group of a connected étale covering of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$ exactly when it is generated by its Sylow $p$-subgroups [@grothendieck1971sga1, Exp. XIII, Remark, pp. 292–293].

**Consequences.** These are proved here, from parts 3 and 4.

1. *Freeness.* If $n \ge 1$, then $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n}$ is free profinite on $x_1, y_1, \ldots, x_g, y_g, \sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_{n-1}$, so $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n} \cong \widehat{F}_{2g+n-1}$. The same holds for the pro-$\mathbb{L}$ groups. Hence, for a curve $U$ of type $(g, n)$ with $n \ge 1$, $\pi_1(U) \cong \widehat{F}_{2g+n-1}$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, and $\pi_1^{p'}(U)$ is free pro-$p'$ of rank $2g + n - 1$ in characteristic $p > 0$. For example, over a field of characteristic $0$, the geometric fundamental groups of $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ and of an elliptic curve minus its origin are both $\widehat{F}_2$. For the first, take $a_1 = 0$, $a_2 = 1$ and $a_3 = \infty$: the free generators $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ generate inertia groups at $0$ and $1$.
2. *Abelianization.* $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g+n-1}$ if $n \ge 1$, and $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0}^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}$. For the pro-$p'$ group, $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ is replaced by $\prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$. This matches the rank $b^{(1)}$ at primes $\ell \ne p$ in [Tamagawa's theorem on tame fundamental groups](tame-fundamental-groups-in-positive-characteristic.md).
3. *Hyperbolicity.* $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n}$ is abelian exactly when $(g, n)$ is one of the non-hyperbolic types $(0,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(0,2)$, $(1,0)$. For these types it is $1$, $1$, $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^2$. So a curve over a field of characteristic $0$ is hyperbolic exactly when its geometric fundamental group is not abelian. For instance, over fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, the hypothesis in [Tamagawa's theorem](tamagawa-grothendieck-conjecture-for-affine-curves.md) that $\bar\Pi = \pi_1(U \otimes_k k^{\mathrm{sep}})$ is not abelian amounts to $U$ being hyperbolic.
4. *What the abstract group remembers.* Let $U$ and $U'$ be curves of types $(g, n)$ and $(g', n')$ over algebraically closed fields of characteristic $0$. Then $\pi_1(U) \cong \pi_1(U')$ if and only if one of the following holds:
   - $n, n' \ge 1$ and $2g + n = 2g' + n'$;
   - $n = n' = 0$ and $g = g'$;
   - $\{(g, n), (g', n')\} = \{(0, 0), (0, 1)\}$.

   So, apart from $\mathbb{P}^1$ and $\mathbb{A}^1$, the profinite group determines the Euler characteristic $2 - 2g - n$ and whether the curve is proper, and nothing more. For example, $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ and an elliptic curve minus a point have isomorphic fundamental groups. A proper curve of genus $g \ge 1$ and the same curve minus a point have isomorphic abelianized fundamental groups, but their fundamental groups are not isomorphic.

**Proofs.**

*Consequence 1.* Let $\Phi$ be the free profinite group on all $2g + n$ letters, and put $w = [x_1, y_1] \cdots [x_g, y_g] \, \sigma_1 \cdots \sigma_{n-1}$. The relator is $w \sigma_n$, and $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n} = \Phi / N$, where $N$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by $w\sigma_n$. Let $F$ be the free profinite group on the letters other than $\sigma_n$.

- The homomorphism $\alpha : \Phi \to F$ that fixes the letters of $F$ and sends $\sigma_n$ to $w^{-1}$ kills $w \sigma_n$, hence $N$. So it induces $\bar\alpha : \widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n} \to F$.
- Let $\beta : F \to \widehat{\Gamma}_{g,n}$ send each letter to its class.
- Then $\bar\alpha \beta$ fixes the letters of $F$. Also $\beta \bar\alpha$ fixes the classes of the letters other than $\sigma_n$, and sends the class of $\sigma_n$ to the class of $w^{-1}$, which is the class of $\sigma_n$ because $w \sigma_n \in N$.

Continuous homomorphisms that agree on topological generators are equal. So $\bar\alpha$ and $\beta$ are inverse isomorphisms. The same argument works with free pro-$\mathbb{L}$ groups. The statements about curves follow from parts 3 and 4.

*Consequence 2.* The maximal abelian quotient of $\Phi / N$ is $\Phi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ divided by the image of $N$. Since $N$ is compact and is the closure of the subgroup generated by the conjugates of the relator, this image is the closed subgroup $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \bar\rho$ generated by the image $\bar\rho$ of the relator. Here $\Phi^{\mathrm{ab}} = \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g+n}$, with the letters as a basis, and $\bar\rho = \sigma_1 + \cdots + \sigma_n$ in additive notation, because commutators map to $0$. If $n \ge 1$, then $\bar\rho$ is part of a basis, so the quotient is $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g+n-1}$. If $n = 0$, then $\bar\rho = 0$, and the quotient is $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}$. The pro-$p'$ case is the same, with $\prod_{\ell \ne p} \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ in place of $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$.

*Consequence 3.* The non-hyperbolic types are those listed ([hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md)).

- For $(0,0)$ there are no generators, and for $(0,1)$ the relation reads $\sigma_1 = 1$.
- For $(0,2)$, consequence 1 gives $\widehat{F}_1 = \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$.
- For $(1,0)$, the relation $[x_1, y_1] = 1$ makes the group abelian, so it equals its abelianization $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^2$.

Now let $(g, n)$ be hyperbolic, that is, $2g - 2 + n \ge 1$.

- If $n \ge 1$, then $2g + n - 1 \ge 2$. By consequence 1 the group maps onto every finite group generated by two elements, such as the symmetric group $S_3$, which is not abelian.
- If $n = 0$, then $g \ge 2$. Send $x_1 \mapsto (1\,2)$, $x_2 \mapsto (1\,2\,3)$, and every other generator to $1$. Every commutator $[x_i, y_i]$ goes to $1$, because $y_i$ does. So the relation is respected, and this defines a continuous homomorphism onto $S_3$.

*Consequence 4.* By part 4 and consequence 1, $\pi_1(U)$ is $\widehat{F}_{2g+n-1}$ if $n \ge 1$ and $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0}$ if $n = 0$. By consequence 2, $\widehat{F}_r^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^r$ and $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0}^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}$. Reducing modulo $2$ shows that $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^a \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^b$ only if $a = b$. This settles the case where $n, n' \ge 1$ and the case where $n = n' = 0$. By symmetry, it remains to treat $n = 0$ and $n' \ge 1$. If $g = 0$, then $\widehat{\Gamma}_{0,0}$ has no generators, so it is trivial, and it is isomorphic to $\widehat{F}_{2g'+n'-1}$ only if $2g' + n' - 1 = 0$, that is, $(g', n') = (0, 1)$. It remains to show that if $g \ge 1$, then $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0}$ is not isomorphic to any $\widehat{F}_r$. Comparing abelianizations, only $r = 2g$ needs an argument. It is enough to find a finite group that is a continuous quotient of $\widehat{F}_{2g}$ but not of $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0}$.

Fix an odd prime $\ell$, let $V = \mathbb{F}_\ell^{2g}$, and on the set $B = V \times \Lambda^2 V$ define
$$(v, a)(w, b) = \left(v + w, \; a + b + \tfrac{1}{2} v \wedge w\right).$$

- *$B$ is a group.* Both bracketings of $(v,a)(w,b)(u,c)$ give $\left(v + w + u, \; a + b + c + \tfrac12 (v \wedge w + v \wedge u + w \wedge u)\right)$. The identity is $(0, 0)$, and the inverse of $(v, a)$ is $(-v, -a)$, because $v \wedge v = 0$.
- *Commutators.* The elements $(0, c)$ are central. A direct computation gives $[(v, a), (w, b)] = (0, v \wedge w)$, so products of commutators are $(0, \sum_i v_i \wedge w_i)$.
- *$B$ is a quotient of $\widehat{F}_{2g}$.* Let $e_1, \ldots, e_{2g}$ be the standard basis of $V$. The subgroup generated by the $(e_i, 0)$ contains every $(0, e_i \wedge e_j) = [(e_i, 0), (e_j, 0)]$, and these generate $\{0\} \times \Lambda^2 V$. It also contains an element $(v, a')$ for every $v \in V$, as a product of powers of the $(e_i, 0)$. So it is all of $B$, and $x \mapsto (e_i, 0)$ on a basis of $\widehat{F}_{2g}$ defines a continuous surjection.
- *$B$ is not a quotient of $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0}$.* Let $\varphi : \widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0} \to B$ be surjective, and let $\bar u_i, \bar w_i \in V$ be the first coordinates of $\varphi(x_i)$ and $\varphi(y_i)$. Projection to the first coordinate is a surjective homomorphism $B \to V$. So the $2g$ vectors $\bar u_1, \bar w_1, \ldots, \bar u_g, \bar w_g$ span $V$, and they form a basis. Then
$$\varphi\big([x_1, y_1] \cdots [x_g, y_g]\big) = \left(0, \; \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^g \bar u_i \wedge \bar w_i\right).$$
This is not the identity, because the wedge products of pairs of distinct members of a basis of $V$ are linearly independent in $\Lambda^2 V$. This contradicts the relation, which holds in $\widehat{\Gamma}_{g,0}$. $\square$

**Context.**

- *Characteristic $p$.* In characteristic $p > 0$ the whole tame fundamental group remembers far more: Tamagawa proved that it determines $p$, $g$ and $n$, with a few small exceptions ([Tamagawa's theorem](tame-fundamental-groups-in-positive-characteristic.md)). Its maximal pro-$p'$ quotient alone carries much less: by part 3 and consequence 1, it depends only on $p$, on $2g + n$ and on whether $n = 0$.
- *Galois actions.* Over a field that is not algebraically closed, the conjugation action of $\pi_1(X)$ on $\Delta_X$ carries much more. Over finite fields, for example, the Frobenius weights on the prime-to-$p$ part of $\Delta_X^{\mathrm{ab}}$ determine $(g, n)$ when $n > 0$ (see [Tamagawa's theorem for affine curves](tamagawa-grothendieck-conjecture-for-affine-curves.md)).
- *Uses.* The identification of $\widehat{F}_2$ with the geometric fundamental group of $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ underlies the [Grothendieck–Teichmüller group](grothendieck-teichmuller-group.md). That an abstract isomorphism of geometric fundamental groups forgets the reduction type is used in [the reconstruction of logarithmic special fibres](reconstruction-of-log-special-fibres-of-p-adic-curves.md).
