**Setting.** The notation is that of the [birational section conjecture](birational-section-conjecture.md). For a smooth complete geometrically integral curve $X$ over a field $K$ of characteristic $0$, $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K} : G_{K(X)} \to G_K$ is the restriction map, where $G_{K(X)}$ is the Galois group of a separable closure $\Omega$ of $K(X)$. A section of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ is geometric if its image lies in a decomposition group at a point of $X$, and that point is then $K$-rational. Every $K$-rational point gives geometric sections [@koenigsmann2005, §1.4, p. 5].

Koenigsmann calls a field $K$ $p$-adically closed if it has characteristic $0$ and a valuation $v$ such that $(K, v)$ is algebraically maximal and $\mathcal{O}_v/p\mathcal{O}_v$ is finite with more than one element [@koenigsmann2005, §2, p. 8]. This holds exactly when $K$ is elementarily equivalent to some finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and exactly when $G_K \cong G_F$ for some finite extension $F$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@koenigsmann2005, Fact 2.1, p. 8]. The entry on the [Galois characterization of $p$-adically closed fields](galois-characterization-of-p-adically-closed-fields.md) uses the name only for fields elementarily equivalent to $\mathbb{Q}_p$ itself. Besides the finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, examples include $\mathbb{Q}_p \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ and the field of Puiseux series over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, as that entry records.

**Statement.** Let $K$ be $p$-adically closed for some prime $p$, or real closed [@koenigsmann2005, Proposition 2.4, p. 10].

1. For every smooth complete curve $X$ over $K$, if $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ has a section, then $X(K) \ne \emptyset$.
2. Every section of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ is geometric for every smooth complete curve $X$ over $K$ if and only if $K$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ or $K = \mathbb{R}$.

Koenigsmann notes that part 1 extends to smooth complete varieties of any dimension, while part 2 does not. If $\dim X > 1$, there may be valuations on $K(X)$ that are trivial on $K$ and have residue field $K$ but do not come from points, and they give sections that are not geometric [@koenigsmann2005, Remark 2.5, p. 11].

**Proof of part 1.** This follows Koenigsmann's proof [@koenigsmann2005, proof of Proposition 2.4, p. 10]. Let $s$ be a section, and let $F \subseteq \Omega$ be the fixed field of $s(G_K)$. Restriction $G_F \to G_K$ is $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ on $s(G_K)$, so it is an isomorphism with inverse $s$. Then $K$ is an elementary substructure of $F$:

- If $K$ is $p$-adically closed, this is Koenigsmann's Fact 2.2: if $K$ is $p$-adically closed and $F/K$ is an extension for which restriction $G_F \to G_K$ is an isomorphism, then $K$ is an elementary substructure of $F$ [@koenigsmann2005, Fact 2.2, p. 9]. He derives it from the Galois characterization of $p$-adically closed fields, and notes that it already follows from Pop's relative characterization.
- If $K$ is real closed, then $G_F$ has order $2$, so $F$ is real closed by the [Artin–Schreier theorem](artin-schreier-theorem.md). An extension of real closed fields is elementary, because the theory of real closed fields is model complete, by Tarski. This standard fact has not been checked in a source here. Koenigsmann treats the two cases together.

Since $K(X) \subseteq F$, the generic point of $X$ is an $F$-rational point. Cover $X$ by finitely many affine open subsets, each defined by polynomial equations with coefficients in $K$. Then "$X$ has a rational point" is a first-order sentence with parameters in $K$. It holds in $F$, so it holds in $K$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2, "if".** Let $K$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, or $K = \mathbb{R}$. Let $s$ be a section of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ and $F$ the fixed field of $s(G_K)$. Steps 1 and 2 are Koenigsmann's [@koenigsmann2005, Lemma 1.7 and its proof, pp. 7–8]. His proof stops at the $K$-rational place of $F$, and steps 3 and 4, written out here, explain why it makes $s$ geometric.

1. *Points on finite layers.* Since restriction $G_F \to G_K$ is an isomorphism, $K$ is algebraically closed in $F$. So each finite subextension $F'$ of $F/K(X)$ is the function field of a smooth complete geometrically integral curve $X'$ over $K$. As $s(G_K) = G_F \subseteq G_{F'}$, the map $s$ is also a section of $\mathrm{pr}_{X'/K}$, so $X'(K) \ne \emptyset$ by part 1.
2. *Compactness.* Each $X'(K)$ is compact, since $K$ is locally compact and $X'$ is projective. For $F' \subseteq F''$ the morphism $X'' \to X'$ induces a continuous map $X''(K) \to X'(K)$. An inverse limit of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces is nonempty, so there are points $P_{F'} \in X'(K)$ compatible under these maps. The union of their local rings is a valuation ring of $F$ that contains $K$ and has residue field $K$. This defines a $K$-rational place $v$ of $F$, which restricts on $K(X)$ to the valuation of a point $P \in X(K)$.
3. *Henselianity.* Every finite extension of $F$ in $\Omega$ is $FK_1$ for a finite extension $K_1$ of $K$, because restriction $G_F \to G_K$ is an isomorphism, and $[FK_1 : F] = [K_1 : K]$. Write $K_1 = K(\alpha)$, and let $f$ be the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K$. Its coefficients lie in the valuation ring of $v$, and its reduction is $f$ itself, which is irreducible over the residue field $K$. So every prolongation of $v$ to $FK_1$ has residue degree at least $\deg f = [FK_1 : F]$, and by the fundamental inequality there is only one prolongation. Hence $v$ has a unique prolongation $w$ to $\Omega$, and $G_F$ fixes $w$.
4. *Conclusion.* So $s(G_K) = G_F$ lies in the decomposition group of $w$ in $G_{K(X)}$, which is a decomposition group at $P$, and $s$ is geometric. $\square$

**Proof of part 2, "only if".** This is summarized from Koenigsmann [@koenigsmann2005, proof of Proposition 2.4, pp. 10–11]. Suppose that $K$ is neither $\mathbb{R}$ nor a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Take a smooth complete curve $X$ over $K \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ with a rational point, and consider it over $K$.

- If $K$ is a proper relatively algebraically closed subfield of $\mathbb{R}$ or of a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, such as $\mathbb{R} \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ or $\mathbb{Q}_p \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, then the ordering or the $p$-adic valuation of $K$ extends to $K(X)$ so that it remains archimedean, or of rank one. The real or $p$-adic closure $F$ of $K(X)$ for this extension gives a section that is not geometric, because $F$ has no nontrivial henselian valuation that is trivial on $K$.
- If $K$ is not a subfield of $\mathbb{R}$ or of a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, then $K$ has a henselian valuation with nontrivial divisible value group and residue characteristic $0$. Koenigsmann prolongs it to $K(X)$ so that its value group is cofinal in the new one. A complement of the inertia subgroup in a decomposition group of the prolongation is then the image of a section that is not geometric, for the same reason.

**Global sections give local points.** Let $K$ be a number field and $X$ a smooth complete curve over $K$. If $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ has a section, then $X(\hat K) \ne \emptyset$ for every completion $\hat K$ of $K$ [@koenigsmann2005, Corollary 2.6, p. 11]. For a complex place there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let $\hat K^{\mathrm{alg}}$ be the algebraic closure of $K$ in $\hat K$. Its absolute Galois group is a decomposition group $D \subseteq G_K$, and $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}^{-1}(D)$ is the absolute Galois group of $\hat K^{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$, so a section of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/K}$ restricts on $D$ to a section of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/\hat K^{\mathrm{alg}}}$. Koenigsmann then applies part 1 to $\hat K^{\mathrm{alg}}$ to get a point in $X(\hat K^{\mathrm{alg}}) \subseteq X(\hat K)$.

**Algebraic $p$-adic numbers.** The fields $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ are elementarily equivalent and have isomorphic absolute Galois groups, as the [Galois characterization](galois-characterization-of-p-adically-closed-fields.md) entry records. By part 2, every section is geometric for every curve over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Over $\mathbb{Q}_p \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ and over $\mathbb{R} \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$, by contrast, every smooth complete curve with a rational point has a section that is not geometric [@koenigsmann2005, Proposition 3.1, p. 13]. Koenigsmann proves this for every large countable field, where a field is large if every curve over it with a smooth rational point has infinitely many rational points [@koenigsmann2005, §3, p. 13]. He gives these two fields as examples. The same proposition shows that, over these fields, the analogous statement for fundamental groups also fails for every smooth complete curve with a rational point.

**The real case.** Let $K = \mathbb{R}$ and let $c$ be complex conjugation. A section $s$ is determined by the involution $s(c)$ of $G_{\mathbb{R}(X)}$. Conversely, every element of order $2$ in $G_{\mathbb{R}(X)}$ maps to $c$, because the kernel $G_{\mathbb{C}(X)}$ is torsion-free by the Artin–Schreier theorem, since $-1$ is a square in $\mathbb{C}(X)$. So the sections of $\mathrm{pr}_{X/\mathbb{R}}$ correspond to the involutions in $G_{\mathbb{R}(X)}$. By the Artin–Schreier theorem, their fixed fields are exactly the real closed fields between $\mathbb{R}(X)$ and $\Omega$, that is, the real closures of $\mathbb{R}(X)$ in $\Omega$ for its orderings. Part 2 says that each such involution lies in a decomposition group at a point of $X(\mathbb{R})$. For fundamental groups the picture is different: Koenigsmann recalls that, by results of Sullivan and Huisman, the sections for the fundamental group of a smooth complete curve over $\mathbb{R}$ correspond to the connected components of $X(\mathbb{R})$ [@koenigsmann2005, Remark 1.2, p. 5].
