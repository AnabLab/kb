**Setting.** Let $G = G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}$, let $K$ be the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_3$, and let $f : G_K \to \mathbb{Z}_3$ and $b : \mathrm{Aut}(G) \to \mathbb{Z}_3^\times$ be as in [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on norm-one units](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md), the *companion entry*, with $p = 3$: $f$ is a surjective homomorphism with $f(sgs^{-1}) = -f(g)$ for $s \notin G_K$, and $f \circ \theta|_{G_K} = b(\theta)\,f$ for every $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ (part 1 (e) there).

Let $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_3)$ be the tame Galois group, $\varphi : G \to \Gamma$ the restriction, and $V = \ker\varphi$ the wild inertia group. As in the companion entry, part 2 (a), fix a splitting $G = V \rtimes \Gamma$ with $\sigma, \tau \in \Gamma$ and elements $x_0, x_1$ generating $V$ as a closed normal subgroup of $G$, subject to
$$r = x_0^{-\sigma}\,\langle x_0, \tau\rangle^g\, x_1^3\, [x_1, y_1] = 1,$$
where $x^\rho = \rho x\rho^{-1}$, $[x, y] = xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$, and $y_1 \in V$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by $x_1, \sigma, \tau$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, (7.5.14), pp. 419–420]. The exponent of $x_1$ is $3^s$ with $s = 1$. Let $F = P_2 \rtimes \Gamma$, with $P_2$ the free pro-$3$ $\Gamma$-operator group on $x_0, x_1$, and $F \to G$ the surjection whose kernel $N$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by $r$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 422]. Let $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_3[[\Gamma]]$, let $\chi : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_3$ be the ring homomorphism given by the character of $\Gamma$ with kernel $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K)$, and let $P = T_1S_- \in \Lambda$ be the central idempotent of the companion entry, part 2 (c), which has $\chi(P) = 1$. Let $\varepsilon_0 : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_3$ be the augmentation. Then $\varepsilon_0(P) = \varepsilon_0(T_1)\,\varepsilon_0(S_-) = 0$, because at each finite level $S_- = 2^{-m}\sum_{i < 2^m}(-1)^i\sigma_2^i$ with $m \ge 1$.

Put $V_1 = V$, $V_2 = \overline{V_1^3[V_1, V_1]}$ and $V_3 = \overline{V_2^3[V_2, V_1]}$. These are the groups $V^1, V^2, V^3$ of the companion entry. Define $P_2^{[1]} = P_2$, $P_2^{[2]}$, $P_2^{[3]}$ in $P_2$ the same way. They are normal in $F$ and map onto $V_1, V_2, V_3$, and $F/NP_2^{[3]} = G/V_3$ (companion entry, proof of part 3).

Use the notation $\mathcal U$, $X_{\mathcal H}$, $X^i_{\mathcal H}$ and $A_{\mathcal H, j}$ of the [one-step lifting criterion](one-step-lifting-criterion-for-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) for $X = G$; here $p^s = 3$. Let $K_0 \subset T$ be the compositum of $\mathbb{Q}_3(\zeta_3)$ and the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_3$ of degree $18$, and $\mathcal H_0 = \mathrm{Gal}(T/K_0)$. Then $\mathcal H_0 \in \mathcal U$: $K_0/\mathbb{Q}_3$ is abelian and tamely ramified of degree $36$, which $p^{2s} = 9$ divides, and $\zeta_3 \in K_0$, so $\mathcal H_0$ acts trivially on $\mu_3$ and lies in $\ker\alpha$. Also $X_{\mathcal H_0} = G_{K_0} \subseteq G_K$.

*Reading $f$ modulo $9$.* Since $f$ is a homomorphism on $G_{K_0}$, it maps $X^1_{\mathcal H_0}$, generated topologically by cubes and commutators, into $3\mathbb{Z}_3$, and $X^2_{\mathcal H_0}$ into $9\mathbb{Z}_3$. So $f$ induces a homomorphism $f_9 : X_{\mathcal H_0}/X^2_{\mathcal H_0} \to \mathbb{Z}/9$. It is surjective, because $f(G_{K_0}) \supseteq f(V) = \mathbb{Z}_3$ (companion entry, part 2 (b)). Each $g \in A_{\mathcal H_0, 2}$ maps $X_{\mathcal H_0}/X^2_{\mathcal H_0}$, the preimage of the trivial subgroup of $\Gamma/\mathcal H_0$, onto itself. For $\lambda \in (\mathbb{Z}/9)^\times$ let
$$S_\lambda = \{g \in A_{\mathcal H_0, 2} : f_9 \circ g = \lambda f_9 \text{ on } X_{\mathcal H_0}/X^2_{\mathcal H_0}\}.$$

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *$b$ modulo $9$ at one level.* An automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\varphi \circ \theta = \varphi$ induces an element of $S_\lambda$ if and only if $b(\theta) \equiv \lambda \pmod 9$.
2. *Automorphisms one step down.* There is an automorphism $\bar\psi$ of $G/V_3$ commuting with the projection to $\Gamma$ such that for every $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$ it induces an element $\psi_{\mathcal H} \in A_{\mathcal H, 2}$, and $\psi_{\mathcal H_0} \in S_4$. It fixes $\sigma$, $\tau$ and $x_0$, it is the identity modulo $V_2$, and it preserves the cyclotomic character modulo $27$: $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} \circ \bar\psi \equiv \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} \pmod{27}$, where $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ modulo $27$ factors through $G/V_3$.
3. *Reduction.* Let $\lambda \in (\mathbb{Z}/9)^\times$ with $\lambda \ne \pm 1$. Suppose that for cofinally many $\mathcal H \subseteq \mathcal H_0$ in $\mathcal U$, some element of $A_{\mathcal H, 3}$ induces an element of $S_\lambda$. Then some automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ has $b(\theta) \equiv \lambda \pmod 9$, and the two-dimensional representation $W$ of $G$ in [the two-dimensional entry](two-dimensional-representation-of-g-qp-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md) is irreducible and Hodge–Tate but not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate. Conversely, if some $\theta$ with $\varphi \circ \theta = \varphi$ has $b(\theta) \equiv \lambda \pmod 9$, then the hypothesis holds for every $\mathcal H \subseteq \mathcal H_0$ in $\mathcal U$.

So the hypothesis of part 3 with $\lambda = 4$ holds one step lower, at every tame level, by part 2. This entry does not prove it at the next step. [The paired-idempotent lift](paired-idempotent-lift-for-the-q3-norm-one-character.md) proves it there, for $\lambda = 4$ and every $\mathcal H \subseteq \mathcal H_0$ in $\mathcal U$, with a changed substitution, and so answers Hoshi's question for $\mathbb{Q}_3$ through part 3.

**Context.** Hoshi asked whether $G_k$ has a Hodge–Tate representation of dimension $2$ that is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate [@hoshi2024, Introduction, p. 2]. For odd $p$ the knowledgebase answers this for every $k \ne \mathbb{Q}_3$ ([restrictions of a two-dimensional representation of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$](restricted-two-dimensional-representations-of-g-qp-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), Corollary). For $\mathbb{Q}_3$, the two-dimensional entry shows that $W \circ \theta$ is Hodge–Tate exactly when $b(\theta)$ is an odd integer, so it suffices to find one $\theta$ with $b(\theta) \ne \pm 1$. The automorphisms built in the companion entry come from Wingberg's lifting theorem in the form of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's (7.5.15), which controls them only modulo $V_2$, and so controls $b$ only modulo $3$ (companion entry, part 2). Modulo $3$ the units are $\pm 1$, so nothing follows. Part 1 says that $b$ modulo $9$ is visible modulo $X^2_{\mathcal H_0}$, one step deeper.

**Proof of part 1.** Such a $\theta$ maps $X_{\mathcal H_0}$ onto itself and so induces an element $\bar\theta$ of $A_{\mathcal H_0, 2}$. As $X_{\mathcal H_0} \subseteq G_K$, the identity $f \circ \theta = b(\theta) f$ on $G_K$ gives $f_9 \circ \bar\theta = b(\theta) f_9$. Since $f_9$ is surjective, $f_9 \circ \bar\theta = \lambda f_9$ holds exactly when $b(\theta) \equiv \lambda \pmod 9$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.**

*(a) The map.* The Frattini quotient $P_2/P_2^{[2]}$ is $\Lambda\bar x_0 \oplus \Lambda\bar x_1$ (companion entry, part 2 (d)). Choose $y \in P_2$ with image $P\bar x_1$, and let $\psi : F \to F$ be the homomorphism of semidirect products over $\Gamma$ with $\psi(x_0) = x_0$ and $\psi(x_1) = x_1y^3$. It exists because $F$ is free in that category [@jannsenwingberg1982, p. 74]. Since $y^3 \in P_2^{[2]}$ and $P_2^{[2]}$ is normal in $F$, the elements $z$ with $\psi(z) \in zP_2^{[2]}$ form a closed subgroup containing $\Gamma$, $x_0$ and $x_1$. So $\psi$ is the identity modulo $P_2^{[2]}$, and in particular $\psi(y_1) = y_1v$ with $v \in P_2^{[2]}$.

*(b) The relation modulo $P_2^{[3]}$.* The factor $A = x_0^{-\sigma}\langle x_0, \tau\rangle^g$ of $r$ lies in the closed subgroup generated by $x_0, \sigma, \tau$, so $\psi(A) = A$, and $\psi(r) = A\,(x_1y^3)^3\,[x_1y^3, y_1v]$. In $P_2/P_2^{[3]}$ the image of $P_2^{[2]}$ is central, since $[P_2^{[2]}, P_2] \subseteq P_2^{[3]}$. So modulo $P_2^{[3]}$ we have $(x_1y^3)^3 \equiv x_1^3y^9$ and $[x_1y^3, y_1v] \equiv [x_1, y_1]$. And $y^9 = (y^3)^3 \in P_2^{[3]}$. Hence $\psi(r) \in rP_2^{[3]}$. This is the computation of the companion entry, part 3, with $y^3$ in place of $x_1^3$.

*(c) An automorphism of $G/V_3$.* $\psi$ maps $P_2$ into $P_2$, hence $P_2^{[3]}$ into $P_2^{[3]}$, and by (b) it maps each conjugate of $r$ into the normal subgroup $NP_2^{[3]}$. So it induces an endomorphism $\bar\psi$ of $F/NP_2^{[3]} = G/V_3$, fixing $\sigma, \tau, x_0$ and the identity modulo $V_2$. Its image is a closed subgroup containing $\Gamma$, so its intersection with the pro-$3$ group $V/V_3$ contains the $\Gamma$-conjugates of $x_0$ and of the image of $x_1y^3$. These generate $V/V_3$ modulo its Frattini subgroup $V_2/V_3$, hence generate it. So $\bar\psi$ is surjective, and as $G/V_3$ is topologically finitely generated, it is an automorphism, by the argument in the companion entry, part 3. It commutes with the projection to $\Gamma$.

*(d) All tame levels.* Let $\mathcal H \in \mathcal U$. Since $V \subseteq X_{\mathcal H}$, $X^1_{\mathcal H} = \overline{X_{\mathcal H}^3[X_{\mathcal H}, X_{\mathcal H}]}$ contains $V_2$, and then $X^2_{\mathcal H} \supseteq V_3$. As $\bar\psi$ commutes with the projection to $\Gamma$, it maps $X_{\mathcal H}/V_3$ onto itself, hence also its second $3$-central term, which is $X^2_{\mathcal H}/V_3$ because $V_3 \subseteq X^2_{\mathcal H}$. So $\bar\psi$ induces $\psi_{\mathcal H} \in A_{\mathcal H, 2}$.

*(e) The congruence modulo $9$.* The maps $f$ and $f \circ \bar\psi$ modulo $9$ are well defined on $V/V_3$, since $f(V_2) \subseteq 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ and $f(V_3) \subseteq 9\mathbb{Z}_3$. Both are homomorphisms $V/V_3 \to \mathbb{Z}/9$ satisfying $h(gug^{-1}) = \chi(g)h(u)$ for $g \in G/V_3$, because $\bar\psi$ commutes with the projection to $\Gamma$, through which $\chi$ factors. On generators:

- $f(x_0) \in 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ (companion entry, part 2 (b)), so $f(\bar\psi(x_0)) = f(x_0) \equiv 4f(x_0) \pmod 9$.
- Modulo $3$, $f$ factors through $V/V_2$ and $f(\lambda\bar u) \equiv \chi(\lambda)f(\bar u)$ for $\lambda \in \Lambda$ (companion entry, part 2 (e)). The image of $y$ in $V/V_2$ is $P\bar x_1$, so $f(y) \equiv \chi(P)f(x_1) = f(x_1) \pmod 3$, and $f(x_1y^3) = f(x_1) + 3f(y) \equiv 4f(x_1) \pmod 9$.

The elements on which $f \circ \bar\psi$ and $4f$ agree modulo $9$ form a closed subgroup of $V/V_3$ stable under conjugation by $G$ and containing $x_0, x_1$, so it is all of $V/V_3$. Now $X_{\mathcal H_0}/V_3 = (V/V_3)\cdot\mathcal H_0$, with $\mathcal H_0 \subseteq \Gamma$ through the splitting, and $\bar\psi$ fixes $\Gamma$ pointwise. Also $f$ vanishes on $\mathrm{Gal}(T/K) \subseteq \Gamma$, which contains $\mathcal H_0$: it is topologically generated by $\sigma^2$ and $\tau$, and $f(\sigma^2) = f(\tau) = 0$ (companion entry, part 2 (b)). So for $u \in V/V_3$ and $h \in \mathcal H_0$, $f(\bar\psi(uh)) = f(\bar\psi(u)) \equiv 4f(u) = 4f(uh) \pmod 9$. Hence $\psi_{\mathcal H_0} \in S_4$.

*(f) The cyclotomic character.* $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ maps $V$ onto $1 + 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ (companion entry, proof of part 3), so $\ell = \tfrac13\log\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is a surjective homomorphism $V \to \mathbb{Z}_3$, invariant under conjugation by $G$, with $\ell(V_2) \subseteq 3\mathbb{Z}_3$ and $\ell(V_3) \subseteq 9\mathbb{Z}_3$. Modulo $3$ it factors through $V/V_2$ and satisfies $\ell(\lambda\bar u) \equiv \varepsilon_0(\lambda)\ell(\bar u)$. So $\ell(y) \equiv \varepsilon_0(P)\ell(x_1) = 0 \pmod 3$ and $\ell(x_1y^3) \equiv \ell(x_1) \pmod 9$. As in (e), $\ell \circ \bar\psi \equiv \ell \pmod 9$ on $V/V_3$. Since $\bar\psi$ fixes $\Gamma$ and $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is a homomorphism, $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} \circ \bar\psi \equiv \chi_{\mathrm{cyc}} \pmod{27}$ on $G/V_3$, where $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ modulo $27$ is defined because $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}(V_3) \subseteq 1 + 27\mathbb{Z}_3$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Apply case 2 of the [one-step lifting criterion](one-step-lifting-criterion-for-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md) with $i = 2$ and $S = S_\lambda$. It gives an automorphism $\theta$ of $G$ with $\varphi \circ \theta = \varphi$ inducing an element of $S_\lambda$, so $b(\theta) \equiv \lambda \pmod 9$ by part 1, and $b(\theta) \ne \pm 1$. By parts 1 and 2 of the two-dimensional entry, which assume only that $p$ is odd, $W$ is irreducible and Hodge–Tate, and $W \circ \theta'$ is Hodge–Tate exactly when $b(\theta')$ is an odd integer. If $b(\theta)$ is not an odd integer, $W \circ \theta$ is not Hodge–Tate. If it is, then $b(\theta^{-1}) = b(\theta)^{-1}$ is not an integer, since $\pm 1$ are the only integers with integer inverses, and $W \circ \theta^{-1}$ is not Hodge–Tate. The converse holds because $\theta$ induces an element of $A_{\mathcal H, 3}$ for every $\mathcal H$, and by part 1 these induce an element of $S_\lambda$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Contrast with Jannsen and Wingberg.* Their substitution $x_1 \mapsto x_1^4$ also gives an automorphism of $G/V_3$, and no automorphism of $G$ induces it (companion entry, part 3). The reason given there is that it moves $\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ modulo $27$: in the notation of (f), $\ell(x_1^4) = 4\ell(x_1)$, and $\ell(x_1)$ is a unit. The automorphism $\bar\psi$ changes $x_1$ only through the idempotent $P$, which the augmentation kills, so by part 2 it passes that test, while still acting by $4$ on the anticyclotomic quotient modulo $9$.
- *The next step.* Part 3 needs, for cofinally many $\mathcal H$, one automorphism of the finite group $X/X^3_{\mathcal H}$ over $\Gamma/\mathcal H$ inducing an element of $S_\lambda$. It need not lift $\psi_{\mathcal H}$, and the choices for different $\mathcal H$ need not be compatible. Proving this along the lines of the companion entry would need the Demuškin relations of the groups $G_{K'}(3)$, for tame $K'$, modulo the fourth term of their $3$-central series in the indexing of that entry. Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg use them only modulo the third term [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, p. 423]. [The paired-idempotent lift](paired-idempotent-lift-for-the-q3-norm-one-character.md) avoids those relations. It works with the single relation $r$ modulo the fourth term of the $3$-central series of $P_2$, and builds an automorphism of $G/V_4$ that induces the required element of $A_{\mathcal H, 3}$ at every $\mathcal H \subseteq \mathcal H_0$ in $\mathcal U$, with $\lambda = 4$. Its substitution sends $x_1$ to $x_1y^3$ with $y$ of Frattini class $(P - Q)\bar x_1$, where $Q$ is the adjoint of $P$, and corrects $x_0$ by an element of the third term. It does not claim that the substitution of part 2 lifts unchanged.
