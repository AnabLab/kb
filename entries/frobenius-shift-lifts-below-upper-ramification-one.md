**Setting.** A local field here is a complete discretely valued field $k$, with finite residue field $\mathbb{F}_q$ of characteristic $p$. Let $T_k$ be its maximal tamely ramified extension in a separable closure $k^s$, and put $P_k = \mathrm{Gal}(k^s/T_k)$, $\Gamma_k = \mathrm{Gal}(T_k/k)$, $I_k = \mathrm{Gal}(T_k/k^{\mathrm{ur}})$ and $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. Let $G_k^v$ be the upper ramification subgroups of $G_k$ (see [the ramification filtration](ramification-filtration.md)). Ganapathy writes $I_k^1$ for $G_k^1$ [@ganapathy2019parahoric, §2.A, p. 1478].

Fix a uniformizer $\pi$ of $k$, roots $\pi^{1/e}$ for $p \nmid e$ with $(\pi^{1/ee'})^{e'} = \pi^{1/e}$, and primitive $e$-th roots of unity $\zeta_e$ with $\zeta_{ee'}^{e'} = \zeta_e$. Let $\tau \in I_k$ send $\pi^{1/e}$ to $\zeta_e\pi^{1/e}$ for every $e$, and let $\sigma \in \Gamma_k$ fix every $\pi^{1/e}$ and act on $k^{\mathrm{ur}}$ as the Frobenius. Step 1 checks that these exist, that $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, and that every element of $\Gamma_k$ is $\sigma^b\tau^x$ with $b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $x \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. As in [the Frobenius shift problem](frobenius-shift-lifting-problem.md), $\alpha_a : \sigma \mapsto \sigma\tau^a$, $\tau \mapsto \tau$ is an automorphism of $\Gamma_k$ for $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and $\alpha = \alpha_1$. For $k = U_f$, the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_2$ of degree $f$, this is the shift of that entry, which may be defined with any such pair $(\sigma, \tau)$.

For a closed normal subgroup $N \subseteq P_k$ of $G_k$, say that $\alpha_a$ *lifts to* $G_k/N$ if some automorphism of $G_k/N$ induces $\alpha_a$ on $\Gamma_k = G_k/P_k$. This makes sense because every automorphism of $G_k/N$ preserves $P_k/N$ (Step 3).

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Equal characteristic.* If $k = \mathbb{F}_q((t))$, every $\alpha_a$ lifts to $G_k$.
2. *Below ramification one.* For every local field $k$ with finite residue field, every $\alpha_a$ lifts to $G_k/G_k^1$. In particular, the Frobenius shift lifts to $G_{U_f}/G_{U_f}^1$ for every $f$.
3. *This does not decide the question.* For $k = \mathbb{Q}_3$, $\alpha$ lifts to $G_k/G_k^1$ by part 2, but not to $G_k$, by the first of the two checks in the problem entry.
4. *A full lift must move the filtration.* Let $f \ge 2$. No automorphism of $G_{U_f}$ that induces $\alpha$ on $\Gamma_{U_f}$ maps $G_{U_f}^v$ onto itself for every $v > 0$.

**The tame 2-adic fields.** Fix an odd $e$, and let $K_c$ ($c \in \mathbb{Z}/e$) be the tamely ramified fields of ramification index $e$ and residue degree $f$ over $\mathbb{Q}_2$, numbered as in part 1 of the reduction in the problem entry. By Step 5, $G_{U_f}^1 \subseteq P_{U_f} \subseteq G_{K_c}$. A lift of $\alpha$ to $G_{U_f}/G_{U_f}^1$ therefore maps $G_{K_c}/G_{U_f}^1$ onto $G_{K_{c+1}}/G_{U_f}^1$, by the argument of part 3 of that reduction, so all these quotients are isomorphic. This applies to the degree-$6$ pair $U_2(\sqrt[3]{2})$, $U_2(\sqrt[3]{2\omega})$ of [tamely ramified 2-adic fields](tamely-ramified-2-adic-fields.md). Unlike the quotients in [the first Frattini layer](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-first-frattini-layer.md), these are defined through the ramification filtration and not by the group alone. So they say nothing directly about $G_{K_c}$ as an abstract group. Over $\mathbb{Q}_3$, the same argument gives $G_{\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{3})}/G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}^1 \cong G_{\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{-3})}/G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}^1$, although the two absolute Galois groups are not isomorphic (part 3).

**Why this route stops here.** Part 2 transports a field automorphism of $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$ through Deligne's theory at level $1$, and level $1$ is as far as it reaches for $U_f$. For a field $F$ of characteristic $2$, the ring $\mathcal{O}_F/\mathfrak{m}_F^2$ of Deligne's level-$2$ datum has characteristic $2$, while $\mathcal{O}_{U_f}/4$ has characteristic $4$, so $U_f$ is not $2$-close to any such field. By part 4, a lift of $\alpha$ to $G_{U_f}$, if one exists, does not respect the whole filtration. Over $\mathbb{Q}_3$ the obstruction comes from $\mu_3 \subset T_{\mathbb{Q}_3}$, on which $\tau$ acts by inversion (heuristic paragraph of the problem entry). There are no such roots of unity in characteristic $p$, and part 1 is consistent with this.

**Proof.**

*Step 1: the tame group.* Let $L/k$ be finite and tamely ramified, with maximal unramified subextension $L_0$. Then $L = L_0(\varpi)$ with $\varpi^e = \varpi_0$ for a prime element $\varpi_0$ of $L_0$, where $e = e(L/k)$ is prime to $p$ [@fesenkovostokov2002, Ch. II, (3.5) Proposition (1), p. 53]. Write $\varpi_0 = \pi\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon$ a unit of $L_0$. Since $X^e - \bar\varepsilon$ and $X^e - 1$ are separable over the residue field, a root $\eta$ of $X^e - \varepsilon$ and the $e$-th roots of unity generate unramified extensions of $L_0$, as in the proof of part (2) of the same proposition (p. 53). As $(\varpi/\eta)^e = \pi$, this gives $L \subseteq k^{\mathrm{ur}}(\pi^{1/e})$. Conversely, $k'(\zeta_e, \pi^{1/e})$ is tamely ramified over $k$ for $p \nmid e$ and every finite unramified $k'/k$ [@fesenkovostokov2002, Ch. II, (3.5) Proposition (2) and Corollary, pp. 53–54]. So $T_k$ is the union of the fields $k^{\mathrm{ur}}(\pi^{1/e})$ with $p \nmid e$.

The polynomial $X^e - \pi$ is Eisenstein over every finite unramified extension of $k$, so $[k^{\mathrm{ur}}(\pi^{1/e}) : k^{\mathrm{ur}}] = e$ [@fesenkovostokov2002, Ch. II, (3.6) Proposition (1), p. 54]. Hence $g \mapsto (g(\pi^{1/e})/\pi^{1/e})_e$ is an isomorphism from $I_k$ onto $\varprojlim_{p \nmid e}\mu_e \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, so $\tau$ exists and topologically generates $I_k$. Next, $\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{ur}}/k) \cong \mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_q/\mathbb{F}_q)$ [@fesenkovostokov2002, Ch. II, (3.3) Corollary, p. 52], which is $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, generated by the Frobenius. The field $k_\pi = k(\pi^{1/e} : p \nmid e)$ meets $k^{\mathrm{ur}}$ in $k$, since each $k(\pi^{1/e})$ is totally ramified. So restriction maps $\mathrm{Gal}(T_k/k_\pi)$ isomorphically onto $\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{ur}}/k)$, and $\sigma$ exists. Both $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}$ and $\tau^q$ are trivial on $k^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and send $\pi^{1/e}$ to $\zeta_e^q\pi^{1/e}$, so they are equal. If $g \in \Gamma_k$ acts on $k^{\mathrm{ur}}$ as the $b$-th power of the Frobenius, then $\sigma^{-b}g \in I_k$, so $g = \sigma^b\tau^x$. By continuity, $\sigma^b\tau\sigma^{-b} = \tau^{q^b}$ for $b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$.

*Step 2: isomorphisms of tame groups.* First, $I_k$ is the only closed normal subgroup $N$ of $\Gamma_k$ with $\Gamma_k/N \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. Indeed, the closed subgroup $C$ generated by $\tau^{q-1} = \sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}\tau^{-1}$ is normal, and the images of $\sigma$ and $\tau$ commute in $\Gamma_k/C$. So $C$ is the closed commutator subgroup, and $\Gamma_k^{\mathrm{ab}}$ contains $I_k/C \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'/(q-1) = \mathbb{Z}/(q-1)$, as $p \nmid q - 1$. The quotient by $I_k/C$ is $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, which is torsion-free, so $I_k/C$ is the torsion subgroup of $\Gamma_k^{\mathrm{ab}}$. A continuous surjection $\Gamma_k \to \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ kills this torsion, so it factors through a surjective endomorphism of $\Gamma_k/I_k \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, which is an isomorphism. Its kernel is therefore $I_k$.

Now let $k'$ be another local field with residue field $\mathbb{F}_q$, with $\sigma', \tau', \alpha'_a$ as above, and let $\psi : \Gamma_k \to \Gamma_{k'}$ be an isomorphism. Then $\psi(I_k) = I_{k'}$, so $\psi(\tau) = \tau'^u$ with $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$. Also $\psi(\sigma)$ generates $\Gamma_{k'}/I_{k'}$, so $\psi(\sigma) = \sigma'^v\tau'^b$ with $v \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^\times$. Applying $\psi$ to the relation gives $\tau'^{uq^v} = \tau'^{uq}$, so $q^{v-1} = 1$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and $v = 1$ by the argument in the lemma of the problem entry. Then $\psi^{-1}\alpha'_a\psi$ fixes $\tau$ and sends $\sigma$ to $\psi^{-1}(\sigma'\tau'^b\tau'^a) = \sigma\tau^{a/u}$. So
$$\psi^{-1} \circ \alpha'_a \circ \psi = \alpha_{a/u}.$$

*Step 3: which shifts lift.* Let $N \subseteq P_k$ be a closed normal subgroup of $G_k$. The group $P_k$ is pro-$p$: for finite Galois $L/k$, its image $\mathrm{Gal}(L/L_1)$, with $L_1$ the maximal tamely ramified subextension, is the maximal $p$-subgroup of the inertia group [@fesenkovostokov2002, Ch. II, (4.4) Corollaries 1 and 2, pp. 58–59]. The proof of the lemma in the problem entry uses only the structure in Step 1, and it shows that $\Gamma_k$ has no nontrivial closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup. So $P_k/N$ is the largest closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup of $G_k/N$, and every automorphism of $G_k/N$ induces one of $\Gamma_k$. The induced automorphisms form a subgroup that contains every inner automorphism. Moreover $\alpha_b\alpha_c = \alpha_{b+c}$, and $\alpha_{(q-1)z}$ is inner for $z \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, by the computation under "Reformulations" in the problem entry, which uses only the relation. Suppose that $\alpha_a$ lifts for some $a$ prime to $q - 1$, and choose an integer $n$ with $na \equiv 1 \pmod{q-1}$. Given $b \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, choose an integer $m \equiv b \pmod{q-1}$. Then $\alpha_b = \alpha_a^{nm} \circ \alpha_{b - nma}$ with $b - nma \in (q-1)\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, so $\alpha_b$ lifts too. If $q = 2$, every $\alpha_b$ is inner and lifts.

*Step 4: equal characteristic.* Let $F = \mathbb{F}_q((t))$ with $q > 2$, take $\pi = t$, write $\sigma_F, \tau_F, \alpha^F_a$ for the elements and shifts of Step 1 for $F$, and let $\zeta$ generate $\mathbb{F}_q^\times$. Each $\mathbb{F}_{q^m}((t)) = F(\mathbb{F}_{q^m})$ is unramified of degree $m$ over $F$, and by the corollary cited in Step 1, $F^{\mathrm{ur}}$ has only one subextension of each degree. So $F^{\mathrm{ur}}$ is the union of these fields, and $T_F = F(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_q, t^{1/e} : p \nmid e)$ by Step 1. Define $\hat g$ on $F^{\mathrm{ur}}$ by $\sum a_i t^i \mapsto \sum a_i\zeta^i t^i$. This is a field automorphism fixing $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_q$, with $\hat g(F) = F$. Choose $\xi_e \in \bar{\mathbb{F}}_q$ with $\xi_e^e = \zeta$ and $\xi_{ee'}^{e'} = \xi_e$, which is possible because these are inverse limits of nonempty finite sets. Since $\hat g$ carries $X^e - t$ to $X^e - \zeta t$, which has the root $\xi_e t^{1/e}$, setting $\hat g(t^{1/e}) = \xi_e t^{1/e}$ extends $\hat g$ to each $F^{\mathrm{ur}}(t^{1/e})$, compatibly in $e$, hence to $T_F$. Extend it further to $F^s$. Conjugation $c(g) = \hat g g \hat g^{-1}$ is then an automorphism of $G_F$, and it induces conjugation by $\hat g$ on $\Gamma_F$.

Elements of $\Gamma_F$ are determined by their actions on $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_q$ and on the $t^{1/e}$. Using $\hat g^{-1}(t^{1/e}) = \xi_e^{-1}t^{1/e}$, one finds that $c(\tau_F)$ fixes $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_q$ and sends $t^{1/e}$ to $\hat g(\xi_e^{-1}\zeta_e t^{1/e}) = \zeta_e t^{1/e}$, so $c(\tau_F) = \tau_F$. Similarly, $c(\sigma_F)$ acts on $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_q$ as the Frobenius and sends $t^{1/e}$ to $\hat g(\xi_e^{-q}t^{1/e}) = \xi_e^{1-q}t^{1/e}$. Now $\xi_e^{1-q} \in \mu_e$, since $\zeta^{q-1} = 1$. So there is a unique $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$ with $\zeta_e^{qa} = \xi_e^{1-q}$ for all $e$, and then $c(\sigma_F)$ and $\sigma_F\tau_F^a$ agree on $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_q$ and on every $t^{1/e}$, since $\sigma_F\tau_F^a(t^{1/e}) = \zeta_e^{qa}t^{1/e}$. So $c(\sigma_F) = \sigma_F\tau_F^a$. For $e = q - 1$ we have $\xi_e^{1-q} = \zeta^{-1}$, which generates $\mu_{q-1}$. So $qa$, and hence $a$, is prime to $q - 1$. Thus $c$ lifts $\alpha^F_a$ to $G_F$, and part 1 follows from Step 3 with $N = 1$.

The automorphism $c$ also preserves $G_F^1$. Indeed, $\hat g$ preserves the valuation of $F$, hence the unique extension of it to $F^s$. So for finite Galois $L/F$, conjugation by $\hat g$ maps each lower ramification group of $\mathrm{Gal}(L/F)$ onto the corresponding group of $\mathrm{Gal}(\hat g(L)/F)$, the Hasse–Herbrand functions of the two extensions agree, and the upper ramification groups correspond. Passing to the limit, $c(G_F^1) = G_F^1$, and $c$ induces an automorphism $\bar c$ of $G_F/G_F^1$ that lifts $\alpha^F_a$.

*Step 5: transport to any local field.* Let $k$ have residue field $\mathbb{F}_q$, with $q > 2$, and let $F = \mathbb{F}_q((t))$. Deligne's truncated datum $\mathrm{Tr}_1(k) = (\mathcal{O}_k/\mathfrak{m}_k, \mathfrak{m}_k/\mathfrak{m}_k^2, \epsilon)$ consists of the field $\mathbb{F}_q$, a one-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_q$-vector space and the zero map to $\mathfrak{m}_k/\mathfrak{m}_k = 0$. So $\mathrm{Tr}_1(k) \cong \mathrm{Tr}_1(F)$, and by Deligne's theorem there is an isomorphism $\varphi : G_k/G_k^1 \to G_F/G_F^1$ [@ganapathy2019parahoric, §2.A, equation (2-1), p. 1478]. Ganapathy's local fields are complete discretely valued fields with perfect residue field, and the theorem is stated for every $m \ge 1$ [@ganapathy2019parahoric, §2 and §2.A, p. 1478].

Next, $G_k^1 \subseteq P_k$. For finite Galois $L/k$ with group $G$, the upper group is $G^1 = G_{h(1)}$ with $h(x) = \int_0^x |G_0 : G_{h(t)}|\,dt$ [@fesenkovostokov2002, Ch. III, (3.5) Corollary and Definition, p. 86, and (3.7) Proposition, p. 87]. Here $h$ is the inverse of the usual function $\varphi_{L/k}$ (Remark 1 on p. 88), so these are the usual upper groups. Since $h(1) \ge 1$, we get $G^1 \subseteq G_1 = \mathrm{Gal}(L/L_1)$ by Step 3, and in the limit $G_k^1 \subseteq P_k$. The same holds for $F$.

By Step 3, $\varphi$ maps $P_k/G_k^1$ onto $P_F/G_F^1$, so it induces an isomorphism $\psi : \Gamma_k \to \Gamma_F$. Then $\varphi^{-1} \circ \bar c \circ \varphi$ is an automorphism of $G_k/G_k^1$ that induces $\psi^{-1}\alpha^F_a\psi = \alpha_{a/u}$ on $\Gamma_k$, by Step 2. As $a/u$ is prime to $q - 1$, Step 3 with $N = G_k^1$ gives part 2. For $q = 2$, part 2 holds by the last sentence of Step 3.

*Step 6: parts 3 and 4.* Part 3: the first check in the problem entry shows that no automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}$ induces $\alpha$, as $\alpha$ would exchange the absolute Galois groups of $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{3})$ and $\mathbb{Q}_3(\sqrt{-3})$.

Part 4: let $\Phi$ be an automorphism of $G_{U_f}$ that induces $\alpha$ and preserves every $G_{U_f}^v$ with $v > 0$. By [Mochizuki's theorem](mochizuki-local-fields-theorem.md), its outer class comes from a field automorphism $s$ of $U_f$, that is, $\Phi$ is conjugation by an extension $\tilde s \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$ of $s$, followed by an inner automorphism of $G_{U_f}$. So $\Phi$ is conjugation by some $g \in G_{\mathbb{Q}_2}$, and $\alpha$ is conjugation by the image $\gamma$ of $g$ in $\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}_2} = \mathrm{Gal}(T/\mathbb{Q}_2)$. Use Step 1 for $U_f$ and for $\mathbb{Q}_2$ with $\pi = 2$ and the same roots $2^{1/e}$ and $\zeta_e$, which is allowed because the answer does not depend on the pair $(\sigma, \tau)$. This gives the same $\tau$, since $I_{\mathbb{Q}_2} = I_{U_f}$, and a Frobenius lift $\sigma_1$ with $\sigma_1\tau\sigma_1^{-1} = \tau^2$ and $\sigma_1^f = \sigma$. Write $\gamma = \sigma_1^j\tau^c$. Then $\gamma\tau\gamma^{-1} = \tau^{2^j}$ must equal $\alpha(\tau) = \tau$, so $2^j = 1$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, and $j = 0$ by the argument in the lemma of the problem entry. Conjugation by $\tau^c$ sends $\sigma$ to $\sigma\tau^{c/q - c}$, so $c(1-q)/q = 1$ and $q - 1$ divides $q$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. But $q - 1 \ge 3$ is odd, so it has a prime factor $\ell \ne 2$, and $q$ is a unit in $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. This contradiction proves part 4. $\square$

**Sources.** Deligne's 1984 paper was not opened. His theorem is used through Ganapathy's restatement, and only the existence of an isomorphism is needed, not its canonicity or functoriality.
