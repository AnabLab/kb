**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite of degree $d$ with residue field of order $q$, and $G = G_K$, with inertia group $I_K$ and wild inertia group $P_K$. Let $T = K^{\mathrm{tr}}$ be the maximal tamely ramified extension and $\Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(T/K) = G/P_K$. As in [tame automorphisms for odd $p$](tame-automorphisms-lift-to-the-absolute-galois-group-for-odd-p.md), choose generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\Gamma$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, where $\tau$ generates tame inertia $I = I_K/P_K$ and $x \mapsto \tau^x$ is an isomorphism $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}' = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell \to I$. Every automorphism $\eta$ of $G$ preserves $P_K$ and so induces an automorphism of $\Gamma$, which is $\beta_{u,a} : \tau \mapsto \tau^u$, $\sigma \mapsto \sigma\tau^a$ for unique $u \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'^\times$ and $a \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, with $\beta_{u,a}\beta_{u',a'} = \beta_{uu',\, a + ua'}$ (same entry, part 2). Call $u$ the *tame twist* of $\eta$.

*Tame levels.* For $m \ge 1$ prime to $p$, let $I_m \subseteq I$ be the closed subgroup generated by $\tau^m$, the unique open subgroup of index $m$ of $I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$. It is normal in $\Gamma$, as $\sigma\tau^m\sigma^{-1} = \tau^{qm}$. Let $T_m \subseteq T$ be its fixed field, the unique extension of $K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ of degree $m$ in $T$, and let $\Omega_{K,m}$ be the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $T_m(\mu_p)$. Then $T_1 = K^{\mathrm{ur}}$ and $\Omega_{K,1}$ is the field $\Omega_K$ of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md). Write $\mathrm{ord}_m(q)$ for the order of $q$ modulo $m$.

For a finite Galois extension $L/K$ inside $T$ of degree prime to $p$, put $\Delta = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$, and let $V_L = G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} \mathbb{Q}_p$ and $I_L \subseteq V_L$ be as in that entry, $I_L$ the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-span of the image of $I_K \cap G_L$. Let $k(L)$ be the largest dimension of an irreducible representation of $\Delta$ over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$. If $\eta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ induces the identity on $\Delta$, then $\eta(G_L) = G_L$, and the induced automorphism $\eta_*$ of $V_L$ commutes with $\Delta$, by the argument of the Setting of that entry.

*The presentation and Kondo's automorphisms.* Identify $G$ with the group $F/(\varrho)$ of Jannsen and Wingberg, as in the Setting of [even degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md): $F = P \rtimes \mathcal{G}$, where $\mathcal{G}$ is generated by $\sigma, \tau$ with the single relation $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, $P$ is the pro-$p$ group normally generated by $x_0, \dots, x_d$, $F$ is free among semidirect products of $\mathcal{G}$ with pro-$p$ groups, and $(\varrho)$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by one relator $\varrho$. For even $d$ the relator is the one written there, called $r$ there. For odd $d$ it is $\varrho = (x_0^\sigma)^{-1}(x_0, \tau)^{g'}x_1^{p^s}[x_1, y_1][x_2, x_3]\cdots[x_{d-1}, x_d]$, with $y_1$ in the closed subgroup generated by $x_1, \sigma, \tau$ ([presentations](local-galois-group-presentations.md), condition 3, where the exponent is called $g$). In both cases $\varrho \in P$: the factor $(x_0, \tau)$ is $y^{\pi/(p-1)}$ for an element $y$ of the closed subgroup generated by $x_0, \tau$ whose image $\tau^{p-1}$ in $\mathcal{G}$ has order prime to $p$, so it maps to $1$ in $\mathcal{G}$, and the other factors visibly lie in $P$. As shown in the Setting of [even degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md), by an argument that does not use the parity of $d$, $P_K$ is the closed normal subgroup of $G$ generated by $x_0, \dots, x_d$, and $\mathcal{G}$ maps isomorphically onto $G/P_K = \Gamma$. Take for $\sigma, \tau \in \Gamma$ above the images of these generators; this is allowed, as they generate $\Gamma$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^q$, and $\tau$ generates $I$, the largest closed normal procyclic subgroup of $\Gamma$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)).

If $d \ge 3$, write $d = 2g + r$ with $r = 1$ for odd $d$ and $r = 2$ for even $d$. Call $x_0, x_1$, and $x_2$ when $d$ is even, the *complementary* generators, and the other $2g$ the *surface* generators $a_i, b_i$: $a_i = x_{2i}$, $b_i = x_{2i+1}$ for odd $d$ and $a_i = x_{2i+1}$, $b_i = x_{2i+2}$ for even $d$. Then $\varrho = \varrho_c\,\delta_G$ with $\delta_G = [a_1, b_1]\cdots[a_g, b_g]$ and $\varrho_c$ in the closed subgroup $F^c$ generated by $\mathcal{G}$ and the complementary generators. Let $\Pi$, $\delta$, $\mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$ and Kondo's homomorphism $\Psi : \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi) \to \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ be as in that Setting, with $\iota(\alpha_i) = a_i$ and $\iota(\beta_i) = b_i$: $\Psi(\varphi)$ is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and on the complementary generators and maps $a_i, b_i$ to $\iota(\varphi(\alpha_i)), \iota(\varphi(\beta_i))$. For odd $d$ the same proof applies: $\Psi(\varphi)$ fixes $\sigma, \tau, x_0, x_1$, hence $y_1$ and $\varrho_c$, and fixes $\delta_G = \iota(\delta)$, so it fixes $\varrho$. The induced automorphisms of $\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}}$ include the integral symplectic group $\mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$ (same Setting). Each $\Psi(\varphi)$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$. Put $\Pi_{\mathbb{Q}} = \Pi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}_p$.

*Hypotheses.* Let $\theta$ be an automorphism of $G$ of finite order $p^a$, and assume one of the following.

- **(H1)** $d \ge 3$; $p \ge 5$, or $p = 3$ and $d$ is odd; and the class of $\theta$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ has only finitely many conjugates. Put $w = r$.
- **(H2)** No further condition. Put $w = d$.

Under (H1), $w < p - 1$; under (H2), $w < p - 1$ means $d \le p - 2$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Structure*, under (H1). Let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension inside $T$ of degree prime to $p$ such that $\theta$ induces the identity on $\Delta = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$. Then $I_L = I_L^c \oplus I_L^s$, where $I_L^c$ and $I_L^s$ are the $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-spans of the images of the complementary and of the surface generators. $I_L^s$ is free over $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$ on the images $\bar a_i, \bar b_i$, and $I_L^c \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]^r$. The automorphism $\theta_*$ preserves both summands, and there is $c \in \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$ with $c^{p^a} = 1$ such that $\theta_*(\lambda\bar x) = \lambda c\,\bar x$ for every surface generator $x$ and every $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$.
2. *Small levels.* Let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension inside $T$ of degree prime to $p$ with $\mu_p \subset L$, such that $\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ and $w \cdot k(L) < p - 1$. Then $\theta_*$ is the identity on $V_L$, and $\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(L(p)/K)$, where $L(p)$ is the maximal pro-$p$ extension of $L$.
3. *Towers.* Let $u$ be the tame twist of $\theta$. Then $u^{p^a} = 1$, and $u_\ell = 1$ for every prime $\ell \not\equiv 1 \pmod p$. Let $m \ge 1$ be prime to $p$ with $u \equiv 1 \pmod m$ and $w \cdot \mathrm{ord}_m(q) < p - 1$. Then $\theta$ maps $G_{\Omega_{K,m}}$ onto itself and induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,m}/K)$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here.

1. *Abelianization.* Assume (H1), or (H2) with $d \le p - 2$. The field $T_{q-1}$ is the maximal abelian tamely ramified extension $K^{\mathrm{ab}} \cap T$, and the following are equivalent: $\theta$ induces the identity on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$; it induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(T_{q-1}/K)$; $u \equiv 1 \pmod{q-1}$. In that case $\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,q-1}/K)$, and $K^{\mathrm{ab}} \subseteq \Omega_{K,q-1}$. All of this holds when no prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$ divides $q - 1$, for instance when the residue degree of $K$ is $1$ or $2$, such as for $K = \mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)$.
2. *What a counterexample needs.* Assume (H1) and $\theta \ne 1$. Either $u \ne 1$, so that $u_\ell \ne 1$ for some prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$; or $u = 1$, $\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$, and there is $m$ prime to $p$ such that $\theta$ is not the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,m}/K)$, and every such $m$ satisfies $r \cdot \mathrm{ord}_m(q) \ge p - 1$.
3. *Tsujimura's question.* Let $d \ge 3$, with $p \ge 5$ or $d$ odd. Let $N$ be a rival copy of $G_K$ of type (a) in [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 2, with $M = N \cap G_K$, and suppose that $p$ divides $(G_K : M)$. Then the automorphism $\theta$ of order $p$ in $NG_K$ that the Corollary of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md) provides satisfies (H1). So it induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,m}/K)$ for every $m$ as in part 3, and on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ if no prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$ divides $q - 1$.

**Lemma.** This is proved here. Let $\Delta$ be a finite group of order prime to $p$, let $r' \ge 1$, and suppose that $r' \dim\psi < p - 1$ for every irreducible representation $\psi$ of $\Delta$ over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$. Let $W$ be a $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-module isomorphic to a submodule of $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]^{r'}$, and $B$ a $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-linear automorphism of $W$ with $B^{p^a} = 1$. Then $B = 1$.

*Proof.* For $b \ge 1$ the field $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^b})$ has degree $(p-1)p^{b-1} = \deg\Phi_{p^b}$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and is totally ramified ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)), so the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_{p^b}$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. The minimal polynomial of $B$ divides $x^{p^a} - 1 = \prod_{b=0}^{a}\Phi_{p^b}$, so it is $\prod_{b \in S}\Phi_{p^b}$ for some set $S$, and $\mathbb{Q}_p[B] \cong \prod_{b \in S}\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^b})$ by the Chinese remainder theorem. If $S \subseteq \{0\}$, then $B = 1$. Otherwise pick $b \in S$ with $b \ge 1$, let $e \in \mathbb{Q}_p[B]$ be the idempotent of the factor $F = \mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^b})$, and put $X = eW \ne 0$. As $B$ commutes with $\Delta$, so does $e$, and $X$ is a $\Delta$-submodule of $W$ on which $e\mathbb{Q}_p[B] \cong F$ acts, commuting with $\Delta$. So $X$ is a nonzero $F[\Delta]$-module. Let $\chi(g) \in F$ be the trace of $g \in \Delta$ on the $F$-vector space $X$. For each of the $[F:\mathbb{Q}_p]$ embeddings $\iota : F \to \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, the representation $X \otimes_{F,\iota} \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ has character $\iota \circ \chi$, whose values are sums of roots of unity of order dividing $N = |\Delta|$, so they lie in $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_N) \cap \iota(F)$. Since $p \nmid N$, $N$ divides $p^f - 1$ for some $f$, and $\mu_N$ lies in the unramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $f$, which contains $\mu_{p^f - 1}$ ([multiplicative groups](multiplicative-group-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). So $\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_N) \cap \iota(F)$ is both unramified and totally ramified over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, hence equal to $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Thus $\chi$ takes values in $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and all the $\iota \circ \chi$ are the same character $\chi$. As $F \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p \cong \prod_\iota \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, the representation $X \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ is the sum of the $X \otimes_{F,\iota} \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, so its character is $[F:\mathbb{Q}_p]\chi$. Choose an irreducible $\psi$ occurring in $X \otimes_{F,\iota}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$. It occurs in $X \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ with multiplicity at least $[F:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge p - 1$. But $X \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ embeds in $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p[\Delta]^{r'}$, in which $\psi$ has multiplicity $r'\dim\psi < p - 1$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** Write $A = \theta_*$, a $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-linear automorphism of $V_L$ preserving $I_L$, with $A^{p^a} = 1$.

*Step 1: generators of $I_L$.* Step 1 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) uses about $L_n$ only that it is a finite Galois extension of $K$ inside $T$, and about its generators only that they normally generate $P_K$. So $I_L \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]^d$, and $I_L$ is the $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-span of the images $\bar x_0, \dots, \bar x_d$. Let $M = M^c \oplus M^s$ with $M^c = \bigoplus_{x_i\ \text{complementary}}\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]e_i$ and $M^s = \Pi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$, on which $\Delta$ acts through the second factor, and let $\pi : M \to I_L$ be the surjection of $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-modules sending $e_i$ to $\bar x_i$ and $[\alpha_i] \otimes \lambda$, $[\beta_i] \otimes \lambda$ to $\lambda\bar a_i$, $\lambda\bar b_i$. Let $R$ be its kernel.

*Step 2: the relation module lies in $M^c$.* Let $F_L \subseteq F$ be the inverse image of $G_L$. As $L \subseteq T$, $P \subseteq F_L$, so $F_L = P \rtimes H$ with $H = \mathrm{Gal}(T/L)$, and $G_L = F_L/(\varrho)$. Conjugation by $F$ makes $F_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$ a $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Delta]$-module, as $F_L$ acts trivially; write $\tilde x$ for the image of $x \in P$. For $n \ge 1$, let $\mathcal{G}$ act on $(\mathbb{Z}/p^n)[\Delta]^{d+1}$ through $\Delta = \mathcal{G}/H$ by left multiplication. By the universal property of $F$ there is a homomorphism from $F$ to the semidirect product of $\mathcal{G}$ with this finite $p$-group that is the identity on $\mathcal{G}$ and sends $x_i$ to the $i$-th basis vector. On $F_L$, followed by the projection to the module, on which $H$ acts trivially, it is a homomorphism sending $fx_if^{-1}$ to $\bar f e_i$, where $\bar f$ is the image of $f$ in $\Delta$. Passing to the limit over $n$ gives a $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Delta]$-linear map $F_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p) \to \mathbb{Z}_p[\Delta]^{d+1}$ with $\tilde x_i \mapsto e_i$. So the $\tilde x_i$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$ in $F_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$, and $e_i \mapsto \tilde x_i$ identifies $M$ with their span $\tilde M$, in such a way that $\pi$ is induced by $F_L \to G_L$. The kernel of $F_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p) \to G_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p)$ is the image of $(\varrho)$, the closed subgroup generated by the images of the conjugates $f\varrho f^{-1}$, $f \in F$, which are the $\bar f\tilde\varrho$. So the kernel of $F_L^{\mathrm{ab}}(p) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p \to V_L$ is $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]\tilde\varrho$. As $\varrho \in P$, $\tilde\varrho \in \tilde M$, and therefore $R$ corresponds to $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]\tilde\varrho$.

Now $\tilde\varrho = \tilde\varrho_c + \tilde\delta_G$. The element $\delta_G$ is a product of commutators of elements of $P \subseteq F_L$, so $\tilde\delta_G = 0$. And $\varrho_c = \varrho\delta_G^{-1}$ lies in $F^c \cap P$. Let $N^c$ be the closed normal subgroup of $F^c$ generated by the complementary generators. Then $N^c \subseteq P$ and $F^c = N^c\mathcal{G}$, so $F^c \cap P = N^c$, as $\mathcal{G} \cap P = 1$. $N^c$ is topologically generated by the elements $n\gamma x_i\gamma^{-1}n^{-1}$ with $n \in N^c$, $\gamma \in \mathcal{G}$ and $x_i$ complementary, whose images are $\bar\gamma\tilde x_i$, as $n \in F_L$. So $\tilde\varrho_c$ lies in the $\mathbb{Z}_p[\Delta]$-span of the complementary $\tilde x_i$, and $R \subseteq M^c$.

Consequently, if $\pi(m_c) = \pi(m_s)$ with $m_c \in M^c$, $m_s \in M^s$, then $m_c - m_s \in R \subseteq M^c$, so $m_s = 0$. Hence $I_L = I_L^c \oplus I_L^s$ with $I_L^c = \pi(M^c)$ and $I_L^s = \pi(M^s)$, and $\pi$ maps $M^s$ isomorphically onto $I_L^s$, so $I_L^s$ is free on the $\bar a_i, \bar b_i$. By Maschke's theorem $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$ is semisimple, and $I_L^c \oplus \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]^{2g} \cong I_L \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]^d$, so comparing multiplicities of simple modules gives $I_L^c \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]^r$.

*Step 3: Kondo's automorphisms.* For $\varphi \in \mathrm{Aut}^\delta(\Pi)$, $\Psi(\varphi)$ induces the identity on $\Delta$, so $\Psi(\varphi)_*$ is $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-linear. It fixes the complementary generators and maps $a_i$ to the word $\iota(\varphi(\alpha_i))$ in the surface generators, which lie in $G_L$, so the image of $\Psi(\varphi)(a_i)$ is $\pi(\varphi^{\mathrm{ab}}[\alpha_i] \otimes 1)$, and likewise for $b_i$. So, transported by $\pi$, $\Psi(\varphi)_*$ acts on $I_L = I_L^c \oplus I_L^s$ as $\mathrm{id} \oplus (\varphi^{\mathrm{ab}} \otimes 1)$, as in Step 2 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md).

*Step 4: finitely many conjugates.* As $G$ is topologically finitely generated ([presentations](local-galois-group-presentations.md)), it has only finitely many open subgroups of index $(G : G_L)$: each is the stabilizer of a point for the action of $G$ on its cosets, given by a continuous homomorphism to a symmetric group, which is determined by the images of finitely many topological generators. So the stabilizer $\mathcal{A}_L = \{\eta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G) : \eta(G_L) = G_L\}$ has finite index in $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$. It contains $\theta$ and every $\Psi(\varphi)$, which induce the identity on $\Delta$. Let $\mathcal{N} \subseteq \mathrm{GL}(V_L)$ be the image of $\mathcal{A}_L$. The outer classes of the conjugates $\eta\theta\eta^{-1}$, $\eta \in \mathcal{A}_L$, lie among the finitely many conjugates of the class of $\theta$. Choose one such conjugate $\theta_j$ in each class that occurs; then every $\eta\theta\eta^{-1}$ is $\theta_j c_h$ with $c_h$ conjugation by some $h \in G$, which preserves the normal subgroup $G_L$ and acts on $V_L$ through the image of $h$ in $\Delta$. So $A$ has finitely many conjugates in $\mathcal{N}$, and the inverse image $Y \subseteq \mathcal{A}_L$ of its centralizer has finite index in $\mathcal{A}_L$. As in Step 4 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), $\Gamma_0 = \{\varphi^{\mathrm{ab}} : \varphi \in \Psi^{-1}(Y)\} \cap \mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$ has finite index in $\mathrm{Sp}(\Pi^{\mathrm{ab}})$.

*Step 5: the action of $\theta$.* $A$ commutes with $\mathrm{id} \oplus (X \otimes 1)$ for every $X \in \Gamma_0$. By the span fact, the $X - 1$ with $X \in \Gamma_0$ span $\mathrm{End}(\Pi_{\mathbb{Q}})$, so $A$ commutes with $0 \oplus (Z \otimes 1)$ for every $Z \in \mathrm{End}(\Pi_{\mathbb{Q}})$. For $Z = \mathrm{id}$ this says that $A$ commutes with the projection onto $I_L^s$ along $I_L^c$, so it preserves both summands. On $I_L^s \cong \Pi_{\mathbb{Q}} \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$, let $S$ be the restriction of $A$. Fix a basis $\omega_1, \dots, \omega_{2g}$ of $\Pi_{\mathbb{Q}}$, with elementary matrices $E_{ij}$, and write $L_\gamma$ and $R_y$ for left and right multiplication on $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$. As $S$ commutes with $E_{11} \otimes 1$, it maps $\omega_1 \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$ into itself, say $S(\omega_1 \otimes y) = \omega_1 \otimes t(y)$, and $S(\omega_i \otimes y) = S(E_{i1} \otimes 1)(\omega_1 \otimes y) = \omega_i \otimes t(y)$; so $S = 1 \otimes t$. As $A$ commutes with $\Delta$, which acts by $1 \otimes L_\gamma$, $t$ is an endomorphism of the left module $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$, so $t(y) = y\,t(1) = yc$ with $c = t(1)$. Then $\theta_*(\lambda\bar a_i) = \pi([\alpha_i] \otimes \lambda c) = \lambda c\,\bar a_i$, and likewise for $\bar b_i$. Finally $1 = S^{p^a} = 1 \otimes R_{c^{p^a}}$, so $c^{p^a} = 1$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Write $A = \theta_*$ and $\Delta = \mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$.

- Under (H1), part 1 applies. Right multiplication $R_c$ is a $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-linear automorphism of the left module $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$ with $R_c^{p^a} = 1$. Since $k(L) \le r k(L) < p - 1$, the Lemma with $r' = 1$ gives $R_c = 1$, so $c = 1$ and $A$ is the identity on $I_L^s$. The restriction of $A$ to $I_L^c \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]^r$ is $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-linear of order dividing $p^a$, and the Lemma with $r' = r$ shows that it is the identity.
- Under (H2), $A$ is a $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-linear automorphism of $I_L \cong \mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]^d$ (Step 1) with $A^{p^a} = 1$, and the Lemma with $r' = d$ shows that it is the identity.

So $A$ is the identity on $I_L$ in both cases. Steps 6, 7 and 8 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) use only that $A$ is the identity on $I_L$, that $\theta$ has order $p^a$ and induces the identity on $G/I_K$ and on $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$, that $G_L$ is normal in $G$, and that $\zeta_p \in L$. They give that $A$ is the identity on $V_L$, that $\theta$ induces the identity on the Demushkin group $G_L(p)$, of rank $[L:\mathbb{Q}_p] + 2 \ge 3$, and that $\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(L(p)/K)$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.**

*(a) The tame twist.* By the composition rule, $\beta_{u,a}^j = \beta_{u^j,\, a s_j}$ with $s_j = 1 + u + \dots + u^{j-1}$. As $\theta^{p^a} = 1$, $u^{p^a} = 1$ and $a s_{p^a} = 0$. For a prime $\ell \ne p$, the torsion of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times = \mu_{\ell - 1} \times U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell}$ of order prime to $\ell$ lies in $\mu_{\ell - 1}$, because $U^1_{\mathbb{Q}_\ell} \cong \mu_{\ell^\infty}(\mathbb{Q}_\ell) \times \mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is pro-$\ell$ ([multiplicative groups](multiplicative-group-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). So $u_\ell = 1$ unless $p \mid \ell - 1$. If $u \equiv 1 \pmod m$, then $s_{p^a} \equiv p^a \pmod m$ is a unit modulo $m$, so $a \equiv 0 \pmod m$. Then $\theta$ maps $\tau$ to $\tau^u \in \tau I_m$ and $\sigma$ to $\sigma\tau^a \in \sigma I_m$, so it induces the identity on $\Gamma/I_m = \mathrm{Gal}(T_m/K)$, which is topologically generated by the images of $\sigma$ and $\tau$.

*(b) Finite levels.* Put $k = \mathrm{ord}_m(q)$. In $\Gamma/I_m$ the image $\bar\tau$ has order $m$ and $\bar\sigma^k\bar\tau\bar\sigma^{-k} = \bar\tau^{q^k} = \bar\tau$. For $n \ge 1$ with $k \mid n$, let $H_{m,n} = I_m \cdot \overline{\langle\sigma^n\rangle}$, the closed subgroup generated by $\tau^m$ and $\sigma^n$. It is normal of index $mn$, since $\bar\sigma^n$ is central in $\Gamma/I_m$ and every element of $\Gamma$ is $\tau^x\sigma^y$ with $y \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ determined by the image in $\Gamma/I \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. Let $L'_{m,n} = T^{H_{m,n}} \subseteq T_m$ and $L_{m,n} = L'_{m,n}(\mu_p)$, a finite Galois extension of $K$ inside $T$, as $K(\mu_p)/K$ is tame. Its Galois group $\Delta$ embeds by restriction in $\Delta' \times C$, where $\Delta' = \Gamma/H_{m,n}$ and $C = \mathrm{Gal}(K(\mu_p)/K)$ is abelian of order dividing $p - 1$. So $[L_{m,n}:K]$ divides $mn(p-1)$, which is prime to $p$ if $p \nmid n$. By (a), $\theta$ induces the identity on $\Delta'$, and it induces the identity on $C$ because it preserves the cyclotomic character ([invariants of $G_K$](local-field-galois-invariants.md)). So it induces the identity on $\Delta$.

*(c) Representations.* The images of $\tau$ and $\sigma^k$ generate an abelian subgroup $A'$ of $\Delta'$ of index at most $k$, so $A = \Delta \cap (A' \times C)$ is an abelian subgroup of $\Delta$ of index at most $k$. Let $\psi$ be an irreducible representation of $\Delta$ over $\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$, and $\chi$ a character of $A$ occurring in its restriction. By Frobenius reciprocity $\psi$ occurs in the induced representation of $\chi$, of dimension $[\Delta : A]$. So $k(L_{m,n}) \le k$.

*(d) The union.* Since $wk < p - 1$, $p \nmid k$. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the set of $n$ with $k \mid n$ and $p \nmid n$; it is nonempty and closed under least common multiples. For $n \in \mathcal{S}$, part 2 applies to $L_{m,n}$, by (b) and (c), so $\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(L_{m,n}(p)/K)$. It remains to show $\Omega_{K,m} = \bigcup_{n \in \mathcal{S}} L_{m,n}(p)$; then, as in the proof of part 2 of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), $G_{\Omega_{K,m}} = \bigcap_{n \in \mathcal{S}} G_{L_{m,n}(p)}$ is preserved by $\theta$ and $g^{-1}\theta(g) \in G_{\Omega_{K,m}}$ for all $g \in G$.

- If $n \mid n'$, then $H_{m,n'} \subseteq H_{m,n}$, so $L_{m,n} \subseteq L_{m,n'}$ and $L_{m,n}(p) \subseteq L_{m,n'}(p)$. Each $L_{m,n}$ lies in $T_m(\mu_p)$, and $L_{m,n}(p)T_m(\mu_p)/T_m(\mu_p)$ is pro-$p$, so the union is a field contained in $\Omega_{K,m}$.
- The intersection of the $H_{m,n}$ over all $n$ with $k \mid n$ is $I_m$, because $\tau^x\sigma^y \in H_{m,n}$ forces $y \in n\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$. So $T_m(\mu_p)$ is the union of these $L_{m,n}$. For such $n = p^jn'$ with $p \nmid n'$, also $k \mid n'$, and $\mathrm{Gal}(L_{m,n}/L_{m,n'})$ embeds in $H_{m,n'}/H_{m,n}$, which is cyclic of order $p^j$; so $L_{m,n} \subseteq L_{m,n'}(p)$ with $n' \in \mathcal{S}$.
- Every finite Galois extension $F'/T_m(\mu_p)$ of $p$-power degree is contained in some $L_{m,n}(p)$, $n \in \mathcal{S}$, by the argument of the third point of that proof, with $L_m$ replaced by $L_{m,n}$. So $\Omega_{K,m}$ is contained in the union. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.**

(1) In $\Gamma$, $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}\tau^{-1} = \tau^{q-1}$, so the closed commutator subgroup contains $I_{q-1}$, and $\Gamma/I_{q-1}$ is abelian, being generated by the images of $\sigma$ and $\tau$, which commute there. So $T_{q-1} = K^{\mathrm{ab}} \cap T$. Restriction maps $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ onto $\mathrm{Gal}(T_{q-1}/K)$, which contains $I/I_{q-1}$, cyclic of order $q - 1$ and generated by the image of $\tau$, on which $\theta$ acts by $\bar\tau \mapsto \bar\tau^u$. So if $\theta$ is the identity on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$, it is the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(T_{q-1}/K)$, and then $u \equiv 1 \pmod{q-1}$. Conversely, suppose $u \equiv 1 \pmod{q-1}$. Then $\theta$ is the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(T_{q-1}/K)$ by (a) of the proof of part 3, and part 3 applies with $m = q - 1$, as $\mathrm{ord}_{q-1}(q) = 1$ and $w < p - 1$. The subgroup of $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ fixing $T_{q-1}$ is the image of $P_K$, which is pro-$p$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). So $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is a pro-$p$ extension of $T_{q-1}$, which contains $\mu_p$ as $K(\mu_p)/K$ is abelian and tame, and $K^{\mathrm{ab}} \subseteq \Omega_{K,q-1}$. Hence $\theta$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,q-1}/K)$ and on its quotient $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$. If no prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$ divides $q - 1$, then $u \equiv 1 \pmod{q-1}$ by part 3. For residue degree $1$, the primes dividing $q - 1 = p - 1$ are smaller than $p$. For residue degree $2$, $q - 1 = (p-1)(p+1)$, and a prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$ dividing $p + 1$ would equal $p + 1$, which is even and larger than $2$.

(2) Suppose $u = 1$. Then $a s_{p^a} = a p^a = 0$ in $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}'$, where $p$ is invertible, so $a = 0$ and $\theta$ induces the identity on $\Gamma$. By part 3, $\theta$ is the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,m}/K)$ whenever $r\,\mathrm{ord}_m(q) < p - 1$. If this held for every $m$ prime to $p$, then $\theta(g)g^{-1}$ would lie in $G_{\Omega}$ for every $g \in G$, where $\Omega$ is the union of the fields $\Omega_{K,m}$. They increase along divisibility: if $m \mid m'$, then $T_m \subseteq T_{m'}$, the degree of $T_{m'}(\mu_p)/T_m(\mu_p)$ divides $m'/m$ and is prime to $p$, and a $p$-extension of $T_m(\mu_p)$ becomes one of $T_{m'}(\mu_p)$ after composing. Every finite subextension of $T/K$ lies in some $T_m$, because its group meets $I$ in an open subgroup, which contains some $I_m$. So $\Omega \supseteq T$. If $F'/T$ is finite Galois of $p$-power degree, generated by $\vartheta$, choose $m$ such that $T_m(\mu_p)$ contains the coefficients of the minimal polynomial of $\vartheta$ over $T$ and of the polynomials expressing its roots in $\vartheta$; then $T_m(\mu_p)(\vartheta)$ is Galois of $p$-power degree over $T_m(\mu_p)$, so $\vartheta \in \Omega_{K,m}$. Thus $\Omega$ contains every finite $p$-extension of $T$. As $\bar K/T$ is pro-$p$, $\Omega = \bar K$, and $\theta = 1$, a contradiction.

(3) By the Corollary of [finite normal subgroups](finite-normal-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), which uses its part 3 (a), there is an automorphism $\theta$ of order $p$ in $NG_K$, lying in neither $G_K$ nor $N$. Its class lies in $NG_K/G_K$, a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ by part 2 (a) of [rival copies](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), so it has finitely many conjugates, and (H1) holds. Apply part 3 and part 1 of the Corollary. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Relation to earlier entries.* For $m = 1$, part 3 is the Theorem of [finitely many outer conjugates](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-with-finitely-many-outer-conjugates.md) under (H1) and that of [small degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-small-degree.md) under (H2), whose levels $K_n(\mu_p)$ have abelian Galois groups. The case $p = 3$ with $d$ even, treated in [even degree](p-power-order-automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-of-even-degree.md) for $m = 1$, is not covered here. The new points are the ramified levels $T_m$, and the nonabelian ones, where the character decomposition of the earlier proofs is replaced by the computation of the relation module from the presentation, in Step 2, and by the Lemma. Kondo's automorphisms alone would not suffice for Step 2: they show only that the projection of $R$ to $M^s$ has the form $\Pi_{\mathbb{Q}} \otimes Y$ for a left ideal $Y$, which forces $Y = 0$ only when $k(L) < 2g$.
- *Examples.* For $K = \mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)$ with $p \ge 5$, $d = p - 1$ is even, $r = 2$ and $q = p$. So every automorphism of $G_K$ of $p$-power order with finitely many outer conjugates induces the identity on $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ and on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,p-1}/K)$, and on $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,m}/K)$ for every $m$ prime to $p$ with $u \equiv 1 \pmod m$ and $2\,\mathrm{ord}_m(p) < p - 1$. The condition on $q - 1$ in Corollary 1 can fail: for $p = 5$ and residue degree $3$, $q - 1 = 124 = 4 \cdot 31$ and $31 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$.
- *What remains for case (a).* By Corollary 2, an automorphism of order $p$ with finitely many outer conjugates, if one exists, either twists tame inertia at a prime $\ell \equiv 1 \pmod p$, or acts trivially on $\Gamma$ but nontrivially on some $\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega_{K,m}/K)$ with $r\,\mathrm{ord}_m(q) \ge p - 1$. Neither is excluded here. In the first case $\theta$ moves the image of $I$ in $\mathrm{Gal}(T_\ell/K)$, and on the level $G_K/[P_K, P_K]$ such twists do lift with order $p$ when $\mu_p \subset K$ ([lifting modulo wild commutators](tame-automorphisms-lift-as-a-group-modulo-wild-commutators.md)). In the second case, on each level $L$ of degree prime to $p$ the only freedom left by part 1 is the unit $c$ on the surface part and a $\mathbb{Q}_p[\Delta]$-linear automorphism of $I_L^c$, both of $p$-power order; the Lemma excludes them only for small representations. If $p$ divides $\mathrm{ord}_m(q)$, the levels inside $T_m$ that see all of $I/I_m$ have degree divisible by $p$, and part 1 does not apply to them at all.
