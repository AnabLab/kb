**Setting.** Let $p$ be any prime, $K/\mathbb Q_p$ finite, $v_K(K^\times)=\mathbb Z$, $e=v_K(p)$ and $\pi$ a uniformizer. Use Serre's upper numbering for $G_K^u$, and put
$$c_n=e\left(n+\frac1{p-1}\right)-1\qquad(n\ge1).$$
For $a\in K$ with $0<|a|_p<1$, write $E_a$ for the Tate elliptic curve. Its uniformization is the $G_K$-equivariant isomorphism $E_a(\bar K)=\bar K^\times/a^{\mathbb Z}$ [@kedlaya2004tate, Theorem 1, p. 3].

**Theorem (deduced here).** The single curve $E=E_{\pi^p}$ satisfies
$$G_K^u\text{ acts trivially on }E[p^n](\bar K)
\qquad\text{for every }n\ge1\text{ and }u>c_n.$$
Nevertheless $E$ has split multiplicative reduction and $V_pE$ is semistable and noncrystalline, with Hodge–Tate weights $0,1$ when $\mathbb Q_p(1)$ has weight $1$.

If $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is unramified, then for every $n\ge2$ the splitting field of $E[p^n]$ has last upper ramification break exactly $c_n$. Thus $G_K^{c_n}$ acts nontrivially at every such level.

**Ramification input.** The [radical splitting-field calculation](ramification-of-uniformizer-radical-splitting-fields.md) gives the last upper break of
$$S_m=K(\zeta_{p^m},\pi_m),\qquad \pi_m^{p^m}=\pi,$$
as $b_m=e(m+1/(p-1))$ for every prime $p$. It applies Hattori's general polynomial criterion, rather than his semistable representation bound with hypothesis $r<p-1$.

**Proof.** Choose compatible $\pi_m$ with $\pi_0=\pi$ and $\pi_{m+1}^p=\pi_m$, and compatible primitive $\zeta_{p^m}$. Define $c(g)\in\mathbb Z_p$ by
$$g(\pi_m)/\pi_m=\zeta_{p^m}^{c(g)}.$$
The Tate-module formula gives
$$\rho_\pi(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_p(g)&c(g)\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$$
[@brinonconrad2009, Example 9.2.1, p. 136]. For $q=\pi^p$, use the compatible roots $q_m=\pi_m^p$, including $q_0=q$. The corresponding cocycle is $p\,c(g)$, and therefore
$$\rho_q(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_p(g)&p\,c(g)\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1}$$

Fix $n\ge1$ and $u>c_n$. Applying [Fontaine's bound](fontaine-ramification-bound-for-finite-flat-torsion.md) to $\mu_{p^n}/\mathcal O_K$ gives $\chi_p(g)\equiv1\pmod{p^n}$ for $g\in G_K^u$. If $n=1$, the other entry in (1) already vanishes modulo $p$. If $n\ge2$, then
$$c_n-b_{n-1}=e-1\ge0.$$
Thus $u>b_{n-1}$, and the radical splitting-field calculation makes $g$ fix $S_{n-1}$. In particular it fixes $\pi_{n-1}$, so $c(g)\in p^{n-1}\mathbb Z_p$ and $p\,c(g)\in p^n\mathbb Z_p$. Equation (1) is the identity modulo $p^n$, proving the assertion at every level.

The curve has split multiplicative reduction [@kedlaya2004tate, Theorem 2(b), p. 4]. Its rational Tate module has Kummer class $q=\pi^p$, whose valuation is nonzero. The [Kummer crystalline criterion](kummer-extensions-and-tate-curve-periods.md) therefore makes it semistable and noncrystalline; this is the unit hyperplane of [@brinonconrad2009, Lemma 8.3.9 and proof, p. 125]. Its two weights are those of $\mathbb Q_p(1)$ and $\mathbb Q_p$.

For the endpoint assertion take $e=1$ and $n\ge2$. Uniformization shows that $E[p^n]$ is generated by the classes of $\zeta_{p^n}$ and $q_n=\pi_{n-1}$. These form a basis: a relation $\zeta_{p^n}^a q_n^b\in q^{\mathbb Z}$ first forces $p^n\mid b$ by valuation, and then $p^n\mid a$. An automorphism fixes both classes exactly when it fixes both representatives, since the ratio of a representative and its conjugate is a root of unity, while $q^{\mathbb Z}$ contains no nonidentity root of unity. Consequently the splitting field is
$$R_n=K(\zeta_{p^n},\pi_{n-1}).$$
It contains $S_{n-1}=K(\zeta_{p^{n-1}},\pi_{n-1})$, whose last break is $b_{n-1}=c_n$ by the same calculation. Compatibility of upper ramification groups with quotients gives a last break at least $c_n$ for $R_n/K$, while the first part gives a last break at most $c_n$. This proves equality. In particular, for $p=2$ and unramified $K/\mathbb Q_2$, the curve $E_{\pi^2}$ attains $c_n=n$ at every $n\ge2$. $\square$

**Scope.** These are the same numerical cutoffs as for finite flat group schemes, imposed simultaneously on one fixed geometric lattice. They therefore do not characterize crystallinity or good reduction. At level one, (1) splits as $\mu_p\oplus\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$; over an unramified base its splitting field is tame, so the endpoint assertion starts at $n=2$. For every prime, the [finite flat model criterion](finite-flat-models-of-tate-curve-torsion.md) shows that $E_{\pi^p}[p^n]$ has no finite flat model for any $n\ge2$, despite passing its numerical cutoff.
