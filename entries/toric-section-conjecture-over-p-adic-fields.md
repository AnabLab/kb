**Setting.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Curves are smooth, projective and geometrically connected [@bresciani2024toric, §1, p. 1]. For a curve $X$ of genus at least $1$, or a torsor $X$ under an abelian variety, write $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ for the Galois sections of $\pi_1(X) \to G_k$ up to conjugation and $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k} \subseteq \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ for the [toric Galois sections](toric-galois-sections.md), which form a subset by the injectivity recorded there. A section is *geometric* if it comes from a point of $X(k)$. The *index* of a curve is the greatest common divisor of the degrees of its closed points [@bresciani2024toric, §1.3, p. 2]. An *étale neighbourhood* $(Y, r)$ of $s \in \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ is a geometrically connected finite étale cover $Y \to X$ with $r \in \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{Y/k}$ mapping to $s$ [@bresciani2024toric, §7, p. 23].

**Theorem (Bresciani).**

1. *Abelian varieties.* For a torsor $B$ under an abelian variety over $k$, the toric Kummer map $B(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{B/k}$ is bijective [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 8.1, p. 25]. For abelian varieties this is [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 1.3, p. 2].
2. *Curves.* For a curve $X$ of genus at least $2$ over $k$, the toric Kummer map $X(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ is bijective [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 1.4, p. 2].
3. *Index.* For such $X$, every non-geometric $s \in \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ has an étale neighbourhood $(Y, r)$ such that $p$ divides the index of $Y$. If $X(k) = \varnothing$, then $Y \to X$ can be taken geometrically Galois of $p$-power degree [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 1.5, p. 2]. More generally, this choice is possible whenever the pro-$p$ section induced by $s$ is not geometric [@bresciani2024toric, §1.3, p. 2, and §10.5, pp. 37–38].

Part 2 is equivalent to the first assertion of part 3, by the injectivity of toric into étale sections, Stix's theorem and the criterion below [@bresciani2024toric, §10, p. 34].

**A criterion for geometric sections.** Let $X$ be a curve of genus at least $1$ over $k$ and $s \in \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$. The following are equivalent [@bresciani2024toric, Proposition 8.6, p. 27]:

- (a) $s$ is toric;
- (b) every étale neighbourhood of $s$ has index $1$;
- (c) every étale neighbourhood $(Y, r)$ of $s$ has $\mathrm{Pic}^1_Y(k) \ne \varnothing$;
- (d) for every étale neighbourhood $(Y, r)$ of $s$, the image of $r$ in $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{\mathrm{Pic}^1_Y/k}$ is geometric.

For genus at least $2$, part 2 identifies the toric sections with the geometric ones inside $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$. Indeed, $X \to \Pi^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ factors through $X \to \Pi^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$, so the usual map $X(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ is the toric Kummer map followed by the inclusion. Then (b), (c) and (d) become criteria for $s$ to be geometric. Condition (d) concerns the abelianized sections studied in [abelianized Galois sections](abelianized-galois-sections.md).

**Corollary (reduction to curves of index $\ne 1$).** Fix $k$ finite over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. The map $X(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ is bijective for every curve $X$ of genus at least $2$ over $k$ exactly when no curve of genus at least $2$ over $k$ whose index is not $1$ has a Galois section. By [Stix's theorem](section-conjecture-period-index.md), a curve with a section has index a power of $p$, so the condition concerns curves of index $p^m$ with $m \ge 1$.

*Proof.* The map is always injective, by [Mochizuki's theorem](section-conjecture-injectivity.md), since $k$ is sub-$p$-adic. If it is bijective for every such curve, then a curve with a section has a rational point, hence index $1$. Conversely, assume that no curve of genus at least $2$ over $k$ with index $\ne 1$ has a section, and let $s \in \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$. If $s$ were not geometric, part 3 would give an étale neighbourhood $(Y, r)$ whose index is divisible by $p$. Here $Y$ is a geometrically connected finite étale cover of $X$, so it is a curve over $k$. By Riemann–Hurwitz, $2g_Y - 2 = \deg(Y/X)(2g_X - 2)$, so $g_Y \ge g_X \ge 2$. Then $r$ is a section of $Y$, which has index $\ne 1$, a contradiction. So $s$ is geometric. $\square$

Bresciani states this informally: Tamagawa's result reduces the $p$-adic conjecture to curves without rational points, and Theorem 1.5 allows one to focus on curves of index different from $1$ [@bresciani2024toric, §1.3, pp. 2–3]. A version for all finite extensions of $k$ at once, with $\mathrm{Br}(X/k) \ne 0$ in place of index $\ne 1$, follows from part 2 and the [Brauer form of the comparison](toric-galois-sections.md) [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 7.6, p. 25]. The two conditions agree by a theorem of Roquette and Lichtenbaum, which Bresciani uses in the proof of Proposition 8.6 [@bresciani2024toric, p. 27].

**Selmer sections over number fields.** Let $k$ be a number field and $X$ a curve of genus at least $2$ over $k$. A section $s \in \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ is *Selmer* if its base change to $k_\nu$ is geometric for every finite place $\nu$, and $\mathrm{Sel}_{X/k}$ denotes the set of Selmer sections [@bresciani2024toric, §1.3, p. 3]. Then
$$\mathrm{Sel}_{X/k} = \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k} \subseteq \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$$
[@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 1.6, p. 3]. Betts and Stix also impose a condition at the infinite places in [their definition](finiteness-of-local-images-of-selmer-sections.md). That condition holds automatically: at a real place every section comes from a real point, by the [real section theorem](real-section-conjecture.md), and Bresciani's proof uses exactly this [@bresciani2024toric, §11, p. 38]. So their finiteness theorem for the local images of Selmer sections is a statement about toric sections; Bresciani mentions it after Theorem 1.6 [@bresciani2024toric, §1.3, p. 3].

Bresciani's toric section conjecture for $X$ over $k$, the bijectivity of $X(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$ [@bresciani2024toric, Conjecture 1.2, p. 2], therefore says exactly that every Selmer section of $X$ is geometric. Injectivity is automatic, since the injective map $X(k) \to \mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ét}}_{X/k}$ factors through $\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{tor}}_{X/k}$.

*Proof outline.* A Selmer section has Selmer neighbourhoods, so these have points at every finite place, and at the real places by the real section theorem. By the Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem their relative Brauer groups then vanish over every finite extension. The [comparison theorem](toric-galois-sections.md) then makes the section toric. Conversely, let $s$ be toric. By part 2 over $k_\nu$ and the criterion above, if $s$ were not geometric at $\nu$, some étale neighbourhood would have index $\ne 1$ over $k_\nu$, hence a non-zero relative Brauer class $b \in \mathrm{Br}(k_\nu)$. The toric gerbe does not commute with the transcendental extension $k_\nu/k$, so Bresciani works over the algebraic closure $F$ of $k$ in $k_\nu$, where base change is allowed. The Brauer–Hasse–Noether theorem lifts $b$ to $\mathrm{Br}(F)$. Since $F \subset k_\nu$ is an elementary extension (Pop), the neighbourhood over $F$ maps to a non-trivial Brauer–Severi variety over $F$. But $s$ induces a toric section over $F$, and a non-trivial Brauer–Severi variety has none [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 11.1 and proof of Theorem 1.6, pp. 38–39].

**Proof outline for parts 1–3.**

- *Abelian torsors.* If a toric section of $B$ is not geometric, a compactness argument gives an étale neighbourhood $B'$ with $B'(k) = \varnothing$ [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 8.4, p. 27]. On $\mathrm{Pic}^0$, the Brauer obstruction is the local Tate pairing [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 8.2, p. 26]. So by local Tate duality, a torsor without points has non-zero relative Brauer group [@bresciani2024toric, Corollary 8.3, p. 26], and then it has no toric sections, a contradiction [@bresciani2024toric, proof of Theorem 8.1, p. 27].
- *Curves.* Applied to $\mathrm{Pic}^1$ of each neighbourhood, part 1 gives each étale neighbourhood $Y$ of a toric section an origin on $J_Y = \mathrm{Pic}^1_Y$ [@bresciani2024toric, Convention 10.2, p. 34]. Pulling back multiplication by $p^n$ along $Y \subset J_Y$ then gives further neighbourhoods $p^{-n}Y \to Y$ [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 10.3, p. 35]. If $s$ is toric and not geometric, Raynaud's theorem (the Manin–Mumford conjecture) and Proposition 10.5 give a neighbourhood with $Y \cap J_Y[p'] = \varnothing$, where $J_Y[p']$ is the prime-to-$p$ torsion [@bresciani2024toric, Proposition 10.4, p. 35]. Passing to $p^{-2}Y$ makes the curve non-hyperelliptic [@bresciani2024toric, Lemma 10.6, p. 37]. Then $Y - Y \subset J_Y$ contains no translate of a positive-dimensional abelian subvariety over $\bar k$. The following input gives $n$ with $p$ dividing the index of $p^{-n}Y$, contradicting criterion (b) [@bresciani2024toric, §10.4, p. 37].
- *The key input.* Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $k$, and let $X \subset A$ be a closed subvariety with $X \cap A[p'] = \varnothing$. Assume that $A$ has potentially good reduction, or that $(X - X)_{\bar k}$ contains no translate of a positive-dimensional abelian subvariety. Then $p^{-n}X$ has index divisible by $p$ for all large $n$ [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 9.1, p. 28]. The proof uses Scanlon's uniform lower bound on the $p$-adic distance from a subvariety to the prime-to-$p$ torsion points not on it, defined over the completed maximal unramified extension [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 9.17, p. 33]. It needs that bound in its strong form [@bresciani2024toric, Remark 9.5, p. 29].

**A geometric byproduct.** Let $X$ be a curve of genus at least $2$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$. For distinct points $x_1, x_2 \in X$ and every integer $n \ge 2$, there is a finite étale cover $Y \to X$ such that $m([y_1] - [y_2]) \ne 0$ in $\mathrm{Pic}^0(Y)$ for all $y_1, y_2 \in Y$ over $x_1, x_2$ and all $m$ prime to $n$ [@bresciani2024toric, Theorem 1.7, p. 3]. It follows from Proposition 10.5, a statement for one prime $p$ whose proof descends to a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and uses Mochizuki's pro-$p$ injectivity [@bresciani2024toric, Proposition 10.5 and its proof, pp. 35–36]. Bresciani asks whether it holds in positive characteristic, and whether $[y_1] - [y_2]$ can be made non-torsion, the case $n = 1$ [@bresciani2024toric, §1.4, p. 3].

**Scope.** Nothing here settles the étale section conjecture over $p$-adic fields, which Bresciani describes as unknown [@bresciani2024toric, §1.3, p. 2]. The toric statement is weaker, because toric sections are only a subset of the étale ones. The étale form needs, in addition, that curves of index $\ne 1$ have no sections.
