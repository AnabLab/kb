**Setting.** Use the notation of [the lower central series of pro-$p$ surface groups](lower-central-series-of-pro-p-surface-groups.md): $p$ is a prime, $g \ge 2$, $D = F/R$ is the pro-$p$ surface group of genus $g$, $\xi_1, \dots, \xi_{2g}$ is the basis of $V = D^{\mathrm{ab}} = \mathrm{gr}_1 D$ given by the generators, $D$ is identified with $\mathrm{Inn}(D)$, $K_i$ is the kernel of $\mathrm{Aut}(D) \to \mathrm{Aut}(D/D_{i+1})$, and $J_i : K_i/K_{i+1} \to \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(V, \mathrm{gr}_{i+1} D)$ is the Johnson homomorphism. For $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ write $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} \in \mathrm{GL}(V)$ for the induced map.

Put $e_i = \xi_{2i-1}$ and $f_i = \xi_{2i}$ for $1 \le i \le g$, and $\omega = \sum_{i=1}^g e_i \wedge f_i \in \Lambda^2 V$.

- *Degree $2$.* Part 1 of that entry identifies $\mathrm{gr}\, D$ with $L/(\rho)$, where $L$ is the free Lie algebra over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ on $X_1, \dots, X_{2g}$, $X_j \mapsto \xi_j$ and $\rho = \sum_i [X_{2i-1}, X_{2i}]$. In degree $2$, $L_2$ is free on the $[X_a, X_b]$ with $a < b$, because these map to the linearly independent elements $X_aX_b - X_bX_a$ of the free associative algebra, into which $L$ embeds (Inputs of that entry). The ideal $(\rho)$ meets $L_2$ in $\mathbb{Z}_p\rho$. So $v \wedge w \mapsto [v, w]$ induces an isomorphism $\Lambda^2 V/\mathbb{Z}_p\omega \cong \mathrm{gr}_2 D$, under which $\mathrm{gr}_2(\alpha)$ becomes the map induced by $\Lambda^2(\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}})$.
- *The module $X$.* Put $X = \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(V, \Lambda^2 V/\mathbb{Z}_p\omega)$, which the previous item identifies with $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_p}(V, \mathrm{gr}_2 D)$. An element $A \in \mathrm{GL}(V)$ with $\Lambda^2(A)\omega \in \mathbb{Z}_p\omega$ acts on $X$ by $A \cdot x = \Lambda^2(A) \circ x \circ A^{-1}$. By part 4(a) of that entry, $J_1(\alpha\kappa\alpha^{-1}) = \alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} \cdot J_1(\kappa)$.
- *Inner part.* Let $\iota : V \to X$ be $\iota(v)(w) = v \wedge w \bmod \omega$. It is equivariant: $A \cdot \iota(v) = \iota(Av)$. By part 4(b) of that entry, with $\mathrm{ad}_1(v) = [v, -]$, $J_1$ maps $DK_2/K_2$ onto $\iota(V)$.
- *The symplectic group.* Let $\mathrm{Sp}$ be the group of $A \in \mathrm{GL}(V)$ with $\Lambda^2(A)\omega = \omega$. For every alternating matrix $M$, $\Lambda^2(A)$ maps $\sum_{a<b} M_{ab}\,\xi_a \wedge \xi_b$ to $\sum_{a<b} (AMA^{\mathsf T})_{ab}\,\xi_a \wedge \xi_b$. With $M = \Omega$ as in [symplectic similitudes lift](symplectic-similitudes-lift-to-pro-p-surface-groups.md), $\mathrm{Sp}$ is therefore the group of $A$ with $A\Omega A^{\mathsf T} = \Omega$. By part 2 of that theorem, every $A \in \mathrm{Sp}$ is $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}}$ for some $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. Let $g \ge 2$ and let $p$ be any prime.

1. *Representation lemma.* Let $U \subseteq X \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ be a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-subspace of dimension $2g$ with $A \cdot U = U$ for every $A \in \mathrm{Sp}$. Then $U = \iota(V) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$.
2. *Inner part of $K_1/K_2$.* $K_1/K_2$ is a free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module of finite rank, and $DK_2/K_2$ is a direct summand of rank $2g$. Let $M \subseteq K_1/K_2$ be a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-submodule of rank $2g$ that is stable under conjugation by every $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(D)$ with $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} \in \mathrm{Sp}$. Then $M \subseteq DK_2/K_2$. In particular, $DK_2/K_2$ is the unique $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$-invariant direct summand of $K_1/K_2$ of rank $2g$.

**Proof of 1.** Write $X_{\mathbb{Q}} = X \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ and $V_{\mathbb{Q}} = V \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$. The following elements of $\mathrm{GL}(V)$ lie in $\mathrm{Sp}$, as one checks on the terms $e_j \wedge f_j$ of $\omega$.

- For $t = (t_1, \dots, t_g) \in (\mathbb{Z}_p^\times)^g$, $h_t$ maps $e_i \mapsto t_ie_i$ and $f_i \mapsto t_i^{-1}f_i$. It fixes each $e_j \wedge f_j$.
- For a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{1, \dots, g\}$, $P_\sigma$ maps $e_i \mapsto e_{\sigma(i)}$ and $f_i \mapsto f_{\sigma(i)}$. It permutes the $e_j \wedge f_j$.
- For $1 \le j \le g$, $s_j$ maps $e_j \mapsto f_j$ and $f_j \mapsto -e_j$, and fixes the other basis vectors. It maps $e_j \wedge f_j$ to $f_j \wedge (-e_j) = e_j \wedge f_j$.
- Let $N_g \in \mathrm{End}(V)$ map $f_g \mapsto e_g$, and for $1 \le i \le g-1$ let $N_i$ map $e_{i+1} \mapsto e_i$ and $f_i \mapsto -f_{i+1}$; both kill the other basis vectors. Then $N_i^2 = 0$, and for $c \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, $1 + cN_g$ maps $e_g \wedge f_g$ to $e_g \wedge (f_g + ce_g) = e_g \wedge f_g$, while $1 + cN_i$ maps $e_i \wedge f_i + e_{i+1} \wedge f_{i+1}$ to $e_i \wedge (f_i - cf_{i+1}) + (e_{i+1} + ce_i) \wedge f_{i+1} = e_i \wedge f_i + e_{i+1} \wedge f_{i+1}$, fixing the other terms.

*Step 1: weights.* Let $\varepsilon_1, \dots, \varepsilon_g$ be the unit vectors of $\mathbb{Z}^g$. For $\mu \in \mathbb{Z}^g$ put $t^\mu = \prod_i t_i^{\mu_i}$ and $X_\mu = \{x \in X_{\mathbb{Q}} : h_t \cdot x = t^\mu x \text{ for all } t\}$. Give $e_i$ weight $\varepsilon_i$ and $f_i$ weight $-\varepsilon_i$. The space $\Lambda^2 V/\omega$ is spanned by the classes of $b' \wedge b''$ for distinct basis vectors $b', b''$, of weight $\nu(b') + \nu(b'')$, which is $\pm\varepsilon_a \pm \varepsilon_b$ with $a \ne b$, or $0$ for $e_a \wedge f_a$. The map sending one basis vector $b$ to the class of $b' \wedge b''$ and the other basis vectors to $0$ lies in $X_\mu$ with $\mu = \nu(b') + \nu(b'') - \nu(b)$. These maps span $X_{\mathbb{Q}}$, so $X_{\mathbb{Q}} = \bigoplus_\mu X_\mu$, and $X_\mu \ne 0$ only for $\mu$ of one of three shapes:

- (i) $\pm\varepsilon_k$;
- (ii) $\pm 2\varepsilon_a \pm \varepsilon_b$ with $a \ne b$;
- (iii) $\pm\varepsilon_a \pm \varepsilon_b \pm \varepsilon_k$ with $a, b, k$ distinct, which needs $g \ge 3$.

In particular $\mu = 0$ does not occur, and all coordinates of weights lie in $\{-2, \dots, 2\}$.

*Step 2: $U = \bigoplus_\mu (U \cap X_\mu)$.* Let $\lambda = 1 + p$, which is not a root of unity in $\mathbb{Q}_p$: for odd $p$ the only root of unity in $1 + p\mathbb{Z}_p$ is $1$, and for $p = 2$ the roots of unity in $\mathbb{Q}_2$ are $\pm 1$. Put $t = (\lambda, \lambda^5, \lambda^{25}, \dots, \lambda^{5^{g-1}})$. Then $h_t$ acts on $X_\mu$ as $\lambda^{m(\mu)}$ with $m(\mu) = \sum_i \mu_i 5^{i-1}$, and $m$ is injective on vectors with coordinates in $\{-2, \dots, 2\}$. So $h_t$ is diagonalizable with distinct eigenvalues on the distinct $X_\mu$. A subspace stable under a diagonalizable operator is the sum of its intersections with the eigenspaces.

*Step 3: the weights of $U$.* Let $w$ be $P_\sigma$ or $s_j$. Then $w^{-1} h_t w = h_{t'}$, where $t'$ permutes the $t_i$ by $\sigma$, or replaces $t_j$ by $t_j^{-1}$. So $w$ maps $X_\mu$ onto $X_{w(\mu)}$, where $w(\mu)$ permutes the coordinates of $\mu$, or changes the sign of the $j$-th coordinate. As $wU = U$, the function $\mu \mapsto \dim(U \cap X_\mu)$ is invariant under all signed permutations of coordinates. The weights of shapes (i), (ii) and (iii) form orbits of sizes $2g$, $4g(g-1)$ and $8\binom{g}{3} = \frac{4}{3}g(g-1)(g-2)$. For $g \ge 2$, $4g(g-1) > 2g$, and for $g \ge 3$, $\frac{4}{3}g(g-1)(g-2) > 2g$, since $(g-1)(g-2) \ge 2 > \frac{3}{2}$. As $\sum_\mu \dim(U \cap X_\mu) = \dim U = 2g$, $U$ meets no $X_\mu$ of shape (ii) or (iii), and $\dim(U \cap X_\mu) = 1$ for each of the $2g$ weights $\mu = \pm\varepsilon_k$.

*Step 4: root operators.* For $N \in \{N_1, \dots, N_g\}$ and $c \in \mathbb{Z}_p$, $(1 + cN)^{-1} = 1 - cN$. So for $x \in X_{\mathbb{Q}}$, $(1 + cN) \cdot x = \Lambda^2(1 + cN) \circ x \circ (1 - cN)$ is a polynomial in $c$ of degree at most $3$ with coefficients in $X_{\mathbb{Q}}$. Its coefficient of $c$ is
$$N \cdot x = N^{(2)} \circ x - x \circ N, \qquad N^{(2)}(v \wedge w) = Nv \wedge w + v \wedge Nw,$$
where $N^{(2)}$ is well defined on $\Lambda^2 V/\omega$ because $N^{(2)}\omega$ is the coefficient of $c$ in $\Lambda^2(1 + cN)\omega = \omega$. Since $U$ is stable under $1 + cN$ for infinitely many $c$, a Vandermonde argument shows that each coefficient maps $U$ into $U$; in particular $N \cdot U \subseteq U$. Also $h_t N_i h_t^{-1} = t^{\alpha_i} N_i$, with $\alpha_i = \varepsilon_i - \varepsilon_{i+1}$ for $i < g$ and $\alpha_g = 2\varepsilon_g$, so $N_i \cdot X_\mu \subseteq X_{\mu + \alpha_i}$.

Let $u$ span $U \cap X_{\varepsilon_1}$. Then $N_i \cdot u \in U \cap X_{\varepsilon_1 + \alpha_i}$. The weights $\varepsilon_1 + \alpha_1 = 2\varepsilon_1 - \varepsilon_2$, $\varepsilon_1 + \alpha_i = \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_i - \varepsilon_{i+1}$ for $2 \le i \le g-1$, and $\varepsilon_1 + \alpha_g = \varepsilon_1 + 2\varepsilon_g$ are not of the form $\pm\varepsilon_k$, as $g \ge 2$. By Step 3, $N_i \cdot u = 0$ for $1 \le i \le g$.

*Step 5: the vectors of weight $\varepsilon_1$ killed by all $N_i$.* An element $x \in X_{\varepsilon_1}$ maps a basis vector of weight $\nu$ into the weight-$(\nu + \varepsilon_1)$ part of $\Lambda^2 V/\omega$. The weight $2\varepsilon_1$ does not occur there, the weight $\varepsilon_1 \pm \varepsilon_k$ part is spanned by $e_1 \wedge e_k$ or $e_1 \wedge f_k$ for $k \ge 2$, and the weight-$0$ part is spanned by the classes of the $e_j \wedge f_j$. So $x \in X_{\varepsilon_1}$ has the form
$$x(e_1) = 0, \quad x(e_k) = a_k\, e_1 \wedge e_k, \quad x(f_k) = b_k\, e_1 \wedge f_k \ (2 \le k \le g), \quad x(f_1) = \textstyle\sum_{j=1}^g c_j\, e_j \wedge f_j,$$
with $a_k, b_k, c_j \in \mathbb{Q}_p$, where $(c_j)$ matters only modulo $\mathbb{Q}_p(1, \dots, 1)$. Evaluate $(N \cdot x)(b) = N^{(2)}(x(b)) - x(Nb)$ on the basis vectors $b$, using that the classes of $e_1 \wedge e_i$, $e_1 \wedge f_i$ for $i \ge 2$ and $e_i \wedge f_{i+1}$ are nonzero in $\Lambda^2 V/\omega$.

- $N_g$: at $b = f_g$ the value is $b_g\, e_1 \wedge e_g - x(e_g) = (b_g - a_g)\, e_1 \wedge e_g$. At every other basis vector $b$ the value is $0$, since $N_g b = 0$ and $N_g^{(2)}$ kills $e_1 \wedge e_k$ for $k \ge 2$, $e_1 \wedge f_k$ for $2 \le k < g$, and $\sum_j c_j e_j \wedge f_j$ (it maps the last to $c_g\, e_g \wedge e_g = 0$). So $N_g \cdot x = 0$ if and only if $a_g = b_g$.
- $N_i$ with $1 \le i \le g-1$: on $\Lambda^2 V$, $N_i^{(2)}$ maps $e_{i+1} \wedge f_{i+1} \mapsto e_i \wedge f_{i+1}$ and $e_i \wedge f_i \mapsto -e_i \wedge f_{i+1}$, and kills $e_j \wedge f_j$ for $j \ne i, i+1$. The nonzero values of $N_i \cdot x$ can only occur at $b = e_{i+1}$, $f_1$ and $f_i$:
  - at $b = e_{i+1}$: $a_{i+1}\, e_1 \wedge e_i - x(e_i)$, which is $0$ for $i = 1$ (as $e_1 \wedge e_1 = 0 = x(e_1)$) and $(a_{i+1} - a_i)\, e_1 \wedge e_i$ for $i \ge 2$;
  - at $b = f_1$: $(c_{i+1} - c_i)\, e_i \wedge f_{i+1} - x(N_if_1)$, where $N_if_1 = -f_2$ if $i = 1$ and $0$ otherwise; so the value is $(c_2 - c_1 + b_2)\, e_1 \wedge f_2$ for $i = 1$, and $(c_{i+1} - c_i)\, e_i \wedge f_{i+1}$ for $i \ge 2$;
  - at $b = f_i$ with $i \ge 2$: $-b_i\, e_1 \wedge f_{i+1} + b_{i+1}\, e_1 \wedge f_{i+1}$.

  At the other basis vectors $b$, $N_ib = 0$ and $N_i^{(2)}(x(b)) = 0$, because $N_ie_1 = 0$ and $N_i$ kills $e_k$ for $k \ne i+1$ and $f_k$ for $k \ne i$.

So the conditions are: $a_g = b_g$; $b_2 = c_1 - c_2$; and $a_{i+1} = a_i$, $b_{i+1} = b_i$, $c_{i+1} = c_i$ for $2 \le i \le g-1$. Hence $a_k = b_k = c_1 - c_2 =: s$ for all $k \ge 2$ and $c_2 = \dots = c_g$. Subtracting $c_2(1, \dots, 1)$ from $(c_j)$, which does not change $x$, gives $x(f_1) = s\, e_1 \wedge f_1$. So $x(v) = s\, e_1 \wedge v$ for every basis vector $v$, that is, $x = s\,\iota(e_1)$. Conversely every $s\,\iota(e_1)$ satisfies the conditions.

*Step 6: conclusion.* By Steps 4 and 5, $u$ is a nonzero multiple of $\iota(e_1)$, so $\iota(e_1) \in U$. By equivariance, $U$ contains $\iota(we_1)$ for every product $w$ of the $P_\sigma$ and $s_j$, and these vectors $we_1$ include $\pm e_k$ and $\pm f_k$ for all $k$. So $\iota(V_{\mathbb{Q}}) \subseteq U$. The map $\iota$ is injective: if $v \ne 0$, choose $w$ with $v \wedge w \ne 0$; then $v \wedge w$ is decomposable, while $\omega$ is not, since $\omega \wedge \omega = 2\sum_{i<j} e_i \wedge f_i \wedge e_j \wedge f_j \ne 0$ in $\Lambda^4 V_{\mathbb{Q}}$ for $g \ge 2$; so $v \wedge w \notin \mathbb{Q}_p\omega$. Thus $\dim \iota(V_{\mathbb{Q}}) = 2g = \dim U$, and $U = \iota(V_{\mathbb{Q}})$. $\square$

**Proof of 2.** The map $J_1$ is continuous on $K_1$, because evaluation $\kappa \mapsto \kappa(x)$ is continuous (proof of part 4(d) of the lower central series entry). So $J_1(K_1/K_2)$ is a compact subgroup of the free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module $X$ of finite rank, hence a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-submodule, free of finite rank. Use $J_1$, which is injective by part 4(a), to regard $K_1/K_2$ as this submodule. By part 4(b), $DK_2/K_2 \cong D/(D \cap K_2) = D/D_2 = V$ is free of rank $2g$. The quotient $(K_1/K_2)/(DK_2/K_2) = K_1/DK_2$ is torsion-free by part 4(c) with $i = 1$, since $D \subseteq K_1$ gives $DK_1 = K_1$ and $D_1K_2 = DK_2$. A finitely generated torsion-free $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module is free, so $DK_2/K_2$ is a direct summand.

Let $M$ be as in the statement. Then $U = J_1(M) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ is a subspace of $X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ of dimension $2g$, and for $A \in \mathrm{Sp}$, choosing $\alpha$ with $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} = A$, we get $A \cdot J_1(M) = J_1(\alpha M\alpha^{-1}) = J_1(M)$. By part 1, $U = \iota(V) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p = J_1(DK_2/K_2) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$. So for $m \in M$ some $p^km$ lies in $DK_2/K_2$, and since $K_1/DK_2$ is torsion-free, $m \in DK_2/K_2$.

Finally, $DK_2/K_2$ is $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$-invariant because $D$ and $K_2$ are normal. If $M$ is another invariant direct summand of rank $2g$, then $M \subseteq DK_2/K_2$, and $(DK_2/K_2)/M$ is a torsion module, since the ranks agree, inside the torsion-free module $(K_1/K_2)/M$. So $M = DK_2/K_2$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Check.* For $g = 2, 3, 4$ an exact rational computation of the vectors in $X_{\mathbb{Q}}$ killed by $N_1, \dots, N_g$ found one line each of weights $\varepsilon_1$, $2\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2$ and, for $g \ge 3$, $\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2 + \varepsilon_3$. This agrees with Steps 1 and 5, but the proof does not use it.
- *Comparison with free groups.* For a free pro-$p$ group $F$ of rank $n$, $\mathrm{Aut}(F)$ maps onto $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z}_p)$, and Tsujimura obtains the uniqueness from the decomposition of $\mathrm{Hom}(V, \Lambda^2 V)$ under $\mathrm{SL}_n$ by Schur functors and the Littlewood–Richardson rule [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Lemma 1.4 and Proposition 1.5, p. 5]. Here $\mathrm{Aut}(D)$ only reaches the symplectic similitudes, and the weight argument above replaces that decomposition. It uses no classification of representations.
- *Use.* With the other ingredients in the [lower central series](lower-central-series-of-pro-p-surface-groups.md) entry, this is what the step $N \subseteq DK_2$ of Tsujimura's argument needs [@tsujimura2026freeaut, Proposition 3.12, pp. 12–13]. It is used in [pro-$p$ surface groups inside their automorphism groups](pro-p-surface-groups-inside-their-automorphism-groups.md).
