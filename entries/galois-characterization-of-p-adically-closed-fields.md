**Statement.** A field $K$ is $p$-adically closed if it is elementarily equivalent to $\mathbb{Q}_p$ in the language of rings. By the Ax–Kochen–Ershov theorem, this holds exactly when $K$ admits a valuation $v$ such that $(K, v)$ is henselian of mixed characteristic $(0, p)$, $v(p)$ is the smallest positive element of the value group $vK$, $vK$ is elementarily equivalent to $\mathbb{Z}$, and the residue field $Kv$ is $\mathbb{F}_p$ [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Theorem 1.5]. The theorem of Efrat and Koenigsmann says that for any field $K$,

$$G_K \cong G_{\mathbb{Q}_p} \iff K \text{ is } p\text{-adically closed}$$

[@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §1, Main Theorem].

The direction from $K$ to $G_K$ is the easy one. The group $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ is small, meaning that it has finitely many open subgroups of each index [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §3.1], and small absolute Galois groups are encoded in the first-order theory of a field [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Proposition 5.1].

**History.** Pop proved the equivalence for fields $K$ whose algebraic closure is $K\bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Theorem 1.7], and conjectured it for all fields. Efrat for $p \ne 2$ and Koenigsmann for all $p$ proved it independently in 1995. Both treat more generally the fields elementarily equivalent to some finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, sometimes also called $p$-adically closed: they show that these are exactly the fields $K$ for which $G_K$ is isomorphic to an open subgroup of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, and the theorem above follows as a corollary [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §1]. The papers of Pop, Efrat and Koenigsmann have not been opened for this entry.

Gitin, Koenigsmann and Stock give a self-contained proof that avoids Galois cohomology [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §1]. Its outline [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §5]:

1. For $q \ne p$ the Sylow $q$-subgroups of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ are isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_q \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_q$, so the [Galois characterization of henselianity](galois-characterization-of-henselianity.md) gives henselian valuations on $K$, and the canonical henselian valuation $v_K$ is nontrivial.
2. $K$ has characteristic $0$. Characteristics other than $p$ are excluded by the residue fields of these valuations, and characteristic $p$ because $\mathbb{Q}_p$ has a cyclic extension of degree $p - 1$ with a cyclic extension of degree $p$ that does not embed in a cyclic extension of degree $p^2$, which cannot happen in characteristic $p$. The prime-to-$p$ roots of unity in $K$ are those of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and $v_K$ has residue characteristic $p$.
3. A transfer lemma to characteristic $p$ and Pop's lemma on fields with $K^\times/K^{\times p}$ finite show that the rank one component of mixed characteristic $(0, p)$ in a standard decomposition of $v_K$ is discrete with finite residue field.
4. Then $Kv_K = \mathbb{F}_p$ and $v_K K$ is elementarily equivalent to $\mathbb{Z}$. Comparing $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} L^\times/L^{\times p}$ for the unique extensions $L$ of $K$ and of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with group $C_{p-1} \times C_{p-1}$ shows that $v_K(p)$ is minimal positive.

**What the group determines.** The group $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ encodes a valuation on $K$ with the properties in the statement, possibly of rank greater than one [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §1]. For example, the field of Puiseux series over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is $p$-adically closed, with a valuation of rank two [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Example 1.6]. The group does not determine the field up to isomorphism: $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and the field $\mathbb{Q}_p^a = \mathbb{Q}_p \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ of algebraic $p$-adic numbers are both $p$-adically closed [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Example 1.6], but only one is countable. The same happens for $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{R} \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$ in the [Artin–Schreier theorem](artin-schreier-theorem.md). In characteristic $p$, the Galois group of $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$ alone does not pin down the field in this way, and [Brauer group data](local-fields-characteristic-p-brauer-characterization.md) is needed.

**Algebraic extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$.** An algebraic extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ is $p$-adically closed if and only if $K \cong \mathbb{Q}_p^a$. With the theorem, an algebraic extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ has $G_K \cong G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ exactly when $K \cong \mathbb{Q}_p^a$. Since $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p^a} \cong G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ by the easy direction, this is Neukirch's characterization of $\mathbb{Q}_p^a$ from his [local theory](neukirch-local-theory.md). Pop's theorem already covers these fields, and Gitin, Koenigsmann and Stock describe it as extending Neukirch's result [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, §1].

*Proof.* The field $\mathbb{Q}_p^a$ is $p$-adically closed [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Example 1.6]. Conversely, let $K$ be algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$ and $p$-adically closed, with $v$ as in the statement.

1. *The value group is $\mathbb{Z}\,v(p)$.* Since $Kv$ has characteristic $p$, integers prime to $p$ have value $0$, so $v(\mathbb{Q}^\times) = \mathbb{Z}\,v(p)$. For $x \in K^\times$, take $a_0, \ldots, a_n \in \mathbb{Q}$, not all zero, with $\sum_i a_i x^i = 0$. Two nonzero terms have the same smallest value, so $(i - j)v(x) \in \mathbb{Z}\,v(p)$ for some $i \ne j$. Given $\gamma \in vK$, let $n \ge 1$ be least with $n\gamma \in \mathbb{Z}\,v(p)$, say $n\gamma = m\,v(p)$. Then $\gcd(m, n) = 1$: otherwise, as $vK$ is torsion-free, dividing by the common factor would contradict the minimality of $n$. Choose integers $s, t$ with $sm + tn = 1$, and put $\delta = s\gamma + t\,v(p)$. Then $n\delta = v(p) > 0$, so $0 < \delta \le v(p)$, with equality only if $n = 1$. As $v(p)$ is the smallest positive element, $n = 1$ and $\gamma \in \mathbb{Z}\,v(p)$.
2. *$K$ embeds in $\mathbb{Q}_p$.* Normalize so that $v(p) = 1$. Then $v$ restricts to the $p$-adic valuation on $\mathbb{Q}$, and the closure of $\mathbb{Q}$ in the completion $\hat K$ is a copy of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Completion does not change the value group $\mathbb{Z}$ or the residue field $\mathbb{F}_p$. So for $x \in \hat K$ with $v(x) \ge 0$ there are digits $a_i \in \{0, \ldots, p - 1\}$ with $v(x - \sum_{i < N} a_i p^i) \ge N$ for every $N$, chosen one at a time. Hence $x$ lies in the closure of $\mathbb{Q}$. Every $x \ne 0$ is $p^{v(x)}$ times an element of value $0$, so $\hat K = \mathbb{Q}_p$. As $K$ is algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, this embeds $K$ in $\mathbb{Q}_p^a$, and $v$ is the restriction of the $p$-adic valuation.
3. *$K$ contains $\mathbb{Q}_p^a$.* Let $\alpha \in \mathbb{Q}_p^a$, with conjugates $\alpha = \alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$ over $K$ in an algebraic closure $\Omega$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Extend the $p$-adic valuation to $\Omega$, and write $v$ also for this extension. Its restriction to the algebraic closure of $K$ in $\Omega$ extends $v$, so it is the unique such extension, since $(K, v)$ is henselian. As $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in $\mathbb{Q}_p$, there is $a \in \mathbb{Q}$ with $v(a - \alpha) > v(\alpha_i - \alpha)$ for $2 \le i \le n$. Krasner's lemma for the henselian field $K$ [@gitinkoenigsmannstock2026, Lemma 2.33] gives $K(\alpha) \subseteq K(a) = K$.

So the embedding of step 2 is an isomorphism $K \cong \mathbb{Q}_p^a$. $\square$

The archimedean counterpart is part 3 of [involutions and real places](involutions-and-real-places.md): an algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ with absolute Galois group of order $2$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{R} \cap \bar{\mathbb{Q}}$.
