**Theorem.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and $X/k$ a smooth, proper, geometrically connected curve of genus at least $2$. Then the map
$$X(k) \longrightarrow \{s : G_k \to \pi_1(X) \text{ a continuous section}\}/\pi_1(X_{\bar k}),$$
sending a rational point to the class of its section, is bijective. The quotient is by conjugation by the full geometric fundamental group [@openai2026padicsections, Theorem 1.1, p. 2].

This answers the [$p$-adic section conjecture](p-adic-section-conjecture.md), the local analogue of [Grothendieck's section conjecture](section-conjecture.md). Injectivity was known: see [injectivity in the section conjecture](section-conjecture-injectivity.md). Surjectivity is the new part.

**Proof.** The proof is in [@openai2026padicsections, §7, pp. 16–18]. Write $K = k(X)$ and $\widetilde K$ for the function field of the universal étale cover, as in the [completion theorem](completion-of-the-universal-etale-function-field.md).

1. *No section at a rank-one valuation* [@openai2026padicsections, Lemma 7.2, pp. 16–17]. Let $w$ be a rank-one valuation of $K$ extending the $p$-adic valuation, and $\tilde w$ a prolongation of $w$ to $\widetilde K$. Then the projection $D_{\tilde w}(\widetilde K/K) \to G_k$ has no continuous section. Suppose it had one. By the corollary of the completion theorem, it lifts to a section $\hat s : G_k \to G_K$ whose image fixes a prolongation of $w$ to $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$. By [Koenigsmann's theorem](birational-sections-over-local-fields.md), the image of $\hat s$ also lies in the decomposition group of a prolongation of $\mathrm{ord}_a$, for some $a \in X(k)$. So the fixed field $M$ of $\hat s(G_k)$ is henselian for two rank-one valuations: $v_1$, with $v_1(p) = 1$, and $v_2$, trivial on $k$. Let $d \in k$ be a nonsquare. It stays a nonsquare in $M$, because $\hat s(G_k)$ maps onto $G_k$. Let $t$ be a uniformizer at $a$, put $z = p^N/t$ with $N > v_1(t)$, and
$$q_r = d + (1-d)\frac{z^r}{1+z^r}.$$
Then $q_r/d - 1 = \frac{1-d}{d}\cdot\frac{z^r}{1+z^r}$ and $q_r - 1 = \frac{d-1}{1+z^r}$. Since $v_1(z) > 0 > v_2(z)$, the values $v_1(q_r/d - 1)$ and $v_2(q_r - 1)$ tend to $+\infty$. In a henselian field, $x$ is a square once $v(x - 1) > 2v(2)$. So for large $r$, $q_r/d$ and $q_r$ are squares in $M$, and hence so is $d$, a contradiction. The argument covers $p = 2$ and non-discrete value groups.
2. *Surjectivity* [@openai2026padicsections, pp. 17–18]. Let $s$ be a section. By the [Pop–Stix theorem](valuative-section-theorem-for-p-adic-curves.md), $s(G_k) \subseteq D_{\tilde w}(\widetilde K/K)$ for a valuation $\tilde w$ of $\widetilde K$ prolonging the $p$-adic valuation, with restriction $w$ to $K$. Abhyankar's inequality gives $w$ rank at most $2$, and step 1 excludes rank $1$. If $w$ has rank $2$, let $u$ be its rank-one coarsening. Then $s(G_k)$ also fixes the corresponding coarsening $\tilde u$ of $\tilde w$. If $u$ is nontrivial on $k$, step 1 applies to $u$. Otherwise, as $X$ is proper, $u$ is the valuation $\mathrm{ord}_a$ of a closed point $a$, and $w$ is of type 2h. The cover $\widetilde K/K$ is unramified at $\mathrm{ord}_a$, so $D_{\tilde u}(\widetilde K/K)$ maps isomorphically onto $G_{k(a)} \subseteq G_k$. As $s(G_k)$ maps onto $G_k$, $k(a) = k$, and $s$ is the section of the rational point $a$.
3. *Injectivity* [@openai2026padicsections, p. 18]. If $a, b \in X(k)$ give conjugate sections, pulling back the multiplication-by-$n$ torsors of the Jacobian $J$ along the Abel–Jacobi map based at $a$ shows that $[b - a]$ lies in $\bigcap_n nJ(k)$. This intersection is $0$, because $J(k)$ has an open subgroup of finite index isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_p^{g[k:\mathbb{Q}_p]}$. So $b$ and $a$ are linearly equivalent, and $a = b$ since the genus is positive.

**What was checked here.** Each step of §7 of the manuscript was rebuilt independently by the lab's research agents, with no gap found; see [issue 227](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/227). Koenigsmann's theorem was checked to give a point of $X(k)$, not only a decomposition group at a point of $X(\bar k)$, as the [entry on his theorem](birational-sections-over-local-fields.md) explains. Pop and Stix's $\tilde K$ was checked to be the manuscript's $\widetilde K$. So the theorem rests on the completion theorem, together with two results already recorded in the graph. For the status of the completion theorem, the main step, see [its entry](completion-of-the-universal-etale-function-field.md).

**Status.** The proof is in a manuscript credited to OpenAI and dated 6 October 2026, which is not peer reviewed [@openai2026padicsections, p. 1]. Before it, the statement was a conjecture: see the local fields paragraph of the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md), and the [valuative section theorem](valuative-section-theorem-for-p-adic-curves.md) for Pop and Stix's reduction of it to a local non-splitting statement.

**Global consequence.** Stix considers the hypothesis (LSC) that the section conjecture holds over every finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and the hypothesis (BM) that the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to rational points on smooth projective geometrically connected curves over number fields [@stix2011bm, §4.1, p. 12]. If (LSC) holds for smooth projective curves of genus at least $2$ over finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, for all $p$, and (BM) holds, then for every smooth projective geometrically connected curve of genus at least $2$ over a number field, rational points correspond bijectively to section classes [@stix2011bm, Theorem 25, pp. 14–15]. The theorem above gives (LSC) in this form. So, given the theorem, the section conjecture over number fields follows from (BM). See [descent obstructions for Galois sections](descent-obstructions-for-galois-sections.md). The manuscript's finite-cover descent criterion and its abelian-target and modular-curve cases are checked in [the finite-descent entry](section-conjecture-from-finite-descent.md), which also checks the further localization restrictions of §8.3. Its elliptic-target case with Selmer corank zero additionally uses a companion theorem: the cited statement matches the application, but the companion's proof was not audited. See [the manuscript note](manuscript-claiming-p-adic-section-conjecture.md) for the distinctions and publication status.
