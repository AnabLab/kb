**Setting.** Let $D$ be a pro-$2$ [Demushkin group](demushkin-group.md) of rank $n \ge 3$ with canonical image $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\}$, so $q(D) = 2$. Use the notation of [the involution theorem for finite canonical image](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-finite-canonical-image.md): $V_D = D^{\mathrm{ab}}/\mathrm{torsion}$, $D^+ = \ker\chi$, $W = (D^+)^{\mathrm{ab}}$, the involution $J$ of $W$ induced by any $x \in D \setminus D^+$, and its eigenspaces $W_\pm$ in $W \otimes \mathbb{Q}_2$. Every automorphism of $D$ preserves $D^+$. $D$ is torsion-free and centre-free ([subgroups of Demushkin groups](subgroups-of-demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three.md), parts 1 and 4); identify $D$ with $\mathrm{Inn}(D) \subseteq A = \mathrm{Aut}(D)$, so that $\alpha c_d\alpha^{-1} = c_{\alpha(d)}$. Let $\Gamma \subseteq \mathrm{GL}(V_D \otimes \mathbb{Q}_2)$ be the image of $A$, which is also the image of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$.

With $(a, b) = a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ and $F$ free pro-$2$ on $x_1, \dots, x_n$, the [classification](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md), items 2 and 3 with $f = \infty$, gives $D \cong F/(r)$ with
$$r = x_1^2(x_2, x_3)(x_4, x_5)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n) \quad (n \text{ odd}), \qquad r = x_1^2(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n) \quad (n \text{ even}, \ n \ge 4).$$
In both cases $r$ abelianizes to $2e_1$, where $e_k$ is the class of $x_k$. So the torsion of $D^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is generated by the class of $e_1$, and $V_D$ has basis $v_2, \dots, v_n$, the images of $e_2, \dots, e_n$.

Following Tsujimura's question as reduced in [elasticity](demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three-are-elastic.md), Corollary 2, call a closed normal subgroup $N \ne D$ of $A$ with $N \cong D$ a *rival copy*, *of type (a)* if $N \cap D$ is open in $D$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Scalars.* Every element of $\Gamma$ with finitely many conjugates in $\Gamma$ is multiplication by some $c \in \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$. Some automorphism of $D$ acts on $V_D$ as $-1$, so $-1 \in \Gamma$ is central.
2. *Finite normal $2$-subgroups.* Every finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ acts on $V_D$ through $\{\pm 1\}$. Every finite normal $2$-subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ has order at most $2$, and if it has order $2$, its generator is central in $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ and acts on $V_D$ as $-1$.
3. *Rival copies of type (a).* Let $N$ be a rival copy of type (a). Then $N \cap D = D^+$, and there is an automorphism $y$ of $D$ of order $2$ with the following properties: its class $\varphi$ in $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ is central; $y$ acts on $W$ as $-1$; $ND = D \cup Dy$; $N = D^+ \cup (D \setminus D^+)y$; and no element of $(D \setminus D^+)y$ has order $2$. Conversely, if $y \in A$ has order $2$, its class in $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ is central, and no element of $(D \setminus D^+)y$ has order $2$, then $N = D^+ \cup (D \setminus D^+)y$ is a rival copy of type (a), and $y$ acts on $W$ as $-1$.

Here $(D \setminus D^+)y$ is the set of products $dy$ in $A$, that is, the automorphisms $c_d \circ y$ with $d \in D \setminus D^+$.

**Corollary.** This is proved here. A class $\varphi \in \mathrm{Out}(D)$ is a coset $Dy$ in $A$, the union of the two cosets $D^+y$ and $(D \setminus D^+)y$ of $D^+$. Call $\varphi$ *admissible* if it is central of order $2$ and its elements of order $2$ form a nonempty subset of one of these two cosets of $D^+$. Then $N \mapsto$ (the nontrivial element of $ND/D$) is a bijection from the rival copies of type (a) to the admissible classes. Its inverse sends $\varphi$ to the union of $D^+$ and the coset of $D^+$ in $\varphi$ that contains no element of order $2$. In particular, case (a) of the reduction occurs for $D$ if and only if $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ has an admissible class.

*Proof.* By part 3, for a rival copy $N$ of type (a), $ND/D = \{1, \varphi\}$ with $\varphi = Dy$ central of order $2$; its coset $D^+y$ contains $y$, and its other coset $(D \setminus D^+)y \subseteq N$ contains no element of order $2$. So $\varphi$ is admissible and $N$ is recovered as stated. Conversely, if $\varphi$ is admissible, choose an element $y$ of order $2$ in it; the coset of $D^+$ containing $y$ is $D^+y$, so $(D \setminus D^+)y$ has no element of order $2$, and part 3 gives the rival copy $D^+ \cup (D \setminus D^+)y$, whose class is $\varphi$. The two maps are inverse to each other. $\square$

**Proof of part 1.**

*Unipotent automorphisms.* For each pair $(a, b)$ of generators forming a commutator of $r$ other than $(x_1, x_2)$, that is $(x_{2i}, x_{2i+1})$ for odd $n$ and $(x_{2i+1}, x_{2i+2})$ for even $n$, with $i \ge 1$, the substitutions $L : b \mapsto ab$ and $R : a \mapsto ba$, fixing the other generators, are automorphisms of $F$ that fix $r$, because $(a, ab) = (a, b) = (ba, b)$ and neither generator occurs elsewhere in $r$. On $V_D$, $L$ is $1 + E_{a \leftarrow b}$ and $R$ is $1 + E_{b \leftarrow a}$, where $E_{a \leftarrow b}$ sends $v_b$ to $v_a$ and the other basis vectors to $0$.

For two adjacent commutators $(a, b)(c, d)$ of $r$, the automorphism $\alpha$ of $F$ of [the nonprocyclic dyadic entry](finite-normal-subgroups-of-demushkin-outer-automorphism-groups-with-nonprocyclic-dyadic-character.md), identity (2), changes only $b$ and $d$, and preserves $(a, b)(c, d)$; so it fixes $r$. By (3) there, it sends $e_b$ to $e_b + e_c$ and $e_d$ to $e_d + e_a$. Use it for consecutive pairs of the previous paragraph, and, for even $n$, also for $(a, b, c, d) = (x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4)$: it fixes $x_1$, hence $x_1^2$, and the two commutators follow $x_1^2$ directly. On $V_D$, where $v_1 = 0$, this last automorphism is $1 + E_{3 \leftarrow 2}$, sending $v_2$ to $v_2 + v_3$ and fixing the other basis vectors. All these linear maps have the form $1 + E$ with $E^2 = 0$.

*The centralizer.* Let $\gamma \in \Gamma$ have finitely many conjugates. Its centralizer has finite index, so for each $u = 1 + E$ above some $u^m = 1 + mE$ with $m \ge 1$ commutes with $\gamma$, and hence $\gamma E = E\gamma$. Write $\gamma_{kl}$ for the $v_k$-coordinate of $\gamma(v_l)$. Commuting with $E_{a \leftarrow b}$ gives $\gamma_{bk} = 0$ for $k \ne b$ (evaluate at $v_k$) and $\gamma(v_a) = \gamma_{bb}v_a$ (evaluate at $v_b$). Commuting with $E_{b \leftarrow a}$ gives the same with $a$ and $b$ exchanged. So $\gamma$ maps each pair-plane $P = \langle v_a, v_b\rangle$ to itself as a scalar $\lambda_P$, and no other basis vector has a component in $P$. For odd $n$ the pair-planes span $V_D$. For even $n$ they span the $v_3, \dots, v_n$ coordinates, so $\gamma(v_2) = c\,v_2$ for some $c$. A bridge between consecutive pairs $(a, b)$, $(c', d)$ is $1 + E$ with $E(v_b) = v_{c'}$, $E(v_d) = v_a$; evaluating $\gamma E = E\gamma$ at $v_b$ gives $\lambda_{P'}v_{c'} = \lambda_Pv_{c'}$, so the scalars agree. For even $n$, evaluating at $v_2$ with $E = E_{3 \leftarrow 2}$ gives $\lambda_{P_1}v_3 = c\,v_3$ for the first pair-plane $P_1 = \langle v_3, v_4\rangle$. So $\gamma$ is a scalar $c$, and $c \in \mathbb{Z}_2^\times$ because $\gamma$ preserves the lattice $V_D$.

*A lift of $-1$.* On a pair-plane, in the basis $v_a, v_b$, $L$ and $R$ have matrices $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and
$$\big(LR^{-1}L\big)^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}^2 = -1.$$
So the automorphism $\tau_P = (L \circ R^{-1} \circ L)^2$ fixes $r$, acts as $-1$ on $P$ and fixes the other $v_k$. Automorphisms for different pairs involve disjoint sets of generators, so they commute. For odd $n$, the product of all $\tau_P$ acts on $V_D$ as $-1$. For even $n$, let $\beta$ be the endomorphism of $F$ with
$$\beta(x_1) = x_2^{-1}x_1x_2, \qquad \beta(x_2) = x_2^{-1}, \qquad \beta(x_k) = x_1^{-1}x_kx_1 \ (k \ge 3).$$
It is the composite $\gamma_1 \circ \beta_0$ of the involution $\beta_0$ sending $x_1 \mapsto x_2^{-1}x_1x_2$, $x_2 \mapsto x_2^{-1}$ and fixing the other generators, and the automorphism $\gamma_1$ conjugating each $x_k$ with $k \ge 3$ by $x_1^{-1}$ and fixing $x_1, x_2$. So $\beta$ is an automorphism. Write $r = KC$ with $K = x_1^2(x_1, x_2) = x_1x_2^{-1}x_1x_2$ and $C = (x_3, x_4)\cdots(x_{n-1}, x_n)$. Then
$$\beta(K) = (x_2^{-1}x_1x_2)\,x_2\,(x_2^{-1}x_1x_2)\,x_2^{-1} = x_2^{-1}x_1x_2x_1 = x_1^{-1}Kx_1, \qquad \beta(C) = x_1^{-1}Cx_1,$$
so $\beta(r) = x_1^{-1}rx_1$, and $\beta^{-1}(r) = \beta^{-1}(x_1)\,r\,\beta^{-1}(x_1)^{-1}$. So $\beta$ induces an automorphism of $D$. It acts on $V_D$ by $v_2 \mapsto -v_2$, fixing $v_k$ for $k \ge 3$. Composed with the product of the $\tau_P$, it acts as $-1$. Scalars are central in $\Gamma$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** Let $\Phi$ be a finite normal subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$. The image of an element of $\Phi$ in $\Gamma$ has finitely many conjugates and finite order, so by part 1 it is a scalar root of unity in $\mathbb{Z}_2^\times$, that is $\pm 1$. Let $\Phi$ be a $2$-group. The kernel of $\Phi \to \{\pm 1\}$ is a finite $2$-group acting trivially on $V_D$, so it is trivial by part 3 of [the involution theorem](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-finite-canonical-image.md). So $|\Phi| \le 2$, and if $\Phi = \{1, \varphi\}$, then $\varphi$ acts as $-1$, and it is central because a normal subgroup of order $2$ is central. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.**

*Necessity.* Let $N$ be a rival copy of type (a), and $M = N \cap D$. By Corollary 2 of [the reduction](demushkin-groups-of-rank-at-least-three-are-elastic.md), $ND/D \cong N/M$ is a nontrivial finite normal $2$-subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ and $(D : M) = (N : M)$. By part 2, $ND/D = \{1, \varphi\}$ with $\varphi$ central, acting on $V_D$ as $-1$, and $(D : M) = (N : M) = 2$. Put $E = ND$, a closed subgroup of $A$ containing $D$ with index $2$, so a pro-$2$ group. $M$ is normal in $A$, as $N$ and $D$ are, and $E/M$ is the direct product of its subgroups $D/M$ and $N/M$ of order $2$ (as in [rival copies of $p$-adic Galois groups](rival-copies-of-p-adic-galois-groups-in-their-automorphism-groups.md), part 1, whose argument uses only that $N$ and $D$ are normal with intersection $M$). Let $P$ be the third subgroup of index $2$ in $E$ containing $M$. Let $\bar d, \bar n, \bar p$ be the nontrivial elements of $D/M$, $N/M$, $P/M$.

Let $X = \mathrm{Hom}(M, \mathbb{Q}_2)$, with $E/M$ acting by $(e \cdot f)(m) = f(e^{-1}me)$, and let $\psi$ be its character. $M$ is open of index $2$ in $D$, so it is a Demushkin group of rank $2n - 2$, and $\psi(1) = \dim X = 2n - 3 + \delta(M)$, where $\delta = 1$ if $q = 0$ and $\delta = 0$ otherwise ([torsion-free extensions](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), setting).

- $D$ and $N \cong D$ are torsion-free Demushkin groups with $q \ne 0$ containing $M$ as an open normal subgroup of index $2$. By part 2 of [the torsion-free extension theorem](torsion-free-finite-extensions-of-demushkin-groups.md), the invariants of $\bar d$ and of $\bar n$ on $X$ have dimension $n - 1$. For an involution, the invariants have dimension $(\psi(1) + \psi(g))/2$, so $\psi(\bar d) = \psi(\bar n) = 2n - 2 - \psi(1)$.
- $X^{E/M} = 0$. Restriction $\mathrm{Hom}(E, \mathbb{Q}_2) \to X^{E/M}$ is injective, as a homomorphism vanishing on $M$ factors through a finite group, and it is onto: for invariant $f$, composing $f$ with the transfer $E^{\mathrm{ab}} \to M^{\mathrm{ab}}$ gives a homomorphism on $E$ whose restriction to $M$ is $4f$, as in the proof of part 2 of the torsion-free extension theorem. Likewise $\mathrm{Hom}(E, \mathbb{Q}_2)$ restricts injectively into the invariants of $E/D = \{1, \varphi\}$ on $\mathrm{Hom}(D, \mathbb{Q}_2) = \mathrm{Hom}(V_D, \mathbb{Q}_2)$. As $\varphi$ acts on $V_D$ as $-1$, these invariants are $0$.

Hence $0 = \sum_{g \in E/M}\psi(g) = \psi(1) + 2(2n - 2 - \psi(1)) + \psi(\bar p)$, so
$$\psi(\bar p) = \psi(1) - (4n - 4) = -(2n - 1) + \delta(M).$$
Since $\bar p$ is an involution, $\psi(\bar p) \ge -\psi(1) = -(2n - 3) - \delta(M)$. So $\delta(M) \ge 1$. Thus $\delta(M) = 1$ and $\psi(\bar p) = -\psi(1)$: $\bar p$ acts on $X$ as $-1$. Now $\delta(M) = 1$ means $q(M) = 0$, so $\chi$, whose restriction is the canonical character of $M$, is trivial on $M$. So $M \subseteq D^+$, and $M = D^+$ as both have index $2$ in $D$.

If $P$ had no element of order $2$, it would be torsion-free, hence a Demushkin group with $n(P) = n$ by part 1 of the torsion-free extension theorem and the index formula, and by its part 2 the invariants of $\bar p$ on $X$ would have dimension $n - 1 + \delta(P) > 0$. They are $0$. So $P$ contains an element $y$ of order $2$. As $D^+$ is torsion-free, $y \in P \setminus D^+$, which is disjoint from $D$ and $N$ because $P \cap D = P \cap N = M$. So $y \in E \setminus D$, $ND = D \cup Dy$, and the class of $y$ is $\varphi$. Conjugation by $y$ acts on $X = \mathrm{Hom}(D^+, \mathbb{Q}_2)$ as $-1$, so $y$ acts on $W$ as $-1$. Finally $E/M = \{1, \bar d, \bar n, \bar p\}$ with $\bar n = \bar d\bar p$, so $N = D^+ \cup D^+xy = D^+ \cup (D \setminus D^+)y$ for any $x \in D \setminus D^+$. As $N$ is torsion-free, no element of $(D \setminus D^+)y$ has order $2$.

*Sufficiency.* Let $y$ be as in the converse, with class $\varphi$. As $D$ is torsion-free and $y \ne 1$, $y \notin D$. Put $E = D \cup Dy$, a closed subgroup. It is normal in $A$: for $\alpha \in A$, $\alpha y\alpha^{-1}y^{-1} \in D$ as $\varphi$ is central. $D^+$ is normal in $A$, being preserved by every automorphism of $D$. As in the proof of part 3 of [the involution theorem](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-finite-canonical-image.md), $E/D^+ \cong (\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$, generated by the images of $x \in D \setminus D^+$ and $y$. Its three subgroups of order $2$ have preimages $D$, $P = D^+ \cup D^+y$ and $N = D^+ \cup D^+xy = D^+ \cup (D \setminus D^+)y$.

- *$N$ is torsion-free.* A nontrivial element of finite order in the pro-$2$ group $N$ has a power of order $2$, which is not in the torsion-free group $D^+$, so lies in $(D \setminus D^+)y$. There is none.
- *$y$ acts on $W$ as $-1$.* The class $\varphi$ is central of order $2$, so by part 1 its image in $\Gamma$ is $\pm 1$. It is not $1$: otherwise $y$ would be an involution acting trivially on $V_D$, and $y = 1$ by [the involution theorem](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-finite-canonical-image.md), part 3. So $y$ acts on $V_D$ as $-1$, and by part 2 of that theorem $y|_{D^+}$ acts on $W \otimes \mathbb{Q}_2$ as $-1$ on $W_+$ and as $-\mu$ on $W_-$, with $\mu = \mu(y|_{D^+}) = \pm 1$ as $y^2 = 1$. Then $xy$ acts on $W$ as $J$ times this, that is, as $-1$ on $W_+$ and as $\mu$ on $W_-$. By part 1 of the torsion-free extension theorem and the index formula, $N$ is a Demushkin group with $n(N) = n$, and by its part 2 the invariants of $xy$ on $\mathrm{Hom}(D^+, \mathbb{Q}_2)$ have dimension $n - 1 + \delta(N) > 0$. So $\mu = 1$: $y$ acts on $W$ as $-1$, $xy$ acts as $-1$ on $W_+$ and as $1$ on $W_-$, its invariants have dimension $\dim W_- = n - 1$, and $\delta(N) = 0$.
- *$N \cong D$.* $\delta(N) = 0$ means $q(N) \ne 0$, so $\chi_N \ne 1$. Its restriction to $D^+$ is $\chi_{D^+} = 1$, so $\mathrm{Im}(\chi_N)$ has order $2$ and equals $\{\pm 1\}$. By the classification, $N \cong D$, as both have rank $n$ and canonical image $\{\pm 1\}$.
- *$N$ is normal in $A$.* Conjugation by $\alpha \in A$ preserves $E$, $D^+$ and $D$, so it permutes $\{P, N\}$. $P$ contains the element $y$ of order $2$ and $N$ has none, so it fixes $N$.

So $N$ is a closed normal subgroup of $A$, isomorphic to $D$, different from $D$, with $N \cap D = D^+$ open in $D$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Where the finite-conjugacy method stops.* For odd $p$ and $q \ne 0$, and for the pro-$2$ groups with nonprocyclic infinite canonical image, case (a) is excluded by showing that finite normal subgroups of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ act trivially on $V_D$ ([finite-conjugacy theorem](outer-automorphisms-of-demushkin-groups-with-finitely-many-conjugates.md), [nonprocyclic dyadic entry](finite-normal-subgroups-of-demushkin-outer-automorphism-groups-with-nonprocyclic-dyadic-character.md)) and that the kernel on $V_D$ has no suitable torsion. For $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) = \{\pm 1\}$ the second step holds (part 2 uses it), but part 1 shows that the first fails: the central element $-1$ of $\Gamma$ is the image of an automorphism, so the image of $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ in $\mathrm{GL}(V_D)$ cannot rule out a central class acting as $-1$. The Corollary turns case (a) for these groups into a question about $\mathrm{Out}(D)$ alone: is there an admissible class? Whether some automorphism acting as $-1$ on $V_D$ has a central outer class, or has order $2$, is not decided here. Nor is whether $\Gamma$ contains scalars other than $\pm 1$.
- *A necessary condition.* By part 3, an admissible class contains an involution acting as $-1$ on $W$. So case (a) cannot occur if no involution of $D$ acts on $(D^+)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ as $-1$.
- *None of the groups is a $G_K(2)$.* For $K/\mathbb{Q}_2$ finite, the canonical image of $G_K(2)$ is infinite ([involutions with infinite canonical image](involutions-of-demushkin-groups-with-infinite-canonical-image.md), Corollary).
- *Sources.* The statements and proofs are not taken from a source, and no literature search was made. No novelty is claimed.
