**Setting.** This follows Balakrishnan, Dan-Cohen, Kim and Wewers [@bdckw2018, §§2.1–2.7, pp. 8–10]. Let $\mathcal{X} \to \mathrm{Spec}\, \mathbb{Z}$ be a regular minimal model of a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) $X$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. They mean one of three things: $\mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{Z}} \setminus \mathcal{D}$ for a reduced horizontal divisor $\mathcal{D}$ with at least three $\mathbb{C}$-points; the regular minimal model of a proper smooth curve of genus at least $2$; or the complement of a nonempty reduced horizontal divisor in the regular minimal model of a proper smooth curve of genus at least $1$ [@bdckw2018, §2.1, p. 8]. Fix a base point $b$, which is an integral point or an integral tangent vector, and an odd prime $p$ of good reduction.

- Let $U$ be the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-unipotent étale fundamental group of $X_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}$ at $b$, and $U_n = U/U^{n+1}$ its quotients along the descending central series. Let $T$ be a finite set of primes containing $p$ and the primes of bad reduction, and $G_T$ the Galois group of the maximal extension of $\mathbb{Q}$ unramified outside $T$ [@bdckw2018, §2.2, p. 8].
- Sending a point $y$ to the class of the torsor of paths from $b$ to $y$ gives a local unipotent Kummer map $j_v : \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_v) \to H^1(G_v, U_n)$ for each prime $v$, and a global one $j : \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}) \to H^1_f(G_T, U_n)$, where the subscript $f$ means crystalline at $p$. These cohomology sets are affine $\mathbb{Q}_p$-schemes of finite type, and the localization maps $\mathrm{loc}_v$ between them are algebraic [@bdckw2018, §§2.3–2.4, p. 9].
- For $v \ne p$, the image of $j_v$ is finite, by a theorem of Kim and Tamagawa [@bdckw2018, Proposition 2.5, p. 9], and it is trivial if $v \notin T$ [@bdckw2018, Remark 2.6, p. 9].
- The *Selmer scheme* and the set of points that are *cohomologically global of level $n$* are
$$\mathrm{Sel}^n(\mathcal{X}) = \bigcap_{v \ne p} \mathrm{loc}_v^{-1}(\mathrm{Im}\, j_v) \subseteq H^1_f(G_T, U_n), \qquad \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_n = j_p^{-1}\bigl(\mathrm{loc}_p(\mathrm{Sel}^n(\mathcal{X}))\bigr)$$
[@bdckw2018, Definitions 2.7, p. 10]. They form a decreasing sequence $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p) \supseteq \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_1 \supseteq \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_2 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z})$, which does not depend on $T$ or on $b$ [@bdckw2018, §§2.7–2.9, pp. 10–12].

**Conjecture (Kim).** For $n$ large, $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_n = \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z})$ [@bdckw2018, Conjecture 3.1, p. 12].

Kim proposed this in his lectures at the IHÉS in February 2012 [@bdckw2018, §3.1, p. 12]. The authors expect a suitable generalization to hold over number fields, but do not formulate one [@bdckw2018, Remark 3.2, p. 13]. For a proper curve, $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}) = X(\mathbb{Q})$ by the valuative criterion, so the conjecture then describes the rational points.

*$S$-integral variant.* Let $S$ be a finite set of primes not containing $p$, and take $T$ to contain $S$ as well as $p$ and the primes of bad reduction [@bdckw2018, §8.1, p. 48]. Dropping the local conditions at $v \in S$ then gives $\mathrm{Sel}^n_S(\mathcal{X}) = \bigcap_{v \ne p,\, v \notin S} \mathrm{loc}_v^{-1}(\mathrm{Im}\, j_v)$ and sets $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_{S,n}$ containing $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}[1/S])$. The authors suggest, without stating it as a conjecture, that $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_{S,n} = \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}[1/S])$ may hold for large $n$ [@bdckw2018, §1.4, p. 3, and §8.1, p. 48].

**Relation to the Tate–Shafarevich and section conjectures.**

- *Two halves.* Consider (SGK), surjectivity of the global Kummer map: for large $n$, $j$ maps $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z})$ onto the classes $P \in \mathrm{Sel}^n(\mathcal{X})$ with $\mathrm{loc}_p(P) \in \mathrm{Im}\, j_p$, the classes that are geometric everywhere locally. Consider also (ILK), injectivity of the local Kummer map: if $x \in \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z})$ and $y \in \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ have $j_p(x) = j_p(y)$ for all $n$, then $x = y$. Trivially, SGK and ILK together imply the conjecture, which implies ILK [@bdckw2018, §3.3, p. 13].
- *The section conjecture.* For a proper hyperbolic curve $X$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ with $b \in X(\mathbb{Q})$, the authors state the [section conjecture](section-conjecture.md) as the bijectivity of $x \mapsto [\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}; b, x)]$ from $X(\mathbb{Q})$ to $H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q}}, \pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}, b))$. Its surjectivity "bears an obvious relationship" to SGK. With $U$ in place of the profinite group, the cohomology set becomes a positive-dimensional variety and surjectivity is no longer plausible, but $j$ may still surject onto the classes that are geometric everywhere locally [@bdckw2018, §3.4, p. 13]. By contrast, for the universal curve of genus $g \ge 5$ with at least one marked point, over the function field of its moduli space and under conditions on the base field, Hain proves that rational points correspond bijectively to conjugacy classes of splittings of the $\ell$-adic unipotent extension. See [Hain's theorem on the generic curve](section-conjecture-for-universal-curves.md).
- *The abelian model.* For an elliptic curve $E$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ whose Tate–Shafarevich group has finite $p$-part, the $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linearized Kummer map is a bijection from $E(\mathbb{Q}) \otimes \mathbb{Q}_p$ onto the classes that are geometric locally at every prime, which form the classical $p$-adic Selmer group [@bdckw2018, §3.4.1, Proposition and Corollary, pp. 14–15]. This is the sense in which the conjecture is "of Tate–Shafarevich type". The abstract calls it "a sort of compromise between the abelian confines of the BSD conjecture and the profinite world of the Grothendieck section conjecture" [@bdckw2018, Abstract, p. 1].
- *Difference in flavour.* The authors call their conjecture "quite different in flavor from the section conjecture and its direct consequences", closer to the Tate–Shafarevich and Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjectures in concreteness and computability, and, like the latter, testable numerically. They also report that Kim showed elsewhere that if $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_n \ne \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ for some $n$, then the section conjecture would in principle give a computable bound on the height of rational points [@bdckw2018, §1.5, p. 3]. That paper of Kim has not been opened for this entry.

**Finiteness.** Let $\mathcal{L}(n)$ be the ideal defining the scheme-theoretic image of $\mathrm{loc}_p : \mathrm{Sel}^n(\mathcal{X}) \to H^1_f(G_p, U_n)$, whose target is isomorphic to affine space. If $\mathcal{L}(n) \ne 0$, then $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_n$ is finite; for this the authors refer to Kim's paper on Selmer varieties, which has not been opened for this entry. The Fontaine–Mazur–Jannsen and Bloch–Kato conjectures imply that for large $n$ the pullback $j_p^* \mathcal{L}(n+1)$ strictly contains $j_p^* \mathcal{L}(n)$, with elements algebraically independent of it. So a point lying in every $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_n$ "should be there for a good reason", and the conjecture says that the reason is that it is integral [@bdckw2018, §1.8, p. 4].

For the thrice-punctured line, [Kim's proof of Siegel's theorem](kim-unipotent-proof-of-siegel-theorem.md) is the prototype of such a finiteness argument. Its Selmer variety imposes no local conditions at the primes $v \ne p$ beyond being unramified outside $T = S \cup \{p\}$, and a count of dimensions shows that for large $n$, and $p$ large relative to $n$, its image in the de Rham fundamental group of level $n$ lies in a proper subvariety.

**Evidence.** All from the same paper.

- *The thrice-punctured line over $\mathbb{Z}$.* Here $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}) = \emptyset$, because $z$ and $1 - z$ cannot both be $\pm 1$. At level $1$, $j_p(z) = (\log z, \log(1 - z))$, so $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_1$ consists of the $z$ with $z$ and $1 - z$ both roots of unity, that is, the primitive sixth roots of unity in $\mathbb{Q}_p$. So the conjecture holds at level $1$ if $p = 3$ or $p \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ [@bdckw2018, §6.1, Proposition, p. 44]. If $p \equiv 1 \pmod 3$, then $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_2 = \emptyset$ exactly when the $p$-adic dilogarithm satisfies $\mathrm{Li}_2(\zeta_6) \ne 0$, a question raised by Coleman [@bdckw2018, §6.2, p. 44]. The authors checked this numerically for $3 \le p < 10^5$ [@bdckw2018, §6.3, p. 45].
- *Punctured elliptic curves of rank $0$.* For $\mathcal{X} = \mathcal{E} \setminus O$ with $\mathcal{E}$ semistable of rank $0$ and $Ш_E[p^\infty]$ finite, the authors describe $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_2$ by explicit equations in iterated Coleman integrals, with constants given by the values of local heights at the bad primes [@bdckw2018, §§1.12–1.13, pp. 6–7]. With $p = 5$ they found $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_2$ for $256$ such curves from Cremona's table [@bdckw2018, §5.8, p. 39]. For the curve 378b3 they found $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_2 = \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z})$, with two points, for $5 \le p \le 97$, although $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{97})_1$ has $89$ points [@bdckw2018, §5.9, p. 40].
- *Rank $1$.* Here $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}) \subsetneq \mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_2$ in the majority of cases, so level $2$ is not enough, which is no contradiction since the conjecture is about large $n$ [@bdckw2018, §5.11, pp. 41–42].
- *Fermat curves.* For $x^l + y^l = z^l$ with $l = 5$ or $7$ and $p \not\equiv 1 \pmod l$, and assuming that the $p$-part of the Tate–Shafarevich group of the Jacobian is finite, the conjecture holds at level $1$ [@bdckw2018, §7.4, Proposition, p. 47].
- *The $S$-integral variant.* For the thrice-punctured line and $S = \{2\}$, $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}[1/2]) = \{2, -1, 1/2\}$, and $\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_p)_{\{2\},2}$ is the zero set of $2\mathrm{Li}_2(z) + \log(z) \log(1 - z)$. Numerically, this zero set is exactly $\{2, -1, 1/2\}$ for $p = 3, 5, 7$. For $p = 11$ level $2$ is not enough: as Coleman noted, $(-1 \pm \sqrt{5})/2$ also lies in the zero set [@bdckw2018, §8.2, pp. 48–49]. As checked here, $z = (-1 + \sqrt{5})/2$ satisfies $1 - z = z^2$, so it is a solution of the unit equation in the ring of integers of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, and it lies in $\mathbb{Q}_{11}$ because $5 \equiv 4^2 \pmod{11}$. This failure is at a fixed level, so it does not contradict the variant.

**Status.** Open as far as the source opened here shows. Its 2017 version verifies the conjecture only in the special cases above, some of them conditionally or numerically. Later work has not been checked for this entry.
