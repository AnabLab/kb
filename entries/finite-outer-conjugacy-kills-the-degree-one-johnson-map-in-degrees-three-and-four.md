**Setting.** Use the Setting of [the degree-one Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-degree-one-johnson-map-on-wild-inertia.md), with its notation $p$ odd, $G=G_K$, $P$, $\Gamma$, $\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma]]$, $\epsilon$, $P_j$, $H$, $\mathcal L_j$, $V=L\oplus V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_d$, $\omega$, $\rho^*=\omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$, $\gamma$, $\lambda_0=\gamma-1$, $\iota_l$, $R_\mu$, its Johnson maps $J_1$ and $J$, and its set $\mathcal S$ of Lemma 4. Here $d=[K:\mathbb Q_p]\in\{3,4\}$, so $r=1$ and there is one surface pair $(a,b)$: $(x_2,x_3)$ for $d=3$ and $(x_3,x_4)$ for $d=4$. Write $\bar a=\bar x_a$, $\bar b=\bar x_b$, $V_{(1)}=V_a\oplus V_b$ and $A=L\oplus C_V$, with $C_V=V_1$ for $d=3$ and $V_1\oplus V_2$ for $d=4$. So $V=A\oplus V_{(1)}$. The indices $1$ and, for $d=4$, $2$ are *complementary*. Put $w=[\bar a,\bar b]\in\mathcal L_2(V)$.

For $\mu\in\Lambda$ and a tensor $Y$, $\mu\cdot Y$ is the diagonal action, as in the Setting there. For $y\in\Lambda$ let $R_y$ also denote right multiplication by $y$ on $\Lambda$, and let $\Delta_2:\Lambda\to\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda=\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^2]]$ be the continuous linear map with $\Delta_2(g)=g\otimes g$ for $g\in\Gamma$. A component of a tensor in $V_k\hat\otimes V_l$ ($k,l\ge1$) has a *coordinate* in $\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda$, and one in $V_k\hat\otimes L$ has a coordinate in $\Lambda$, through $\pi_k,\pi_l$ and $\bar x_0\mapsto1$. For $\nu\in\Lambda$ let $\nu^\omega$ be its image under the continuous ring automorphism of $\Lambda$ with $\rho\mapsto\omega(\rho)\rho$. As $\rho\bar x_0=\omega(\rho)\bar x_0$, for $k\ge1$
$$\nu\cdot[\bar x_k,\bar x_0]=[\nu^\omega\bar x_k,\bar x_0].\tag{0}$$
The map $\nu\mapsto\nu^*$ extends to a continuous anti-automorphism of $\Lambda$: $(\rho\rho')^*=\rho'^*\rho^*$, and $1^*=1$.

**Theorem (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\in\{3,4\}$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Let $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ have finitely many conjugates in $\operatorname{Out}(G)$ and induce the identity on $G/[P,P]$. Then $\alpha(x)x^{-1}\in[P,[P,P]]$ for every $x\in P$, that is, $J_1(\alpha)=0$.

**Corollary 1 (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. Every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha_0$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_0$ that induces the identity on $G/[P,P]$ and on $P/[P,[P,P]]$. Two such $\alpha_0$ differ by $c_h$ with $h\in[P,P]$.

**Corollary 2 (proved here).** Let $p$ be odd, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$ and $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$. Every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ with finitely many outer conjugates is $c_g\circ\alpha_1$ for some $g\in G$ and some $\alpha_1$ inducing the identity on $G/[P,[P,P]]$, unique up to $c_k$ with $k\in[P,[P,P]]$. So every outer class with finitely many conjugates lies in the kernel of $\operatorname{Out}(G)\to\operatorname{Out}(G/[P,[P,P]])$.

For example, let $p\ge5$ and let $K$ be unramified over $\mathbb Q_p$ of degree $3$ or $4$. Then $[K(\mu_p):K]=p-1\ge4$, as in the example of [the higher cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-higher-cocycle-classes-of-wild-inertia.md): $\mathbb Q_p(\mu_p)/\mathbb Q_p$ is totally ramified of degree $p-1$, so it meets $K$ only in $\mathbb Q_p$. So Corollary 2 applies to such $K$.

**Lemma A (diagonal tensors; proved here).**

- (a) For $x,y\in\Lambda$, if $(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_y)\Delta_2(x)=0$, then $x=0$ or $y=0$.
- (b) For $k\in\Gamma$ and $x,y\in\Lambda$: $(R_k\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_y)\Delta_2(x)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_{k^{-1}y})\Delta_2(xk)$.
- (c) Let $U=\ker\omega$. Then $(V\hat\otimes V)^U=L\otimes L$. Hence every continuous $\Lambda$-linear $\bar J:V\to\mathcal L_2(V)$ has $\bar J(\bar x_0)=0$.
- (d) If $m\in\Lambda$ and $mk=\omega(k)m$ for all $k\in\Gamma$, then $m=0$.
- (e) If $y\in\Lambda$ and $1\otimes y=y\otimes1$, then $y=\epsilon(y)1$.

*Proof.* The homeomorphism $h(g_1,g_2)=(g_1,g_1^{-1}g_2)$ of $\Gamma^2$ descends to $(\Gamma/U')^2$ for every open normal $U'$, since $(g_1u)^{-1}g_2u'\in U'g_1^{-1}g_2U'=g_1^{-1}g_2U'$. So it induces a continuous linear automorphism $h_*$ of $\mathbb F_p[[\Gamma^2]]$.

(a) For $g,k\in\Gamma$, $h(g,gk)=(g,k)$, so $h_*(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_k)\Delta_2(g)=g\otimes k$, and by bilinearity and continuity $h_*(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_y)\Delta_2(x)=x\otimes y$. If $x,y\ne0$, choose continuous functionals $f,f'$ on $\Lambda$ with $f(x)=f'(y)=1$, which exist at a finite level. Then $(f\otimes f')(x\otimes y)=1$.

(b) For $x=g$ both sides are $gk\otimes gy$; extend linearly and continuously in $x$ and $y$.

(c) $V\hat\otimes V$ is the direct sum of the $U$-stable pieces $W\hat\otimes W'$, with $W,W'\in\{L,V_1,\dots,V_d\}$. On $L\otimes L$, $U$ acts trivially. On $L\otimes V_k$ and $V_k\otimes L$, which are copies of $\Lambda$, $u\in U$ acts by left multiplication, so there are no nonzero invariants by Lemma 0 of [unitarity](unitarity-of-central-scalars-on-wild-frattini.md). On $V_k\hat\otimes V_l\cong\Lambda\hat\otimes\Lambda$, $u$ acts by left multiplication on both factors, and $h(ug_1,ug_2)=(ug_1,g_1^{-1}g_2)$, so $h_*$ turns this into left multiplication on the first factor. If $z$ is invariant, then $(\mathrm{id}\otimes f)(h_*z)\in\Lambda^U=0$ for every continuous functional $f$, so $h_*z=0$ as in the proof of Lemma 3 (a) of the degree-one theorem, and $z=0$. For $\bar J$: $u\cdot\bar J(\bar x_0)=\bar J(u\bar x_0)=\bar J(\bar x_0)$ for $u\in U$, so $\bar J(\bar x_0)\in L\otimes L$. It is antisymmetric, as $\mathcal L_2(V)$ consists of antisymmetric tensors, and $\bar x_0\otimes\bar x_0$ is symmetric; so $\bar J(\bar x_0)=0$.

(d) Apply the continuous anti-automorphism $g\mapsto g^{-1}$ of $\Lambda$: the image $m'$ of $m$ satisfies $k^{-1}m'=\omega(k)m'$, so $m'$ is invariant under left multiplication by the open subgroup $U$. So $m'=0$ by Lemma 0 of unitarity, and $m=0$.

(e) Apply $\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}$. $\square$

**Lemma B (one-pair normal form; proved here).** Let $d\in\{3,4\}$, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $J\in\mathcal S$, with reduction $\bar J$. Then:

- (N0) $\bar J(\bar x_0)=0$;
- (N1) there is $c\in A$ with $\bar J(\bar a)=[\bar a,c]$ and $\bar J(\bar b)=[\bar b,c]$;
- (N2) for every complementary $k\ge1$, $\bar J(\bar x_k)=Q_k+m_k\cdot w$ with $Q_k\in\mathcal L_2(A)$ and $m_k\in\Lambda$.

*Proof.* (N0) is Lemma A (c).

*Kondo's automorphisms.* As in step 1 of the degree-one theorem, (ii') there gives $\mathcal L_2(\bar M)\bar J=\bar J\bar M$ for every $M$ in the group generated by $u:a\mapsto a+b$ and $l:b\mapsto b+a$, acting on $V$ $\Lambda$-linearly on $\bar a,\bar b$ and trivially on $A$. This group contains $(ul^{-1}u)^2=-1$ on $\bar a,\bar b$. The arguments (1a), (1b) and the first half of (1c) there, for $i=1$, use only these three matrices. Since $V=A\oplus V_{(1)}$, they give: $\bar J(\bar a)=\iota_a(\varphi)$ and $\bar J(\bar b)=\iota_b(\varphi)$ for one $\varphi\in\Lambda\hat\otimes A$; and for every complementary $k\ge1$, $\bar J(\bar x_k)=Q_k+Z_k$ with $Q_k\in\mathcal L_2(A)$, where $Z_k$ has components only in $V_a\hat\otimes V_b$, with coordinate $z_k$, and in $V_b\hat\otimes V_a$, with coordinate $-z_k^{\mathsf T}$.

*The boundary twist.* Let $T=T^{(1)}_1$ be the boundary twist of the pair, which is tame-trivial and trivial on $P/P_3$ by Lemma 2 (a) of [interval twists](boundary-twists-of-every-surface-pair-have-infinite-outer-conjugacy.md). Let $\delta$ be the reduction of $J(T)$. By step 1 of [abelianized wild inertia](tame-trivial-automorphisms-with-finite-outer-conjugacy-act-trivially-on-abelianized-wild-inertia.md), $\delta$ is $\Lambda$-linear, kills $A$, and has $\delta(\bar a)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar a]=2\bar a\bar b\bar a-\bar b\bar a\bar a-\bar a\bar a\bar b$ and $\delta(\bar b)=[[\bar a,\bar b],\bar b]=\bar a\bar b\bar b-2\bar b\bar a\bar b+\bar b\bar b\bar a$. By (iii') of the degree-one theorem, $\delta^{(2)}\bar J-\bar J^{(3)}\delta=\operatorname{ad}(\bar y)$ with $\bar y\in\mathcal L_3(V)$. At $\bar x_0$ both terms on the left vanish, since $\delta(\bar x_0)=0$ and $\bar J(\bar x_0)=0$. So $[\bar y,\bar x_0]=0$, and $\bar y=0$ by Lemma 2 (a) there, with $\chi$ the coordinate of $L$ along $\bigoplus_kV_k$. Hence $\delta^{(2)}\bar J=\bar J^{(3)}\delta$.

- *At $\bar a$.* The computation in the bullet at $\bar a$ of step 3 of the degree-one theorem uses only $\bar J(\bar a)=\iota_a(\varphi)$, $\bar J(\bar b)=\iota_b(\varphi)$, the formula for $\delta$ and the identity $\delta^{(2)}\bar J(\bar a)=\bar J^{(3)}\delta(\bar a)$. The projection there onto the pattern $\bar a\bar a\bar b A$ gives $\varphi=1\otimes c$ with $c=(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_A)(\varphi)\in A$. As $\iota_a(1\otimes c)=[\bar a,c]$, this is (N1).
- *At complementary $\bar x_k$, $k\ge1$.* Here $\delta(\bar x_k)=0$, so $\delta^{(2)}\bar J(\bar x_k)=0$, and $\delta^{(2)}Q_k=0$ as $\delta$ kills $A$. So $\delta^{(2)}Z_k=0$. For $\mu\in\Lambda$ and a word in $\bar a,\bar b$, the coordinate of $\mu\cdot(\text{word})$ is $\Delta_3(\mu)$ times the coefficient, with $\Delta_3(\rho)=\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\rho$, because the coordinates are $\Lambda$-linear. Take the component of $\delta^{(2)}Z_k$ in the pattern $\bar a\bar a\bar b\bar b$. For $z_k=\mu\otimes\nu$, so that $Z_k=\mu\bar a\otimes\nu\bar b-\nu\bar b\otimes\mu\bar a$, it comes from $-\mu\cdot\bar a\bar a\bar b$ in $\delta(\mu\bar a)\otimes\nu\bar b$ and from $\nu\cdot\bar a\bar b\bar b$ in $\mu\bar a\otimes\delta(\nu\bar b)$. The terms from $\nu\bar b\otimes\mu\bar a$ begin with $\bar b$ or end with $\bar a$. By linearity and continuity, $-(\Delta_3\otimes\mathrm{id})(z_k)+(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Delta_3)(z_k)=0$ in $\Lambda^{\hat\otimes4}$. Apply $\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}$, using $(\epsilon\otimes\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta_3=\mathrm{id}$ and $(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})\Delta_3=\Delta_2$: this gives $z_k=\Delta_2(m_k)$ with $m_k=(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id})(z_k)$. Since $\Delta_2(m)^{\mathsf T}=\Delta_2(m)$, the components of $m_k\cdot w$ have coordinates $\Delta_2(m_k)$ and $-\Delta_2(m_k)$. So $Z_k=m_k\cdot w$, which is (N2). $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** As in the proof of the degree-one theorem, it suffices to show that $\bar J=0$ for every $J\in\mathcal S$. Then $J=pJ'$ with $J'\in\mathcal S$, so $J\in\bigcap_kp^k\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathcal L_2)=0$; apply this to $J_1(\alpha)$.

Fix $J\in\mathcal S$, and let $c$, $Q_k$, $m_k$ be as in Lemma B. Write $c=c_0\bar x_0+\sum_k\gamma_k\bar x_k$, over the complementary $k\ge1$, with $c_0\in\mathbb F_p$ and $\gamma_k\in\Lambda$. If $B$ is the action on $V$ of a tame-trivial automorphism, then by (i') of the degree-one theorem Lemma B also applies to $\bar J_B=\mathcal L_2(B)\bar JB^{-1}$. In particular, for complementary $k\ge1$, $\bar J_B(\bar x_k)$ has no component in $V_{(1)}\hat\otimes A$, none in $V_a\hat\otimes V_a$, and $\bar J_B(\bar a)$ has none in $V_a\hat\otimes V_a$. We write $\mathcal L_2(B)(\mu\cdot Y)=\mu\cdot\mathcal L_2(B)Y$, which holds as $B$ is $\Lambda$-linear.

*Odd $d=3$.* Here $(a,b)=(2,3)$, $A=L\oplus V_1$ and $w=[\bar x_2,\bar x_3]$. For $\nu\in\ker\omega$, part 3 of [the mixing theorem](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md) gives a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $B=B_\nu$ on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_0$ and $\bar x_2$, and has
$$B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\nu\bar x_2,\qquad B\bar x_3=\bar x_3-\beta_\nu\bar x_2-\nu^*\delta_0\bar x_1,$$
where $\beta_\nu=\nu^*\delta'\nu$, with $\delta'$ the element called $\delta$ in part 1 of the mixing theorem, and $\delta_0=\delta'-\delta'^*$. By part 1 there, the image of $\delta_0$ under a ring homomorphism $\Lambda\to\mathbb F_p$ is nonzero, so $\delta_0\ne0$. Also $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\nu\bar x_2$ and $B^{-1}\bar x_2=\bar x_2$.

(o1) $\gamma_1=0$. We have $\bar J_B(\bar x_2)=\mathcal L_2(B)[\bar x_2,c]=[\bar x_2,Bc]=[\bar x_2,c]+[\bar x_2,\gamma_1\nu\bar x_2]$. Its component in $V_2\hat\otimes V_2$ vanishes, and it has coordinate $1\otimes\gamma_1\nu-\gamma_1\nu\otimes1$. By Lemma A (e), $\gamma_1\nu=\epsilon(\gamma_1)\epsilon(\nu)1$. For $\nu=\lambda_0$ this is $0$, as $\epsilon(\lambda_0)=0$, so $\gamma_1=0$ by Lemma 2 of [the central-element construction](p-power-order-automorphisms-fix-surface-generators-modulo-the-frattini-subgroup-of-wild-inertia.md). So $c=c_0\bar x_0$.

(o2) $c_0=0$. The antisymmetric tensor $Q_1\in\mathcal L_2(L\oplus V_1)$ has no component in $L\otimes L$, and its components in $L\otimes V_1$ and $V_1\otimes L$ are $\bar x_0\otimes y$ and $-y\otimes\bar x_0$ for one $y\in V_1$. So $Q_1=[\kappa\bar x_1,\bar x_0]+R_1$ with $\kappa\in\Lambda$, where $R_1\in V_1\hat\otimes V_1$ is antisymmetric, with coordinate $r$. Let $\nu\in\ker\omega$. By Lemma B and (0),
$$\bar J_B(\bar x_1)=\mathcal L_2(B)\big(Q_1+m_1\cdot w-\nu\cdot[\bar x_2,c_0\bar x_0]\big)=[\kappa\bar x_1+\kappa\nu\bar x_2,\bar x_0]+(B\otimes B)R_1+m_1\cdot[\bar x_2,B\bar x_3]-c_0[\nu^\omega\bar x_2,\bar x_0].$$
Only the first and last terms have a slot in $L$. So the component in $V_2\hat\otimes L$, which vanishes, has coordinate $\kappa\nu-c_0\nu^\omega$. For $\nu=\lambda_0$, $\lambda_0^\omega=\lambda_0$ as $\omega(\gamma)=1$, so $(\kappa-c_0)\lambda_0=0$ and $\kappa=c_0$, by Lemma 2 of the central-element construction. As $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$, $\omega\ne1$; choose $\rho$ with $\omega(\rho)\ne1$ and put $\nu=\rho-\omega(\rho)$. Then $\nu^\omega=\omega(\rho)\rho-\omega(\rho)$, and $c_0\nu=c_0\nu^\omega$ gives $c_0(1-\omega(\rho))\rho=0$. So $c_0=0$ and $\kappa=0$. Thus $c=0$, so $\bar J$ vanishes on $V_2\oplus V_3$ by (N1) and $\Lambda$-linearity, and $Q_1=R_1$.

(o3) $m_1=0$ and $R_1=0$. Now $\bar J_B(\bar x_1)=(B\otimes B)R_1+m_1\cdot[\bar x_2,\bar x_3-\beta_\nu\bar x_2-\nu^*\delta_0\bar x_1]$. Its component in $V_2\hat\otimes V_1$ vanishes. As $B(g\bar x_1)=g\bar x_1+g\nu\bar x_2$, the term $(B\otimes B)R_1$ contributes $(R_\nu\otimes\mathrm{id})r$. The term $-m_1\cdot[\bar x_2,\nu^*\delta_0\bar x_1]$ contributes $-(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_{\nu^*\delta_0})\Delta_2(m_1)$, as for $m_1=g$ it is $-(g\bar x_2\otimes g\nu^*\delta_0\bar x_1-g\nu^*\delta_0\bar x_1\otimes g\bar x_2)$. The other terms have no component there. So
$$(R_\nu\otimes\mathrm{id})r=(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_{\nu^*\delta_0})\Delta_2(m_1)\qquad(\nu\in\ker\omega).\tag{1}$$
Let $k\in\Gamma$. As $\ker\omega$ is an ideal, (1) holds for $\nu=\lambda_0$ and for $\nu=\lambda_0k$. Since $R_{\lambda_0k}=R_kR_{\lambda_0}$, applying $R_k\otimes\mathrm{id}$ to (1) for $\lambda_0$ and using Lemma A (b) gives
$$(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_{k^{-1}\lambda_0^*\delta_0})\Delta_2(m_1k)=(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_{(\lambda_0k)^*\delta_0})\Delta_2(m_1)=\omega(k)\,(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_{k^{-1}\lambda_0^*\delta_0})\Delta_2(m_1),$$
as $(\lambda_0k)^*=k^*\lambda_0^*=\omega(k)k^{-1}\lambda_0^*$. The element $k^{-1}\lambda_0^*\delta_0$ is nonzero: $\lambda_0^*=\gamma^{-1}-1$, left multiplication by it is injective by Lemma 2 of the central-element construction, and $\delta_0\ne0$. By Lemma A (a), $m_1k=\omega(k)m_1$ for every $k\in\Gamma$, so $m_1=0$ by Lemma A (d). Then (1) for $\nu=\lambda_0$ reads $(R_{\lambda_0}\otimes\mathrm{id})r=0$, so $r=0$ by Lemma 3 (a) of the degree-one theorem. So $\bar J(\bar x_1)=0$.

So $\bar J$ vanishes on $\bar x_0$, $\bar x_1$, $V_2$ and $V_3$, hence on $V$.

*Even $d=4$.* Here $(a,b)=(3,4)$, $A=L\oplus V_1\oplus V_2$ and $w=[\bar x_3,\bar x_4]$. For a tensor $Q\in A\hat\otimes A$ and $W,W'\in\{L,V_1,V_2\}$, write $Q_{WW'}$ for its component in $W\hat\otimes W'$.

(e1) *The transvection $T_1$.* By step 5 of abelianized wild inertia, $T_1$ fixes $\mathcal G$ and every $x_i$ except $x_2\mapsto x_2x_1$; it is tame-trivial with $(T_1)_H-1$ of square $0$. So (ii') applies to its action $\bar T_1=1+\bar N$ on $V$, where $\bar N(\mu\bar x_2)=\mu\bar x_1$ and $\bar N$ kills the other summands. At $\bar a=\bar x_3$: $[\bar x_3,c]=\bar J(\bar T_1\bar x_3)=\mathcal L_2(\bar T_1)[\bar x_3,c]=[\bar x_3,c+\gamma_2\bar x_1]$, so $\iota_3(1\otimes\gamma_2\bar x_1)=0$ and $\gamma_2=0$, as $\iota_3$ is injective. At $\bar x_1$, as $\bar T_1$ fixes $V_{(1)}$, $(\bar T_1\otimes\bar T_1)Q_1+m_1\cdot w=Q_1+m_1\cdot w$, so $(\bar T_1\otimes\bar T_1)Q_1=Q_1$. At $\bar x_2$, $(\bar T_1\otimes\bar T_1)Q_2+m_2\cdot w=\bar J(\bar x_2+\bar x_1)=Q_2+Q_1+(m_2+m_1)\cdot w$. Comparing components in $V_3\hat\otimes V_4$ gives $\Delta_2(m_1)=0$, so $m_1=0$, and $(\bar T_1\otimes\bar T_1)Q_2=Q_2+Q_1$.

*Claim.* If $Q\in\mathcal L_2(L\oplus V_2)$ and $(\bar T_1\otimes\bar T_1)Q=Q$, then $Q=0$. Indeed, for $W\in\{L,V_2\}$ the component of $(\bar T_1\otimes\bar T_1)Q-Q=(\bar N\otimes1+1\otimes\bar N+\bar N\otimes\bar N)Q$ in $V_1\hat\otimes W$ is $(\bar N\otimes1)Q_{V_2W}$, and $\bar N$ maps $V_2$ isomorphically onto $V_1$. So $Q_{V_2W}=0$, hence $Q\in L\otimes L$, and $Q=0$ by antisymmetry.

(e2) *The mixing automorphisms.* For $\lambda\in\ker\omega$, part 2 of the mixing theorem gives a tame-trivial automorphism whose action $B=B_\lambda$ on $V$ is $\Lambda$-linear, fixes $\bar x_0,\bar x_2,\bar x_3$, and has $B\bar x_1=\bar x_1+\lambda\bar x_3$ and $B\bar x_4=\bar x_4-\lambda^*\bar x_2$. So $B^{-1}\bar x_1=\bar x_1-\lambda\bar x_3$.

- *$\gamma_1=0$.* As in (o1), $\bar J_B(\bar x_3)=[\bar x_3,c]+[\bar x_3,\gamma_1\lambda\bar x_3]$ has no component in $V_3\hat\otimes V_3$, so $\gamma_1\lambda_0=0$ and $\gamma_1=0$. So $c=c_0\bar x_0$.
- *$c_0=0$.* Write $Q_{1,L V_1}+Q_{1,V_1L}=[\kappa_1\bar x_1,\bar x_0]$ with $\kappa_1\in\Lambda$, as in (o2). As $m_1=0$, $\bar J_B(\bar x_1)=\mathcal L_2(B)\big(Q_1-\lambda\cdot[\bar x_3,c_0\bar x_0]\big)=(B\otimes B)Q_1-c_0[\lambda^\omega\bar x_3,\bar x_0]$. In $(B\otimes B)Q_1$, only $[\kappa_1\bar x_1,\bar x_0]$ reaches $V_3\hat\otimes L$, giving $[\kappa_1\lambda\bar x_3,\bar x_0]$, because $B$ fixes $\bar x_0$ and $V_2$. So $\kappa_1\lambda=c_0\lambda^\omega$ for all $\lambda\in\ker\omega$, and $\kappa_1=c_0=0$ exactly as in (o2). So $c=0$, and $\bar J$ vanishes on $V_3\oplus V_4$.
- *$Q_1=0$.* Now $\bar J_B(\bar x_1)=(B\otimes B)Q_1$. For $W\in\{L,V_1,V_2\}$ its component in $V_3\hat\otimes W$ vanishes, and its coordinate is $(R_\lambda\otimes\mathrm{id})$ applied to that of $Q_{1,V_1W}$, as $B(g\bar x_1)=g\bar x_1+g\lambda\bar x_3$. For $\lambda=\lambda_0$, Lemma 3 (a) of the degree-one theorem gives $Q_{1,V_1W}=0$, and by antisymmetry $Q_{1,WV_1}=0$. So $Q_1\in\mathcal L_2(L\oplus V_2)$, and $Q_1=0$ by (e1) and the claim.
- *$m_2=0$ and $Q_2=0$.* As $B^{-1}\bar x_2=\bar x_2$, $\bar J_B(\bar x_2)=(B\otimes B)Q_2+m_2\cdot[\bar x_3,\bar x_4-\lambda^*\bar x_2]$. Its component in $V_3\hat\otimes V_2$ vanishes. With $q$ the coordinate of $Q_{2,V_1V_2}$, this gives
$$(R_\lambda\otimes\mathrm{id})q=(\mathrm{id}\otimes R_{\lambda^*})\Delta_2(m_2)\qquad(\lambda\in\ker\omega).\tag{2}$$
This is (1) with $\delta_0$ replaced by $1$, and the argument of (o3), with $k^{-1}\lambda_0^*\ne0$, gives $m_2=0$ and $q=0$. The components of $\bar J_B(\bar x_2)$ in $V_3\hat\otimes L$ and $V_3\hat\otimes V_1$ vanish too, and they have coordinates $(R_\lambda\otimes\mathrm{id})$ of those of $Q_{2,V_1L}$ and $Q_{2,V_1V_1}$. As before, $Q_2\in\mathcal L_2(L\oplus V_2)$. By (e1), $(\bar T_1\otimes\bar T_1)Q_2=Q_2+Q_1=Q_2$, and $Q_2=0$ by the claim.

So $\bar J$ vanishes on $\bar x_0$, $\bar x_1$, $\bar x_2$, $V_3$ and $V_4$, hence on $V$. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 1.** For $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge5$ this is the corollary of the degree-one theorem. For degree $3$ or $4$, the corollary of [the cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-wild-commutators.md) gives $\alpha=c_g\circ\alpha_0$ with $\alpha_0$ inducing the identity on $G/[P,P]$. It represents the outer class of $\alpha$, so it has finitely many outer conjugates, and the theorem gives $J_1(\alpha_0)=0$. Uniqueness is proved as in the corollary of the degree-one theorem, which does not use the degree. $\square$

**Proof of Corollary 2.** As $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$. The proof of the corollary of [the theorem modulo the third lower central term](finite-outer-conjugacy-is-inner-modulo-the-third-lower-central-term-of-wild-inertia.md) applies word for word. Its only use of degree at least $5$ is the corollary of the degree-one theorem, which Corollary 1 replaces. Its other input, part (a) of its theorem, needs only $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$, $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ and $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge3$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Method.* The proof for degree at least $5$ compares two surface pairs three times: to make the coefficient $\varphi$ common to all pairs, to get the relation $R_\rho\varphi=\rho\cdot\varphi$ from the units on one pair, and to evaluate the boundary-twist equation at a generator the twist does not touch. With one pair, the last is done at $\bar x_0$, where $\bar J$ vanishes by equivariance. The relation is replaced by conjugation with the mixing automorphisms, which move $\bar x_1$ into the pair. As $\bar J_B(\bar a)$ cannot have a component in $V_a\hat\otimes V_a$, this kills the part of $c$ on $\bar x_1$, and $T_1$ kills the part on $\bar x_2$. Then, through (0), the cyclotomic part of $c$ meets a coordinate of $\bar J(\bar x_1)$, and the parameter $\rho-\omega(\rho)$ separates the two. The boundary twist cannot see the multiples $m_k\cdot w$ of the symplectic element, since $\delta^{(2)}w=0$ by the Jacobi identity. They are killed by equivariance instead: identity (1) for all $\nu\in\ker\omega$ forces $m_k$ to transform by $\omega$ under right multiplication.
- *Consistency.* For $d=4$, step 5 of abelianized wild inertia gives an automorphism $T'$ that fixes $\mathcal G$, is trivial on $H$, and has $J_1(T')=p^sj$ with $\bar\jmath(\bar x_2)=[\bar x_1,\bar x_2]\ne0$. So $J_1(T')\ne0$, as $\mathcal L_2$ is torsion-free, and for $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ the theorem shows that $T'$ has infinitely many outer conjugates. This agrees with [complementary twists](complementary-twists-have-infinitely-many-outer-conjugates.md), which proves it for every even $d\ge4$.
- *What remains.* [The higher Johnson theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-higher-johnson-maps-on-wild-inertia.md) and [the higher cocycle theorem](finite-outer-conjugacy-kills-the-higher-cocycle-classes-of-wild-inertia.md) still need degree at least $5$, as Kondo's matrices $M_{ii'}$ in their step 2 compare two pairs. So for degree $3$ or $4$ and $[K(\mu_p):K]\ge4$, it is open whether finite-conjugacy outer classes die in $\operatorname{Out}(G/P_e)$, as they do in degree at least $5$. The coordinate $X_{01}$ for $[K(\mu_p):K]=2$, the case $\mu_p\subseteq K$ and innerness itself are untouched. This continues item 3 of [research issue 535](https://github.com/AnabLab/kb/issues/535).
- *Scope.* Odd $p$, every $s$, $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\in\{3,4\}$ and $\mu_p\not\subseteq K$ for the theorem; $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\ge3$ for the corollaries. Everything is proved here from the linked entries on main, whose sources were not reopened. No novelty is claimed beyond the knowledgebase.
