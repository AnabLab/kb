**Setting.** Let $k/\mathbb Q_2$ have degree $d$, with tame quotient $\pi:G_k\twoheadrightarrow\Gamma$. Let $\mathcal R$ be an irreducible simply-laced root system, with positive roots $\mathcal R^+$, simple roots $\Delta$, rank $r=|\Delta|$ and $N=|\mathcal R^+|$. Put $Q=2^a\ge4$, and choose a generator $\lambda$ of $\mathbb F_Q^\times$. In the split simply connected Chevalley group of type $\mathcal R$ over $\mathbb F_Q$, let
$$
P=\langle x_\beta(u):\beta\in\mathcal R^+,\ u\in\mathbb F_Q\rangle.
$$
The cyclic group $T=\langle s\rangle$ of order $Q-1$ acts by
$$
s\bigl(x_\beta(u)\bigr)=x_\beta(\lambda^{\operatorname{ht}(\beta)}u).
\tag{1}
$$
This is the diagonal automorphism obtained by assigning $\lambda$ to each simple root [@steinberg1967chevalley, §10, Lemma 58]. Set $H=P\rtimes T$, and let $p:H\twoheadrightarrow T$ be projection. All homomorphisms below are continuous, and actual maps, including conjugates, are counted separately.

**Theorem (proved here).** For every epimorphism $\epsilon:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow T$,
$$
\#\{F:G_k\twoheadrightarrow H:pF=\epsilon\pi\}
=C_{\mathcal R,Q}(d),\qquad
C_{\mathcal R,Q}(d)=Q^{r+(d+1)(N-r)}
\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(Q^d-Q^j).
\tag{2}
$$
This count is positive exactly when $d\ge r$. Every epimorphism $b:H\twoheadrightarrow D$ to a finite quotient of $\Gamma$ kills $P$, so $b=\bar b p$. For every epimorphism $q:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow D$,
$$
\#\{F:G_k\twoheadrightarrow H:bF=q\pi\}
=C_{\mathcal R,Q}(d)\,
\#\{\epsilon:\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow T:\bar b\epsilon=q\}.
\tag{3}
$$
Consequently every $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$ preserves all these marked counts, on replacing $q$ by $q\alpha$.

**Proof.** *1. Root-height kernels.* The source inputs are the unique ordered root coordinates and, in the simply-laced case in characteristic two, the relations
$$
x_\beta(u)x_\beta(v)=x_\beta(u+v),\qquad
[x_\beta(u),x_\gamma(v)]=
\begin{cases}
x_{\beta+\gamma}(uv),&\beta+\gamma\in\mathcal R,\\
1,&\beta+\gamma\notin\mathcal R
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
$$
for distinct positive roots; here $[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$. See [@steinberg1967chevalley, §3, Lemma 15, its examples and corollary; Lemmas 16–18 and following Corollaries 1–4]. The coefficient is $\pm1$ before reduction to characteristic two. There are no higher factors in this simply-laced commutator relation.

Let $P_h$ be generated by the root groups of height at least $h$. Root coordinates and (4) give
$$
|P|=Q^N,\qquad [P_b,P_c]\subseteq P_{b+c},\qquad
P_h/P_{h+1}\cong\mathbb F_Q^{\,\#\{\beta:\operatorname{ht}(\beta)=h\}}.
\tag{5}
$$
The subgroups $P_h$ are normal in $P$ and stable under $T$, and are trivial above the largest root height.

Every nonsimple positive root $\beta$ is $\delta+\gamma$ with $\delta$ simple and $\gamma$ positive of height one less. To check this, normalize root lengths to squared length two and write $\beta=\sum n_\delta\delta$. Since $2=(\beta,\beta)=\sum n_\delta(\beta,\delta)$, some simple root in its support has positive pairing with $\beta$. For nonsimple $\beta$ this pairing is one, so reflection in that simple root gives the positive root $\beta-\delta$. Formula (4), with one parameter equal to one, now shows
$$
[P,P_h]=P_{h+1},\qquad
\Phi(P)=[P,P]=P_2,\qquad P/P_2\cong\mathbb F_Q^r.
\tag{6}
$$
For the middle equality, $P/P_2$ is elementary abelian, while every root group of height at least two is a commutator. In particular $|P_2|=Q^{N-r}$.

For $h=2,4,8,\ldots$, put $W_h=P_h/P_{2h}$, omitting trivial layers. It is elementary abelian: (5) makes its root-group generators commute, and each has order two. It is a Frattini kernel in $H/P_{2h}$, because $P_h\subseteq\Phi(P)$ and the Frattini comparison in the [noncentral lifting theorem](noncentral-frattini-layers-in-dyadic-lift-counts.md) applies. By (6),
$$
(W_h)_P=P_h/P_{h+1}.
$$
On this quotient $s$ acts by $\lambda^h\ne1$: the odd integer $Q-1>1$ does not divide a power of two. Thus $(W_h)_{H/P_{2h}}=0$. The same lifting theorem applies successively to
$$
H/P_{2h}\longrightarrow H/P_h.
$$
Starting at $H/P_2$, every epimorphism has precisely
$$
\prod_{h=2,4,8,\ldots}|P_h/P_{2h}|^{d+1}
=|P_2|^{d+1}=Q^{(d+1)(N-r)}
\tag{7}
$$
lifts to $H$, all surjective. The finite product telescopes, including the last truncated layer.

*2. The simple-root quotient.* Fix $\epsilon$, and write $U=\mathbb F_Q$ for the scalar $G_k$-module given by $\epsilon\pi:G_k\twoheadrightarrow T$. Both $U$ and its binary linear dual have no invariants. Local duality and the Euler characteristic therefore give
$$
H^2(k,U)=0,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb F_Q}H^1(k,U)=d
$$
[@milne2006adt, I, Corollary 2.3, p. 28; Theorem 2.8, p. 31]. Maps to $H/P_2=U^r\rtimes T$ above $\epsilon\pi$ are $r$-tuples of cocycles. Restriction to $G_L=\ker(\epsilon\pi)$ injects $H^1(k,U)$ into $\operatorname{Hom}(G_L,U)$: a cocycle vanishing on $G_L$ factors through the odd-order group $T$, whose positive-degree cohomology vanishes by averaging.

The image of $G_L$ in $U^r$ is stable under $T$, hence is an $\mathbb F_Q$-subspace since $\lambda$ generates the field over $\mathbb F_2$. It is proper exactly when a nonzero $\mathbb F_Q$-linear functional annihilates it, equivalently when the coordinate cohomology classes are dependent. Thus the quotient map is surjective exactly when these $r$ classes are independent. There are $\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(Q^d-Q^j)$ such ordered tuples, each with $Q^r$ cocycle representatives because $(U^r)^{G_k}=0$. Multiplying by (7) proves (2), including the zero count when $d<r$.

*3. All tame markings.* A finite dyadic tame quotient has odd cyclic inertia and cyclic quotient by inertia. Every $2$-subgroup injects into that cyclic quotient and is cyclic. Hence $b(P)$ is cyclic and kills $[P,P]=P_2$. By (6), it is also elementary abelian, so has order at most two. The restriction of $b$ to $P/P_2$ is consequently $T$-invariant, since a group of order two has no nontrivial automorphisms. It vanishes because $s-1$ is invertible on this scalar module. Thus $b(P)=1$.

Every map $pF:G_k\twoheadrightarrow T$ kills wild inertia and is uniquely $\epsilon\pi$. Sum (2) over the $\epsilon$ with $\bar b\epsilon=q$ to obtain (3). Precomposition by $\alpha$ bijects these $\epsilon$ with those satisfying $\bar b\epsilon=q\alpha$, proving invariance. $\square$

**A nonabelian moving kernel (proved here).** For type $D_4$, use the roots $\{\pm e_i\pm e_j:i<j\}$ and the simple roots
$$
\delta_1=e_1-e_2,\quad\delta_2=e_2-e_3,\quad
\delta_3=e_3-e_4,\quad\delta_4=e_3+e_4.
$$
There are twelve positive roots and four simple roots. Both
$$
\beta=\delta_1+\delta_2=e_1-e_3,\qquad
\gamma=\delta_2+\delta_3+\delta_4=e_2+e_3
$$
have height at least two, and $\beta+\gamma=e_1+e_2$ is a root. Thus (4) gives $[x_\beta(1),x_\gamma(1)]=x_{e_1+e_2}(1)\ne1$, so $\Phi(P)=P_2$ is nonabelian.

Take $Q=4$, so $T=C_3$. The normal closure in $H$ of $\{f^{-1}s(f):f\in\Phi(P)\}$ is all of $P_2$. Indeed it contains each height-two root group, since $\lambda^2-1\ne0$. Repeatedly subtracting simple roots as in Step 1 and using normality then includes every higher root group. In this example the moving Frattini kernel itself is nonabelian, while the height-three root groups are fixed by $T$. The theorem nevertheless gives
$$
C_{D_4,4}(d)=4^{4+8(d+1)}\prod_{j=0}^{3}(4^d-4^j),
$$
positive for every $d\ge4$.

**Scope.** For type $A_r$, $N=r(r+1)/2$ and the formula recovers [unitriangular counts](unitriangular-dyadic-marked-counts.md) with matrix size $r+1$. Types $D_r$ and $E_6,E_7,E_8$ supply further targets. The proof requires the specified height-scaling action and $Q\ge4$. It makes no assertion for other odd-order actions, multiply-laced or twisted groups, or the trivial scalar action over $\mathbb F_2$. It excludes these finite count obstructions without producing a full tame-automorphism lift or settling dyadic classification.
