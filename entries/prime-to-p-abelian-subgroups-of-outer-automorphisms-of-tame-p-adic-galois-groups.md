**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd, $G = G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, $V \subset G$ the wild inertia group and $\mathcal{G} = G/V$. As in [automorphisms of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on norm-one units](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md), proof of part 2, which follows Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg, fix a splitting $G = V \rtimes \mathcal{G}$ and generators $\sigma, \tau$ of $\mathcal{G}$ with $\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1} = \tau^p$, where $\sigma$ lifts Frobenius and $\tau$ topologically generates the tame inertia group $\mathcal{T}$. Let $\omega : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{F}_p^\times$ give the action on $\mu_p$, so $\omega(\sigma) = 1$ and $\omega(\tau)$ has order $p - 1$. A *level* is a finite tamely ramified Galois extension $K'/\mathbb{Q}_p$ containing $\zeta_p$ and the unramified quadratic extension; levels are cofinal, and $\Lambda = \mathbb{F}_p[[\mathcal{G}]]$ is the inverse limit of the group algebras $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$, $\Gamma' = \mathrm{Gal}(K'/\mathbb{Q}_p)$. Let $*$ be the continuous $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear anti-automorphism of $\Lambda$ with $\rho^* = \omega(\rho)\rho^{-1}$ for $\rho \in \mathcal{G}$. For a level $K'$, the element $\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} \in \mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$ of that entry satisfies $\rho\,\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \omega(\rho)\,\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$ for every $\rho \in \Gamma'$ (its proof of part 2 (d)).

Say that a unit $u$ of $\Lambda$ has the *three properties* if it is central, $uu^* = 1$, and $u\,\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'} = \kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$ in $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$ for every level $K'$. The lifting argument of the companion entry, proof of part 2 (d), uses only these properties of the unit it starts from.

**Theorem.** This is proved here, from a construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg and a splitting theorem of Nishio.

1. *Groups of automorphisms.* For every finite abelian group $E$ of order prime to $p$, $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ has a subgroup isomorphic to $E$ whose elements all induce the identity on $G/V$. It maps isomorphically onto a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G)$.
2. *Tame fields.* Let $K \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ be a finite tamely ramified extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Every such $E$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ whose elements induce the identity on $G_K/V$, and this subgroup maps isomorphically onto a subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$. In particular $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ has elements of order $n$ for every $n \ge 1$ prime to $p$.
3. *Explicit units.* For $n \ge 1$ prime to $p$, there are a central idempotent $E_n \in \Lambda$ and an element $\sigma_{p'}$ of $\mathcal{G}$, both defined in step (a) of the proof, such that
$$\eta = \big(1 + (\sigma_{p'} - 1)E_n\big)\big(1 + (\sigma_{p'} - 1)E_n^*\big)$$
is a unit of order $n$ with the three properties. Here $\sigma_{p'}$ is the prime-to-$p$ part of $\sigma$, and $E_n$ averages over a closed normal subgroup of $\mathcal{G}$ of order prime to $p$ that contains $\mathcal{T}$ and $\sigma_{p'}^n$.

**Examples.** For $p = 3$, the [earlier construction](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) gives only groups of exponent $2$, and part 2 adds, for instance, $\mathbb{Z}/4$ and $\mathbb{Z}/5$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_3})$. For $p = 5$ it adds $\mathbb{Z}/3$, whose order does not divide $p - 1 = 4$. In each case these are classes of automorphisms of finite order that act trivially on the tame quotient.

**Context.** Nishio asks whether every finite subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ is quasi-geometric; for $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$ this asks whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ is torsion-free ([finite subgroups of outer automorphism groups](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md), consequence 2). For odd $p$ his own subgroups need $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 3$, and a given nontrivial finite group $F$ of order prime to $p$ occurs among them once $[k:\mathbb{Q}_p] \ge 2|F| + 1$ (consequence 3 there). For abelian groups, part 2 removes the condition on the degree for tame $k$. The [finite abelian subgroups](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) found earlier come from central units that are constant on the blocks cut out by characters of tame inertia, with constants in $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, which is why their exponents divide $p - 1$. Here the units come from the group algebra of a finite cyclic quotient $\mathbb{Z}/n$ of the Frobenius direction, whose unit group contains $\mathbb{F}_{p^r}^\times$ for $r$ the order of $p$ modulo $n$.

- By consequence 1 of the Nishio entry, no nontrivial element of the image of these groups in $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$ lies in a quasi-geometric subgroup, in particular in any conjugate of $\mathrm{Aut}(K)$.
- These automorphisms do not settle the case $p = 3$ of [a two-dimensional representation of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ that is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate](two-dimensional-representation-of-g-qp-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md). For $\gamma$ of finite order, the exponent $b(\gamma)$ of that entry is a root of unity in $\mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, which for $p = 3$ is $\pm 1$.
- Whether $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p})$ has elements of order $p$ is not settled here. The splitting step cannot produce them, because the kernel it splits off is pro-$p$. The units used here, being central, commute; noncentral units that are unitary for the Jannsen–Wingberg element give [nonabelian finite subgroups](nonabelian-finite-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md). The case $p = 2$ is not covered, because the construction of Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg assumes $p$ odd.

**Proof.**

*(a) An idempotent.* Let $\pi' \in \widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ be the idempotent with $\pi'\widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and put $\sigma_{p'} = \sigma^{\pi'}$. The tame inertia group $\mathcal{T}$, the image of the inertia group in $\mathcal{G}$, is isomorphic to $\prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, and $\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T} \cong G/I$ is isomorphic to $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ [@nishio2026finite, Lemma 2.1 (ii), (iii), p. 8], topologically generated by the image of the Frobenius lift $\sigma$. Fix $n \ge 1$ prime to $p$, and let $H_n \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ be the closed subgroup generated by $\mathcal{T}$ and $\sigma_{p'}^n$.

- $H_n$ is normal, because it contains $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}$ is abelian. Under $\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} = \mathbb{Z}_p \times \prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, with $\sigma \mapsto 1$, the image of $H_n$ is the closed subgroup generated by $n\pi'$, which is $0 \times n\prod_{\ell \ne p}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. So $H_n$ is an extension of a profinite group of order prime to $p$ by another, and its images in all finite quotients have order prime to $p$.
- Reduction modulo $n$ gives a surjective homomorphism $\epsilon_n : \mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T} \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{Z}/n$. It kills $\mathcal{T}$ and $\sigma_{p'}^n$, hence $H_n$, and $\epsilon_n(\sigma_{p'}) = 1$, because $\pi' \equiv 1 \pmod n$.

For a level $K'$, let $\bar H$ be the image of $H_n$ in $\Gamma'$. It is a normal subgroup of order prime to $p$ containing the image of $\tau$, and we put $e_{K'} = |\bar H|^{-1}\sum_{h \in \bar H} h \in \mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$. If $K' \subseteq K''$ are levels, $\bar H''$ maps onto $\bar H'$ with all fibres of size $|\bar H''|/|\bar H'|$, so $e_{K''}$ maps to $e_{K'}$. So these elements define $E_n \in \Lambda$. At each level, $e_{K'}$ is an idempotent, $h\,e_{K'} = e_{K'}h = e_{K'}$ for $h \in \bar H$, and $e_{K'}$ is central because $\bar H$ is normal. Write $\kappa\lambda$ for $\kappa_{K'}\lambda_{K'}$ when the level is fixed.

1. *For every polynomial $v \in \mathbb{F}_p[x]$, the element $v(\sigma_{p'})E_n$ is central in $\Lambda$.* It commutes with $\sigma$, since $\sigma_{p'}$ is a power of $\sigma$ and $E_n$ is central. For every $j$, $\sigma_{p'}^{-j}\tau\sigma_{p'}^{j}$ lies in $\mathcal{T} \subseteq H_n$, so $\tau\sigma_{p'}^jE_n = \sigma_{p'}^j(\sigma_{p'}^{-j}\tau\sigma_{p'}^j)E_n = \sigma_{p'}^jE_n = \sigma_{p'}^jE_n\tau$. As $\sigma$ and $\tau$ topologically generate $\mathcal{G}$, and $\mathbb{F}_p[\mathcal{G}]$ is dense in $\Lambda$, the claim follows.
2. *A ring homomorphism.* $\sigma_{p'}^nE_n = E_n$, since $\sigma_{p'}^n \in H_n$. So $v \mapsto v(\sigma_{p'})E_n$ is a ring homomorphism from $\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x^n - 1)$ to the ring $E_n\Lambda$, whose identity is $E_n$.
3. *The adjoint.* At each level, $e_{K'}^* = |\bar H|^{-1}\sum_{h}\omega(h)h^{-1} = |\bar H|^{-1}\sum_{h}\omega(h)^{-1}h$, substituting $h \mapsto h^{-1}$. Also $\sigma_{p'}^* = \sigma_{p'}^{-1}$, because $\omega(\sigma) = 1$.
4. *Values on $\kappa\lambda$.* As $\omega(\tau) \ne 1$, the restriction of $\omega$ to $\bar H$ is a nontrivial homomorphism to $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$, so $\sum_{h \in \bar H}\omega(h) = 0$: multiplying the sum by $\omega(h_0) \ne 1$ permutes its terms. Since $h\,\kappa\lambda = \omega(h)\kappa\lambda$, this gives $e_{K'}\kappa\lambda = |\bar H|^{-1}\big(\sum_h \omega(h)\big)\kappa\lambda = 0$ and $e_{K'}^*\kappa\lambda = |\bar H|^{-1}\sum_h \omega(h)^{-1}\omega(h)\,\kappa\lambda = \kappa\lambda$. Also $\sigma_{p'}^{j}\kappa\lambda = \kappa\lambda$ for every $j \in \mathbb{Z}$, as $\omega(\sigma_{p'}) = 1$.
5. *A detecting quotient.* Let $\rho_n : \Lambda \to \mathbb{F}_p[\mathbb{Z}/n]$ be the continuous ring homomorphism induced by $\epsilon_n$, and $g = \epsilon_n(\sigma_{p'})$, a generator. It factors through $\mathbb{F}_p[\Gamma']$ for the levels $K'$ containing the unramified extension of degree $n$, which are cofinal. At such a level, $\epsilon_n$ kills $\bar H$, so $\rho_n(E_n) = 1$ and $\rho_n(E_n^*) = |\bar H|^{-1}\sum_h\omega(h)^{-1} = 0$, by the vanishing in 4 applied to $\omega^{-1}$.

*(b) Units.* Let $U(n)$ be the group of units $v$ of $\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x^n - 1)$ with $v(1) = 1$. For $v \in U(n)$ put
$$a_v = v(\sigma_{p'})E_n + 1 - E_n,\qquad \eta_v = a_v\,(a_v^*)^{-1}.$$
For $v = x$ this is the unit $\eta$ of part 3, since $(a_x^*)^{-1} = 1 + (\sigma_{p'} - 1)E_n^*$ by the formula $a_v^* = E_n^*v(\sigma_{p'}^{-1}) + 1 - E_n^*$ below.

- *Central units and a homomorphism.* As $E_n$ is idempotent, $a_va_w = a_{vw}$, and $a_1 = 1$. So $a_v$ is a unit with inverse $a_{v^{-1}}$, and it is central by (a) 1. Since $*$ is an anti-automorphism, $a_v^*$ is a central unit too, and so is $\eta_v$. As all these elements commute, $v \mapsto \eta_v$ is a homomorphism from $U(n)$ to $\Lambda^\times$.
- *$\eta_v\eta_v^* = 1$.* We have $\eta_v^* = \big((a_v^*)^{-1}\big)^*a_v^* = a_v^{-1}a_v^*$, so $\eta_v\eta_v^* = a_v(a_v^*)^{-1}a_v^{-1}a_v^* = 1$ by centrality.
- *$\eta_v$ fixes $\kappa\lambda$ at every level.* By (a) 4, $a_v\kappa\lambda = v(\sigma_{p'})e_{K'}\kappa\lambda + \kappa\lambda - e_{K'}\kappa\lambda = \kappa\lambda$. By (a) 3, $a_v^* = E_n^*v(\sigma_{p'}^{-1}) + 1 - E_n^*$. By (a) 4, $v(\sigma_{p'}^{-1})\kappa\lambda = v(1)\kappa\lambda = \kappa\lambda$, and then $a_v^*\kappa\lambda = e_{K'}^*\kappa\lambda + \kappa\lambda - e_{K'}^*\kappa\lambda = \kappa\lambda$. Hence $(a_v^*)^{-1}\kappa\lambda = \kappa\lambda$ and $\eta_v\kappa\lambda = \kappa\lambda$.
- *Injectivity.* By (a) 5, $\rho_n(a_v) = v(g)$ and $\rho_n(a_v^*) = \rho_n(E_n^*)\rho_n(v(\sigma_{p'}^{-1})) + 1 - \rho_n(E_n^*) = 1$. So $\rho_n(\eta_v) = v(g)$. As $x \mapsto g$ is an isomorphism $\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x^n - 1) \to \mathbb{F}_p[\mathbb{Z}/n]$, $\eta_v = 1$ forces $v = 1$.

So $v \mapsto \eta_v$ embeds $U(n)$ in the group of units of $\Lambda$ with the three properties. Since $x \in U(n)$ has order $n$, part 3 follows.

*(c) The groups in $U(n)$.* As $p \nmid n$, the polynomial $x^n - 1$ is prime to its derivative $nx^{n-1}$, so it is a product of distinct monic irreducible factors $f$. By the Chinese remainder theorem, $\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(x^n - 1)$ is the product of the fields $\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(f)$, and $v \mapsto v(1)$ is the projection to the factor $f = x - 1$. So $U(n)$ contains $\prod_{f \in S}(\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(f))^\times$, embedded with all other components equal to $1$, for every set $S$ of factors not containing $x - 1$. If the roots of $f$ have multiplicative order $d$, the class of $x$ in the field $\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(f)$ has order $d$, so the cyclic group $(\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(f))^\times$ contains a cyclic subgroup of order $m$ for every $m \mid d$.

Now let $E \cong \mathbb{Z}/m_1 \times \cdots \times \mathbb{Z}/m_k$ with each $m_i > 1$ prime to $p$. Choose distinct primes $q_1, \ldots, q_k$ not dividing $p\,m_1\cdots m_k$, and put $d_i = m_iq_i$ and $n = d_1\cdots d_k$, which is prime to $p$. As $p \nmid d_i$, there is a primitive $d_i$-th root of unity in $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$. It is a root of $x^n - 1$, and we let $f_i$ be its minimal polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_p$. The $f_i$ are distinct, because $q_i$ divides the order $d_i$ of the roots of $f_i$ but not $d_j$ for $j \ne i$. They differ from $x - 1$, because $d_i > 1$. So $E$ embeds in $\prod_i(\mathbb{F}_p[x]/(f_i))^\times \subseteq U(n)$.

*(d) Conclusion.* Let $E$ be as in part 1, embedded in some $U(n)$ by (c), and let $U \subseteq \Lambda^\times$ be its image under $v \mapsto \eta_v$. By (b), $U$ is a finite group isomorphic to $E$, of order prime to $p$, consisting of units with the three properties. Steps (c) and (d) of the proof in [finite abelian subgroups](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) use only this about their group of units. Step (c) applies the companion lifting argument to each $u \in U$ and gets an automorphism of $G/V^2$ fixing the images of $\sigma, \tau, x_0$ and acting on $V/V^2$ $\Lambda$-linearly with $\bar x_1 \mapsto u\bar x_1$; these form a group isomorphic to $U$, by centrality and because $\bar x_1$ generates a free $\Lambda$-module. Step (d) splits off a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ mapping isomorphically onto this group, by Nishio's pro-$p$ kernel theorem and his splitting corollary. This gives a subgroup $\Gamma \subseteq \mathrm{Aut}(G)$ isomorphic to $E$ whose elements induce the identity on $G/V$. Being finite, it maps isomorphically into $\mathrm{Out}(G)$ by consequence 1 of the [Nishio entry](finite-subgroups-of-p-adic-outer-automorphism-groups.md). This proves part 1. Step (f) of the same proof restricts $\Gamma$ injectively to $G_K$ for tamely ramified $K$, with image acting trivially on $G_K/V$ and mapping isomorphically into $\mathrm{Out}(G_K)$; it uses only that $\Gamma$ is finite and acts trivially on $G/V$. For the last assertion of part 2, take $E = \mathbb{Z}/n$. $\square$

*Example check.* For $p = 3$ and $n = 4$, $x^4 - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)(x^2 + 1)$ over $\mathbb{F}_3$, with $x^2 + 1$ irreducible because $-1$ is not a square modulo $3$. The element $x \in U(4)$ has order $4$, so $\eta_x$ gives an automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}$ of order $4$ whose class in $\mathrm{Out}(G_{\mathbb{Q}_3})$ has order $4$.

**Remark.** As for the earlier groups, the subgroups $\Gamma$ are not canonical: their elements are determined only modulo the pro-$p$ group of automorphisms inducing the identity on $G/V^2$.

**Taken from the sources without re-verification.** The same inputs as in the [finite abelian subgroups](finite-abelian-subgroups-of-outer-automorphisms-of-tame-p-adic-galois-groups.md) entry, used here through it: Neukirch, Schmidt and Wingberg's computation that the substitution preserves the Demuškin relations modulo third $p$-central terms, which they derive from Jannsen and Wingberg's description of those relations; the freeness of the $\Lambda$-module generated by $\bar x_1$, which they credit to Jannsen and Wingberg, §2; the lifting theorem (7.5.15), which they attribute to Wingberg; and the profinite Schur–Zassenhaus theorem behind Nishio's Corollary 1.4 [@nishio2026finite, proof of Corollary 1.4, p. 7].
