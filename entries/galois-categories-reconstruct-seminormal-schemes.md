**Setting.** For a quasicompact quasiseparated scheme $X$, $\operatorname{Gal}(X)$ is the Barwick–Glasman–Haine [topological category of geometric points and specializations](galois-categories-reconstruct-normal-schemes.md). Each object lies over a scheme point, and $x\to y$ exists only if the point under $x$ lies in the closure of the point under $y$. The objects minimal for this order are those over closed points [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, Construction 0.0.1, p. 6, and §14.4.3, p. 232]. Over a field $k$, retain $\operatorname{Gal}(X)\to\operatorname{Gal}(\operatorname{Spec}k)\simeq BG_k$ [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, §14.4.8, p. 233]. As in [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, Theorem 14.4.7, p. 233], write $\operatorname{Fun}^{\min}_{BG_k}(\operatorname{Gal}X,\operatorname{Gal}Y)$ for the groupoid of continuous functors over $BG_k$ carrying minimal objects to minimal objects, with natural isomorphisms over $BG_k$.

A field $k$ *satisfies étale reconstruction* if, for all finite-type $k$-schemes $X,Y$, the map
$$
\operatorname{Hom}_k(X^{\mathrm{awn}},Y)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{pin}}_k(X^{\mathrm{awn}}_{\mathrm{\acute et}},Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}})
$$
is bijective. The target is the set of isomorphism classes of geometric morphisms of étale topoi over $(\operatorname{Spec}k)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ that send closed points to closed points, called *pinned* [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Definition 2.19, Convention 2.23, Proposition 2.28(2.28.3) and Definition 2.29, pp. 13–15]. Here $c:X^{\mathrm{awn}}\to X$ is the absolute weak normalization, a universal homeomorphism. It is the seminormalization in characteristic $0$ and the perfection in characteristic $p$ [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Theorem 1.13(1.13.1) and Corollary 1.15, p. 6].

Sub-$p$-adic fields satisfy étale reconstruction [@carlsonstix2024, Theorem A, p. 2]. So do fields finitely generated over their prime field, of transcendence degree at least $1$ in positive characteristic [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Theorem 0.1, p. 2]. Both theorems state condition (2.28.2) of the cited proposition; Carlson–Stix restrict to coherent topoi, which does not change the hom-groupoids between étale topoi.

**Theorem.** Let $k$ satisfy étale reconstruction, and let $X,Y$ be finite-type $k$-schemes. Then
$$
\operatorname{Hom}_k(X^{\mathrm{awn}},Y)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Fun}^{\min}_{BG_k}(\operatorname{Gal}X,\operatorname{Gal}Y),
\qquad f\longmapsto \operatorname{Gal}(f)\circ\operatorname{Gal}(c)^{-1},
$$
is an equivalence from a set to a groupoid. Moreover, $\operatorname{Gal}X\simeq\operatorname{Gal}Y$ over $BG_k$ if and only if $X^{\mathrm{awn}}\cong Y^{\mathrm{awn}}$ over $k$.

**Special cases.**

- Let $k$ be sub-$p$-adic, for example a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, or finitely generated over $\mathbb Q$. If $X$ is seminormal, then $\operatorname{Hom}_k(X,Y)\simeq\operatorname{Fun}^{\min}_{BG_k}(\operatorname{Gal}X,\operatorname{Gal}Y)$, and seminormal finite-type $k$-schemes are determined up to $k$-isomorphism by their Galois categories over $BG_k$. Normal rings are seminormal [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Proposition 1.2(1.2.4), p. 4], so this contains the [normal case](galois-categories-reconstruct-normal-schemes.md).
- Let $k$ be finitely generated of characteristic $p$ and transcendence degree at least $1$. Then $\operatorname{Hom}_k(X^{\mathrm{perf}},Y)\simeq\operatorname{Fun}^{\min}_{BG_k}(\operatorname{Gal}X,\operatorname{Gal}Y)$. Barwick–Glasman–Haine had left positive characteristic open [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, §0.5, p. 16]. Finite fields are not covered.
- The minimal-object condition cannot be dropped. In characteristic $0$, a continuous section of $G_{k(t)}\to G_k$ gives a geometric morphism $(\operatorname{Spec}k)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\to(\mathbb P^1_k)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ over $k$ landing at the generic point [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Example 2.30, p. 15]. By Steps 2–4 below it is a continuous functor $BG_k\to\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb P^1_k)$ over $BG_k$ that misses the minimal objects and comes from no $k$-point.

Carlson–Haine–Wolf note that, over such fields, condensed refinements of the category of points of $X_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$, including the Galois category, determine absolutely weakly normal schemes. They deduce this from their statement about isomorphisms [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Remark 2.37, p. 16]. The morphism-level form above is proved here.

**Proof.** This is the argument by which Barwick–Glasman–Haine deduce Theorem 14.4.7 from Voevodsky's theorem [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, p. 233], with étale reconstruction for 1-topoi as input.

1. *Topoi.* Since $c$ is a universal homeomorphism over $k$, $c_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ is an equivalence over $(\operatorname{Spec}k)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ [@stacks2026, Proposition 59.45.4, Tag 03SI]. It is a homeomorphism on underlying spaces, so composing with its inverse preserves pinned morphisms. Hence $f\mapsto f_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\circ c_{\mathrm{\acute et}}^{-1}$ is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_k(X^{\mathrm{awn}},Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{pin}}_k(X_{\mathrm{\acute et}},Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}})$. The target is a full subgroupoid of the hom-groupoid over $(\operatorname{Spec}k)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ and is equivalent to a set [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Notation 2.2 and Proposition 2.22, pp. 10 and 13].
2. *From 1-topoi to ∞-topoi.* Let $\mathbf X$ be the ∞-topos of sheaves of spaces on the 1-site of étale $X$-schemes. It is 1-localic, and its 0-truncated objects form $X_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, Examples 3.1.3 and 3.2.7, pp. 49 and 52]. As $\mathbf Y$ is 1-localic, truncation gives an equivalence $\operatorname{Fun}_*(\mathbf X,\mathbf Y)\simeq\operatorname{Fun}_*(X_{\mathrm{\acute et}},Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}})$ [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, Notation 3.2.2 and Definition 3.2.3, p. 51]. It is induced by the functor $(-)_{\le0}$, so it respects composition. Passing to maximal subgroupoids and to fibres over the structure maps to the base identifies maps $\mathbf X\to\mathbf Y$ over $\operatorname{Spec}k$ with $\operatorname{Hom}_k(X_{\mathrm{\acute et}},Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}})$. The underlying locale of an ∞-topos is its poset of $(-1)$-truncated objects [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, Notation 3.2.2, p. 51], and these are 0-truncated. So corresponding morphisms induce the same map $|X|\to|Y|$.
3. *Exodromy.* Finite-type $k$-schemes are quasicompact and quasiseparated, so $\operatorname{Map}(\mathbf X,\mathbf Y)\to\operatorname{Map}_{\mathbf{Str}^\wedge_\pi}(\operatorname{Gal}X,\operatorname{Gal}Y)$ is an equivalence [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, §12.1.7, p. 189]. It is induced by the functor $\operatorname{Gal}$ [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, Definition 12.1.3, p. 188], so it identifies the fibres over $\operatorname{Spec}k$ and over $\operatorname{Gal}(\operatorname{Spec}k)\simeq BG_k$. Every geometric morphism $X_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\to Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ is coherent [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Corollary A.9(A.9.1), p. 36]. Coherence agrees for 1-topoi and their 1-localic ∞-topoi [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, §3.6.9, p. 64], so reading §12.1.7 with coherent morphisms only changes nothing.
4. *Pinned means minimal-preserving.* The underlying category of $\operatorname{Gal}X$ is naturally the category of points of $\mathbf X$, which are the geometric points of $X$ [@barwickglasmanhaine2020, Construction 12.1.5, p. 189]. So the functor underlying $\operatorname{Gal}(\varphi)$ sends a point $x$ to $\varphi\circ x$. The scheme point under a point of the topos is the induced point of its locale of subterminal objects, whose space is $|X|$ [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Recollection 2.4 and Example 2.5, pp. 10–11]. Hence $\varphi\circ x$ lies over $|\varphi|(x_0)$, where $x_0$ is the point under $x$. Every scheme point has a geometric point over it, and the minimal objects are those over closed points. So $\operatorname{Gal}(\varphi)$ preserves minimal objects if and only if $|\varphi|$ preserves closed points, that is, $\varphi$ is pinned.

Steps 1–4 give the equivalence, and functoriality of $\operatorname{Gal}$ shows that $f$ goes to $\operatorname{Gal}(f)\circ\operatorname{Gal}(c)^{-1}$.

For the last claim, Steps 2 and 3 compose to a fully faithful functor from étale topoi over $(\operatorname{Spec}k)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ to profinite stratified spaces over $BG_k$. A fully faithful functor reflects equivalences, so equivalences $\operatorname{Gal}X\simeq\operatorname{Gal}Y$ over $BG_k$ correspond to equivalences $X_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\simeq Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ over $(\operatorname{Spec}k)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$. Via $c_{\mathrm{\acute et}}$ for $X$ and for $Y$, these correspond to $k$-isomorphisms $X^{\mathrm{awn}}\cong Y^{\mathrm{awn}}$. This uses Proposition 2.36(2.36.1) of Carlson–Haine–Wolf, which applies because $X^{\mathrm{awn}},Y^{\mathrm{awn}}$ are topologically of finite type over $k$ [@carlsonhainewolf2025, Lemma 1.27 and Proposition 2.36, pp. 8 and 16].
