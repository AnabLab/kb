**Definition.** Let $p$ be a prime. A pro-$p$ group $G$ is a *Demushkin group* if [@labute1967, p. 106]:

1. $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} H^1(G, \mathbb{Z}/p) < \infty$,
2. $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} H^2(G, \mathbb{Z}/p) = 1$, and
3. the cup product $H^1(G, \mathbb{Z}/p) \times H^1(G, \mathbb{Z}/p) \to H^2(G, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ is a nondegenerate bilinear form.

Serre gives the same definition, with the spelling Demuškin [@serre1963, §3, p. 146].

**Invariants.**

- *Rank.* $n(G) = \dim H^1(G, \mathbb{Z}/p)$ is the minimal number of topological generators. Condition 2 means that $G \cong F/(r)$ for a free pro-$p$ group $F$ of rank $n$ and a single relation $r \in F^p(F, F)$, where $(r)$ is the closed normal subgroup generated by $r$ [@labute1967, p. 106].
- *The number $q$.* The abelianization is $G/(G, G) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^{n-1} \times \mathbb{Z}_p/q\mathbb{Z}_p$ for a uniquely determined power $q = q(G)$ of $p$, with the convention $p^\infty = 0$ [@labute1967, p. 106].
- *The canonical character.* There is a unique continuous homomorphism $\chi : G \to \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$ with the following property. Write $I_j(\chi)$ for $\mathbb{Z}/p^j$ with $G$ acting through $\chi$. Then $H^1(G, I_j(\chi)) \to H^1(G, I_1(\chi))$ is surjective for every $j \ge 1$ [@labute1967, p. 106 and Theorem 4]. The image $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ is an invariant of $G$, and $q$ is the largest power of $p$ with $\mathrm{Im}(\chi) \subseteq 1 + q\mathbb{Z}_p$ [@labute1967, Corollary to Theorem 4]. Serre introduced $\chi$ to classify the case $q = 2$ [@labute1967, p. 106].

**Duality.** If $G$ is infinite, equivalently $n \ge 2$, then $G$ has cohomological dimension $2$ [@serre1963, 9.1], and $\chi$ is the character by which $G$ acts on its dualizing module $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ [@serre1963, 9.3]. Labute attributes the first fact to Tate [@labute1967, p. 107, Remark (1)]. Every open subgroup $H$ of an infinite Demushkin group $G$ is again a Demushkin group, with $n(H) - 2 = (G : H)(n(G) - 2)$ [@serre1963, 9.2].

**Examples.**

- The only finite Demushkin group is $\mathbb{Z}/2$, with $n = 1$ [@serre1963, §9, p. 153].
- For $g \ge 1$, the pro-$p$ group with generators $x_1, \dots, x_{2g}$ and the single relation $(x_1, x_2)(x_3, x_4) \cdots (x_{2g-1}, x_{2g})$ is a Demushkin group with $n = 2g$ and $q = 0$ [@labute1967, p. 106]. This is the relation of the fundamental group of a compact orientable surface of genus $g$, and Serre remarks that it would be easy to prove directly that the pro-$p$ completion of that fundamental group is a Demushkin group [@serre1963, Remark c after Théorème 3.1].
- If $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is finite and contains a primitive $p$-th root of unity, then the Galois group of the maximal $p$-extension of $K$ is a Demushkin group. See [maximal pro-$p$ Galois groups of $p$-adic fields](maximal-pro-p-galois-groups-of-p-adic-fields.md).

The [classification of Demushkin groups](classification-of-demushkin-groups.md) shows that $n$ and $\mathrm{Im}(\chi)$ determine $G$.
