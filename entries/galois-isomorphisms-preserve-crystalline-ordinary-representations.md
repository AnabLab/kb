**Setting.** Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite, $I_K$ its inertia group, $\chi$ the cyclotomic character, and use the conventions and the facts (a)–(d) of [the de Rham criterion](de-rham-criterion-for-single-weight-filtrations.md); in particular $\mathbb{Q}_p(1)$ has Hodge–Tate weight $1$. A single-weight filtration of $V$ is *ordinary* if it is defined over $K$ with $J = I_K$. That is, $0 = F_0 \subsetneq F_1 \subsetneq \cdots \subsetneq F_m = V$ are $G_K$-stable, and $I_K$ acts on $F_i/F_{i-1}$ by $\chi^{h_i}$, so each $F_i/F_{i-1}$ is an unramified representation twisted by $\mathbb{Q}_p(h_i)$. The $h_i$ may repeat and need not be ordered. For a representation $X$ put
$$H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X) = \ker\big(H^1(K, X) \to H^1(I_K, X)\big),$$
with continuous cohomology throughout, and write $H^1_f$, $H^1_g$ for Bloch and Kato's subspaces, as in [Bloch–Kato local conditions](bloch-kato-local-conditions.md).

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *Finite parts.*
   - (a) If $X$ is de Rham with all Hodge–Tate weights $\le 0$, then $H^1_f(K, X) = H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X)$, of dimension $\dim H^0(K, X)$.
   - (b) If $W$ is de Rham with all Hodge–Tate weights $\ge 1$, then $H^1_f(K, W)$ is the annihilator of $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, W^\vee(1))$ under the cup-product pairing $H^1(K, W) \times H^1(K, W^\vee(1)) \to H^2(K, \mathbb{Q}_p(1))$.
2. *Semistability.* A representation with an ordinary single-weight filtration is semistable if and only if it is de Rham.
3. *Transport.* Let $L/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite, $\alpha : G_L \to G_K$ a continuous isomorphism, and $V$ a representation of $G_K$ with an ordinary single-weight filtration. Then $V \circ \alpha$ is semistable if and only if $V$ is, and crystalline if and only if $V$ is.

**Remarks.**

- *Tate twists.* For $W = \mathbb{Q}_p(r)$ with $r \ge 1$, $I_K$ acts on $W^\vee(1) = \mathbb{Q}_p(1-r)$ through $\chi^{1-r}$. For $r \ge 2$ this has no inertia invariants, so $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}} = 0$ by fact (f) below and part 1(b) gives $H^1_f(K, \mathbb{Q}_p(r)) = H^1(K, \mathbb{Q}_p(r))$. For $r = 1$, part 1(b) says that $H^1_f(K, \mathbb{Q}_p(1))$ is the annihilator of the unramified homomorphisms $G_K \to \mathbb{Q}_p$. Both agree with the dimensions in Bloch and Kato's table [@blochkato1990tamagawa, Example 3.9, p. 359]. The [Kummer-extension entry](kummer-extensions-and-tate-curve-periods.md) identifies the crystalline hyperplane for $r = 1$ with the Kummer classes of units.
- *Why transport works.* Part 1(b) describes $H^1_f(K, W)$ through inertia and cup products alone, and crystallinity of an ordinary representation is a condition on successive extension classes in such groups. Outside ordinary representations crystallinity is not preserved in general: the Lubin–Tate representations of fields $k \ne \mathbb{Q}_p$ in [the overview of Aut-intrinsic Hodge–Tate representations](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md) are crystalline, but under that overview's hypotheses some of their composites with automorphisms of $G_k$ are not even Hodge–Tate.
- *Scope.* The theorem assumes the filtration is $G_K$-stable with all of $I_K$ acting on its pieces through powers of $\chi$. The extension to filtrations defined only over a finite extension, including crystallinity and semistability over $K$ itself, is proved in [canonical weight filtrations and potentially ordinary transport](canonical-weight-filtrations-and-potentially-ordinary-transport.md).

**Facts used.**

- (e) *Crystalline pieces.* Crystallinity over $K$ can be checked on $I_K = G_{\widehat{K^{\mathrm{un}}}}$ [@brinonconrad2009, Proposition 9.3.1, p. 146]. Trivial representations are crystalline, as $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ has invariants $K_0$ under $G_K$ [@brinonconrad2009, §9.1, p. 131]. So unramified representations are crystalline. $\mathbb{Q}_p(h)$ is crystalline [@brinonconrad2009, Example 9.1.12, p. 135]. Crystalline representations are closed under subquotients, tensor products and duals [@brinonconrad2009, Theorem 5.2.1, p. 68, with Proposition 9.1.6, p. 131], and they are de Rham [@brinonconrad2009, Proposition 9.1.9, p. 133]. They are semistable, since $D_{\mathrm{cris}}(V) = D_{\mathrm{st}}(V)^{N=0}$ [@brinonconrad2009, §9.3, p. 146] and $\dim_{K_0} D_{\mathrm{st}}(V) \le \dim V$ [@brinonconrad2009, Theorem 5.2.1 (1), p. 68]. So every piece of an ordinary single-weight filtration is crystalline and semistable.
- (f) *Unramified classes.* Let $M = X^{I_K}$, a representation of $G_K/I_K$, which is topologically generated by a Frobenius $\Phi$.
  - $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X)$ is the image of inflation from $H^1(G_K/I_K, M)$, and inflation is injective. If a cocycle $c$ is a coboundary on $I_K$, subtracting a coboundary makes it vanish on $I_K$. Then $c(gh) = c(g)$ and $c(hg) = h\,c(g)$ for $h \in I_K$, and since $hg = g\,(g^{-1}hg)$, $c(g) \in M$; so $c$ is inflated. If an inflated cocycle is the coboundary of $x \in X$, then $x$ is fixed by $I_K$, so the class is already $0$ in $H^1(G_K/I_K, M)$.
  - $\dim H^1(G_K/I_K, M) = \dim H^0(K, X)$. A continuous cocycle is determined by its value at $\Phi$, and coboundaries take the values $(\Phi - 1)M$, so $H^1$ embeds in $M/(\Phi-1)M$. Every class occurs. Write $M = M_1 \oplus M'$ with $\Phi - 1$ nilpotent on $M_1$ and invertible on $M'$; both summands are $\Phi$-stable and closed, hence $G_K/I_K$-stable. $M'/(\Phi-1)M' = 0$. For $m \in M_1$, the finite sum $c(\Phi^n) = \sum_{j \ge 0} \binom{n}{j+1} (\Phi-1)^j m$ satisfies the cocycle identity for $n \ge 0$, hence for all $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ as both sides are polynomial in the exponents. It is $p$-adically continuous in $n$, so it extends through $G_K/I_K \cong \widehat{\mathbb{Z}} \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ to a continuous cocycle. Hence $\dim H^1 = \dim M/(\Phi-1)M = \dim \ker(\Phi - 1) = \dim H^0(K, X)$.

**Proof of part 1.** (a) Let $\iota : M = X^{I_K} \to X$ be the inclusion. By (f), every class in $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X)$ is $\iota_*(c')$ for some $c' \in H^1(K, M)$ inflated from $G_K/I_K$. The extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ by $M$ with class $c'$ is a representation on which $I_K$ acts trivially, so it is crystalline by (e), as is $M$. By Bloch and Kato's criterion [@blochkato1990tamagawa, remark after (3.7.4), p. 354], $c' \in H^1_f(K, M)$. The map $H^1(K, -) \to H^1(K, B_{\mathrm{cris}} \otimes -)$ is natural, so $\iota_*$ maps $H^1_f(K, M)$ into $H^1_f(K, X)$. Hence $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X) \subseteq H^1_f(K, X)$. All weights of $X$ are $\le 0$, so $t_X = 0$ by fact (c) of the de Rham criterion, and $\dim H^1_f(K, X) = \dim t_X + \dim H^0(K, X) = \dim H^0(K, X)$ [@blochkato1990tamagawa, Corollary 3.8.4, p. 355]. By (f) this is also $\dim H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X)$, so the two subspaces are equal.

(b) $X = W^\vee(1)$ is de Rham by facts (a) and (b) of the de Rham criterion, with weights $1 - h \le 0$ for the weights $h$ of $W$, since $C \otimes W^\vee$ is the $C$-dual of $C \otimes W$. Bloch and Kato show that $H^1_f(K, W)$ and $H^1_f(K, X)$ are exact annihilators under this pairing [@blochkato1990tamagawa, Proposition 3.8, p. 354]. By (a), $H^1_f(K, X) = H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X)$. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.** A semistable representation is potentially semistable, hence de Rham by the [p-adic monodromy theorem](p-adic-monodromy-theorem.md). Conversely, let $V$ be de Rham with an ordinary single-weight filtration. By (e) the pieces $F_i/F_{i-1}$ are semistable. If $F_i$ is semistable, then $F_{i+1}$ is a de Rham extension of the semistable $F_{i+1}/F_i$ by $F_i$, de Rham as a subrepresentation of $V$, so it is semistable [@berger2002differential, Theorem 6.2, p. 62]. By induction $V = F_m$ is semistable. No ordering of the weights is needed. Berger's Corollary 6.3 is the case of decreasing weights [@berger2002differential, Corollary 6.3 and its proof, pp. 62–63]. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** By the [functorial invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md), $\alpha(I_L) = I_K$ and $\chi_K \circ \alpha = \chi_L$. So the same subspaces $F_i$ form an ordinary single-weight filtration of $V \circ \alpha$, with the same $h_i$. Applying everything to $\alpha^{-1}$, it suffices to prove that $V \circ \alpha$ is semistable, resp. crystalline, when $V$ is.

*Semistable.* If $V$ is semistable, it is de Rham by part 2. Then $V \circ \alpha$ is de Rham by part 3 of the de Rham criterion, and semistable by part 2.

*Crystalline: reordering.* Let $V$ be crystalline. Apply the following moves to its ordinary single-weight filtration while one applies. Each keeps the filtration ordinary.

- *Merge.* Suppose $h_i = h_{i+1} = h$. Let $A = F_i/F_{i-1}$, $B = F_{i+1}/F_i$, $Q = F_{i+1}/F_{i-1}$, and let $W = \mathrm{Hom}(B, A)$, $E_W$ and $e \in H^1(K, W)$ be as in fact (d) of the de Rham criterion. As $I_K$ acts trivially on $W$, $W$ is unramified, so it is crystalline by (e), with $0$ as its only weight. $E_W$ is crystalline as a subrepresentation of $B^\vee \otimes Q$, so $e \in H^1_f(K, W)$ by Bloch and Kato's criterion. By part 1(a), $e \in H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, W)$. So the extension $E_W$ splits on $I_K$, and $I_K$ acts trivially on it, as it does on $W$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p$. Then $I_K$ acts by $\chi^h$ on $E_W \otimes B$, and on its quotient $Q$ under evaluation. Deleting $F_i$ from the flag therefore leaves an ordinary single-weight filtration.
- *Swap.* Suppose $h_i < h_{i+1}$. $Q = F_{i+1}/F_{i-1}$ is crystalline, hence de Rham, so it splits by part 1 of the de Rham criterion over $F = K$. Exchange the two pieces as in the proof of part 2 there. The pieces are unchanged up to isomorphism, so the filtration stays ordinary.

As there, the process stops, with $h_1 > h_2 > \cdots > h_m$.

*Crystalline: induction.* We prove, by induction on $m$, that if $V$ is crystalline with an ordinary single-weight filtration of length $m$ with strictly decreasing weights, then $V \circ \alpha$ is crystalline, for every $L$ and $\alpha$. For $m = 1$, $I_L$ acts on $V \circ \alpha$ by $\chi_L^{h_1}$, so $V \circ \alpha$ is crystalline by (e). For $m \ge 2$, let $F = F_{m-1}$, $B = V/F$, $W = \mathrm{Hom}(B, F)$ and $X = W^\vee(1)$, and let $e \in H^1(K, W)$ be the class of $E_W$ as in fact (d).

- $F$ is crystalline, with an ordinary filtration of length $m - 1$ with decreasing weights, so $F \circ \alpha$ is crystalline by induction. $B \circ \alpha$ is crystalline by the case $m = 1$.
- The subspaces $\mathrm{Hom}(B, F_i)$ form an ordinary single-weight filtration of $W$ with weights $h_i - h_m \ge 1$. $W$ is crystalline, as $F$ and $B$ are, so it is de Rham with Hodge–Tate weights $h_i - h_m$, all $\ge 1$, by fact (c) of the de Rham criterion. The same holds for $W \circ \alpha = \mathrm{Hom}(B \circ \alpha, F \circ \alpha)$.
- By fact (d), $V$ is crystalline exactly when $e \in H^1_f(K, W)$, and $V \circ \alpha$ is crystalline exactly when $\alpha^* e \in H^1_f(L, W \circ \alpha)$, because $E_W \circ \alpha$ is the corresponding extension for $V \circ \alpha$. By part 1(b), these subspaces are the annihilators of $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X)$ and of $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(L, X \circ \alpha)$.
- Pullback $\alpha^*$ is an isomorphism on cohomology in each degree. It commutes with cup products and with the evaluation $W \otimes X \to \mathbb{Q}_p(1)$, and it is injective on $H^2(K, \mathbb{Q}_p(1)) \to H^2(L, \mathbb{Q}_p(1))$, where $\chi_K \circ \alpha = \chi_L$ identifies the coefficients. Restriction to inertia commutes with $\alpha^*$ because $\alpha(I_L) = I_K$, so $\alpha^*$ maps $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X)$ onto $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(L, X \circ \alpha)$.

So $e$ annihilates $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(K, X)$ exactly when $\alpha^* e$ annihilates $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(L, X \circ \alpha)$. As $V$ is crystalline, $V \circ \alpha$ is crystalline. With the reordering, this proves that $V \circ \alpha$ is crystalline whenever $V$ is. $\square$
