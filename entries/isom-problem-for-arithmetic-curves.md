**Setting.** For a number field $K$ and a set $S$ of primes of $K$ containing the set $S_\infty$ of archimedean primes, let $K_S/K$ be the maximal extension unramified outside $S$ and $G_{K,S} = \mathrm{Gal}(K_S/K)$. For finite $S$ this is the étale fundamental group of the arithmetic curve $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{K,S}$ [@ivanov2015, §1, p. 1]. Write $S_p$ for the set of primes of $K$ above a rational prime $p$.

**Question.** For $i = 1, 2$, let $K_i$ be a number field and $S_i$ a finite set of primes of $K_i$ that contains $S_\infty$ and $S_{p_i}$ for some rational prime $p_i$.

1. *Weak form.* If $G_{K_1,S_1} \cong G_{K_2,S_2}$, must $K_1 \cong K_2$?
2. *Functorial form.* Is every isomorphism $\sigma : G_{K_1,S_1} \to G_{K_2,S_2}$ induced by a unique field isomorphism $\tau : K_{2,S_2} \to K_{1,S_1}$ with $\tau(K_2) = K_1$, in the sense that $\sigma(g) = \tau^{-1} g \tau$ for all $g$?

Ivanov presents his conditional result below as an approach to "the (weaker form of) Isom-conjecture of Grothendieck" for arithmetic curves [@ivanov2015, §1, p. 2]. The sources give no single precise form, and the hypotheses above are this entry's choice. They are the setting of the partial results below, and they make $G_{K_i,S_i}$ infinite, since $K_{i,S_i}$ then contains the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_{p_i}$-extension of $K_i$. The functorial form is modelled on the functorial theorem for [large infinite sets of primes](restricted-ramification-neukirch-uchida.md).

**Some hypothesis on $S$ is needed.** This is checked here. With $S_i = S_\infty$, a case the question above excludes, the weak form fails, because $G_{\mathbb{Q},S_\infty}$ and $G_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1}),S_\infty}$ are both trivial. The proof uses three standard facts, which have not been checked in a source here: Minkowski's bound $\sqrt{|d_M|} \ge (\pi/4)^{r_2} m^m/m!$ for a number field $M$ of degree $m$ with $r_2$ complex places, the tower formula for discriminants, and the fact that the primes dividing $d_M$ ramify in $M$.

- Let $M \ne \mathbb{Q}$ be a number field of degree $m \ge 2$. As $r_2 \le m/2$ and $\pi/4 < 1$, we get $\sqrt{|d_M|} \ge T(m) = (\pi/4)^{m/2} m^m/m!$. Here $T(2) = \pi/2 > 1$, and $T(m+1)/T(m) = (\sqrt{\pi}/2)(1 + 1/m)^m \ge \sqrt{\pi} > 1$ because $(1 + 1/m)^m \ge 2$. So $|d_M| > 1$, some prime ramifies in $M$, and $M \not\subseteq \mathbb{Q}_{S_\infty}$. Hence $\mathbb{Q}_{S_\infty} = \mathbb{Q}$.
- Let $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})$ and let $M \subseteq K_{S_\infty}$ be a finite extension of $K$ of degree $d \ge 2$. Then $M/K$ is unramified at every prime, and $M$ is totally imaginary of degree $2d$, so $|d_M| = |d_K|^d = 4^d$. Minkowski's bound gives $2^d \ge (\pi/4)^d (2d)^{2d}/(2d)!$, that is, $(8/\pi)^d \ge R(d) = (2d)^{2d}/(2d)!$. But $R(2) = 32/3 > 6.5 > (8/\pi)^2$, and $R(d+1)/R(d) = (1 + 1/d)^{2d}(2d+2)/(2d+1) > 4 > 8/\pi$, so $R(d) > (8/\pi)^d$ for every $d \ge 2$. This contradiction shows $K_{S_\infty} = K$.

**Known.**

- *Large infinite sets of primes.* For sets of primes satisfying density conditions both forms are proved, and the weak form is proved under further assumptions for stable sets, which can have arbitrarily small positive density; see [restricted ramification](restricted-ramification-neukirch-uchida.md). For stable sets a direct application of the Chebotarev density theorem is no longer possible, and further arguments are needed [@ivanov2017, §1, p. 2].
- *Function fields.* For function fields of curves over finite fields and finite $S$, the analogous question was answered by Tamagawa [@ivanov2017, §1, p. 1]; see [Tamagawa's theorem](tamagawa-grothendieck-conjecture-for-affine-curves.md). Ivanov writes that the number field analogue of Tamagawa's proof seems to be out of reach for now [@ivanov2017, §1, p. 1], and that, in contrast with curves over finite fields, almost nothing is known about arithmetic curves [@ivanov2014, §1, p. 1].
- *The boundary.* Suppose that $S$ contains all primes over two rational primes, one of them $p$. Then $G_{K,S}$ together with the $p$-cyclotomic character on an open subgroup determines the decomposition groups at the finite primes of $S$. An isomorphism respecting the cyclotomic characters matches these decomposition groups, and preserves residue characteristics and degrees when they are full local groups. The cyclotomic character can be dropped under a finiteness hypothesis on Tate–Shafarevich groups when $p$ is odd, or for the primes not over $p$ under the Greenberg conjecture. Also, $G_{K,S}$ and $p$ determine $[K:\mathbb{Q}]$, $r_1(K)$ and $r_2(K)$. See [decomposition groups at the boundary](local-correspondence-at-the-boundary.md).
- *Points off the boundary, conditionally.* Assume the generalized Riemann hypothesis for all finite subextensions of $K_{i,S_i}/K_i$. Let $\sigma : G_{K_1,S_1} \to G_{K_2,S_2}$ be an isomorphism that induces a local correspondence at the boundary, where each $S_i$ contains all primes over at least two rational primes and $K_{i,S_i}$ realizes the maximal local extension at each prime of $S_i$. Assume that technical conditions, (C4) together with (C2) or (C3), hold for $K_i, S_i$, and that $h_{L_1}\mathrm{Reg}_{L_1} = h_{L_2}\mathrm{Reg}_{L_2}$ for corresponding finite subextensions $L_1, L_2$ that are large enough. Then $\sigma$ induces compatible bijections between the closed points of $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{L_1,S_1}$ and $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{L_2,S_2}$, characterized by $\sigma(D_{\mathfrak{p}}) = D_{\sigma_*(\mathfrak{p})}$ and preserving residue characteristics and absolute inertia degrees [@ivanov2015, Theorem 1.3]. If moreover $K_1/\mathbb{Q}$ is normal, then $K_1 \cong K_2$ [@ivanov2015, Corollary 1.4].
  - Condition (C3), for example, asks that for every finite subextension $L$ of $K_S/K$ and all primes $\mathfrak{p} \ne \mathfrak{q}$ of $L$, the decomposition group of $\mathfrak{q}$ in the maximal extension of $L$ unramified outside $S$ and completely split at $\mathfrak{p}$ is infinite [@ivanov2015, §4.1, p. 12]. Ivanov writes that these conditions are still not known to hold for arithmetic fundamental groups [@ivanov2015, §1, p. 2].
  - The method follows Tamagawa's, with the Tsfasman–Vlăduţ theorem, a generalization of the Brauer–Siegel theorem, in place of the Lefschetz trace formula [@ivanov2015, §1, p. 3]. Since $h_L \mathrm{Reg}_L$ is read off from the residue at $s = 1$ of the Dedekind zeta function, the result says roughly that $G_{K,S}$ with these residues determines $K$ when $K/\mathbb{Q}$ is normal [@ivanov2015, §1, p. 2]. Ivanov writes that it is completely unclear how to recover the regulators from $G_{K,S}$. The $p$-adic volume of the unit lattice can be recovered when $S_p \subseteq S$, but the method would fail with it, because the $p$-adic analogue of the Brauer–Siegel theorem fails [@ivanov2015, §1, p. 3].

**Status.** A literature search in November 2026 found no answer. The restricted-ramification results found all assume sets of primes of positive density, but the search was not exhaustive.
