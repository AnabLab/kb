**Setting.** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$, let $\ell$ be a prime, and let $g, r \ge 0$ with $2g - 2 + r > 0$. Let $\mathcal{M}_{g,r}$ be the moduli stack over $k$ of smooth proper curves of genus $g$ with $r$ ordered disjoint marked points, and let $\mathcal{M}_{g,[r]}$ be the moduli stack of [hyperbolic curves](hyperbolic-curve.md) of type $(g, r)$, whose $r$ cusps form a finite étale divisor and carry no ordering. The forgetful map $\mathcal{M}_{g,r} \to \mathcal{M}_{g,[r]}$ is finite étale and Galois with group $\mathfrak{S}_r$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definitions 1.1 and 1.2, p. 54].

Let $\Delta_{g,r}$ be the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of the geometric fundamental group of a fibre of the universal punctured curve. The universal curve gives the *pro-$\ell$ universal outer monodromy representation* $\rho_{g,[r]} : \pi_1(\mathcal{M}_{g,[r]}) \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_{g,r})$. Write $\rho_{g,r}$ for its restriction to $\pi_1(\mathcal{M}_{g,r})$, and $\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{g,[r]}$ for its restriction to $\pi_1(\mathcal{M}_{g,[r]} \otimes_k \bar k)$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 1.3 and Remark 1.3.1, pp. 55–56]. Since $\pi_1(\mathcal{M}_{g,r})$ maps onto $G_k$, the image of $\rho_{g,r}$ contains arithmetic as well as geometric monodromy.

A hyperbolic curve $X$ of type $(g, r)$ over $k$ has a classifying morphism $\mathrm{Spec}\, k \to \mathcal{M}_{g,[r]}$. Composing the section of $\pi_1(\mathcal{M}_{g,[r]}) \to G_k$ that it induces with $\rho_{g,[r]}$ gives the pro-$\ell$ outer Galois representation $\rho_X : G_k \to \mathrm{Out}(\Delta_{g,r})$ of $X$. Its image lies in $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{g,[r]})$, and is defined up to conjugation by $\mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{g,[r]})$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 1.3 (iii), p. 56].

**Definition** [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 2.2, p. 62]. Let $\Sigma$ be a nonempty set of primes. A hyperbolic curve $X$ of type $(g, r)$ over $k$ is

- *$\Sigma$-monodromically full* if $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_X) \supseteq \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{g,r})$ for every $\ell \in \Sigma$;
- *strictly $\Sigma$-monodromically full* if $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_X) = \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{g,[r]})$ for every $\ell \in \Sigma$, or equivalently $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_X) \supseteq \mathrm{Im}(\rho^{\mathrm{geom}}_{g,[r]})$;
- *quasi-$\Sigma$-monodromically full* if $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_X)$ is open in $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{g,[r]})$ for every $\ell \in \Sigma$.

For $\Sigma = \{\ell\}$ one says *$\ell$-monodromically full*, and so on. Hoshi introduced the term, and Matsumoto and Tamagawa had studied the notion before [@hoshi2011monodromic, Introduction, p. 49]. The same definitions apply to a closed point of a base scheme $S$ with respect to a family of hyperbolic curves over $S$, with the image of the monodromy of the family in place of the universal one [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 2.1, pp. 60–61].

**Elementary relations.**

- Strictly full implies full, and full implies quasi-full [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remark 2.2.1, p. 62].
- $X$ is *split*, meaning that its cusps are all $k$-rational, exactly when $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_X) \subseteq \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{g,r})$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 1.5 (i) and Remark 1.5.1 (i), p. 57]. So a split curve is $\Sigma$-monodromically full exactly when $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_X) = \mathrm{Im}(\rho_{g,r})$ for every $\ell \in \Sigma$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remark 2.2.7, p. 64].
- For $r \le 1$, full and strictly full agree. For $r \ge 2$, a strictly full curve is not split [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remarks 2.2.3 and 2.2.4, p. 63].
- Each of the three properties passes from $X$ to every hyperbolic partial compactification of $X$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remark 2.2.5, p. 63]. Quasi-fullness does not change under finite extensions of $k$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remark 2.2.6, pp. 63–64].

**The analogy with complex multiplication.** For an elliptic curve $E$ over a number field $k$, Serre proved that the following are equivalent: $E$ has no complex multiplication over $\bar k$; the $\ell$-adic representation $G_k \to \mathrm{Aut}(T_\ell E)$ has open image for every $\ell$; it is surjective for some $\ell$; it is surjective for all but finitely many $\ell$ [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Introduction, p. 232]. The image of the geometric fundamental group of the moduli stack of elliptic curves under the $\ell$-adic universal monodromy is $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell) \subseteq \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell) \cong \mathrm{Aut}(T_\ell E)$. From this point of view Hoshi regards (quasi-)monodromic fullness as the analogue for hyperbolic curves of having no complex multiplication [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Introduction, p. 232].

Hoshi supports the analogy as follows. Call a field *generalized sub-$\ell$-adic* if it embeds in a finitely generated extension of the fraction field of $W(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_\ell)$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, §0, p. 52]. Fields finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$ are examples, since they embed in finitely generated extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$. Let $X$ of type $(g, r)$ be quasi-$\Sigma$-monodromically full.

1. *No special symmetry.* Suppose that $k$ is generalized sub-$\ell$-adic for some $\ell \in \Sigma$. Then $\mathrm{Aut}_{\bar k}(X \otimes_k \bar k)$ is isomorphic to the automorphism group $G_{g,r}$ of a general curve of type $(g, r)$. This group is trivial if $2g - 2 + r \ge 3$, is $\mathbb{Z}/2$ for types $(1,1)$, $(1,2)$ and $(2,0)$, is $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$ for type $(0,4)$, and is $\mathfrak{S}_3$ for type $(0,3)$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definitions 3.1 and 3.3, Propositions 3.2 and 3.4, pp. 68–69]. The proof identifies $\mathrm{Aut}_{\bar k}(X \otimes_k \bar k)$ with the local centralizer of $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_X)$ in $\mathrm{Out}(\Delta_{g,r})$. When $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_X)$ is open in $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{g,[r]})$, this local centralizer is that of $\mathrm{Im}(\rho_{g,[r]})$, which is contained in the local centralizer of the image for a curve with automorphism group $G_{g,r}$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, proof of Proposition 3.4, pp. 69–70].
2. *Irreducible Jacobian.* Suppose that $g \ge 1$ and that $k$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then for every $\ell \in \Sigma$ and every finite extension $k'$ of $k$ with $X(k') \ne \emptyset$, the $\ell$-adic Tate module of the Jacobian of the compactification of $X$ is irreducible as a $G_{k'}$-module. In particular this Jacobian is simple over $\bar k$ [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 3.5, Remark 3.5.1 and Proposition 3.6, p. 70].
3. *No CM factor.* Under the same hypotheses, no simple abelian variety over $\bar k$ with complex multiplication has a nonzero map to that Jacobian [@hoshi2011monodromic, Definition 3.7 and Proposition 3.8, p. 71]. For type $(1,1)$ this says that the elliptic curve has no complex multiplication [@hoshi2011monodromic, Remark 3.7.1, p. 71].

Matsumoto and Tamagawa asked whether the analogue of Serre's equivalences holds. Hoshi showed that it does not; see [the Matsumoto–Tamagawa problem](matsumoto-tamagawa-problem-on-monodromic-fullness.md), which also gives explicit criteria in type $(0,4)$.

**Existence.** Matsumoto and Tamagawa proved that for each prime $\ell$ there are many $\ell$-monodromically full hyperbolic curves over number fields [@hoshi2013matsumototamagawa, Introduction, p. 232]. Hoshi proves the following version. Let $k$ be finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}$, let $S$ be a connected, regular and separated $k$-scheme of finite type, let $X$ be a hyperbolic curve over $S$, and let $\Sigma$ be a finite nonempty set of primes. Then the closed points of $S$ that are strictly $\Sigma$-monodromically full with respect to $X/S$, regarded as a subset of $S(\mathbb{C})$, are dense for the complex topology. If $S$ is rational, the $k$-rational points that are not form a thin set in the sense of Hilbert's irreducibility theorem [@hoshi2011monodromic, Theorem 2.3, p. 65]. The proof uses that $k$ is Hilbertian, and that the image of the pro-$\ell$ outer monodromy of $X/S$ is topologically finitely generated [@hoshi2011monodromic, Lemmas 2.4 and 2.5, pp. 65–67]. For the universal curve this gives a dense set of complex points of the coarse moduli space of $\mathcal{M}_{g,[r]}$ that come from $\Sigma$-monodromically full curves [@hoshi2011monodromic, Corollary 2.6, p. 67].

**Only pro-$\ell$.** The profinite analogue never holds over a number field. There, the image of the profinite outer Galois representation of any hyperbolic curve meets the image of the profinite geometric universal monodromy trivially. Hoshi reports this from Matsumoto–Tamagawa and Hoshi–Mochizuki [@hoshi2011monodromic, Introduction, pp. 50–51].

**Use.** Monodromic fullness is the hypothesis under which the kernel of the pro-$\ell$ outer Galois representation [determines a curve of genus $0$](pro-l-kernels-determine-monodromically-full-genus-zero-curves.md). The universal monodromy here is the one whose kernel is described by [Oda's prediction](oda-prediction-on-monodromy-fixed-fields.md).
