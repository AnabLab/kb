**Setting.** A *full formation* $\mathcal{C}$ is a family of finite groups closed under quotients, subgroups and extensions. Let $\Sigma(\mathcal{C})$ be the set of primes $p$ with $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} \in \mathcal{C}$. Then $\Sigma(\mathcal{C})$ contains every prime exactly when $\mathcal{C}$ contains all finite solvable groups [@hoshi2014conditional, §0, p. 7].

Let $k$ be a number field and $X$ a smooth projective geometrically connected curve over $k$, with function field $k(X)$. Write $\Delta^{\mathcal C}_{k(X)/k}$ for the maximal pro-$\mathcal{C}$ quotient of $G_{\bar k \cdot k(X)}$, and $\Pi^{\mathcal C}_{k(X)/k}$ for the quotient of $G_{k(X)}$ by the kernel of $G_{\bar k \cdot k(X)} \to \Delta^{\mathcal C}_{k(X)/k}$, so that there is an exact sequence $1 \to \Delta^{\mathcal C}_{k(X)/k} \to \Pi^{\mathcal C}_{k(X)/k} \to G_k \to 1$. A *pro-$\mathcal C$ birational Galois section* of $X/k$ is a section of $\Pi^{\mathcal C}_{k(X)/k} \to G_k$. For a [hyperbolic curve](hyperbolic-curve.md) $U$, *pro-$\mathcal C$ Galois sections* are defined in the same way from $\pi_1(U)$. A section is *geometric* if its image lies in a decomposition group at a closed point of the smooth compactification, which is then $k$-rational [@hoshi2014conditional, Definitions 1.1–1.3, pp. 8–9]. When $\mathcal C$ consists of all finite groups, these are the birational Galois sections of the [birational section conjecture](birational-section-conjecture.md), and the conjecture says that all of them are geometric.

*Local points.* Let $\mathfrak p$ be a finite prime of $k$ whose residue characteristic lies in $\Sigma(\mathcal C)$, and let $s$ be a pro-$\mathcal C$ birational Galois section. Then there is a unique $x_{\mathfrak p} \in X(k_{\mathfrak p})$ such that, for every open $U \subseteq X$, the restriction to $G_{\mathfrak p}$ of the pro-$\mathcal C$ section of $U$ induced by $s$ has image in a decomposition group at $x_{\mathfrak p}$. It is the *$k_{\mathfrak p}$-valued point associated to $s$* [@hoshi2014conditional, Definitions 2.5 and 2.6, p. 15; Proposition 2.9, p. 16]. Hoshi derives its existence from [Pop's metabelian theorems](metabelian-p-adic-birational-sections.md). When $\Sigma(\mathcal C)$ contains every prime, the points $x_{\mathfrak p}$ form a point $x_{\mathbb A}$ of $X(\mathbb{A}_k^f) = \prod_{\mathfrak p} X(k_{\mathfrak p})$, where $\mathbb A_k^f$ is the ring of finite adeles and the equality holds because $X$ is proper [@hoshi2014conditional, Remark 2.2.1, p. 13]. If $s$ is geometric at $x \in X(k)$, then $x_{\mathfrak p} = x$ for every $\mathfrak p$ [@hoshi2014conditional, Remark 2.6.1, p. 15]. A section that has local points in this sense at every finite prime is *locally geometric* [@hoshi2014conditional, Definition 2.5, p. 15].

*Ramification.* A pro-$\mathcal C$ Galois section $s$ of a hyperbolic curve $U$ over $k$ is *cuspidal* if its image lies in a decomposition group at a cusp. It is *unramified almost everywhere* if the composite of $s$ with the conjugation action $\Pi^{\mathcal C}_{U/k} \to \mathrm{Aut}(\Delta^{\mathcal C}_{U/k})$ is unramified at all but finitely many finite primes [@hoshi2014conditional, Definition 4.3, p. 26]. If $\Sigma$ is a finite set of primes, every geometric pro-$\Sigma$ section of a hyperbolic curve over a number field is cuspidal or unramified almost everywhere [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition 4.4, p. 26]. This rests on Hoshi's local analysis over a finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, recorded in more detail in [prime-to-p Galois sections of p-adic curves](prime-to-p-sections-of-p-adic-curves.md):

- If $p \in \Sigma$, no pro-$\Sigma$ section is even potentially unramified. Hoshi takes a characteristic open subgroup $H$ of the geometric pro-$\Sigma$ group $\Delta^\Sigma_{U/K}$ such that the finite étale cover $Y$ of $U$ corresponding to $H \cdot \mathrm{Im}(s)$ has a smooth compactification $Y^{\mathrm{cpt}}$ of genus at least $1$. Since $H$ is characteristic, it is enough to show that inertia has infinite image in $\mathrm{Aut}(H)$, so $U$ may be replaced by $Y$. The conjugation action on $\Delta^\Sigma_{Y/K}$ induces an action on the quotient $\Delta^\Sigma_{Y^{\mathrm{cpt}}/K}$, and inertia acts on $H^2(\Delta^\Sigma_{Y^{\mathrm{cpt}}/K}, \mathbb{Z}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p(1)$ through the $p$-adic cyclotomic character, whose image is infinite [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition A.2 (i) and its proof, pp. 33–34]. The compactification is needed. If $U$ is affine, then so is $Y$, and the geometric pro-$\Sigma$ group of an affine curve is free pro-$\Sigma$, so its $H^2$ with $\mathbb{Z}_p$ coefficients vanishes.
- If $p \notin \Sigma$ and $s$ is geometric at a point $x \in U(K)$, not a cusp, then $s$ is unramified exactly when the hyperbolic curve $U \setminus \{x\}$ has good reduction over the integers of $K$. The proof uses [Tamagawa's good reduction criterion](oda-good-reduction-criterion.md) [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition A.7, p. 38].
- If $p \notin \Sigma$ and $U$ is proper with good reduction, every pro-$\Sigma$ section of $U$ is unramified. Hoshi calls this a Galois-section version of the valuative criterion of properness [@hoshi2014conditional, Proposition A.8 and Remark A.8.1, p. 39].

If $\Sigma$ is infinite, infinitely many primes of $k$ have residue characteristic in $\Sigma$, and by the first item a pro-$\Sigma$ section is ramified at all of them. This is why the conditions below use pro-$\ell$ sections for a single prime $\ell$.

**Theorem 4.1 (any number field).** Let $\mathcal C$ contain all finite solvable groups, and let $s$ be a pro-$\mathcal C$ birational Galois section of $X/k$. Then $s$ is geometric if and only if there are a set $T$ of finite primes of Dirichlet density $0$ and a closed subscheme $Z \subseteq X$, finite over $k$, with $x_{\mathfrak p} \in Z$ for every finite $\mathfrak p \notin T$. The same holds for a locally geometric pro-$\mathcal C$ Galois section of a hyperbolic curve, with $Z$ in its smooth compactification [@hoshi2014conditional, Theorem 4.1, p. 23].

*Proof outline.* For a locally geometric section of a hyperbolic curve, geometricity may be checked after a finite extension of $k$, so $k$ may be taken totally imaginary. The finite étale covers through which $s$ factors inherit the condition on local points, so it suffices to show that $X^{\mathrm{cpt}}(k) \ne \emptyset$ for each such cover $X$ whose compactification $X^{\mathrm{cpt}}$ has genus at least $2$. Since $k$ has no real places, the local points of $s$ form part of an adelic point that survives finite abelian descent, and [Stoll's finite support theorem](finite-support-for-finite-abelian-descent.md) gives $Z(k) \ne \emptyset$. A birational section reduces to this case, because the section of $X$ it induces is locally geometric with the same local points [@hoshi2014conditional, proof of Theorem 4.1, pp. 23–24; Proposition 2.11, pp. 17–18].

**Corollary 4.2 (curves mapping to abelian varieties).** Let $\mathcal C$ contain all finite solvable groups. Suppose that there is a nonconstant morphism $f : X \to A$ to an abelian variety over $k$ whose Mordell–Weil and Tate–Shafarevich groups over $k$ are finite. Then every pro-$\mathcal C$ birational Galois section of $X/k$ is geometric, and, if $X$ is a hyperbolic curve, so is every locally geometric pro-$\mathcal C$ Galois section [@hoshi2014conditional, Corollary 4.2, p. 24]. Hoshi writes that the corollary was essentially proved by Stoll [@hoshi2014conditional, p. 24]. For all finite groups it is the consequence for these curves of the theorem of [Harari and Stix](birational-sections-and-the-brauer-manin-obstruction.md), but the proof does not use the Brauer group. The two finiteness conditions make the natural map from $A(k)$ to the completed Selmer group $\varprojlim_n \ker\big(H^1(k, A[n]) \to \prod_{\mathfrak p} H^1(k_{\mathfrak p}, A)\big)$ an isomorphism. The section of $\pi_1(A)$ induced by $s$ defines a class in this group, so it comes from a point $a \in A(k)$. Since the local Kummer maps are injective, $f(x_{\mathfrak p}) = a$ for every $\mathfrak p$, so every local point lies in the finite fibre $f^{-1}(a)$ and Theorem 4.1 applies [@hoshi2014conditional, proof of Corollary 4.2, pp. 24–25]. Hoshi notes that this yields curves all of whose prosolvable birational Galois sections are geometric [@hoshi2014conditional, Remark 4.2.1, p. 25].

**Theorem A (small number fields).** Let $k$ be $\mathbb{Q}$ or an imaginary quadratic field, and let $s$ be a birational Galois section of $X/k$, with $\mathcal C$ all finite groups. The following are equivalent [@hoshi2014conditional, Theorem A, p. 4]:

1. $s$ is geometric.
2. (i) There is a finite morphism $\phi : X \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$ with $\phi(x_{\mathfrak p}) \in \mathbb{P}^1(k_{\mathfrak p}) \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ for every finite $\mathfrak p$. (ii) For every open $U \subseteq X$ that is a hyperbolic curve, there is a prime $\ell_U$ such that the pro-$\ell_U$ Galois section of $U/k$ induced by $s$ is cuspidal or unramified almost everywhere.
3. There is a finite morphism $\phi : X \to \mathbb{P}^1_k$ such that $\phi \circ x_{\mathbb A}$ is an $\mathbb{A}_k^f$-valued point of $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$. Unwinding the definition, this says that $\phi(x_{\mathfrak p}) \in k_{\mathfrak p} \setminus \{0, 1\}$ for every $\mathfrak p$, and that $\phi(x_{\mathfrak p})$ and $1 - \phi(x_{\mathfrak p})$ are units of $\mathfrak{o}_{\mathfrak p}$ for all but finitely many $\mathfrak p$.
4. There are a finite set $T$ of finite primes and a closed subscheme $Z \subseteq X$, finite over $k$, with $x_{\mathfrak p} \in Z$ for every $\mathfrak p \notin T$.

This is the case of all finite groups of Theorem 4.5, which applies to any full formation $\mathcal C$ such that the section is locally geometric, only finitely many primes lie outside $\Sigma(\mathcal C)$, and $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathfrak o_k)$ is killed by an integer whose prime factors lie in $\Sigma(\mathcal C)$. Condition (4) then only concerns primes whose residue characteristic lies in $\Sigma(\mathcal C)$. All three hypotheses hold when $\mathcal C$ contains all finite solvable groups [@hoshi2014conditional, Theorem 4.5 and the remark on its hypotheses, pp. 26–27]. Theorem A assumes nothing about Tate–Shafarevich groups. Hoshi reads $(1) \Leftrightarrow (3)$ as an analogue of the Harari–Stix theorem for the tripod $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ in place of an abelian variety. Finiteness of $\mathfrak o_k^*$ plays the role of finiteness of the Mordell–Weil group, the condition on $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathfrak o_k)$ that of the Tate–Shafarevich group, and the integrality in (3) is automatic for abelian varieties, which are proper [@hoshi2014conditional, Remark 4.5.1 (i)–(ii), pp. 30–31].

*Proof outline.* $(1) \Rightarrow (2)$ follows from Proposition 4.4, and $(2) \Rightarrow (3)$ from the local analysis above: the pro-$\ell_U$ section of $U = \phi^{-1}(\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0,1,\infty\})$ is not cuspidal, its image on the tripod is unramified almost everywhere, and Proposition A.7 turns this into integrality of $\phi(x_{\mathfrak p})$ at almost every $\mathfrak p$ [@hoshi2014conditional, proof of Theorem 4.5, pp. 27–28]. The step $(3) \Rightarrow (4)$ uses the following fact about $\mathbb{G}_m$. For $k$ as above, a section of $\pi_1(\mathbb{G}_m) \to G_k$ whose local sections at the finite primes are those of a finite idele $(a_{\mathfrak p})$ comes from a unique $c \in k^*$, and then $a_{\mathfrak p} = c$ for every $\mathfrak p$ [@hoshi2014conditional, Lemma 3.5, p. 22]. The proof uses that $\mathfrak o_k^*$ is finite [@hoshi2014conditional, proof of Lemma 3.4, p. 22]. Applied to the section induced by $s$ on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, \infty\}$ through $\phi$, it shows that $\phi(x_{\mathfrak p})$ is one element $c$ of $k$ for every $\mathfrak p$ [@hoshi2014conditional, p. 4]. So (4) holds with $Z = \phi^{-1}(c)$. For $(4) \Rightarrow (1)$, embed $X$ in $\mathbb{P}^N_k$ and choose coordinates over $k$ such that every $x_{\mathfrak p}$ lies in the torus $\mathbb{G}_m^N$. Condition (4) makes each coordinate of $x_{\mathbb A}$ a finite idele, so the same fact puts every coordinate of $x_{\mathfrak p}$ in $k$, and $X(k) \ne \emptyset$. Applying this to every finite cover through which $s$ factors shows that $s$ is geometric [@hoshi2014conditional, pp. 4–5, and proof of Theorem 4.5, pp. 29–30]. For general $\mathcal C$ the local coordinates agree with elements of $k^*$ only up to roots of unity. In $(3) \Rightarrow (4)$ Hoshi controls these with the theorem of Evertse, Schlickewei and Schmidt on linear equations in multiplicative groups, and in $(4) \Rightarrow (1)$ he obtains points over a field generated by roots of unity, which suffice [@hoshi2014conditional, proof of Theorem 4.5, pp. 29–30].

**Theorem B: reduction to the projective line.** Let $k$ be $\mathbb{Q}$ or an imaginary quadratic field. For a birational Galois section $s$ of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$, write $s^{\mathbb P}$ for the Galois section of $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ that it induces. The following are equivalent [@hoshi2014conditional, Theorem B, pp. 5–6, and Corollary 4.6, pp. 31–32]:

1. Every birational Galois section of every smooth projective geometrically connected curve over $k$ is geometric.
2. Every birational Galois section of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ is geometric.
3. Every birational Galois section $s$ of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ satisfies: (i) there are three distinct $a, b, c \in \mathbb{P}^1(k)$ with $x_{\mathfrak p} \notin \{a, b, c\}$ for every finite $\mathfrak p$; and (ii) for some prime $\ell$, the pro-$\ell$ Galois section of $\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}$ induced by $s$ is cuspidal or unramified almost everywhere.
4. Every birational Galois section $s$ of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ satisfies (i), and (ii) either $s^{\mathbb P}$ is cuspidal, or for some prime $\ell$ the composite of $s^{\mathbb P}$ with the $\ell$-adic representation $\pi_1(\mathbb{P}^1_k \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}) \to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ of the Legendre family $y^2 = x(x-1)(x-u)$ is unramified at all but finitely many primes of $k$.

Hoshi writes that it was not clear to him whether these conditions always hold [@hoshi2014conditional, p. 6].

*Proof outline.* $(1) \Leftrightarrow (2)$ holds because every smooth projective curve over $k$ is the normalization of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ in the finite extension of $k(\mathbb{P}^1)$ corresponding to an open subgroup of $G_{k(\mathbb{P}^1)}$. Geometric sections satisfy (3) and (4) by Remark 2.6.1 and Proposition 4.4. Conversely, given $s$, Hoshi applies an automorphism of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ carrying $a, b, c$ to $0, 1, \infty$. Since (3) and (4) are assumed for every section, they also hold for the transformed one, whose local points now avoid $\{0, 1, \infty\}$. Then $s^{\mathbb P}$ is not cuspidal, and condition (ii), through Proposition A.7 or through the criterion of Serre and Tate for good reduction of abelian varieties, gives condition (3) of Theorem A for the identity map of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ [@hoshi2014conditional, proof of Corollary 4.6, pp. 32–33].

*The Legendre step, checked here.* Let $\mathfrak p$ be a prime not dividing $2\ell$, and $\lambda \in k_{\mathfrak p} \setminus \{0, 1\}$. The curve $E_\lambda : y^2 = x(x-1)(x-\lambda)$ has discriminant $16\lambda^2(\lambda-1)^2$ and $j$-invariant $256(\lambda^2 - \lambda + 1)^3/(\lambda^2(\lambda - 1)^2)$. If $\lambda$ and $1 - \lambda$ are units, the discriminant is a unit and $E_\lambda$ has good reduction. Otherwise $v(\lambda) = -m < 0$, $v(\lambda) = m > 0$ or $v(\lambda - 1) = m > 0$, and in each case $v(j) = -2m < 0$. Then $E_\lambda$ has potentially multiplicative reduction, so it does not have good reduction over any finite extension of $k_{\mathfrak p}$. By the Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion, $T_\ell E_\lambda$ is unramified at $\mathfrak p$ exactly when $\lambda$ and $1 - \lambda$ are units. If $x_{\mathfrak p} \notin \{0, 1, \infty\}$, then, restricted to $G_{\mathfrak p}$, the composite in (4)(ii) is the representation of $G_{\mathfrak p}$ on $T_\ell E_{x_{\mathfrak p}}$, because $s^{\mathbb P}$ restricted to $G_{\mathfrak p}$ is the section of the point $x_{\mathfrak p}$. So, once the local points avoid $\{0, 1, \infty\}$, condition (4)(ii) says exactly that $x_{\mathfrak p}$ and $1 - x_{\mathfrak p}$ are units for all but finitely many $\mathfrak p$. This is condition (3) of Theorem A for the identity map.

*Relation to Stix's Theorem B, checked here.* After moving $a, b, c$ to $0, 1, \infty$, the section $s^{\mathbb P}$ is birationally liftable and not cuspidal, since every local point of a cuspidal section is its cusp. By [Stix's Theorem B](birational-sections-with-local-conditions.md), $x_{\mathfrak p}$ and $1 - x_{\mathfrak p}$ are then units for a set of primes of Dirichlet density $1$. Condition (3) of Theorem A asks for this at all but finitely many primes. So, over these fields, the birational section conjecture is equivalent to the following: every birational Galois section of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ satisfies (i), and for some such $a, b, c$ the set of primes where this integrality fails is finite. The forward direction holds because a geometric section has a constant local point in $k$. The converse is $(3) \Rightarrow (1)$ of Theorem A for the identity map, followed by $(2) \Rightarrow (1)$ of Theorem B. Stix's theorem already shows that the exceptional set has density $0$.

**Relation to Stix's 2012 preprint.** Hoshi writes that the referee pointed out Stix's later preprint, whose results are similar to some of his and stronger. He pairs $(1) \Leftrightarrow (2)$ of his Theorem A with Stix's Theorem A, $(1) \Leftrightarrow (4)$ of his Theorem B with Stix's Theorem C, Lemma 3.5 with Stix's Proposition 4, Theorem 4.1 with Stix's Corollary 9, and Corollary 4.2 with Stix's Corollary 11 [@hoshi2014conditional, p. 6]. Stix's Theorem C characterized, over $\mathbb{Q}$, the birationally liftable sections that are cuspidal or come from rational points by the behaviour of the $2$-dimensional $\ell$-adic representations attached to all families of elliptic curves over open subsets of $X$ [@stix2012birational, Theorem C, p. 2]. The journal version of Stix's paper deleted this theorem and its section because of a gap in the proof, according to the comments on that version in the arXiv listing [@stix2012birational, arXiv comments on v3]. Hoshi's own proof of $(1) \Leftrightarrow (4)$ in Theorem B does not use Stix's paper, which he cites only in this comparison: it rests on Theorem 4.5, Proposition A.7 and the criterion of Serre and Tate [@hoshi2014conditional, proof of Corollary 4.6, pp. 32–33]. The surviving results of Stix are in [birationally liftable sections with local conditions](birational-sections-with-local-conditions.md).
