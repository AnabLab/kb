**Setting.** Fix a prime $p$ and let
$$G=\langle x,y,z\mid[x,y]=1\rangle_{\text{pro-}p}=\mathbb Z_p^2*\mathbb Z_p,$$
with $p$-Zassenhaus filtration $G_{(n)}$ and the kernel $n$-unipotent property as in [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md). As in [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md), $G$ *detects degree $m$* over $\mathbb F_p$ if every $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$ has $\rho(g)\ne1$ for some continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$. By its Lemma 1, if $G$ detects every degree $m\le n-1$, it has the kernel $n$-unipotent property. Put $N(p)=(p+1)(p^2-p+3)$. The counterexample shows that $G$ fails the property for every $n\ge n_0$, where $n_0=N(p)+3$ if $p=2$ and $n_0=N(p)+4$ if $p$ is odd, and [polynomial factor algebras](polynomial-factors-double-the-unipotent-detection-range.md) give the property for $n\le2p+2$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here. For every prime $p$ and every $m\le2p+3$, $G$ detects degree $m$ over $\mathbb F_p$, by representations of the form $\rho_{S,v}$ below. So $G$ has the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_p$, and hence over every finite field of characteristic $p$, for every $n\le2p+4$: for $n\le8$ if $p=2$, and for $n\le10$ if $p=3$.

**Corollary.** Let $K$ be the field of Corollary 1 of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md), which contains a primitive $p$th root of unity and has $G_K(p)\cong G$. Then $G_K(p)$ satisfies the equality of the [kernel-unipotent conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md) for every $n\le2p+4$ and fails it for every $n\ge n_0$. For $p=2$ this leaves $9\le n\le17$.

**Notation.**

*Words.* Let $T=\mathbb F_p\langle X,Y,Z\rangle$ and $\bar R=T/(XY-YX)$, graded by degree and by the number of letters $Z$. Write $\bar R_m$ for degree $m$, $\bar R^{(k)}$ for $k$ letters $Z$, and $P^{(k)}$ for the component of $P\in\bar R$ in $\bar R^{(k)}$. Let $a_l=(X_l,Y_l)$, $l\ge0$, be pairs of commuting variables. For a word $W=W_0ZW_1Z\cdots ZW_k$ with $W_0,\ldots,W_k$ words in $X,Y$, put
$$\kappa(W)=\prod_{l=0}^kX_l^{\#_X(W_l)}Y_l^{\#_Y(W_l)}\in\mathbb F_p[a_0,\ldots,a_k],$$
and extend linearly to the span of the words with $k$ letters $Z$. For $k=2$ this is the map $\kappa$ of Step 4 of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md). It kills every $U(XY-YX)V$, so it is defined on $\bar R^{(k)}$. Call a word *normal* if in each $W_l$ every $X$ precedes every $Y$. Every word equals a normal word in $\bar R$, and $\kappa$ sends distinct normal words with $k$ letters $Z$ to distinct monomials, so $\kappa$ is injective on $\bar R^{(k)}$. For $P\in\bar R^{(i)}$ and $Q\in\bar R^{(j)}$,
$$\kappa(PQ)=\kappa(P)(a_0,\ldots,a_i)\,\kappa(Q)(a_i,\ldots,a_{i+j}),\tag{K}$$
because the last segment of a word of $P$ and the first segment of a word of $Q$ together form segment $i$ of their product. For $P\in\bar R_m^{(k)}$, $\kappa(P)$ is homogeneous of degree $m-k$. Its component $\kappa_\delta(P)$ of multidegree $\delta=(\delta_0,\ldots,\delta_k)$ in the pairs $(a_0,\ldots,a_k)$ comes from the words whose letters $Z$ sit at the positions $\sigma_l=l+\delta_0+\cdots+\delta_{l-1}$, $1\le l\le k$. This is a bijection between the multidegrees with $\sum_l\delta_l=m-k$ and the $k$-element subsets $S$ of $\{1,\ldots,m\}$; write $\delta(S)$ for the multidegree of $S$.

*Differences.* Let $\mathcal T_k\subseteq\mathbb F_p[a_0,\ldots,a_k]$ be the subring generated by the coordinates of $d_l=a_{l-1}-a_l$ for $1\le l\le k$. Every $f\in\mathcal T_k$ satisfies $f(a_0+t,\ldots,a_k+t)=f(a_0,\ldots,a_k)$ for a further pair of variables $t$.

*The space $\mathcal W_m$.* Let $\mathcal W_m\subseteq\bar R_m$ be the subspace of those $P$ with

- (W0) $P^{(0)}\in\mathbb F_pX^m+\mathbb F_pY^m$ if $m$ is a power of $p$, including $m=1$, and $P^{(0)}=0$ otherwise;
- (W1) $\kappa(P^{(k)})\in\mathcal T_k$ for every $k\ge1$.

*Group algebra.* $G$ is the right-angled Artin pro-$p$ group of the graph with vertices $x,y,z$ and the single edge $\{x,y\}$. Let $A(G)$, $\mathrm{in}_k$, the corner functionals $\kappa_\rho$ and the algebra map $\mu:T\to A(G)$ with $\mu(X),\mu(Y),\mu(Z)=\mathrm{in}_1(x),\mathrm{in}_1(y),\mathrm{in}_1(z)$ be as in the Setting of [induced subgraphs decide the property](small-induced-subgraphs-decide-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md). By Lemma 4(b) there, $\mu(X)$ and $\mu(Y)$ commute, so $\mu$ induces $\bar\mu:\bar R\to A(G)$. By formula (1) of [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md), $\rho(h)=1+\kappa_\rho(\mathrm{in}_m(h))E_{1,m+1}$ for $h\in G_{(m)}$ and continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$.

*Representations.* Let $m\ge1$ and $S=\{\sigma_1<\cdots<\sigma_k\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$, with $0\le k\le m$. Put $\sigma_0=0$, $\sigma_{k+1}=m+1$ and $J_l=\{j:\sigma_l<j<\sigma_{l+1}\}$ for $0\le l\le k$, and let $v_0,\ldots,v_k\in\mathbb F_p^2$, with $v_l=(v_l',v_l'')$. In $(m+1)\times(m+1)$ matrices put $S_l=\sum_{j\in J_l}E_{j,j+1}$, $A=\sum_lv_l'S_l$, $B=\sum_lv_l''S_l$ and $C=\sum_{j\in S}E_{j,j+1}$. Consecutive integers never lie in different sets $J_l$, since those are separated by elements of $S$, so $S_lS_{l'}=0$ for $l\ne l'$ and $AB=\sum_lv_l'v_l''S_l^2=BA$. By Step 2 of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md), sending $x,y,z$ to $1+A,1+B,1+C$ defines a continuous $\rho_{S,v}:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$. For $k=2$ these are the decoupled representations there.

*(E) Evaluation.* For $P\in\bar R_m$ and $\rho=\rho_{S,v}$ with $|S|=k$,
$$\kappa_\rho(\bar\mu(P))=\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(k)})(v_0,\ldots,v_k).$$
Indeed, lift $P$ to $\sum_Wc_WW$ in $T_m$. Then $\bar\mu(P)$ is the class of $\sum_Wc_W(w_1-1)\cdots(w_m-1)$, where $w_j\in\{x,y,z\}$ is the generator named by the $j$th letter of $W$, and the linear extension of $\rho$ maps this to $\sum_Wc_WW(A,B,C)$. By Step 3 of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md), the $(1,m+1)$ entry of $W(A,B,C)$ is the product over $j$ of the $(j,j+1)$ entries of its $j$th factor. At $j\in S$ only $C$ has a nonzero such entry, namely $1$, and at $j\in J_l$ only $A$ and $B$ do, namely $v_l'$ and $v_l''$. So the product is $0$ unless the letters $Z$ of $W$ sit exactly at $S$, and then it is $\kappa(W)(v_0,\ldots,v_k)$. Those words make up $\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(k)})$.

**Lemma 1 (closure).** (a) $X,Y,Z\in\mathcal W_1$. (b) If $P\in\mathcal W_i$ and $Q\in\mathcal W_j$, then $[P,Q]=PQ-QP\in\mathcal W_{i+j}$. (c) If $P\in\mathcal W_i$, then $P^p\in\mathcal W_{pi}$.

*Proof.* (a) holds because $1=p^0$ and $\kappa(Z)=1$. Let $\mathcal A$ be the subspace of $P\in\bar R$ with $P^{(0)}=0$ and $\kappa(P^{(k)})\in\mathcal T_k$ for all $k\ge1$. By (K), $\mathcal A$ is closed under multiplication, since a polynomial in the differences of $a_0,\ldots,a_i$ times a polynomial in the differences of $a_i,\ldots,a_{i+j}$ is a polynomial in the differences of $a_0,\ldots,a_{i+j}$. Let $e\ge0$ and $Q\in\mathcal A\cap\bar R^{(k)}$. By (K), $\kappa(X^{p^e}Q-QX^{p^e})=(X_0^{p^e}-X_k^{p^e})\kappa(Q)=(X_0-X_k)^{p^e}\kappa(Q)$, and $X_0-X_k=\sum_{l=1}^k(X_{l-1}-X_l)$, so $[X^{p^e},Q]\in\mathcal A$, and likewise $[Y^{p^e},Q]\in\mathcal A$. Hence $\mathrm{ad}(P_0)$ preserves $\mathcal A$ for every $P_0\in\mathbb F_pX^{p^e}+\mathbb F_pY^{p^e}$, and so does $\mathrm{ad}(P_+)$ for $P_+\in\mathcal A$.

(b) Write $P=P_0+P_+$ with $P_0=P^{(0)}$, so that $P_+\in\mathcal A$, and $Q=Q_0+Q_+$ likewise. Then $[P_0,Q_0]=0$, because $\bar R^{(0)}=\mathbb F_p[X,Y]$ is commutative, and $[P_0,Q_+]$, $[P_+,Q_0]$ and $[P_+,Q_+]$ lie in $\mathcal A$. So $[P,Q]\in\mathcal A\cap\bar R_{i+j}\subseteq\mathcal W_{i+j}$.

(c) With $P=P_0+P_+$ as in (b): if $P_0=cX^i+c'Y^i$, then $P_0^p=cX^{pi}+c'Y^{pi}$, as $X,Y$ commute and $c^p=c$, while $P_0=0$ if $i$ is not a power of $p$. Also $P_+^p\in\mathcal A$. The other terms of $(P_0+P_+)^p$ are the products of $b$ factors $P_0$ and $p-b$ factors $P_+$ in some order, with $0<b<p$. Apply Lemma 1 of [induced subgraphs decide the property](small-induced-subgraphs-decide-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md) with $z_1=\cdots=z_b=P_0$ and $z_{b+1}=\cdots=z_p=P_+$. Its left side is $b!(p-b)!$ times the sum of these products for this $b$, and its right side is a sum of terms $\mathrm{ad}(z_{\tau(1)})\cdots\mathrm{ad}(z_{\tau(p-1)})(P_+)$, which lie in $\mathcal A$. As $b!(p-b)!$ is prime to $p$, the sum lies in $\mathcal A$. So $P^p\in\mathcal W_{pi}$. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (initial forms).** For $k\ge1$ and $h\in G_{(k)}$, $\mathrm{in}_k(h)\in\bar\mu(\mathcal W_k)$.

*Proof.* The proof of Lemma 2(b) of [induced subgraphs decide the property](small-induced-subgraphs-decide-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md) applies with $\bar\mu$ and $\mathcal W_k$ in place of $\mu$ and $\mathcal P_k$, and Lemma 1 here in place of Lemma 2(a) there. In outline: $\mathrm{in}_1(G)$ is spanned by $\bar\mu(X),\bar\mu(Y),\bar\mu(Z)$. For $k\ge2$, $G_{(k)}$ is generated modulo $G_{(k+1)}$ by the commutators $[h_1,h_2]$ with $h_1\in G_{(i)}$, $h_2\in G_{(j)}$ and $i+j=k$, and the $p$th powers of elements $h_1\in G_{(\lceil k/p\rceil)}$, while $\mathrm{in}_k$ is a homomorphism that kills $G_{(k+1)}$. Moreover $\mathrm{in}_k([h_1,h_2])=[\mathrm{in}_i(h_1),\mathrm{in}_j(h_2)]$, and $\mathrm{in}_k(h_1^p)$ is $\mathrm{in}_{k/p}(h_1)^p$ if $p$ divides $k$ and $0$ otherwise. Induction on $k$ and Lemma 1 give the claim. $\square$

**Lemma 3 (forms vanishing at the rational points).** For a pair $a=(X,Y)$ put $\Lambda(a)=X^pY-XY^p$. Let $h\in\mathbb F_p[a_0,\ldots,a_k]$ be multihomogeneous of multidegree $\delta$, with $h(v_0,\ldots,v_k)=0$ for all $v_0,\ldots,v_k\in\mathbb F_p^2$. If $\delta_l\le p$ for every $l$, then $h=0$. If $\delta_l\ge p+1$ for exactly one $l$, then $\Lambda(a_l)$ divides $h$.

*Proof.* First let $F$ be a binary form of degree $d$ vanishing on $\mathbb F_p^2$. For nonzero $r=(r',r'')\in\mathbb F_p^2$ and any $s$ with $r,s$ a basis, write $F(\alpha r+\beta s)=\tilde F(\alpha,\beta)$. The coefficient of $\alpha^d$ is $F(r)=0$, so $\beta$ divides $\tilde F$. Since $r'Y-r''X$ is a nonzero multiple of $\beta$, it divides $F$. These linear forms, for representatives $r$ of the $p+1$ lines of $\mathbb F_p^2$, are pairwise non-proportional, so their product divides $F$. With the representatives $(1,t)$, $t\in\mathbb F_p$, and $(0,1)$, the product is $-X\prod_t(Y-tX)=-X(Y^p-X^{p-1}Y)=\Lambda(a)$. So the space $\mathcal K_d$ of binary forms of degree $d$ vanishing on $\mathbb F_p^2$ is $0$ for $d\le p$ and $\Lambda\cdot\mathcal F_{d-p-1}$ for $d\ge p+1$, where $\mathcal F_e$ is the space of binary forms of degree $e$.

In general, choose complements $\mathcal F_{\delta_l}=\mathcal K_{\delta_l}\oplus\mathcal C_l$. Evaluation on $\mathbb F_p^2$ is injective on each $\mathcal C_l$, so evaluation on $(\mathbb F_p^2)^{k+1}$ is injective on $\mathcal C_0\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal C_k$, as a tensor product of injective linear maps into the spaces of functions on the factors. The multihomogeneous forms of multidegree $\delta$ are $\bigotimes_l\mathcal F_{\delta_l}=\bigotimes_l\mathcal C_l\oplus\sum_l\mathcal K_{\delta_l}\otimes\bigotimes_{l'\ne l}\mathcal F_{\delta_{l'}}$, and the second summand vanishes on every point. Hence $h\in\sum_l\mathcal K_{\delta_l}\otimes\bigotimes_{l'\ne l}\mathcal F_{\delta_{l'}}$, which gives both claims. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (detection in each number of letters $Z$).** Let $m\le2p+3$, $P\in\mathcal W_m$ and $k\ge0$ with $P^{(k)}\ne0$. Then $\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(k)})(v)\ne0$ for some $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ with $|S|=k$ and some $v\in(\mathbb F_p^2)^{k+1}$.

*Proof.* Put $f=\kappa(P^{(k)})$, which is nonzero by injectivity and homogeneous of degree $D=m-k$, and write $f_\delta$ for its multihomogeneous components. Suppose that every $f_\delta$ vanishes on $(\mathbb F_p^2)^{k+1}$; we show $f=0$.

*$k=0$.* By (W0), $f=cX_0^m+c'Y_0^m$, and $f(1,0)=c$, $f(0,1)=c'$, so $f=0$.

*$k\ge1$.* By (W1), $f=\Phi(d_1,\ldots,d_k)$ for a polynomial $\Phi$ in $k$ pairs of variables. For $v\in(\mathbb F_p^2)^{k+1}$ and indeterminates $s_0,\ldots,s_k$, $f(s_0v_0,\ldots,s_kv_k)=\sum_\delta f_\delta(v)\,s_0^{\delta_0}\cdots s_k^{\delta_k}=0$, that is,
$$\Phi(s_0v_0-s_1v_1,\ \ldots,\ s_{k-1}v_{k-1}-s_kv_k)=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p[s_0,\ldots,s_k].\tag{Z}$$

*$k=1$.* Take $v_0=(1,0)$ and $v_1=(0,1)$: then $\Phi(s_0,-s_1)=0$, so $\Phi=0$.

*$k=2$.* For a nonzero $r\in\mathbb F_p^2$ write $\theta_r(d)=r'd''-r''d'$ for $d=(d',d'')$. Fix $r$ and some $\bar r\notin\mathbb F_pr$, and use the coordinates $d_i=y_ir+y_i'\bar r$. For $v=(r,r,\bar r)$, (Z) says that $\Phi$ vanishes at $d_1=(s_0-s_1)r$, $d_2=s_1r-s_2\bar r$, that is, at $y_1'=0$ with $y_1=s_0-s_1$, $y_2=s_1$ and $y_2'=-s_2$ independent. So $y_1'$ divides $\Phi$, and so does $\theta_r(d_1)=y_1'\theta_r(\bar r)$, as $\theta_r(\bar r)\ne0$. Likewise $v=(\bar r,r,r)$ gives $d_1=s_0\bar r-s_1r$ and $d_2=(s_1-s_2)r$, so $\theta_r(d_2)$ divides $\Phi$. The $2(p+1)$ forms $\theta_r(d_1)$ and $\theta_r(d_2)$, for $r$ running over representatives of the lines, are pairwise non-proportional, hence pairwise non-associate primes of $\mathbb F_p[d_1,d_2]$. Their product has degree $2p+2>m-2=\deg\Phi$, so $\Phi=0$.

*$k\ge3$.* Now $D=m-k\le2p$, so in a multidegree $\delta$ of $f$ at most one $\delta_l$ is at least $p+1$. By Lemma 3, $f_\delta=0$ if every $\delta_l\le p$, and $\Lambda(a_l)$ divides $f_\delta$ if $\delta_l\ge p+1$. So
$$f=\sum_{l=0}^k\Lambda(a_l)\,g_l,$$
with each $g_l$ homogeneous of degree $D-p-1\le p-1$, and $g_l=0$ if $D\le p$. Adding a variable $T$ to every $X_l$ fixes $f$, as $f\in\mathcal T_k$; comparing coefficients of $T$ gives $Ef=0$, where $E=\sum_l\partial/\partial X_l$ is a derivation. As $E(\Lambda(a_l))=pX_l^{p-1}Y_l-Y_l^p=-Y_l^p$,
$$0=Ef=\sum_l\tau_l,\qquad\tau_l=-Y_l^pg_l+\Lambda(a_l)\,E(g_l).$$
Every monomial of $\tau_l$ has degree at least $p$ in the pair $a_l$, and $\tau_l$ is homogeneous of degree $D-1\le2p-1$, so no monomial occurring in $\tau_l$ occurs in any $\tau_{l'}$ with $l'\ne l$. Hence every $\tau_l=0$. Suppose $g_l\ne0$, and write $g_l=X_l^jh$ with $h$ not divisible by $X_l$. Then $E(g_l)=jX_l^{j-1}h+X_l^jE(h)$, the first term being absent if $j=0$, and $\Lambda(a_l)=X_l(X_l^{p-1}Y_l-Y_l^p)$. Dividing $\tau_l=0$ by $X_l^j$ gives
$$Y_l^ph=(X_l^{p-1}Y_l-Y_l^p)\bigl(jh+X_lE(h)\bigr).$$
Setting $X_l=0$ gives $(1+j)\,Y_l^p\,h|_{X_l=0}=0$ with $h|_{X_l=0}\ne0$, so $j\equiv-1$ modulo $p$. As $j\le\deg g_l\le p-1$, this forces $j=p-1=\deg g_l$, so $g_l=cX_l^{p-1}$ with $c\ne0$. But then $E(g_l)=-cX_l^{p-2}$ and
$$\tau_l=-cX_l^{p-1}Y_l^p-c\,(X_l^pY_l-X_lY_l^p)X_l^{p-2}=-cX_l^{2p-2}Y_l\ne0,$$
a contradiction. So every $g_l=0$ and $f=0$. $\square$

**Proof of the theorem.** Let $m\le2p+3$ and $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$. By Lemma 2, $\mathrm{in}_m(g)=\bar\mu(P)$ with $P\in\mathcal W_m$. By Jennings' theorem, as quoted in the Setting of [induced subgraphs decide the property](small-induced-subgraphs-decide-the-kernel-unipotent-property.md), $\mathrm{in}_m(g)\ne0$, so $P\ne0$ and some $P^{(k)}\ne0$. Lemma 4 gives $S$ and $v$ with $\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(k)})(v)\ne0$. For $\rho=\rho_{S,v}$, (E) gives $\kappa_\rho(\mathrm{in}_m(g))\ne0$, so $\rho(g)=1+\kappa_\rho(\mathrm{in}_m(g))E_{1,m+1}\ne1$. This proves detection in every degree $m\le2p+3$, and Lemma 1 of [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md) gives the kernel $n$-unipotent property over $\mathbb F_p$ for $n\le2p+4$. Over a finite field $\mathbb F\supseteq\mathbb F_p$ it follows because $U_n(\mathbb F_p)\subseteq U_n(\mathbb F)$ and every continuous homomorphism to $U_n(\mathbb F)$ kills $G_{(n)}$, by Step 1 of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md). $\square$

The corollary follows from the theorem and Corollary 1 of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md).

**Proposition (undetected elements need three letters $z$).** This is proved here. Let $m\le N(p)+1$, and let $g\in G_{(m)}\setminus G_{(m+1)}$ satisfy $\rho(g)=1$ for every continuous $\rho:G\to U_{m+1}(\mathbb F_p)$. Then every $P\in\mathcal W_m$ with $\bar\mu(P)=\mathrm{in}_m(g)$, and by Lemma 2 there is one, satisfies $P^{(0)}=P^{(1)}=P^{(2)}=0$: it is a combination of words with at least three letters $Z$. By the theorem, no such $g$ exists for $m\le2p+3$.

*Proof.* By (E) and formula (1), $\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(k)})(v)=0$ for all $S$ and $v$. The cases $k=0$ and $k=1$ of the proof of Lemma 4 used only this and (W0), (W1), so $P^{(0)}=P^{(1)}=0$. For $k=2$ they give (Z) for every $v\in(\mathbb F_p^2)^3$. With $q=p$, the proof of part (a) of the proposition in [no fixed finite field detects every Zassenhaus degree](no-fixed-finite-field-detects-every-zassenhaus-degree-of-zp2-free-product-zp.md) derives from (Z) alone, from its choice of $v_0,v_1,v_2$ for each factor $\ell$ of $\Psi_p$ onwards, that the polynomial $\Psi_p$ of degree $N(p)$ divides $\Phi$. As $\deg\Phi=m-2<N(p)$, $\Phi=0$, so $\kappa(P^{(2)})=0$ and $P^{(2)}=0$. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *The mechanism.* A representation $\rho_{S,v}$ sees only the words whose letters $Z$ sit exactly at $S$, and evaluates them at one rational vector per run. So an element escapes all of them exactly when every multihomogeneous component of its $\kappa$ vanishes at all rational points. By the argument of Lemma 3, this means that each component lies in the ideal generated by the forms $\Lambda(a_l)$, the run-by-run version of the invisible polynomials of $\mathbb Z_p^2$ in Example (i) of [the invisible-ideal theorem](unipotent-detection-in-free-pro-p-products.md). Initial forms cannot do this in low degree because of (W1): their $\kappa$ is invariant under a common translation of all runs, and the derivative $E$ of that invariance separates the runs while $D\le2p$. For $k=2$ the obstruction is the polynomial $\Psi_p$ of the counterexample, which first appears in degree $N(p)$.
- *Where the proof stops.* For $m=2p+4$ the cases $k\ne3$ still go through, $k=2$ by the argument of the proposition. For $k=3$, $D=2p+1$, and $Ef$ may contain monomials of degree $p$ in two runs, so the $\tau_l$ need not vanish separately.
- *Computer check.* This is not part of the proof. Let $\mathcal V_m\subseteq\bar R_m$ be spanned recursively by $X,Y,Z$ in degree $1$, by the brackets of elements of lower degrees, and by the $p$th powers of elements of $\mathcal V_{m/p}$. Lemma 1 gives $\mathcal V_m\subseteq\mathcal W_m$, and the proof of Lemma 2 gives $\mathrm{in}_m(G_{(m)})\subseteq\bar\mu(\mathcal V_m)$. A calculation in normal words found $\dim\mathcal V_m=3,5,5,15,24,55,120,285,640,1524$ for $m=1,\ldots,10$ at $p=2$, and found that the functionals $P\mapsto\kappa_{\delta(S)}(P^{(|S|)})(v)$ have full rank on $\mathcal V_m$ for $m\le10$ at $p=2$ and for $m\le9$ at $p=3$. If correct, this extends the theorem at $p=2$ to $n\le11$. It also confirmed (W1) for a basis of $\mathcal V_m$ for $m\le8$ at $p=2$ and $m\le7$ at $p=3$. A second calculation tested the polynomial statement behind the case $k\ge3$ directly: no nonzero $\Phi$ of degree $D$ in $k$ pairs satisfies (Z) for all $v$, for $p=2$ with $k=3$ and $D\le11$, $k=4$ and $D\le8$, $k=5$ and $D\le7$, and $k=6$ and $D\le5$, and for $p=3$ with $k=3$ and $D\le8$. For $D\le2p$ and every $k\ge3$ this is proved by the case $k\ge3$ of the proof of Lemma 4, which uses only $Ef=0$ and the vanishing at rational points. But for $p=2$, $k=3$ and $D=12$ there is, up to scalars, exactly one such $\Phi$, and for $D=13$ a $6$-dimensional space of them. So translation invariance alone does not decide degree $m=15$ at $p=2$. For $D\le13$, no nonzero such $\Phi$ is $\kappa(P)$ for a combination $P$ of brackets $[[\ell_\alpha,\ell_\beta],\ell_\gamma]$ of the elements $\ell_\mu$ of Step 3 of [the counterexample](kernel-unipotent-conjecture-fails-for-zp2-free-product-zp.md), whose images $\kappa$ are given by (K) and Step 4 there.
- *What remains open.* For $p=2$, the property is not decided for $9\le n\le17$. By the proposition, a failure there needs an element whose initial form has at least three letters $z$. The case $k\ge3$ of Lemma 4 used only translation invariance, which by the calculation above does not suffice in every degree below $N(p)$. Extending the theorem much further would also need the bracket structure of initial forms. If Lemma 4 held for all $m\le N(p)+1$, then $G$ would have the property for $n\le N(p)+2$, and for $p=2$ the counterexample bound $n_0=18$ would be exact.

**Status.** The theorem, the corollary and the proposition are proved here. They were not found in the sources opened for [the conjecture](kernel-unipotent-conjecture.md), and no separate literature search was made. The calculations in the remarks are not part of the proof.
