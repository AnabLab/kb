**Setting.** Let $p$ be odd and $k \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $d$ and odd residue degree $f$. Let $E \subset \bar{\mathbb{Q}}_p$ be the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, called $K$ in [the two-dimensional representation of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$](two-dimensional-representation-of-g-qp-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md). Since $f$ is odd, $E \not\subseteq k$, because the unramified subextensions of $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ have degree dividing $f$. So $K = kE$ has degree $2$ over $k$, and it is unramified, being generated over $k$ by roots of unity of order $p^2 - 1$: it is the unramified quadratic extension of $k$. Let $\iota$ generate $\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)$; it restricts to the nontrivial automorphism of $E$. Let $\varepsilon : G_k \to \{\pm 1\}$ be the character with kernel $G_K$. Fix $\delta \in E$ with $\delta \notin \mathbb{Q}_p$ and $\delta^2 \in \mathbb{Q}_p$, so $\iota(\delta) = -\delta$, and put $f_\delta(x) = \mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_p}(\delta x)$ for $x \in K$.

Let $W$ be the representation of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ on $E_+$ of part 1 of that entry, and put
$$\rho = W|_{G_k}.$$
On $G_E$, $W$ is the Lubin–Tate representation $\rho_E$ of $E$ for the uniformizer $p$. A representation of $G_k$ is *Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate* if its composite with every automorphism of $G_k$ is Hodge–Tate [@hoshi2024, Definition 1.3, p. 3]; see [the overview](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md).

*The anticyclotomic functional.* Inertia $I_k$ of $k$ is also that of $K$. For $g \in I_k$ the image of $g$ in $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $\mathrm{rec}_K(u_g)$ for a unique $u_g \in \mathcal{O}_K^\times$ ([local class field theory](local-class-field-theory.md)). Put $\Phi_0(g) = f_\delta(\log u_g)$, a continuous homomorphism $I_k \to \mathbb{Q}_p$. Its image $f_\delta(\log\mathcal{O}_K^\times)$ is compact, and it is nonzero, because $\log\mathcal{O}_K^\times$ spans $K$ and $f_\delta \ne 0$, the trace form being nondegenerate. So $\Phi_0(I_k) = p^m\mathbb{Z}_p$ for some $m \in \mathbb{Z}$. Let $P_k$ be wild inertia and put $\Phi = p^{-m}\Phi_0|_{P_k}$.

**Theorem.** This is proved here.

1. *The representation.* $\rho$ is an irreducible Hodge–Tate representation of $G_k$ of dimension $2$, and $\rho|_{G_K} = \rho_E|_{G_K}$.
2. *A necessary condition.* $\Phi : P_k \to \mathbb{Z}_p$ is surjective, and $\Phi(gvg^{-1}) = \varepsilon(g)\Phi(v)$ for $g \in G_k$ and $v \in P_k$. If $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ and $\rho \circ \theta$ is Hodge–Tate, then $\Phi \circ \theta = c\,\Phi$ on $P_k$ for some $c \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$.
3. *Failure.* Suppose $d \ge 3$ and $k(\mu_p) \ne K$. Then there is an automorphism $\theta$ of $G_k$, inducing the identity on $\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{tr}}/k)$, such that $\rho \circ \theta$ is not Hodge–Tate. So $\rho$ is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate.

**Corollary (dimension two for odd $p$).** Let $p$ be odd and $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ finite with $k \ne \mathbb{Q}_3$. Then $G_k$ has an irreducible Hodge–Tate representation of dimension $2$ that is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate.

Hoshi asked whether such a representation exists [@hoshi2024, Introduction, p. 2]. The knowledgebase had answers for $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with $p \ge 5$ and for every $k$ containing a quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. The new case is that of fields with no quadratic subfield, such as the unramified cubic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_3$ or $\mathbb{Q}_p(p^{1/3})$; they have odd degree. The remaining odd case, $\mathbb{Q}_3$, is answered by [the paired-idempotent lift](paired-idempotent-lift-for-the-q3-norm-one-character.md), which constructs an automorphism of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_3}$ acting on the norm-one principal units of the unramified quadratic extension by an exponent $b \equiv 4 \pmod 9$. So for every odd $p$ and every finite $k/\mathbb{Q}_p$, $G_k$ has such a representation. Dimension $2$ is the least possible, since Hodge–Tate characters are Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate ([the overview](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md), part 1).

**Proof of part 1.** By part 1 of the two-dimensional entry, $W$ is a continuous Hodge–Tate representation with $W|_{G_E} = \rho_E$. Restriction to an open subgroup keeps a representation Hodge–Tate (the first standard fact in the proof of the Proposition of [the non-Galois entry](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md)), so $\rho$ is Hodge–Tate. As $G_K \subseteq G_E$, $\rho|_{G_K} = \rho_E|_{G_K}$. This is irreducible by part 1 of [restricted Lubin–Tate representations](restricted-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), applied to the field $K$ and its subfield $E$, which is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. So $\rho$ is irreducible. $\square$

**Proof of part 2.**

*(a) Surjectivity.* $I_k/P_k$ has order prime to $p$, as $P_k$ is the pro-$p$ Sylow subgroup of $I_k$ ([wild inertia](wild-inertia-of-a-p-adic-field.md)). It maps onto the pro-$p$ group $\Phi_0(I_k)/\Phi_0(P_k)$, which is therefore trivial. So $\Phi_0(P_k) = p^m\mathbb{Z}_p$ and $\Phi(P_k) = \mathbb{Z}_p$.

*(b) Equivariance.* For $g \in G_K$, $gvg^{-1}$ and $v$ have the same image in $G_K^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Let $s \in G_k \setminus G_K$, so $s$ acts on $K$ as $\iota$. Reciprocity commutes with conjugation, $\mathrm{rec}_K(\iota x) = s\,\mathrm{rec}_K(x)\,s^{-1}$ [@neukirchschmidtwingberg2008, Chapter III, §2, p. 170; (7.1.5), p. 375], so $u_{svs^{-1}} = \iota(u_v)$. As $\log$ commutes with $\iota$, $\iota(\delta) = -\delta$ and $\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_p} \circ \iota = \mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_p}$,
$$f_\delta(\iota y) = \mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb{Q}_p}\big(\iota(\iota(\delta)\,y)\big) = -f_\delta(y) \qquad (y \in K),$$
so $\Phi(svs^{-1}) = -\Phi(v)$.

*(c) The condition.* Let $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ with $\rho \circ \theta$ Hodge–Tate. $\theta$ preserves $I_k$ ([invariants](local-field-galois-invariants.md)), and $G_K$ is the only open subgroup of index $2$ of $G_k$ containing $I_k$, because $k$ has only one unramified quadratic extension. So $\theta(G_K) = G_K$; let $\theta_K \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_K)$ be the restriction. Then $(\rho \circ \theta)|_{G_K} = \rho_E|_{G_K} \circ \theta_K$ is Hodge–Tate. By part 2 of [restricted Lubin–Tate representations](restricted-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), for the field $K$ and its quadratic subfield $E$, the automorphism $(\theta_K)_+$ of $K_+$ maps $H_E = \{x \in K : \mathrm{Tr}_{K/E}(x) \in \mathbb{Q}_p\}$ onto itself, and $H_E$ is the kernel of $f_\delta$ (step 6 of its proof). So $f_\delta \circ (\theta_K)_+$ vanishes on $H_E$, and $f_\delta \circ (\theta_K)_+ = c\,f_\delta$ with $c \in \mathbb{Q}_p^\times$.

For $g \in I_k$, facts (i) and (ii) of [the non-Galois entry](non-galois-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md), for $G_K$, give $\theta_K^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathrm{rec}_K(u_g)) = \mathrm{rec}_K(\theta_K^\times(u_g))$ and $\log\theta_K^\times(u_g) = (\theta_K)_+(\log u_g)$. Hence
$$\Phi_0(\theta(g)) = f_\delta\big((\theta_K)_+(\log u_g)\big) = c\,\Phi_0(g).$$
As $\theta(P_k) = P_k$, $\Phi \circ \theta = c\,\Phi$ on $P_k$, and $\mathbb{Z}_p = \Phi(\theta(P_k)) = c\,\mathbb{Z}_p$ gives $c \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$. $\square$

**Proof of part 3.** Identify $G_k$ with the presented group of the Setting of [the mixing entry](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md), applied to $k$, with $\mathcal{G} \cong \Gamma = \mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{tr}}/k)$ and $\bar V = P_k/P_k^{(2)}$. Reduce $\Phi$ modulo $p$. The result is a surjective homomorphism $P_k \to \mathbb{F}_p$ that kills $p$-th powers and commutators, hence $P_k^{(2)}$, so it induces a nonzero continuous homomorphism $\bar\Phi : \bar V \to \mathbb{F}_p$. By part 2, $\bar\Phi(\gamma v) = \varepsilon(\gamma)\bar\Phi(v)$ for $\gamma \in \mathcal{G}$, acting on $\bar V$ by conjugation, where $\varepsilon(\gamma)$ is the value at the image of $\gamma$ in $G_k$. This $\varepsilon$ is the character of the mixing entry: it is trivial on $\tau \in I_k \subseteq G_K$, and $\varepsilon(\sigma) = -1$, as $\sigma$ maps to a topological generator of $\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{ur}}/k)$, on which $\varepsilon$ is nontrivial. The hypothesis $k(\mu_p) \ne K$ says $\omega \ne \varepsilon$ (Setting of the mixing entry).

By part 4 of the mixing entry, as $d \ge 3$, there is $\theta \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$ with $\theta(\gamma) \in \gamma P_k^{(2)}$ for $\gamma \in \mathcal{G}$, so inducing the identity on $\Gamma$, such that $\bar\Phi \circ \bar\theta \notin \mathbb{F}_p\bar\Phi$. If $\rho \circ \theta$ were Hodge–Tate, part 2 would give $\Phi \circ \theta = c\,\Phi$ with $c \in \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, and reducing modulo $p$, $\bar\Phi \circ \bar\theta = \bar c\,\bar\Phi$. So $\rho \circ \theta$ is not Hodge–Tate. $\square$

**Proof of the Corollary.** There are three cases.

- $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$ with $p \ge 5$: part 3 of [the two-dimensional entry](two-dimensional-representation-of-g-qp-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md).
- $k$ contains a quadratic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$: the Corollary of [restricted Lubin–Tate representations](restricted-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md).
- Otherwise $k \ne \mathbb{Q}_p$, as $k \ne \mathbb{Q}_3$. Then $d \ge 3$, since a quadratic $k$ is its own quadratic subfield. The residue degree $f$ is odd, since otherwise $E \subseteq k$. And $k(\mu_p) \ne K$. Indeed, suppose $\mu_p \subset K$. The field $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_p)$ is totally ramified of degree $p - 1$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ ([cyclotomic towers](p-adic-cyclotomic-tower.md)), and its Galois group embeds in $\mathrm{Aut}(\mu_p) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/p)^\times$, so it is cyclic of even order $p - 1$. Its unique quadratic subfield $E_1$ is ramified, so $E_1 \ne E$, and $EE_1 \subseteq K$ is Galois of degree $4$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with group $(\mathbb{Z}/2)^2$. As $EE_1$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, $[EE_1 : EE_1 \cap k] = [EE_1k : k] \le [K : k] = 2$. So $EE_1 \cap k$ has degree $2$ or $4$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and contains one of the three quadratic subfields of $EE_1$, contrary to the case assumption. Now parts 1 and 3 apply. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Comparison with $\mathbb{Q}_p$.* For $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$ the inertia part of the anticyclotomic line is $1$-dimensional, and $\Phi \circ \theta = b(\theta)\Phi$ always holds; Hodge–Tate-ness then depends on whether $b(\theta)$ is an odd integer, and the automorphisms of [the norm-one-unit entry](automorphisms-of-the-galois-group-of-qp-scale-norm-one-units.md) control $b(\theta)$ only modulo $p$. That is why $p = 3$ needed the separate modulo-$9$ construction of [the paired-idempotent lift](paired-idempotent-lift-for-the-q3-norm-one-character.md). For $d \ge 3$ the $\varepsilon$-part of $\bar V$ has dimension $d$, and it suffices to move the line of $\bar\Phi$ modulo $p$, with no integrality condition.
- *Not a formal consequence of earlier entries.* $\rho|_{G_K}$ is the restriction of $\rho_E$ to $G_K$, and [restricted Lubin–Tate representations](restricted-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md) shows that it is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate as a representation of $G_K$ when $K \ne E$. That concerns automorphisms of $G_K$, which need not extend to $G_k$. Here the automorphisms are those of $G_k$, restricted to the characteristic subgroup $G_K$. On the relevant functional, Kondo's symplectic automorphisms fix the complementary directions, so automorphisms of [the mixing entry](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md) that move a complementary generator into a surface pair are needed.
- *Haar measure.* Under the hypotheses of part 3, the classes keeping $\rho \circ \theta$ Hodge–Tate lie in a closed subgroup of Haar measure zero, by the Corollary of [infinite projective orbits of unramified quadratic wild characters](unramified-quadratic-wild-characters-have-infinite-projective-orbits.md). That proof combines the exact surface transvections with the mixing automorphisms used here, requiring only their prescribed action modulo $P_k^{(2)}$.
- *Not covered here.* $\mathbb{Q}_3$ in dimension $2$, which [the paired-idempotent lift](paired-idempotent-lift-for-the-q3-norm-one-character.md) answers; the case $p = 2$; and the case $k(\mu_p) = K$, where, by the proof of the Corollary, $k$ has a quadratic subfield and the earlier entry applies.
- *Status of the sources.* The theorem and its proof are not stated in the sources opened for this entry. Through [restricted Lubin–Tate representations](restricted-lubin-tate-representations-are-not-aut-intrinsically-hodge-tate.md) it rests on Hoshi's lemma on abelian Hodge–Tate representations and on Kondo's arXiv preprint; through [the mixing entry](automorphisms-of-p-adic-galois-groups-mixing-complementary-and-surface-generators.md) it rests on the Jannsen–Wingberg presentation and the corrected lifting theorem.
