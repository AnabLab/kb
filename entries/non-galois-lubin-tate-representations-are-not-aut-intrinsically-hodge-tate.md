**Statement.** Let $k$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ of degree $d \ge 2$, and suppose that $p$ is odd or $\sqrt{-1} \in k$. Fix a uniformizer $\pi$. Let $\chi_\pi : G_k^{\mathrm{ab}} \to \mathcal{O}_k^\times$ be the inverse of the reciprocity isomorphism $\mathrm{rec}_k : (k^\times)^\wedge \to G_k^{\mathrm{ab}}$ followed by the projection to $\mathcal{O}_k^\times$ that kills $\pi$, and let $\rho_{k,\pi} : G_k \to \mathcal{O}_k^\times \subset \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(k_+)$ be $G_k \to G_k^{\mathrm{ab}} \xrightarrow{\chi_\pi} \mathcal{O}_k^\times$ followed by multiplication on $k$. This is the Lubin–Tate representation of [Hodge–Tate representations not preserved by automorphisms](aut-intrinsic-hodge-tate-representations.md) [@kondo2026, Introduction, p. 2]. Let $E_{\mathrm{out}} \subseteq \mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ be the set of classes $\alpha$ with $\rho_{k,\pi} \circ \tilde\alpha$ Hodge–Tate, for a representative $\tilde\alpha$ of $\alpha$.

1. $\rho_{k,\pi}$ is Hodge–Tate.
2. $E_{\mathrm{out}}$ is contained in a closed subgroup of $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ of Haar measure zero. In particular, $\rho_{k,\pi}$ is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge–Tate.

Kondo proves part 2 when $k$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ [@kondo2026, Theorem 3.5 and Corollary 3.6], and his link between Hodge–Tate-ness and $\alpha_+$ assumes this [@kondo2026, Definition 1.4, Theorem 2.2 and Theorem 2.6]. The proof below, written out here, removes the hypothesis. The new cases are the fields that are not Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. An example is $\mathbb{Q}_p(\sqrt[3]{p})$ with $p = 3$ or $p \equiv 5 \pmod 6$. It is cubic, as $x^3 - p$ is Eisenstein. For these $p$, a primitive cube root of unity $\zeta$ is not in $\mathbb{Q}_p$: for $p \equiv 5 \pmod 6$ the group $\mathbb{F}_p^\times$ has no element of order $3$, and for $p = 3$ the number $-3$ is not a square. So $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta)$ is quadratic and cannot lie in a cubic field, and the other roots $\zeta^{\pm 1}\sqrt[3]{p}$ are not in $\mathbb{Q}_p(\sqrt[3]{p})$.

**The algebra $W_k$.** This is proved here. Let $\tilde k$ be the Galois closure of $k$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ inside $\bar k$, let $H = \mathrm{Gal}(\tilde k/k)$ and $m = [\tilde k : k]$, and let $\Sigma$ be the set of the $d$ embeddings $k \to \tilde k$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, on which $H$ acts by $h \cdot \tau = h \circ \tau$. Let $W_k$ be the set of maps $k \to k$ of the form $\sum_{\tau \in \Sigma} c_\tau \tau$ with $c_\tau \in \mathbb{Q}_p$.

- *(a) Size.* A map $\sum_\tau c_\tau \tau : k \to \tilde k$ takes values in $k$ exactly when $c$ is constant on $H$-orbits. So $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p} W_k$ is the number of $H$-orbits on $\Sigma$. This is at most $d$, with equality exactly when $k$ is Galois, and then $W_k = \mathbb{Q}_p[\mathrm{Gal}(k/\mathbb{Q}_p)]$, the algebra in Kondo's criterion [@kondo2026, Remark 1.5 and Theorem 2.6].
- *(b) Products.* $W_k$ is a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-subalgebra of $\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(k_+)$ containing the identity.
- *(c) Units.* If $\beta \in W_k$ is bijective, then $\beta^{-1} \in W_k$. So $W_k^\times = W_k \cap \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(k_+)$ is a closed subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(k_+)$.

*Proof.* (a) By Dedekind's lemma, distinct embeddings $k \to \tilde k$ are linearly independent over $\tilde k$. As $k$ is the fixed field of $H$, the map $\beta = \sum_\tau c_\tau \tau$ takes values in $k$ exactly when $h \circ \beta = \beta$ for all $h \in H$, that is, $\sum_\tau c_\tau (h \circ \tau) = \sum_\tau c_\tau \tau$. By independence, this says $c_{h \circ \tau} = c_\tau$. So the orbit sums span $W_k$, and they are independent by Dedekind's lemma again. All orbits are points exactly when every $h \in H$ fixes every $\tau(k)$. These fields generate $\tilde k$, so this means $H = 1$, that is, $k = \tilde k$. Then $\Sigma = \mathrm{Gal}(k/\mathbb{Q}_p)$.

(b) The inclusion $k \subseteq \tilde k$ lies in $\Sigma$, so the identity is in $W_k$. Let $\beta = \sum_\tau c_\tau \tau$ and $\beta' = \sum_{\tau'} c'_{\tau'} \tau'$ be in $W_k$. Since $\tilde k/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is normal, each $\tau$ extends to some $\tilde\tau \in \mathrm{Gal}(\tilde k/\mathbb{Q}_p)$. For $x \in k$, $\beta'(x) \in k$, so $\tau(\beta'(x)) = \tilde\tau(\beta'(x)) = \sum_{\tau'} c'_{\tau'} (\tilde\tau \circ \tau')(x)$, and $\tilde\tau \circ \tau' \in \Sigma$. So $\beta \circ \beta'$ is a $\mathbb{Q}_p$-combination of elements of $\Sigma$. It maps $k$ into $k$, so it lies in $W_k$.

(c) By Cayley–Hamilton, $\beta^{-1}$ is a polynomial in $\beta$, as in [@kondo2026, Remark 1.5]. $W_k$ is a finite-dimensional subspace, hence closed. $\square$

**Hodge–Tate-ness forces $\alpha_+ \in W_k$.** For $\alpha \in \mathrm{Aut}(G_k)$, the group-theoretic reconstruction of $k$ gives an automorphism $\alpha^\times$ of $\mathcal{O}_k^\times$ and an automorphism $\alpha_+$ of $k_+$, depending only on the class of $\alpha$ in $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ [@kondo2026, §0, Groups of MLF-type, p. 4]. The map $\alpha_+$ is additive and continuous, hence $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linear [@kondo2026, §1, p. 5]. None of the following uses that $k$ is Galois, or any hypothesis on $p$.

- *(i)* The image of inertia in $G_k^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $\mathrm{rec}_k(\mathcal{O}_k^\times)$, and it is recovered from $G_k$ [@hoshi2021, Definition 3.10 (i), Proposition 3.6 and Proposition 3.11 (i)]. So $\alpha^\times(\mathcal{O}_k^\times) = \mathcal{O}_k^\times$ and $\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathrm{rec}_k(x)) = \mathrm{rec}_k(\alpha^\times(x))$ for $x \in \mathcal{O}_k^\times$.
- *(ii)* $\log_k \circ \alpha^\times = \alpha_+ \circ \log_k$ on $\mathcal{O}_k^\times$, because $k_+$ is reconstructed from the image of inertia through the logarithm [@hoshi2021, Definition 3.10 (vi) and Proposition 3.11 (iv)] [@kondo2026, proof of Theorem 2.6, p. 8].

*Proposition.* If $\rho_{k,\pi} \circ \alpha$ is Hodge–Tate, then there are integers $i_\sigma$, for $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(\tilde k/\mathbb{Q}_p)$, with
$$\alpha_+(x) = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{\sigma} i_\sigma\, \sigma(x) \quad \text{for all } x \in k.$$
In particular $\alpha_+ \in W_k^\times$. For Galois $k$ this is one direction of Kondo's Theorem 2.6. The Proposition holds for every $p$, including $p = 2$ with $\sqrt{-1} \notin k$.

*Proof.* This is proved here. It uses two standard facts. Restricting a Hodge–Tate representation $V$ of $G_k$ to an open subgroup $G_{k'}$ keeps it Hodge–Tate: by Tate's theorem $B_{\mathrm{HT}}^{G_{k'}} = k'$, so taking $G_{k'}$-invariants of $B_{\mathrm{HT}} \otimes_k D_k(V) \cong B_{\mathrm{HT}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V$ gives $D_{k'}(V) = k' \otimes_k D_k(V)$, where $D_k(V) = (B_{\mathrm{HT}} \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V)^{G_k}$. And a direct sum is Hodge–Tate exactly when each summand is, since $D_k$ is additive and $\dim_k D_k(V) \le \dim V$ always. Put $U = G_{\tilde k}$, and let $j : U^{\mathrm{ab}} \to G_k^{\mathrm{ab}}$ be induced by the inclusion.

1. *Pass to $\tilde k$.* Let $\psi = \chi_\pi \circ \alpha^{\mathrm{ab}} \circ j : U^{\mathrm{ab}} \to \mathcal{O}_k^\times$. Then $\rho_{k,\pi} \circ \alpha$ restricted to $U$ is $U \to U^{\mathrm{ab}} \xrightarrow{\psi} \mathcal{O}_k^\times$ acting on $k_+$, and it is Hodge–Tate. A $k$-basis of $\tilde k$ identifies $\tilde k_+$ with $k_+^m$, compatibly with multiplication by elements of $k$. So the representation $U \to U^{\mathrm{ab}} \xrightarrow{\psi} \mathcal{O}_{\tilde k}^\times \subset \mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Q}_p}(\tilde k_+)$ is $m$ copies of it, and is Hodge–Tate.
2. *Use that $\tilde k$ is Galois.* By Hoshi's lemma for the Galois field $\tilde k$, with coefficient field $\tilde k$, there are integers $i_\sigma$ and an open subgroup $J$ of the inertia group of $\tilde k$ on which $\psi$ agrees with $\prod_\sigma \tilde\chi_\sigma^{i_\sigma}$, where $\tilde\chi_\sigma$ is $\sigma$ applied to the analogue of $\chi_\pi$ for $\tilde k$ [@hoshi2024, Definition 1.7 and Lemma 1.8]. By (i) for $\tilde k$, the image of $J$ in $U^{\mathrm{ab}}$ is $\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(U')$ for an open subgroup $U'$ of $\mathcal{O}_{\tilde k}^\times$, and $\tilde\chi_\sigma(\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(y)) = \sigma(y)$ for units $y$. So $\psi(\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(y)) = \prod_\sigma \sigma(y)^{i_\sigma}$ for $y \in U'$.
3. *Compute $\psi$.* Local reciprocity is compatible with norms: $j \circ \mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k} = \mathrm{rec}_k \circ N_{\tilde k/k}$, which follows from the finite-level statement by passing to the limit [@milnecft, III, Lemma 3.2, p. 107]. With (i), for $y \in \mathcal{O}_{\tilde k}^\times$,
$$\psi(\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(y)) = \chi_\pi\big(\alpha^{\mathrm{ab}}(\mathrm{rec}_k(N_{\tilde k/k}\, y))\big) = \chi_\pi\big(\mathrm{rec}_k(\alpha^\times(N_{\tilde k/k}\, y))\big) = \alpha^\times(N_{\tilde k/k}\, y),$$
the last step because $\alpha^\times(N_{\tilde k/k}\, y)$ is a unit. Hence $\alpha^\times(N_{\tilde k/k}\, y) = \prod_\sigma \sigma(y)^{i_\sigma}$ for $y \in U'$.
4. *Take logarithms.* The logarithm of $\tilde k$ extends $\log_k$, and commutes with each $\sigma$. As $N_{\tilde k/k}\, y = \prod_{h \in H} h(y)$, (ii) turns the identity of step 3 into $\alpha_+(\mathrm{Tr}_{\tilde k/k}(z)) = \sum_\sigma i_\sigma \sigma(z)$ for $z \in \log(U')$. This is an open subgroup of $\tilde k_+$, so it spans $\tilde k$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and both sides are $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linear in $z$. So the identity holds on all of $\tilde k$. For $x \in k$, $\mathrm{Tr}_{\tilde k/k}(x) = mx$, which gives the formula. Grouping the $\sigma$ by their restrictions to $k$ writes $\alpha_+$ as a $\mathbb{Q}$-combination of elements of $\Sigma$, so $\alpha_+ \in W_k$, and $\alpha_+ \in W_k^\times$ by (c). $\square$

**Proof of part 1.** This is proved here. Define $\psi$ as in step 1 with $\alpha = \mathrm{id}$. The computation of step 3 gives $\psi(\mathrm{rec}_{\tilde k}(y)) = N_{\tilde k/k}\, y = \prod_{h \in H} h(y)$ for every unit $y$, so $\psi$ agrees with $\prod_{h \in H} \tilde\chi_h$ on the whole inertia group of $\tilde k$. So by Hoshi's lemma in the other direction, with $i_h = 1$ for $h \in H$ and $i_\sigma = 0$ otherwise, the representation of $U$ on $\tilde k_+$ given by $\psi$ is Hodge–Tate [@hoshi2024, Lemma 1.8]. By the identification in step 1, its summand $\rho_{k,\pi}|_U$ is therefore Hodge–Tate. Hodge–Tate-ness descends along the finite Galois extension $\tilde k/k$: for $V = k_+$ with $G_k$ acting through $\rho_{k,\pi}$, the module $D_{\tilde k}(V)$ is a $\tilde k$-vector space of dimension $\dim V$ with a semilinear action of $H$, so $D_{\tilde k}(V) = \tilde k \otimes_k D_{\tilde k}(V)^H$ by Galois descent. As $D_{\tilde k}(V)^H = D_k(V)$, this has dimension $\dim V$ over $k$. For Galois $k$, Kondo instead gets crystallinity from Lubin–Tate theory [@kondo2026, Remark 2.4].

**Proof of part 2.** This is proved here. If $d = 2$, then $k$ is Galois over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, and this is Kondo's theorem [@kondo2026, Theorem 3.5]. So let $d \ge 3$, and write $g = (d-1)/2$ for odd $d$ and $g = (d-2)/2$ for even $d$, so that $g \ge 1$ and $d \le 2g + 2$. Let $r = d - 2g \in \{1, 2\}$.

Kondo fixes a basis $y_1, \dots, y_d$ of $k_+$ coming from the Jannsen–Wingberg or Diekert generators [@kondo2026, Lemma 1.3], and shows that $\Phi : \mathrm{Out}(G_k) \to \mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, $\alpha \mapsto \alpha_+$, is a continuous homomorphism [@kondo2026, Lemma 1.6]. Its image $I$ contains the matrices $\mathrm{diag}(I_r, A)$ with $A \in \mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ [@kondo2026, Definition 3.2 and Lemma 3.3]. Neither result assumes that $k$ is Galois. Here $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}$ is the isometry group of a nondegenerate alternating form on $\mathbb{Q}_p^{2g}$; the argument below does not depend on which one.

Let $K = I \cap W_k^\times$, a closed subgroup of $I$. By the Proposition, $E_{\mathrm{out}} \subseteq \Phi^{-1}(K)$. It is enough to show that $K$ has infinite index in $I$. Then the closed subgroup $\Phi^{-1}(K)$ has infinite index in the profinite group $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ [@kondo2026, §1, p. 6], because $\Phi$ maps it onto $K$ and maps $\mathrm{Out}(G_k)$ onto $I$. A closed subgroup of infinite index has Haar measure zero, because it has infinitely many disjoint cosets of equal measure.

Suppose instead that $K$ has finite index in $I$. Being closed, it is open in $I$. So the $A \in \mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ with $\mathrm{diag}(I_r, A) \in K$ form an open subgroup $V$. The $\mathbb{Q}_p$-span of these matrices lies in $W_k$. It contains $\mathrm{diag}(I_r, I_{2g})$ and every $\mathrm{diag}(0, A - I_{2g})$ with $A \in V$. Let $N \subseteq M_{2g}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$ be the span of the $A - I_{2g}$ with $A \in V$.

*Claim: $N = M_{2g}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$.*

- $N$ is closed under products, because $(A - I)(B - I) = (AB - I) - (A - I) - (B - I)$ and $AB \in V$.
- After conjugating $V$ and $N$ by a matrix that takes the form to the standard one $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & I_g \\ -I_g & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $V$ is an open subgroup of the standard $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$. So it contains every element congruent to $I$ modulo $p^n$, for some $n$. This includes $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} I & S \\ 0 & I \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} I & 0 \\ S & I \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ for every symmetric $S \in p^n M_g(\mathbb{Z}_p)$; a direct computation shows these preserve the form.
- Hence $N$ contains $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & S \\ 0 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ S & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ for every symmetric $S \in M_g(\mathbb{Q}_p)$.
- Their products give $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} SS' & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & SS' \end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Products of two symmetric matrices span $M_g(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, since $E_{ii} E_{ii} = E_{ii}$ and $E_{ii}(E_{ij} + E_{ji}) = E_{ij}$ for $i \ne j$.
- Multiplying these by $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & I \\ 0 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ I & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right)$ gives both off-diagonal blocks.

This proves the claim. Therefore the span lies in $W_k$ and contains every $\mathrm{diag}(\lambda I_r, B)$ with $\lambda \in \mathbb{Q}_p$ and $B \in M_{2g}(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, a space of dimension $1 + 4g^2$. But $\dim W_k \le d \le 2g + 2 < 1 + 4g^2$ for $g \ge 1$, a contradiction. $\square$

**Remarks.**

- *Galois fields.* For $d \ge 3$ the same argument proves Kondo's theorem for Galois $k$. Kondo compares Lie group dimensions when $d \ge 5$, and uses that $\mathrm{Sp}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ has no abelian open subgroup when $d = 3, 4$ [@kondo2026, proof of Theorem 3.5]. Comparing $\mathbb{Q}_p$-linear spans instead handles every $d \ge 3$ at once, because a span can be larger than a group's dimension. The quadratic case still needs Kondo's explicit automorphism.
- *What is not shown.* Whether the converse of the Proposition holds for non-Galois $k$ is not settled here. For Galois $k$ Kondo proves it with integer coefficients [@kondo2026, Theorem 2.6]. The theorem above does not need it.
- *$p = 2$ with $\sqrt{-1} \notin k$.* This case is not covered. Kondo's Lemma 3.3 rests on a presentation that needs $p$ odd or $\sqrt{-1} \in k$ [@kondo2026, Theorem 1.2 and Remark 3.1]. When $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is unramified, Galois or not, the conclusion of part 2 still holds by [outer automorphisms when adjoining $i$ is unramified](unramified-dyadic-outer-automorphisms.md), part 3. Its proof combines the Proposition above with Diekert's presentation and a trace-hyperplane argument, using that every element of $W_k$ maps $\mathbb{Q}_p$ into itself, since $\tau(1) = 1$. The case where $k(\sqrt{-1})/k$ is ramified is not treated.

**Status of the sources.** Kondo's paper is an arXiv preprint, and Hoshi's is published. Milne states the norm compatibility of the local Artin map, but defers its proof [@milnecft, III, Lemma 3.2, p. 107]. The theorem and the Proposition are deductions written out here. They are not stated in any of the sources opened, which treat only Galois $k$.
