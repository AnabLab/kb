**Setting.** A *Kodaira fibration* is a non-isotrivial morphism $f : X \to B$ from a smooth projective complex surface onto a smooth projective curve, all of whose fibres are smooth projective curves of one genus $g$ [@xu2025, Definition 1.0.4]. The base then has genus at least $2$ and $g \ge 3$ [@xu2025, p. 3]. For $b \in B$ there is an exact sequence of topological fundamental groups
$$1 \to \pi_1(X_b) \to \pi_1(X) \to \pi_1(B) \to 1,$$
and an algebraic section of $f$ gives a splitting, well defined up to conjugation by $\pi_1(X_b)$. Xu asks whether the resulting map $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{top}}$, from algebraic sections to splittings modulo $\pi_1(X_b)$-conjugation, is bijective [@xu2025, Question 1.0.5]. For a single compact Riemann surface over a point the corresponding map is surjective and never injective, which is why the base must carry monodromy [@xu2025, p. 3]. A Kodaira fibration has only finitely many algebraic sections, by Manin's geometric Mordell theorem [@xu2025, p. 4]. Write $H = H_1(X_b, \mathbb{Z})$, which is isomorphic to $H^1(X_b, \mathbb{Z})$ as a $\pi_1(B)$-module by Poincaré duality, and $\rho$ for the monodromy action of $\pi_1(B)$ on it. Its image is infinite [@xu2025, Remark 2.0.2].

**Statement.**

1. *Injectivity (Xu).* If $H^{\rho(\pi_1(B))} = 0$, then $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{top}}$ is injective [@xu2025, Theorem 1.0.6 and Corollary 3.1.5].
2. *Failure of surjectivity (Lee–Serván).* If $f$ has an algebraic section, there is a double cover $B' \to B$ such that the base change $f' : X' = X \times_B B' \to B'$ is a Kodaira fibration whose $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{top}}$ is not surjective [@xu2025, Proposition 1.0.8 (2) and Appendix A].
3. *Splitting without sections (Xu).* Some Kodaira fibration has a split fundamental group sequence and no algebraic section [@xu2025, Corollary 1.0.12]. This refutes Xu's weak topological section conjecture, that a Kodaira fibration has an algebraic section exactly when its sequence splits [@xu2025, Conjecture 1.0.9]. A proof is given below.
4. *Large monodromy does not suffice.* Some Kodaira fibration has $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{top}}$ injective but not surjective, with monodromy of finite index in $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$. This is proved here from 1 and 2. Xu had asked whether bijectivity might hold when the monodromy has large image [@xu2025, p. 4].

**How 1 is proved.** For an abelian scheme $\mathcal{A} \to B$ over a smooth projective curve, splittings of $1 \to H_1(\mathcal{A}_b, \mathbb{Z}) \to \pi_1(\mathcal{A}) \to \pi_1(B) \to 1$ up to conjugacy are classified by $H^1(\pi_1(B), H_1(\mathcal{A}_b, \mathbb{Z}))$, giving a map $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{ab}}$ from the group of algebraic sections [@xu2025, §3.1, p. 8]. Xu identifies it with a boundary map of the exponential sequence of the dual abelian scheme [@xu2025, Lemma 3.1.1], and shows that it is injective exactly when the monodromy has no invariants, using Simpson's polystability of Higgs bundles coming from variations of Hodge structure [@xu2025, Proposition 3.1.3]. For the relative Jacobian of a Kodaira fibration with an algebraic section, $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{ab}}$ is never surjective [@xu2025, Corollary 3.2.5]. Part 1 follows by mapping $X$ into the relative Jacobian by Abel–Jacobi relative to an algebraic section, which on fundamental groups is abelianization of $\pi_1(X_b)$ [@xu2025, proof of Corollary 3.1.5, p. 11].

**The Lee–Serván sections.** The appendix fixes a closed disc $D \subset B$ and a trivialization $f^{-1}(D) \cong \Sigma_g \times D$, and puts $B_1 = B_2 = B \setminus \mathring{D}$ and $X_1 = X_2 = f^{-1}(B \setminus \mathring{D})$. Then $B' \cong B_1 \cup_\partial B_2$ and $X' \cong X_1 \cup_\partial X_2$, glued along the boundary by a reflection, and $f'$ restricts to $f$ on each $X_i$ [@xu2025, Appendix A, p. 21]. By van Kampen, $\pi_1(B')$ is the amalgam of the $\pi_1(B_i)$ over $\pi_1(S^1)$. Given an algebraic section $s$ of $f$ and $\gamma \in \pi_1(\Sigma_g)$, the splitting $s_\gamma$ of $f'$ is $s_*$ on $\pi_1(B_1)$ and $\gamma s_*(\cdot)\gamma^{-1}$ on $\pi_1(B_2)$ [@xu2025, Definition A.0.1 and Lemma A.0.2]. If $s_\gamma$ and $s_\delta$ are $\pi_1(\Sigma_g)$-conjugate, then $[\delta] - [\gamma] \in H$ is monodromy invariant [@xu2025, Lemma A.0.3]. As $H/H^{\rho(\pi_1(B))}$ is infinite, this gives infinitely many classes of splittings, against finitely many algebraic sections [@xu2025, Appendix A, p. 23].

**On Xu's proof of 3.** Xu adapts Stix's neighbourhood argument: $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{top}}$ is surjective exactly when the weak conjecture holds for every connected finite étale cover $X'' \to X$ with connected fibres over $B$ [@xu2025, Proposition 4.0.3 and Corollary 4.0.9]. Part 3 then follows from part 2 [@xu2025, Corollary 4.0.10]. The direction used for part 3, from the weak conjecture to surjectivity, rests on the converse in [@xu2025, Corollary 4.0.8]: if an algebraic section $s$ lifts compatibly to every neighbourhood of a splitting $x$, then $x$ is conjugate to the splitting of $s$. Compatible lifts give, for each finite-index subgroup $U \supseteq x(\pi_1(B))$ of $\pi_1(X)$, a coset $g_U U$ with $g_U^{-1} s_*(\pi_1(B)) g_U \subseteq U$, compatible as $U$ shrinks. Such a system defines an element of the profinite completion of $\pi_1(X_b)$, and the source does not say how to obtain a conjugating element of $\pi_1(X_b)$ itself. This step is not checked here, and the proof below avoids it.

**Proof of 3.** This argument is written out here. Let $f$ be a Kodaira fibration with an algebraic section $s$. Such $f$ exist, by Bregman's theorem [@xu2025, Proposition 2.0.3] applied to any Kodaira fibration. Take $f'$ and $s_\gamma$ as above, with base point on the gluing torus. Put $\Delta = \pi_1(\Sigma_g)$, $\Pi = \pi_1(X')$, $G = \pi_1(B')$ and $\Gamma = \rho(\pi_1(B))$, so that $H = \Delta^{\mathrm{ab}}$.

*Step 1.* $G$ acts on $H$ by conjugation through any splitting. On $\pi_1(B_1)$ and on $\pi_1(B_2)$ this action factors through $\pi_1(B \setminus \mathring{D}) \to \pi_1(B)$ and $\rho$, since the fibres over $B \setminus \mathring{D}$ are identified with $\Sigma_g$ through the same trivialization over $D$. That map is onto, so for every $n$ the invariants of $H/nH$ under $\pi_1(B_1)$, under $\pi_1(B_2)$ and under $\Gamma$ coincide.

*Step 2.* For a prime $p$, let $N_p$ be the kernel of $\Delta \to H/pH$. It is characteristic in $\Delta$, hence normal in $\Pi$, and $E_p = \Pi/N_p$ is an extension of $G$ by $H/pH$. Call splittings $x, y$ of $\Pi \to G$ *$p$-equivalent* if their images $\bar x, \bar y$ in $E_p$ are conjugate by an element of $H/pH$. This is an equivalence relation, coarser than $\Delta$-conjugacy. Writing $H/pH$ additively, $c(\ell) = \bar x(\ell)\bar y(\ell)^{-1}$ satisfies $c(\ell\ell') = c(\ell) + \ell \cdot c(\ell')$, and $\bar x = h\bar y h^{-1}$ exactly when $c(\ell) = h - \ell \cdot h$ for all $\ell \in G$. For $x = s_\gamma$ and $y = s_\delta$, the definition gives $c = 0$ on $\pi_1(B_1)$ and $c(\ell) = (1 - \ell) \cdot v$ on $\pi_1(B_2)$, where $v$ is the image of $[\gamma] - [\delta]$. Two such cocycles agree once they agree on the generating set $\pi_1(B_1) \cup \pi_1(B_2)$. So $s_\gamma$ and $s_\delta$ are $p$-equivalent exactly when some $h$ is invariant under $\pi_1(B_1)$ with $v - h$ invariant under $\pi_1(B_2)$, which by Step 1 means that $v \in (H/pH)^\Gamma$.

*Step 3.* Choose $\ell_0 \in \pi_1(B)$ and $v_0 \in H$ with $w = \rho(\ell_0)v_0 - v_0 \neq 0$. Let $k$ be the number of algebraic sections of $f'$, and choose a prime $p > k$ with $w \notin pH$. For $0 \le a < p$ choose $\gamma_a \in \Delta$ with image $a v_0$ in $H$. For $a \neq b$, $\rho(\ell_0)$ moves $(a - b)v_0$ by $(a - b)w \notin pH$, so the $s_{\gamma_a}$ are pairwise not $p$-equivalent. Each algebraic section is $p$-equivalent to at most one of them, so some $x = s_{\gamma_a}$ is $p$-equivalent to no algebraic section.

*Step 4.* Put $\Pi'' = N_p \cdot x(G)$. Since $\Pi = \Delta \cdot x(G)$ and $\Pi'' \cap \Delta = N_p$, it has index $p^{2g}$ in $\Pi$. Let $X'' \to X'$ be the corresponding connected finite étale cover and $f'' : X'' \to B'$ the composite. As $\Pi'' \to G$ is onto, the fibres of $f''$ are connected, and they are Galois covers of the fibres of $f'$ with group $H/pH$. So $f''$ is a smooth projective family of curves of genus $p^{2g}(g-1) + 1$ on a smooth projective surface. It is not isotrivial: otherwise every fibre of $f'$ would be a quotient of one curve $C$ by a subgroup of the finite group $\mathrm{Aut}(C)$, leaving finitely many isomorphism classes, whereas $f'$ is not isotrivial. So $f''$ is a Kodaira fibration, and $x$ is a splitting of its sequence, since $x(G) \subseteq \Pi'' = \pi_1(X'')$.

*Step 5.* Let $t''$ be an algebraic section of $f''$, with image $t$ in $X'$. After choosing base points, the splitting of $t''$ gives a splitting $y$ in the class of $t$ with $y(G) \subseteq g\Pi''g^{-1}$ for some $g \in \Pi$. Write $g = d \cdot x(\ell)$ with $d \in \Delta$. Since $x(\ell) \in \Pi''$, the splitting $y' = d^{-1}yd$ is in the class of $t$ and $y'(G) \subseteq \Pi''$. In $E_p$ the image of $\Pi''$ is $\bar x(G)$, and a splitting is determined by its image, so $\bar y' = \bar x$. Then $t$ is $p$-equivalent to $x$, against Step 3. So $f''$ has a split sequence and no algebraic section. $\square$

The same $f''$ has splittings but no algebraic sections, so its $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{top}}$ is not surjective, as in [@xu2025, Corollary 4.0.10].

**Proof of 4.** This argument is written out here. For $g \ge 4$, Xu's moduli construction gives Kodaira fibrations whose monodromy image has finite index in $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$, and hence no invariants [@xu2025, Lemma 2.0.1]. Bregman's theorem gives a Kodaira fibration $f$ with an algebraic section, the same fibre genus and the same monodromy image in the mapping class group [@xu2025, Proposition 2.0.3], so the same image in $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$. Apply part 2 to $f$. The monodromy of $f'$ is $\rho$ composed with $\pi_1(B') \to \pi_1(B)$. This map is onto, because in the appendix's description it is induced on $\pi_1(B_1)$ by the inclusion $B \setminus \mathring{D} \subset B$. So $f'$ has the same monodromy image as $f$, without invariants, and its $\mathrm{sec}_{\mathrm{top}}$ is injective by part 1 and not surjective by part 2. $\square$
