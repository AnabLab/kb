**Setting.** Wintenberger fixes a prime $p$ and works with local fields whose residue field is perfect of characteristic $p$ [@wintenberger1983, p. 59]. Let $K$ be one, with separable closure $\bar K$, and let $(G_K^u)_{u \ge -1}$ be the [ramification filtration](ramification-filtration.md) of $G_K$ in upper numbering. An extension $L \subseteq \bar K$ of $K$ is *arithmetically profinite* (APF) if $G_K^u G_L$ is open in $G_K$ for every $u \ge -1$. For such $L$, put $G_L^0 = G_L \cap G_K^0$, and let $\psi_{L/K}$ be the increasing bijection of $[-1, \infty)$ with $\psi_{L/K}(u) = u$ for $u \le 0$ and $\psi_{L/K}(u) = \int_0^u (G_K^0 : G_L^0 G_K^v)\, dv$ for $u \ge 0$ [@wintenberger1983, 1.2.1]. Every finite separable extension is APF. So is every Galois extension of $K$ with finite residue extension whose Galois group is a $p$-adic Lie group, in particular every abelian extension with finite residue extension of a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$. In characteristic $0$ this is due to Sen [@wintenberger1983, 1.2.2].

Let $L/K$ be an infinite APF extension, and let $\mathcal{E}_{L/K}$ be the set of finite extensions of $K$ inside $L$. The *field of norms* is $X_K(L) = \varprojlim_{E \in \mathcal{E}_{L/K}} E^\times \cup \{0\}$, where the transition maps are the norms. Multiplication is componentwise, and the sum of $(\alpha_E)$ and $(\beta_E)$ has $E$-component $\lim_{E'} N_{E'/E}(\alpha_{E'} + \beta_{E'})$ [@wintenberger1983, 2.1.1 and Theorem 2.1.3 (i)].

**Theorem.** Let $L/K$ be an infinite APF extension, and $k_L$ the residue field of $L$.

1. $X_K(L)$ is a local field of characteristic $p$, its valuation $v$ is discrete with $v(X_K(L)^\times) = \mathbb{Z}$, and a natural embedding of $k_L$ into $X_K(L)$ maps $k_L$ isomorphically onto the residue field of $X_K(L)$ [@wintenberger1983, Theorem 2.1.3 (ii)]. Its ring of integers is complete, since the bijection of [@wintenberger1983, Proposition 2.3.1] identifies it with the ring $A_K(L)$, which is complete [@wintenberger1983, Proposition 2.2.4].
2. For a separable algebraic extension $M$ of $L$, let $X_{L/K}(M)$ be the direct limit of the fields $X_K(L')$ over the finite extensions $L'$ of $L$ inside $M$. This defines an equivalence from the category of separable algebraic extensions of $L$ to that of $X_K(L)$ [@wintenberger1983, 3.2.1 and Theorem 3.2.2]. In particular, $X_{L/K}(\bar K)$ is a separable closure of $X_K(L)$, and $G_L = \mathrm{Gal}(\bar K/L)$ is identified with $G_{X_K(L)}$ [@wintenberger1983, Corollary 3.2.3].
3. Under this identification, $G_{X_K(L)}^{\psi_{L/K}(u)} = G_K^u \cap G_L$ for every $u \ge -1$. This is the case $M = \bar K$ of [@wintenberger1983, Corollary 3.3.6], which is stated for every Galois extension $M$ of $K$ containing $L$.

**Attribution.** The results were announced in two notes by Fontaine and Wintenberger, and Wintenberger writes that the paper owes many of its ideas to Fontaine [@wintenberger1983, p. 60]. Scholze attributes the isomorphism of Galois groups to Fontaine and Wintenberger [@scholze2012, p. 2].

**The cyclotomic tower.** This corollary is proved here from the theorem.

*Corollary.* Let $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$ be finite, and let $\mathbb{F}_q$ be the residue field of $L = K(\mu_{p^\infty})$. Then $[\mathbb{F}_q : \mathbb{F}_p] \le [K:\mathbb{Q}_p]$, and $G_L \cong G_{\mathbb{F}_q((t))}$. If $K$ is unramified of degree $n$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, then $q = p^n$. So every $G_{\mathbb{F}_q((t))}$ is the absolute Galois group of an algebraic extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$.

*Proof.* Since $\Phi_{p^m}(x + 1)$ is Eisenstein, $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^m})$ is totally ramified of degree $\varphi(p^m)$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$. This standard fact is used without being checked in a source. So $[K(\zeta_{p^m}) : K] \ge \varphi(p^m)/[K:\mathbb{Q}_p]$, and $L/K$ is infinite. The residue degree of $K(\zeta_{p^m})$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ equals its residue degree over $\mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^m})$, which is at most $[K(\zeta_{p^m}) : \mathbb{Q}_p(\zeta_{p^m})] \le [K:\mathbb{Q}_p]$. So $\mathbb{F}_q$ is finite, of degree at most $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p]$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$. If $K$ is unramified of degree $n$, then $K(\zeta_{p^m})$ has ramification index at least $\varphi(p^m)$, residue degree at least $n$ and degree at most $n\varphi(p^m)$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, so its residue degree is $n$, and $q = p^n$.

The group $\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)$ embeds into $\mathrm{Aut}(\mu_{p^\infty}) \cong \mathbb{Z}_p^\times$, so $L/K$ is abelian with finite residue extension, hence APF [@wintenberger1983, 1.2.2]. By part 1, $X = X_K(L)$ is a field of characteristic $p$, complete for a discrete valuation, containing a copy of $\mathbb{F}_q$ that maps isomorphically onto its residue field. For a uniformizer $\pi$ of $X$, the ring map $\mathbb{F}_q[[t]] \to \mathcal{O}_X$ that is the identity on $\mathbb{F}_q$ and sends $t$ to $\pi$ is an isomorphism. It is surjective, because successive approximation writes each element of $\mathcal{O}_X$ as $\sum_{i \ge 0} c_i\pi^i$ with $c_i \in \mathbb{F}_q$, and the series converges because $\mathcal{O}_X$ is complete. It is injective, because nonzero elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$ are units, so such a series with first nonzero coefficient $c_j$ has valuation $j$. Hence $X \cong \mathbb{F}_q((t))$, and part 2 gives $G_L \cong G_{\mathbb{F}_q((t))}$. $\square$

*The Kummer tower.* Scholze states that $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p(p^{1/p^\infty})}$ and $G_{\mathbb{F}_p((t))}$ are canonically isomorphic [@scholze2012, Theorem 1.1]. With the corollary for $K = \mathbb{Q}_p$, this gives $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p(\mu_{p^\infty})} \cong G_{\mathbb{Q}_p(p^{1/p^\infty})}$. For odd $p$ these fields are not isomorphic, as checked here. Since $x^{p^n} - p$ is Eisenstein, $\mathbb{Q}_p(p^{1/p^n})$ has degree $p^n$ over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, while $\zeta_p$ has degree $p - 1$, which does not divide $p^n$. So $\mathbb{Q}_p(p^{1/p^\infty})$ contains no primitive $p$-th root of unity, and a field isomorphism would have to carry $\zeta_p$ to one, since it fixes the coefficients of $\Phi_p$.

**What this says about Galois groups of fields.**

- *Characteristic.* The corollary gives fields of characteristic $0$ whose absolute Galois group is that of $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$. So the characteristic of $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$ is not determined by its absolute Galois group, which is one reason why the [characterization of $\mathbb{F}_q((t))$](local-fields-characteristic-p-brauer-characterization.md) needs extra data. For finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ it is different: a field with $G_K \cong G_F$ for a finite $F/\mathbb{Q}_p$ is elementarily equivalent to a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$, so it has characteristic $0$ (see [fields with $p$-adic absolute Galois groups](fields-with-p-adic-absolute-galois-groups.md)).
- *Lost information.* For finite $K/\mathbb{Q}_p$, the group $G_K$ determines $[K:\mathbb{Q}_p]$ and the maximal subfield of $K$ abelian over $\mathbb{Q}_p$ (see [the classification](isomorphism-classes-of-p-adic-galois-groups.md)). By the corollary, the isomorphism type of $G_{K(\mu_{p^\infty})}$ depends only on the residue field of $K(\mu_{p^\infty})$.
- *Ramification.* Part 3 matches the filtration that $G_K$ induces on $G_L$ with the ramification filtration of the field of characteristic $p$, after the change of variable $\psi_{L/K}$. Isomorphisms of Galois groups that preserve ramification filtrations come from field isomorphisms, by [Mochizuki](mochizuki-local-fields-theorem.md) for finite extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ and by [Abrashkin](abrashkin-local-grothendieck-theorem.md) for complete discrete valuation fields with finite residue fields of any characteristic.

**Tilting.** For a complete valued field $E$ with perfect residue field of characteristic $p$, Wintenberger defines a perfect field $R(E)$ of characteristic $p$, whose multiplicative monoid is the inverse limit of copies of $E$ under $x \mapsto x^p$ [@wintenberger1983, 4.1.1 and Theorem 4.1.2]. When $L/K$ is strictly APF and $M$ is a separable algebraic extension of $L$, $R(\hat M)$ is identified with the completion of the perfect closure of $X_{L/K}(M)$, and $R(\hat M)$ plays a large role in the study of certain $p$-adic representations [@wintenberger1983, p. 59]. The [tilt](perfectoid-tilting-and-galois-groups.md) $K^\flat$ of a perfectoid field $K$, a construction of Fontaine, has the same description as a multiplicative monoid, and Scholze's Theorem 1.3 states that the absolute Galois groups of $K$ and $K^\flat$ are canonically isomorphic [@scholze2012, Definition 1.2 and Theorem 1.3].
